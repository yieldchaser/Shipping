The Atlantic waters have been muddied with the Nigerian tax question, but on the whole owners who offer on Nigerian barrels have understandably been creating elaborate wording to protect themselves. ## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2023-06-21 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

After so many weeks in the doldrums, it’s refreshing to see material movements in the LNG freight market; however, those that enjoy a steady, ordered existence may prefer to skip to the rates this week. The volatility in the LNG markets that led to a contango emerging and then softening, and to an arbitrage that opened and then closed, has now filtered through to shipping, and more specifically to how traders and portfolios will manage their fleets as we get closer to winter. The swing between theoretical lowest- and highest-case scenarios for ship utilisation during the second half of the year (considering voyage distances and any floating plays) can mean many LNG players are facing up to being overly-long or worryingly-short, or anything in-between. The result is a fascinating mix of strategies, with some vessels being withdrawn from the market and held internally, while at the same time other ships are being shown for winter and are generating interest. On the spot side, the market is firming off the back of more enquiries and the redistribution of some Atlantic vessels into the Pacific. There were no newbuilding orders reported this week.
