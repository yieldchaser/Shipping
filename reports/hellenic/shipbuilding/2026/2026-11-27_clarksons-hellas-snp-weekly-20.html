CLARKSONS HELLAS – SnP WEEKLY

Iran insists the strait stays closed until its demands are met, while Washington has widened sanctions on Iranian networks. Saudi infrastructure was also targeted again, with the Jazan and Ras Tanura refineries hit this week. Brent is heading for a weekly gain of around 7% ahead of Sunday’s OPEC+ meeting.

Tanker rates rebounded across most segments this week after the recent correction. VLCC spot earnings rose 11% w/w to around $303,900/day, supported by firmer US Gulf–China rates, although they remain over 40% below a month ago. Suezmaxes gained 10% to $204,000/day and Aframaxes 9% to $181,500/day, with North Sea rates up 18%. Product tankers also firmed:

LR1s rose 14% to $85,400/day, MRs gained 10% to $45,200/day and LR2s edged up to $134,400/day, while Handysizes fell 30%.

On the wet S&P front, sentiment is broadly unchanged from last week. The softening in freight over the past month has yet to be reflected in asset values, but overall tanker activity has slowed noticeably compared with previous months, and the crude segment in particular has seen little movement.

Interest has instead shifted towards chemical tankers ranging from 13,000 dwt units to J19s and tonnage up to 25,000 dwt.

These are seeing strong interest on both sides, with more owners looking to sell and a steady flow of buyer enquiries.

In dry bulk, Capesize rates firmed this week, with TCE earnings up 13% w/w to $46,300/day, led by the Pacific, where round voyages gained 27%. Australia–China iron ore freight rose 15% w/w.

The smaller sizes were broadly stable: Kamsarmaxes rose 7% to $20,500/day, while Ultramaxes and Handysizes held at around $23,000/day and $19,500/day.

Looking ahead, the forward curve sits below spot, with Capesize Cal-27 FFAs at $29,800/day. Even so, the longer-term demand outlook remains constructive, as the Simandou ramp-up and growing Guinean bauxite exports add long-haul cargoes. These, together with imported iron ore replacing Chinese domestic production, an support tonne-mile demand even while Chinese steel output remains subdued.

On the dry S&P front, buying appetite continues to follow the trend of recent weeks. Far Eastern buyers remain fairly active, with steady enquiries for Panamaxes and Supramaxes of around 20 years of age.

One segment standing out this week is eco Ultramaxes, both Japanese and Chinese-built, which are drawing interest from Western and Eastern buyers alike. In the larger sizes,
the recent firming in Capesize charter rates has revived buyer interest, particularly for quality 15-year-old units but also for Chinese built older tonnage of around 20 years.

All in all, it was a week of recovery on the freight side, with VLCCs, Suezmaxes and Capesizes all posting double-digit gains. On the S&P front, however, buyers are becoming more selective.

Activity in the crude tanker segment has been somewhat slower, while chemical tankers, eco Ultramaxes and Capes are where most of the interest is right now.

As we head into December, activity is likely to ease ahead of the holiday period, although with the situation in Hormuz still unresolved and OPEC+ meeting this Sunday, geopolitical developments could still bring bouts of volatility.

Dry Cargo

Whilst the Supramax, AFRICA NOVA (ABT 57K DWT, 2012, NINGBO BEILUN) was sold at USD 13.8m.
