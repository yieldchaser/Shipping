CLARKSONS HELLAS – SnP WEEKLY

Hellenic Shipping News  |  13 November 2026  |  https://www.hellenicshippingnews.com/clarksons-hellas-snp-weekly-18

In the aftermath of the US midterm elections, oil prices have softened and freight rates have started to ease, at least for the time being.Asset values, however, remain relatively firm, as shipowners, traders and charterers seek to assess the evolving market dynamics and anticipate what lies ahead, particularly in relation to the operational inefficiencies that have driven tanker freight rates to extraordinary levels since March.

Even if an agreement is reached between the US and Iran, a swift return to normality in tanker operations is unlikely.

Security concerns, insurance requirements and the gradual unwinding of shuttle tanker operations and ship-to-ship (STS) transfers will continue to disrupt established trading patterns and constrain operational efficiency.

Consequently, the normalisation of tanker markets is likely to be a gradual process, rather than an immediate response to any political breakthrough.

Meanwhile, in the dry bulk segment, Capesize earnings have strengthened further, reaching approximately USD 43,900/day and their highest levels in six weeks.

Improved Atlantic activity, increasing Guinean bauxite exports and the faster-than-expected ramp-up of the Simandou project are providing additional support to the segment. With Simandou’s first-phase capacity of 60 mtpa potentially being reached ahead of schedule, further increases in iron ore volumes could provide a positive demand driver for Capesize tonnage.

Recent market momentum continues to underpin buyer appetite for geared tonnage aged around 10–15 years, spanning from Handysize upto Ultramax segments. Meanwhile, within the non-geared sector, we have observed increased interest in Post-Panamax vessels.
