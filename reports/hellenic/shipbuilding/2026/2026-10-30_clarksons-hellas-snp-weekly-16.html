CLARKSONS HELLAS – SnP WEEKLY

Hellenic Shipping News  |  30 October 2026  |  https://www.hellenicshippingnews.com/clarksons-hellas-snp-weekly-16

This week, tanker activity has increased compared with the previous few weeks across most segments.

VLCC spot rates appear to have established a near-term floor of around $500,000/day, with the global average across the mainstream roots currently assessed at $564,300 today.

In the product tanker market, contrary to much of this year, MR TCE averages are now at similar levels across both Atlantic and Pacific basins, with earnings captured globally being assessed at $86,500/day. Buying appetite continues to hold at firm, stable levels across all tanker segments as we observe a stabilization of the asset prices as well.

Meanwhile, according to Associated Press, US officials have cautioned regional allies about the possibility of renewed military strikes lasting several days following the 3 November elections, alongside potential Iranian retaliation against energy infrastructure.

Such developments could result in sustained damage to energy infrastructure or disruptions to shuttling operations, potentially reducing cargo availability for onward VLCC voyages and a reduction in cargo volumes could ultimately offset the positive impact of tighter vessel availability. Conversely, a decisive military outcome or a peace agreement enabling the resumption of safe, direct voyages could bring additional tonnage back into the market and put downward pressure on freight rates.

Nevertheless, we consider both scenarios unlikely in the near term.

On Dry, despite a weaker start to the week, Capesize has shown positive momentum, with both basins continuing to strengthen, reflecting an improved market sentiment overall.

Brazilian iron ore and Guinean bauxite exports remain firm despite a higher y-o-y vessel availability in the South Atlantic. On the non-geared space, we notice healthy interest in 15–20-year-old, but also on modern ECO Capesize and Kamsarmax.

On the geared space, buying interest is widespread, but there is an evident persisting Far Eastern appetite for vintage Supramax tonnage.

Dry Cargo

In the Capesize sector, CAPE ALLIANCE (ABT 206,190 DWT, 2007, IMABARI) was sold in the high USD 30m’s.

In the Supramax segment, CHARADE (ABT 58,109 DWT, 2013, TSUNEISHI CEBU, C:4X30T) was sold at a price in the region of USD 22.5m to undisclosed interests, whilst BAHAMAS L (ABT 56,141 DWT, 2012, MITSUI SB (TAMANO), C:4X30.5T) was sold to clients of ADNOC at USD 22.25m.

Finally, in the Handysize sector, CETUS TIGER (ABT 37,059 DWT, 2013, SAIKI HVY. IND., C:4X30T) was sold to clients of HTK Shipping at USD 18.3m, while ASTRO POLARIS (ABT 37,054 DWT, 2014, ONOMICHI DOCKYARD, C:4X30T) was sold to Greek interests at USD 20m.
