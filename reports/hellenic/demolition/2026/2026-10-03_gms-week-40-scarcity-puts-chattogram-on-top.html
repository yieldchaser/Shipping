GMS Week 40 – Scarcity Puts Chattogram On Top

Hellenic Shipping News  |  03 October 2026  |  https://www.hellenicshippingnews.com/gms-week-40-scarcity-puts-chattogram-on-top

The Gulf supplied another reminder this week that improved cargo flows do not equal normal shipping conditions.Several tankers were reportedly struck by unknown projectiles while transiting the Strait of Hormuz, followed by another tanker incident on October 1 in which a vessel caught fire, with the crew reported safe. Gulf crude movements have recovered significantly from earlier disruption, but passage risk, security and insurance remain firmly embedded in the voyage calculation.

Oil nevertheless eased into Friday as improving Middle East crude exports took some pressure out of the immediate supply story. Brent traded around USD 101 per barrel and WTI near USD 89, both below the extremes seen through September but still far removed from pre-conflict levels. The barrels are increasingly finding a route. The cost and risk of moving them remain another matter.

Dry freight finally gave some ground, although not enough to materially improve recycling supply. The Baltic Dry Index recovered modestly to 3,140 on Thursday after four consecutive declines, with Capesizes at 5,010, Panamaxes at 2,379 and Supramaxes at 1,794. The headline index is well below last week’s levels, but earnings remain sufficiently attractive to keep many ageing vessels employed rather than heading for recycling. Softer freight is useful. Cheap freight would be more useful.

Currencies provided no common direction. The Indian Rupee weakened toward 96.3 against the Dollar before Friday’s Gandhi Jayanti holiday, while the Pakistani Rupee remained around 277 and Bangladesh’s interbank reference stayed close to 123.1. The Turkish Lira slipped beyond 49 to the Dollar. Pakistan delivered the week’s fresh regional inflation number, with September CPI easing to 10.26% from August’s 11.1%, providing some macro relief without materially altering Gadani’s buying equation.

At the beaches, physical activity remains considerably healthier than the fresh sales pipeline. Chattogram has processed a substantial run of previously secured tonnage, including large gas and tanker units, while Gadani has also received and delivered vessels secured during its earlier buying run. Alang remained quieter through the local holiday period. The distinction that defined September therefore survives into October: yards are working, but today’s owners are still not replenishing tomorrow’s supply quickly enough.

The commercial hierarchy changed more decisively this week. Bangladesh moved ahead of Pakistan to reclaim the top position across the conventional board, with Chattogram recyclers bidding more aggressively for the very limited selection of available candidates. Pakistan moved into second place as part of its immediate requirement has already been satisfied by vessels secured and delivered over recent months, although underlying demand remains firm. India held its conventional levels and continues to find stronger interest in specialist tonnage. The important point is that the change in leadership has not been driven by a sudden increase in vessel supply. Buyers are competing harder because the opposite remains true. Bangladesh has taken the lead. The shortage remains the market.
