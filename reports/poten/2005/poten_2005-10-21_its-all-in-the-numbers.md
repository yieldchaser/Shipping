# It's All in the Numbers

To be sure, the Hurricanes of 2005 have had substantial effects on U.S. refineries, inventories, imports, and consumption. This week we take numbers from the Energy Intelligence Agency and analyze how substantial the drop-off has been.

Prior to the hurricanes, production and inventories of gasoline, diesel, and jet fuel were above year earlier levels, while imports were slightly below 2004 levels. Post-Katrina and Rita, production and inventories of these three crucial products are below year earlier levels, but imports are well above last year's levels. Indicative consumption (imports + production + inventory draws or minus inventory builds) is higher now versus year earlier levels, as shown in the previous chart. The following table shows the year-to-year apparent consumption.

Apparent Refined Products Consumption (June through mid-October) Gasoline Gasoil Jet Fuel Total 2004 10.23 4.14 1.75 16.12 2005 10.53 4.09 1.78 16.40 It is true that consumption has dropped since September 1. But it is also true that the previous six-weeks' consumption of gasoline, gasoil, and jet fuel is above year earlier levels for the same period. So the situation is not as dire as it might seem to be.
