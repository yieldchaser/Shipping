# Thriving Through the Storm

As the winds of misfortune shape the revival of the World's economic future, a maxim - perhaps forgotten by some - still rings ever true. In real estate ventures, the accepted recipe for success is "location, location, location" and nowadays some players in the shipping industry see this recipe as "relationship, relationship, relationship." At the 15th annual Joint Shipping Conference held in New York City this week, the importance of established relationships emerged as a central theme. Ship owners, bankers, and other industry professionals were gathered there to ponder the question of "How Will Shipping Survive the Perfect Storm?"

With a seemingly pervasive sentiment, attendees viewed the current "storm" pattern as different from prior downturns weathered by the shipping industry. Additionally, common to both bankers and ship owners was their focus on the importance of relationships in structuring deals. According to conference panelists, banks are currently deleveraging and these measures have significantly impeded the availability of ship financing. This constriction may continue for, perhaps, another 18 months. During their flight to quality, banks seem to prefer customers with whom they have established relationships. But even their established customers have witnessed substantial adjustments in traditional banking relationships. While the shape of vessel financing may be undergoing a nip and tuck facelift, established and strong relationships - as always - remain a pivotal key to launching deals.

Some participants wondered if the shipping industry was entering a period reminiscent of one characteristic of a decade ago. Back then, the sale and purchase of assets provided a ready source of revenue to some owners. This breed of owner placed value on acquiring vessel assets at seemingly bargain prices with the intention of subsequently selling them at better prices. Sometimes a low acquired priced vessel might be retained - even though its owner saw a higher resale price - if the expectations for the asset's future time charter rates were strong. Should the tacit of buying low in the hope of selling higher become more widely adopted, opportunities for attracting private capital may reappear. Additionally, even those owners who are focused on the financial power of extended time charters to carry their vessels - and perhaps less on the relative price of the asset - may now also look specifically to acquire valued assets with prices deemed to be low.

Some ship owners are in a mode to just survive this economic downturn. Other players - perhaps with deeper pockets - seem poised, however, to not only survive, but more importantly, to resume their drive to thrive as these uncertain times abate. Once this disorienting economic fog is perceived as lifting, some owners may seize the moment to begin accumulating vessels at what they consider attractive values. But asset bottom fishing in these turbulent economic times is not a sport for the slow- footed. By the time a market bottom is widely perceived, asset hunters late to the party might find themselves outmaneuvered by the astute early birds.
