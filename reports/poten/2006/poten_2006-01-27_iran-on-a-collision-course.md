# Iran - On a Collision Course

The United Nations Security Council may take up the issue of trade sanctions against Iran depending on developments related to their nuclear intentions. If this occurs, Iran is threatening to curb its oil exports. This issue has oil markets nervous (but not tanker markets just yet) as Iran is a large source of crude oil exports. The following chart traces Iran exports since 1965. Exports peaked at 5.6 million bpd in the 1970s, collapsed during the Revolution and have been in a recovery mode since then. Current exports are about 2.5 million bpd net of production of 4.1 million bpd and domestic consumption of 1.6 million bpd.

Can The World Survive Without Iran? Assuming that Iran stopped exporting 2.5 million bpd, an estimated 1.5 million bpd of incremental production may be available from Saudi Arabia and Russia. This would leave a deficit of 1 million bpd. The BTC pipeline is in the process of coming on stream and will help to close the gap with its expected 1 million bpd output by the end of 2006. The U.S. Strategic Petroleum Reserve, with close to a billion barrels in salt caverns, can sustain a 1 million bpd drawdown for nearly three years. Thus it seems possible that through SPR drawdowns and stepped up production in Saudi Arabia and Russia that cessation of Iranian exports in the short-to-medium term would not bring the world to its knees, but rather simply increase crude oil prices. While the Iran leadership may look forward to threatening global economies, that doesn't necessarily mean that they will do so without risking potential adverse internal consequences. Oil importers other than the United States may not sit idly by and watch their economies crumble with equanimity. In summary, the world would survive a stoppage of oil exports from Iran; the real question is "Will Iran?"
