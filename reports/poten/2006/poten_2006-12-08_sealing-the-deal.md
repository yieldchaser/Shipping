# Sealing The Deal

The significant increase in newbuilding tanker prices is well known. From $67.3 million in October of 2003, prices climbed rapidly over the next 18 months and have remained relatively steady at a price in the range of $128 million. With a strong orderbook for all vessel sectors, the market for newbuilding vessels does not appear to be losing momentum. This phase-out regulation has led to increased ordering at shipyards, but there are several other initiatives that may further increase the price of tankers or keep them at present levels. Vessel design changes and coating standards are just two of the issues under consideration in the regulatory arena today.

Regulatory programs are designed to have an industry-wide effect with increased crew, vessel and environmental safety in mind. Though not imposed by a regulatory body, the Common Structural Rules (CSR) have been adopted by major classification societies to reduce competition for approved designs on the basis of steel weight of a vessel and emphasize vessel reliability and safety. While these specific guidelines have been set for the structure of vessels, other regulations are designed to protect the vessel from material deterioration on the inside.

### A Primer for Coatings

Protective coatings have traditionally been applied to cargo tanks in vessels that are designed to carry clean products. The newbuilding market is seeing increased interest on coating larger vessels. While many owners are realizing the benefit of coated cargo tanks from a commercial perspective, specifically in the clean product trades, the protective benefits for coated cargo and ballast tanks in crude carriers are also being recognized.

Today, coating of ballast tanks is required according to existing IMO regulations. By mid-2008, the IMO will impose a new set of guidelines to perfect the coating application process known as the Performance Standards for Protective Coating (PSPC.) The standards have been developed in an effort to protect ballast tanks from premature corrosion that can have catastrophic consequences. The PSPC will affect those vessels ordered on or after July 1 st 2008, or for delivery date on or after January 1 st 2012. The primary points of the IMO's regulation are that the coating performance standards should be applied to all ballast and void spaces on all ships, and that the target coating life should be at least 15 years.

Currently, performance standards for cargo tank coatings are under consideration. The IMO expects that coatings will be mandatory on all cargo tanks and ballast tanks on crude and product carriers in the future, and all will be subject to performance standards.

The price of coating a vessel has increased marginally making the incremental cost to an owner an increasingly smaller percentage of the total price of the vessel. Aside from practicalities associated with better protection of the asset, the commercial value of coated cargo tanks will increase as product trades develop in the future. With vessel prices at all time highs, owners are finding it a bit easier to splurge on the coating. With expected increased regulations in the future, owners should find the higher coating costs today a bit easier to justify economically, and in the long term will have a vessel what will be able to better withstand the rigors of worldwide trading.
