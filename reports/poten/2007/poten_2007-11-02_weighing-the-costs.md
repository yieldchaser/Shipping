# Weighing the Costs

### Bunker Prices & WS Rates

While it is sometimes hard for the general public to engender sympathy for shipowners that have enjoyed record earnings in recent years, the fact remains that in today's market fuel costs are an increasingly higher percentage of total earnings. 2007 $40,357 **Laden Consumption assume VLCC (92 MT per day) / Handymax (34 MT per day) At present, the two vessel segments that are getting hardest hit by higher bunker prices in are the VLCCs and the Handymaxes. At current market levels, on a VLCC Arabian Gulf - Far East voyage over 60% of total voyage revenue goes toward paying the fuel bill, as compared to a year ago when 31% of total revenue went to fuel. In the Handymax market today, just over 50% goes to fuel on a Caribs-up trade as compared to 35% a year ago. What is interesting is that voyage revenue for these trades is essentially the same today as compared to last years' levels, yet the earnings are a smaller percentage.

### Few Signs of Relief

Clearly, the increase in bunker prices can be tied to higher overall crude oil prices, but relative price strengths of products is a key driver for refiners looking to increase margins. Unfortunately for shipowners, the current price strength of fuel oil to crude oil is only about 65%. With gasoline and other light refined products over 100%, refiners are likely to maximize production of those light transportation fuels that can lead to higher margins. With little incentive for refiners to ease lighter fuel production, a bunker price that feels expensive today, may feel like a bargain in a few months.
