# Expensive Gas for the Golden State

### California refinery outages help product tankers

California has regularly been on the front pages of the newspapers in recent weeks with the forest fires that raged in several locations and with deliberate power outages meant to prevent additional fires. Much less prominent, there were some news reports of several tankers diverting gasoline cargoes, loaded in Europe, to California. Normally California imports only small volumes of gasoline and imports from Europe are infrequent, so we were wondering what is going on here.

Gasoline prices in California are typically higher than they are in the rest of the U.S. This is in part because California charges higher gasoline taxes than any other U.S. state. According to the American Petroleum Institute (API), California has combined federal, state and local taxes of 80.45 cents per gallon, compared to a U.S. average of 54.70 cents.

California has a large population and 13% of all U.S. motor vehicles are registered in California. To reduce emissions and the environmental impact, the state has enacted stricter gasoline standards than other states, which increases the cost of the gasoline and also makes it more difficult to import California spec gasoline. Additionally, there are no pipelines available to bring petroleum products into California from other refining areas in the country, so imports are normally on tankers.

California is largely independent for its petroleum product needs. The state has 11 refineries that produce California spec gasoline with a total crude oil processing capacity of 1.9 Million barrels per day (Mb/d). The refineries are mainly concentrated in the Los Angeles and San Francisco areas. According to the State of California, the state consumed 1.01 Mb/d of gasoline in 2019. Our import statistics show that California imported only 28.3 Kb/d of gasoline this year, a tiny fraction of the consumption. The whole U.S. West Coast is relatively isolated from the rest of the country and production and demand are finely balanced.

Even though none of the refineries were directly impacted by the wildfires or the power outages that have affected many of the citizens of California, several refineries were hit by planned and unplanned outages in recent weeks. According to press reports, 2 refineries reported unplanned flaring incidents and one reported planned flaring. The Chevron El Segundo refinery FCC unit might be down for several weeks.

As a result of the outages, gasoline prices at the pump have increased to more than $4/gallon (see first graph) in recent weeks. While this might sound like a good price for many of our readers in Europe, the average gasoline price in the U.S. is currently around $2.55/gallon.

Traders have noticed the price spike in California and several tankers have changed their destination from the US East Coast to the US West Coast. For example, the Ridgebury Katerine Z was reportedly fixed by Litasco in early October for a voyage from the Amsterdam-Rotterdam-Antwerp range (ARA) for a transatlantic voyage. The vessel initially showed a destination of New York and, on Oct 6, switched its destination to Los Angeles. Some charterers fixing Atlantic voyages have asked for US West Coast options. Since the beginning of the month, 9 additional product fixtures from Asia to the US West Coast have been reported.

We don't expect this situation to last very long as the refinery outages will most likely get resolved relatively quickly but the situation shows the vulnerability of the region to supply or demand shocks. The US West Coast is far from producing areas and it takes time for ships to reach the area in case of similar issues. As the above import graph shows, there have been several spikes in imports in the last two years, providing opportunities for owners.
