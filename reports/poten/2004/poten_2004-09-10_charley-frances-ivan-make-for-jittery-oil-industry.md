# Charley, Frances & Ivan Make for Jittery Oil Industry

### Charley, Frances & Ivan

Make for Jittery Oil Industry Anyone who doubted or maybe just forgot that the end of summer into early fall is hurricane season, let the rule of three forever be the reminder. Hurricane Charley made landfall in Florida on Aug. 13 destroying an estimated $15 billion worth of property and homes. But it wasn't until Hurricane Frances blew over the Bahamas and into Florida last weekend that the oil industry had reason to worry. Just before Labor Day, Frances blew through the Bahamas, knocking the Bahamas Oil Refining Company International (BORCO) Terminal in Freeport out of commission. Now Hurricane Ivan is menacing the Caribbean after causing several deaths and an untold amount of damage on the island of Grenada. Ivan is currently carving its path through Jamaica and Cuba on its way to Florida's west coast. With winds reaching 145 mph, Ivan is expected to cause even more damage. The catch is that Ivan's predicted path is as wide as Mexico's Yucatan Peninsula and the west coast of Florida. Weather forecasters are doubtful that it will hit as far west as Texas. The US Gulf is responsible for 25% of US oil refining capacity and the potential for severe damage is a cause for jitters. With a potential cessation of production from offshore drilling rigs in the US Gulf, oil prices have increased to $44.60 as of Friday, an increase of $1.25 from Tuesday's price.

The hurricane would have to hit Louisiana or Texas for it to have any significant impact on US oil imports. With crude inventories trending down (see graph below) a potential threat to oil imports comes at an awkward time.
