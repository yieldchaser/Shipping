   MARKET OVERVIEW 
 
This week, US tariffs once again took centre stage as the US will collect duties of between 10% and 12.5% on 
imports from most major trading partners, its biggest move yet to reconstruct President Donald Trump’s 
tariff wall that was struck down by the Supreme Court. President Trump’s tariffs, imposed under Section 301 
of the Trade Act of 1974, are facing legal challenges. The duties cover goods from more than 80 countries, 
representing 99.4% of U.S. trade, and are officially aimed at combating forced labour. Critics argue the 
administration is using Section 301 to recreate the broad tariff regime previously struck down by the 
Supreme Court under IEEPA. Legal experts remain divided: some believe the measures exceed the statute’s 
intended scope, while others note Section 301 gives the government wide discretion. Businesses are 
advised to plan around the tariffs, as court challenges could take considerable time.  
 
On the other hand, the global energy scene is facing unprecedented vulnerability as the secondary 
maritime workaround designed to bypass Persian Gulf blockades comes under severe threat. For months, 
major Middle Eastern oil producers relied heavily on overland pipeline infrastructure to transfer crude to 
western Red Sea terminals, effectively maintaining export volumes despite the near -total closure of the 
Strait of Hormuz.  
However, this critical alternative route has been severely compromised following an aggressive surge in 
maritime strikes along the southern Red Sea corridor. The resumption of regional missile and drone 
attacks targeting commercial vessels near the Bab el-Mandeb Strait has effectively opened a secondary 
maritime front, pushing global crude benchmarks back above one hundred dollars a barrel for the first 
time in two months and exposing the extreme fragility of regional bypass strategies.  
 
This sudden escalation along the western coast stems from a rapid deterioration in local political 
agreements, culminating in a formal maritime blockade declaration against regional shipping. Following 
localised military strikes that disrupted regional aviation hubs, coastal forces retaliated by launching 
targeted attacks against active commercial oil tankers, setting multiple vessels ablaze and forcing several 
others to make abrupt U-turns. With the Bab el-Mandeb Strait historically handling a significant portion of 
global seaborne trade, this renewed anti-shipping campaign directly threatens the millions of barrels of 
crude currently loaded at western terminals for export to major Asian markets. The simultaneous  
disruption of both primary Middle Eastern waterways has created a compounding logistical crisis, 
doubling war-risk insurance premiums overnight and leaving commercial fleet operators with minimal 
safe transit options.

The global dry bulk market demonstrated underlying resilience, with the benchmark BDI  snapping out of a 
multi-day decline at the end of week to rise to 2,743 points. On a weekly comparison, this reflects a 
broader cooling of 3.42% across the main index, largely influenced by a turbulent week in the Capesize 
segment. Although Capesize spot rates experienced sharp volatility earlier in the week, the BCI rebounded 
to 4,285 points as Australian iron ore exports stabilised following a brief eight-hour protected stoppage by 
BHP workers at Port Hedland—where average vessel waiting times hit multi-month highs of 8.7 days. In 
contrast, BPI dropped to 2,024 points, while the BSI slipped to 1,694 points. 
 
Beyond immediate rate movements, market attention is closely watching key logistical bottlenecks, 
particularly in the Panama Canal. Despite reporting strong fiscal third-quarter performance with daily 
transits averaging 35 vessels and total transit revenue reaching US$4.8 billion (up 17% y-o-y), the Panama 
Canal Authority warned that the probability of a severe El Niño event has surged to 81%. This looming 
weather threat raises the strong likelihood of future draft and daily booking restrictions, positioning canal 
transit constraints as a critical variable for dry bulk trade flows moving into the second half of the year.  
Segment Highlights 
Capesize: Capesize faced downward pressure this week as returning vessels delayed by Chinese port 
disruptions expanded Pacific tonnage length, driving competition on the West Australia to China route. 
Slowing fixture activity across Atlantic long-haul routes and rising bunker costs further dampened 
sentiment, though stable iron ore supply from Brazil helped keep the market active. Despite the underlying 
spot caution, route averages rebounded from earlier lows, lifting Pacific R/V to US$30,875’s, and T/A trips to 
US$46,050’s. 
 
Panamax / Kamsarmax: Panamax drifted softer overall this week as slowing N. Atlantic cargo volumes, 
sluggish USG grain activity, and an accumulation of prompt ballast tonnage in the East combined to 
weigh down owner sentiment across both basins. While Brazilian grain demand and steady South 
American cargo flows initially provided a relative floor, an influx of ballasting ships arriving from Asia 
created a two-tier market in the South and capped forward rate momentum. Spot returns faced a general 
decline across major routes, dragging T/A rates down to US$20,400’s with Pacific R/V to US$15,550’s. 
 
Supramax / Ultramax: Atlantic rates held a firm yet mixed stance supported by a steady influx of USG and 
East Med cargoes against limited prompt tonnage, even as T/A rates slipped to US$28,950’s and F/H trips 
eased to US$29,950 per day. Across Asia, slowing Chinese coal demand and weaker northern sentiment 
capped upward momentum, though consistent SE Asian activity and steady Indian Ocean cargo flows 
helped cushion Pacific R/V at US$16,980’s.  
 
Handysize: Handysize remained balanced across Europe this week, while the USG and South Atlantic 
faced ongoing pressure from expanding tonnage availability. Both Atlantic and Pacific basins continue to 
await stronger cargo flows and increased fixture activity to establish a clearer market direction. Amidst 
this cautious backdrop, spot returns drifted slightly lower, bringing T/A rates down to US$16,750’s and Inter-
Pacific routes to US$15,350’s per day.

Global energy markets surged for a fifth consecutive session as escalating maritime threats in the Red 
Sea placed critical Middle Eastern export channels under severe strain. Brent crude rose 4.21% to pass 
US$98.03 per barrel, while West Texas Intermediate climbed 3.16% to reach US $89.57, extending cumulative 
price gains to nearly 20% over the past fortnight. The latest price surge follows Houthi strikes on two Saudi -
linked tankers, the Encelia and Layla, as they navigated the Bab el-Mandeb Strait. This development 
directly threatens Saudi Arabia’s primary workaround via the Red Sea port of Yanbu, through which the 
kingdom had successfully rerouted more than 70% of its crude exports to circumvent the Persian Gulf 
blockade. With vessels transporting crude to major Asian buyers like India and China turning away from 
the chokepoint, concerns over simultaneous supply bottlenecks across both primary regional waterways 
have intensified. 
 
Operations through the Strait of Hormuz remain exceptionally hazardous, though a handful of stealth 
transits continue to take place. Over a 24-hour period, three VLCCs carrying an estimated 6 million barrels 
of crude successfully exited the Persian Gulf into the Gulf of Oman. Carrying cargoes from Iraq, Qatar, and 
the UAE, all three supertankers made the transit with their automated positioning transponders 
deactivated to avoid detection and lower attack risks. Although one ship is currently signalling  an arrival in 
Rizhao, China, for mid-August, mainstream charterers remain overwhelmingly hesitant to commit 
tonnage without firm safety assurances.  
 
Segment Highlights 
VLCC: Middle East VLCC market rose by roughly 5% as escalating attacks in the Strait of Hormuz and Gulf 
of Oman tightened tonnage, with ongoing geopolitical risks expected to keep regional rates firm. 
Conversely, the Atlantic market is set to soften as owners flee Middle Eastern hazards, boosting 
transatlantic and eastbound demand from alternative hubs like the USG and West Africa. 
 
Suezmax: West African market faces a slightly softer trajectory this week as a steady stream of ballast 
vessels fleeing Middle East risks expands Atlantic supply and caps overall momentum. However, this 
downward pressure is tempered by consistent cargo flows out of West Africa and the CPC, while tight 
regional VLCC availability and surging U.S. Gulf Aframax rates continue to bolster underlying sentiment 
across the basin. 
 
Aframax: Mediterranean market remains strong in the high WS300s, supported by delays in Trieste and 
potential ballast departures despite a growing local tonnage list and risks of Black Sea loading 
cancellations. Meanwhile, the Middle East sector faces a slightly softer trend as a buildup of available 
vessels overshadows steady short-haul demand and lingering geopolitical risk premiums around the 
Strait of Hormuz. 
 
LR1 / LR2: Middle East LR2 rates surged by roughly 25% w-o-w as escalating geopolitical risks triggered 
fierce competition among charterers and caused available tonnage to tighten rapidly. The upward 
momentum is expected to build further this week as shipowners hold out for higher quotes and demand 
spills into alternative regional loading hubs like Fujairah, Oman, and the Red Sea.

   CONTAINERS  
 
The global container shipping market continued its post-peak correction this week as the SCFI index fell to 
close at 3,062.95 points, driven primarily by easing supply conditions and slowing cargo bookings across 
the U.S. West Coast and South America. In contrast, U.S. East Coast routes posted slight rate gains due to 
restricted vessel supply tied to extended sailing distances via the Cape of Good Hope, Panama Canal 
constraints, and lingering European port backlogs.  
 
Meanwhile, Middle East trades bucked the broader downward trend, surging as escalating security risks in 
the Strait of Hormuz—including recent vessel attacks and an Iranian port blockade—prompted a sharp 
drop in transits; in response, carriers like Maersk introduced a new US$1,000 per container fee on July 23rd 
for Gulf-bound cargo while managing alternative land bridge connections from the Red Sea. Overall, with 
lines balancing rate discounts against tactical capacity management and Hapag- Lloyd signalling mid-
July rate defence measures, the market appears positioned for a cycle of moderate corrections and 
short-term rebounds rather than a severe drop.

Market Insights 
 
Alang, India: The ship recycling market in Alang saw local steel plate prices firm slightly, driven by a 
temporary tightness in supply as heavy monsoon rains hampered cutting operations faster than a 
weakening rupee could offset. However, this price recovery remains fragile, with overall sentiment 
subdued due to weak domestic demand for finished steel and a quiet conclusion to the mid July tide 
window as several vessels remain anchored off the coast waiting for the next tide cycle.  
 
The local market dynamics unfold against broader economic headwinds, as escalating Middle East 
tensions have pushed global crude oil prices over US$100 per barrel and driven the Rupee toward record 
lows against the USD. 
 
Despite these pressures, the Indian shipbreaking sector received a landmark boost this week through a 
major regulatory breakthrough from the European Union. In a draft proposal opened for public 
consultation, the European Commission recommended two Alang r ecycling facilities, YSI Recyclers and 
Shree Ram, for inclusion on the official EU list of approved ship recycling yards.  
 
Historically, European regulations have restricted flagged tonnage to facilities within Europe, Turkey, and 
select non-Asian regions due to environmental and safety concerns; however, years of extensive capital 
investments and compliance with the Hong Kong International Convention have paved the way for these 
Indian yards to gain recognition.

Chattogram, Bangladesh: Chattogram maintained a firm undertone, with ship recyclers showing

Longterm industrial prospects for the Chattogram region remain promising, bolstered by major 
infrastructure pushes such as the groundbreaking of the 600-acre Chinese Economic and Industrial Zone

domestic demand for recycled steel.

Gaddani, Pakistan: Gadani recyclers are actively seeking candidates amidst an ongoing shortage of 
scrap metal. This supply deficit, exacerbated by reduced scrap flows from neighbouring Iran, continues to 
provide underlying support for local recycling demand despite a persistent scarcity of available 
international tonnage.  
 
Local ship recyclers kept their price offers firm across the board, matching a late-week rally in domestic

optimism rests heavily on recent fiscal reforms, including reduced property withholding taxes and 
subsidised housing initiatives, which are expected to boost construction activity once the monsoon clears.  
 
Currently, Gadani recyclers remain well-positioned to compete with Indian yards on price.

Aliaga, Turkey: Aliaga recorded a slight positive correction, with both imported raw scrap and domestic 
finished steel prices edging up by roughly US$5/MT. However, this slight firming in steel values failed to 
boost vessel offers, as cautious yard operators kept purchasing rates unchanged between US$263 ~285 
per LDT, leaving Turkish recyclers structurally uncompetitive against South Asian buyers.  
 
This sluggish activity reflects broader domestic financial hurdles, where a continuously weakening Turkish 
lira degrades purchasing power for dollar-denominated hulls, and a high central bank interest rate of 37% 
makes operational financing exceptionally costly amidst a tight global candidate pool.

US$390–US$395/t CFR maintained a wide bid-offer gap and capped larger volumes.

   COMMODITIES, BUNKERS & RATES 
Commodities Focus 
 
Copper futures advanced toward US$6.53 per pound to approach seven-week peaks, supported by 
shrinking exchange inventories in London and Shanghai as well as a multi -year high in the Yangshan 
import premium at $115 per ton. This physical tightness has been reinforced by domestic smelter 
maintenance, restricted scrap supply, typhoon-related safety stockpiling in China, and active cargo 
redirection into the United States ahead of anticipated import tariffs.  
 
In the energy sector, thermal coal futures held near US$131 per ton following transport bottlenecks in 
Indonesia, where prolonged dry weather along the Barito River in Kalimantan hampered barging 
operations and forced select miners to declare force majeure.

daily average of 2.79 million tons in June and monthly imports rose 6.4% year -on-year to 112.69 million 
tons.

peak of 166.9 million tons, although upside momentum continues to be tempered by persistent property 
sector drags, where first-half real estate investment fell 18% and new construction starts dropped 23.4%
