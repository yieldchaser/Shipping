Crude Tanker Comments  
VLCCs had a very strong week. Deferred WAFR cargoes returning to the market and 
steady Brazil and USG demand come as prompt VLCC supply is particularly thin in the 
Atlantic. The result has been a shift in leverage towards owners; USG-East has climbed 
from USD 21 Mn to USD 25 Mn, whilst WAFR-East moved from around WS 148 to WS 
220. Brazil has performed very well, too, with Eastbound business moving from WS 
200.5 to WS 215. The Brazil-East vs. WAFR-East differential briefly stretched to around 
20 points, four times the usual spread, largely due to the strong Suezmax market; 
Brazil, meanwhile, remained firmly a VLCC market. Today’s WS 215 suggests that Brazil 
charterers now have to compete more for tonnage, reducing the differential. 
East of Suez has been equally strong. WS 759 was rumoured for inside AG-East, 
Fujairah-East reached WS 220, and Yanbu-East went on subs at WS 345. Demand is on 
the rise, and Middle East security concerns support tonne-miles. There are some signs 
that owners should keep their expectations in check; later dates, particularly ex-
Fujairah, show more availability, and a USG-China fixture reported at USD 24.4 Mn 
failed. For now, though, the balance remains firmly with owners; demand is building, 
tonnage is scarce, and the broader tanker markets continue to provide a strong floor.
The Premier League returns this week, and the Suezmax market shares similarities 
with a typical league campaign in last-minute substitutions, expensive fees, and points 
being put on the board. WAFR continued as it left off, with several late August and 
early September cargoes left over from last week and some replacement business 
boosting sentiment. Rates jumped from WS 260 to WS 330! Guyana followed suit, with 
rates now at 130KT x WS 330, roughly 55 points up on the week. The USG, however, 
has been quiet, with the more economical Aframaxes taking some business. 
But there is now a feeling that rates have topped out in the Atlantic, as owners look to 
lock in ships at these levels and charterers try to break other teams’ momentum, in 
this case by not working any more cargoes until next week. CPC rates fell to WS 550, 
25 points down. The higher earnings saw many ships prioritise this business over more 
vanilla Atlantic runs. East of Suez also saw rates move up, and Fujairah-East is now at 
WS 355! If you were a shipowner playing Fantasy Premier League with the Suezmax 
market, you would feel pleased with the number of points secured this week.
Owners enjoyed a steadily firming Med Aframax market this week. Early on, USG 
freight levels motivated vessels to ballast West. A continued flow of European tonnage 
to the USG, along with moderate cargo activity, tightened local supply and pushed 
rates up. Midweek, sentiment became more cautious as signs of weakness emerged in 
the USG, discouraging ballasting. Still, by the end of the week, owners held the upper 
hand; sufficient activity and limited tonnage availability allowed incremental rate gains 
and left room for more rises next week. TD19 sits at WS 257 on Friday.
The North Sea Aframax market remained broadly stable this week despite support 
from stronger markets elsewhere. At the beginning, rates held steady as vessels 
continued to ballast across the Atlantic. By Tuesday, firming conditions in the 
Mediterranean and USG suggested that North Sea levels would become increasingly 
difficult to repeat. A pickup in activity then helped maintain rates, although TD7 was 
much lower than TD25, encouraging further ballasting. By Thursday, local rates had 
surprisingly failed to respond despite the large TD25 premium, with activity still 
insufficient to tighten the market significantly. TD7 ends the week at WS 200.
Product Tanker Comments
It was not a very busy week for AG LR2s, but the market nevertheless remains firm. It 
is worth noting that much of the week’s activity has been taking place in the shadows, 
but either way, there is continued pressure on tonnage supply for what demand is out 
there. Westbound fixing has mostly been conducted privately, but USD 6.5 Mn has 
been bounced around the market basis UKC via Bab el-Mandeb. Naphtha East runs 
have not been thoroughly tested publicly, but freight can be estimated at WS 235 for 
TC1. There is little else going on for LR2s, but sentiment is still firm.
There is a much more positive outlook for AG LR1s to close the week; rates have 
ticked up when tested, and the tonnage list remains under pressure, with several 
vessels disappearing. TC5 jumped from WS 195 to WS 220 midweek but then demand 
dialled down. Westbound demand has been patchy, but this business has also firmed 
– a USD 300,000  jump on last-done levels to USD 4.2 Mn had been agreed basis UKC 
via Bab el-Mandeb, and next-done levels likely sit up at USD 4.5 Mn for the same. Not 
much demand has existed outside of Eastbound naphtha and Westbound ULSD and 
jet fuel business, but any levels tested are expected to be firmer.
It was largely an unexciting week for MRs in the region, with rates flat after some 
initial softening at the start of the week. Demand has not been overwhelming but has 
steadily spread across the week, and the majority has loaded out of WCI. TC17 
dropped to WS 275, and this was subsequently repeated several times. The list is still 
lean, and there could have been an opportunity for MR rates to bounce back by riding 
the coattails of the firming LRs, but this did not materialise. There is little demand 
outside of TC12 – latest paying WS 210 – and TC17, but it probably would not take 
much to increase the market temperature.
European LR1 activity unexpectedly strengthened due to an influx of available 
tonnage, creating chartering opportunities despite softer rates early in the week. 
Middle Eastern market strength supported owner sentiment and established a firm 
rate floor. LR2s remained tight, but weaker demand limited gains. Strong Middle 
Eastern markets should support firmer European freight levels going forward.
It was yet another trying week for Med MR owners, with volumes unable to keep up 
with an ever-growing tonnage list. Fundamentally, there should be further corrections 
here, but with TA paying 37KT x WS 115, West Med tonnage has opted to ballast 
across to the greener grass. A significant fleet remains in the East Med for end-month 
dates, which is likely to maintain pressure next week. Med-UKC last tested at 37KT x 
WS 140, with X-Med assessed at 37KT x WS 155. Longer hauls have largely remained 
untested this week, with WAFR and Brazil assessed at WS 160 and WS 165, 
respectively. 
Med Handy activity was lacklustre throughout the week, and thus there have been 
more corrections. Despite some owners attempting to hold at 30KT x WS 170, others 
have succumbed to sustained tonnage competition, and at the time of writing, 30KT x 
WS 165 is the consolidated rate. Rates have hit the floor reached in June, and as was 
the case two months ago, owners are struggling to justify moving their units, with 
bunkers still significantly hindering earnings. Expect rates to flatline here for at least 
the first half of next week, when the list will look bulky for end-months dates. A 
seriously active start is needed to give any bullish optimism for the second half.
