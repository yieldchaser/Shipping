## Market Report Content

CHARTERING MARKET AT HAND: 
COASTERS AND MINIBULKERS // WEEK 37
Coaster market: Continent & Baltic Sea market strengthens while Ukrainian 
Danube rally loses steam
Following the rally seen over the past few weeks, 
charterers have managed to halt the rise in freight 
rates ex Ukrainian Black Sea ports (even despite 
further growth of congestion while waiting to pass 
through the Sulina Canal, which now exceeds two 
weeks). In any case, it is currently impossible to 
objectively assess transportation costs from Reni and 
Izmail, since charterers are ready to pay $15-20/t more 
for vessels open inside the Danube or the Sulina than 
for those opening in the Black Sea. Similarly, charterers 
are willing to pay significantly more if the shipowner is 
willing to transit through Bystre. Note that shipments 
to the eastern Mediterranean have clearly slowed 
down, while traffic towards the Marmara Sea has 
picked up. “To be honest, this week is quieter than 
recent ones,” a Turkish shipowner commented to ISM. 
Rates for shipments to the Marmara Sea have seen a 
particularly significant upturn over the week (up $6-
8/t). For instance, rates for shipment of 3-7,000 t of 
wheat from Izmail or Reni to Marmara have increased 
to mid-high $80s/t, while those towards EgyptMed 
have inched up to $103-107/t. “We see more and more 
so called backhaul/return cargoes to the Danube, 
which are actually being fixed at not that high rates, 
since many owners are ready to take those cargoes in 
order to have an advantage for the next employment 
after the discharge,” a local broker added, sharing his 
thoughts on current market conditions. Freight rates 
for shipments from other Black Sea ports, as well as 
those in the neighboring Mediterranean Sea as a 
whole, have not seen any major shifts. Cargo volumes 
have grown slightly only in the eastern Mediterranean, 
while a surplus of tonnage persists throughout the

The market in the Baltic and North Seas continues 
to demonstrate seasonal growth in activity and 
freight rates. This trend is mainly supported by an 
increase in grain offer for shipments from European 
ports. Shipowners are successfully pushing rates up, 
although freights are often rising not as rapidly as 
owners had anticipated (up €1-3/t by the end of the 
week). Thus, the deal for spot shipment of 4,000 t of

Without a normalization of exports from Ukraine 
and Russia, there are frankly few signs of an upturn 
in the Black and Mediterranean Seas, since export 
outlook for European wheat is moderate, while the 
region’s steel market will almost certainly remain weak 
in the near term; also, the uptick in urea shipments 
will hardly be sufficient to move rates up given the 
very long tonnage list. The North European market 
will most likely continue to strengthen, although a 
possible influx of tonnage from the Mediterranean 
region could significantly complicate the situation for 
shipowners.
