Crude Tanker Comments  
The VLCC market began the week on steady ground; however, this has since given way 
to heightened uncertainty East of Suez and a softening sentiment in the Atlantic. In the 
East, initial momentum from steady cargo volumes and regular fixtures was halted by 
the escalating conflict around the SoH. Following a wave of regional attacks, with 
around six tankers hit since the weekend alone, activity inside the AG has become 
muted. While a lone cargo out of Basrah has recently emerged, it is seen by many as a 
market tester as this terminal has recently suspended loading. Meanwhile, voyages 
originating from the Red Sea and outside the AG are slowly starting to continue, albeit 
with much caution.
In the Atlantic, the Americas have been the primary area of action, though the latest 
fixtures have confirmed a softening sentiment across both the USG and even more so 
in Brazil, where a low market quote triggered a sharp downward correction. WAF 
remains muted on the surface. Whilst the Atlantic position lists continue to tighten, 
this is unlikely to put much pressure on charterers; the disruption in the Middle East is 
weighing heavily on owners, who are likely to explore alternative business outside the 
region to escape the volatility.
Despite Trump trying to divert from the escalating crisis in the Middle East and 
seemingly inevitable heavy losses in the upcoming midterms by doubling down on the 
rigging of the 2020 presidential election, Suezmax rates in West Africa have gradually 
depreciated this week. TD20 has slid to around WS 227.5 at the end of the week, 
having started it at WS 235.
CPC rates have also trended downwards, with CPC/Med done at WS 270 on four 
occasions, while last done is WS 265, albeit on a vessel that has Russian history.
There has been little sign of life on the other side of the Atlantic either. Guyana has 
been quiet and its load programme is pretty slim for first decade dates. USG/TA 
remains flat on Suezmaxes, with the Aframaxes outcompeting them on a USD per T 
basis and lump sum freight basis.
However, the East of Suez has fared a little better. Rates have firmed as a Fujairah/East 
voyage done at WS 230, which is up by roughly 30 points from the start of the week.
Aframax owners in the Med have not only managed to maintain the momentum of 
last week through this, they have propelled rates back to where they were in mid- 
April. Cargoes have flowed into the market and encountered a tight tonnage list, giving 
owners the impetus to drive the market higher. As of Thursday evening, TD19 was up 
to WS 390 – a seismic increase of almost 160 points since the beginning of the week – 
and, with charterers scrambling to secure tonnage, there is scope for rates to rise even 
further.
In the North Sea, the strength in the Atlantic market towards the end of last week put 
it on solid footing at the beginning of this week. Charterers continued to snap up what 
limited tonnage was available and, even though the week ends on a quieter note, the 
strength of the surrounding markets will ensure that TD7 climbs higher moving into 
next week. On Thursday evening, TD7 was assessed at WS 230, marking an increase of 
almost 80 points since the start of the week.
Product Tanker Comments
The LR2s have gone through a week where virtually nothing has taken place. This is 
very rare indeed; we are mindful of one failure off STS Sohar and one quiet naphtha 
deal on subs for STS Vadinar. As always, one never assumes one sees the whole 
picture and some quiet fixing could be happening, but the surface level reporting is 
where one gets the ‘read’ of the market and this week it reads like horror fiction.
The only potential positive is that ship supply remains balanced. In most other 
marketplaces over the years where demand dries up like this, that would only go 
hand-in-hand with a very long position list. However, the fleet has largely been 
distributed to other corners of the globe, and the Afra segment of course has retained 
a lot of the tonnage all year, which has only grown further this week, with at least 
another four CPP ships switching over to the Med DPP trade this week as this market 
rallied hard and was much more interesting than the CPP alternative.
This positivity can only be described at best as a sleeping giant; only when demand 
returns could any improvements be actualised. Cargo throughput from the AG is very 
small and will remain this way until refiners and off-takers have total assurance that 
vessel traffic will come to lift the oil; this can’t happen until the SoH is free-flowing, 
and we are a very long way from that. Trade virtually stopped this week across all 
merchant shipping sectors with a massive deterioration from diplomacy to more 
attacks on merchant ships, which led to tragic loss of life on a VLCC. Crew willingness 
to trade in this dangerous part of the world has been an integral part of freight 
negotiations; that has now of course become a lot harder, which throws up another 
barrier in this mire. Vessel insurance has lifted a lot too, which is understandable with 
one owner informing us yesterday that premia have gone up x5 since last week. A 
further reason as to the deathly silence this week is refining issues in Yanbu, which 
has led to a 20-day shut down for repairs, but we believe this might have now come to 
an end and the hope is that Red Sea exports could resume next week.
The AG LR1 market remains paralysed. The market remains soft as tonnage far 
outstrips current demand levels and this can be seen clearly by the few fixtures out in 
public. TC5 ex-Sohar dropped to WS 150, a 25-point drop from last week. Westbound 
demand has been limited but USD 2.85 Mn had been agreed for a jet cargo Sikka/UKC 
via BEM. There has been some off-market activity which has quietly thinned out the 
list vs last week, but there is certainly some way to go before we can see this starting 
to improve freight levels. We have seen flutters of activity inside the AG, but given 
recent escalations, Indian crew are not permitted to transit SoH as ordered by the 
Indian government, and other owners are so more cautious of risk levels, so crossings 
have taken a downturn. Shipping remains at the mercy of ongoing and seemingly 
worsening war conditions.
In the UKC MR markets, it was a missed opportunity for owners this week. TC2 was at 
WS 140-145 on Monday and poised for an increase. However, owners fell into the 
trap of not seeking out information and taking open market cargoes as gospel. Activity 
levels have been more than enough to at least sustain the market at WS 145 but 
unfortunately, we close at WS 140 and look soft going into next week. The fixing 
window will move out to end-3rd decade, which looks well supplied with decent 
tonnage. The USG shows signs of promise, to the detriment of  TC2; however, this will 
likely widen the TA-WAF spread.
