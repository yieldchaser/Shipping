CHARTERING MARKET AT HAND: 
COASTERS AND MINIBULKERS // WEEK 29
Coaster market divergence: frates soar ex Ukraine, while EU and MENA come 
under heavier pressure
As expected, the small-tonnage shipping market 
has undergone considerable structural shifts as a result 
of the escalation of the air and naval conflict between 
Russia and Ukraine. Following several Russian attacks 
on vessels in the area of the Greater Odessa ports, 
shipowners have started refusing en masse to call 
at Ukrainian ports . Most subs have not been finally 
lifted, and many shipments have been cancelled. 
The gap in expectations is extremely wide, as most 
charterers are unable to raise rates, while owners 
willing to operate from POC demand a very substantial 
premium. “Obviously, it’s very risky; besides, we have 
to pay a huge crew bonus and an AWRP that is 3-4 
times higher,” a local shipowner commented. Freight 
rates ex POC have risen by $2.5-4/t over the week. For 
instance, the deal for shipment of 6,000 t of wheat 
from Chornomorsk to the North Adriatic is discussed 
at mid $40s/t, while transportation of 3,000 t of corn 
from Odesa to Cyprus is estimated by brokers at mid 
$30s/t. Note that the Danube ports are much more 
preferable for shipowners, but vessel count has shrunk 
even in this area, so rates have added $1-2/t. Speaking 
of shipments from Russian ports, charterers who dare 
to work on such biz so far manage to curb any rate 
upturns thanks to the influx of a huge number of sea-
river vessels from the Azov Sea, where shipping has 
effectively come to a standstill. By the end of the week, 
freights for shipments from Novorossiysk, Tuapse and 
Taman have grown by mere $0.5-1/t. Thus, shipment of 
5-6,000 t of steels from Novorossiysk to Marmara costs 
$19.5-20/t. Meanwhile, the segment for shipments

Russian-flagged sea-river vessels are now competing 
specifically for such cargoes. An about 10-15% increase 
in cargo volume vs. last week was completely offset 
by an upturn in the number of 2-12k dwt vessels open 
in the Black and Mediterranean Seas (up to 327 vessels 
compared to 287 units a week earlier). Given the influx 
of the sea-river fleet, it is quite logical that the number 
of up to 6k dwt ships has grown most significantly (by 
nearly 30%, to 162 vessels) in the Black, Marmara and 
Aegean Seas, as well as in the eastern Mediterranean. 
In addition, many of the usual so-called “Ukraine 
callers” and “Russia callers” are now also focusing on 
shipments from neighboring countries. Even despite 
the rapid rise in bunker prices, rates for shipments 
from Romania, Bulgaria and TBS have dropped by 
$0.25-0.5/t this week. As for the Mediterranean Sea , 
freights are staying at last week’s levels, although TCEs 
have still sagged by $0.15-0.3k daily due to an increase 
in MGO prices (more than $100/t up over the week).
 
The North European coaster market remains weak, 
which is quite typical for mid-July. The shortage of new 
cargo offers persists, while the tonnage list remains 
very long in all parts of the basin (the total vessel 
count has decreased by only 6 units over the week). 
Local shipowners are often willing to make additional 
concessions in an effort to avoid prolonged periods of 
idling, although they do not hurry to voice such deals 
publicly on the market.

 No fresh shipments have been made in the Azov 
Sea since July 10; only vessels that were loaded earlier 
are moving toward the Black Sea. Note that part of 
the fleet was transferred to the Caspian Sea , which 
put downward pressure on freight rates ex Astrakhan, 
Makhachkala and river ports ($2-3/t down).
 
***
 
Next week, the market segmentation or clustering 
will almost certainly continue in the Black Sea (unless 
some kind of non-aggression agreement regarding 
vessels is suddenly reached between Russia and 
Ukraine). Rates ex Ukrainian ports may continue 
to rise, especially as the offer of new-crop grain is 
gradually increasing, while shipments from the EU, 
Turkey and North Africa may decline slightly further 
amid intensifying competition among shipowners. 
Uncertainty regarding future bunker price trends will 
only add to the nervousness on an already “shaky” 
market. Note that the western Mediterranean and 
even North Europe may also soon experience an 
inflow of tonnage from the eastern Mediterranean 
and the Marmara Sea, which will only exacerbate the 
tonnage surplus in these regions.
