The EU's seaborne coal imports softened further in 2025, declining by -2.6% y-o-y to 63.7 mln tonnes, from 65.4 mln tonnes in 2024, as Europe progressively abandoned coal as a source of energy and embraced natural gas and renewables.Now, however, in Jan-Jul 2026, the EU imported 38.5 mln tonnes, up +5.5% y-o-y from 36.5 mln tonnes, interrupting that downtrend.Looking at individual member states, the Netherlands is by far the largest single point of entry, accounting for 47.5% of the EU's seaborne coal imports in Jan-Jul 2026 at 18.3 mln tonnes, up +17.5% y-o-y, although a large share of these volumes is transhipped onwards to inland European end-users via Rotterdam and Amsterdam rather than consumed domestically.Poland followed with 4.6 mln tonnes in Jan-Jul 2026, up +22.2% y-o-y, and Germany with 4.0 mln tonnes, down -6.3% y-o-y, after a +23.3% y-o-y increase to 7.5 mln t in 2025.Italy recorded the sharpest fall amongst the majors, down -25.1% y-o-y to just 1.5 mln tonnes in Jan-Jul 2026, following a -17.2% y-o-y decline to 3.3 mln t in 2025.In terms of sources of the shipments, Europe was heavily dependant on Russia, but this drastically changed after the war in Ukraine — although Russian volumes have since been recovering.
In 2024, 5.9% of the EU's seaborne coal imports were sourced from Russian ports, which also includes Kazakh coal shipped through Russian ports, rising to 7.5% in 2025.In Jan-Dec 2025, coal imports to the EU from Russian ports increased by +23.2% y-o-y to 4.8 mln tonnes.In Jan-Jul 2026, imports from Russian ports rose further, by +28.8% y-o-y to 3.8 mln t, or 9.9% of the EU's total coal imports.The most important supplier to Europe in Jan-Jul 2026 was Australia, accounting for 27.5% of Europe's imports.In 2024, volumes from Australia stood at 19.3 mln t, but in 2025 they corrected down by -9.2% y-o-y to 17.5 mln t.In Jan-Jul 2026, coal volumes from Australia to the EU increased by +21.3% y-o-y to 10.6 mln t.The second largest supplier to Europe is the USA, accounting for 27.3% of the EU's imports in Jan-Jul 2026.In 2024, imports from the USA stood at 17.9 mln t, and in 2025 edged up +1.2% y-o-y to 18.1 mln t.In Jan-Jul 2026, volumes from the USA to the EU declined by -2.8% y-o-y to 10.5 mln t.In third place was Colombia, with a 13.8% share.In Jan-Jul 2026, volumes from Colombia to the EU declined by            -3.3% y-o-y to 5.3 mln t.

MARKET REPORT –WEEK 32/2026
The Capesize market advanced strongly over the week, building on the late-July recovery as typhoon-related concerns and robust iron ore demand tightened tonnage supply across both basins. Rates surged through midweek before steadying, closing markedly higher on all major iron ore routes.The Pacific led the rally. The Western Australia–Qingdao route (170,000 mt iron ore) jumped USD 1.35/wmt to USD 14.35/wmt on 3 August as an approaching typhoon expected to affect central China raised vessel-delay concerns, with a rising number of mining majors seeking prompt tonnage. Momentum accelerated to USD 15.00/wmt on 4 August and a weekly high of USD 16.20/wmt on 5 August, driven by typhoon-induced scheduling inefficiencies and a shrinking fleet around the tight laycan window. The route then eased 30 cents/wmt 
to USD 15.90/wmt on 6 August and held there on 7 August as activity turned rangebound, for a net weekly gain of USD 1.55/wmt.The Atlantic firmed in tandem. The Tubarao–Qingdao route climbed from USD 34.60/wmt on 3 August to USD 36.10/wmt on 5 August amid a flurry of Brazil-to-Qingdao fixtures for early-September loading dates and firmer freight derivative (FFA) values. It edged down to USD 36.00/wmt on 6 August and was unchanged on 7 August, up USD 1.40/wmt over the week, with offers heard around USD 37/wmt for mid-September dates. North Atlantic fronthaul activity was also brisk.The South Africa route (Saldanha Bay–Qingdao) tracked the trend, rising to a weekly high of USD 26.40/wmt on 5 August before easing to close at USD 26.25/wmt on 7 August, a net gain of 85 cents/wmt.
Overall, the market held firm into the weekend. With typhoon disruption still in focus and tonnage fundamentals tight, sentiment remained well supported despite the midweek plateau.
