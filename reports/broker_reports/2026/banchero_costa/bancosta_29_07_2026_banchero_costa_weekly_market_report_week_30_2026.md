BRAZIL’S IRON ORE EXPORTS1H 2026 was another positive period for global seaborne iron ore trade. In Jan-Jun 2026, global loadings of iron ore increased by +1.9% y-o-y to 836.7 mln t, based on AXS Marine vessel tracking data. This builds on the +3.5% y-o-y growth recorded in full year 2025. Exports from Australia increased in Jan-Jun 2026 by +2.6% y-o-y to 472.1 mln tonnes. From Brazil, exports rose by +1.4% y-o-y in Jan-Jun 2026 to 182.0 mln t. From Canada, there was a -14.0% y-o-y decrease to 25.2 mln tonnes.  From South Africa, volumes increased +0.6% y-o-y to 26.6 mln t.  From India, increased +4.2% y-o-y in Jan-Jun 2026 to 15.3 mln t. Iron ore import demand remains robust in China. Iron ore imports into China increased by +6.1% y-o-y in Jan-Jun 2026 to 638.4 mln t. Imports into Japan declined by            -5.8% y-o-y to 45.1 mln t.  To the EU, imports decreased by -6.6% y-o-y to 33.3 mln t. Volumes into South Korea were broadly flat, down -0.3% y-o-y to 36.1 mln t.  Imports into Malaysia increased by +2.6% y-o-y to 12.4 mln tonnes.  To Vietnam increased by +17.1% y-o-y to 14.0 mln t. To Oman, volumes were down             -21.4% y-o-y to 5.2 mln t, to Saudi Arabia by -56.5% y-o-y to 2.3 mln t, to Bahrain -70.2% y-o-y to 1.8 mln t.
Brazil is currently the second largest exporter of iron ore in the world, after Australia.In Jan-Dec 2025, Brazil accounted for 23.0% of global iron ore shipments, after Australia's 55.0%. Canada was third with a 3.7% share, followed by South Africa in fourth place with 3.1% and India with 1.6%. In Jan-Jun 2026, Brazil's share eased to 21.8%, against Australia's 56.4%, with South Africa moving up to third at 3.2%, ahead of Canada at 3.0%.In Jan-Dec 2025, iron ore exports from Brazil increased by +5.9% y-o-y to 398.3 mln t.In Jan-Jun 2026, growth slowed markedly, with volumes up just +1.4% y-o-y to 182.0 mln t.After a soft start to the year (28.6 mln t in Jan 2026, down from 37.8 mln t in Dec 2025), Brazil's exports recovered through the half, reaching 36.1 mln t in Jun 2026, up from 33.2 mln t in May 2026 and the strongest month of 2026 so far.About 50% of exports from Brazil in Jan-Jun 2026 were loaded on VLOCs (including Valemaxes), about 46% was loaded on Capesize tonnage, about 3% on Panamaxes, about 1% on Post-Panamaxes and less than 1% on Supramaxes. Looking at major loading ports for iron ore in Brazil in Jan-Jun 2026, we have: Ponta da Madeira (69.4 mln tonnes), Tubarao (39.1 mln t), Sepetiba/Itaguai (38.2 mln t),
Guaiba (15.6 mln t), Acu (12.8 mln t), Ponta Ubu (6.7 mln t). Mainland China is still by far the top destination for Brazilian iron ore, accounting for 72.8% of Brazil's exports in Jan-Dec 2025 and 71.5% in Jan-Jun 2026.Shipments to China increased by +7.1% y-o-y to 289.8 mln t in Jan-Dec 2025, and by a further +1.9% y-o-y to 130.1 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026.Volumes to Malaysia (which is mostly for transhipment to other Asian destinations) increased by +2.8% y-o-y in Jan-Dec 2025 to 19.7 mln tonnes, and by +8.8% y-o-y in Jan-Jun 2026 to 10.6 mln t.Exports from Brazil to the EU declined by -23.1% y-o-y to 13.0 mln tonnes in Jan-Dec 2025, and fell a further -21.4% y-o-y to 5.8 mln tonnes in Jan-Jun 2026.Shipments from Brazil to Oman dipped by -9.9% y-o-y to 11.2 mln t in Jan-Dec 2025 and collapsed by       -59.9% y-o-y to 2.4 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026, to Bahrain declined by -10.3% y-o-y to 9.1 mln t in 2025 and by        -67.8% y-o-y to 1.5 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026To Egypt shipments declined by         -16.2% y-o-y to 3.9 mln t in 2025 but rebounded by +77.7% y-o-y to 2.9 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026, whilst those to Turkey increased by +28.1% y-o-y to 5.5 mln t in 2025 and by a further +26.8% y-o-y to 3.4 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026.

MARKET REPORT –WEEK 30/2026
The Capesize market staged a broad-based recovery over the week, reversing the previous week's losses as firming freight derivative (FFA) values, a sustained climb in bunker prices and improving tonnage demand lifted rates across all major iron ore routes.The week opened on a soft footing. The Western Australia–Qingdao route (170,000 mt iron ore) slipped a further 80 cents/wmt to USD 11.00/wmt on 20 July, weighed down by ample Pacific tonnage supply, declining FFA values and a thin cargo list. Sentiment turned on 21 July, however, with several participants declaring the market had "bottomed out" as bid levels firmed in both basins. The route then advanced steadily — to USD 11.10, USD 11.75 and USD 12.15/wmt — before a further 75-cent/wmt gain carried it to USD 12.90/wmt on 24 July, a weekly rise 
of USD 1.90/wmt. Persistent charterer–owner standoffs slowed the pace of fixing, but a surge in fresh Pacific demand and firm week-end owner resistance underpinned the advance.The Atlantic led the recovery. The Tubarao–Qingdao route held at USD 31.95/wmt on 20 July before climbing on tightening ballaster supply for the August loading window and rising bunker costs, gaining USD 1.15/wmt on 22 July alone. It closed at USD 34.60/wmt on 24 July, up USD 2.65/wmt over the week, with offers heard in excess of USD 35/wmt for H2 August laycans. Fronthaul activity also firmed, with North Atlantic fixtures concluded from Sept-Îles and Pointe Noire.The South Africa route (Saldanha Bay–Qingdao) followed suit, easing to USD 23.50/wmt early in the week before rising to USD 25.40/wmt on 
24 July, a net weekly gain of USD 1.90/wmt.Overall, the market closed on a distinctly bullish note, with elevated bunker prices and a lengthening Pacific cargo list expected to keep bid and offer ideas supported into the following week.

The Asia-Pacific Panamax market opened on a firm note but turned progressively bearish through the week, weighed down by a persistently overtonnaged Pacific basin and thin cargo replenishment. Rates edged up on Monday 20 July despite stagnant trading and firmed to mid-week highs around 21 July, before sentiment deteriorated. Elevated spot tonnage, easing port congestion releasing further vessels for late-July dates, and a softening 
east coast South America market combined to pressure owners' ideas. By 24 July many owners of modern tonnage were electing to ballast rather than fix at depressed Pacific levels.On the key Australian coking coal routes, Hay Point–Qingdao peaked at USD 18.65/mt on 21–22 July before easing to USD 17.85/mt by 24 July, down from USD 18.40/mt the preceding Friday. Hay Point–Paradip traced a similar 
arc, slipping to USD 20.65/mt. The Indonesian thermal coal trade also softened, Samarinda–Guangzhou easing to USD 8.90/mt from a mid-week USD 9.30/mt.Fixtures included a 75,000 mt Hay Point–Dhamra/Haldia metallurgical coal cargo at USD 17.40/mt FIO. Firmer bunker prices added to the negative tone, with participants awaiting clearer direction.

On handies, repositioning destinations such as ECSA and USG/USEC continued to see owners discounting as usual. USG/USEC held steady just above USD 11,000/day.
