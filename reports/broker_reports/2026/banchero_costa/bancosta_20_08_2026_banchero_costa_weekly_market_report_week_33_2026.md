UNITED STATES CRUDE OIL EXPORTSAfter a marginal decline in 2024, when global crude oil loadings decreased by -0.2% y-o-y, things picked up in 2025, with full-year volumes increasing by +1.8% y-o-y. In Jan-Jul 2026, however, global crude oil loadings declined by -6.1% y-o-y to 1,190.6 mln tonnes, excluding all cabotage trade, according to vessels tracking data from LSEG. Exports from the Arabian Gulf plunged by -30.8% y-o-y to 351.8 mln t in Jan-Jul 2026, and accounted for 29.5% of global seaborne crude trade, down sharply from the historical norm of about 40%, reflecting the ongoing war in the Persian Gulf. Exports from Russian ports (including oil of Kazakh origin) increased by +7.1% y-o-y to 141.8 mln t in Jan-Jul 2026, or 11.9% of global trade. From South America, exports surged by +33.1% y-o-y to 162.9 mln t. From the USA, exports increased by +17.1% y-o-y to 120.9 mln t in Jan-Jul 2026. From West Africa, exports decreased by -10.4% y-o-y to 91.0 mln t. From ASEAN exports rose by +13.9% y-o-y to 70.5 mln t in Jan-Jul 2026 (this inevitably includes trans-shipped Iranian and Russian cargoes). In terms of demand (measured by arrival at discharge ports), the top single-country seaborne importer of crude oil in Jan-Jul 2026 was China, accounting for 19.5% of global trade. Volumes into China declined by -18.2% y-o-y to 233.1 mln t in Jan-Jul 2026, from 285.1 mln t in Jan-Jul 2025.Imports into the EU27 increased by +1.9% y-o-y to 268.6 mln t, accounting for 22.5% of global trade, which now 
makes the bloc as a whole a larger buyer than Mainland China. To ASEAN, imports decreased by -8.6% y-o-y to 152.7 mln t. Imports to India were essentially flat at -0.4% y-o-y to 138.9 mln t in Jan-Jul 2026. To South Korea, imports declined by -13.4% y-o-y to 70.5 mln t. To Japan, imports fell by -19.2% y-o-y to 52.5 mln t in Jan-Jul 2026. Imports into the USA increased by +4.7% y-o-y to 72.8 mln t in Jan-Jul 2026. The United States have been arguably the biggest winner from the sanction regime imposed on Russia and from OPEC's attempt to support oil prices through cutting production quotas. Exports from the USA sharply increased in recent years, to the point that it is now the third largest exporter in the world, after Saudi and Russia. The USA now account for 8.2% of global crude oil loadings. Crude oil exports from the USA increased by +22.9% y-o-y in 2022 and by +19.5% y-o-y in 2023. 2024, however, was disappointing, with exports declining marginally by -0.3% y-o-y to 197.4 mln tonnes. 2025 was even more disappointing, with full-year exports declining by -7.8% y-o-y to 182.0 mln t, the lowest since 2022. In Jan-Jul 2026, however, exports rebounded by +17.1% y-o-y to 120.9 mln tonnes, buoyed by a particularly strong Apr-May (21.6 mln t and 22.6 mln t respectively), likely as buyers sought alternatives to disrupted Arabian Gulf supply.About 50 percent of international crude exports from the USA in Jan-Jul 
2026 were loaded in Corpus Christi, about 17 percent from Houston, about 8 percent from Galveston, about 7 percent from Bayport, about 5% from LOOP, about 5% from Beaumont. Given persisting infrastructure limitations, only 43 percent of crude oil volumes loaded at USA ports in Jan-Jul 2026 were loaded on VLCCs, down from 51 percent previously. Most VLCC cargoes are loaded in Corpus Christi, Galveston and LOOP. About 22 percent of volumes are loaded on Suezmaxes, and 33 percent is loaded on Aframaxes. In terms of destinations for the shipments, it is quite diversified, with about 43% to Europe, 40% to Asia, and the rest to the Americas and Africa. Direction Europe, about 43.6 mln tonnes (36.1 percent of the total) were shipped from the USA to the European Union in Jan-Jul 2026, up sharply by +27.0% y-o-y, whilst about 6.5 mln t (5.4% of the total) went to the UK. Specifically, 13.2 mln tonnes were shipped in Jan-Jul 2026 to the Netherlands, 7.4 mln t to France, 5.7 mln t to Italy, 5.5 mln t to Spain, 4.0 mln t to Germany, 2.0 mln t to Sweden, 1.2 mln t to Denmark. Direction Asia, about 15.2 mln t were shipped from the USA to S. Korea in Jan-Jul 2026, up +22.8% y-o-y, and 12.1 mln t to Japan, up more than fivefold y-o-y, whilst only 3.7 mln t went to India, down -55.1% y-o-y. To Mainland China volumes collapsed by   -46.1% y-o-y in Jan-Jul 2026 to just 1.6 mln tonnes, very far from the 13.8 mln t in Jan-Jul 2023.

MARKET REPORT –WEEK 33/2026
The Capesize market gave back much of the previous week's typhoon-driven gains, softening through midweek before a modest late-week correction. The Pacific led the decline. The Western Australia–Qingdao route (170,000 mt iron ore, plus/minus 10%) eased 10 cents/wmt to USD 15.80/wmt on 11 August amid lacklustre trading and a persistent bid-offer gap, owners having initially resisted lower ideas on continued Pacific tonnage tightness. The route then fell USD 1.35/wmt to USD 14.45/wmt on 12 August as Chinese ports gradually resumed operations in the aftermath of Typhoon Dolphin, Changjiangkou excepted, and a further 90 cents/wmt to USD 13.55/wmt on 13 August as tonnage supply was seen building; a mining major fixed Dampier–Qingdao at around mid-USD 13/wmt for 28–30 August laycan. 
A rebound to USD 14.15/wmt on 14 August, helped by firmer freight derivative (FFA) values and few offers quoted, trimmed the net weekly loss to USD 1.75/wmt.The Atlantic proved more resilient. Tubarao–Qingdao slipped 40 cents/wmt to USD 35.60/wmt on 11 August on thin South Atlantic volumes, recovered to USD 35.95/wmt on 12 August as fresh orders emerged, eased to USD 35.65/wmt on 13 August and closed at USD 35.80/wmt, down only 20 cents/wmt on the week. Fixtures reported included a Capesize from Tubarao to Qingdao at about mid-USD 35s/wmt for 5–11 September laycan and a Newcastlemax from Itaguai to Qingdao at low-mid USD 36/wmt for 14–16 September. North Atlantic fronthaul enquiry was steady, with a Sept-Îles–Qingdao fixture heard at mid-high USD 41/wmt.
The South Africa route (Saldanha Bay–Qingdao) was little changed, ranging USD 26.20–26.40/wmt and closing at USD 26.35/wmt, up 10 cents/wmt.Cargo availability remained ample in both basins, but charterers showed little urgency to cover, leaving sentiment cautiously steadier into the weekend.

Pacific Panamax sentiment strengthened over the week to 14 August, the Asia-Pacific market drawing support from a firming East Coast South America market, tighter prompt tonnage and lingering weather delays.
