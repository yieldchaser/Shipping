   MARKET OVERVIEW 
 
South Asian energy processors are actively overhauling their crude procurement tactics by chartering 
tonnage to lift cargoes directly from within the Persian Gulf rather than paying elevated delivered rates. In 
response to softening domestic maritime crew advisories and growing resistance toward alternative 
Atlantic origins, major Indian refiners have transitioned toward purchasing regional stems on a direct 
loading basis, awarding tenders to specialised international fleet operators willing to navigate the 
contested Strait of Hormuz. This pivot has allowed buyers to capture substantial savings, even as 
operational and security risks across the waterway remain an ongoing reality.  
 
The primary incentive driving this is an extraordinary pricing campaign from Iraq , who face severe 
competitive disadvantages due to their deeper geographic position. To compensate buyers for exorbitant 
war-risk insurance premiums and chartering rates, Iraq state authorities have lowered official selling 
prices to historic depths, offering medium and heavy grades at unprecedented discounts of up to US$37 
per barrel below regional benchmarks. With limited capacity to transport, Ba ghdad is using these historic 
rebates to protect market share against neighbouring producers who enjoy direct pipeline access to 
alternative coastlines. While these markdowns have lured back adventurous buyers, the broader trade 
remains constrained by a shortage of vessels willing to take on PG stems. 
 
Meanwhile, operational bottlenecks have surfaced further east at China’s premier ship- refuelling center in 
Zhoushan, where commercial vessels face extensive bunkering queues stretching up to two weeks instead 
of the customary 48 hours. These mounting delays stem from an acute shortage of marine residual fuel, 
as regional domestic refineries have heavily prioritized gasoline and diesel output to capture lucrative 
light-distillate margins at the expense of lower-value heavy residues. Spot premiums for compliant VLSFO 
have consequently surged to levels more than a quarter higher than competing hubs across Southeast 
Asia, eroding the port’s price competitiveness. Seasonal industrial slowdowns and holiday factory pauses 
are expected to provide short-term relief to the congestion. 
 
While in the Middle East, Iran is facing mounting economic and military pressure seven months into the 
conflict with the US. Inflation is nearing 90%, the rial has weakened sharply, and Iran reportedly loaded no 
crude for export in September as the US blockade tightened. Meanwhile,  Middle East crude flows through 
the Strait of Hormuz have recovered close to pre-war levels, reducing some of Tehran’s earlier leverage. 
However, maritime risks remain elevated, highlighted by a crude tanker being struck by an unidentified 
projectile off Oman. With additional US naval forces heading to the region, shipping, insurance and oil 
markets remain exposed to renewed escalation.

Dry bulk market entered October with robust fundamentals, supported by expanding ton -mile demand, 
longer average hauls, and firm second-hand asset values, even as daily index readings experienced a 
brief correction heading into the fourth quarter. Global ton-mile demand expanded 6.7% y-o-y to 16.31 
trillion in the first half of 2026, outpacing the 3.2% growth in actual cargo volumes and active fleet capacity 
as average voyage distances lengthened by roughly 3.4%. Long-haul trades from West Africa have been a 
primary catalyst, with Guinea’s iron ore shipments totalling 11.81 million tons through August and steady 
grain support from a 6.4% rise in global soybean movements to 122.6 million tons.  
 
In the physical spot market, the benchmark Baltic Dry Index eased on Fri day to 3,178 points, marking a 7.3% 
w-o-w decline as larger bulkers faced downward pressure. The Capesize index slipped to 5,103 points —
down 12.9% from last week, while the Panamax index eased to 2,390 points. Bucking the trend among the 
larger sizes, the Supramax index advanced to 1,797 points—its highest mark since August 2022, as 
aggressive buyer interest in prompt tonnage that has seen dry bulk asset values appreciate by an 
average of 13% this year. 
 
Segment Highlights 
Capesize: Capesize softened noticeably across all key routes this week, weighed down by expanding 
ballast tonnage, easing Chinese port congestion, and reduced pre-holiday iron ore enquiry from Australia 
and Brazil. Reflecting this widespread correction, major route earnings declined across the board, with 
Pacific r/v tumbling to US$34,950’s and T/A returns settling at US$58,750’s per day. Trading remains 
sluggish due to China's Golden Week holidays, though early-stage fixtures for November programs could 
help establish a rate floor. 
 
Panamax / Kamsarmax: Panamax held a broadly steady tone this week as rate advance across the 
firmer Atlantic basin effectively offset emerging softness in the Pacific. Atlantic rates drew strength from 
tightening prompt tonnage and expanding USG grain stems, whereas persistent regional oversupply and 
thinning pre-holiday inquiry weighed on the East, lifting T/A returns to US$21,670’s while Pacific r/v and 
Indonesian r/v eased to US$20,750’s and US$17,900’s, respectively. Ongoing grain and coal flows provide 
support, though momentum is tempered by quieter Asian fixture activity. 
 
Supramax / Ultramax: Supramax retained a generally firm footing with average freight rates rising, 
supported by robust European scrap demand, steady South Atlantic activity, and resilient intra- Asian 
cargo flows. A pre-holiday slowdown in NOPAC volumes and softening across North America and the 
Mediterranean kept wider gains measured, nudging spot benchmarks lower as T/A earnings settled at 
US$29,550’s and Pacific r/v adjusted to US$17,900’s per day.  
 
Handysize: Handysize saw a mixed week, as healthy conditions across the Continent and Mediterranean 
contrasted with a quieter, softening Pacific basin affected by subdued cargo enquiry. Selective charterer 
interest in the Atlantic helped sustain positive forward momentum on major runs, with F/H  earnings 
climbing to US$15,000’s and Inter-Pacific routes advancing to US$16,850’s. Holiday slowdowns across Asia 
keep trading activity relatively restrained this week.

Mounting expectations of enduring transit bottlenecks across the Middle East are reshaping forecasts and 
sustaining extraordinary freight rates. A newly released monthly survey of 30 commodity analysts and 
economists revised the projected full-year average for Brent crude up to US$89.05 per barrel, while West 
Texas Intermediate expectations climbed to US$83.90. Market observers increasingly view the Strait of 
Hormuz as structurally impaired, pointing to prolonged disruptions that keep liquid flows well below their 
historical 19 to 20 million BPD baseline.  
 
Meanwhile, downstream Asian demand presents mixed signals: while China’s imports rebounded from 
June's decade lows to nearly 9 million BPD in August, elevated crude costs and surging freight rates led 
forecasters to trim fourth-quarter projections to between 9.2 and 9.3 million barrels daily.  
 
Segment Highlights 
VLCC: MEG rates softened marginally at the start of the week as the phased restart of Saudi Arabia's East-
West pipeline restored crude flows to Yanbu, however, by Friday, heavy reliance on Oman STS transfers 
eased, with trips to China climbing to WS1,145. In the Atlantic, similar easing was seen, but rates recovered 
for 260,000mt WAFR/China, improving to WS519. 
 
Suezmax: Suezmax closed higher as VLCC cargo-splitting demand marginally recovered and nudging the 
market, with 130,000mt Nigeria/UKC boosted to WS728. A fundamentally tight Atlantic basin supported by 
anticipated Brazilian demand kept sentiment firm this week, even as muted activity in Fujairah exerted 
short-term drag. USG/UKC ended the week at WS566.  
 
Aframax: MEG rates surged roughly this week as charterers actively secured private tonnage, keeping 
shipowners firmly in control amid tight vessel availability. Across the Mediterranean, charterers are moving 
quietly to cover requirements against a balanced position list, with TD19 Ceyhan/Lavera easing slightly to 
WS625, owner sentiment remains resilient. 
 
LR1 / LR2: LR2 in the MEG climbed higher this week, propelled by higher ton-mile demand from expanding 
long-haul runs to Asia. TC1 MEG/Japan ended the week at WS927 as shipowners maintain a strong 
negotiating advantage. Similarly, LR1 on the TC5 closed at WS951.  
 
MR: Far East MR closed higher, propelled by robust Asian demand for petroleum products ahead of the 
winter season. In the USG, TC14 also shot up by some 120 points to WS395. Similarly, TC17 MEG/E. Africa

   CONTAINERS  
 
Container shipping market halted its upward climb this week as the SCFI index edged down 20 points to 
settle at 3,662.30 points, snapping an eight-week streak of consecutive gains. This subtle plateau reflects 
an easing across transpacific lanes as Chinese manufacturing pauses for Golden Week, though firm U.S. 
demand—underpinned by data centre investments, early peak shipments, and an extended bilateral 
trade truce reducing tariffs on US$30 billion of goods—has kept rates remarkably resilient compared to 
earlier summer baselines.  
 
According to Xeneta, despite spot rates cooling as available capacity expanded by 15% to the U.S. West 
Coast and 21% to the U.S. East Coast since July, shippers are still paying an average of US $2,691 (up 31%) 
and US$1,280 per FEU (up 18%) more than mid-year levels. Downward index momentum was further 
mitigated by physical supply constraints; while Chinese port bottlenecks began tapering off in late 
September, Linerlytica noted that global port congestion continues to tie up over 11% of the world’s cellular 
fleet, with persistent queues across Southeast Asia and the Middle East helping regional feeder and Gulf 
spot rates climb by 5.4% week-on-week amid unresolved Strait of Hormuz risks.

Market Insights 
 
Alang, India: Alang navigated a subdued trading environment in the opening week of October, as the 
combination of national holidays and acute vessel shortages brought deal -making to a virtual standstill. 
Domestic plate values managed a modest recovery following late September's sharp decline, but this 
improvement was partially offset by a softer rupee, which continues to elevate buying costs for dollar-
denominated ships. With the candidate pool remaining sparse, open market pricing spreads have 
narrowed, leading Alang’s shipbreakers to become noticeably more selective in their bidding. While plot 
operators are eager to secure tonnage to maintain cutting schedules, the lack of candidate flow and 
competitive pressure from neighbouring beaches have encouraged buyers to exercise discipline. 
 
This restraint is set against an increasingly watchful macroeconomic climate across the broader Indian 
economy as financial markets look ahead to next week’s Reserve Bank of India monetary policy meeting. 
With annual consumer inflation firming toward 4.8% and global energy prices remaining buoyant, financial 
analysts widely anticipate that the central bank may consider raising the benchmark repo rate from 5.25% 
to 5.50% to anchor domestic price expectations and defend currency stability. Even with potential 
borrowing adjustments on the horizon, India's underlying industrial momentum remains  exceptionally 
sturdy, evidenced by an 8% expansion in industrial production and strong domestic infrastructure outlays 
that continue to provide a firm floor under rerollable scrap consumption.

Chattogram, Bangladesh: Chattogram made a spirited push into the final quarter, with firm sentiment 
and competitive bidding propelling Bangladesh back to the top of the board. Even though actual

Fresh data from Bangladesh Bank shows that overseas worker remittances reached US$8.59 billion during

country’s foreign currency liquidity.  
Gross foreign exchange reserves have remained well anchored above US$36 billion, comfortably standing 
near US$22 billion under the IMF’s BPM6 benchmark, providing the banking sector with the stability 
required to facilitate Letters of Credit without foreign exchange bottlenecks.

   COMMODITIES, BUNKERS & RATES 
Commodities Focus 
 
Steel rebar futures climbed to roughly CNY 3,109 per ton, touching a one -week high after the World Steel 
Association reported that worldwide crude steel output contracted by 1.2% y-o- y in August to 144.2 million 
tons and slipped 3.3% m-o-m from July’s 149.2 million tons, with China’s monthly drop of 2.3 million tons 
accounting for almost half of that global slowdown. Although China retains its role as the dominant 
producer by supplying 51.7% of the world's steel, its domestic mills continue to battle weak builder  demand 
and compressed profit margins, prompting the China Iron and Steel Association to urge steelmakers to 
curb runs and draw down stock.  
 
Iron ore futures echoed this modest stabilization, lifting toward CNY 710 per ton from eight -week lows as 
factory activity in China returned to growth during September alongside government credit programs
