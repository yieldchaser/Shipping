## Main
Last week's improving recycling sentiment has encountered a changing international backdrop. Crude oil has retreated sharply, domestic steelprices have weakened across several destinations, and freight continues to fluctuate. Yet recycling indications remain unchanged across all four markets. Buying appetite remains healthy, particularly in Bangladesh, although the persistent shortage of available candidates continues to prevent stronger sentiment from translating into fresh transactions.
Oil supplied the week's most striking movement. Brent retreated towards USD 99 per barrel on Friday, while WTI traded around USD 91, reversing much of last week's advance. Expectations of easing Middle Eastern tensions encouraged the correction, although renewed security incidents in the Gulf and Strait of Hormuz demonstrated that shipping risks remain unresolved. Lower crude may ease operating expenses, but uncertainty surrounding regional passage continues to influence tanker employment and owners' recycling decisions.
Dry freight offered mixed signals. The Baltic Dry Index recovered to 2,740 on Thursday, November 5, from 2,712 a week earlier, having fallen to 2,590 during the intervening sessions. Capesizes strengthened to 4,191 and Panamaxes improved to 1,956, while Supramaxes eased to 1,824. The uneven recovery continues to provide ageing vessels with trading alternatives, restricting the supply of recycling candidates.
Steel markets moved against recyclers. Indian plate declined considerably during the week, while Pakistan recorded a further correction and Chinese steel continued to soften. Bangladesh remained unchanged, although a strengthening Dollar placed additional pressure on the Taka amid substantial foreign currency requirements for energy imports. Weaker steel realisations are restricting purchasing flexibility, even as recycling price indications remain unchanged.
India also faced an important regulatory hurdle. On November 4, the European Parliament's ENVI Committee voted to object to the proposed inclusion of Indian ship recycling yards on the European approved list, citing environmental concerns over beaching methods. The objection is not a final rejection. A full parliamentary vote is expected later this month, while the final binding decision remains subject to EU Member State approval. The development introduces uncertainty into Alang's expanding compliance ambitions.
At the beaches, Bangladesh continues to demonstrate the strongest conventional buying appetite, supported by expectations of improving seasonal demand. Pakistan remains static amid limited fresh supply, while India continues to pursue opportunities in specialist and higher-value vessels. Turkey maintains its selective approach. Physical activity nevertheless continued, with a sizeable tanker arriving at Chattogram, additional tonnage reaching Alang and a previously secured bulker completing delivery at Gadani.
The overall market remains stable despite softer steel and changing energy conditions. Buyers are maintaining their positions, but freight earnings and second-hand alternatives continue to discourage owners from releasing ageing ships. Bangladesh has the confidence, India retains its specialist strengths, and Pakistan and Turkey remain measured. The bids are holding their ground. The missing ingredient remains a steady supply of willing sellers.

Chattogram continues to display encouraging recycling sentiment, with end buyers maintaining ***Buyer Confidence.*** strong price indications and a healthy appetite for suitable vessels. Conventional recycling indications remain unchanged across all categories, preserving Bangladesh's leading position in the sub-continent. The resilience of the market reflects continuing yard requirements and buyer confidence rather than any substantial improvement in domestic steel consumption.

Local steel remained unchanged at BDT 66,000 per ton throughout the week, providing a stable ***Dollar Pressure.*** foundation for vessel pricing without additional upward momentum. Currency conditions, however, have become more challenging. Substantial energy import requirements continue to contribute to demand for foreign currency, placing additional pressure on local purchasing costs. Although recyclers retain strong buying intentions, the combination of elevated energy expenses and currency pressure requires careful margin management, particularly while domestic finished- steel demand has yet to demonstrate a sustained recovery.

Attention continues to turn towards the approaching dry season, when improving weather ***Seasonal Prospects.*** conditions and anticipated construction activity are expected to support steel consumption and yard operations. # INDIA

# ALANG NAVIGATES EUROPEAN HEADWINDS

An important development emerged this week in India's continuing efforts to secure inclusion of its ship recycling facilities on the European approved list. On November 4, the European Parliament's ***Regulatory Crosswind.*** Environment, Public Health and Food Safety (ENVI) Committee voted to object to the proposed inclusion of Indian yards, citing environmental concerns surrounding beaching methods. The objection represents a political hurdle rather than a final legal rejection. The proposal is expected to proceed to a full Parliament Plenary vote later this month, while the final binding decision remains subject to EU Member State approval. The development introduces uncertainty into a compliance initiative that has attracted considerable industry attention and carries potential long-term importance for Alang.

Domestic steel conditions provided considerably less encouragement this week. Alang plate declined from INR 43,300 per ton last Friday to INR 40,700 by November 6, having briefly recovered from its ***Plate Retreats.*** midweek low. The Dollar equivalent also weakened despite an improvement in the Rupee, which benefited from lower crude prices and renewed foreign investment inflows. The softer steel market limits recyclers' purchasing flexibility, particularly when competing against stronger conventional buying positions in Bangladesh and Pakistan. China's continuing steel-price correction adds to the cautious regional backdrop. Conventional recycling indications nevertheless remain unchanged, with Indian buyers maintaining discipline rather than chasing tonnage at levels unsupported by recoverable material values.

Alang's overall position remains broadly consistent with recent weeks. # GADANI WAITS FOR THE NEXT WAVE

Gadani has experienced another relatively uneventful week, with recycling sentiment remaining ***Supply Stalls.*** broadly static and the availability of fresh candidates continuing to disappoint. Although buyers retain an interest in commercially suitable vessels, the market has shown little urgency to pursue additional tonnage. The more measured approach follows the substantial buying activity recorded earlier in the year, when a greater number of vessels were secured to satisfy immediate yard requirements. Bangladesh continues to provide strong competition for conventional candidates, but Pakistani recyclers remain capable of responding when appropriate opportunities emerge. The absence of fresh supply is therefore restricting activity rather than reflecting a meaningful deterioration in underlying demand.

Domestic steel prices weakened further during the reporting period, with local plate declining ***Plate Eases.*** from PKR 188,000 per ton last Friday to PKR 183,000 by November 6. The latest inflation figures offered some encouragement, with Pakistan's annual CPI easing to 9.6% in October from 10.3% in September. Although the improvement provides a measure of macroeconomic relief, financing considerations and weaker steel values continue to favour disciplined vessel acquisition rather than aggressive bidding.
