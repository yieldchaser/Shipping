## Main
After several weeks of improving confidence, recycling buyers are beginning to have second thoughts abouthow aggressively to pursue fresh tonnage. Bangladesh's recent optimism has been tempered by weaker steel, political unrest and sluggish domestic demand, prompting some buyers to reconsider their purchasing ideas or delay commitments. Elsewhere, sentiment remains selective rather than distressed. Recycling indications are unchanged across all four destinations, although the shortage of available candidates continues to restrict fresh business.
Dry freight surrendered part of last week's gains, with the Baltic Dry Index easing from 3,020 on November 12 to 2,901 on Thursday, November 19. Capesizes declined to 4,514 and Supramaxes slipped to 1,814, while Panamaxes edged higher to 2,132. Despite the correction, employment opportunities remain sufficiently attractive for many ageing vessels, limiting the incentive for owners to consider recycling.
Crude oil continued its downward trajectory, with Brent trading around USD 90 per barrel on Friday and WTI near USD 82. Recovering Middle Eastern exports and expectations of greater supply have outweighed continuing regional security concerns. Lower energy costs may provide some relief to recyclers and steel producers, although uncertain Gulf trading conditions continue to influence vessel employment and disposal decisions.
Regional steel markets offered mixed signals. Bangladesh plate retreated from last week's improvement, while Indian steel recovered towards Friday and Pakistani prices strengthened marginally. Chinese quotations weakened further. These movements have provided little justification for stronger recycling bids, particularly where domestic finished-steel consumption remains disappointing.
Bangladesh faces the week's most challenging domestic developments, with political unrest, disruptions in the garment sector and weak local demand prompting greater buyer caution. India continues to pursue specialist opportunities and has sought Italian support for European approval of selected recycling facilities. Pakistan remains measured, while Turkey faces subdued steel demand and continuing production disruption at a major mill.
Waterfront activity nevertheless continued. Alang and Chattogram recorded further arrivals and deliveries, while Gadani received another bulker. These movements primarily reflect previously secured tonnage rather than fresh transactions. No new recycling sales were reported during the week.
The market therefore enters the final weeks of November with buyers reassessing their positions rather than abandoning them. Bangladesh's earlier enthusiasm has given way to greater caution, while India, Pakistan and Turkey remain selective. Owners, meanwhile, still have sufficient trading alternatives to resist recycling. The price board has not moved, but the conviction behind it is being tested. For now, the market is not changing its prices. It is having second thoughts about paying them.

## <u>BANGLADESH</u>

# CONFIDENCE MEETS A HARDER REALITY

Chattogram's previously improving recycling sentiment has encountered renewed resistance, with ***Buyer Caution.*** buyers adopting a more cautious approach amid deteriorating domestic conditions. Political unrest, incidents of arson and growing security concerns have increased uncertainty across Bangladesh. Continued disturbances in the ready-made garment sector, including factory closures and disruptions to production, are adding pressure to an already challenging economic environment. These developments have weakened business confidence at a time when the domestic ship recycling industry was anticipating a seasonal recovery.

Domestic steel provided little encouragement this week, with plate declining from BDT 67,500 to ***Steel Retreats.*** BDT 66,400 per ton and remaining at that level through Friday. Weak finished-steel consumption, elevated operating expenses and limited domestic sales are restricting recyclers' purchasing flexibility. Although conventional recycling indications remain unchanged, several end buyers are now expressing interest at lower levels or postponing commitments until market conditions become clearer. The distinction between published indications and executable buying interest is becoming increasingly important.

Energy supplies continue to fluctuate, although the situation has become relatively manageable as overall demand moderates ahead of winter. Nevertheless, interruptions remain an operational concern for steel producers and recycling facilities. Foreign currency exposure also continues to influence purchasing decisions, with Bangladesh Bank's November 19 spot reference around BDT

124.66 to the Dollar. Import financing costs, weaker steel realisations and uncertainty over future domestic demand are encouraging recyclers to protect margins rather than pursue additional tonnage aggressively. Physical activity continued at Chattogram, with the 5,471 LDT bulk carrier LIAN YI DA 2 HAO

Buyers remain interested in suitable opportunities, but greater stability in domestic conditions and a meaningful recovery in steel consumption will be necessary to restore stronger purchasing momentum. With the anticipated seasonal improvement yet to materialise, Chattogram enters the coming weeks with caution taking precedence over competition.

## INDIA

## ALANG KEEPS ITS OPTIONS OPEN

Alang continues to maintain a stable and selective recycling market, supported by steady interest in specialist and higher-value vessels. Conventional competition with Bangladesh and Pakistan remains ***Selective Interest.*** challenging, although India's established recycling infrastructure and technical capabilities provide opportunities beyond ordinary steel recovery. Buyers continue to assess candidates according to their equipment, non-ferrous content and potential recoverable value, rather than relying solely on conventional vessel categories. With fresh recycling supply remaining restricted, purchasing decisions continue to favour commercially suitable opportunities over volume.

Domestic steel prices fluctuated within a relatively narrow range during the week. Plate began at INR 40,800 per ton, softened to INR 40,700 and subsequently recovered to INR 41,000 by Friday. The ***Steel Recovers.*** corresponding Dollar equivalent finished around USD 435.01 per ton. The improvement offers some support to recycling margins, although finished-steel demand remains cautious and competition from neighbouring destinations continues to influence conventional buying interest. Recent Rupee strength has also been somewhat supportive of Dollar purchasing calculations, without materially changing the market's underlying position.

India sought Italian support this week for European approval of selected ship recycling facilities, although the approval process remains unresolved. Alang's progress towards broader international ***Regulatory Progress.*** recognition remains commercially relevant, particularly for owners seeking recycling arrangements that satisfy increasingly demanding environmental and compliance requirements. Nevertheless, the immediate market remains governed by vessel availability, steel realisations and the economics of individual purchases.

Waterfront activity continued, with the 7,065 LDT mining vessel CORA arriving on November 20 and the 1,529 LDT fishing trawler WILL arriving on November 17. The 2,057 LDT chemical tanker TULIP was delivered on November 17, while D.V. EARL, also recorded at 2,057 LDT, completed delivery on November 12. These movements demonstrate Alang's ability to accommodate a varied vessel intake despite limited fresh market transactions.

India consequently remains commercially active within its established specialist segment, while maintaining discipline on conventional tonnage. Its competitive position continues to depend on vessel composition, recoverable materials and the development of future recycling opportunities. With steel showing only modest improvement, buyers are unlikely to abandon that selective approach.

## <u>PAKISTAN</u>

## GADANI HOLDS A STEADY COURSE

Gadani continues to maintain stable recycling sentiment, with buyers showing measured interest ***Buyers Patient.*** in suitable tonnage despite the limited availability of fresh candidates. The purchasing urgency witnessed earlier in the year has moderated as previously secured vessels have helped satisfy immediate yard requirements. Nevertheless, underlying demand remains present, and Pakistani recyclers retain the capacity to compete when commercially attractive opportunities become available. Bangladesh's increasingly cautious approach may also provide some competitive breathing room, although there has been insufficient fresh activity to establish a meaningful change in regional buying positions.

Domestic steel prices strengthened modestly during the week, with plate increasing from PKR ***Steel Advances.*** 186,000 to PKR 187,000 per ton on November 17 and remaining unchanged thereafter. The corresponding Dollar equivalent improved to approximately USD 675.46 per ton. Although the increase offers some support to recycler margins, local finished-steel demand remains subdued, while imported scrap continues to provide an alternative source of raw material for domestic mills. These conditions favour disciplined vessel acquisitions rather than aggressive bidding.

The Pakistani Rupee has remained comparatively stable, with the Dollar trading around PKR 276.5 in the official market towards Friday. Currency stability provides a degree of predictability for Dollar-denominated purchases, although financing costs and the limited strength of downstream steel consumption remain important considerations. Conventional recycling indications are consequently unchanged, reflecting an orderly market rather than renewed buying urgency.

Gadani recorded the arrival of the 7,800 LDT bulk carrier VIKAN on November 12, providing an ***Fresh Arrival.*** addition to the existing recycling pipeline. The movement follows a quieter period at the waterfront and offers some welcome physical activity, although it should not be interpreted as evidence of a newly concluded recycling sale.

Pakistan enters the final weeks of November with a relatively balanced market position. Buyers remain interested, domestic steel has improved modestly and immediate purchasing requirements appear manageable. However, sustained progress will depend on stronger finished-steel demand and greater availability of recycling candidates at commercially workable levels. Gadani remains ready to respond when the right vessels emerge, without the urgency to chase every opportunity.
