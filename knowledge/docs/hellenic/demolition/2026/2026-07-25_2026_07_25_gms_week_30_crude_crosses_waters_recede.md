## Main
The thirteenth consecutive night of American strikes closed out the week, and for the first time since this conflict began, the map grew. Yemen's Houthisstruck two Saudi oil tankers in the Red Sea and threatened a blockade of the Bab al-Mandab Strait, the route Saudi barrels had been taking around a shut Hormuz. Washington answered with threats of military punishment for further attacks on shipping and floated billing the damage to frozen Iranian assets, while both sides ruled out near-term talks. Asked about a ceasefire, President Trump said Iran needs "more of the same." The war that spent months confined to one strait has developed ambitions.
Oil did what oil does when chokepoints multiply. Brent crossed USD 100 on Thursday for the first time since May before easing to USD 97.63 by Friday, with WTI near USD 89.77, still leaving crude up roughly ten percent on the week. A third front opened quietly in the Black Sea, where drone attacks on tankers loading at the Caspian Pipeline Consortium terminal on July 17, 19, and 21 halted operations, forcing Kazakhstan, which routes some four-fifths of its exports through the terminal, to cut production. Asian buyers have begun pricing Saudi crude via the Suez Canal and around the Cape of Good Hope, itineraries last considered state-of-the-art in 1869 and during the Ever Given's brief tenancy, respectively.
Freight, which spent last week ignoring the war, spent this week ignoring its own rally. The Baltic Dry Index fell more than six percent last week to 2,752 and kept going, touching 2,671 on Monday, its lowest since July 2, as the Capesize index dropped 5.6% and then another 5.1% to 3,889 on softer iron ore and coal enquiry and improving vessel availability. The Supramax index, contrary as ever, climbed to 1,738, its highest since August 2022. Last week's edition noted that cargo fundamentals were winning the argument "for another week." The market, rarely so punctual, took the deadline literally. ATERS RECEDE, WINDOW WAITS
The confirmed death toll from this month's floods and landslides has reached 57, according to the
The toll.
Ministry of Disaster Management and Relief, with three deaths added in the most recent daily
reporting period. Cox's Bazar accounts for 32 of the dead, including thirteen Rohingya refugees
killed in camp landslides; Chattogram has recorded fifteen, Bandarban six, Rangamati three, and
Moulvibazar one. More than a million people across seven districts were affected at the peak. The
waters are now receding: 111 shelters remain open with roughly 4,000 people still in them, down
from tens of thousands, and allocations of emergency cash and rice continue to reach the worst-
affected areas. Forecasters are still monitoring the river basins, but the operative word this week,
for the first time this month, is recovery.
The commercial thaw is visible. Chattogram port operations have largely normalised, with average
Recovery begins. vessel waiting back near a day and a half, and a threatened 48-hour prime mover and flatbed strike

meaningful capacity since the rains began, with a second window to follow August 12 to 15. The
