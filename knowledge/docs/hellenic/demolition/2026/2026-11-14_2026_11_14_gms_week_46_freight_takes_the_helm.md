## Main
Another week has passed without a meaningful change in global ship recycling conditions,although the trading market has strengthened its position once again. Dry freight has continued its recovery, crude oil has softened further and regional steel prices have offered some encouragement. Recycling indications remain unchanged across all four destinations, with healthy buying interest still frustrated by the persistent shortage of available candidates. Owners continue to enjoy worthwhile trading alternatives, leaving recyclers with limited opportunities to convert their appetite into fresh business.
Dry freight provided the week's clearest movement. The Baltic Dry Index advanced to 3,020 on Thursday, November 12, from 2,740 a week earlier, extending its recovery to seven consecutive sessions. Capesizes strengthened to 4,836, Panamaxes reached 2,117 and Supramaxes improved to 1,831. The broad-based improvement has reinforced employment prospects for ageing bulk carriers, reducing the immediate incentive for owners to consider recycling. For buyers awaiting a sustained freight correction, the market has once again moved in the opposite direction.
Oil prices continued to retreat, with Brent trading around USD 95.50 per barrel on Friday and WTI near USD 88. Expectations of reduced military escalation in the Middle East have weighed on crude, although continuing attacks on shipping and energy infrastructure demonstrate that regional security risks remain unresolved. Lower energy costs may offer some relief to recycling operations and steel production, but uncertain trading conditions continue to influence tanker employment and owners' disposal decisions.
Steel markets offered a somewhat firmer picture. Bangladesh plate strengthened during the week, while Pakistan recovered modestly from its recent correction. Indian steel also improved towards Friday, although Chinese quotations remained unchanged. These movements provide some welcome support to recycler margins without creating a compelling case for higher vessel bids. Currency conditions remain particularly important in Bangladesh, where Dollar appreciation continues to complicate purchasing calculations despite central bank intervention.
Physical waterfront activity also continued, although fresh transactions remain limited. Chattogram recorded further deliveries and smaller vessel arrivals, while Alang received another chemical tanker and updated the delivery status of previously arrived tonnage. Gadani reported no new movements. These developments reflect the progression of earlier purchases rather than a significant replenishment of the recycling sales pipeline.
Bangladesh continues to lead conventional buying sentiment, supported by improving seasonal conditions and stronger local steel. India retains its specialist advantages despite regulatory uncertainty, while Pakistan remains competitive but measured. Turkey continues to operate selectively amid mixed steel-market developments, including disruption at a major domestic mill.
The overall market remains stable, with no major change in its underlying commercial direction. Buyers are maintaining their positions, but stronger freight has once again demonstrated why owners remain reluctant to release ageing vessels. Steel may be providing some encouragement, yet profitable trading continues to offer a more persuasive alternative. For now, the beaches have the interest and the capacity. The trading market still has the upper hand.

## <u>BANGLADESH</u>

# SEASONAL WINDS FAVOUR CHATTOGRAM

Chattogram continues to display the strongest conventional recycling appetite across the sub- ***Seasonal Momentum.*** continent, with improving dry-season conditions adding further confidence to an already positive market. End buyers remain interested in suitable bulkers, tankers and container vessels, although the persistent shortage of candidates continues to restrict fresh negotiations. Expectations of stronger construction activity and more favourable yard operating conditions have supported an increasingly bullish outlook. However, available tonnage remains limited, and growing competition for suitable vessels could place additional pressure on purchasing margins. The stronger sentiment reflects genuine interest among recyclers, but the market still requires a more consistent flow of ships before that confidence can translate into meaningful transaction volumes.

Domestic plate improved from BDT 66,000 to BDT 67,500 per ton during the week, providing a ***Steel Strengthens.*** firmer foundation for recycling purchases. The corresponding Dollar equivalent also strengthened, although some breakers remain cautious about the relationship between prevailing demolition offers and local steel realisations. Limited vessel availability could encourage buyers to stretch their purchasing ideas beyond levels fully supported by current downstream conditions. While the approaching dry season is expected to stimulate construction and steel consumption, that anticipated improvement must still develop into sustained demand. Stronger steel provides encouragement, but protecting recoverable margins remains essential as buyers compete for scarce candidates.

Foreign currency conditions remain a considerable concern. LC rates are hovering around BDT ***Dollar Pressure.*** 125.50 to the Dollar, complicating the financing of internationally priced vessel acquisitions. Bangladesh Bank's interventions have so far helped contain further volatility, preventing currency conditions from undermining the market's positive momentum more severely. Nevertheless, continued Dollar appreciation could absorb some of the benefits of stronger domestic steel and restrict future purchasing flexibility. Currency exposure therefore remains an important consideration despite the otherwise encouraging outlook.

At the waterfront, the 3,421 LDT LPG tanker B-LPG SOPHIA completed delivery on November 9, followed by the 9,759 LDT wood chip carrier CRIMSON SATURN, delivered November 8. The 2,594 LDT general cargo vessel FOTEX arrived November 8, while the 751 LDT fishing trawler BILENE arrived November 11. These movements maintain physical activity, although they represent previously secured tonnage rather than new sales. Chattogram enters the coming weeks with positive sentiment, improving steel and seasonal expectations. The demand is present. The challenge is securing suitable vessels without allowing enthusiasm to outrun commercial fundamentals.

## INDIA

## ALANG FINDS VALUE BEYOND THE BID

Alang continues to maintain a constructive but selective recycling market, with its strongest opportunities remaining concentrated in specialist and higher-value tonnage. Conventional ***Specialists Lead.*** competition with Bangladesh and Pakistan remains challenging, although India's established recycling infrastructure allows buyers to pursue vessels whose commercial value extends beyond ordinary steel recovery. LNG and LPG carriers, reefers, passenger vessels and units containing attractive non-ferrous material continue to generate interest. These ships require a different assessment of recoverable equipment, material composition and technical handling requirements. Accordingly, India's conventional ranking does not fully reflect its ability to compete for specialised candidates. The market remains focused on finding commercially worthwhile opportunities rather than pursuing additional volume at the expense of margins.

Domestic steel provided modest encouragement following the substantial correction recorded last week. Alang plate began the reporting period around INR 40,800 per ton, briefly weakened to INR ***Plate Recovers.*** 40,700 and subsequently recovered to INR 41,000 by Friday. The Dollar equivalent improved to approximately USD 435.01 per ton, providing some relief to recyclers after recent weakness. Nevertheless, the recovery remains relatively modest and has not materially narrowed the conventional purchasing gap with neighbouring destinations. Currency movements, financing costs and cautious finished-steel demand continue to influence buying decisions. Recyclers therefore remain disciplined, particularly when assessing ordinary steel tonnage against the stronger competitive positions available elsewhere.

India's ambitions to secure European approval for selected recycling facilities remain subject to uncertainty following the November 4 objection by the European Parliament's ENVI Committee. The ***Regulatory Watch.*** objection concerns the proposed inclusion of Indian yards on the European List and does not constitute a final rejection.

Physical activity continued during the week, with the 12,915 LDT chemical tanker STOLT CONFIDENCE, previously recorded as arriving on November 6, now shown as delivered on the same date. The 2,057 LDT chemical tanker D.V. EARL also arrived November 6, reinforcing Alang's diverse vessel intake. Conventional recycling indications remain unchanged, with buyers maintaining a measured approach despite somewhat improved steel conditions. India's competitive strength continues to lie in the details of individual vessels, particularly where equipment, non-ferrous content and specialist handling offer additional value. Alang is not competing for every available hull. It remains focused on the opportunities that best suit its strengths.

## <u>PAKISTAN</u>

## GADANI RESISTS THE RUSH

Gadani experienced another subdued trading week, with recycling sentiment remaining broadly ***Demand Patient.*** stable and fresh candidate availability continuing to disappoint. Buyers retain an interest in commercially suitable bulkers and tankers, although the urgency that characterised earlier purchasing activity has moderated considerably. Previously secured vessels have progressively satisfied immediate yard requirements, allowing recyclers to approach new opportunities with greater discipline. Bangladesh continues to provide strong regional competition, particularly for conventional tonnage, but Pakistani buyers remain capable of responding when commercially attractive candidates become available. The quieter transaction environment therefore reflects limited vessel supply and reduced purchasing urgency rather than a fundamental deterioration in demand.

Domestic plate recovered modestly during the reporting period, strengthening from PKR 183,000 ***Steel Rebounds.*** to PKR 186,000 per ton on November 12 and remaining unchanged through Friday. The corresponding Dollar equivalent improved to approximately USD 669.79 per ton, offering some relief following the earlier correction. Nevertheless, local finished-steel consumption remains cautious, while imported scrap continues to provide an alternative source of raw material for domestic production. The Pakistani Rupee has remained comparatively stable, providing some predictability in Dollar-denominated purchasing calculations. October inflation eased to 9.6%, although financing conditions and downstream demand still favour disciplined acquisition rather than aggressive competition. The steel improvement is encouraging, but not sufficient to justify a substantial change in buying behaviour.

No new vessel movements were reported at Gadani this week, following the delivery of the 5,700 ***Supply Awaited.*** LDT bulk carrier SPARROW on November 5. That earlier movement advanced the pipeline of previously purchased vessels, but fresh candidates have yet to replenish the supply available for future yard requirements. While reduced immediate urgency allows recyclers to protect margins, prolonged scarcity could eventually leave operators seeking additional tonnage. Buyers must therefore balance present purchasing discipline against the need to maintain adequate work for the coming months. Suitable vessels can still attract meaningful interest, provided acquisition costs remain aligned with achievable steel realisations. Pakistan consequently enters the second half of November with stable underlying sentiment, slightly improved steel conditions and limited fresh transactional momentum. The market remains competitively positioned behind Bangladesh, although there is little incentive for recyclers to pursue marginal opportunities at inflated levels. Gadani retains the infrastructure, purchasing capacity and interest to respond when suitable ships emerge. For now, patience remains the preferred strategy. The buyers have not withdrawn. They are simply waiting for the right opportunity.
