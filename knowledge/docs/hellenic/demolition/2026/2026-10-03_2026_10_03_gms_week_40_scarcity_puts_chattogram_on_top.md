## Main
The Gulf supplied another reminder this week that improved cargo flows do not equal normal shipping conditions.Several tankers were reportedly struck by unknown projectiles while transiting the Strait of Hormuz, followed by another tanker incident on October 1 in which a vessel caught fire, with the crew reported safe. Gulf crude movements have recovered significantly from earlier disruption, but passage risk, security and insurance remain firmly embedded in the voyage calculation.
Oil nevertheless eased into Friday as improving Middle East crude exports took some pressure out of the immediate supply story. Brent traded around USD 101 per barrel and WTI near USD 89, both below the extremes seen through September but still far removed from pre-conflict levels. The barrels are increasingly finding a route. The cost and risk of moving them remain another matter.
Dry freight finally gave some ground, although not enough to materially improve recycling supply. The Baltic Dry Index recovered modestly to 3,140 on Thursday after four consecutive declines, with Capesizes at 5,010, Panamaxes at 2,379 and Supramaxes at 1,794. The headline index is well below last week's levels, but earnings remain sufficiently attractive to keep many ageing vessels employed rather than heading for recycling. Softer freight is useful. Cheap freight would be more useful.
Currencies provided no common direction. The Indian Rupee weakened toward 96.3 against the Dollar before Friday's Gandhi Jayanti holiday, while the Pakistani Rupee remained around 277 and Bangladesh's interbank reference stayed close to 123.1. The Turkish Lira slipped beyond 49 to the Dollar. Pakistan delivered the week's fresh regional inflation number, with September CPI easing to 10.26% from August's 11.1%, providing some macro relief without materially altering Gadani's buying equation.
At the beaches, physical activity remains considerably healthier than the fresh sales pipeline. Chattogram has processed a substantial run of previously secured tonnage, including large gas and tanker units, while Gadani has also received and delivered vessels secured during its earlier buying run. Alang remained quieter through the local holiday period. The distinction that defined September therefore survives into October: yards are working, but today's owners are still not replenishing tomorrow's supply quickly enough.
The commercial hierarchy changed more decisively this week. Bangladesh moved ahead of Pakistan to reclaim the top position across the conventional board, with Chattogram recyclers bidding more aggressively for the very limited selection of available candidates. Pakistan moved into second place as part of its immediate requirement has already been satisfied by vessels secured and delivered over recent months, although underlying demand remains firm. India held its conventional levels and continues to find stronger interest in specialist tonnage. The important point is that the change in leadership has not been driven by a sudden increase in vessel supply. Buyers are competing harder because the opposite remains true. Bangladesh has taken the lead. The shortage remains the market.

## <u>BANGLADESH</u>

# CHATTOGRAM WANTS THE HULLS

Chattogram has entered the final quarter with a noticeably stronger appetite and has now moved ***Bids Rise*** back to the top of the sub-continent board. After a quieter Q3 transaction cycle, end buyers are competing more aggressively for available units, pushing GMS indications higher across dry, tanker and container tonnage. The move is being driven primarily by requirement. Yard capacity is available, fresh candidates remain scarce and buyers increasingly have to improve their numbers when workable vessels appear.

The U-Ming controlled **ASIA CEMENT NO.3**, 3,520 LDT, provides a useful example of that ***Top Spot*** returning appetite, having been reported sold at USD 446/LT on an 'as is' Taiwan basis. The basis is not directly comparable with a delivered Chattogram quotation, but the transaction confirms the commercial direction. At the same time, the waterfront remains considerably busier than the fresh sales sheet. SC Discovery XLVI, 2,428 LDT, was delivered September 30, while the substantial 28,773 LDT gas carrier Mutiara and 3,060 LDT refrigerated cargo vessel NEPT were delivered September 29. Merc, Phil, Haku and B-LPG Sophia all arrived September 28, adding further tonnage to an already active waterfront.

Local steel supplied no equivalent rally. Bangladesh plate remained unchanged throughout the ***Supply Thin*** supplied period at BDT 64,000 per ton, approximately USD 522.24 per ton. This makes the rise in recycling indications more significant because the improved bid is not simply following a stronger domestic steel market. Requirement and candidate scarcity are doing more of the work. GMS indications have consequently moved to **USD 510/LDT for dry bulk, USD 530/LDT for tankers** **and USD 540/LDT for containers**, placing Bangladesh first on the Week 40 board. Chattogram now has the strongest conventional numbers, healthy waterfront activity and increasingly visible end- buyer appetite. What it still lacks is volume. The bid has returned to the top. The vessels have not returned with it.

# GMS WeeklyPage 4 of 8

# <u>PAKISTAN</u>

# GADANI STEPS BACK NOT OUT

Gadani has surrendered the top position this week as Bangladesh returned with the stronger ***Lead Lost*** conventional bid. The change follows several months in which Pakistani recyclers competed aggressively for scarce tonnage and secured a meaningful pipeline of vessels. As those ships have progressively arrived and delivered, some of the most urgent requirement has been satisfied. The resulting move lower should therefore be read as normalisation rather than a disappearance of demand.

The latest port position supports that interpretation. The 11,060 LDT tanker Yue was delivered ***Demand Firm*** September 25, while the 7,381 LDT bulker Portland 11 arrived September 28 and the 5,143 LDT bulker Maestro arrived September 24. Lina F remains another earlier arrival. Gadani therefore has physical tonnage feeding the waterfront, which reduces the need for recyclers to chase every candidate with the intensity seen during August. Local fundamentals have not weakened materially. Pakistan plate remained unchanged throughout the supplied period at PKR 195,000 per ton, approximately USD 696.06 per ton. Currency has similarly remained stable around the recent 277 area to the Dollar. The adjustment in vessel pricing therefore reflects relative buying urgency rather than a deterioration in steel or currency support.
