## Main
The dry freight retreat gathered pace this week, bringing awaited relief for recycling buyers without yet producingthe ships they need. The Baltic Dry Index recorded a steep weekly fall, led by Capesizes, while recycling indications across all four destinations remained unchanged. Owners now face a less comfortable outlook for larger bulkers, but one week's correction cannot overturn months of profitable trading. Freight has blinked. The sellers have not.
The Strait of Hormuz supplied a troubling counterweight. Singapore-registered GFS Pride was struck on October 13 and subsequently sank after catching fire; GFS Giselle sustained hull damage in a separate incident the next day. All 43 crew members were reported safe. The attacks sharpened concerns over passage, security and insurance, keeping disruption in voyage calculations. For ageing tankers, altered routes and restricted effective availability can still make further employment more attractive than recycling.
Oil markets also absorbed an intervention. On October 15, the International Energy Agency announced plans to accelerate release of approximately 100 million barrels remaining under its March emergency stock programme, following around 330 million barrels already released. Brent nevertheless remained around USD 105 to 106 per barrel on Friday. Additional inventories may ease supply pressure, but cannot remove Gulf shipping risks. The cost of disruption survives the arrival of extra barrels.
Dry bulk was the more immediate source of encouragement for recyclers. The Baltic Dry Index fell from 2,973 on October 8 to 2,545 on Thursday, October 15, a decline of more than 14%. Capesizes dropped from 4,512 to 3,322, while Panamaxes eased to 2,356. Supramaxes moved against the trend, improving to 1,856. The uneven retreat matters: larger bulkers may be feeling pressure, but smaller and mid-sized ships continue to find worthwhile employment. Falling indices alone do not deliver vessels to the beaches.
Steel supplied no common regional signal. Bangladesh plate rose from BDT 64,000 to BDT 66,000 per ton despite subdued finished-steel demand. Indian plate finished at INR 42,300, below last Friday, although the stronger Rupee lifted its Dollar equivalent to USD 445.17. Pakistan eased to PKR 195,000, while Turkey faced weak domestic steel consumption and currency pressure. India's September inflation rose to 5.54%, complicating financing and demand. None of this altered the recycling price board.

Chattogram added several bulker and tanker arrivals, and Gadani delivered two previously secured bulkers. These movements represent ships already in the pipeline, not newly agreed sales. Bangladesh retains the conventional pricing lead, Pakistan remains competitive, India concentrates on specialist value, and Turkey holds its established regional position. No fresh market sales were reported this week.
The next test is whether dry freight declines for long enough to change owners' decisions. The correction is real, but tanker employment remains supported by Gulf disruption, while smaller dry sectors have surrendered little ground. Recycling buyers have maintained their numbers without securing a fresh supply of candidates. The market has started moving in their direction, although it has not arrived there yet. Owners will need more than one uncomfortable week before the beaches see the benefit.

## <u>BANGLADESH</u>

# CHATTOGRAM NEEDS MORE THAN STEEL

Chattogram remains at the top of the conventional recycling rankings, where buying appetite ***Buyers Ready.*** continues to exceed the supply of workable candidates. Recyclers are still prepared to compete for suitable bulkers, tankers and container units, but their leading position has not translated into a meaningful increase in new commitments. Freight earnings and second-hand alternatives still give owners reasons to keep trading. Conventional recycling indications remain unchanged across all vessel categories, maintaining Bangladesh's competitive advantage over neighbouring destinations. The demand exists; the opportunity to turn it into business remains scarce.

Local steel gave Chattogram a welcome lift, with plate rising from BDT 64,000 to BDT 66,000 per ***Plate Advance.*** ton on October 11 and holding through Friday. Its Dollar equivalent increased from USD 522.24 to USD 538.56 per ton. The move improves the steel underpinning of vessel pricing, although demand for finished products remains subdued. At the waterfront, the 7,078 LDT bulker Uniorder arrived October 15 and the 8,275 LDT tanker Green October 14, following three smaller tankers earlier in the week. Petrova was delivered October 9, while the 911 LDT Pacific Leader is recorded as an arrival on October 6. These are movements of previously secured ships, not new recycling sales.

Attention is turning towards the approaching dry season, when construction activity and improved ***Seasonal Watch.*** operating conditions may lift local steel demand. That improvement remains an expectation rather than an established trend. The upcoming Durga Puja banking closure from October 20 to 24, including weekly holidays, also makes advance coordination of documentation and settlement arrangements important ahead of the October 25 to 28 delivery tides. Limited banking services at port-related branches may continue during the holiday period. With domestic consumption still slow, stronger plate quotations alone may not support a higher bid. Chattogram has the leading conventional numbers and buyers to use them, but its next breakthrough depends on owners releasing suitable ships.

# <u>PAKISTAN</u>

# GADANI'S PIPELINE COMES ASHORE

Gadani's waterfront has continued to receive the benefit of vessels secured during its earlier buying ***Pipeline Delivers.*** run. Maestro, a 5,143 LDT bulker, was delivered on October 14, following the 7,381 LDT Portland 11 on October 9. These movements advance the existing pipeline rather than signal fresh transactions, and have helped satisfy some immediate yard requirements. Bangladesh remains ahead on conventional indications, but Pakistan retains competitive numbers and buyers willing to consider workable candidates. The more measured approach now reflects reduced urgency, not an absence of interest.

Steel began the week at PKR 192,000 per ton, or USD 691.39, before recovering to PKR 195,000, ***Steel Settles.*** approximately USD 702.20, on October 12 and holding there through Friday. The increase reversed the early dip but left the market marginally below last week's closing PKR 196,000. With domestic finished-steel demand still cautious and imported scrap a competing source of supply, recyclers have limited incentive to chase vessel prices materially higher. The Rupee remains relatively stable and September inflation eased to 10.26%, providing some financial predictability without transforming the underlying demand picture.

Conventional recycling indications remain unchanged, with Pakistan retaining second place on ***Margins Matter.*** the sub-continent rankings. The shortage of fresh recycling candidates continues to frustrate transactional activity, but buyers also have reason to be selective after receiving tonnage secured during the stronger August market. When owners bring appropriate units to market, Gadani should remain a credible competitor. For now, attention remains on margin discipline and downstream steel sales rather than indiscriminate competition. Ships are reaching the plots, even if the next wave of purchases is still waiting to be agreed.
