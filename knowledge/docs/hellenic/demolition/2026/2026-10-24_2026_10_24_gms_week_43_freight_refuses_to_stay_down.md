## Main
Last week's dry freight retreat appeared to offer recycling buyers the opening they had been waiting for. It did not last.The Baltic Dry Index recovered from 2,545 on October 15 to 2,693 on Thursday, October 22, led by improving Capesize earnings. Although still below earlier October levels, the recovery has restored some confidence in continued trading. For recyclers hoping that softer freight would finally release ageing bulkers, the window of opportunity has already narrowed.
The energy market supplied a more worrying development. Renewed Houthi attacks in Saudi Arabia heightened concerns over Middle Eastern oil supplies, prompting Saudi Aramco to evacuate its Dhahran headquarters on Thursday. Brent climbed above USD 115 per barrel intraday on Friday, its highest level since early May, while WTI moved above USD 100. With Gulf shipping risks and elevated insurance costs continuing to disrupt normal trading patterns, ageing tankers still have compelling reasons to remain employed rather than head for recycling.
Dry bulk's recovery was uneven. Capesizes strengthened to 3,848, while Supramaxes advanced to 1,890. Panamaxes, however, retreated to 2,151, illustrating the different pressures facing individual vessel segments. The broader message remains unchanged: freight earnings and second-hand alternatives continue to discourage owners from releasing sufficient recycling candidates. One week's decline was encouraging. Its partial reversal has reminded buyers how quickly that encouragement can disappear.
Steel markets offered little common direction. Indian plate strengthened during the week, while Bangladesh, Pakistan and China remained unchanged. Domestic finished-steel consumption continues to disappoint across much of the sub-continent, limiting the benefit of otherwise stable steel quotations. Turkey weakened marginally against the international market, although its central bank reduced interest rates from 37% to 36%, offering modest financing relief amid continued currency pressure.
Physical waterfront activity nevertheless continued. Alang received additional general cargo and reefer tonnage, Chattogram completed several smaller vessel deliveries, and Gadani welcomed another previously secured bulker. These movements sustain yard operations without signalling a corresponding improvement in fresh recycling sales. The distinction remains important: ships are reaching the beaches, but the next pipeline is still far from full.
Bangladesh continues to display the strongest conventional buying appetite, although suitable candidates remain scarce. Pakistan retains healthy underlying demand with greater purchasing discipline, while India continues to benefit from specialist opportunities and improving local steel. Turkey remains cautious as weak domestic consumption restricts further buying enthusiasm.
The final quarter is therefore developing much as it began, with buyers seeking tonnage that owners have little urgency to release. Freight has recovered, steel offers mixed support, and recycling demand remains largely intact. The beaches are ready for more business. The ships, for now, still have other plans.

## <u>BANGLADESH</u>

# CHATTOGRAM LOOKS TO THE DRY SEASON

Chattogram continues to display healthy recycling demand, although the persistent shortage of ***Appetite Persists.*** available candidates is restricting fresh transactional activity. End buyers remain interested in suitable bulkers, tankers and container vessels, with yard requirements supporting the market despite disappointing domestic steel consumption. Local plate remained unchanged at BDT 66,000 per ton throughout the week, offering stability but little encouragement for further improvement. The underlying challenge remains the imbalance between demand for ships and owners' willingness to release them. Freight earnings and second-hand values continue to offer attractive alternatives, leaving Chattogram buyers competing for a limited selection of workable tonnage.

The waterfront has remained active, with four smaller vessels completing delivery during the ***Berths Turn.*** week, including three tankers and a general cargo unit. Larger previously secured vessels remain in the pipeline, providing further work for recycling yards once delivery arrangements are completed. Attention is now turning towards the October 25 to 28 delivery tides, although the extended Durga Puja banking closure from October 20 to 24 has required additional coordination of documentation and settlement arrangements. Essential services at designated port-related bank branches are expected to support necessary transactions. Physical movements remain encouraging, but the number of fresh candidates continues to fall short of buyers' requirements.

Sentiment remains cautiously positive as Bangladesh approaches the dry season. Recyclers are ***Season Approaches.*** hopeful that improving construction activity and more favourable operating conditions will gradually support local steel consumption. For the moment, however, finished-steel demand remains sluggish, with mills and stockists showing little urgency to rebuild inventories. Seasonal improvement therefore remains an expectation rather than an established recovery. Chattogram enters the coming weeks with available yard capacity, willing buyers and stable steel values. What it needs next is stronger downstream consumption and a more consistent flow of recycling candidates. The demand is present. The anticipated seasonal recovery must still prove itself.

# <u>PAKISTAN</u>

# GADANI KEEPS ITS OPTIONS OPEN

Gadani continues to maintain healthy underlying demand, although the urgency that Gadani ***Urgency Eases.*** continues to maintain healthy underlying demand, although the urgency that characterised its earlier purchasing activity has moderated considerably. A steady arrival of previously secured vessels has helped satisfy some immediate yard requirements, allowing recyclers to approach new opportunities with greater discipline. The latest arrival adds to physical waterfront activity, but the wider market remains constrained by the limited supply of fresh recycling candidates. Buyers are still interested in suitable bulkers and tankers, provided acquisition costs can be justified against domestic steel returns. The present caution should therefore be viewed as a more balanced purchasing approach rather than evidence of a substantial deterioration in sentiment.

Domestic plate remained unchanged at PKR 195,000 per ton throughout the week, providing a ***Steel Anchors.*** stable but unremarkable foundation for further vessel buying. The Pakistani Rupee also remained comparatively steady against the Dollar, offering predictability in transaction calculations. Finished-steel consumption continues to face a cautious environment, while imported scrap remains an important competing source of raw material for local steel production. September inflation eased to 10.26%, although financing and purchasing conditions remain challenging. With little fresh support from steel or currency movements, recyclers have limited incentive to pursue higher acquisition costs. Protecting margins remains an important consideration as yards assess future requirements.

Pakistan remains competitively positioned within the sub-continent, with buyers willing to ***Patience Pays.*** consider workable candidates when appropriate opportunities emerge. Competition from Bangladesh continues to influence purchasing decisions, particularly where vessels can attract interest from multiple destinations. Nevertheless, Gadani's ability to secure and receive tonnage during recent months has improved its immediate operating position. The next challenge is ensuring that today's measured purchasing activity does not leave yards short of candidates in the coming months. For now, buyers are content to maintain their interest without repeating August's exceptional urgency. Gadani has retained its appetite, but no longer feels compelled to chase every available hull.
