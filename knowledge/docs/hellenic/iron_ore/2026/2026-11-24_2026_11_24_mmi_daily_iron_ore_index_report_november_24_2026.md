## Main
Iron ore futures, Qingdao port spot products and seaborne quotes firmed in tandem today, while the high-grade port composite indexdiverged slightly. DCE most-traded closed at 658.5 yuan/mt, up 0.69% from Nov 23. All eight Qingdao spot products including PB Fines, Newman Fines, Mac Fines and Carajas Fines gained 5 yuan/wmt; port 61% and 58% indices climbed to 642 and 601 yuan/wmt while the 65% port composite index edged down 2 yuan to 800 yuan/wmt. Seaborne 61% and 65% composite indices rose to USD 89.30 and 105.55/dmt (each up USD 0.15/dmt) and all 12 seaborne brands advanced USD 0.10-0.20/dmt; the Friday-updated Domestic Ore Composite Price Index retained its Nov 20 quote of 792.40 yuan/mt (down 1.06% from Nov 19 on that date).
On the fundamental side, Western Australia route freight rose notably while overall supply and port inventory stayed elevated. Latest C5 freight climbed 5.34% to USD 15.20/mt on Nov 23 while C3 edged down 0.14% to USD 34.78/mt. In the week to Nov 20, global iron ore shipments stood at 35.2588 million mt (+10.88% WoW) and Chinese port arrivals at 27.0447 million mt (+1.04% WoW); total ten-port inventory was 109.2582 million mt on Nov 19 (up 1.3027 million mt, +1.21% WoW); surveyed daily hot metal was 2.3479 million mt on Nov 18 (down 15,000 mt WoW) with the BF operating rate at 86.86% (down 0.37 percentage point). Iron ore prices may trade on a steady-to-firm note in the short term, though upside room remains constrained. Consecutive futures gains, underlying mill demand and firmer C5 freight bolster spot sentiment, but rebounding overseas shipments, elevated ten-port inventory and relatively low hot metal output continue to cap upward elasticity. Mill margin recovery and winter restocking pace remain key to track; without sustained expansion in spot turnover, ore prices may stay within a range-bound pattern.
