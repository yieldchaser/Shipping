## Main
Iron ore futures and seaborne composite indices edged higher today, while port spot products and seaborne brands diverged narrowly.DCE most-traded closed at 654.0 yuan/mt, up 0.23% from Nov 20. Five Qingdao spot products including PB Fines and Newman Fines held flat, Mac Fines gained 2 yuan/wmt, and Carajas Fines and PB Lump slipped 2 yuan and 3 yuan/wmt; port 61%, 58% and 65% indices stood at 639, 597 and 802 yuan/wmt. Seaborne 61% and 65% composite indices rose slightly to USD 89.15 and 105.40/dmt while the 12 seaborne brands saw 4 gainers, 1 unchanged and 7 decliners; the Friday-updated Domestic Ore Composite Price Index retained its Nov 20 quote of 792.40 yuan/mt (down 1.06% from Nov 19 on that date). On the fundamental side, overseas shipments rebounded notably while port inventory stayed elevated. In the week to Nov 20, global iron ore shipments climbed to 35.2588 million mt, up 3.4585 million mt (+10.88% WoW), and Chinese port arrivals reached 27.0447 million mt, up 278,900 mt (+1.04% WoW). Total ten-port inventory stood at 109.2582 million mt on Nov 19, up 1.3027 million mt (+1.21%) WoW; surveyed daily hot metal was 2.3479 million mt on Nov 18 (down 15,000 mt WoW) with the BF operating rate at 86.86% (down 0.37 percentage point). On Nov 20, C3 ocean freight dipped 0.60% to USD 34.83/mt while C5 rose 1.62% to USD 14.43/mt. A cautious, range-bound near-term view remains appropriate. Steadying futures and resilient mainstream medium-grade spot prices offer floor support, but rebounding global shipments, elevated ten-port inventory and subdued hot metal output continue to cap upside room. Mill margin developments and the pace of winter restocking remain key to monitor; predominantly needs-based procurement could keep ore prices fluctuating within a narrow range.

# Key View

Iron ore futures and seaborne composite indices edged higher today, while port spot products and seaborne brands diverged narrowly. DCE most-traded closed at 654.0 yuan/mt, up 0.23% from Nov 20. Five Qingdao spot products including PB Fines and Newman Fines held flat, Mac Fines gained 2 yuan/wmt, and Carajas Fines and PB Lump slipped 2 yuan and 3 yuan/wmt; port 61%, 58% and 65% indices stood at 639, 597 and 802 yuan/wmt. Seaborne 61% and 65% composite indices rose slightly to USD 89.15 and 105.40/dmt while the 12 seaborne brands saw 4 gainers, 1 unchanged and 7 decliners; the Friday-updated Domestic Ore Composite Price Index retained its Nov 20 quote of 792.40 yuan/mt (down 1.06% from Nov 19 on that date).

On the fundamental side, overseas shipments rebounded notably while port inventory stayed elevated. In the week to Nov 20, global iron ore shipments climbed to 35.2588 million mt, up 3.4585 million mt (+10.88% WoW), and Chinese port arrivals reached 27.0447 million mt, up 278,900 mt (+1.04% WoW). Total ten-port inventory stood at

109.2582 million mt on Nov 19, up 1.3027 million mt (+1.21%) WoW; surveyed daily hot metal was 2.3479 million mt on Nov 18 (down 15,000 mt WoW) with the BF operating rate at 86.86% (down 0.37 percentage point). On Nov 20, C3 ocean freight dipped 0.60% to USD 34.83/mt while C5 rose 1.62% to USD 14.43/mt. A cautious, range-bound near-term view remains appropriate. Steadying futures and resilient mainstream medium-grade spot prices offer floor support, but rebounding global shipments, elevated ten-port inventory and subdued hot metal output continue to cap upside room. Mill margin developments and the pace of winter restocking remain key to monitor; predominantly needs-based procurement could keep ore prices fluctuating within a narrow range.

# Market Commentary · Imported Ore

Futures & spot DCE most-traded I2701 closed at 654.0 yuan/mt, up 1.5 yuan (+0.23%) from 652.5 on Nov 20. Qingdao PB Fines (628), Newman Fines (628), BRBF (683), Newman Lump (793) and SSF (490 yuan/wmt) all held flat from the previous trading day; Mac Fines gained 2 yuan to 633 yuan/wmt, while Carajas Fines fell 2 yuan to 781 yuan/wmt and PB Lump slipped 3 yuan to 780 yuan/wmt. Futures continued to edge higher while mainstream medium-grade fines held steady and high-grade fines and lump softened slightly; single-day divergence mainly reflects trading pace across products rather than a trend shift in end-use demand. SGX official current-month FEFX26 was last priced at USD 90.50/dmt, with quote time Nov 23, 17:29:03 and preparation time 17:43:58 (UTC+8).

D rivers The seaborne 61% and 65% indices rose to USD 89.15 and 105.40/dmt (+0.06% and +0.38%), widening their grade spread from USD 15.90 to 16.25/dmt; the port 61% index edged down 1 yuan to 639 yuan/wmt while the 58% and 65% indices held at 597 and 802 yuan/wmt. Across the 12 seaborne brands, 4 rose, 1 held flat and 7 declined, diverging slightly from the composite indices due to sampling windows, forward cargo liquidity and spot negotiation differences; one-day spread changes do not imply a reversal in blending preferences. RMB wet-basis and USD dry-basis quotes require separate tax and currency assessment.

D emand Latest surveyed daily hot metal was 2.3479 million mt on Nov 18, down 15,000 mt (-0.63%) from Nov 11; the BF operating rate fell 0.37 percentage point to 86.86%. Routine furnace maintenance kept hot metal output relatively soft, leaving mill procurement focused on needs-based restocking. Apparent finished steel consumption, mill profitability and the pace of winter raw material restocking remain key to monitor; without a clear rebound in hot metal output, upside elasticity for ore prices may stay capped.

Inventory & In the week to Nov 20, global iron ore shipments rose to 35.2588 million mt, up 3.4585 million mt (+10.88%) supply from 31.8003 million mt on Nov 13; Chinese port arrivals reached 27.0447 million mt, up 278,900 mt (+1.04%) from 26.7658 million mt on Nov 13. Total ten-port inventory stood at 109.2582 million mt on Nov 19, up 1.3027 million mt (+1.21%) WoW: fines fell 480,500 mt to 83.4195 million mt, concentrates rose 1.2156 million mt to

9.5879 million mt, lump gained 603,300 mt to 14.6763 million mt and pellets dipped 35,700 mt to 1.5745 million mt. Higher overseas shipments will feed into arrivals over upcoming voyage cycles, keeping overall supply ample alongside elevated ten-port stocks; inventory and shipment/arrival periods differ, and shipping lags can cause asynchronous moves across stages.
Cost & margin Latest C3 freight was USD 34.83/mt on Nov 20, down 0.21 (-0.60%) from Nov 19; C5 rose 0.23 (+1.62%) to 14.43. A further modest gain on the Western Australia route alongside a slight dip on the Brazil route will transmit to forward landed costs depending on laycans and contract settlement terms. With port spot prices consolidating narrowly, steelmaking margins still require comprehensive evaluation alongside ex-works steel prices, coke price-cut developments, exchange rates, grades, taxes and inventory valuation.

# Market Commentary · Domestic Ore

Prices Updated every Friday, the Domestic Ore Composite Price Index stood at its latest valid quote of 792.40 yuan/mt as of Nov 20, down 8.48 yuan (-1.06%) from 800.88 on Nov 19 on that date. The national composite index and regional iron ore concentrates differ in grade, wet/dry basis and tax treatment, so absolute price levels are not directly comparable.

Supply & Surveyed daily hot metal fell 15,000 mt WoW to 2.3479 million mt on Nov 18, keeping mill purchasing of demand domestic concentrates cautious and volume-controlled during furnace maintenance; beneficiation plant operating rates, environmental requirements and winter transport conditions also continue to vary across regions. With imported port spot prices holding largely steady, local domestic-imported price spreads still require evaluation alongside grades and delivered plant costs.

Outlook The domestic ore market may remain steady-to-soft with regional divergence in the near term. Tight local availability of quality concentrates paired with periodic mill restocking could offer partial support, whereas subdued hot metal output and pressure on finished steel sales may keep cost control weighing on upside room. Beneficiation plant shipping sentiment and winter restocking pace remain key to watch.
