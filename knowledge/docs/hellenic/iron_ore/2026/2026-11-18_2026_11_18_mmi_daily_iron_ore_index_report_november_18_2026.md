# Key View

Iron ore futures and spot prices rebounded modestly together today. DCE most-traded closed at 646.0 yuan/mt, up 0.86% from the previous close. All eight Qingdao spot products rose 3 yuan/wmt, seaborne 61% and 65% indices climbed to USD 88.70 and 104.00/dmt, and all 12 brands moved higher. The rebound indicates near- term quotation support, but a single-day gain does not establish a broad strengthening in procurement demand.

Supply-demand and freight indicators remained mixed. Latest surveyed daily hot metal edged down to 2.3479 million mt on Nov 18 (down 15,000 mt WoW) and the blast furnace operating rate slipped 0.37 percentage point to

86.86%, while C3 and C5 ocean freight fell another 0.82% and 3.38% on Nov 17. Chinese arrivals fell 13.70% WoW in the week to Nov 13, whereas latest available ten-port stocks remain 107.9555 million mt as of Nov 12; lower arrivals or a one-day rebound do not prove that inventory pressure has cleared. A cautious, range-bound near-term view remains appropriate. Firmer prices, lower arrivals and softer ocean freight offer interim support, while lower hot metal output, existing inventory accumulation and the risk of limited restocking follow-through constrain upside. Improved mill margins accompanied by steady restocking could reinforce support; predominantly needs-based buying could keep prices fluctuating with trading and resource composition.
