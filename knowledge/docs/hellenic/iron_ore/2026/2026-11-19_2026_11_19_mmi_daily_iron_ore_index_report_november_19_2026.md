# Key View

Iron ore futures and spot prices extended modest gains today with slight divergence across products. DCE most-traded closed at 648.0 yuan/mt, up 0.31% from the previous close. Seven Qingdao spot products including PB Fines and Carajas Fines rose 1 yuan/wmt while PB Lump slipped 2 yuan/wmt; seaborne 61% and 65% indices rose to USD 89.10 and 105.00/dmt with all 12 seaborne brands higher, whereas the port 58% index fell 3 yuan to 594 yuan/wmt. Offers remained resilient overall, though mild one-day moves do not establish a broad strengthening in procurement demand.

Fundamental data showed continued inventory accumulation alongside softer demand indicators. Latest ten-port inventory climbed to 109.2582 million mt on Nov 19, up 1.3027 million mt (+1.21%) WoW, as higher concentrate and lump stocks outweighed modest draws in fines and pellets. Surveyed daily hot metal fell to 2.3479 million mt on Nov 18 (down 15,000 mt WoW) and the BF operating rate slipped 0.37 percentage point to 86.86%. On Nov 18, C3 ocean freight edged up 0.57% to USD 35.13/mt while C5 dipped 0.29% to USD 13.70/mt.

A cautious, range-bound near-term view remains appropriate. Steady futures and spot offers together with a modest draw in port fines inventory provide interim support, but higher total ten-port inventory, lower hot metal output and needs-based mill purchasing continue to cap upside room. Improved mill margins accompanied by winter restocking could firm support; continued inventory build and limited buying follow-through could keep ore prices fluctuating narrowly around trading activity and product structure.
