# Key View

Iron ore prices weakened broadly today. DCE most-traded closed at 671.0 yuan/mt, down 1.83% from the previous close, and all eight Qingdao spot products declined. The 61%, 58% and 65% port indices fell 4, 3 and 3 yuan, respectively; seaborne 61% and 65% indices eased to USD 90.20 and 106.65/dmt. Most brands fell while SP10 edged higher, highlighting resource differences rather than a broad trend inferred from one product.

Inventory pressure is an important constraint. Ten-port stocks rose 3.2766 million mt WoW on Nov 12, with fines and lump contributing most of the increase. Latest surveyed daily hot metal rose 5,600 mt, but the improvement in consumption was modest. Earlier higher arrivals still require purchasing and port outbound volume to absorb them. Different samples and reporting periods mean the stock increase should not be attributed entirely to one factor.

A cautious, range-bound near-term view remains appropriate. Slightly higher hot metal and a steady domestic index provide some support, while weaker futures and spot prices, port stock accumulation and some higher transport costs constrain a rebound. Better mill orders and margins accompanied by sustained purchases could strengthen support; slow stock absorption and needs-based buying could leave ore prices fluctuating under pressure.

# Market Commentary · Imported Ore

Futures & spot DCE most-traded I2701 closed at 671.0 yuan/mt, down 12.5 yuan (-1.83%) from 683.5 on Nov 11. Qingdao PB Fines were 638, Newman Fines 638, BRBF 686, Newman Lump 803 and SSF 500 yuan/wmt, all down 8 yuan. Mac Fines fell 10 yuan to 641, Carajas Fines 4 yuan to 808 and PB Lump 6 yuan to 800. Joint futures and spot declines indicate pricing pressure, but do not directly quantify trading or procurement strength. SGX official current-month FEFX26 was last priced at USD 91.25/dmt, with quote time Nov 12, 17:38:33 and preparation time 17:56:32 (UTC+8).

Drivers The seaborne 61% and 65% indices fell 1.20% and 0.42%, widening their grade spread; all three port grade indices declined. Most seaborne brands softened while SP10 rose, potentially reflecting resource quality, quotation timing or procurement composition. A single brand rebound does not establish broad demand improvement. USD dry-basis and RMB wet-basis prices differ in taxes and exchange-rate treatment and should not be translated directly into realizable arbitrage spreads.

Demand Latest surveyed daily hot metal at 242 mills was 2.3629 million mt on Nov 11, up 5,600 mt (+0.24%) from Nov 4; the blast furnace operating rate rose 0.17 percentage point to 87.23%. Modestly improved consumption provides some support, but the limited gain is not enough to offset stock-building pressure. Better orders and margins accompanied by sustained buying could strengthen spot support; predominantly needs-based restocking could constrain a durable rebound.

Inventory & Ten-port stocks were 107.9555 million mt on Nov 12, up 3.2766 million mt (+3.13%) from Nov 5. Fines were supply

83.9000 million mt (+2.4953 million mt), concentrates 8.3723 million mt (+136,900 mt), lump 14.0730 million mt (+674,900 mt) and pellets 1.6102 million mt (-30,500 mt). Fines and lump accounted for most of the total increase. In the latest week to Nov 6, global shipments fell 7.89% to 31.6051 million mt while Chinese arrivals rose 33.30% to 31.0133 million mt. Voyage lags and reporting periods differ; the latest stock build cannot be attributed entirely to a single supply or demand factor.
Cost & margin Latest C3 freight was USD 36.16/mt on Nov 11, down 0.35 (-0.96%) from Nov 10; C5 rose 0.11 (+0.80%) to 13.78. Costs diverged across the two routes, with actual transmission depending on contracts and settlement timing. Lower seaborne and port prices may alter trading and steelmaking costs, but grades, exchange rates, taxes and inventory valuation require separate assessment; lower ore prices alone do not establish improved margins.

# Market Commentary · Domestic Ore

Prices The Domestic Ore Composite Price Index was 792.92 yuan/mt on Nov 12, unchanged from Nov 11. The national composite and regional concentrates differ in grade, wet/dry basis and taxes; absolute prices are not directly comparable, and an unchanged composite does not establish unchanged transaction prices in every region.

Supply & The latest modest rebound in surveyed hot metal may offer some support to domestic ore consumption, while demand mine and beneficiation supply, regional transport and resource quality still create differences. Lower imported ore quotations may alter substitution choices at some mills, subject to grades and delivered costs. The composite alone does not establish nationwide tightness or sustained procurement improvement.

Outlook Regional divergence may persist in the near term. Tight availability of good-quality resources accompanied by mill purchases could support local offers; limited improvement in orders and margins, with needs-based buying and price pressure from buyers, could restrain gains.
