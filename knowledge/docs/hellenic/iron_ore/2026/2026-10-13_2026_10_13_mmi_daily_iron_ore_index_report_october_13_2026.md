A combination of margin repair and stronger orders could gradually generate additional purchases and support prices. For now, cost improvement should be distinguished from demand improvement, and gains in individual brands should not be treated as an overall trend reversal.

### Market Commentary · Imported Ore

Drivers Seaborne quotations softened again after the previous day’s broad recovery, alongside declines in domestic futures and composite port indices, showing that the price recovery lacks persistence. Lower costs may ease some pressure on mills, but end-user orders, finished-steel prices and actual purchases jointly determine demand support. Gains in individual brands should not be read as a broad improvement in demand; transaction volumes and restocking schedules remain important.

Demand SMM’s Oct 13 review cited projected hot metal output losses from blast furnace maintenance of 1.9011 million mt for Oct 10–16, up 78,600 mt WoW, highlighting the constraint from maintenance schedules. This covers a week still in progress rather than a realised decline in daily hot metal. Mill enquiries were reasonable but overall transactions remained limited, with purchases focused on immediate needs. The second coke cut has taken effect, but additional buying still depends on finished-steel margins and orders.
