## Main
On the second session of the holiday China stayed dark across the board and the seaborne market againpriced alone: the MMi 61% seaborne index fell to USD 92.35/dmt (-0.70%) and the 65% to 111.15 (-0.54%). Across the two closed sessions the 61% is down USD 1.85 (-1.96%) and the 65% USD 0.85 (-0.76%), widening the index-basis high-low spread from USD 17.80 to 18.80 - the same direction as the port spot spread reaching 200 yuan/wmt before the break. At the brand level, though, the structure inverted overnight: yesterday low grades led the fall, today they stood alone on the upside. Mainstream mid grades fell USD 0.45-0.70 (PB Fines -0.75%, Newman Fines -0.60%, Mac Fines -0.49%) and the Brazilian fines led the declines (Blend -1.73%, SSF -1.49%), while SP10 rose USD 1.55 to 80.10 (+1.97%) and RTX Fines held at 87.15. Taken together, low grades were first knocked down by holiday liquidity and then caught by a different logic. That logic surfaced today: on Sep 30 CSN Mineração cut its 2026 iron ore guidance from 45-47 million mt to 39-41 million, suspended low-grade processing at the Pires Complex dry plant, reduced third-party purchases and raised C1 cash cost guidance from USD 22.0-23.5 to 25.0-26.0/mt. The reason it gave was neither demand nor ore prices but margin pressure from high Brazil-China freight. SMM's weekly review frames it as the quarter's first case of high freight converting directly into production cuts, with a weighty line attached: "high freight has gone from a cost problem to a supply problem". The chain is worth writing out: freight spikes → landed margins on low-grade ore are squeezed out → miners shut dry plants and cut guidance → low-grade supply contracts. Recent issues of this report have treated falling freight as bearish because it reopens room for Brazilian shipments; CSN is a reminder that freight cuts both ways on supply, and that it shows up at the low-grade end first. Three constraints still apply: the cut is only about 6 million mt at the guidance midpoint, limited once spread across Q4, and explicitly reversible - and SGX closed down 1.43% on the day it was announced, which is the market's own verdict. SMM's weekly review also set the post-holiday coordinates: an SGX range of USD 90-95/mt with the centre of gravity still drifting lower; 1.6839 million mt of maintenance losses land in the first week back, while port stocks build through the holiday and mill deliveries bunch up, so neither side of the balance is likely to favour prices at the reopen. A coke cut in early October could put in a floor, but SMM is explicit that margin repair feeding through to higher hot metal "will not happen in the first week back"
