## Main
On the second session of the holiday China stayed dark across the board and the seaborne market againpriced alone: the MMi 61% seaborne index fell to USD 92.35/dmt (-0.70%) and the 65% to 111.15 (-0.54%). Across the two closed sessions the 61% is down USD 1.85 (-1.96%) and the 65% USD 0.85 (-0.76%), widening the index-basis high-low spread from USD 17.80 to 18.80 - the same direction as the port spot spread reaching 200 yuan/wmt before the break. At the brand level, though, the structure inverted overnight: yesterday low grades led the fall, today they stood alone on the upside. Mainstream mid grades fell USD 0.45-0.70 (PB Fines -0.75%, Newman Fines -0.60%, Mac Fines -0.49%) and the Brazilian fines led the declines (Blend -1.73%, SSF -1.49%), while SP10 rose USD 1.55 to 80.10 (+1.97%) and RTX Fines held at 87.15. Taken together, low grades were first knocked down by holiday liquidity and then caught by a different logic. That logic surfaced today: on Sep 30 CSN Mineração cut its 2026 iron ore guidance from 45-47 million mt to 39-41 million, suspended low-grade processing at the Pires Complex dry plant, reduced third-party purchases and raised C1 cash cost guidance from USD 22.0-23.5 to 25.0-26.0/mt. The reason it gave was neither demand nor ore prices but margin pressure from high Brazil-China freight. SMM's weekly review frames it as the quarter's first case of high freight converting directly into production cuts, with a weighty line attached: "high freight has gone from a cost problem to a supply problem". The chain is worth writing out: freight spikes → landed margins on low-grade ore are squeezed out → miners shut dry plants and cut guidance → low-grade supply contracts. Recent issues of this report have treated falling freight as bearish because it reopens room for Brazilian shipments; CSN is a reminder that freight cuts both ways on supply, and that it shows up at the low-grade end first. Three constraints still apply: the cut is only about 6 million mt at the guidance midpoint, limited once spread across Q4, and explicitly reversible - and SGX closed down 1.43% on the day it was announced, which is the market's own verdict. SMM's weekly review also set the post-holiday coordinates: an SGX range of USD 90-95/mt with the centre of gravity still drifting lower; 1.6839 million mt of maintenance losses land in the first week back, while port stocks build through the holiday and mill deliveries bunch up, so neither side of the balance is likely to favour prices at the reopen. A coke cut in early October could put in a floor, but SMM is explicit that margin repair feeding through to higher hot metal "will not happen in the first week back"

### Market Commentary · Imported Ore

Futures & spot A second consecutive closed session in China. Yuan spot, the three port spot indices and the DCE board were all unquoted; the tables carry their last Sep 30 values with a marker. Offshore, SGX most-traded settled at USD 93.43/dmt on Oct 1 (-0.07, -0.07%), essentially flat; on Oct 2 the seaborne indices read 92.35 for the 61% (-0.70%) and 111.15 for the 65% (-0.54%). Two basis notes: first, “prices as of Oct 2” applies only to seaborne products — the effective cut-off is Sep 30 for yuan spot and port spot and Oct 1 for SGX. Second, SMM's two descriptions of the holiday's length differ — the Oct 1 cost table calls it “an eight-day National Day holiday” while the Oct 2 weekly review says the market is “closed from Oct 1 through Oct 7” — so this report draws no conclusion on the reopening date.

Drivers The first genuine supply-side variable of the holiday arrived this issue. SMM's weekly review reports that on Sep 30 CSN Mineração cut its 2026 iron ore production guidance to 39–41 million mt from 45–47 million, suspended low-grade processing at the Pires Complex dry plant and reduced third-party ore purchases, raising C1 cash cost guidance to USD 25.0–

26.0/mt. The company's stated reason is margin pressure from high Brazil–China freight, and it says output can be restored once conditions improve. SMM's read is blunt: this is the quarter's first case of high freight converting directly into production cuts, and “high freight has gone from a cost problem to a supply problem”. SMM also notes the cut is only about 6 million mt at the midpoint of guidance, limited once spread across Q4, and explicitly reversible — SGX in fact closed lower on Sep 30, which tells you how much the market priced it.
Demand No new demand data. SMM's weekly review lays out the full five-period hot metal series: 2.4080, 2.4028, 2.3991, 2.3943 and

2.3882 million mt, with WoW declines of 5,200, 3,700, 4,800 and 6,100 mt — consistently in one direction, with the last three drops widening. Basis note: the review is headlined “five straight weeks of decline”, referring to those five readings; counted by the number of WoW declines it is the fourth in a row, which is the basis this report uses. Both describe the same data. The review also notes that two furnaces entering maintenance and two restarting broadly offset, so the fall came from operating intensity itself. Maintenance losses of 1.6839 million mt are due in the first week back (Oct 3–9).
Inventory & No new weekly data. The 10-port total still reads 103.02 million mt as of Sep 30 (+430,000 mt WoW), and SMM notes the build supply has slowed from the prior period's 680,000 mt. Global shipments were 35.51 million mt (+3% WoW, with Australia and non- mainstream origins up and Brazil still easing) against arrivals in China of 29.46 million mt (-6%); both remain high in absolute terms, so the comfortably supplied backdrop is unchanged. Freight was last published Sep 29 at USD 40.26/mt for C3 and USD 14.13/mt for C5. The CSN cut is worth watching: if Brazil–China freight stays at current levels, SMM believes comparable low-grade capacity faces the same margin pressure and the cuts may not stop at one producer.

Cost & margin No new cost table; the Oct 1 print of an average 2.45 yuan/mt loss (narrowed from 6.12) still stands. The weekly review adds two useful quantifications. First, on Sep 30 the DCE rose 0.50% while SGX fell 1.43%, narrowing the internal-external spread from -23.6 to -9.1 yuan in a single session. Second, the yuan's central parity firmed 138 basis points over the week to

6.7351, offsetting import costs — but on a yuan-converted basis SGX still fell 18.6 yuan/mt, not enough to change direction. That is consistent with the note in our Oct 1 issue: margin improvement driven by the currency does not reflect ore strength and should not be extrapolated.
