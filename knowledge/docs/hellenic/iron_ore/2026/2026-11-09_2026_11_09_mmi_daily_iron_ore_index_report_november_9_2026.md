Product quotations and composite indices have different samples; this does not by itself establish inconsistent pricing.

Drivers The seaborne 61% and 65% composite indices fell 0.49% and 0.32%, respectively, and all 12 brand quotations eased against the previous report, while RMB futures and major port products held steady. Quotation timing, resource composition and procurement schedules may contribute to these differences. A slightly wider seaborne grade spread alongside a narrower port spread does not establish a change in blending demand. Dry/wet bases, taxes and exchange rates also prevent direct comparison of absolute prices across the two markets.

Demand The latest 242-mill survey showed daily hot metal output of 2.3573 million mt on Nov 4, down 4,800 mt from Oct 28, and a blast furnace operating rate of 87.06%, down 0.24 percentage point. Existing weekly indicators have yet to show incremental support from ore consumption. Better margins and orders accompanied by sustained purchases could strengthen spot support; predominantly needs-based restocking could make stock absorption more challenging as arrivals rebound.

Inventory & Ten-port stocks were 104.6789 million mt on Nov 5, up 643,600 mt (+0.62%) from Oct 29. Fines were 81.4047 supply million mt (-413,500 mt), concentrates 8.2354 million mt (+705,000 mt), lump 13.3981 million mt (+382,900 mt) and pellets 1.6407 million mt (-30,800 mt). In the week to Nov 6, global shipments fell 2.7068 million mt (-7.89%) to 31.6051 million mt, while Chinese arrivals rose 7.7470 million mt (+33.30%) to 31.0133 million mt. Voyage lags can explain differing directions in shipments and arrivals. Higher arrivals may add to subsequent port absorption pressure, but the earlier stock build cannot be attributed entirely to that arrivals period.

Cost & margin Latest C3 freight was USD 36.71/mt on Nov 6, down 0.05 (-0.14%) from Nov 5; C5 was 13.35, down 0.13 (-0.96%). Slightly lower freight may ease import transport costs, with actual transmission depending on contract pricing and settlement timing. Lower seaborne quotations alongside steady major port prices may alter some trading spreads, but exchange rates, grades, taxes and inventory costs require separate assessment; this does not directly imply improved steelmaking margins.
