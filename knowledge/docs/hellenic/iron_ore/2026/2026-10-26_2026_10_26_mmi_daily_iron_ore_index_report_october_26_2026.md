## Main
Domestic futures and port prices weakened while seaborne quotations were relatively stable. DCE most-traded closed at 671.5 yuan/mt, down0.52%; the 61%, 58% and 65% port indices fell to 654, 608 and 813 yuan/wmt. The seaborne 61% index held at USD 89.20/dmt, the 65% eased to 106.75, and all 12 brand quotations were unchanged from the previous report. Divergent performance indicates that price support still differs across markets. Supply flows are recovering. In the week to Oct 23, arrivals in China rose 12.47% WoW and global shipments increased 2.83%. Higher arrivals may add to available spot material, but inventory changes also depend on unloading and mill collections. The latest modest increase in hot metal supports baseline consumption without necessarily implying discretionary restocking. Sustained transactions remain the key test of near-term direction. Needs-based buying could leave higher arrivals and existing stocks constraining upside; improved orders and margins followed by stronger collections would support the market. Supply flows, port trading and mill profitability should be assessed together rather than treating a single day of flat seaborne quotations as a broad trend improvement

# Market Commentary · Imported Ore

Futures & spot DCE most-traded I2701 closed at 671.5 yuan/mt, down 0.52% from Oct 23. Qingdao PB Fines were 640, Newman Fines 640, Mac Fines 646, SSF 502 and Carajas Fines 815 yuan/wmt, each down 3 yuan. BRBF fell 6 yuan to 690, Newman Lump fell 13 yuan to 820 and PB Lump fell 8 yuan to 815. SGX official current-month FEFV26 was last priced at USD 90.70/dmt, timestamped Oct 26, 17:54:57 (UTC+8). SMM’s daily review described needs-based mill purchases and limited spot transactions.

Drivers Domestic futures and port quotations fell while the seaborne 61% index and brand quotations were broadly steady, showing short-term differences across markets. The larger decline in the low-grade port index may influence blending economics, but a spread change alone does not establish a sustained shift in demand structure. Price support still needs confirmation from transactions and mill buying.

Demand The latest SMM survey of 242 mills showed daily hot metal output of 2.3584 million mt on Oct 21, up 6,400 mt from Oct 14, with a blast furnace operating rate of 87.16%. Improved production may support baseline consumption, but restocking also depends on mill stocks, finished-steel orders and margins. If purchases mainly cover immediate needs, the modest output increase may offer limited support for ore prices.

Inventory & Global shipments were 33.4494 million mt in the week to Oct 23, up 2.83% WoW. Australia shipped 19.7148 supply million mt (+5.77%) and Brazil 8.2370 million mt (+3.50%). Arrivals in China rose 12.47% to 27.6076 million mt. The latest ten-port stocks were 104.6534 million mt on Oct 22, up 632,200 mt from Oct 15. Higher arrivals may increase available material, but further inventory pressure also depends on unloading and mill collections.

Cost & margin Oct 23 freight was USD 34.09/mt for C3, up 1.76% from the previous reading, and 14.16 for C5, down 0.28%. Route-specific costs differed, while actual landed costs also depend on quotations, contracts and exchange rates. Lower port selling prices may squeeze returns on some trades, but price direction alone cannot establish a margin; import trading and steelmaking profitability should be assessed separately. SMM’s assessment published on the morning of Oct 26 put the period’s average import margin at 4.57 yuan/mt, up 3.54 yuan from

1.03; this does not represent mill profitability after the current session’s close.
# Market Commentary · Domestic Ore

Prices The latest Domestic Ore Composite Price Index was 797.36 yuan/mt on Oct 23, up 0.13% from the previous reading. SMM’s Oct 26 Shandong brief quoted 64% basic concentrate at 757 yuan/mt, up 2 yuan, on a dry- basis, pre-tax ex-works basis. This regional quotation is not directly comparable with the national index.

Supply & SMM reported mostly normal mine operations in Shandong, virtually no stocks at miners and nearby traders, demand and scheduled shipments at smaller plants and traders. Local resources and contract execution may support prices, but mill purchasing still depends on margins and orders; the regional description does not represent national inventory conditions.

Outlook SMM expects Shandong concentrate prices to remain relatively stable near term. Tight regional supply and sustained collections could support offers, while continued mill cost control and cautious buying may limit upside.
