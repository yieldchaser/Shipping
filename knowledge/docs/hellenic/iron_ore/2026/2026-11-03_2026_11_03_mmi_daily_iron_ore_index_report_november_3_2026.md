## Main
The previous recovery did not persist, with domestic futures and spot prices retreating today. DCE most-traded closed at 671.0 yuan/mt,down 0.52%. The 61%, 58% and 65% port indices were 651, 605 and 813 yuan/wmt: the low-grade index held steady while the others eased. The seaborne 61% and 65% indices were USD 90.70 and 106.40/dmt, with all 12 brands lower, indicating fragile price support. Supply flows and baseline consumption offer some cushion. Latest Chinese arrivals fell sharply, ten-port stocks declined and hot metal previously rose modestly. These observations cover different periods and do not by themselves establish stronger demand today. Actual restocking still depends on orders, margins and mill stocks, with transactions needed to confirm the transmission from production to buying. The near-term test is whether price adjustments generate persistent collections. Needs-based buying could leave stocks and profitability constraining upside, while stronger orders alongside margin repair could improve support. Divergent C3 and C5 freight also calls for route-specific cost assessment. A cautious, range bound view remains appropriate rather than extrapolating a single move into a trend reversal.

# Market Commentary · Imported Ore

Futures & spot DCE most-traded I2701 closed at 671.0 yuan/mt, down 0.52% from Nov 2. Qingdao PB Fines were 640, Newman Fines 640, BRBF 688, SSF 502, Carajas Fines 812 and PB Lump 810 yuan/wmt, each down 5 yuan; Mac Fines were 648 (-2) and Newman Lump 810 (-10). SGX official near-month FEFX26 (November 2026) was last priced at USD 90.85/dmt, timestamped Nov 3, 17:49:40 (UTC+8). SMM’s daily review reported needs- based mill buying and moderate overall transactions.

Drivers Futures and most spot quotations pulled back after the month’s opening recovery, leaving the rebound dependent on persistent transactions. The seaborne 65% index fell more than the 61%, narrowing their spread from USD 16.00 to 15.70/dmt. Resource composition and buying schedules may influence relative pricing, but one session is insufficient to establish a lasting change in demand preferences.

Demand The latest 242-mill survey showed daily hot metal output of 2.3621 million mt on Oct 28, up 3,700 mt, with a blast furnace operating rate of 87.30%. Whether stronger baseline consumption generates discretionary restocking depends on mill stocks, finished-steel orders and margins. Environmental restrictions and maintenance may affect subsequent production, leaving actual transactions important to assessing buying intensity. SMM’s Nov 3 review cited projected blast furnace maintenance losses of 1.6983 million mt for Oct 31– Nov 6, down 57,900 mt from the preceding week. This is a whole-week maintenance estimate, not daily hot metal or realised additional output.

Inventory & Global shipments were 34.3119 million mt in the week to Oct 30, up 2.58% WoW, while arrivals in China fell supply

15.73% to 23.2663 million mt. The latest ten-port inventory was 104.0353 million mt on Oct 29, down 618,100 mt from Oct 22. Lower arrivals ease near-term incoming pressure, but stock absorption also depends on unloading and mill collections rather than one flow measure establishing broad supply tightness.
Cost & margin Nov 2 freight was USD 36.72/mt for C3, up 0.46 (+1.27%), and 14.38 for C5, down 0.56 (-3.75%). Lower port selling prices may squeeze some trading returns while transport costs diverge across routes. Contracts, exchange rates and settlement periods determine actual margins; import trading profitability should not be equated with steelmaking or coking earnings. SMM’s assessment published on the morning of Nov 3 put the period’s average import margin at 2.19 yuan/mt, up 2.15 yuan from 0.04; this import trading measure should not be treated as post-close mill profitability for the current session.

# Market Commentary · Domestic Ore

Prices The latest Domestic Ore Composite Price Index was 791.29 yuan/mt on Oct 30. SMM’s Nov 2 Shandong brief quoted 64% basic concentrate at 754 yuan/mt, down 3 yuan, on a dry-basis, pre-tax ex-mine quotation payable by acceptance bill. Grade and pricing terms distinguish this regional quotation from the national index.

Supply & SMM described mostly normal mine operations and good sales in Shandong, improved relative value for demand domestic concentrates and comparatively active mill purchases. These regional observations do not establish a nationwide demand recovery; resources, contracts and actual collections should be tracked separately.

Outlook SMM expects Shandong concentrate prices to remain range-bound near term. Persistent regional buying improvement could support prices, while continued pressure on mill margins could limit upside.
