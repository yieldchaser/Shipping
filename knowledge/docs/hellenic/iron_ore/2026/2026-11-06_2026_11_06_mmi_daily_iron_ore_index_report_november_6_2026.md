SMM's daily review described an initial futures rise followed by a retreat, subdued mill enquiries and spot trading, and inventory growth weighing on the rebound.

Drivers RMB futures and port spot prices diverged from USD seaborne quotations. The seaborne 61% and 65% composite indices rose 0.22% and 0.23%, while both corresponding port indices fell 2 yuan and the 58% held steady. Quotation timing, resource composition and procurement schedules may all contribute; differing pricing bases cannot be treated as directly realisable arbitrage returns. A one-day narrowing of port grade spreads also does not establish a structural shift in blending demand.

Demand The latest 242-mill survey showed daily hot metal output of 2.3573 million mt on Nov 4, down 4,800 mt from Oct 28, and a blast furnace operating rate of 87.06%, down 0.24 percentage point. The modest decline in hot metal suggests physical consumption has yet to provide incremental support. Mill orders, margins and sustained purchasing remain key; predominantly needs-based restocking could limit the scope for a price rebound.

Inventory & Ten-port stocks were 104.6789 million mt on Nov 5, up 643,600 mt from Oct 29. Fines were 81.4047 million mt supply (-413,500 mt), concentrates 8.2354 million mt (+705,000 mt), lump 13.3981 million mt (+382,900 mt) and pellets 1.6407 million mt (-30,800 mt). A total build alongside product divergence cannot be attributed entirely to demand. Latest shipment and arrival data cover the week to Oct 30: global shipments were 34.3119 million mt (+2.58%) and Chinese arrivals 23.2663 million mt (-15.73%). Voyage lags mean these series do not describe identical same-week supply changes.

Cost & margin Nov 5 C3 freight was USD 36.76/mt, up 2.57% from Nov 4, while C5 was 13.48, up 0.97%. Higher freight may raise some import transport costs, although contract pricing and settlement timing affect transmission. Rising seaborne quotations alongside lower port selling prices may narrow some trading spreads. Exchange rates, grades and tax bases still require separate calculation; this alone does not establish a change in steelmaking profitability.

Outlook Regional differences may persist in the near term. Tight local availability accompanied by mill purchases could support offers; limited improvement in margins and orders, alongside cautious procurement, could restrain gains.
