### Clarkson Hellas Ltd.

# Desk Talk

On the geopolitical front, Hormuz remains the main flashpoint. Tanker attacks continue almost daily, with Iran claiming more vessels hit mines this week, while US–Iran talks to reopen the strait have stalled, keeping shipping exposed to further disruption. Separately, Trump, Putin and Xi agreed at their first three- way summit in Shenzhen on 19 November to increase the production and supply of refined petroleum products. If followed through, higher refinery output would support product tanker demand, while the agreement could also pave the way for a further weakening of US sanctions on Russia, potentially reshaping trade flows and bringing part of the sanctioned fleet back into mainstream trading.

Tanker rates have continued to correct from their October highs. VLCC spot earnings fell 11.5% this week to around $270,000/day, almost 60% lower month on month, and Suezmaxes eased to about $153,000/day. Aframaxes were the exception, rising nearly 20% w/w to around $141,500/day on the back of a strong USG–Europe market. Product tankers also softened: LR2s slipped to $127,300/day and MRs dropped 30% w/w to $46,100/day.

On the wet S&P front, the recent correction in freight rates has not yet filtered through to asset values. Buyer appetite remains firm, particularly for tonnage positioned close to the AG and able to load promptly, and owners continue to receive competitive offers for such units. No recent transaction has yet confirmed a decline in values, but asking prices for crude tankers have begun to soften. Sellers are also showing greater willingness to divest older tonnage while values remain historically elevated, so the coming weeks will be key in showing whether the softer freight environment translates into a broader repricing of assets.

In dry bulk, Capesize rates eased 3% this week to $41,300/day, while Dalian iron ore prices fell to a four- year low on weaker Chinese data. China’s October pig iron output dropped 4.1% y/y and crude steel output 1.6%. Coal offered little support: although Chinese raw coal output fell 5.8% y/y, thermal power generation declined 7.3% and northern port inventories rose 2.8% w/w, which limits the case for higher imports.

In the dry S&P market, Far Eastern buyers are shifting their focus from early-2010s Chinese-built Supramaxes and Panamaxes towards older, circa-2006 Japanese-built units. Vintage Handysizes built between 2000 and 2005 are also attracting healthy interest from the same buyer group.

Overall, freight markets across both sectors have softened, but asset values have so far proved resilient. With geopolitical risks still elevated and year-end approaching, we expect the coming weeks to set the tone for both markets heading into 2027.

22.8m to Middle Eastern interests. Meanwhile, the TESS-58 Supramax **BAO SHENG** (ABT 58K DWT, 2011, TSUNEISHI ZHOUSHAN) was acquired at USD 20m by c/o ADNOC. In the Handysize sector, after inviting offers earlier this month, the **AFRICAN OWL** (ABT 34K DWT, 2016, NAMURA) has been committed to Turkish interests at USD 21.5m, whilst the OHBS **UBC SANTOS** (ABT 32K DWT, 2008, SAIKI) has been sold in the mid-USD 11m range. On the smaller side, the vintage sister vessels **EAST WIND I** (ABT 28K DWT, 2002, IMABARI) and **ABK TIGER** (ABT 28K DWT, 2002, IMABARI) have been sold at USD 5.85m and USD 5.3m, respectively.
