## LNG
The LNG market remained under pressure this week, with an abundance of prompt vessel availability in the Atlantic continuing to weigh on freight rates. Increased fixing activity and firmer requirements offered little support to rates, as ample tonnage allowed charterers to maintain aggressive bids.
On the BLNG1 Gladstone-Tokyo route, rates declined $2,800 week-on-week to settle at $50,600/day. Pacific rates remained comparatively supported to the West.
The BLNG2 Sabine-Isle of Grain route fell $8,900 to settle at $20,800/day, with the Atlantic remaining considerably oversupplied amid limited arbitrage opportunities and increased FOB cargo sales.
Similarly, the BLNG3 Sabine-Tokyo route declined $15,200 to close at $40,400/day, reflecting the continued weakness in the Atlantic and reduced demand for longer-haul voyages.
Period rates also softened, with the six-month assessment falling $1,900 to $42,300/day, the one-year assessment down $2,267 to $50,633/day, and the three-year assessment easing $700 to $70,300/day.

## LPG
The LPG market firmed this week, with a notable pick-up in activity in the US Gulf. Close to around 10 cargoes were fixed or worked for the upcoming month, steadily absorbing available tonnage and tightening the position list.
On the BLPG1 Ras Tanura-Chiba route, rates settled at $252.40/tonne, with TCE earnings closing at $246,826/day.
The BLPG2 Houston-Flushing route softened marginally, declining $0.67 week-on-week to settle at $173.83/tonne, with TCE earnings down $479 to $204,608/day.
Similarly, the BLPG3 Houston-Chiba route eased $0.43 to close at $310.43/tonne, while TCE returns fell $427 to settle at $189,690/day. Despite the slight decline, sentiment in the US Gulf has strengthened, with further activity expected.
