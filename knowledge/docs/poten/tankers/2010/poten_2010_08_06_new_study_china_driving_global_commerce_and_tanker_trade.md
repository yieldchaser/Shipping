## New Study: China: Driving Global Commerce and Tanker Trade
Last month, the International Energy Agency (IEA) announced that in 2009, China surpassed the United States to become the world's largest energy consumer. Measured in combined use of crude oil, nuclear, coal, natural gas and other energy sources, China consumed 2.25 billion tons of oil equivalent in 2009, 4% more than the United States. Rather than heralding the beginning of a new era in energy, the announcement merely confirmed what has been clear for some time; China's growth will be a central driver for the international economy in the years to come.
For the tanker market, China has been an increasingly important factor since the early 1990s, when domestic crude oil production began to lag behind the nation's explosive oil demand growth, as shown in the chart below. Going forward, China's ultimate effect on the tanker market will be determined by the interaction of its economic development, oil demand, refining capacity additions, as well its environmental initiatives and ongoing efforts to amass controlled tonnage. These and other factors surrounding the PRC's impact on the tanker market over the next decade will be addressed in Poten & Partners' upcoming multi‐client study, China: Driving Global Commerce and Tanker Trade.

## Net Imports
Any attempt to project China's development must grapple with its evolving role as both a dominant force in the international economy and a country with considerable domestic challenges. China has indicated that coming years will see it aim to address domestic economic inequality and the environmental consequences of it rapid growth. China is also likely to continue facing pressure to encourage increased domestic consumption as part of the "re‐ balancing" of the international economy called for by economists. This mixture of domestic and international factors will be important in determining the degree to which China's rapidly expanding refining capacity is used to meet domestic demand and how much of its production will find its way into export markets.
