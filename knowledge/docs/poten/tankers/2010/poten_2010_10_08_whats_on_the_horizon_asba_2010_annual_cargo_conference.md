## ASBA 2010 ANNUAL CARGO CONFERENCE
Last week industry experts gathered in Miami at the Eden Roc Hotel for the Annual ASBA Cargo Conference. This year's excellent panel of speakers were asked to confront the question of "What's on the Horizon?" Monday morning the conference opened with Jeanne Cardona, 'Executive Director of ASBA,' introducing the Tanker Panel. The topics discussed varied from the process of U.S Flag vessel recycling to the implication of Sanctions on Iran. Following this, the dry cargo panel provided a detailed and experienced insight into the alumina, bauxite and coal trades as well as the likes of James Russell-Walling of 'Eagle Bulk Shipping' and John Wobensmith of the newly formed publicly listed company 'Baltic Trading' presenting an owner's perspective of the market. This concluded a very engaging morning of lectures.
Special mention should, however, be directed towards Ken Applegate, 'Vice President of Transportation at Valero Energy Corporation' who highlighted the conference with an enlightened discussion on the hotly debated topic of the impact of carbon emission proposals on the maritime sector.
Shipping is not explicitly covered by the United Nation's Kyoto Protocol, thus the industry remains largely unregulated. The issue has been faced with mixed emotion and there have been calls to develop new international agreements on climate change for when the Kyoto Protocol expires in 2012. Therefore, Mr. Applegate's discussion was of particular relevance to "What's on the Horizon?" as despite the fact that the Congress has been unable to gain consensus to pass legislation, the US Environmental Protection Agency expanded its interpretation of the Clean Air Act (CAA) to extend to the regulation of Greenhouse Gas (GHG) emissions.
Mr. Applegate highlighted the numerous enforcement "tools" that the EPA has at its disposal including: renewable fuel standards, Corporate Average Fuel Economy (CAFE standards) and new source emission regulations. Due to mandated vehicle and fuel efficiency increases brought on by the modified CAFE standards demand for petroleum‐based transportation fuels could decrease by 2.4%. Consequently, this negative impact may also result in 1.8 - 2.4 million job losses not to mention potentially fewer required product imports.

## Total Fleet
The issue of carbon emission proposals is very much prevalent as even this week a similar proposal to cut carbon emissions from new ships was met with failure. Delegates of International Maritime Organization (IMO) Marine Environment Protection Committee met to discuss technical and operational measures aimed at reducing greenhouse gas emissions from ships, however, there was little consensus on the proposals. Perhaps, estimates that show how the world's shipping industry accounts for about 3% of total carbon emissions and transports more than 90% of global trade makes it harder for these proposals to gain traction.
ASBA President and Director Thomas Roberts concluded a very successful conference that provided an insightful cross section of the shipping industry. Mr. Roberts emphasized that even though the market is experiencing a lull in performance there was definitely no lack of enthusiasm from individuals within the industry as the conference experienced another record breaking attendance. The delegates were still not granted with a defining message as to what exactly is on the horizon but one must be positive that a tropical storm does not re emerge.
