## Capitalizing Together
Capitalizing Together 4 th Annual Capital Link Forum The buzz of finance in shipping returned to the Metropolitan Club in New York City this past Thursday as Capital Link hosted the 4th annual Invest in International Shipping forum. The single day event included a sea of shipowners, finance houses, bankers and market analysts all of whom contributed via panels and general market discussions.
Attendees listened intently as panelists from the capital markets, tankers and drybulk gave their respective take on the health of the shipping market and pressing issues for the year to come. Again, turnout for the forum was impressive as each of the panels were graced with full audiences eager to get the inside scoop from the titans of the industry.
Even as 2009 revealed a more melancholy shipping market for all around, forum attendees were looking forward to positive signs of 2010. As shown in the chart below, the Poten Tanker Index (PTI); a reflection of tanker market earnings from multiple sectors; and Shipping Equity Index provide a small but nevertheless brighter market than just six months ago.

## Chart: PTI vs Equity Index
The PTI is up 72 points or over 250% since the end of August 2009, following a brisk start to tanker markets in the beginning of the year, signaling signs of a recovering freight market.

## The Tanker Romp
The afternoon was a tanker filled romp with panelists reaching across both the crude oil and refined product tanker vessel sectors. Mr. Fotis Giannakoulis, VP of Research at Morgan Stanley moderated the crude oil tanker panel which consisted of Mr. Evangelos Marinakis, CEO, Crude Carriers Corp., Mr. Jeffrey Pribor, CFO, General Maritime Corporation, Mr. Mats Berglund, Senior VP & Head of Crude Transportation Strategic Business Unit - OSG, Mr. Peter Evensen, Executive VP, TK Corporation and Mr. Nikolas Tsakos, CEO, Tsakos Energy Navigation.
Mr. Giannakoulis gave opening remarks on capital raising activities, commenting on how successful each of the five panelists' companies had been over the past six months. Below are a few which were mentioned: o Growing crude volumes from West Africa and the North Sea to China have been driving ton‐mile demand growth for VLCCs.
o Arabian Gulf and West Africa to West Coast India is a growing trade as Indian refiners continue to add on new capacity.
o Peak oil is not in the equation as new and growing producers such as Brazil, Russia and the Caspian continue to support global crude oil demand.
o On ship financing, more capital is required up front for purchases, however many owners are raising funds thereby keeping debt to a minimum which has given them the ability to stay afloat in tough markets.
o Shipowners commented that they are going to continue to look for guidance from oil companies and traders on how to diversify and operate their fleets.
Following the crude oil panel, Ms. Megan McCurdy, Head of Marine Research & Consulting at Poten & Partners moderated the product tanker panel which consisted of Ms. Lois Zabrocky, Senior VP & Head of International Product Carrier Strategic Business Unit, Mr. Gregory McGrath, CFO, Omega Navigation and Mr. Jesper Bo Hansen, President, Torm - USA.
Ms. McCurdy presented a few slides to open up the panel, highlighting the tough Western refining economics of late, new trade development and the swath of new refineries and expansions on the horizon which are set to increase global capacity by 6.4 mbd by 2014. Below are a few interesting points brought up by the panelists: o While there have been some delays with new refinery capacity East of the Suez, Western refiners have, and will continue to face fierce competition from newer and lower cost refiners.
o The Trans‐Atlantic refined product trades have retained strength through the trough and continue to be relatively lucrative despite significant additions of new ship supply.
o Incremental refined product demand from the West may very well be met by export refineries based East of the Suez and will be moved on LR2s and LR1s creating tonne‐ mile demand growth.
o Far Eastern refineries are keeping up with domestic demand, however as automobile ownership increases and the global economy strengthens more and more barrels will need to be imported.

## Supply Story
In addition to speaking on behalf of their respective tanker trades, there was much discussion in regards to the elephant looming in the back of the room - newbuild orders. While last year's tanker panel focused primarily on long‐term charter coverage, this year focused quite a bit on supply as shipowners pointed out that there are still a vast amount of ships scheduled to be delivered in the coming years, especially as 2009 encountered "slippage" or delays in both the crude oil and product tanker sectors.
In 2009, orderbook delays ranged as high as 55% for the MR sector, however there were still over 100 deliveries, growing the fleet to nearly 1,300 ships. Shipowners on both panels expressed their concerns, yet unique and strategically timed chartering and operating techniques such as storage, slow steaming and even the occasional weather delay have helped to parlay the effects of new ship supply entering the market. Crafty operating methods coupled with the prospect of more cancellations or delays in the orderbook and even the occasional conversion from tanker order to drybulk order will surely help to keep a lid on the supply/demand imbalance.
The week of Connecticut Maritime Association's Shipping Conference and Capital Link's 'Invest in International Shipping' Conference always proves to be an exciting week and good opportunity to spend time with friends in the global shipping industry - book your calendars for March 2011!
