## Summary
Term Oil in the Market
This summer, deteriorating conditions in the spot tanker market have encouraged some vessel owners to seek protection in term charters. Although assessments for one‐year term charters softened during August, the momentum behind longer term charter rates strengthened over the summer. Through this reversal of the traditional relationship between short and long‐term charter deals, the term charter market has displayed a mixture of short‐term concern about future conditions and long‐term optimism among shipowners.

## Term Oil in the Market
This summer, deteriorating conditions in the spot tanker market have encouraged some vessel owners to seek protection in term charters. Although assessments for one‐year term charters softened during August, the momentum behind longer term charter rates strengthened over the summer. Through this reversal of the traditional relationship between short and long‐term charter deals, the term charter market has displayed a mixture of short‐term concern about future conditions and long‐term optimism among shipowners.
After a period of stagnation during which few vessel owners were willing to commit to longerterm deals and rates remained untested, assessments for three and five‐year term charter deals on VLCCs began a gradual improvement at the beginning of the year. Assessments for a five year term charter have improved from an average of $37,000 per day in April to $42,750 per day in August, as shown in the chart below.

## Long Term Focus
The total number of reported term charter deals of at least one year in duration across vessel sectors has increased in each of the last three months, as shown in the chart below. Though this year's activity levels in the term charter market to date remain below historical averages, the amount of term charter transactions completed between January and August of this year has remained essentially consistent with last year, suggesting a continuing but gradual recovery.
Within the recent uptick in activity, there have been long‐term charters reported at rates almost 20 percent higher than the last reported similar deal. This indicates that while the sense among some vessel owners that spot market rates will continue to struggle in the near‐term is gaining hold; those owners also see possible savings in committing to slightly higher rates for what may be a much healthier market in the outer years.

With developing world demand centers such as China and India continuing to provide crucial support for international oil markets and ton‐mile demand, it could take only a slightly‐stronger than expected uptick in U.S. demand to provide employment for some of the looming deliveries currently driving fear into the hearts of tanker owners. In addition, as freight rates and crude oil prices remain near recent lows while traders look ahead to winter heating oil demand, a widening contango adds the tantalizing possibility of an increase in floating storage during the fourth quarter.
Only time will tell whether the fourth quarter sees oil demand rise sufficiently to change the market's near‐term conditions, but the strengthening of long‐term term charter rates already witnessed signals a vote of confidence in the tanker market's prospects in the years beyond its immediate challenges.
