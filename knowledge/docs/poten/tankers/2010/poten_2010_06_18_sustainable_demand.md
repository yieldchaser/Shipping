## Sustainable Demand
The rally which has brought VLCC earnings on the Arabian Gulf - Far East voyage to a five month high has been driven by the continued strength in Eastern demand and improving sentiment across markets. Although short‐term tonnage supply in the Arabian Gulf is starting to catch up with demand, positive sentiment mounts for large ship sectors on a fundamental level.
The chart below shows that freight rates on the benchmark Ras Tanura - Chiba voyage have generally been on the rise since late May. Delays at Chinese ports were credited by some as helping to clear the final hurdle past the WS 100 mark for the first time since February, but a rush of fixtures in advance of a recent holiday break may have played a more important role in setting the stage for the improvement. In just the past two weeks, earnings have shot up dramatically, from about $41,800 per day on June 1 st to current earnings of about $78,900 per day, as shown in the graph below. This market pop is helping sustain some post‐Posidonia euphoria for several lucky shipowners.

## Rippling Effects
Inquiries for east‐bound fixing activity have reportedly been a bit quiet this week due to the Dragon Boat Festival in Taiwan and China. In advance of the holiday, however, an overnight surge in third‐decade June fixtures by Chinese charterers forced owners to recalculate their freight rate ideas. This recalculation kicked off a chain reaction that will likely continue to play out next week, as business presumably resumes at its normal pace.
With the spike in freight rates tempting some ships to hold out for higher‐earning east‐bound cargoes, the resulting scarcity of vessels looking to go West lent some support to Arabian Gulf - US Gulf freight rates, which rose moderately this week. The impact was also felt in West Africa, where freight rates for voyages to the Far East gained 10 WS points this week. Continued demand for voyages headed to India helped magnify this effect, which was notable in that it occurred despite stagnation in Suezmax rates.

## Further on the Road
While the rhythms of the Chinese market may have provided the immediate push, an improving demand picture in Western markets has also contributed to recent strengthening in rates. An increase in gasoline demand reported by the Energy Information Administration earlier this week supported the positive sentiment about demand recovery. The cargo count for June has reached 106, the highest since December 2008, suggesting that more oil is indeed flowing into the system. What's more, the Western share of those cargoes is in line with recent performance, suggesting the beginnings of a sustained recovery.

## Looking Ahead?
It is interesting to note rumblings that charterers already actively pursuing tonnage with fixing dates later than mid‐July have played a role in driving recent activity levels. This suggests a sentiment, at least among some, that recent strength in earnings will continue. Let's hope that the optimists are right and the recent freight rate strengthening is a reflection of what the world really needs - economic recovery.
