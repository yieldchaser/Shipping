## In Like A Tiger...
In the opening of 2010, the freight market has acted like a tiger as the freight rates have surged across the board. Just a year ago, market fundamentals seemed far bleaker while tanker freight rates were hanging on, waiting for the proverbial "other shoe to drop." A year ago, Poten & Partners conducted an open survey on the tanker and oil markets ( In the Year of the Ox , January 23, 2009) to gather popular opinions on the upcoming year. At that time, expectations of mergers, acquisitions, and volatility were common themes. In practice, 2009 somehow seemed to fall short on those fronts. Read on to see how clairvoyant you were:

## Economics and Banking
The vast majority of replies to the survey believed that a recovery in the US economy would take a year or more, with 46% believing economic improvement would take a year and 32% thinking it would take at least two. Economic improvement can hinge on many factors, but by using the Dow Jones Industrial average as one initial indicator of confidence there has been some improvement. Since January 2009, when the closing high was around 9,100 points, there has been an improvement of roughly 11% ‐ good news considering Jan‐08 to Jan‐09 was down by over 30%.
When asked about the next investment bank failure, respondents had little reason to expect governments to intervene to the extent they did. The Obama Administration stepped in and less than one month later, on February 17 th , 2009 a $787 billion package was put into place to help revive the economy - thus helping to put a stop to any explicit major investment bank failures. However, as shown in the chart below, failures have still been significant in the smaller banking sectors, averaging 11 per month in 2009 and 9 in January 2010 alone.

## Crude Oil Demand and Price
When polled about expectations on whether crude oil demand would remain flat, increase or decrease, respondents were evenly divided amongst the three. Based on the latest market report, the year‐on‐year increase in first quarter 2010 worldwide crude oil consumption is estimated to be 1.65 million barrels per day, or about 2%, according to the IEA.
The crude oil price outlook remained less clear. At the time of the survey, the oil price was around $50 per barrel. When asked whether price would hit $15 per barrel, $100 per barrel or $200 per barrel, most people (60%) replied none of the above. However, 34% expected that prices would hit $100 per barrel. The chart below shows that crude oil prices have climbed throughout 2009, but did not hit the $100 per barrel mark; prices topped around $80 per barrel.

## Major Mergers and Freight Rates

## 73% Answered
When asked about whether the first merger would take place amongst tanker companies or major oil companies, 66% of people expected tanker companies to converge. Although many expected consolidation in 2009, the market did not produce any major mergers.

## Asset Prices and Newbuildings
The relative values in the asset markets have continued to leave many waiting for the flurry of activity on the back of strained financial conditions within companies. While the secondhand sale and purchase market seems to have seen its share of transactions, the newbuilding market has continued to remain quiet, with the notable exception of a few national oil company orders having been placed. Last year, based on 62% of replies, the survey suggested that the VLCC market would see between 10 - 20 newbuilding orders. The large order placed at a South Korean yard was reportedly for 12 vessels, putting it right in the expected range.
Although the market was largely illiquid, newbuilding VLCC prices were assessed at an average of $102.5 million in December 2009, in line with expectations that prices would be within $100 ‐ $124 million (56% of replies). This reflects a decrease in asset price of around 40% for the year.

## ...Out like a lamb?
With some of the expected events having yet‐to‐materialize by the end of 2009, does that mean 2010 is poised for some shakeups? Perhaps the short‐term upward price pressure on assets will convince the market that the bottom has passed, spurring some interest in shipping assets, or companies on the whole, and also create some opportunities for mergers, acquisitions or newbuilding orders. The challenge still seems to be the lack of available credit to facilitate transactions and the realization that banks still need to honestly evaluate their exposure. Until then, expect the market to coast without any material structural changes.
