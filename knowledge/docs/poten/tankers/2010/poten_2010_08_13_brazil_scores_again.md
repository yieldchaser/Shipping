## Brazil Scores Again
Brazil's rapidly‐expanding economy has earned it a place among the developing world's economic powerhouses, but it is the country's burgeoning status as a crude oil exporter which has enhanced its importance to the tanker industry. Recent years of offshore crude oil discoveries could put Brazil among the world's largest oil producers while long‐term supply contracts with overseas end users make it a significant source of future ton‐mile demand.
With population growth which has moderated as its economy has continued to develop rapidly, Brazil managed to stabilize the energy intensity of its economy in recent years, as seen in the chart below. This has left Brazil in a good position to expand its role as an energy exporter while its economy continues to develop in the years ahead. Having successfully met its goal of self‐ sufficiency in crude oil as of 2009, the methodical allocation of its existing and future resources will be the key to Brazil's continued success.

## GDP Per Capita ($USD)
Brazil's effort to expand its domestic oil production through the exploration of its offshore resources began decades ago and is starting to make a contribution to its overall energy position. The EIA estimates Brazil's reserves at 12.6 billion barrels, with the majority of supplies located in deep water off its southeastern coast. Apart from producing supplies which will eventually enter international markets, development of these offshore resources has helped turn Brazil into an important market for shuttle tankers.
The most important of Brazil's recent offshore discoveries, the Tupi field located in the offshore Campos Basin, is the largest Western Hemisphere oil discovery in 30 years. Discovered in 2006, Tupi is expected to contain as much as 8 billion barrels of recoverable reserves. In addition, the Jupiter and Cariocoa fields in the region have helped make Brazil an important contributor to growth in non‐OPEC oil production expected in the years to come.
While the country once imported about 80% of its crude oil requirements, Brazil became a net exporter of oil in 2009, when its crude oil production reached an average of just under 2.5 mb/d. With several projects which have come on‐stream in recent years building towards maximum production, the International Energy Agency sees the trend of increasing production continuing this year, with production expected to reach 2.67 mb/d. Its use of hydroelectric power for electricity generation has allowed it to dedicate more of these supplies for export.
While Brazil has long exported its crude oil, its status as a net crude oil exporter is only just starting to be reflected in reported dirty fixture volumes. As shown in the chart below, there have been fifty‐six reported Suezmax fixtures from Brazil so far in 2010, surpassing 2009's total and best reflecting how the upward trend in crude oil exports from Brazil will impact the tanker market. The eight reported VLCC fixtures from Brazil this year, on track to surpass the 12 reported in 2009, also suggest Brazil's potential as a significant source of ton‐mile demand. Not surprisingly, voyages to the U.S. Gulf on Suezmaxes have been the most frequently reported fixtures from Brazil, but as use of its crude oil expands along with production, voyages to more distant markets are expected to take on an increased role in the country's export mix.
