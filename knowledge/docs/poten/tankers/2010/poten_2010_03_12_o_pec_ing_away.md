## O PEC - ing Away
When the Organization of Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC) meets next week in Vienna, they will help formulate a consensus about the future of global economic recovery. Recent weeks have brought some varied, but generally positive signals of economic recovery in the US while crude oil prices have remained near the range identified as "fair" by some of OPEC's most influential voices. OPEC raised its 2010 demand projection earlier this week and despite assurances from some of the group's oil ministers that production will remain unchanged, some speculation that rates may indeed change always remains an undercurrent on the eve of an OPEC meeting.
Responsible for about 40 percent of the world's crude oil, OPEC has kept levels steady since cutting production dramatically last year in an attempt to stabilize ailing crude oil markets. As shown in the chart below, OPEC production fell from almost 32 million barrels per day in July 2008 to 28.9 million barrels per day in January 2009. OPEC now expects demand for its crude oils to average 28.94 million barrels per day this year, a 190,000 barrel per day increase over the group's previous estimate.

## Greater Expectations
OPEC expects worldwide demand to grow by 1.1% this year, reaching 86.6 million barrels per day by the fourth quarter to average 85.24 million barrels per day for the year, as shown in the chart below. Repeating what has become a recurring mantra among forecasters of oil demand, OPEC highlighted that this demand growth is expected to be the result of expanding consumption in countries outside the OECD, especially India and China. The International Energy Agency (IEA) similarly trumpeted oil demand growth in non‐OECD countries in raising its own projection of 2010 worldwide demand to 86.6 million barrels per day in a recent report. Although OPEC cautioned that even current levels of production are likely in excess of market demand, its upward demand revision joins a number of indications that economic conditions are improving.
1Q 2010 2Q2010 3Q2010 4Q2010 2010 Average Some positive news about the US economy has led to increased optimism that the United States will experience stronger GDP growth this year. These indicators have included strengthened activity in stock and commodities markets as well as a slowdown in the rate of job losses. In late February, the Commerce Department raised its estimate of GDP growth in the fourth quarter of 2009 to nearly 6% and just this week it reported an unexpected 0.3% rise in retail sales (excluding autos, gasoline and building materials).

## Compliance: The Key
Despite these inklings of economic recovery, one reason why OPEC may choose not to make any changes to stated production levels next week is the fact that some of its members are already exceeding the limits already in place. After maintaining historically high levels of compliance to production quotas introduced last year, recent months have seen this compliance slip. OPEC production, excluding Iraq, exceeded the target production of 24.8 million barrels per day by almost two million barrels per day last month, according to the group's recent monthly report.
With the cartel's members already responding to the signals of demand recovery and the specter of increasing future Iraqi production hanging over the group, OPEC ministers will have plenty to discuss next week. While it is true that OPEC's production quotas may ultimately determine the export volumes which propel tanker markets, indications that demand is not only stabilizing but also growing will provide physical and psychological support for the worldwide tanker market.
