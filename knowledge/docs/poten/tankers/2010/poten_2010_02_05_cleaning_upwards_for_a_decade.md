## Summary
Cleaning Upwards for a Decade
At the turn of the millennium, India's crude oil refinery capacity stood at just over 1 mbd, and was a net importer of clean petroleum products. Now, following a decade of aggressive growth, India is seated within the top five countries in terms of refinery capacity and is a net exporter of refined petroleum products. ## Cleaning Upwards for a Decade
At the turn of the millennium, India's crude oil refinery capacity stood at just over 1 mbd, and was a net importer of clean petroleum products. Now, following a decade of aggressive growth, India is seated within the top five countries in terms of refinery capacity and is a net exporter of refined petroleum products.
Refining giant Reliance Industries is one of the driving forces behind this remarkable transformation, currently accountable for roughly 1.2 mbd or 42% of India's capacity. As shown in the table below, the top ten refiners account for just over 50% of worldwide refining capacity, with India responsible for 3% of the total. The US continues to dominate overall at 17.8 mbd or 20% of global capacity, however due to poor economic conditions some of its facilities were either idled or permanently taken offline in 2009. Closures of refining capacity down the road could mean more barrels will have to be imported, and India is a likely candidate in terms of ability to meet stringent US product specifications.

## Sourcing the Inputs
Concurrent with the run up in refinery capacity, crude oil movements to India have grown by leaps and bounds. Based on reported spot fixture activity, there were 92 more VLCC crude oil cargoes discharged in India in 2009 versus the amount seen in 2000. This increase of 85% over the ten‐year span has added demand for 7 VLCCs dedicated to Indian supply which equated to demand for roughly 16 VLCCs in 2009.

## More Ton‐miles on the Horizon?
If the crude oil continues to be sourced from the same regions as 2009, an increase of 1.3 mbd in India's refining capacity would create an incremental increase of roughly 40% or 7 more VLCCs by 2013. However, if the load ratios change over the next four years such that West Africa and the Caribbean offset cargoes from the Arabian Gulf, the incremental demand for VLCCs could have more striking growth.
The impact of increases in refining capacity in the Asia‐Pacific region is certainly shaking things up among the traditional Western refining community and created a great deal of uncertainty about the forward development of trade patterns. Pending the continued growth of Indian refiners, shipowners could see increases in ton‐mileage bound for India carrying crude oil in addition to the increased chartering of LR2s and LR1s out of India carrying product to both Eastern and Western markets.
