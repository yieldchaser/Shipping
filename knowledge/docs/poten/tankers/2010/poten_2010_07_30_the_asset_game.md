## The Asset Game
One can't help but notice a disconnect between slipping freight rates, weak demand fundamentals and the surge of newbuilding tanker orders seen in recent months. Asset values are derived from the expected earning of that asset over its life, but with the tanker market sending mixed signals on the pace of recovery, other forces are ostensibly in play. Recent months have seen many new orders placed at shipyards, particularly in the large crude oil tanker space. When it comes to decision making about where to place the big bucks, what has gotten into the shipowners' psyche?

## VLCC Newbuilding Price
AG ‐ FE (260kt) Over the past two years, a lack of liquidity in both financing ability and the amount of deal flow left a tanker asset market that was largely untested. Not surprisingly, the credit crunch was largely responsible for this turn of events. Since March of 2010, however, the perception that there were assets to be had on the cheap has helped bring tankers back into fashion. Interestingly, it has mostly come in the form of newbuilding activity rather than secondhand purchases of existing assets. Since the start of the year, 25 VLCCs have been reportedly ordered along with more than 35 Suezmaxes. The secondhand market in these sectors has not been quiet either, with 19 and 15 transactions, respectively.

## Perception & Reality
For better or worse, expectations, momentum, and emotion are factors that can significantly impact shipowners' decision making. Although more intangible than raw materials costs, these perceived drivers can have a substantial impact and can trigger a run‐up in prices quite easily.
Companies often face pressures from investors and stakeholders to employ capital when it is granted. Those that have raised money (either through debt or equity) or been given clearance from various Boards of Directors are perhaps more likely to pull the trigger sooner than later. Once volume and momentum are back in the game, so returns the asset play opportunity. The more deals that happen, the more opportunity players have to make money; and so the cycle continues.
Some of those jumping back into the "Asset Game" believe that the bottom of the cycle has come. Other potential buyers remain on the sidelines, presumably waiting for another dip in prices. With the recent surge in activity having kicked off the Game's latest round, it is likely that they will be watching more than just the spot market in deciding when to make their move.
