## VLCC ton-mile demand continues to show healthy growth
Global VLCC trades are dominated by two regions: exports are primarily coming from the countries around the Arabian Gulf and the destination of most of the worlds' crude oil is Asia. The most recent movement data from Lloyd's List Intelligence confirms this once again. VLCC ton-mile demand in 2016 was a solid 6.6% higher than in 2015, driven largely by a 10% increase in exports from the Middle East.
The total number of VLCC voyages in 2016 was 3,989, an increase of 296 voyages (8%) over 2015. About 77% of the voyages last year originated in the Middle East and more than 95% of the net growth in VLCC traffic in 2016 originated in this region. A closer look at the increase in VLCC movements from 2015 to 2016 shows that several long-term trends are continuing: the Middle East represents the vast majority of the extra crude exports (287 out of 296 voyages) and, not surprisingly, most of this additional crude oil (about 65%) ends up in Asia (China, India, Singapore, Japan).
Outside of Asia, there are two other destinations that show significant increases: the Middle East and the U.S. Gulf. Intra- Middle Eastern voyages, mainly from the Arabian Gulf to the Red Sea are up 18% (+41 voyages) as Saudi Arabia moves more crude from its main oil fields in the east of the country to its refineries on the west coast (primarily Yanbu). Unfortunately for shipowners, these are short-haul voyages and add little to global ton-mile demand. This is not the case for the other growth area: voyages from the Middle East to the U.S. Gulf. These are long-haul voyages and increases on this route provide significant support to the VLCC market.
It is not all positive for the VLCC trades. A few major routes experienced a decline in traffic last year. As India sourced more crude oil from the Middle East, it reduced its purchases from West Africa, which is a net-negative from a ton-mile perspective. This shift in purchases by India may have been the result (at least partially) of production problems in Nigeria. Another negative was the decline in VLCC exports from Latin America, in particular the reduction in movements from storage terminals in the Caribbean to Asia.
An interesting development in 2016 was the emergence of VLCCs involved in crude oil exports from the United States. After the crude oil export ban was lifted, the U.S. has started to export crude oil to destinations in Europe, Latin America and Asia. The short-to-medium haul destinations are mostly served by Aframax and Suezmax tonnage, but to be competitive in long-haul destinations in Asia, charterers need to use VLCCs. In 2016, six cargoes of U.S. crude oil were loaded on VLCCs for shipment to Asia. Since the United States currently lacks the port infrastructure to accommodate VLCCs, these vessels were loaded by reverse lightering from Aframaxes. In the first quarter of 2017, another six vessels have already been loaded with U.S. crude, a clear indicator of the increased use of VLCCs in this trade.
As mentioned before, VLCCs discharge disproportionately in Asia. Imports into Asia/Australia represented 77% of total ton-mile demand in 2016, down slightly from 80% of the total in 2015. This small reduction is largely due to the partial shift of crude oil exports originating in the Caribbean and West Africa to the Middle East, lowering average distances.
Compared to Asia, the VLCC movements into the U.S., Europe and the rest of the world are fairly small. This ratio is not expected to change. If anything, the balance will probably tip further towards Asia.
All this growth in ton-mile demand has not translated into higher rates for VLCCs in 2016. On the contrary; VLCC rates were significantly lower in 2016 than the year before, despite higher vessel demand. This was primarily a result of increased newbuilding deliveries, which led to higher tonnage availability.
