## Oil market fundamentals
The recent Oil Market Report (OMR) from the International Energy Agency (IEA) was unremarkable in its lack of revisions to its forecasts. The agency kept both oil demand growth estimates and non-OPEC supply forecasts roughly unchanged, while providing small baseline adjustments to demand. Beneath this forecasting calm, however, the agency highlighted the conflicting forces that are leading to greater oil market uncertainty. Increasing tension between ample crude and tighter product stocks, tepid oil demand and accelerating security concerns in the Middle East are all contributing to the confusion. Rising refining margins are encouraging higher-than-expected crude runs, potentially buoying tanker demand, but supply disruptions and demand destruction remain a medium-term concern.

## PADD3 crude stocks
back to normal The IEA, in its assessment of global inventory positions, cited a weak seasonal build of OECD industry oil stocks of only 10.6 MB in July and a counter-seasonal draw of 21.8 MB in August, led by the 23.7 MB drop in crude stocks. The US crude situation drove much of this activity, and some of the counter-seasonal effect was from the timing of Isaac in August, when statistically, September is the peak month for GoM production losses, given the higher incidence of hurricanes and major hurricanes (Category 3 or greater) during the month.
Alternatively, the agency did point out that the OECD inventory cover looks more comfortable on a forward demand basis, given the diminishing prospects for global oil demand growth. Total OECD stock cover was 58.3 days in July, or 0.6 days above a year ago. Nevertheless, refiners need to eliminate the dichotomy between ample crude stocks and tight product inventories through higher crude runs. Market signals are functioning properly, as higher product prices and refining margins are encouraging this. As a result, the IEA has lifted its forecast for 3q12 global crude runs by 210 kbpd from its previous report. The agency now estimates global runs at 75.7 mbpd in 3q12, or 450 kbpd higher yoy, and suggests that refiners will maintain that rate in 4q12, as shown in the following chart.

## Global Crude Runs
This would imply higher-than-forecast crude runs in Europe, once refiners complete Autumn maintenance and a slower seasonal decline in US crude runs. Much of the anticipated US destocking that would have suppressed crude tanker demand in 4q12 occurred in 3q12, bringing forward the freight rate pain so evident this summer. With crude stocks at normal levels in PADD3, any incremental crude runs to meet distillate shortages in the Atlantic Basin would slow the decline in US crude imports. Still, although the US crude stock situation has improved, recovering domestic crude production and crude run and inventory seasonals, suggest limited upside in crude imports for the remainder of the year.
Meanwhile, other uncertainties surround the oil markets and tanker sectors. Planned and unplanned production outages continue to hamper the oil supply chain, but the recent acceleration of Islamist anti-US and anti-Western violence in North Africa, the Middle East and Asia is worrying. When juxtaposed to the euphoric release of animal spirits in the commodity and capital markets following the US Federal Reserve's announcement of a third round of quantitative easing, the current market risk/reward assessment seems surreal. Numerous market commentators suggest that the previous Fed round of quantitative easing flooded the commodity markets with liquidity that sparked food price spikes, leading to social unrest and the Arab Spring that toppled governments. To have this level of social unrest and anti-Western violence in the developing world -- before commodity trading desks can work their odd magic on grain markets struggling with drought conditions and low stocks, and with oil markets facing supply and refining constraints -- is sobering. Central banks are flooding the markets with additional monetary largesse to force investors into risk assets. This deliberate and fundamental mispricing of risk did not serve the global economy well in 2008, and may be providing a repeat performance.
