## Summary
growth forced market
Facing rapid fleet growth and limited organic volume from its traditional load regions, Suezmaxes have had to fight for market share from other dirty vessel classes. Continued deliveries of Suezmaxes have driven 9% growth in the fleet this year, overwhelming tepid demand growth in its traditional load regions of West Africa, the Black Sea and the Med. Instead, Suezmax fixtures have cropped up in atypical load regions - with higher AG liftings and a surge in Caribs-East cargoes providing the tonne- mile impetus to keep sector utilisations from crashing any lower than they have.

80% forcing earnings below operating costs 135 BS-Med 130 WAFR-USAC 42 Suezmax deliveries this year driving 9.3% yoy fleet growth VLCC fleet growth is moving away from its 8% growth pace. Although the pace of VLCC fleet growth has punished earnings, VLCCs have enjoyed an organic demand benefit from increased AG production and from Asian crude import sourcing trends. The rising supply of light/sweet crude in the Atlantic Basin has tightened the Brent-Dubai crude price spread, which has encouraged Asian refiners to lift additional volumes of West African and other Brent-linked Atlantic Basin crudes, typically on VLCCs. Moreover, the continued demand from other Asian refiners for heavier crudes, in order to support growing upgrading capacity, has supported VLCC demand for Caribs, Mexican and Brazilian stems.

## Higher Saudi and Iraqi
volumes displacing Iranian flows to Europe Meanwhile, the rise in Suezmax Caribs-East volumes may look tiny in comparison, but the tonne-mile effect has been substantial. The rise of this trade, as well the growing AG volumes, contributed to an estimated 10% yoy jump in Suezmax tonne-mile demand during 1h12, helping to absorb the surge in fleet growth. Of course, this was part of the overall 7% rise in dirty tonne-miles during the same period, benefitting from a 1.5 mbpd global inventory build during 1h12 on anxiety over Iran. With uncertain demand fundamentals and high crude stocks, the dirty tanker market is suffering from a normalisation in trade and sustained fleet growth. Continued Suezmax deliveries into 2013 suggest more market share competition with other sectors.
