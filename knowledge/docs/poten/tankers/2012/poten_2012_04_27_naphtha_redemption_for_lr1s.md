## Summary
After languishing below operating costs for almost six months, LR1 earnings on the benchmark Baltic TC5 route, carrying 55,000 MT of naphtha AG-Japan, have more than doubled during the past two weeks, to above $13,000/day. The LR1 sector has suffered recently from brisk fleet growth, rising by 7.9% during 2011; slowing Asian industrial production growth, limiting petrochemical naphtha demand; competition with MRs for new Indian refinery naphtha production and elusive arbitrages. After collapsing to $1,000/day in October 2011, earnings on the TC5 route recovered slightly, but averaged only $5,200/day for 1q12.

## Naphtha Redemption for LR1s?
After languishing below operating costs for almost six months, LR1 earnings on the benchmark Baltic TC5 route, carrying 55,000 MT of naphtha AG-Japan, have more than doubled during the past two weeks, to above $13,000/day. The LR1 sector has suffered recently from brisk fleet growth, rising by 7.9% during 2011; slowing Asian industrial production growth, limiting petrochemical naphtha demand; competition with MRs for new Indian refinery naphtha production and elusive arbitrages. After collapsing to $1,000/day in October 2011, earnings on the TC5 route recovered slightly, but averaged only $5,200/day for 1q12. This performance has prompted growing disenchantment with the sector, with expectations for 2012 sliding steadily, as demonstrated by the decline in implied TCEs from the TC5 contract for 2q12.

## LR1 & LR2 Spot TCE Earnings
55 AG-Japan TC5 FFA 2q12 The fortunes for LR1 reversed dramatically in early-April with a surge in naphtha fixtures from Asian ethylene producers, as 2.4 mtpa of naphtha cracker capacity in Japan and Korea came back on line during April, following Spring maintenance. This pick up in Asian activity out of the AG soaked up available supply, and as the Med-Japan naphtha arbitrage opened up modestly, traders secured additional tonnage for this long-haul route, further reducing AG availability.
Although the burst of tonnage supply in 2011 and weaker fundamentals has produced a painful market environment for owners, the forward outlook for LR1s was never as dire as spot conditions suggested. After averaging 36 newbuilding deliveries per year during 2006-10, the year 2011 was the last exaggerated delivery year, with 25 vessels, before the LR1 orderbook shrank to 21 vessels expected for 2012 and only 12 units in 2013. With depressed rates encouraging demolition and suppressing ordering urges, typical slippage would suggest that LR1 net fleet growth could slow to 2.4% year-over-year for 2012.
With LR1 supply finally slowing, any meaningful improvement in tonnage demand could prompt a recovery. Although the naphtha trades have been critical to LR demand, other regional product imbalances, particularly in mid-distillates, could drive LR1 demand during the next several years. Continued closures of uneconomic refining capacity in Europe would further widen the regional structural diesel deficit from 981 kbpd in 2011, to approximately 1.2 mbpd by 2016. Improving refinery complexity in Russia, Europe's main diesel supplier, as well as AG and Indian refinery capacity growth, would support jet and gasoil LR1 trades.

## Chart: vs Dubai Crude
Although the continued growth of Chinese petrochemical demand would support continued naphtha demand, tempered by the rise in domestic refinery capacity, the near-term naphtha outlook is still clouded by the forward prospects for Asian industrial production. Singapore naphtha cracks versus Dubai crude are still languishing near minus $5/bbl, despite the surge in recent cargo buying. Margins collapsed in November, as a diesel-driven refining environment produced a surplus of naphtha to market requirements, but this downdraft in crack spreads also occurred as Asian industrial production began to show clear signs of slowing. In November 2011, both Chinese purchasing managers indices (PMI) -- the official index by the China Federation of Logistics and Purchasing for the National Bureau of Statistics and the private HSBC index -- slipped below 50, a level indicating manufacturing contraction. The official index has since rebounded to 53.1 in March, indicating expansion, but the HSBC index has languished below 50, rising slightly to 49.1 in April. Although the HSBC index features a statistical sampling of smaller firms than the official indices, the gap is the largest since early-2009. Given the importance of resumed Chinese growth to the global economy and shipping, this sort of divergence is unsettling, particularly in light of recessionary signals in Europe and signs of slowing in the U.S.
