## After the Stock Build
In this week's Petroleum Status Report, the US Energy Intelligence Agency (EIA) reported that US commercial crude inventories rose a higher-than-expected 3.7 MB last week, to 379.5 MB, the highest level since 1990. Crude oil prices declined on the news, also confronted by electoral uncertainty and recessionary conditions in Europe and weaker Chinese economic statistics. US crude stocks have jumped by 14.3 MB, or 511 kbpd, during the past four weeks, supported by a 3.5 MB rise in Cushing stocks, still awaiting an outlet in the Seaway pipeline reversal that starts on May 17th. Still, US crude inventories excluding Cushing, at 335.4 MB, are near previous peaks in the weekly data, as shown in the chart below.

## Total US
US excl Cushing Source: EIA The inventory builds should not be surprising. Saudi Arabia has led a sharp rise in OPEC production, to 31.62 mbpd in April, which is prompting global stock building during the seasonal slide in crude runs, given the uncertainties surrounding the Iranian sanctions. Recent fixture data and EIA statistics have reflected the surge in westbound shipments from the Arabian Gulf (AG), with Saudi exports to the US jumping to 1.6 mbpd during the past four weeks and imports from the AG hitting 2.4 mbpd.
Despite this AG crude influx, overall crude import volumes have not risen excessively, as average crude imports of 8.7 mbpd during the first four months of 2012 are virtually unchanged from 2011, suggesting a shift in the crude slate that also supports a build for the Motiva expansion. Moreover, last week marked the seasonal peak in crude stocks, as inventories typically rise by 364 kbpd during the four weeks leading to early-May, before declining during the summer on higher crude runs.
In fact, with US crude runs during the first four months of 2012 running at 14.52 mbpd, or 283 kbpd higher year-over-year (yoy), the primary driver for expanding crude stocks has been higher US crude oil production, driven by the dramatic growth in shale oil output. During the first four months of 2012, crude production has averaged 6.05 mbpd, or 510 kbpd higher yoy, leading to crude supply of 14.76 mbpd, against crude inputs of 14.52 mbpd during the period.
