## BRIC House
The pressure to diversify crude oil sources continues to mount for purchasers of Iranian crude oil. Today, buyers not only risk economic marginalization by the United States and Europe, but also physical supply disruptions as rising tensions call into question the viability of supplies. To that end, India is forging relationships with other producers of heavy crude oil. Exporters such as Mexico and Brazil provide politically palatable alternative sources. While these two countries already send barrels to India, incremental exports can be expected as India is weaned off of Iranian supplies.

## Gulf,
The Arabian Gulf will continue to be the dominant crude oil supplier to India; however the growing reliance on West African, Mexican, Venezuelan and Brazilian imports will present ways for shipowners to improve vessel utilization. VLCCs that engage in the Arabian Gulf - West trade can minimize the overall ballast time by picking up a nearby second cargo in one of these Atlantic export locations.

## Shuffling Supplies
The crude oil supply and demand dynamics in Brazil in particular present a unique opportunity to shipowners. Brazil takes a pragmatic approach to crude oil marketing by selling domestic grades as refining margins or international prices warrant. With this approach, Brazil often ends up importing lighter, sweeter grades from places like the Arabian Gulf or West Africa and exporting domestically produced heavy, sour barrels.
As India looks to replace heavy, sour Iranian grades, its relationship with Brazil will likely strengthen. Back haul opportunities will increasingly emerge to the benefit of VLCC and Suezmax owners alike. Although the net effect on ton-mile demand could be mitigated through improved vessel utilization, an offset could arise as Iranian barrels are forced into markets farther afield.
