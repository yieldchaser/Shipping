## Summary
2013 by 1.07 mbpd
In addition to cutting demand estimates in its recent Oil Market Report, the International Energy Agency (IEA) sliced its forecasts for OPEC production capacity during 2013 by 1.07 mbpd since its December 2011 report. Baseline revisions and cuts in Iranian capacity, due to tighter sanctions, featured in the estimate reduction, which leaves AG capacity on a much slower trajectory. Combined with new refineries coming on line in the region, the new estimates imply a much lower level of crude export capacity available for VLCCs.

## Iranian sanctions cutting
exports and capacity estimates This has left the AG region with only a 0.12 mbpd gain in capacity for 2013, as Iraqi and UAE capacity gains and the start-up of the Saudi Manifa field in late-2013 just manage to outpace Iranian losses. Estimates based upon the IEA's December update forecasts suggest that the AG could only see 0.71 of capacity growth during 2013-16, while total OPEC sustainable production capacity could rise by 1.78 mbpd during the four-year period. With three Saudi Aramco refineries with a total of 1.2 mbpd of distillation capacity potentially coming on line during the period, and with the UAE's 417 kbpd Ruwais project progressing, this level of AG production capacity growth would imply a 0.9 mbpd reduction in AG crude export capacity during the period.

## OPEC spare capacity
rises as cartel backs away from stock builds Slower development of non-OPEC crude projects or stronger-than-expected oil demand growth could push the call on OPEC crude higher, boosting oil tanker demand, but would also cut into OPEC spare capacity, bringing it back towards 3%. This pattern of constrained and then relaxed commodity supply chains is providing a useful price rationing process for the global economy, but an oversupplied dirty tanker sector still remains caught the middle.
