## Summary
A more optimistic tanker
Like tanker company earnings reports, the Poten Weekly Tanker Opinion has made for grim reading in recent weeks, with discussions of flagging IEA demand outlooks, rising US crude production driving sharp cuts in crude imports and the prospects of scrapping 15-year old tankers. The relentlessly-negative tone is due, not so much to the dire current earnings environment, but to the uncertain global macroeconomic outlook. Dirty tanker supply should slow meaningfully during 2013, and provide a powerful underpinning to the next tanker market recovery, but until the global economy rebounds and supports strong oil demand growth and crude runs, near-term tanker prospects will remain mired in low utilisations and earnings.

## Chinese demand and
European recovery needed This demand growth is critical, given the utilisation hole that the dirty tanker fleet has found itself. As shown in the chart on the following page, dirty tanker utilisation has plunged by 13-14 percentage points since market highs in 2008. The collapse emerged in two stages. The first downdraft came in 2009, from the recessionary 7% plunge in dwt demand and a 4% rise in the operating fleet, limited by floating storage. Following a small rebound in utilisations in 2010, fleet growth hit 8% in 2011, so that a slight decline in dwt demand provided the next leg down in dirty tanker utilisations.

## Dirty Tanker Fleet Utilisation, Pct
operating at low utilisations, plentiful supply keeps rates and volatilities low, but more important, a large rise in utilisations is required to bring the market back to a level of tightness that encourages charterer competitiveness.
Seasonal demand should provide some near-term earnings relief to owners, but to achieve financially-sustainable earnings, utilisations must jump by at least five percentage points from current levels, with a ten-point surge required to provide desired returns on capital. With fleet supply growth still running at a 6-7% pace, the dirty tanker market would require a drastic rebound in demand to achieve desired utilisation levels. Even if aggressive demolition slows dirty supply growth to near zero in 2013, the demand growth requirement is still sizable, for a fleet still struggling with low utilisations.
