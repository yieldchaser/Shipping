## FSU Crude Runs
These shifting flows have created crude shortfalls for refineries in Central Europe, as exports have been diverted elsewhere. Indeed, the IEA notes that approximately 484 kbd worth of refineries in the region have suspended runs or closed in October due to declining Druzhba flows. Although an unexpected outage at Surgutneftegas' Kirishi refinery near St. Petersburg - the other major destination of BPS-2 - provides the potential for temporary relief in December, FSU export flows are poised to continue to shift east. Reflecting the expectation of permanency in this shift, the government in the Czech Republic purchased a minority stake in the Transalpine Pipeline, which sources crude via Trieste, in order to gain access to alternatives. Asian-bound pipelines have continued to operate at capacity, further suggesting that east-bound demand for export capacity is indeed significant. It is certainly possible that other Central European refineries continue to get squeezed as producers take advantage of increasing optionality. This extremely fluid situation bears continued monitoring as a part of a broader theme of energy flows shifting to the east. FSU states, and Russia in particular, are surrounded by economies projected to trend in opposite directions, yet the directional nature of their export infrastructure is still in flux to reflect this shifting reality. Baltic Sea Aframax demand has risen at the expense of Suezmaxes in the Black Sea recently, but currently ramping-up Asian export capacity via Kozmino looms as a potential detriment for western FSU export volumes and Asian ton-mile demand, while benefitting short-run Aframaxes from Kozmino.
