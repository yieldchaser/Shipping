## Summary
Chinese Crude Sourcing Offers Continued Support to VLCCs
The rapid growth of Chinese oil product demand, refining capacity and crude imports has been a supportive feature of crude tanker demand for years, and is poised to continue this trend. Recent shifts in Chinese crude import volumes and sourcing patterns have provided a recent boost to tanker demand in 1h12 and have allowed VLCC earnings to recover. Many of these crude import shifts are part of a secular trend that will continue to be supportive for the sector, but some of the recent activity is more ephemeral, suggesting that demand growth may slow during the balance of 2012.

## AG
Africa Lat Amer FSU/Other Asia The pattern of African imports, in contrast, has been subject to the movement of the price spread between Brent and Dubai crudes, which determines the relative attractiveness of Brent price-linked grades in West Africa (WAF) to Asian refiners. As OPEC reduced the output of heavy/sour AG grades during the financial crisis and subsequent recession, the Brent-Dubai spread tightened to virtually zero in 2009-10, prompting a 500-600 kbpd year-over-year (yoy) jump in WAF imports to China.
During 2011, the Libyan crisis removed 1.3 mbpd of light/sweet crude from the market, sending the Brent-Dubai spread to $8/bbl, and forcing a 300-400 kbpd yoy drop in WAF liftings and keeping lighter grades in the Atlantic Basin. The return of Libyan crude exports in late-2011 allowed the spread to move lower and provide a small recovery in Chinese imports from WAF.
This 1q12 sequential recovery in WAF imports, along with the jump in Caribs and Latin American liftings, provided a substantial boost to Chinese crude import tonne-mile demand, lifting it by 50 billion tonne-miles, or 12.9%, over 4q11 levels. Combined with a jump in US import of AG grades and North Sea crude movements to Asia, the increased demand for VLCCs allowed TCE earnings on the AG-Japan route to almost double in 1q12 versus the previous quarter, to average $24,600/day.
Still, with global liquids production averaging 90.7 mbpd during 1q12 and 91.0 mbpd in April/May, versus 1h12 demand of 89.0 mbpd, refiners have been engaged in aggressive stock building, on concerns over Iranian sanctions and other security issues. The Chinese have been major contributors to the global inventory build, with May's 6.00 mbpd in crude imports implying a 25.7 MB stock build (830 kbpd) on more modest gains in crude runs. The following chart shows the yoy changes in Chinese crude runs and crude imports and the recent divergence between slowing crude runs and acceleration in crude imports.

## Crude Imports
Crude Runs The Chinese have committed to building 500 MB of strategic petroleum reserve (SPR) capacity by 2016, having completed Phase I of 103 MB in April 2009, and are currently filling the 169 MB of Phase II storage, due to be completed by 2013. A consistent pace of storage filling would suggest and incremental 150-175 kbpd of crude imports to reach 272 MB in 2013, while maintaining 25- 30 days of crude inventories for constantly increasing Chinese refining capacity would imply another 50-75 kbpd of extra crude imports for commercial storage.
Obviously, Chinese crude imports are outpacing these rateable levels and SPR storage is likely to be filling rapidly. Based upon implied stock building, Chinese inventories could have increased by as much as seven days of crude runs since the end of 2011. In comparison, the recent build of the US SPR starting in 2001 took 37 months to increase the days of inventory by seven days. With Chinese crude runs only showing a 285 kbpd yoy rise in 1q12 and expected to increase by only 375-400 kbpd yoy in 2q12, on slowing industrial activity, any slowing of inventory building could reverse much of the crude tanker demand gains in early-2012.
Longer-term, the dramatic growth in Chinese crude imports and their sourcing patterns have profoundly influenced VLCC demand. Although the 3.8% average annual growth in the VLCC fleet during 2006-11 has presented challenges to the sector, the 11.8% average annual surge in Chinese crude imports over the period, and the growing contribution of Chinese crude import tonne-mile demand have supported VLCC demand. The chart below illustrates how Chinese import demand has taken a progressively larger portion of total VLCC employment. In 2011, more than one fifth of total VLCC employment was dedicated to Chinese crude imports, and this proportion has doubled since 2005. That figure is impressive in its own right, but over that same time period the VLCC fleet also grew by more than 25%. To achieve this proportion with this fleet growth, the number of VLCCs employed for Chinese imports increased by a factor of 2.6 between 2005 and 2011.

## China Share of VLCC Demand
This trend in the Chinese share of VLCC demand should continue, as Chinese refiners lift an additional 1.1 mbpd of AG crudes, 0.4 mbpd of Latin American grades and 0.8 mbpd of WAF cargoes during 2011-16. In fact, this forecast pattern of Chinese imports would imply that VLCCs capture 83% of incremental Chinese tonne-mile demand during the period. This figure is impressive, but implies approximately 70 VLCCs worth of incremental demand from 2011 to 2016. With the current VLCC orderbook near 100 vessels, other importing regions need to add to VLCC demand to help rebalance supply and demand.
