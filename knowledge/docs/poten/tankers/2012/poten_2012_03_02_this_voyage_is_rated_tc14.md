## This Voyage is Rated TC14
The refined product imbalance between the United States and Europe is a structural blessing for trading companies and ship owners alike. Ship owners can increase the utilization of their assets with very little, if any, repositioning costs by securing cargoes both coming and going. Likewise, trading companies are able to capture arbitrage opportunities on top of existing contract movements. Recently, increased US Gulf exports of clean products have focused the attention on this traditional back‐haul trade lane. Charterers have had mismatched hedging instruments for the ex‐US Gulf trade lane as paper traded on the opposite trade lane, TC2, has been primary tool utilized. The introduction of the new Baltic Exchange Clean Tanker Index - TC14 ‐ should be seen as a positive development for the market as it will enable participants to more effectively manage freight exposure and could facilitate increased trade.
The chart below shows both the front‐haul (UK Continent to US Gulf) and the back‐haul (US Gulf to UK Continent) trade lanes on a Worldscale basis. The ratio between the two rates highlights the shortcomings and challenges of using TC2 as a hedge for the TC14 trade.

## WS Rate Ratio (TC14/TC2)
TC14: USG ‐ UKC (38,000 MT) TC2: UKC ‐ USAC (37,000 MT) Relative Rate Strength The Baltic Exchange has just recently introduced the TC14 index on a trial basis. The new route is assessed basis Houston - Amsterdam on 38,000 MT for vessels with a maximum age of 15 years and major oil company approval.
According to the Baltic Exchange, the volumes of clean forward freight agreements have trended higher over the past six months suggesting some positive momentum in the use of paper instruments for hedging purposes. In fact, the total number of contracts traded for February 2012 was 17,743, about 12% higher than the volumes traded for dirty freight futures.

## Paper Covers Physical
The backhaul trade had typically been opportunistic and seasonal in nature as incremental distillate was pushed out of the US Gulf coast. Today, the structurally long distillate position in the US Gulf has lent support to the steady volumes moving out of the region. The long‐term average monthly number of fixtures ex‐ US Gulf is 14. However, in the past six months, the average number of cargoes increased to 20 per month.

## USG ‐ UKC Reported Spot Fixture Activity
As domestic consumption in the United States continues to languish, it can be expected that new US refining capacity will push additional product into foreign markets. The growth of exports volumes plus the accessibility of freight paper markets could allow traders to take better advantage of market arbitrage opportunity mitigating the freight risk component of the transaction.
