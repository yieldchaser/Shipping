## Crude Inventories - Friend or Foe to Tankers?
The most recent OPEC Monthly Oil Market Report (www.opec.org) listing of world oil inventories shows an uptrend, but does this presage a potential cutback in tanker cargoes if there is a return to "normalcy," given relatively flat oil consumption in Western Europe and OECD Pacific regions and marked increases in North American consumption?
There are two general types of inventory- inventory held by commercial interests and government sponsored Strategic Petroleum Reserves. Commercial inventories are made up of crude oil and oil products, while Strategic Petroleum Reserves (SPRs) are government-controlled and owned inventories of primarily crude oil dedicated to buffer potential interruptions in global oil flows.

## North America
Western Europe OECD Pacific A closer scrutiny of the data shows that North America commercial inventory has remained fairly flat with little change between 1997 and 2004. Oil inventories in Western Europe increased by just under 30 million barrels since 1997, which was compensated by a nearly equal decline in inventories in OECD Pacific (Japan, Korea, Australia, and New Zealand).
In contrast with these mixed inventory levels, the consumption days on these commercial inventories have slipped across the board. North American consumption days stand at 48, versus 52 in 1997; Western Europe has notched down from 60 to 59 in 2004, and OECD Pacific levels plummeted to 48 from 1997's 58.

The SPR in North America (United States) has increased by 110 million barrels since 1997; Western Europe has increased by 35 million barrels, as has OECD Pacific (Japan).
These inventory level increases have had minimal effects on the consumption days they can cover due to increased demand. For example, according to the report North America's 110 million barrel SPR increase only yields it roughly 2 more consumption days than in 1997: 26 versus 24. Western Europe's consumption days also moved only two days, from 21 in 1997 to 23 in 2004, on the 35 million barrel SPR increase. The same stockpiling in the OECD Pacific only yielded one incremental consumption day, moving 1997's 43 up to 2004's 44.

For oil companies, the chief uncertainty is associated with demand whereas governments' focus on uncertainty is associated with supply. We could conclude that governments are more worried about the world oil situation than oil companies, but that is not necessarily true. Oil companies, in viewing inventory uncertainty with regard to supply, take into consideration inventory held by governments. If oil companies conclude that the strategic oil reserves held by governments cover their uncertainty with regard to oil supplies, there's no need to double up.

The overall impact has been a reduction in total inventories from 83 days of consumption to 80 days of consumption since 1997. So What Does This Mean for Tanker Owners? It is true that world oil inventories have increased in the last seven years. However, this situation does not portend a decrease in tanker demand. In fact, the fall in consumption days covered both by commercial inventories and overall worldwide inventories seems to indicate that demand growth has outstripped supply growth. Countries and customers are burning through oil at greater speeds in theory, increasing the cushion that commercial companies and governments require. It becomes thus necessary for these companies to buy up more oil for storage, a requirement that will become incrementally more difficult as tonnage supply is eaten up by China's imminent build-up of a Strategic Petroleum Reserve of its own. Increasing inventories that have lagged the consumption habits of their consumers in fact paint an optimistic picture of tanker demand. The future appears even brighter.
