## Summary
One of the most interesting dynamics of the 2005 energy market is the historically high level of refining margins that were achieved despite relatively high crude prices and marine transportation costs. The following items would certainly be on refiners, producers, and tanker owners 2006 wish list: 2 That oil consumption remains relatively immune to high oil prices. Oil prices hit $40 per barrel in May 2004, $50 in October 2004 after Hurricane Ivan, $60 per barrel oil in June 2005, and $70 in August after Hurricane Katrina with little apparent effect on the big consumers (United States, China, and India).

## Holiday Wishes!
One of the most interesting dynamics of the 2005 energy market is the historically high level of refining margins that were achieved despite relatively high crude prices and marine transportation costs. The following items would certainly be on refiners, producers, and tanker owners 2006 wish list: 2 That oil consumption remains relatively immune to high oil prices. Oil prices hit $40 per barrel in May 2004, $50 in October 2004 after Hurricane Ivan, $60 per barrel oil in June 2005, and $70 in August after Hurricane Katrina with little apparent effect on the big consumers (United States, China, and India).
2 That oil consumption will continue to reflect growing population of motor vehicles in China, which was 10 million in 1995, 27 million in 2005, and is expected to double to over 50 million in 2010. China, in second place as an oil consumer, may be "on the road" to first place.
2 That crude oil production constraints will not materialize as some had feared and that crude oil availability will continue to increase from North and West Africa, Russia, and the new BTC pipeline that is coming on stream.
2 That OPEC will follow through on its announced intention to increase production capacity by 5 million bpd over the next five years to 38 million bpd. This would show that the world oil system is still capable of responding to increased demand and higher prices.
2 That refineries will continue to meet demand even though they are operating at near maximum capacity. The world's refineries seem to be handling the increased load despite temporary setbacks such as hurricanes. Actual refinery constraints might not even materialize if India and China continue to build capacity at their planned rates.
2 That the IEA assessed growth for next year's world oil consumption of 2.2% (compared to this year's assessed growth of 1.4%) and OPEC's assessed growth for next year of a bit more modest 1.9% are both close to the mark.
2 That increasing Atlantic basin exports to the Pacific basin keep on track such as Venezuela's growing export of crude and fuel oil to China.
2 That the entire energy industry continue its focus on safety so that we can all enjoy the fulfillment of our wish list.
