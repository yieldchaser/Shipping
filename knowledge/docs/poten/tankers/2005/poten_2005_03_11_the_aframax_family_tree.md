## The Aframax Family Tree
Family trees allow us to figure out who is related to whom- here we are going to figure out the statistical relationships between Aframax rates, timecharter rates, newbuilding prices, and second hand values. The chart below shows the relationship between newbuilding prices and second hand values.

## Newbuilding Prices
Second Hand (5 Yr) Values There is a strong relationship between newbuilding prices and second hand values. Normally there is a discount between the two reflecting the age difference, but in strong markets, having a ship is better than waiting for a ship 18 or more months after it has been ordered. Thus the discount has virtually disappeared since 2003. Tanker owners who had purchased a five-year old Aframax in 2002 have doubled their investment. Of course to realize that profit, they would have to sell at this time.

## Newbuilding Prices
1 Yr Timecharter 3 Yr Timecharter While not quite as strong as the relationship between newbuilding prices and second hand values, there was a strong family resemblance between the three up to last year. The logic of the relationship is that a strong market drives up term charter rates, which in turn increases owners' desires for new tonnage. Their inquiries and orders provide an incentive for shipyards to increase quotes for newbuildings. Recently newbuilding prices seem to be departing from term charter rates reflecting continuing orders to shipyards whose order books are full.

Not All Family Trees Are the Same The Aframax family relationship does not necessarily hold for other tanker types where there may be a stronger relationship between the volatility of the spot market and term charter rates. Focusing on Aframaxes, it appears that any further weakening of spot rates for Aframaxes will affect term charter rates and consequently the desire of owners to place more orders, which, in time will affect newbuilding quotes.
