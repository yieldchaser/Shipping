## From Whence Doth the Business Cometh?
This week we look at the principal charterers for clean cargoes for Aframax, Panamax and Handymax tankers. Excluding failed fixtures, the Poten Spot Fixture Database records 3,271 fixtures in 2004. The great majority of these were for Handymax tankers.

## 2004 Clean Fixture Activity by Tanker Type
Mirror, Mirror on the Wall, Who Was the Biggest Charterer of Them All Of the 123 charterers, the top 30 charterers, or 24% of the total, account for 80% of the number of fixtures. The top 9 charterers, shown below, accounted for half of fixtures with known charterers. Shell was the most active charterer by far, nearly equal to the number of fixtures done by the next two most popular charterers BP and Vitol.

## Total Clean Fixtures for Leading Charterers 2004
Aframax - Small in Number, Big in Size Forty-five charterers fixed spot voyages of clean trading Aframax tankers of which the top 17, or 38%, accounted for 80% of the business. The top five charterers concluded half of the charters. The chart on the next page shows the top 9 charterers who accounted for two-thirds of fixture activity with known charterers. Vitol, Shell, and Addax took the top three positions.

## Cargill
Panamax - Small in Number and Not Quite so Big in Size A total of 53 charterers fixed Panamaxes for clean trading of which the top 9 accounted for 50% of fixtures with known charterers. The top 20, or 37%, accounted for 80% of all fixtures with known charterers. Vitol, Itochu, and Shell took the top three positions.

## Chevron Texaco
Handymaxes - Large in Number and Dominating the Market A total of 113 charterers fixed Handymaxes for clean trading of which the top 9 charterers accounted for 50% of fixtures with known charterers and the top 27, or 24%, accounted for 80% of all fixtures. Shell, BP, and Chevron Texaco were the most active.

## Only a Few Count
The Pareto effect or 80/20 rule, where most of the activity in an industry is conducted by relatively few companies, holds in fixing tankers. In dirty cargoes, major oil companies dominate the few; but not in clean cargoes where oil traders join ranks with Big Oil to be counted among the few.
