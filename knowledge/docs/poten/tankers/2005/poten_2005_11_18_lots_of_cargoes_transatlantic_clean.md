## Lots of Cargoes
Transatlantic Clean This week we will be taking a look at clean cargoes in the transatlantic trade in terms of volume and cargo lot size. These transaltlantic clean cargoes are going up as a consequence of U.S. refinery capacity failing to expand commensurate with oil consumption, plus a leveling off of the Caribbean refineries. Thus incremental clean product has to be imported from more distant sources. This year's jump in fixture activity is partly due to greater imports to satisfy demand, and also partly from the impact of Hurricane Katrina on refinery operations (fixtures for 2005 as of mid-November are already 17% ahead of full-year 2004).
Lot's of Cargoes, But What About Cargo Lot Size? The most common lot sizes for clean cargoes in transatlantic trade are 33,000 tons and 37,000 tons. As the following chart shows, the smaller cargoes included in the range of 30,000-35,000 tons are falling whereas the larger cargoes, included in the range 35,000-40,000 tons, are rising rapidly.

## Fixture Activity by Cargo Lot Size
Larger sized cargoes between 60,000 and 70,000 tons have numbered an average of 35 cargoes per year ranging between 25 and 45 cargoes per year. The exception was in 2000 when 55 cargoes were carried in this cargo range. Cargoes above 70,000 tons do not display a clear trend and made up only 3% of all market fixtures in 2005. The chart on the next page shows the percentage distribution of cargo lot sizes between 2000, 2002, and 2005. As noted, 2000 was the peak year in shipments in large cargo lots accounting for 42% of all cargoes fixed on the spot market. Other years show a more consistent level of less than half this share. Focusing on the 33,000 ton cargo lot size, the share of cargoes held constant between 2000 and 2002 at 25% and has subsequently fallen to 10%. The 37,000 ton cargo lot size share was 21% in 2000, jumped to 51% in 2002, and is currently 73% of all cargoes in the transatlantic clean cargo trade.

## Cargo Lot Size Distribution
Transatlantic Clean Cargoes Why the marked shift in cargo sizes from 33,000 tons to 37,000 tons on transatlantic voyages? One of the main reasons is the rising deadweight and cubic meter capacity of Handymax tankers between 37,500 and 50,000 dwt. Average cubic capacity for ships built in 2005 is 7% and deadweight capacity is 8.5% above 1990-built vessels.

## And the Outlook?
With such a commanding share of the market, it is difficult to see how 37,000 ton cargo lot sizes can increase in market share to any significant degree. For cargo lot sizes to continue growing, Panamax and Aframax cargoes would have to become more common. The chief impediments to significant growth in Panamax and Aframax cargoes on this trade are terminal restrictions on both sides of the Atlantic as well as availability of larger-sized parcels. Thus we expect to see 37,000 ton cargoes to continue to dominate this market for the foreseeable future.
