## Are We Moving Towards Chaos?
What is chaos? Chaos is the inherent unpredictability of the behavior of a natural system. Volatility is therefore a measure of chaos. The following chart shows that the short-term swings between highs and lows for Aframax earnings are becoming more pronounced.

## Caribs Upcoast
Intra-Med Singapore Far East The standard deviation is associated with the risk of price or rate changes and is a good measure of volatility. The higher the standard deviation, the greater the risk of an adverse price or rate change as price stability is low. The lower the standard deviation, the less the risk as price stability is high. Unstable systems are difficult to predict, at least in the short term, and make it difficult for participants to plan. If we look upon the standard deviation as an indicator of the inherent risk associated with Aframax rates, there has been a significant increase in the risk faced by Aframax owners and charterers particularly in the Caribbean as seen in the following chart.

This is an enormously wide set of probable outcomes!

## Running Standard Deviation of Tanker TCE Earnings
While the standard deviation for Panamaxes trading Caribs upcoast accompanied the increasing volatility with Aframaxes during the first two cycles, volatility has remained essentially unchanged during the last cycle. For Handymaxes trading Caribs upcoast, the trend is up, but far more subdued than that for Panamaxes and Aframaxes.
Is Cargo Volume the Cause? The chart on the next page shows the total cargo tons carried on Aframaxes Caribs upcoast on a monthly basis. Interestingly cargo tons have declined from a peak in 2002 and have basically leveled out with a fairly stable standard deviation. The drop off in cargo volume that has occurred is related strictly to vessels fixed on the spot market, and is not an indication of the actual volume moved, as many more vessels employed in this region are owned or period chartered now than they were in 2002. The Caribs upcoast spot market is "thinner" because of the increased role of owned and chartered-in tonnage and cargoes carried under contracts of affreightment that are not reported as spot charters. It would appear that this lack of depth is partly responsible for the more intense rate cycles, as are the impacts of a lack of spare capacity in oil production, and, of course, the weather on the last rate cycle.

Under these circumstances of a thinner market, less spare capacity to handle variations in oil demand, and the weather, owners and charterers have a greater challenge in deciding what position to take. Should owners and charterers seek the comfort of a timecharter or enter into a shipping derivative to stabilize earnings at the cost of sacrificing profits or stabilize freights at the cost of not benefiting from a subsequent fall in the market? Or should each throw caution to the wind and ride the roller coaster of a chaotic market no matter what comes? Decreased spot activity shows that lately charterers and owners have preferred to reduce the volatility to which they are exposed by entering into term charters.
