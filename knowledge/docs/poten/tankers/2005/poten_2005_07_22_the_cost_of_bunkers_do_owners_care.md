## The Cost of Bunkers, Do Owners Care?
The cost of bunkers is a major component of an owner's voyage expense. In paying for the bunkers, the owner is creating a source of revenue for oil companies. Oil companies receive back a portion of what they pay out in freight costs. The following chart shows the WS rate paid by oil companies to move a cargo AG/East.

## WS Rate for VLCCs Trading AG/East
This freight rate becomes a freight bill to be paid by the oil company once the cargo is delivered. The chart on the next page shows the freight revenue for a single VLCC voyage AG/East and the portion of the freight revenue that represents the bunker cost .

## Bunker Cost
Freight Revenue Net of Bunkers The above chart seems to suggest that only a small portion of the freight revenue is related to bunkers. The portion of the bunker cost contained in the freight revenue, shown below, has varied from a low of 6% in November 2004 to a high of 51% in April and August of 2002. The average portion of freight revenue to cover bunkers since 2000 has been 21%.

## Portion of Freight Revenue to Pay for Bunkers
Fluctuations in the portion of freight revenue to cover bunkers reflect changes in bunker prices and in the spot market. Of the two, the latter is much more significant. With the recent weakening of the spot market, the portion of freight represented by bunkers has jumped to over 40%. The Net Effect After paying for voyage charges (bunkers and canal/port charges) and commissions, the owner can calculate the vessel's daily earnings. Excluding the first half of 2002, the last five years have been one of the most prosperous periods in tanker history. As seen below, owners have not really been overly concerned about the increased costs they have had to pay for bunkers because of their spectacular earnings.

## Lest We Forget
The record proportion of the bunker cost to the freight revenue was 100%, which occurred during the tanker depression of the 1980s when VLCC freight rates fell to WS17. The hapless owner, after carrying a cargo halfway around the world, was given a check for the freight, which he turned over and endorsed back to the oil company to pay for the bunkers. The recent jump of bunker costs to over 40% of freight revenue would normally be of concern were it not for the cash cushion generated from the unprecedented level of earnings over the last few years.
