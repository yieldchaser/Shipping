## Investors- What Do They Know?
Tanker rates and equity values have parted company once again. The following chart shows the historical values of the Poten Tanker Index and the Equity Index. The tanker index is the average timecharter equivalent rate for crude carrier categories (Panamax to VLCC) weighted by the number of vessels in each category. The Equity Index is the monthly average price per share of the principal publicly traded tanker owning companies. Both are indexed to a value of 100 for January 1997. Currently, the PTI is 157.2, near its historical average of 140.3, whereas the Equity Index is 397.4, more than double its average of 169.7.

## Era I
Era I was a time when vessel earnings and stock prices were closely correlated. The R Square from the inception of the two indices in January 1997 to June 2000 was a respectable 73%. The Equity Index hit its lowest level at 59.7 in February, 1999. Post Era I Since then there have been significant divergences between the two indices shown below.

## Equity Less Tanker Indices
Generally speaking the equity index has been higher than the tanker rate index except during times of peak tanker rates. The divergence between the two indices has never been greater as it is now. Era II After an initial spike in early 2001, the equity index followed the declining tanker rate index that started in late 2002. The PTI reached its lowest historical level of 42.5 in August, 2002. Owners' earnings for the AG/Far East voyage on a 260,000 dwt VLCC were a paltry $5,400 per day as compared with a ten year average of $38,100. Thus investors correctly perceived that the fall-off in tanker rates was not transient, but would persist for a period of time.

## Era III
In late 2002, the tanker market changed like no one could possibly imagine with the sinking of the Erika . Although rates would have gone up somewhat from growing demand as the world economies continued to expand after the Southeast Asian economic crisis, the reduction in available supply by charterers avoiding older single hull tankers worked wonders for tanker owners of modern tonnage. Charter rates spiked even more than investors anticipated. From its historical low, the PTI climbed to 304.4 in March 2003, nearly 160 points above the Equity Index. Earnings on the same VLCC voyage were nearly 18 times higher at $87,200 per day than at the PTI all-time low. Throughout this period, investors persistently believed that tanker rates would go up. Equity values did not fall when tanker rates retrenched; they kept rising, correctly predicting that tanker rates would soon rise again. The last six months have been especially volatile for the PTI index, while the Equity Index has been steadily increasing. These indices both hit their peaks at the end of November 2004: 498.6 for the Equity Index and 553.6 for the PTI. VLCC earnings for AG/Far East voyages hit a record $218,100 per day, nearly triple the March 2003 levels and 40 times their August 2002 lows. Recently, however, the PTI has fallen off and now stands at 157.2 with VLCC earnings falling to $47,600 per day. These levels are still well above the ten year average of $38,100 per day. Nevertheless, equity investors expect rates to move further upwards as seen in the current Equity Index of 397.4. Last week the Equity Index hit its greatest difference over the PTI with a spread of 272.5 points. What Now? In examining the history of equity values versus tanker rates since mid-2002, we have to give investors high grades for being able to forecast future tanker rates. Since the tanker market's sharp decline starting in late 2004, equity values have changed only marginally, opening up the widest gap ever seen between tanker and equity indices. Investors are saying that as far as they're concerned, the latest retreat in tanker rates is but a blip on the screen. They are telling us that they expect the two indices will narrow again not by the equity index falling, but by the tanker index rising. That's the message in the chart- let's hope the investors are right again!
