## The $70 Million Dollar Man
In the 1970's, one of the most popular TV shows was "The Six Million Dollar Man." The premise of the show was that for $6 million dollars the U.S. government was able to rebuild a star athlete who had suffered a near fatal accident into a superhuman. This week we will talk about the $70 Million Dollar Man. Depending of course on your view, only about half of you will consider this man superhuman. One of the key decisions that charterers make is the balance in their program between term and spot coverage. In addition to determining the required number of vessels to take on period charter, the length of the charter must be decided and finally the "right" rate to pay. What is interesting about 2004 is the wide variance between the time charter rates that were available at the end of 2003 and the equivalent earnings (TCE) that were being earned at the time on the spot market and eventually for all of 2004. As term chartering managers approached the end of 2003, they were facing a three-year term market with rates around $31,000 per day for a VLCC and a spot market with a TCE of just over $87,000 per day. What is amazing is that very few 3-year charters were concluded despite numerous owners who expressed interest in doing term business. For the sake of illustrating this disparity, suppose that an oil company chartered in a vessel for three years on December 2003 to commence January 2004 when spot rates were about $87,000 per day and a 3-year timecharter could be obtained for $31,000 per day. Where would the charterer and the owner stand today? As seen below, the charterers aggregate charter hire payments for the past 12 months would have been $20 million less if they had taken on a timecharter versus relying on the spot market. Consequently, and fortunately for the owner, most charterers were fighting to get the owner to lower the charter rate another $500 per day. Rather than paying this additional "premium", charterers in effect said, "We do not want to pay the $182,500 additional charter hire per year ($500 per day times 365 days). We will face the risk of high rates." By not coming to terms with owners a year ago and paying this small premium, charterers have been left with a $20 million higher freight bill for VLCCs in 2004.
