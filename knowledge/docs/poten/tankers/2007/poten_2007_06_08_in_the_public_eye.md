## In The Public Eye
As the stock markets in the United States continue on their bull run, tanker equities are no exception. With substantial gains in 2007 to date, listed tanker companies seem to be getting the public recognition for which they have been striving. The chart below shows the Poten Tanker Index, an indicator of the freight markets, and the Poten Equity Index, compiled of tanker stocks. Public tanker companies are in an interesting position as they are faced with the challenge of increasing shareholder value on a continual basis. This is particularly daunting in today's market as owners are making chartering and purchase decisions under volatile freight conditions and in consideration of historically high vessel prices.

## Ups and Downs
One sector in particular that has been of interest to several public companies has been the product carrier segment. In the past year there has been considerable ordering activity of coated vessels and sale and purchase of existing ships. This sector, as shown in the chart below, experiences a high degree of rate variability. Overall there has been an upward trend in this market, but since the start of 2005 rates have ranged anywhere between $9,700 and $38,000 per day.

## UKC - USAC TCE
Trendline Investors and market consensus has traditionally reacted to short- term rates. It is more difficult to rationalize an investment of $48 million dollars per product carrier, when day rates are under $10,000 per day. With better information and a more educated investor base, tanker companies can be valued in expectation of longer-term fundamentals.

## Asset Values
Companies continue to acquire vessels although asset prices remain very high by historical standards. Asset plays have proven to be quite lucrative for vessel owners, particularly resales of shipbuilding contracts with forward delivery. With high steel and raw materials prices and relatively limited berth capacity, prices are anticipated to remain firm for the next several years. The primary challenge is justifying investments at record prices in an unknown freight environment. The public companies are faced with the challenge of balancing long-term trends with Wall Street pressure on a quarterly basis.
In today's tanker market, rate seasonality has become less apparent while the market still experiences a degree of overall cyclicality. As the investment community becomes better versed in the factors driving the cyclicality in the market it can better digest longer- term fundamentals and think outside the 'quarterly' box.
