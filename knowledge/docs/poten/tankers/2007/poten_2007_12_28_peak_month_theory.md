Peak Month Theory
As the split-second when unseen electrons magically switch the date in our computers from this year to the next approaches, we thought it fitting to reflect on the split-second changes in rates that can occur in the spot tanker market. Looking back on 2007, the following chart highlights that VLCC rates from the AG to Far East were, on average, mostly below the monthly Worldscale rate experienced in the prior eleven years. But while rates were less than inspiring during the first ten months of 2007, they did not establish a new monthly low, either.

## "Volatility" = Monthly High TCE minus Yearly Average TCE
But using yearly averages masks the month-to-month volatility in TCEs. If the highest monthly TCE is removed from its yearly average - as a means of canceling the highest earning month's contribution - a different ranking order emerges for AG - Far East VLCC TCEs. Without the boost from December's average daily time charter earnings of $205,500 per day, the TCE average of the other eleven months in 2007 falls back into a middle ranking. Seven of the last twelve years' highest monthly average TCE's occurred in the fourth quarter of the year. Viewing volatility as the difference between the highest monthly TCE and the yearly TCE average without this high, the following chart of years with their highest TCEs in the fourth quarter suggests that the magnitude of volatility has increased recently.

## Difference: Highest-Average, $/day
With roughly 110 spot VLCC fixtures monthly in a fleet some 500 vessels strong, tanker owners have about a 20 percent chance of being lucky and hitting the jackpot in a peak month. Lately, though, it seems that owners participating in the fourth quarter spot market stand a better chance of being lucky than in the other quarters of the year.
