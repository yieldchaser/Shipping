## The Stock Story
Just when some were beginning to think the tanker market had begun its decline, spot market rates have rebounded across all markets. In particular, rates in the Atlantic Basin have been at the famed "fourth quarter 2004" for a couple of weeks. In our Tanker Opinion, "The Knife Edge", we addressed the lack of VLCC availability in the Arabian Gulf that sparked the jump in rates, but the longevity of such a rally is really defined by market fundamentals. The chart below compares the weekly crude oil stocks in the US for the past three years. In general, crude oil stocks in the United States can serve as one indication of where the tanker market may be headed in the coming weeks. With VLCCs on some trades already surpassing the $200,000 per day mark equivalent (TCE) basis, some charterers may be beginning to wonder just how much more upward rates movement can be expected.

## Thousand Barrels
Over the past few weeks, substantial stock draws have taken place in the US during the fourth quarter. Stock levels this week are 15 million barrels less than the first week of October. With steady demand for refined products, expect demand for crude oil to remain firm.

## Rate Recap
In the past month, rates have increased nearly 900% for VLCCs loading in the Arabian Gulf. One-month ago, the TCE for AG - USG (280kt) was $15,000 per day. That number has increased over ten fold to $155,000 per day. The chart below shows the daily timecharter equivalents for the voyages Arabian Gulf - Far East, Arabian Gulf - US Gulf and West Africa - US Gulf. Rates in the Atlantic have increased substantially as well, 550% in the same one-month period.

## AG - USG (280kt)
AG - FE (260kt) WAF - USG (260kt) It appears that rates in the East have tended to lead those in the West. If this pattern is to continue in the next couple of weeks, rates in the West may have the capacity to increase further still.
The current crude oil stock data indicates that further imports are likely to be required. With these fundamentals in place, higher vessel demand will likely give rise to further rate pressure through the balance of the year. With US refiners striving to replace inventory in 2008 continued strength can be expected into the New Year.
