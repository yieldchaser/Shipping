## Not So Fast
After last week's stock market tumble, many individuals and businesses paused to think about the stability and autonomy of the US and Chinese economies. The drop that was precipitated by other stock market declines in the Far East, left many wondering whether or not recent strength in the market was based on fundamentals or was simply a result of momentum. While the market has recovered slightly over the last several trading days, it still remains slightly lower than beginning of the year levels. The chart below shows the S&P 500 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average since the start of 2005. Although the stock market may appear to be suffering slightly, there is a consensus that the recent activity was more of a correction than a cause for any long term concern.

S&P 500 Although the stock markets have taken a dip, freight rates have remained firm and both crude oil and refined product demand have continued to stay at record levels. With many quick to believe that long- running strength in the freight and equity markets are doomed to come to an end, there is reason to believe that growth will at least continue in the near-term. After all, it is said that "economists have correctly predicted nine of the last five recessions."
