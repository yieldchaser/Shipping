## Short Positions
Momentum in the sale and purchase and time charter tanker markets has historically been associated with general strengths and trends in the spot market. With the fourth quarter officially underway, owners appear to be cautiously waiting to see if, when and how the spot market is going to recover. It is no secret that asset prices and term charter rates have remained firm despite weakness in the spot market, but one area in particular that continues to exhibit strength is the 1-year MR time charter market. Chart below shows the spot market rates for Medium-Range (MR) product carriers, basis Caribbean - US (38kt), and the one-year and three-year time charters.
TCE CAR - USAC (38kt) TC - 1 Yr TC - 3 Yr The average spot time charter equivalents (TCEs) for the 2005- 2007 year-to-date period are approximately $21,250 per day, assuming 90% utilization, whereas the one-year time charter has been considerably higher averaging $25,400 per day It is striking that the volatility seen in the spot market does not seem to have any ripple effect on the one-year time charter rate.

## MR Time Charters (12 Mo.)
18 Reported YTD The activity level for the shorter-term charters is relatively stable as the increase in activity has been in the longer-term charters. Year-to- date nearly 100 charters of over 12 month duration have been concluded as compared to only about 30 in the comparable period in 2004.
The most active participants for the one-year timecharters are trading companies. Traders are often keen to seek time charters as they have internal shipping requirements, are well versed in freight paper trading and can offset transportation costs through commodity trading profits - a combination that makes for efficient management of physical ships and freight. Opportunity Knocks Owners that are holding out for higher spot market earnings may be missing opportunities to achieve strong rates short-term time charters. Those with many ships are in a key position to capitalize on this market structure by keeping some vessels in the pure spot market and putting some out on short-term time charters.
Imagine this win-win situation; traders get the ships they need to better capture commodity trading opportunities, while ship owners realize comparatively better earnings.
