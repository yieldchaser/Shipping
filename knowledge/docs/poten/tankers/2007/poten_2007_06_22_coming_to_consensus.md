## Coming to Consensus
During the past week, Marine Money and JP Morgan presented the 20 th Annual Marine Money Week in New York. Shipowners, leading executives, financiers and analysts came together to discuss the most relevant topics facing the shipping industry today. By design, the most interesting part of this industry-wide event is the confluence of opinions and outlooks across sectors. Generally, in this type of forum it is uncommon that any consensus is ever reached. However, one area that many seemed to agree on this week was how freight rates have continued to exceed Wall Street expectations. While many are keen to look for the beginning of the end of the bull market, fundamentals continue to prolong the rally.

## Profits at Work
While the future of the freight market remains to be seen, fundamentals for wet and dry bulk are closely linked to the health of the world economies. One highlight of Wednesday's program was the introduction given by Larry Kudlow, host of CNBC's "Kudlow & Company." He spoke of the overall health of the economy in the United States and abroad and preached the benefits of letting markets operate freely. As for the future, he indicated that the current strength of company profits is a testament of real growth. According to Mr. Kudlow, companies are able to invest profits in new ventures and expanding their businesses, the world can expect sustainable economic health.
Mr. Kudlow's points set the tone for the rest of the event. As sourcing capital is an integral part of growing a shipping business, the debate between public and private wages on.
