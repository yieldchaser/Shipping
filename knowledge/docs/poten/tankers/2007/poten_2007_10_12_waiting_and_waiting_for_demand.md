## Waiting and Waiting for Demand
Refineries are ingenious places. Just pour in crude oil and then wait for their output of high quality fuels and other valuable products to appear. Synchronizing both overall output volumes and the portions of each fuel grade with swings in fuel demand, however, can challenge refiners. Unseasonably warm weather along the US East Coast lately has left refiners with distillate inventories at comfortable seasonal levels but also has confronted them with gasoline inventories that are below their seasonal low over the previous five years.

## From Whence Cometh Demand?
With current gasoline inventories below their recent historic lows, an expectation of higher crudes runs to boost gasoline reserves might prevail. But what if these inventory levels merely reflect an increased level of manufacturing and distribution efficiency which negates the need for higher levels?
Recent processing levels in US refineries have lagged those in the similar period of last year. But raising crude oil runs now will produce more distillates, too. Until East Coast temperatures drop enough to stimulate demand for home heating oil, any increase in distillate production would just end up in tankage.

## Aug wk 2
The first half of 2007 saw US gasoline demand average about 140 thousand bbls/day over the 9.16 MM bbls/day average in this period of 2006. Lately, however, gasoline demand has mirrored that of the same period last year.

## Oct wk 1
US refiners are wrestling with the challenges of adapting their operations to meet not only a seasonal shift in demand for fuel type, but also an overall demand that is similar to that of last year's. So while demand remains fundamentally strong, it will need to increase significantly before the marine transportation system begins to tighten.
