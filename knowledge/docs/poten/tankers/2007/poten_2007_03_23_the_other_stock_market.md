## Summary
The Other Stock Market
Since they attained 5-year highs 7 weeks ago, US gasoline stock levels have fallen by more than 8% to 210 million barrels. At the same time crude oil stocks (excluding the SPR) have risen from 314 million barrels to 329 million barrels thus far in 2007. We can trace this simultaneous increase in crude stocks and decrease in gasoline stocks to low refinery utilization - the question is, where will the market go from here, given that peak summer driving season is quickly approaching?

## Refinery Utilization and Gasoline Imports
Current refinery utilization of 86.3% is well below the 5-year average utilization rate of 90.6%; in fact US refinery utilization has been below 5-year averages for all of 2007. At the same time, gasoline imports have actually been quite low relative to historical levels, remaining below historical average imports for each of the past four weeks.

## What is the Net Effect
Persistent declines in gasoline stocks coupled with high crude prices has led to US retail gasoline prices that have increased by almost 40 cents per gallon (or nearly 20%) in the past nine weeks. What is interesting is that there has been very little change in refinery utilization over that period. Though some of this non-reaction can be explained by refinery maintenance work, some credit must also go to lower refinery margins, especially in comparison to the margins that companies have enjoyed over the last several years.

## What is the Next Step
On the brink of peak summer driving season, US gasoline production and US gasoline stock levels will increase in the short term. These increases will lead to higher imports and higher domestic refinery runs. Bringing gasoline imports back up to 5-year averages would require an additional 140,000 barrels per day of gasoline imports. Assuming these imports come from the UK/Continent region this increase would equate to additional demand for approximately 12 fully employed MRs.
In summary, with a relatively balanced supply/demand outlook for the short term, any type of incremental delay in the logistics system will only put further pressure on prices both for products and for ships.
