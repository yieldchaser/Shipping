## First Signs of Spring
While New Yorkers are eagerly anticipating the arrival of spring, many tanker owners are happy to prolong the arrival of the annual April slump in freight rates. During April, VLCC rates tend to recede from strong winter levels, but rates on average this month have been higher than previous years, thus far. The chart below shows the historical average time charter equivalents for VLCCs AG - US Gulf (280kt). Spot VLCC rates have begun to evidence the first signs of softening this week. Due to the recent build-up of available tonnage in the Arabian Gulf awaiting cargoes, rates may continue to soften in the very near term. The downward pressure on rates should not be misinterpreted as a long-term softening in the market, but rather as seasonal cyclicality in anticipation of summer.

## Defining Fundamentals
United States refinery utilization has continued to creep up over the last several weeks, edging slightly higher than levels seen this time last year. The chart below shows the refinery utilization as a percentage of operable capacity. Historically, refinery utilization has continued to increase through the summer implying that larger crude oil stock draws are to be expected during the next few months that will result in increased crude oil imports.

## Week 6
At this point, the EIA statistics have shown that gasoline demand has been growing at a healthy pace despite continued high prices. Recent statistics indicate that demand is 2.5% higher than the same period last year while retail prices are nearly 5% more.

## Impending Imports
The clean market has seen strong rates in recent weeks as there has been steady demand for imported gasoline. Going forward, the increases in refining capacity and utilization will continue to create demand for crude oil to replenish stocks and feed domestic refineries.
If import levels continue to follow historic trends, an overall increase in can be expected through the late spring. The chart below shows the crude oil import levels as recorded by the EIA, including that which goes into the Strategic Petroleum Reserve. Last week, imports were similar to the levels in the same period of the last few years.

## Week 19
Rates for larger tankers particularly VLCCs, can be expected to weaken in the near term. As the spring season progresses and summer approaches, demand pressures for crude oil will give rise to firmer rates. The spot market, from a tanker owning perspective, should begin to see some late-spring sunshine.
