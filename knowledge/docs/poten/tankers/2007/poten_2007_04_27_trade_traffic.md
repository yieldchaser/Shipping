## Trade & Traffic
New York City was the scene of an enlightening lunchtime discussion by the current United States Maritime Administrator, Sean Connaughton. The occasion was the quarterly Port of New York Kings Point Fort Schuyler Luncheon. Mr. Connaughton highlighted some of the key challenges that the Maritime Administration (MARAD) is facing, but also some of the opportunities that exist in the United States.
Commercial transportation within the United States is facing many difficult challenges. Strained infrastructure has led to congestion on roadways, ports and the railway system. As shown below, as the domestic economy expands and trade increases, infrastructure conditions are likely to worsen if creative solutions are not found.

## Alternative Means
The areas in the United States that experience the most amount of freight traffic are those that are more densely populated. The I-95 highway corridor that runs between Washington D.C., New York and Boston sees more than 1.2 million hours of truck delays each year. Other areas such as Chicago, Atlanta, Charlotte, Los Angeles and San Francisco are also plagued by heavy amounts of commercial truck traffic. The map below shows some of the main navigable waterways and metropolitan areas in the United States that may be used as alternatives to highways.
MARAD analysis suggests that a typical barge could handle 456 containers. That same amount of cargo would require 228 double stacked railcars and 456 trucks. If one barge would require one unit of energy, where railway would require four units and trucks would require nearly nine units. The efficiency that could be achieved through further use of inland waterways is quite remarkable.

## Making the Investment
Although the use of inland waterways is one way to alleviate road congestion, there are still challenges ahead for the large coastal ports that receive goods from overseas. Expanding the existing ports is a logical solution to reducing congestion and delays, but there are many obstacles preventing these expansions. Namely, the amount of waterfront property is becoming scarcer, projects are costly and there are environmental and regulatory barriers - not to mention public opposition. In New York and New Jersey, for example, much of the land that was once zoned commercial is now re-zoned as residential.
With the United States economy staying healthy, it is anticipated that trade and demand for goods will continue to grow domestically. US ship and barge operators are working to make available creative solutions that avoid the nation's highways. With the help of the Maritime Administration, industry will work to overcome the challenges ahead.
