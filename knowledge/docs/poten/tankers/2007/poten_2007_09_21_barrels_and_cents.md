## Dollar Discounted Crude Oil

## Dollar Equivalent in Local Currency, $/bbl
Spot Brent Bought in US$ Spot Brent Bought by Euros Spot Brent Bought by Chinese Yuan Spot Brent Bought by Indian Rupee By expressing Brent's price evolution in the dollar equivalent to each of the three currencies, the chart above suggests that exchange rate declines have lessened the impact of Brent's price rise relative to these currencies. For example, from last year September to this week, Brent's dollar price rose about 28 percent. But for those countries whose currency is the Euro, the increase was only 17 percent, cushioned by the weaker dollar and its exchange rate. A similar comparison shows a rise of only 21 and 12 percent for the Chinese Yuan and the Indian Rupee, respectively.

## Desperately Seeking Balance
Barring outside forces - such as production quotas - markets usually find some sort of equilibrium. But the demand for petroleum has usually been slow to contract even from what the popular vernacular terms as "high prices." Declining exchange rates seem to partially shield overseas consumers from the full impact of price increases and thereby interfere with the typical resizing of oil demand by price. Should exchange rates continue to subsidize oil's affordability overseas, the use of dollar denominated barrels oil may even lift the price point which finally forces its demand to meet its supply.
