## Summary
Volatility Redux
Owners of tankers are accustomed to weathering spot markets with occasionally bouncy and volatile rates. An analysis of ten year's worth of Arabian Gulf to Far East VLCC Worldscale rates, however, suggests that since 2002 swings in these rates have grown noticeably greater than those of the prior five years. From the following chart, compared to the period 1997 to 2002, the standard deviation - as a gauge of volatility - of monthly rates from 2003 through August 2007 is nearly 60 percent higher.

## Averages Mask Swings
As the chart below illustrates, August 2007's monthly average timecharter equivalent (TCE) rate of $22,000 per day is some $14,000 per day less than the ten year average for that month. Perhaps some comfort can be drawn from the usual rise of both TCE rates and their volatility during the fourth quarter of a year. Of course, the past behavior of seasonal rate trends does not necessarily indicate their level in the future.

## Rate Lethargy
VLCC timecharter equivalent rates for AG to Far East voyages are hovering at their lowest levels experienced so far in 2007 (about $13,000 per day), even though August's fixture activity for this route is above average.

## WE - 01/05/2007
While both rate and volatility late-year statistical trends might repeat themselves again this year, they might not, either. Perhaps a more tangible hope can be placed in OPEC's recent lifting of production quotas also lifting both VLCC fixture activity and rates.
