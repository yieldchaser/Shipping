## The Bottom Line
The annual change in flat rates does not only signify the fluctuations in freight components, but creates increased volatility in tanker owner revenues. The Worldscale rate in the spot market will generally move in 2.5-point increments, but for smaller vessels it is not uncommon to see jumps of 50 WS points in a given day. For larger vessels, even small movements in rates can yield large changes in revenues. For example, consider the voyage Ras Tanura - Yokohama on 260k: a movement from WS 100 to WS 105 using 2006 flat rate of $14.90/MT yields a differential of $193,700 on a lumpsum basis. The same change in rates using 2007 flat rate of $17.43/MT yields a differential of $226,600 on a lumpsum basis. In 2007, incremental changes in the market will certainly have a greater effect on the owner's account.
