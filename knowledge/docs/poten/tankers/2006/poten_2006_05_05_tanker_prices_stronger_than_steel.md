## Tanker Prices: Stronger than Steel

## VLCC Newbuild Price
Newbuild prices are the result of a number of factors, the most influential of which are speculation for strong future earnings, shortness of berth capacity, and rising steel prices.

## Playing By New Rules
On April 1, 2006 Common Structural Rules (CSR) for Tankers took effect and their implementation has placed additional upward pressure on prices.
The CSR was established by a venture of three classification societies, known as the Joint Tanker Project: Lloyd's Register, American Bureau of Shipping and Det Norske Veritas. The initiative was founded in order to establish a basis for ship design and thereby a standard for increased safety. Key design issues addressed by the CSR also include load issues and efforts to stem corrosion. CSR also aims to limit the potential for competition between the classification societies on the basis of minimum safety standards.

## Heavy Lifting
Overall, the amount of steel required for each ship is set to increase between 3-7% depending on the size of the vessel. Given current approximate steel price increase of around $600/ton and an estimated 40,000 tons of steel in a VLCC, expect at least another $1 million increase in prices. When analyzing the likelihood of defects in the vessels trading today, it is clear that this type of a program is necessary. With heightened public awareness of casualty in transit the industry is under a microscope. Safety for crew and cargo are of the utmost importance, for owners and charterers alike. In light of the changes to result from the CSR, many owners seized the opportunity to boost their order books in the beginning of 2006. Looking at the newbuilding activity for the first quarter 2005 versus the first quarter 2006, it can be deduced that some owners were trying to beat the deadline.
