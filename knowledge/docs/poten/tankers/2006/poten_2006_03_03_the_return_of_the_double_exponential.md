## Summary
The Return of the "Double Exponential
For the first time in 20 years we are seeing the re-emergence of the "Double Exponential." The Double Exponential occurs when energy consumption growth not only reflects exponential growth in population, but also exponential growth in per capita energy (or oil) consumption. By plotting world energy consumption along with world population, we can see that energy growth outpaced population growth until the oil crisis in 1973 (actually this relationship had been true for a long time previous to the oil crisis). Growth in both per capita energy consumption and in population boosts energy growth and hence the "Double Exponential" curve.

## > Population Growth
After the oil crisis of 1973, the world entered an era of high cost energy where efficiency and conservation heavily influenced consumption. Moreover the historical linkage between economic and energy growth was broken. Before the oil crisis, 5% economic growth meant 5% energy growth; today the relationship is 5% economic growth and about 3% energy growth in the United States and about 1% energy growth in Europe. Part of the disconnect was the U.S. and European economies shifting from a focus on manufacturing to a focus on service. A Flat Trend Is Broken The following curve highlights two periods when rapid growth in per capita energy was substantially cut off: in 1974 and again in 1979-1983. These were occurrences of peak oil prices, the latter during the Iranian Revolution. Per capita consumption more or less leveled off after this at about 10.5 barrels per person per year until 2000. Now per capita consumption appears to be having another spurt.

## Oil Price Spikes
The surge in per capita energy consumption means that energy demand is now growing faster than population. Focusing on oil, the chart on the next page shows annual per capita consumption for four select nations. The difference in per capita consumption between the established and emerging economies is quite stark.

## What Happens If?
China and India represent a total population of about 2.5 billion in a world of 6.5 billion. They are starting from an extremely low per capita consumption of oil. If these nations remain on track with their economic development, we will see oil supplies continuing to be strained. Assuming that the oil comes from somewhere, and this may be a problem, tanker cargoes will be growing and heading more eastward than westward, particularly Atlantic basin cargoes. If you really want to blow your (tanker) mind, think of what oil consumption would be like if India and China achieved only half the level of economic activity of Taiwan or Korea. We'll leave this to your imagination for now.
