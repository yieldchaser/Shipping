## Caveat Emptor
The review each year of the largest charterers for both clean products and crude oil (see the market opinion of January 6 th ) generates some very interesting commentary from the industry. Owners are curious to know more about the locations, dates, and sizes of cargoes that are moving in the market. Charterers invariably comment that the report significantly understates the volume of business that they conduct in the spot market. One must keep in mind that the spot market fixtures in our reports only take into account fixtures that are actually reported- those done in the "market" arena. We do not have access to information relating to fixtures that are done directly between charterers and owners, those that move under contracts of affreightment or with direct arrangements in a tanker pool. Charterers also fix ships on consecutive voyages where only the first voyage is of market knowledge and subsequent voyages are not recorded. We clearly understate the number of actual fixtures for charterers, but assess that their relative standing is still valid.
With These Caveats in Mind... The chart on the next page illustrates the ten most active spot clean charterers in 2005. Their cumulative activity accounted for 39% of all fixtures. The top charterers holding the top three positions remain unchanged with respect to last year's standings, but other charterers in the top 10 have shuffled positions .

## PDI
Of the 418 fixtures, 64% or 268 loaded in the Arabian Gulf. Most were naphtha cargoes to Japan and other destinations in Asia, but there were 49 clean cargoes to northern Europe. 54 fixtures loaded in the UKC of which 33 were destined to the United States in response to increasing clean product imports. Roughly 20% of the 160 Aframaxes currently on order are coated indicating owners' perception of increased involvement in clean trading.

## Panamaxes
There were 1,487 Panamax spot fixtures, of which 938 were dirty. The ten leading charterers for the 549 clean spot fixtures are shown below. Collectively, these charterers also accounted for 43% of all Panamax clean spot fixtures during 2005.

## Titan
298 of the 549 fixtures or 54% were AG liftings of which 51 were to northern Europe with most of the remainder to Japan and other Asian destinations. The next largest movement is 74 fixtures between the UKC and the US, again in response to growing U.S. imports. A whopping 82 of the 119 Panamaxes on order are coated indicating that the past employment trade of fuel oil and other dirty cargoes may be not where future employment lies.

## Handymaxes
Of the total of 3,028 spot fixtures involving Handymaxes, 2,661 were clean. The top ten charterers of clean Handymax fixtures accounted for 43% of all fixtures.
The United States dominates Handymax employment with 432 fixtures between the UKC and the United States and 414 fixtures between the Caribbean and the United States accounting for nearly a third of all fixtures. The next largest category is intra-Asia that accounts for 641 or nearly one quarter of all fixtures.

## Conclusion
The total number of clean spot fixtures increased from 2004 to 2005 by a relatively small number (about 70 fixtures). Looking ahead in 2006, the number of fixtures is expected to be well above 2005 levels as refining capacity in the United States remains at full capacity requiring increased imports to meet incremental demand. Moreover continued growth in clean products demand in India and China will account for more cargoes, although future expansion of refinery capacity may eventually have an impact on the product supply/demand balance.
