## 2006 IEA Outlook: Let the Good Times Roll?
2006 IEA Outlook: Let the Good Times Roll? July 7, 2006 The International Energy Agency (IEA)'s latest Oil Market Report forecasts worldwide oil demand to reach 84.9 million barrels per day (bpd) over 2006, a 1.5% increase over last year's 83.66 million bpd. One major feature of this outlook is the United States' limited participation in demand growth. This outlook shows US demand increasing by just 170 thousand bpd, or by 0.8% over 2005. Other OECD demand growth is also weak. By contrast, non-OECD demand grows by over 3%. This week we take a look at what this outlook means for tanker demand and charter rates.

## non-OECD Demand
The IEA's oil demand projection is consistent with an outlook recently published by the Oil and Gas Journal. Both call for stronger worldwide demand while anticipating slow US demand. One major reason is oil prices: the Oil and Gas Journal anticipates that US domestic oil prices will jump 5.5% from $50.23 in 2005 to $53 on average in 2006.

## Middle East
Africa As shown above, the IEA's forecast hinges on high rates of growth in China and the Middle East (including India). The two regions account for more than half of the total demand increase forecasted for full-year 2006. The First Half 2006 Tanker Market The level of oil demand is only one of the drivers of charter hire rates. Changing trade patterns are perhaps an even larger factor. As non-OECD countries have widened their search for oil supplies, there has been high growth in VLCC fixtures bound for the Far East and India loading in the AG, West Africa, and the Caribbean. These longer-haul routes have driven ton- mile demand upwards and boosted tanker rates. While oil demand grew by just 0.8%, ton-mile demand increased by a robust 14.3% (see table below). This in turn boosted time charter equivalents by 6.75%. 1 High VLCC rates have in turn brought up rates on smaller vessels, as well.

## What Lies Ahead
The outlook for the rest of 2006 is for stronger charter hire rates to predominate. The IEA forecasts 2H2006 to reach 85.51 million bpd, a 2.1% increase over 2H2005 demand of 83.76 million bpd. But this outlook is not without certain risks.
As yet the level of oil prices have not caused consumers to significantly curtail their oil demand. But higher product specifications (i.e. Ethanol and Ultra-Low Sulfur Diesel standards) and potential supply disruptions from hurricanes in the US Gulf could all conspire to drive oil prices to such stratospheric heights. At the same time, continuing strength in charter hire rates depends on high-growth countries such as China and India purchasing oil from distant suppliers, thus maintaining high ton-mile demand levels.
If the IEA outlook proves correct, and barring extraordinary developments, VLCC rates should therefore maintain their high levels, and tanker rates for other vessel sizes should also remain high for the rest of 2006.
