## Turning on the Taps
When considering crude production in West Africa, it is often a first inclination to consider what is not being produced. Civil unrest and other supply disruptions, specifically in Nigeria, have been the focus of attention for several months. The reality is that as this region is becoming increasingly important for meeting global oil demand, many of the producers are stepping up to the task. Once shut-in production is being returned, new projects are coming online and freight rates have been on a general upward trend since April.

The Proof is in the Rates In July and so far in August, there has been a significant increase in reported spot liftings out of West Africa. VLCCs saw a reported 34 spot market liftings in July as compared to 32 the month prior. 83 Suezmax spot liftings were reported for July, compared to only 58 for June.

## Time Charter Equivalent WAF - USAC (130kt)
The chart above shows the Suezmax time charter equivalents (TCE) for voyages West Africa to the U.S. Atlantic Coast. For the first half of this year rates mirrored those seen in 2005. Since June, however, rates have increased substantially when compared to the same period a year ago. The high demand for tonnage due to the increase in cargos will continue to put upward pressure on rates.

## Ensuring Supply
While disruptions in Nigeria have plagued many of the long-existing channels, replacement supply has recently started to come through other terminals in Nigeria, Erha and Bonga in particular. As the region regains lost production more cargoes will flow from these African nations. The quality and quantity of the crude and position within the global trading patterns will further this region's importance for both shipowners and charterers alike.
