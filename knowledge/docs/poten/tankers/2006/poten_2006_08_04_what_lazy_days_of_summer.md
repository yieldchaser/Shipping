## What Lazy Days of Summer?
For most of the United States, this past week saw one of the seasonal characteristics of summer- a brutal heat wave. This follows on from the heat wave that gripped Europe a few weeks ago. While most things slow to an easy pace during this weather, the tanker market, refinery operations and those that work in the industry are realizing that there may not be any "lazy days of summer" this year.
The Gulf Coast states, aside from being blanketed by heat, are also getting prepared for the impending hurricane season. The threat of Tropical Storm Chris, although no longer of imminent concern, served as a reminder that hurricane season may be closer than perhaps anticipated.

## Up and Running
U.S. refiners have been aggressively restoring capacity since outages resulting from the past hurricane season. Refinery utilization has now consistently been over 90% for two months, levels not seen since a year ago August.

## % Utilization
Today, refineries are currently processing over 17.3 million barrels per day of crude, higher than the average operating capacity for 2005 of 16.6 million barrels per day and better than the average for 2004 of 16.8 million barrels per day. Products Craze Even with refinery capacity at relative highs as compared to the last year, the demand for imported products is still very strong. According to the EIA, the last week saw an estimated 4.5 million barrels per day of imported products move to the U.S. This is an increase of roughly 40% as compared to imports in the last week of June. Of the total imports, 1.4 million barrels of those- over 30% - entered through the US Gulf Coast. This type of activity has contributed to higher rates for product carriers moving to the U.S. Gulf. In the past month, rates have increased 50 Worldscale points for Caribbean to U.S. Gulf and 60 WS points for UK Continent to U.S. Atlantic Coast. Based on 38,000 MT parcel size, this translates to an increase in earnings of roughly $6,800 to $24,400 per day from the Caribbean and $7,500 to a healthy $26,200 per day from Europe. Driving Demand As summer is typically the high season for gasoline consumption, product stocks are lower as a result of increased demand and the changeover from MTBE to ethanol blended gasoline. ## (000 Barrels)
2006 - Gasoline Stocks 2005 - Gasoline Stocks 2006 - Prices 2005 - Prices In terms of total gasoline stocks, this year looks very similar to 2005, but the price of gasoline is much higher. With the relatively tight stock levels of today, there is less of a cushion in the face of hurricane season. Given that the forward market for gasoline is in backwardation, meaning that future prices are lower than current spot prices, there is less incentive to build up storage. The effects of a hurricane are physically devastating to a region, but with today's supply picture being what it is, expect the upward price implications on gasoline at the pump to be just as drastic.
