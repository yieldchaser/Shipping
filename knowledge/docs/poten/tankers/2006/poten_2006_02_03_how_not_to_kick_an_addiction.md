## How Not to Kick an Addiction
For those of you around the world who could stay up late enough to watch President Bush's State of the Union on Tuesday, it was some great theatre. The President correctly pointed out that "America is addicted to oil", but fell well short of offering any type of real solutions that would actually move the United States closer to anything resembling energy independence. In summary, the President's principal aim is not to reduce demand for oil, but to "make our dependence on Middle Eastern oil a thing of the past." This 30 year mantra has been repeatedly uttered by nearly every president since the oil crisis in 1973. President Bush proposed several solutions, including reliance on technology to enhance gasoline mileage efficiency and greater production of ethanol. Imports of ethanol are currently prohibited as a gasoline additive as only domestically produced ethanol made from corn can be used. Ethanol in gasoline as a substitute for MBTE is viewed as a not so hidden subsidy for corn growers. The President proposes that ethanol made from agricultural wastes and non-agricultural crops be allowed into the gasoline stream. This would probably not raise the heckles of the corn lobby because ethanol from cellulose rather than sugar and grains is a technology under development. Furthermore since vapor pressure considerations limit the ethanol content in gasoline, no one need worry about a massive influx of ethanol from cellulose into the U.S. gasoline stream. The best part was that he proposed to accomplish all of this by spending the equivalent of about two VLCC newbuildings. Are Americans Addicted to Gasoline? Absolutely! The chart on the next page shows weekly gasoline consumption and gasoline prices since last summer. Gasoline consumption is calculated as the total of gasoline produced by domestic refineries, gasoline imports, and changes to gasoline inventories. A decrease in gasoline inventories adds to consumption whereas an increase in gasoline inventories reduces consumption.

## Impact Katrina & Rita
The chart shows that for gasoline below $3 per gallon, automobile usage is independent of price. We drove more in the summer as gasoline prices rose because this was the time to take the family on a vacation. We drove less in the autumn as gasoline prices fell because the kids were back in school. However although hybrid car sales spurted and large sized SUVs sales declined, America's favorite automobile is still large sized SUVs. But price discounting on large sized SUVs was enough to send Ford and General Motors into the red.
The Only Way to Break an Addiction... ... is to raise the threshold of pain to a level that breaking an addiction is preferable to continuing it. People break the smoking and alcohol habits when their consequences overwhelm their enjoyment. With regard to gasoline consumption, there is only one threshold of pain that Americans will respond to- a hefty price increase. Considering that they did not respond to $3 per gallon prices last summer, prices between $4 or $5 will probably be necessary for people to begin changing their driving habits to cut the gasoline addiction.
The irony of the situation is that the President has a ready reason to raise gasoline prices. Economists call it internalizing an externality. The externality is the hundreds of billions of dollars being spent for oil security: the cost of the war in Iraq. If this cost were transformed into an oil security tax on gasoline, the addiction might not be broken, but certainly weakened. For reasons that must certainly escape Europeans who pay $6 or so for a gallon of gasoline, no American politician has the intestinal fortitude to even suggest an effective cure for an addiction. It's all a matter of palliatives today as it was yesterday as it most probably will be tomorrow- no politician intent on reelection is interested in a cure.

## Cutting Middle East Imports
The reality is that the Middle East is not a major supplier of crude oil to the United States. As seen on the next page, Saudi Arabia is a source of about 14% of U.S. crude oil imports, Iraq about 5%, Kuwait about 2% and other Middle East 1% for a total of 22%. Current imports of 2.2 million bpd, if reduced by 75%, is equivalent to 1.7 million bpd. And the President wants to set the achievement goal data of 2025. Some sacrifice! Saudi Arabia and Kuwait have been a staunch allies of the United States, have acted responsibly in the past to maintain crude oil prices within some reasonable range, and certainly must be as dismayed with Iran's nuclear intentions as the United States and Europe. If the United States is worried about security of supply, a couple of Atlantic basin exporters should be viewed with a wary eye, not the Middle East.
