## Peak Oil? Not in Kuwait!
The issue of peak oil has received considerable attention over the past year or so as crude prices remain significantly above their long term averages. One country that is taking action to be sure that the world is not running out of oil is Kuwait.

## In The Beginning
Kuwait's geographic location at the top of the Arabian Gulf helped establish the country as a trading hub. Oil was first discovered in the 1930s and the first export of crude oil was in 1946. Kuwait has shared in the ups and downs of the oil market since then, but with clear vision, strong leadership and fairly strict adherence to goals and plans, Kuwait today is a stabilizing force in the world crude oil market.

## Keeping up the Pace
With estimated reserves at about 100 billion barrels, Kuwait holds about 10% of the world's reserves. Despite producing over 2.0 million barrels per day for the past several years, the proven reserves have remained steady.
By the end of 2005 the country's production reached five-year highs at about 2.6 million barrels per day. Estimates are that sustainable crude production capacity for Kuwait is currently around 2.6 million bpd, but Kuwait has much more aggressive plans for its major export - crude oil.

## Ambitious Plans - Project Kuwait
Kuwait's primary fields are those known as Greater Burgan and those fields under exploration in the North of the country. Greater Burgan is thought to be the second largest deposit of oil in the world. Production out of Greater Burgan, comprised of Magwa, Burgan and Ahmadi fields, is around 1.6 million bpd making up the major source of the country's current output. But Kuwait has very ambitious expansion plans over the next 15 to 20 years that call for capital expenditures of an estimated $40 billion. It is expected that with this type of investment, the total production from Kuwait will reach the project's goal of 4.0 million barrels per day by 2020. The map on the next page shows the relative sizes of the fields as well as some of the current production. With careful planning and cooperation, it is expected that these fields will keep Kuwait on pace for meeting the challenges of meeting the worlds increasing demand for crude oil and refined products.

## Key Role in Refining
Refineries at Mina Abdulla, Mina Al-Almadi and Shuaiba combine for a total crude refining capacity of about 885 k bpd. Strategic plans call for expanded refining capacity with the construction of a new refinery at Al Zour. This is intended to increase the total refining capacity for Kuwait to over 1.4 million barrels per day. The Al Zour refinery is expected to start production in 2010 and is intended to support local power plants with half of its throughput in the form of low-sulphur fuel oil. Although domestic product consumption will increase, crude recovery and supply will continue to boost exports.
In December 2005, Kuwait signed a memorandum of understanding with PetroChina for the construction of a petrochemical plant and refinery in the Guangdong province. The refinery is slated to have production capacity of between 200-400,000 bpd and a total cost estimated at $5 billion. Kuwait is also being considered as a possible partner for Sinopec for the expansion of the refinery at Maoming, also in the Guangdong province. Kuwait is also keenly interested in expanding its refining into the United States and has been seeking a partner to help develop a site for a planned 400,000 barrel per day refinery that would meet increased demand.

## Helping Crude and Products Get to the Market
Kuwait Petroleum Company (KPC) has a strong presence in the spot market where they are among the top 5 for the crucial Arabian Gulf/West trade. In addition to spot and term chartering VLCC's, KPC maintains an active presence in the clean products and fuel oil market. KOTC, the Kuwait Government owned tanker-company serves as the transportation arm for KPC. The company is currently undergoing a newbuilding program for VLCC's and product tankers that will keep it in the forefront of safety and technological innovation.

## The Future Looks Bright
With long-term crude production plans in place, sophisticated refinery expansions and grass roots refining projects on schedule, aggressive international refinery expansion plans, a well respected worldwide marketing group, Kuwait will be at the forefront of meeting the "Peak Oil" challenge.
