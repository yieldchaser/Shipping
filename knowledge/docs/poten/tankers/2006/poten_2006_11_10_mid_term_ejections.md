## Mid-Term Ejections
Whether one identifies with the Republicans or the Democrats, this week's turn of events in Washington, DC was certainly interesting. The 2006 Mid-Term Elections saw Democrats take the majority in both the Senate (51%) and the House of Representatives (54%) for the remainder of George W. Bush's term in office. The effects of the re- balance of power will undoubtedly be widespread. As the Democratic Party has many key initiatives to enact, Republicans and the President will have a more difficult time implementing their existing agenda. The map below represents the Senatorial victories for those states where elections were held. The states that are shown in dark blue saw a change in representation from Republican to Democrat, where the lighter shaded states did not see a change in the representative Party.
Aside from the rest of the American public, the business community is especially eager to see if and how the business environment may change. With energy at the forefront of many domestic and foreign policies, the oil and gas industry is especially anxious.

## Changing the Committees
Perhaps the most drastic initial makeover will be in the House of Representatives committee leadership. A pervasive concern with the anticipated changes is the amount of oversight that is likely to occur, drawing out the legislative process. The comprehensive nature of many of today's issues mean they are likely to be scrutinized by various committees. Energy and Commerce, Government Reform, International Relations, Ways and Means, Resources and Environment and Public Works are all likely to see dramatic new views of their leaders.
The domestic energy companies will be impacted by this change of power as some prominent members of the House of Representatives have expressed their opinions on key issues facing the industry. Legislation that has protected companies that pursue geological development refinery upgrades and exploration may come under review. Curtailing tax incentives for oil companies are likely to be on the agenda for some Representatives.

## Understanding Industry
Issues concerning climate change, emissions and alternative fuel sources are also likely to be at the forefront of discussions. These issues are likely to be addressed by both Democrats and Republicans alike- perhaps finding common ground. While the creation of various incentives may work in the short-term, the market for new fuels technology, such as bio fuel and ethanol, will only be regulated in the long-term by supply and demand. As many in the industry know, policy makers are not necessarily champions of practical and economic solutions. Often the solutions that work are not enacted by legislative decree, but are brought about by forces in the market.
