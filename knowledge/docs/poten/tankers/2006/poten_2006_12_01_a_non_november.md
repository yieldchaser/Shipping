## A Non-November
The VLCC spot market has followed up on a very strong third quarter with an extremely weak fourth quarter to-date. Strong summer rates, at a time of the year when rates have been relatively weak historically, may have created some false hope about the freight prospects for the end of the year. There was much reason to believe that this year's fourth quarter was poised to be just as strong as 2004 and 2005. The chart below shows the historical fourth-quarter time charter equivalents for VLCCs trading Arabian Gulf to the US Gulf on 280kt. During this same week in 2004 and 2005, rates were in the $100,000 per day range. In November of this year, rates averaged $37,400 per day. While charterers are enjoying a break in transportation costs, many owners are asking, "What happened to the fourth quarter?"

## Still Stocked
The combination of low refinery utilization and high crude oil stocks has led to lower demand for crude oil imports in the US. The chart below shows refinery utilization. Currently, refinery utilization is 88%, versus 90% in November of 2005 and 94% in November 2004.

## Refinery Utilization (%)
Crude oil stocks in the United States are still well above historic levels at over 340 million barrels. The chart below shows crude oil stocks in the United States.
Demand for refined products remains strong in the United States. Low refinery utilization has led to a drawing down of product stocks. According to the EIA, gasoline demand is 9.3 million barrels per day, 100,000 barrels higher than demand a year ago. Strong demand has most recently led to increased activity in the product tanker market, especially in terms of gasoline movements from Europe.
The US refining system peaked in utilization in September of this year at nearly 94%. This 6% drop in refinery utilization from September to this week represents a decrease in crude throughput of 1.02 million barrels per day. With demand for refined products expected to remain strong, refiners will work to maximize output and restore utilization. The increase in refinery utilization will draw down crude stocks at which point increased crude oil imports will be required.
