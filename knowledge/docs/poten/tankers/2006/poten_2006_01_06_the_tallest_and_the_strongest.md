## The Tallest and the Strongest
Now that the 2005 National Championship College Football (American style) have been settled it's time to look at the Worldwide Chartering Championship for the oil and dirty petroleum (DPP) market. Surprisingly, the total activity in the sector is essentially unchanged from last year. Total dirty spot fixtures rose by just 19 fixtures from 10,412 in 2004 to 10,433 in 2005.
Fixture volume for all vessel sizes largely unchanged in 2005 versus 2004: none of the vessel sizes carrying dirty cargoes saw volume changes of greater than 100 fixtures. Aframaxes gained the most ground, with 85 more reported fixtures in 2005 than in 2004. VLCCs lost the most ground, losing 78 reported fixtures in 2005 versus 2004.

## Top Charterers
Although Texas won the Football championship, they would only hold the number three spot with London based companies the clear winners. The top ten charterers for dirty fixtures are shown below. The major oil companies dominate the list with Shell, BP, and ExxonMobil taking top places and accounting for 17% of all dirty fixtures. Consistent with prior years, the top ten charterers account for over 40%, and the top 20 more than half of all fixtures.

## VLCCs
The top ten charterers for VLCCs in 2005 are shown in the chart on the following page. Cumulatively, they account for 44% of the 1,797 spot fixtures. Shell, ExxonMobil, and Unipec were the largest spot charterers of VLCCs in 2005.

## Suezmaxes
The top ten charterers for Suezmaxes account for 47% of the 2,132 dirty spot fixtures in 2005. Sun, Shell, BP, and Total were the most active spot charterers of Suezmaxes in 2005

## Aframaxes
The top ten charterers for Aframaxes account for 45% of 4,641 dirty spot fixtures in 2005. Shell, Vitol, and BP were the predominant charterers of dirty spot Aframax vessels in 2005.

## Panamaxes
The top ten charterers for Panamaxes account for 63% of the 858 dirty spot fixtures in 2005. Glencore, Ursa, Shell, and Valero were the leading charterers of spot dirty Panamaxes in 2005.

## Conclusion
Following robust fixture growth in 2004, spot market volumes remained largely unchanged in 2005 across major vessel sizes. With fixture volume at such strong levels, overall vessel demand is high and rates are up. Many leading charterers changed places but did not replace each other over 2005 versus 2004, with major integrated oil companies holding onto strong rankings. With incremental demand forecast to grow in 2006, it is likely that spot market figures will be higher and a potential new Worldwide Chartering Champion crowned.
