## BTC
(Bound To China?) The loading of 600,000 barrels of Azeri crude from the terminus of the Baku-Tbilisi-Ceyhan (BTC) pipeline marked an important milestone in the crude oil and potentially the tanker markets. "At capacity" is a commonly used phrase when discussing today's worldwide crude and refined products markets. It's no secret that with very little spare capacity, crude markets have been increasingly jittery. However, the operational commencement of the BTC pipeline may alleviate some pressure on oil prices, while increasing demand for tanker tonnage.

## Baku-Tbilisi-Ceyhan Pipeline
It's A Long Way From Baku...
Starting in Azerbaijan at Baku, the pipeline runs through Tbilisi, Georgia ending up at Ceyhan, a Turkish port on the eastern side of the Mediterranean. The pipeline runs just over 1,000 miles and the pipeline itself has a capacity of 10 million barrels of oil. The inventory holding cost of filling this pipeline is about $700 million at today's crude prices. It will take several months to reach throughput capacity of 1 million barrels per day, as enough pressure must be generated to move the massive amount of cargo. It is estimated that the pipeline will meet intended capacity by the end of 2006. ...And Even Longer to China The type of crude to be produced from the pipeline is relatively light and low in sulfur, similar to the crude produced in West Africa. The crude is expected to end up in refineries on the East Coast of the United States as well as Europe and even the Far East- particularly China. Due to proximity, some refineries in the Mediterranean will also be looking into these cargoes, although these refineries are typically designed for heavier crude.

## Fixtures Ex- Ceyhan
The chart on the previous page shows the historical number of fixtures out of Ceyhan for Suezmaxes and VLCCs. The number of liftings has declined significantly as supply to the port from the Iraq-Turkey Crude Pipeline has experienced interruption in the past few years. This year has not seen any spot market fixtures!
Current spot tanker market activity in the Mediterranean, including the Black Sea, tends to be on an intra-zone basis as most cargoes stay in the Mediterranean. In the past twelve months there have been approximately 700 Suezmax fixtures and 50 VLCC fixtures to load in the MED. Over 30 Suezmax and 3 VLCC fixtures were to East of Suez.

## West to East
The main driver for tanker trade activity is the incremental quantity of crude being transported to existing and new destinations. Since the Caspian Sea crude is light and sweet it could create a new trade to the Far East, specifically China who has exhibited growing demand for sweet crude. The tonnage of choice for this new trade route will be Suezmaxes as these vessels can transit the Suez Canal fully laden. With an oil market that seems well supplied with crude but has prices at historical highs, it would seem that this incremental supply will be welcome. With a tanker market that also appears well supplied but has rates at levels above historical averages, this incremental demand will keep rates firm in the Atlantic Basin especially if the crude oil heads east.
