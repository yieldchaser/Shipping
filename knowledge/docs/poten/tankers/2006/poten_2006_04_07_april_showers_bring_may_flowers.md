## Summary
April Showers Bring May Flowers
Now that the bottom seems to have fallen-out of the spot tanker market, owners, charters and investors have been politely asking, "What's next?" A brief analysis of the past couple of year's market may shed some light. For the past five years the second and third quarters have been significantly weaker than the first and fourth. If the first week of April is any indication 2006 will be following this trend.

## April Showers Bring May Flowers
Now that the bottom seems to have fallen-out of the spot tanker market, owners, charters and investors have been politely asking, "What's next?" A brief analysis of the past couple of year's market may shed some light. For the past five years the second and third quarters have been significantly weaker than the first and fourth. If the first week of April is any indication 2006 will be following this trend.

## What happened?
It seems hard to believe, but just three weeks ago, spot rates were at WS 100 and daily earnings were over $50,000 per day. Today rates for a voyage from the Arabian Gulf are currently at levels averaging just below WS 60 pushing earnings down to around $25,000/day. It's Not The Volume The chart below shows the total number of reported spot liftings per month out of the Arabian Gulf by discharge destination. The average number of spot liftings for the first thee months of 2005 and 2006 are essentially the same, about 104 per month. However, if last year provides any signal of what to look for in the next few months, expect to see an increase in the number of liftings to the West for the remainder of the spring and summer.

## WEST
EAST Based on reported March fixtures, 18 liftings are already scheduled for April for the long-haul voyages from the AG to the UK Continent, US Gulf and Canada. This is slightly better than in the past. These long-haul voyages significantly increase ton-mile demand.

## Stacking Up!
It should not come as a surprise that the number of vessels available in the Arabian Gulf in the next 30 days is significantly larger today than just a couple of months ago. Rates that typically experience upward pressure because of poor weather conditions may be experiencing some springtime relief. Improved weather in the US Gulf creates an environment for more timely lightering and overall improved discharge time for VLCCs and Suezmaxes.

## Spring Has Sprung
It was not until the end of May last year that rates dropped below WS 60 as hard as it may be to believe they actually went briefly below WS 50 before strongly recovering. The underlying demand for crude remains strong and the factors causing today's weak spot market are seasonal in nature. The fundamental shift to long-term weakness that some are expecting has not quite arrived.
