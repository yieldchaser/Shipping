## Slumdogs of the Caribbean
The Caribbean market has been up and down, but more of the latter since the opening of 2009. Though for shipowners near and dear to the West Indies, the simple economics of supply and demand are playing a tougher game these days.
Aframaxes and Panamaxes have demonstrated the ability to earn over $60,000 and $50,000 per day; respectively, but have been in the sub-$10,000 per day range recently. The chart below shows the monthly average rates for Aframaxes and Panamaxes on the Caribbean - US Gulf trade. The historically volatile market has slid into a lull on the back of crude market fundamentals and subsequent lighter activity across the board.
PLC - Houston Aframax 70kt TCE PLC - Houston Panamax 50kt TCE A slower market provides the opportunity to take a closer look at the rate progression week-on-week. The chart below shows a glimpse of the flat-lining of the Caribbean - US Gulf market on Aframaxes and Panamaxes to current day levels of $7,500 and $14,300 per day, respectively. Only in the past three days have Panamaxes shown signs of revival with a few spot fixtures that pushed the market up above $10,000 per day for the first time in weeks. Perhaps, more striking is the course being carved by Aframax and Panamax demurrage rates at levels far exceeding earnings.

## Panamax Demurrage
Aframax Demurrage In February to date, demurrage assessments for Aframaxes and Panamaxes have been 2.2 and 2.7 times that of daily timecharter equivalent earnings on average. Due to crude storage, lower refinery runs and less crude production in the region, the Caribbean has become a stagnant market with each part of the supply chain waiting for the downstream link to open up some demand. The current slow nature of crude movements has caused shipowners and charterers alike to slow down ship movements, sitting on cargoes with the ability to collect demurrage or take advantage of the spot to fixture crude price relationship.

## All's Quiet
The Caribbean tanker is quiet for now and is certainly at the mercy of the barrel price. Without the flow of crude oil greasing the wheels of the US refining system, stocks will continue to build. At some point the forward price structure must react to an amply supplied market. At such a point, the release of stocks into the system may cause the Caribbean market to once again be on the go.
