## Changing Almost in a Blink
As the Obama Administration continues to rev its policy engines, sacred cows of many self-interest groups may become road-kill on the United States' bio-fueled road towards energy independence. For instance, by the Administration's willingness to reconsider whether states should set auto emission limits stricter than the Environmental Protection Agency's, the bedraggled US automotive industry is now being haunted by the de facto spectre of producing cars with greater fuel efficiency. In the fuel camp, word from the EPA on how to incorporate a lifecycle analysis of the carbon footprint of fuels used to meet the Renewable Fuel Standard, is awaited anxiously. The decision was deferred by the Bush Administration. Resolution by the Obama Administration will signal where it stands in the evolving international debate surrounding bio-fuel sustainability. By using an objective criterion such as lifecycle analysis to assess overall economic and environmental impact of fuel, the air might quickly be cleared of any marketing claims of renewability that are even remotely misleading.
While expanding use of home-grown fuels was a theme during 2008's Presidential election campaign, their presence grows sharply through 2020 under the current renewable fuels standard even without a new mandate to push additional bio-fuel volumes into gasoline.

## Shoehorning in Ethanol
To keep reformulated and conventional gasoline's ethanol content at no more than its present level of 10 percent in 2020, either an increase in the number of vehicles using E85 fuel (85 percent ethanol in hydrocarbon gasoline) or an increase in gasoline consumption is necessary. With better auto gas mileage a centerpiece of the new Administration's push towards energy independence, gasoline consumption might not see too much of a lift. Bear in mind, though, that ethanol's poorer mileage efficiency will bloat gasoline's apparent use. If the fleet of E85 vehicles does not expand significantly by the time 2020 rolls around, the experience of other ethanol- fueling nations could likely be adapted to handle concentrations higher than 10 percent in ordinary US automobiles.
Whether or not it is either technologically possible or economically practical to perfect advanced bio-fuels and expand their production quickly is irrelevant. It is a government's prerogative to drag technology and industry into realms deemed to be important strategically.

## In the Year 2020
What lies ahead for the tanker industry servicing the US in 2020?
Given indications made on last year's campaign trail, one can envisage a landscape where US gasoline is forced to ingest quantities of ethanol even greater than those presently mandated. This landscape could also see the accumulation of better gasoline mileage vehicles in the fleet squelching gasoline consumption. As US refineries in the future twist their processing to chase more and more diesel production, they may find themselves also producing hydrocarbon-based gasoline blendstocks unwanted in domestic markets. Hence, it does not seem unreasonable to speculate that the "good old days" of copious volumes of US gasoline exports might return - enter product tankers - if overseas markets appear. Clearly, any change towards gasoline exports will evolve over time. So it seems safe to blink - at least for now!
