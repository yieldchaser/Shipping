## All the President's Men
This week the global market continued to feel the weight of the 800 pound gorilla pressing down, dashing any hopes of a near-term economic recovery. The Dow Jones Industrial Average closed below the 8,000 level by the end of the week - sadly much farther below last February 2008's 12,000+ levels. Facing the stark economic conditions, the US and world citizens are looking to their fiscal and governmental leaders for solutions before the wounds to their wallets become irreparable. Now as the economic fumbles of Lehman Brothers, Bernie Madoff and other Wall Street giants are moved to the news media's back burner, many Americans have shifted their gaze to their new Chief Executive Officer.
In the midst of the turmoil, it is interesting to note how Big Oil has fared comparatively. Although oil companies have produced a broad spectrum of results a few have weathered the stings of the economic downturn and managed to maintain a rigid backbone. It may be times like this when the "Change" train of President Obama is tested on the pledges of pulling away from black gold. While many American industries reach out to the Federal purse for handouts, it is unlikely Big Oil will engender much Washingtonian sympathy.

## Infrastructure and Science
Tax Relief Legislators commonly load these acts with pet projects or seemingly incongruous initiatives to grease their constituencies. A plan as all encompassing as this one touches on most sectors of life and work for Americans. With its political hands generally tied, the oil industry awaits its fate. Related industries also stand to be impacted as a result of the act, primarily transportation.
Across the House, Senate and Obama Administration it seems many are still in denial of the fact that America is a petroleum and hydrocarbon based economy. Without a clear-cut realistic energy policy in place, haphazard 'green' spending is even worse, taking money that could be more wisely spent elsewhere in this uncertain economic time, or better yet - saved!

## Crawl to Run
In an attempt to apply historical good business practice to today's debacle, hearken back to the founding fathers of the fossil fuel industry. A concept that brought unparalleled success was simply to build then refine. Before the struggling United States economy can learn a new energy dance, it must first learn how to walk again.
At this time, $43 billion has been allocated to the energy industry, with $3.4 billion of that specifically for "Fossil Energy Research and Development," though it is uncertain how much support will find its way to existing oil and gas companies and their workforce. Instead of jamming through short-sighted legislation with potentially hazardous long-term impacts, the US government could take a lesson from the leaders of the oil industry; in times of trouble look to cut costs. Living beyond our means through irresponsible and rampant spending sounds like the type of behavior that got us here in the first place.
