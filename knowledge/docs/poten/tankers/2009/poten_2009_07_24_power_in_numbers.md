## Power in Numbers
As if an extension of the arm of US bureaucracy, the tanker market is slugging along relentlessly with freight levels capable of beating down even the most optimistic. Unlike the US Congress which takes summer recess for the month of August, tankers are here to stay. However, after July's routing of positive returns on both the dirty and clean side ‐ which left earnings on some voyages with negative earnings ‐ the futilitarian thought process may have many shipowners looking for a break from what could be a long month ahead.
If this is this case then why haven't tankers started to lay‐up? From a one‐dimensional perspective, a standard voyage timecharter equivalent calculator will tell us that VLCCs traveling from the Arabian Gulf (AG) to the Louisiana Offshore Oil Platform (LOOP) in the US Gulf on Worldscale 25.0 will earn roughly $4,000 per day round trip in the current market. At these levels, shipowners are hard pressed to come up with cash for financing and even standard day‐to‐day operating costs such as crewing, maintenance, etc. However, with the use of clever chartering tactics to increase utilization, returns could be buoyed up a few thousand dollars.
In the map below is an example triangulation pattern for a VLCC originating out of the AG. In this trade technique, the vessel is able to steam laden to the LOOP terminal, ballast to West Africa, load cargo and steam to China, then ballast back to the AG.

## Does it Add Up?
The use of triangulation provides owners with the ability to push the utilization of their vessel over 100%, increasing the timecharter equivalent (TCE) for the duration of the voyage. For example, a VLCC making the standard voyage from the Arabian Gulf to LOOP would load and discharge a single cargo before returning to the Arabian Gulf for another voyage after roughly 76 days round‐trip. This would limit an owner to a utilization of roughly 90%, assuming a few idle or waiting days over the duration of the voyage. In a favorable market, the standard practice of laden out, ballast back may be acceptable, however in the current state of the market shipowners looking to turn a profit will likely need to do more.
With the help of triangulation, the negative or near negative earnings which are reported in the market of late can be staved off. No, this is not an act of defying gravity; it merely requires that extra planning to ensure that voyages like the one on the previous page take place. By lining up cargoes for the ballast trip, such as in West Africa for a VLCC returning to the Arabian Gulf from LOOP, an owner can effectively push utilization over 150% and in this example push earnings from $4,000 per day to over $18,000 per day as seen in the chart below.

The benchmark trade, UK Continent to the US Atlantic Coast on 37kt of cargo, is currently earning roughly $6,000 per day. However, an MR will often steam to the US Gulf following discharge to pick up another cargo before going back the UKC. This simple backhaul cargo has the potential to push earnings up and over the $10,000 per day range - a move many have already been doing for a long time now.

## Effectively Earning
So even as the assessed timecharter equivalent dips below $0 per day or struggles to match break‐even costs, owner's are not willing to throw in the towel yet. Simple freight economics may point the current market to lay‐ups and premature scrapping but there is more than meets the eye. Tanker owners have not likely faced the pinch on a widespread basis. Unwillingness to remove ships from the fleet suggests that weak market sentiment has not yet broken shipowner's eternal optimism.
