## And the Winner is......
Trade in all sorts of clean petroleum products during 2008 seemed vigorous on the spot market. Although the volume of reported spot market fixtures ebbed and flowed, a comparison of activity between years may still reveal emerging trends. From an activity of 5,574 fixtures in 2007, the reported clean spot market fixtures captured in 2008 retreated to 4,913. It is important to note that this reported activity is only a slice of the total fixture volume worldwide. The most dramatic falloff occurred in the Small sector (25,000 to 37,499 DWT). We assess this is due to a move to an increase in contract coverage for the larger operators and pools rather than a reflection of lower demand for this vessel class.

## Changes at the Top
The big news for 2008 is Shell's return to the top spot (they were also last year's top charterer in the dirty spot market, too.) Despite the change in the number one ranking, nine out of the top ten most active charterers during 2008 were also in the ranks of 2007's top ten charterers. Their activity in 2008, however, brought considerable jockeying of relative rankings among the returning nine charterers. Additionally, ConocoPhillips entered into tenth place in 2008 (thanks to an increase in trans-Atlantic activity), while ExxonMobil slipped off the list.

## The Big Units
Aframax (80,000 - 119,999 DWT) Activity in 2008's Aframax (LR2) market showed a decrease in reported spot fixtures of 71 (over a 13 percent drop) over 2007. Shell and Vitol retained their first and second rankings, respectively, last year. SK moved from third to sixth place as Marubeni moved up to third. New entries into the top ten slots last year were YNCC, Formosa and Lotte.

## Lotte
The predominant clean trade for Aframaxes continues to be the Arabian Gulf - Far East naphtha trade with nearly 44 percent of total fixture activity.

## Panamax (60,000 - 79,999 DWT)
As with the Aframax sector, both Shell and Vitol vied for the top two Panamax (LR1) charterer positions. Main spot market trades in 2008 for clean Panamaxes were Arabian Gulf - Far East and Arabian Gulf - UK Continent.

## Small (25,000 - 37,499 DWT)
Once again, Litasco, Vitol, ST Shipping and Shell filled the top four positions of the Small vessel sector. Although reported spot market activity may not fully reflect this sector's total trade, it does help to highlight active charterers.

## What's Ahead for 2009?
As the economy of the world seeks more solid footing in 2009, some distortions to clean petroleum product trade routes seem not only likely but even inevitable. Although near-term demand for clean products remains unclear, delays to projects being considered by the refining industry are the real drivers in the shipping market.
Just a year ago, export-oriented refineries seemed to show a clear future of clean product trade development. Now, however, it is on the shoulders of the tanker market to fill any gaps in spot demand. Complicated product specifications and regulations not only keep trade patterns shifting and erratic but also create arbitrage opportunities aplenty, especially when cold weather occurs in one region or when some other disruption hits the logistical supply chain.
Clean product tanker trading is really a demand driven market. With Americans seeming to have given up on their appetite for smaller cars now that the price of gasoline has fallen in half, enough demand should materialize to keep this fleet actively engaged for 2009.
