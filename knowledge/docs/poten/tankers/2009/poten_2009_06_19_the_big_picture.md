## Statistical Review of World Energy 2009,
The Big Picture 2009 BP Statistical Review of World Energy In the Statistical Review of World Energy 2009, BP provides a long term historical view of the energy markets, and a clear recap of what is arguably one of the most volatile years for energy ‐ 2008. Tony Hayward, BP's Group Chief Executive summed up the analysis by saying, "Our data confirms that the world has enough proved reserves of oil, natural gas and coal to meet the world's needs for decades to come. The challenges the world faces in growing supplies to meet future demand are not below the ground, they are above ground. They are human, not geological." Although crude oil consumption grew by 1.4% in 2008, it marked the slowest growth since 2001.
The recent rally in crude oil prices suggest concerns that supply of crude oil will become strained in the near future. Upward movements in price beg the question: how much oil actually remains? Thanks to BP's extensive database, some comfort can be offered to the oil consuming public. Worldwide proven oil reserves have risen roughly 47% since 1980 to over 1.25 trillion barrels. ## (Billion Bbls)
Currently, the Organization of Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC) controls 76% of reserves, non‐OPEC countries account for 14% and the Former Soviet Union has 10%.
The reserve to production ratio has remained in the 40 year range since 1998 (coal's reserve to production ratio is 120 years). The development of unconventional sources that is likely to arise over the next decades will serve to expand reserves globally.

## Identifying the Growth
Since 1995, world oil consumption has grown by roughly 14.8 mbd. While the OECD countries have traditionally held the lion's share of this pie, the distribution has changed in recent years. Over this period, the FSU has accounted for only 2.5 mbd of growth while Emerging Market Economies (EMEs) have accounted for over 12.3 mbd, or 83% of the incremental new demand.
Most notably, for the first time non‐OECD countries surpassed OECD countries in total 2008 energy consumption. In the charts below is a breakdown of energy consumption by type and by consumer base. It is likely that the EMEs will be responsible for the marginal growth given that these nations account for about 82% of the world population.

## In the Mix
Not surprisingly, oil continues to provide the predominant source of energy worldwide, 35% of total consumption. Oil saw a decline of 0.6%, very similar to nuclear, year on year, while coal, gas and hydro sources saw an increase of between 2.5‐3% each. Nuclear and hydro still remain small components of the overall energy mix, accounting for less than 6% each.
In light of current market uncertainties, the historical context provided below helps identify patterns to look for in the future. Following the global energy crisis which ended in 1979 and led to a glut of oil supply in 1980, the world economy recovered and energy demand rebounded accordingly.

As the direction of crude oil prices seem to be the new barometer of economic recovery, small changes in barrel prices could work wonders on broader confidence.
