## Talking Turkey
For many in the tanker industry, 2009 has been a tough year. Shipowners and charterers alike can commiserate about low demand for crude oil and refined products as well as the limping pace of growth in many economies. That said, the Thanksgiving holiday seems to spark some excitement that the market could be poised for a turnaround ‐ if only based on past years' experience! The 2007 Thanksgiving holiday began one of the most extraordinary rallies in the history of the tanker market. Spot VLCC rates jumped by 50 points overnight and kicked off a 12 month period of unusually strong earnings for shipowners. This time last year, with the burgeoning economic crisis dominating the headlines, tanker markets were fighting off some negativity and still performing despite the gloomy broader sentiment. Since there is some strengthening in the spot tanker market at present, is another spike just a few days away?
Seasons Come...
In addition to heightened seasonal demand for crude oil, November often brings weather conditions, such as increased fog and tropical storms, which cause delays and lead to ships out of position. These drivers give additional support, if only temporary, to freight rates.
The most basic variable which can increase volatility in rates is related to oil demand and it is most easily represented through changes in the number of tanker liftings. There has been an average of 64 percent difference between monthly highs and lows in the number of liftings from the Arabian Gulf between 1995 and 2008. As shown in the chart below, the number of spot liftings from the Arabian Gulf has demonstrated less volatility this year and remained below monthly averages throughout 2009. In periods of heightened demand, month‐on‐month availability of ships can make the difference between an average and extraordinary market.

## ......And Seasons Go
Aside from fluctuations in demand, volatility in the tanker market can be divided into two broad categories; unforeseen delays and psychology of market participants. While the extreme volatility that drove the market to new highs in late 2007 and early 2008 has receded, participants in the tanker industry should continue expecting the unexpected.
This year, the most excitement came at the end of the first week of June. At the time, rates on the benchmark Arabian Gulf - Far East route were WS 37.5 with timecharters earning about $17,100 per day. By the end of the second week of June, rates had jumped to WS 52.5 and timecharter earnings to $37,900 per day. This jump helped raise the average timecharter equivalent on the voyage from $7,324 in May to $27,559 in June and demonstrated how market psychology can contribute to volatility in tanker markets.

## Happy Thanksgiving!
While it is easy for some shipowners to look back fondly on the perfect storm of surging demand and tight tonnage which began just before Thanksgiving 2007, 2009 rate trends have served as a reminder that what comes up, must come down.
An additional reminder to those participating in tomorrow's holiday; resist the temptation to take a post‐dinner nap, you could just miss a jump in the market!
