## Trick or Treat ?
As the Halloween holiday comes creeping in, many shipowners find themselves wondering if the next few weeks will bring a trick or a treat from the markets. The economy in the United States seemed to have received a treat earlier this week when the Department of Commerce reported 3.5 percent economic growth during the third quarter of this year. The GDP growth beat economists' expectations and fueled a spike in crude oil and other commodity prices. The first signs of growth in over a year also inspired speculation that the economic recovery is moving into its next phase. While this good news has yet to translate into direct support for crude oil demand and freight rates, small leading indicators suggest the tanker market may be poised for a slight recovery.
Spot market VLCC availabilities in the Arabian Gulf have been on the decline, which has undoubtedly helped to secure stronger earnings for owners, especially those with vessels heading east. Down significantly from early 2009 highs, current availabilities of VLCCs 30 days out point to less of a supply overhang, providing owners with vessels in the region more leverage.
The reduction and eventual flattening of recent VLCC availabilities has caused earnings on two benchmark VLCC voyages from the Arabian Gulf to rise simultaneously, as shown in the chart below. Earnings on those two voyages have improved since the second week in October, with the more active Arabian Gulf - Far East route displaying a $10,000 improvement to $27,500 per day.

## Better than Candy Corn
While Arabian Gulf liftings headed to the West have remained low, shipowners have been encouraged to keep a weather eye on the fuel oil market originating in the Caribbean and US Gulf. Vessels in the Caribbean can capitalize on the steady trade ferrying fuel oil to China and have become one factor whittling down the number of VLCCs waiting in the Arabian Gulf. October saw two more liftings on the Caribbean - Far East voyage than had been reported in either of the previous two months, as shown in the chart below. Because some cargoes currently being fixed in the region are still being assigned November laycans, there is a chance the trend will continue for November.

## CAR/USG ‐ Far East
Stronger earnings on voyages out of West Africa have likely also attracted a few vessels to reposition to that market. Increased slow‐steaming, reportedly of roughly 1‐2 knots, and resulting voyage extensions could also be decreasing the number of vessels competing for cargoes in the Arabian Gulf.

## Conclusion
While supply fundamentals point to the next phase in a road to recovery‐ slow but steady increases in earnings, the salvation will ultimately lie within the hands of consumers. Demand west of the Arabian Gulf has shown some promise over the past quarter as GDP growth has proved economists wrong. However, this market can be chock full of tricks, especially when everyone is looking for treats.
