## Summary
Leveraging Flat Rates
Each year, the Worldscale Association adjusts the flat rates which serve as the starting point for negotiating the cost of carrying crude oil and refined products on tankers. Under normal conditions, this annual revision ritual does not have a dramatic impact on tanker markets, aside from forcing owners and charterers to adjust to the new rates during the first weeks of the year. Following five straight years of flat rate increases, Worldscale's downward revision of its 2010 flat rates, announced yesterday, reverses some 2009's usual characteristics rates and sets the stage for another round of negotiation between owners and charterers at year's end.

## Leveraging Flat Rates
Each year, the Worldscale Association adjusts the flat rates which serve as the starting point for negotiating the cost of carrying crude oil and refined products on tankers. Under normal conditions, this annual revision ritual does not have a dramatic impact on tanker markets, aside from forcing owners and charterers to adjust to the new rates during the first weeks of the year. Following five straight years of flat rate increases, Worldscale's downward revision of its 2010 flat rates, announced yesterday, reverses some 2009's usual characteristics rates and sets the stage for another round of negotiation between owners and charterers at year's end.
After a 38% average rise in flat rates in 2009, the decrease among the 111 rate revisions announced by Worldscale yesterday averaged 22%. As shown in the chart below, the 2010 flat rate decrease marks an end to recent flat rate increases on key tanker voyages. In general, the rates for longer routes recorded bigger drops, but the impact of these changes will vary from market to market.

## AG ‐ LOOP
The rising price of bunkers has been the central force driving the annual bumps in Worldscale flat rates in recent years. The Worldscale bunker assumption is an average of assessments of 380 cSt bunker fuel between October 1 to September 30 provided by LQM Petroleum Services. As shown in the chart below, 2009 flat rates were calculated using $554/mt while the 2010 flat rates have been calculated on an average price of $341/mt.

## HFO
In addition to adjustments for bunker prices, the annual rate revisions to Worldscale rates are also designed to reflect changes in port costs and transit fees. This year, Worldscale opted to retain its language relating to existing Emissions Control Areas and announced it will make allowances at all EU ports for cost of 0.1% sulfur, which will be required from January 1, 2010 in compliance with the EU Sulphur Directive. The organization also announced yesterday that it would now take an average exchange rate during the same October - September timeframe it uses for bunkers in order to minimize the impact of currency fluctuations.
Usually, the annual revision of Worldscale flat rates has been greeted with a modest rise in the spot market. This year, that reaction is far from ensured as owners will likely take the opportunity to renegotiate using new Worldscale flat rates, endeavoring to recoup some of the this year's loses. One can expect that the current earning environment for owners, which barely allows them to recover operating costs on some voyages, will spur them to push for even harder for more favorable rates.
