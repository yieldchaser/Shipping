## Can growing ton-mile demand turn the market?
The freight markets for 2018 have so far been disappointing for crude oil tankers. For the VLCC market, the supply-demand balance and the resulting freight rates are predominantly driven by OPEC production and exports. Suezmaxes and Aframaxes typically have more diverse trading patterns. This week we will take a look at what may be behind the disappointing returns for these medium sized crude oil tankers.
Suezmaxes have suffered more than most other segments, as some of their traditional markets have declined dramatically in recent years. However, examining the Suezmax trades over recent years shows that these ships have been effective in finding alternative employment. It might be a surprise to some readers that, over the first four months of this year, ton-mile demand for Suezmax tankers actually increased by 7% compared to the prior year as Suezmaxes performed on average 27.5 more voyages per month. The strongest growth was on the Middle East to India route, which increased from an average of 28 cargoes per month in 2017 to 39 this year (Jan-Apr). A large part of the growth was due to increased Iranian exports to India, which could be threatened in the second half of the year by the reinstatement of U.S. sanctions on Iran. Suezmax tankers also managed to recapture some market share in West Africa from VLCCs as less West African crude moved to Asia. Europe bought more West African crude, resulting in an increase of about 10 monthly voyages to NW Europe and the Mediterranean. Suezmaxes also benefited from the growing exports from the U.S. Gulf, mainly for short and medium haul voyages to Canada (+2.8 voy/m) and Europe (+4.3 voy/m).
Suezmax trades were negatively affected by the dispute over oil exports between the Kurds in Northern Iraq and the Iraqi government after the vote for independence in September 2017. Suezmax voyages from Ceyhan declined from 12 voyages per month in early 2017 to just 3.5 in 2018.
While the demand side was overall positive for Suezmax tankers, the supply side spelled trouble: over the last 12 months, the Suezmax fleet increased by 39 ships, equivalent to 7%, thereby offsetting the growth in demand.
For Aframax tankers the situation is mixed. Ton-mile demand from crude oil trades increased by 9% in the first four months, even though the number of voyages decreased. Russian exports to NW Europe declined, as did Venezuelan exports to the U.S. Gulf. Both are short haul trades and the impact on ton- mile demand was limited. Growth areas included the AG to South Asia (mainly Pakistan) trade and exports from Libya (+13.2 voy/m), primarily to South Europe. Aframaxes also benefited from growing exports from the U.S. Gulf with more cargoes to Canada (+2.5 voy/m) and Europe (+8.7 voy/m).
Dirty product trades, which represent about 12% of the dirty Aframax trade, declined 7% and negatively affected Aframax employment. Fuel oil exports from the Middle East to Pakistan declined as Pakistan switched more of its power generation to natural gas. Russian exports to Europe declined as well. These declines were partially offset by increased intra-Asian trade.
As LR2 tankers are employed in both clean and dirty trades, one needs to include the clean trades to understand the overall health of the Aframax market. As has been true most of the time in recent years, clean Aframax demand increased at a faster pace than the crude trades: Ton-mile demand increased by 11% and the number of monthly voyages increased as well. Exports from the Middle East to NW Europe increased, only partially offset by a decline in shorter haul imports from the Baltic. Long-haul exports from the Middle East increased at the expense of the short-haul intra-Middle East trade.
The total Aframax fleet increased by only 10 vessels or about 1% of the fleet, helped by relatively strong demolition numbers.
Looking at the earnings, both Suezmax and Aframax segments appear to be in dire straits. However, underlying ton-mile demand growth is healthy, sowing the seeds of a recovery if supply growth can be contained.
