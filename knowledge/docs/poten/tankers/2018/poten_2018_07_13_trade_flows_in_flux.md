## Do changes in trade flows impact the tanker market?
For today's weekly opinion we decided to take a look at almost 5 years of crude oil trade data (2014 - 2018) to see if we can discern any interesting trends or developments. Are certain segments growing faster than others? How about shifts in export or import regions. Do we notice anything that we did not expect?
First, let's look at the overall crude oil and dirty product trade for the period 2014-2018. To make the earlier years com- parable with 2018, we look only at the first 6 months of the year. The data shows that the dirty tanker trade showed steady growth in the period 2014-2017, increasing almost 5% in the first 6 months of 2015 and still growing 2.2% and 3.3% in 2016 and 2017 respectively. However, in 2018 to date, the total dirty tanker trade (measured in Metric Tons) has been down 1.3% compared to the same period one year ago (TonMiles: -1.6%). In Asia, modest increases in China and India could not compensate for declines in Japan and Asean countries, so overall ton-mile demand from Asia was down 1%. The biggest decline in import demand, however, came from the United States (-15%). On the other hand, U.S. exports were one of the few bright spots in 2018 to date, with ton-mile demand more than double that of the same period last year. The long-haul trade to China was responsible for almost 60% of that increase.
A review of the largest trade routes confirms the changes in crude flows. The largest VLCC route is from the Arabian Gulf to the China Sea (China, Taiwan, Korea) has not changed since 2014. This route, which is more than twice the size of the next route (AG - Japan) represents some 30% of the total VLCC trade. However, the AG - USG trade, which ranked no. 4 in 2014 has declined both in absolute and relative terms. Its volume declined by 33% and it now only ranked 6 th . In contrast, the USG - China Sea VLCC trade currently ranks 9 th . This trade did not exist prior to 2017. The increase in USG VLCC exports has coincided with a decline in exports from Venezuela. Another VLCC trade that has been in decline recently (and quite volatile overall) is the one from AG to Europe. After a 55% increase from 2016 to 2017, this trade has declined by 42% so far this year.
Shifting gears to the Suezmax segment; this trade has developed differently from the VLCCs. Suezmax employment has increased steadily since 2014, with a healthy 5% increase so far this year. A lot of Suezmaxes are employed in shuttle trades offshore Brazil (No. 1 in the ranking) and in moving Alaskan crude to the U.S. West Coast (No. 3), but these trades are not growing and generate relatively little ton-mile demand due to the short distances involved. The same applies to the 2 nd largest trade: AG to India. A growing Suezmax trade is the AG Southern Europe trade, but this is to a large extent driven by rising exports from Iran, which could be in jeopardy in the second half of the year. West Africa exports have been relatively stable in recent years after significant volatility in 2014 - 2016. So far in 2018, Suezmax trades from the AG, West Africa, the Black Sea, the U.S. Gulf and from the UKC are growing, while trades originating in the Far East, the Eastern Med and Venezuela have been struggling.
In the Aframax segment, the intra-Asean Far East (incl. Indonesia, Thailand, Malaysia, Singapore, etc.) has surpassed the Caribbean market as the largest Aframax trading area. Another Asian Aframax trade that is growing in importance is the export trade out of Kozmino to China, Korea and Taiwan. So far in 2018, this is the 3 rd largest Aframax route (in MT), recently surpassing certain trades in the North Sea and the Mediterranean. Overall, Aframax employment is down in 2018 versus 2017 in terms of number of voyages, and barrels moved, but ton-miles are slightly up as average distances increased.
Overall, crude oil tanker demand has been relatively flat and rates are down as a result of increases in fleet size. However, there are a lot of developments hidden under the surface that belie the relative stability of aggregate tanker demand.
