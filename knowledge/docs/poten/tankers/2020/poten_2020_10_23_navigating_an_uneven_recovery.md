## Covid-19 creates diverging paths for product demand
When analyzing demand prospects for product tankers, analysts take into account differences in projections for the various refined products, such as gasoline, diesel, and jet fuel. Regional variations are also being considered. Some areas are growing faster than others and certain regions are short of certain products, while others are traditionally long. Covid-19 has upended a lot of these traditional relationships and typical rules of thumb no longer work. This has had significant implications for the product tanker market.
When Covid hit, first in China and then rapidly spreading across the world, all product categories suffered as many countries went into lockdown. A quick analysis of the OECD demand data in the October IEA Oil Market Report (OMR) shows that all product types showed double-digit declines when comparing Q2 2020 with Q4 2019: Gasoline demand went down 24%, Jet Fuel/Kerosine declined 65% and Diesel dropped by 16%. The declines were the highest in OECD Europe and the lowest in OECD Asia/Oceania, with the Americas somewhere in the middle.
Comparing the averages of Q2 2020 with July 2020 (the latest month available in the IEA OMR), shows a robust rebound in Europe and the Americas. In OECD Asia/Oceania, the recovery was less pronounced. Gasoline demand was up 16% in July 2020 compared to Q2, but Jet Fuel and Diesel demand still contracted somewhat. In OECD Europe, Gasoline demand was up 46%, Jet Fuel 50% and Diesel 15%. That meant that Gasoline and Diesel demand in Europe in July 2020 were almost on par with Q4 2019. However, Jet Fuel remained 59% below last year's number.
Covid restrictions were relaxed during the summer and demand for road transportation fuels recovered, but most people remained very reluctant to fly. Business travel in particular has taken a big hit. Many people are still working from home and have gotten used to remote ways of doing business. It remains to be seen if business travel will ever get back to pre-pandemic levels. The trend in the Americas was directionally similar to that in Europe, but as the Covid situation in OECD Americas (which includes Mexico, Chile and Colombia, in addition to the United States and Canada) remained more challenging, the recovery was not as strong. Gasoline and Diesel demand in the Americas is still 9% and 13% respectively below the Q4 2019 numbers.
Since July, the situation has worsened in both Europe and the Americas. While we don't have demand data yet for August and September, we expect that the rapid recovery in demand has slowed down on both sides of the Atlantic as Covid-19 has entered a second wave. The situation in OECD Asia is different. While Covid is not completely gone, it appears to be much more under control in OECD Asia, which could help turn things around quicker.
China has been the exception. They were the early victims of Covid-19, but after they brought it under control, it has not resurfaced in a big way. China compares favorably with the OECD countries. Other than Jet Fuel, which is still down, demand for all other products has recovered.
The impact on the product tanker market has been dramatic. Early during the pandemic, freight rates skyrocketed as an oversupply of products caused charterers to use product tankers for floating storage. However, after the market adjusted, storage demand dried up and the freight market collapsed.
With high product inventories and subdued demand globally, it is unlikely that rates will recover quickly. China is the only country so far that seems to be on a path to recovery, but it has ample domestic refining capacity and it appears unlikely at the moment that China will be a catalyst for the product tanker market in Asia. The economy of India, the other Asian powerhouse has been struggling and the medium-term outlook for India's product demand is highly dependent on how successful it is in its efforts to contain Covid-19.
The situation in the Western countries remains dire with a second wave of Covid-cases confronting both sides of the Atlantic. A swift approval and rollout of an effective vaccine is needed to turn this situation around.
