## OPEC Production and VLCC Rates - What It Means
Last week OPEC announced a reduction of about 1 million bpd to stem falling oil prices. The key question is what this means for VLCC rates. The following chart shows VLCC rates and AG exports.

## AG/FE
AG/USG AG Exports There is a closer agreement with AG/West VLCC rates with AG exports (R- Square of 75%) than AG/East VLCC rates (R-Square of 66%). Curiously there is no statistical relationship between AG imports into the United States and AG/West VLCC rates.
The following chart shows the relative volatility between AG exports and U.S. imports from the Arabian Gulf. Fluctuations in AG exports are much greater than AG imports to the United States, which makes WS rates AG/West more statistically related to the ups and downs of AG exports.

## Trend Drifting Down
% AG Exports to U.S. AG Exports U.S. AG Imports While U.S. oil imports from the AG have remained relatively flat at 2.5 million bpd, the percentage of U.S. oil imports from the AG with respect to AG exports is trending down. The reason is rising AG exports to the Far East; most notably China. Using round numbers, the U.S. consumes 20 million bpd and imports half of our oil needs, thus AG imports of 2.5 million bpd represent 12.5% of total U.S. demand and 25% of total oil imports. Any interruption in AG exports will have a far greater impact in the Pacific basin than in the Atlantic basin. We ran statistical experiments comparing VLCC AG/West rates with world oil consumption, and price in terms of $/barrel and Euros/barrel. Including these variables along with AG exports provided very little improvement in the statistical model. Thus the best predictor of AG/West VLCC rates is AG exports. The chart on the next page is the scatter diagram of AG/West VLCC rates with AG exports (latest available export figures are October). One can see that there is a relationship between freights rates and export volumes, as one might expect. But as life would have it, it is not an entirely consistent relationship. The outliers are those values which do not neatly fit our attempts to model reality with nice straight lines.

## Outliers
So What Are We Saying? The theoretical impact of a change of one million bpd of AG exports on the AG/West VLCC rates is 28 Worldscale points whereas the same change on AG/East VLCC rates is 38 Worldscale points. The market has already made some allowance for a prospective drop in AG exports. Current rates AG West of W187.5 is down from W210 for a drop of 22.5 WS points whereas current rates AG/East of W227.5 is down from W300, a drop of 72.5 WS points. The question really is not so much about the impact of tanker rates, rather if OPEC will maintain a cut in production given the lack of any real sign of a slowdown in either U.S. or China's economic activity. Until we see some signs of a demand slowdown, we feel that rates will remain somewhere between strong and stronger.
