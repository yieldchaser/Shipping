## Summary
China Syndrome
China is the talk of the shipping industry from tankers to bulkers to container vessels. And there is little wonder- China is driving up rates in all sectors. Focusing on oil, China can only be described in terms of exponential growth.

## China Syndrome
China is the talk of the shipping industry from tankers to bulkers to container vessels. And there is little wonder- China is driving up rates in all sectors. Focusing on oil, China can only be described in terms of exponential growth.

## China as Exporter
Average Annual Growth in Bpd Consumption Production 1970-1980 120,000 150,000 1980-1990 50,000 65,000 1990-2002 260,000 50,000 During the 1970s, annual gains in production slightly exceeded consumption. During the 1980s, however, annual gains in consumption slightly exceeded production. Now, these gains in consumption are rapidly outpacing production gains causing imports to expand. An annual 260,000 bpd gain in consumption resulting from a 50,000 bpd annual gain in production, or 210,000 bpd yearly average growth in imports, may not seem overly impressive. Yet, in comparison to past years, growth in imports is phenomenal. Imports are to be expanding by 1 million bpd every five years based on historic averages. Yet, current annual gains in imports are higher than expected. While current growth is optimistic, it may not be sustainable in the long-term. Activity since 1990 has been an anomaly in comparison to previous decades. Worse yet, China's economy may be subject to recession like other Asian countries in the late 1990s. With recent reports of other SARS cases, the economy may be subject to further short-term obstacles.
For Now The Ayes Have It So far it appears that the ayes have it. The SARS outbreak proved simply to be a hiccup in China's booming economy. If things remain on track, especially accelerating oil imports, our assessment of 2003 consumption of 5.4 million bpd, an 8% increase from 2002, will be augmented with another 300,000 bpd gain in 2004. This will bring consumption up to 5.7 million bpd. This increment of 700,000 bpd in consumption over the next two years net of 100,000 bpd in production gains will increase imports by 600,000 bpd in 2004. This will be a 14% overall increase in consumption for the two-year period. The chart on the next page shows tanker activity in terms of the number of spot market fixtures. Total spot fixtures in 2001 were 640. Activity climbed to 790 in 2002 and concluded 2003 with 850 fixtures. It is clear that the primary beneficiaries of increasing oil imports to China are VLCCs and Aframaxes. Both benefit from the upsurge in crude oil imports, but Aframaxes also benefit from increased fuel oil imports.

## Chart: # Spot Fixtures by Tanker Type
Oil imports in terms of tons of cargo on vessels fixed in the spot market totaled 59 million tons in 2001, 78 million tons in 2002, and 89 million tons in 2003 for nearly 25% annual growth over the two-year period. The chart on the next page shows the relative change in loading areas for spot market fixtures. Given declining Indonesian exports and limited growth for other Pacific basin exporters, China is becoming more dependent on Arabian Gulf and West African crude, which is good news for VLCC owners. This trend will become more pronounced as China's imports increase since Pacific basin exporters simply cannot fill the gap.
China Syndrome The movie China Syndrome was a fictional account of a potential nuclear reactor meltdown in the United States, which, had it occurred would have formed a molten mass that would have sunk into the earth's crust "all the way to China." At the moment the China Syndrome economic meltdown is not in the cards. The Chinese objective is to quadruple their GDP by 2020 for a compound economic growth of 7.2% per year. To do this, the Chinese government recognizes that the domestic economy must expand to absorb factory output, which means a hike in domestic personal income.
We should not underestimate China's importance in the world of oil. Speculations have been made that in 2004 China will surpass Japan as the world's second largest oil importer. China accounts for nearly 30% of incremental global demand for oil, and perhaps as much, if not more, of incremental tanker demand.
