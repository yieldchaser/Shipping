## Who is the Biggest of the Clean?
Who is the Biggest of the Clean? In our January 9, 2004 opinion report, "The More Things Change...The More They Stay the Same," we tracked the top charterers in the spot market for dirty cargoes. But the dirty market is only one part of the tanker market. Who are the largest charterers of clean fixtures? Recall that 20% of charterers in the dirty spot market are doing a solid 80% of the business, and perhaps predictably the clean charterers are doing about the same. In both 2002 and 2003, 20% of charterers did about 75% of the business, but there were slightly fewer fixtures in 2002 than 2003.

## Small Ship, Big Sister
Poten's internal databases of clean cargoes tracked 3,576 total fixtures in 2002 and 4,032 in 2003, excluding failed fixtures. A total of 152 different charterers fixed ships in this market in 2003. As they do in the dirty market, Shell tops the spot chartering list in the clean product market. As shown below, the top five charterers accounted for 27% of all fixtures and the top 20 accounted for 55% of all the spot fixtures.

## Flexible Fleet
As you might suspect, Handymaxes remain the number one choice for charterers to fix clean cargoes and Shell remains the number one charterer in this ship size. For Handymaxes, fixtures are fairly evenly spread throughout the world, with the most active load zones being the Far East (19.5%), Southeast Asia (19.1%) and the Caribbean Basin (17.5%).

## The Second Tier Vessels
Panamaxes finished well behind Handymaxes with 345 fixtures of which 114 were completed by the top five charterers. The Arabian Gulf loadings accounted for 57% of the total number of fixtures. In second place was the UKC with 16% of fixtures, with the Med a distant third at 6%.

## Vitol
Shell Itochu BP Addax Aframaxes totaled 289 fixtures of which the top five did 99. As seen in the chart below, Shell and Vitol tied for first. 72% of all fixtures are Arabian Gulf loadings. The most common cargoes are naphtha to Japan.

Vitol PDI Addax BP Largest Vessels, Smallest Number of Fixtures Only eight Suezmax tankers were chartered to carry clean cargo in 2003. The charterers included Shell, Morgan Stanley, Glencore, Addax and BP. Charterers used the largest of the lot, U/VLCC tankers, sparingly in 2003 with only three charterers fixing one voyage each - Formosa, OTC and S Oil. The ships were used to mostly carry gasoil cargoes. Two VLCC loadings were in the Arabian Gulf and the one ULCC loaded in the Far East. The Future For 2004, Handymax tankers will again be the vessel of choice for clean market charterers. However, with about two-thirds of Panamax newbuildings being coated, charterers will have greater flexibility in choice of vessel size for moving clean products. This may become the sector to watch in the future.
