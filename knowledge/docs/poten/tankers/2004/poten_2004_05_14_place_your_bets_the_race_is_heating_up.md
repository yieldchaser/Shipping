## Place Your Bets
The Race Is Heating Up!
Ever watch a horse race? First one horse is in the lead, then another. The crowd hollers and moans as one moves ahead and the other falls behind. Tanker demand and oil demand can be likened to a horse race. When tanker demand gets ahead of oil demand, the crowd of owners moans as rates fall; when oil demand gets ahead of tanker demand, the crowd of owners cheers with wild abandon. Oil demand is currently climbing. The question is: Will it get ahead of tanker demand?

## World Oil Consumption
The previous chart shows that oil prices roughly follow demand. In 2000, the world economies peaked with a simultaneous climax of oil demand and prices. Then the dot.com bubble burst. Although the U.S. economy tanked and stagnation marked other economies, emerging economies took up the slack to keep world oil consumption flat. Oil prices declined somewhat, but not markedly. Now, both the U.S. economy and emerging economies are growing and oil demand is climbing as are oil prices. As we know, oil prices hit a record high of just over $41 per barrel this week. The following chart shows that both tanker supply (Panamax size and larger) and world oil demand are both escalating sharply.

## MM Dwt
Rising oil demand means a greater demand for tankers to transport oil. So we would expect higher tanker rates. But rates are not set by tanker demand alone, but by the relationship between tanker supply and tanker demand.
As seen in the graph on the next page, VLCC Worldscale rates were generally low in 2002. Rates peaked in late 2002 and early 2003 only to drop down by summer 2003. This turned out to be a seasonal lull in demand. As the graph below shows, VLCC rates peaked in early 2004 and then dropped in the early months of 2004, but are now moving back up.

## WS Rates AG to FE 2002 to Present
Will Oil Demand Overtake Tanker Supply? World oil consumption and crude carrier demand both grew about 3% in 2003 but things may be changing now. The most recent month of the US Department of Energy's data shows that worldwide oil consumption is over 82 million bpd compared to December 2002 levels of about 76 million bpd. ## (MM Dwt)
Maybe It's Not a Narrow Race Rates have generally weakened from their historical highs of January because ship deliveries got ahead of growth in oil demand. In the short-run, it looks like oil growth will beat out tanker supply keeping rates relatively strong. So if you're going to place your bets, place them now because it looks like the market is already turning back up.
