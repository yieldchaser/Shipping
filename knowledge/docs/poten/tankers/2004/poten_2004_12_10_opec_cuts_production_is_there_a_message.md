## OPEC Cuts Production- Is There a Message?
OPEC announced today a reduction of about 1 million bpd to stem falling oil prices. Saudi Arabia stated that it has been acting responsibly by increasing production to moderate oil prices. Now that prices have "moderated" to $43 (33.7 euros) per barrel as compared to $31 (26.7 euros) per barrel last November, up 39% in dollars and 26% in euros, action is warranted to prevent a price collapse. The following chart shows AG exports and oil prices since 2001 (EIA data available through September).

## Economics at Work
The following chart plots oil prices versus AG exports. As the world economy improves, oil demand increases. This causes OPEC/AG oil exports to rise, putting upward pressure on prices.

## (2001-Sept 2004)
There is a fairly good correlation between oil prices and AG exports (65% of the variation in oil prices can be described by variation in AG exports). This degree of correlation can be interpreted to mean that it is easier for OPEC to keep prices high when demand is high and that it is more difficult to maintain price when demand is low. AG exports do vary. Since 2001, the low was in April 2002 of 18.4 million bpd and the most recently published EIA data is 23.8 million barrels per day, a rather impressive 5.4 million bpd (29%) swing in exports.

## Inventories Building?
Comments in the press seem to indicate that the recent softness in oil prices may stem from an accumulation of crude and heating oil inventories, shown on the next page.

## Million Bbls
Crude oil inventories have been in an upward trend since 2001. Although modern financial inventory management is to keep inventories low in order to dedicate corporate capital resources to more productive outlets, crude inventories are also insurance against uncertainty of supply. Past interruption of Venezuelan exports and the on again, off again threats to Nigerian supply would certainly warrant an increase in crude inventories. And from a financial viewpoint, a climbing price for oil makes inventory accumulation look pretty astute. While heating oil inventories are not out of line with past levels, the recent drop in inventories at a time of year when inventories are normally climbing presages greater refinery input to restore inventories in case of a cold winter in the coming months. So Where Do We Stand? A falling price and a cutback in AG exports may be temporary. No one has suggested that China or any industrial nation is using less crude. Inventories are not out of line when producer uncertainty is taken into consideration. Threats of disruption in supply will place a floor under oil prices, and tanker rates for that matter. It is not the time to abandon ship!
