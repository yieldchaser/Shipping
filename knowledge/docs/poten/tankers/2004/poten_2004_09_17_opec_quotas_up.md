With every OPEC announcement to raise production, some other world event thwarts its intention to reduce oil prices. ## 1st Qtr 2004
During the past four years, world oil demand has increased by 5 million bpd to more than 80 million bpd as seen in the graph on the preceding page. OPEC has increased its production steadily since last year to nearly 30 million barrels per day, which meets nearly 40% of the world's oil demand. Yet crude oil prices continue to rise. They finished out the day at $45.59 Friday.

## West Texas Monthly Crude Prices
Continued sabotage of Iraqi pipelines, Russia's impoundment of Yukos' assets, and horrendous weather each contribute to supply uncertainty. Even though OPEC production is stable, the rest of the world is not.
Saudi Arabia: Pumping As Fast As They Can Saudi oil production is already above its August production quota of 8.4 million bpd with an estimated production of 9.6 million bpd. Yet it is not filling the gap it once did. Saudi Arabia was once a bigger powerhouse in the industry providing nearly 16% of oil production to world demand. As seen in the graph on the next page, its contribution to satisfying world demand has declined somewhat since its peak in 1992. Moreover with a reputed capacity of 12.5 million bpd, its actual production has not exceeded 10 million bpd. One wonders if Saudi Arabia is at its peak production now.

## % of Production
Are We At The Edge? As world oil demand continues to grow, Saudi Arabia will be less able to contribute to satisfying demand. Next year, one million bpd of Caspian crude are presumed to come through the BTC (Baku-Tbilisi-Ceyhan) pipeline. Further gains are expected from North and West Africa and the Gulf of Mexico. But demand is also expected to grow at one million bpd per year. This seems to be a neck-in-neck horserace. It is possible that high oil prices are not so much a sign but the fact that we are straining the world's capacity to produce oil. If this is true, do not expect a significant drop in oil prices.
