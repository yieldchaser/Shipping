## The Perfect Storm
Rising Gasoline Prices Several trends are in place to bring together the perfect confluence of events to raise gasoline prices now nearing $3 per gallon in some areas of the nation. The national average of $1.81 per gallon is the highest price since March 1981 when gasoline prices peaked at an inflation adjusted $2.99 per gallon .

## Consumption Climbing
Gasoline consumption is in an upward trend as the populations of people and automobiles expand.

## America's Love for the Automobile!
As seen in the previous chart, there is also a marked seasonality to gasoline consumption peaking during the summer vacation season. In the coming months, gasoline consumption should increase unless high gasoline prices defer travel plans. But this should not be more than marginal as those traveling have motel, food and entertainment expenses that generally far outweigh the cost of filling up the tank.

## Gasoline Inventory Low
While U.S. gasoline inventory is not at a record low, it is lower than last year at this time. The record low in gasoline inventory occurred during last year's summer driving season, which, under the circumstances, could happen again this summer.

## Chart: Refinery Capacity Stagnant
U.S. environmental laws have precluded the oil companies from building domestic refineries during the past 30 years. But refinery capacity has increased from refinery "creep"- incremental additions to capacity and debottlenecking activities that increased refinery capacity over time. The following chart suggests that refinery creep now seems dormant. Part of the explanation is that regulatory requirements on gasoline specifications have actually reduced refinery output, canceling out the benefit of refinery creep.

## Refinery Creep Dead?
As apparent from the above, refineries, like ships, need to be removed from service for maintenance. Normal maintenance is scheduled at the start of the year after the peak heating oil season and before the start of the peak gasoline season. Refinery output is heading up, but it still has a ways to go to reach its full potential of about 16 million bpd of crude input.

## Lest We Forget
While gasoline consumption, refinery output, and inventories do influence prices, the most significant driver is crude oil prices, which, as we well know, are escalating. The chart on the next page shows just how close gasoline and crude oil prices are related, although there have been departures from close harmony. A major departure between the two occurred in the late summer of 2003, which if we refer to the chart on gasoline inventory, occurred when gasoline inventories sunk to record lows causing prices to spike.

## Trend is Up and Away!
What This Means for Tanker Owners One would think that MR or handymax product carrier rates would be booming. They were until last month when LR1 or Panamax product carriers carried a large portion of gasoline imports cross-Atlantic, which put pressure on handymax rates serving the Caribbean market.

## Caribs Up Coast
In the last few days, handymax rates have improved. Our prognosis of increasing gasoline imports to keep gas lines from forming supports product carrier rates taking off again. Be it as it may, Happy Motoring!
