## How Do You Say "Limited Partnership" in German?
How Do You Say "Limited Partnership" in German? January 30, 2004 The tanker market is welcoming a relatively new purchasing structure thanks to the German government's tax code. What is known as "KG" is an abbreviation for "Kommanditgesellchaft," which means "limited partnership." The KG scheme has been around for years and has financed a large number of containerships and bulk carriers. In the past few years, KG financings have turned to tankers. The primary benefit of the KG structure is that it allows investors to use tax losses from vessel ownership that can then be used as a write off to reduce personal tax obligations.

How Does It Work - Start with the Money...
In order to purchase a vessel, the KG raises money from private individuals generally through financial intermediaries. This money becomes the "equity" source of capital, which typically covers 40% of the vessel purchase price. Conventional bank loans cover the balance.
The bank loan payments are covered primarily by a time charter and, in the event of a default, the vessel is used as collateral for the balance of the loan. The time charterer could be with a third party but it is usually chartered back to the seller. The seller/time charterer maintains commercial control over the vessel. In some cases, the vessel is time chartered out at a higher rate with the owner locking in a margin. In other cases, the vessel trades on the spot market or is placed into one of the major pools depending on its size. At today's high market rates, the spot market is preferred.
The time charter often runs from 5 to 10 years and is set at a level to cover operating costs, debt repayment obligations, and a return on the equity investment. The time charterer generally has the option to purchase the vessel from the KG at certain points during the time charter period.
Add in Tax Breaks...
The KG, as a German entity, can depreciate the vessel for tax purposes at an accelerated rate. This accelerated depreciation expense is a key component of the investment criteria as the tax losses in the early years are passed to the equity investors. These investors, who are generally high- income individuals, can see a significant reduction in their tax obligations.
The KG, in order to function by the tax code, is obligated to enter into a ship management agreement with a German entity. The German entity can then either operate the vessel through a third party, or in some cases, through a German subsidiary.
Decide on the Tonnage Tax...
At a stipulated point in time, the equity owners must declare whether they desire to be taxed based on the earnings of the vessel or on its tonnage. The latter eliminates income taxes on vessel profits that are no longer shielded by accelerated depreciation.
Thus this is an effective method of enhancing the after tax return to the investors. This will also be reflected in the KG's ability to offer competitive time charter rates. The benefits to Germany are in having shipping companies that are responsible for operating, or arranging for the operations of a vessel reside in the country, which, in turn, generates other business for the German maritime community.
The antecedent to the KG scheme was the Norwegian KS scheme which had originally been established as a means to providing tax shelters for the proverbial Norwegian doctors and lawyers. However the KS scheme has been significantly revamped greatly reducing its ability to shield taxable income. Today KS deals are done essentially without tax consequences where deals must stand on their commercial merit alone. And Let It Grow...
Since 1999, there have been more than 60 reported KG transactions involving tankers with a total value of close to $3 billion. In 2003 alone, more than 40 transactions have been completed by some of the most well known companies in the industry. As long as the current tax structure remains in place in Germany, we anticipate that the popularity of this financing mechanism will continue to grow.
