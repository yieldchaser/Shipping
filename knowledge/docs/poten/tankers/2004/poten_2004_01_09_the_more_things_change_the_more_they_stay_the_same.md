## Summary
The More They Stay the Same
The 2003 tanker market will be remembered as one of considerable volatility enhanced by fluctuating natural gas prices, European Union double- hull regulations and increased demand which saw rates soar from below cash breakeven in the summer to record World Scale levels in December. On the cusp of the new year, owners of VLCCs and Suezmaxes received upwards of $90,000/day. One thing that has not changed, however is that the majority of spot market activity each year is created by relatively few charterers.

## VLCCs - Big Ships, Small Number of Charterers
Of the top 20 charterers last year, only four - Lukoil, Tamoil, Reliance and Hess - did not fix VLCCs. A little more than a third of the 244 charterers that fixed at least one vessel greater than 50,000 dwt, also fixed a VLCC (92 to be precise) in 2003. Oil majors Shell and ExxonMobil are the largest charterers in this segment, with Vela next followed by the Indian Oil Company, who has increased its oil trading from West Africa, with ChevronTexaco ranking fifth.

## Suezmaxes Rank Behind Aframaxes
With about 2,100 spot charters concluded on Suezmaxes, this tanker size outpaced VLCCs by more than 18% despite being only about 60% of the size of the VLCC fleet (about 260 Suezmaxes vs. 430 VLCCs). Shell and ExxonMobil again lead the pack, with Sunoco, BP and Total each chartering more than 100 ships.

## Aframaxes - Crown Jewel of the Spot Market
By far, Aframaxes saw the most fixtures in 2003. Shell used the agile fleet overwhelmingly, more than double any other charterer. Although more than 200 different charterers fixed Aframaxes, 15 charterers accounted for 52% of the activity. Once again, Shell and ExxonMobil are numbers one and two.

## Panamax - The Trading Companies Lead the Way
The mix of top charterers in terms of fixture activity in this market segment reflects the nature of this trade. Fuel oil, the primary commodity transported, is actively traded.
