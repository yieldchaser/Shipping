## Bargain Hunting
Newbuilding asset prices have continued to escalate through 2008 to date and are strikingly above long-term historical averages. Since January of this year, VLCC prices have increased almost $10 million to a current assessed level of $158 million. The other large crude oil carriers, Suezmax and Aframaxes have increased a more modest $6.5 and $4.5 million respectively. Interestingly, these vessel price increases have been progressing at different rates depending on sector. VLCC prices seemed to have led the others with the largest jump in March of this year. In search of the most economic alternative, buyers must determine breakeven rate levels to meet their required returns. At today's prices, there is a differential of about $60 million between a VLCC and a Suezmax newbuilding. At $158 million, VLCCs have a break-even level of about $46,000 per day for a 20-year assumed useful life; by comparison, Suezmaxes break-even at just over $31,000 per day at $95 million. In order to recover the difference in capital costs of about $60 million, VLCC owners are hoping to earn at least $15,000 more per day for the life of the vessel.

WAF - USA (130kt) On average since January 2000, VLCCs have earned $56,000 per day as compared to Suezmaxes which have earned $39,000 per day - a difference of $17,000 per day which is enough of a margin even for today's newbuilding prices. In the past 18 months, however, this differential has nearly doubled to over $30,000 per day in these two markets suggesting that the spread in earnings appears to be outweighing the spread in the asset prices. It would seem that one of the variables will have to give, yet the upward pressures at the shipyards indicate prices will not soften substantially in the near term. In fact continued upward pressure to VLCC prices will logically trigger further price increases in other sectors as well. It's time to catch up.
