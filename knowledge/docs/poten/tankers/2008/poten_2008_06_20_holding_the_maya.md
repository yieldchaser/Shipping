## Summary
Holding the Maya
After reaching a zenith of 3.46 million bbls/day in late 2003, Mexico's crude oil production has, as the chart below highlights, declined. At the end of 2007, production had declined from this peak by nearly 500 thousand bbls/day or about 14.5 percent - essentially returning production rates to levels typical of the late 1990's. Declining production rates, at a time when Mexico is not only pushing domestic refineries harder for more products but also striving to expand them or perhaps even build new ones, does not bode well for this nation continuing its recent level of crude oil exports in the future.

## Holding the Maya
After reaching a zenith of 3.46 million bbls/day in late 2003, Mexico's crude oil production has, as the chart below highlights, declined. At the end of 2007, production had declined from this peak by nearly 500 thousand bbls/day or about 14.5 percent - essentially returning production rates to levels typical of the late 1990's. Declining production rates, at a time when Mexico is not only pushing domestic refineries harder for more products but also striving to expand them or perhaps even build new ones, does not bode well for this nation continuing its recent level of crude oil exports in the future.

## Thousand bbl/day
Mexican crude oil imports into the US Gulf Coast, as illustrated in the chart below, averaged some 1.365 million bbls/day in 2007 or just over 150 thousand bbls/day less than the average level imported in 2004. This drop reflects imports of 221 thousand bbls/day less of Mexican crude oils that contain lower than 3 percent sulfur but 69 thousand bbls/day more of those oils having greater than 3 percent sulfur. About 75 percent of the drop in the lower than 3 percent sulfur category is attributable to lower imports of oils with less than 1 percent sulfur.

But those refiners seeking to replace declining supplies of convenient lower sulfur Mexican oils will likely be forced into much longer distance markets for supplies. To replace the remaining 175 thousand bbls/day of (mainly less than one percent sulfur) Mexican imports, US Gulf Coast refiners would likely seek crude oils similar in characteristic.
Given the region's abundant production of low sulfur crude oils, West Africa would be a likely source for any volumes intended to replace any missing low sulfur Mexican ones. The current trade of West African crude oil to the US Gulf Coast - as the chart below highlights - is dependant on both VLCCs and Suezmaxes.
