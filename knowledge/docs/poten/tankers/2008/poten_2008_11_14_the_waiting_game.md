## The Waiting Game
The combination of general uncertainty and little activity in the sale and purchase market make the task of assessing asset prices challenging. With few reported deals acting as a benchmark, pessimism has been the primary driver in weakening vessel prices. As shipyards and the market formulate prices, there are some commonalities regardless of sector: steel, labor, engines, piping and finishings. Until recently, both the rising cost of components and the competition for berth space served to increase prices. Now, although the fundamentals of different shipping sectors vary, many believe a downward trend price is pervasive. ## Assessed Asset Price
In the past few weeks, prices have - in theory - declined. Nevertheless, levels today are generally in line with prices at the start of this year. Weakening sentiment on tanker asset prices may have been prompted by the collapse in the dry bulk market, but one can argue these prices were only just correcting from record highs.

## Take it to the bank
The financing challenges represent a large concern for shipowners and yards. The chart represents the current orderbook value estimated at $590 billion for all vessels and the anticipated required financing by sector. Assuming 75% financing requirement, this implies financing of about $445 billion over the next three years.

## Orderbook Cost
Anticipated Financing While it is generally understood that banks will be hard pressed to add further shipping loans to their portfolios for the remainder of this year, the expectations are that lending will resume in 2009. The required debt needed to finance the orderbook is quite substantial, but the aftermath of bank restructuring may allow for capital to once again become available.
It is anticipated that in the current weak market, shipowners with mixed dry bulk and tanker fleets might come under pressure on the loan-to-value of the dry fleet. These companies may tap tanker assets as a means of collecting cash to improve leveraged positions. The disposal of such assets could potentially deflate tanker prices further in the near term.
It seems that asset opportunities are available especially to those willing to act - when others just seem content to wait on the sidelines. As capital becomes more readily available in the New Year, asset prices could once again be on the rise.
