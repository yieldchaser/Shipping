## A Man A Plan A Canal - Panama
The Panama Canal has become one of the integral waterways for world maritime commerce. Since the Canal opened for transit in 1914 it has facilitated the transportation of more than 200 million tons of cargo each year on over 14,000 ships. Although discussed for many years, in April a proposal indicated Panama's plans to expand the Canal to accommodate more traffic and larger vessels came together. If the plans are approved by a public referendum at the end of the year shipowers can expect to be able to bring much larger ships through the Canal.

## Suezmaxes Through Panama!
Currently, the Canal is comprised of three sets of locks, each with two lanes. The Gatun Locks are on the on the Atlantic side and both the Pedro Miguel Locks and the Miraflores Locks on the Pacific side. The plans call for the construction of another set of locks; one on either side of the isthmus. The current beam restriction of 32.4 meters for vessels transiting the canal limits cargo sizes to about 50,000 tons per transit. The expansion will allow for ships with a beam up to 49 meters, LOA of 366 meters a draft of 15 meters. The increased allowances will permit Suezmax size tankers to transit the Panama Canal.

## Why Expand?
According to the Panama Canal Authority (ACP) some of the objectives of the expansion are to maintain the Canal's competitiveness as a viable maritime route and increase Canal payments to the National Treasury. The payments to the National Treasury are derived in three ways: on a per net ton basis, as a payment for public services and as a payment based on Canal operations. In 2005, the total amount of these direct payments from the Canal to the National Treasury amounted to $489 million. This number is expected to triple by 2015. Delays for the canal have proven to be a 'choke point' for tanker owners in recent months. Currently delays at the Canal can amount to almost a full week, increasing the costs to charterers. In early April, the ACP created an auction for one additional booking slot per day, with the expectation that the price for this slot would open at around $35,000. In fact, slot prices have ranged between $65,000 to $150,000 per crossing. The rapid increase in bidding for the slots is driven primarily by competition from other vessel sectors, specifically reefers, whose cargo is time sensitive.
The amount of delays serves as an indication to the maritime community that there is a need for more capacity in the Canal. Assessment It is clear that widening the Canal will improve the traffic flow in the region. However, there is also a strong possibility that overtime the structural changes will open the Canal to more traffic overall, largely from container vessels which could further complicate traffic patterns. Improved traffic conditions will change the way crude and products are parceled and shipped between the Atlantic and Pacific. If the opposite conditions arise and delays worsen due to increased traffic charterers could stand to be paying even more in demurrage. Could VLCCs someday transit the canal? That would be some plan.
