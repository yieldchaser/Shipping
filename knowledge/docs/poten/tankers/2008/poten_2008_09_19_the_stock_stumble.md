## The Stock Stumble
The tumult of the last few days on Wall Street seems to reaffirm the old adage: "the stock market has correctly predicted nine of the last five recessions." In March, the stock market was initially rattled by the demise of Bear Stearns, arguably the largest banking casualty of the credit crisis before this past week. Investor confidence was certainly tested as stock markets slid and widely-held equities plummeted in value on Monday. By Tuesday, the Federal Reserve's decision to aid insurance giant AIG seemed to stave off a broader meltdown. Stock markets rebounded by the end of the week proving that the impending sense of doom might have been slightly exaggerated. ## Steadying Supply
For the past few years, strong freight rates and the prospect of single hull phase-outs have spurred an interest in fleet renewal of tankers. Money was plentiful, available berths at shipyards were booked and asset prices increased. Since the start of 2007, prices for newbuilding VLCCs have increased an average of $1.5 million per month. So far in 2008, the tanker orderbook for newbuildings has grown from about 185 VLCCs to over 260 VLCCs today. Shipyards saw approximately 100 new orders placed worth an incremental $15 billion! ## New Orders
Newbuilding Price The tune has changed somewhat in the past few weeks however, with more credit contractions further complicating liquidity. Shipyards are now at comfortable capacities with scheduled deliveries booked through 2011 and the lack of financing will serve to further moderate the ordering spree in the immediate term. Crude Oil Demand On a short-term basis, psychology can contribute to sharp swings in markets, either stock or shipping, however fundamentals will pervade in the longer-term. Lower crude oil prices could help lessen the pressure on the economy and it stands to reason that crude oil demand could regain some legs as a result.
Demand for ships is not going away, but the wait-and-see approach will no doubt be widely adopted on the asset side. Shipowners are certainly facing a sparser lending environment; however, the uncertainty in the market will create opportunities for some gutsier players that are ready to strike.
