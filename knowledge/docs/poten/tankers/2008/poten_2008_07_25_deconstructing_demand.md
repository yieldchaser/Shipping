## Deconstructing Demand
Although crude oil prices have been over $100 per barrel for several months, worldwide demand has yet to be materially affected. Some recent evidence indicates that demand in the United States may now be on the decline; in addition, it seems that United States consumption might be losing its title as the barometer of worldwide sentiment. The International Energy Agency pegged global oil demand for the second quarter of 2008 at just over 86 million barrels per day, marking a decline of less than one percent from the previous quarter. ## Looking East
The real growth story for demand continues to be East of Suez, primarily in China, India and other emerging economies. The two major factors facilitating demand growth in these countries are industrialization and the roles of government in commodity markets. The growth of the industry in these economies has contributed to a rapid rise in transportation and industrial fuel consumption. The political environment has enabled ravenous consumption growth by protecting end consumers from the rapid escalation of prices.
Although some countries have decreased subsidies offered to motorists, there is little reason to believe these countries will incorporate free market pricing in the near future. Continued growth in the East, supplied by areas other than the Arabian Gulf, should be the main incremental ton-mile driver in the shipping market.
