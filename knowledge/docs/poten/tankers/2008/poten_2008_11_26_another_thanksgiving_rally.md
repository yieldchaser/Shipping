## Another Thanksgiving Rally?
On this day last year, the tanker market was on the brink of one of the most dramatic rate spikes of all time - although no one saw it coming. On the Wednesday before the traditional Thanksgiving holiday in the United States, VLCC rates were Worldscale 85 and ships were earning about $44,000 per day. What a difference a day makes as rates spiked 50 points on Thursday leading to the strongest 12 month period for tanker rates in recent history. The primary cause of last November's run-up has been widely analyzed and included the sharp increase in worldwide VLCC fixture activity, as noted below, combined with limited vessel availability in the Arabian Gulf.

## Blame it on 4Q!
The fourth quarter on average (November in particular) experiences the highest freight rates. Yet this year has to-date proven to be an exception to the rule driven no doubt by reduced VLCC spot market activity. November's 96 Arabian Gulf liftings were lower than the previous months of August, September and October, which averaged 105 liftings in line with the two-year average. As shown in the preceding chart, the number of liftings from the Arabian Gulf can fluctuate between a minimum of 95 and a maximum of 116, with worldwide activity ranging from 127 to 155. Since this November's activity was on the low-end of this range, shuffling of the fixture schedules would seem to favor higher activity in December.

## Are there enough ships?
The chart below shows the daily number of VLCCs that will be available in the next 30 days in the Arabian Gulf. The vessel availability today is at generally the same level as it was last year. VLCC availability is generally inversely related to rates and even a slight contraction in supply can have a substantial effect on the psychology of shipowners and charterers.

## Expect the unexpected
On the day before Thanksgiving last year, it was difficult to see the run-up brewing. Generally softer sentiment in the market often leads some to believe it's the beginning of the end, but the tanker market has certainly been known to prove pessimists wrong. It can be challenging and lonely to find bright spots in the market today. But it does not take much - just a few ships out of position (or too many in position) - to send the market spiraling.
