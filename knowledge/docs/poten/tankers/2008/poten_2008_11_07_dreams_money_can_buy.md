## Dreams Money Can Buy
The United States of America has a new president. The "Chief Executive" position is to be passed over to Senator Barack Obama in January 2009 and it seems that the new team in the White House has their work cut out for them. The next year will likely prove to be trying for the American people for a variety of reasons both economic and social. Change will come, but it will be largely influenced by the general state of the economy. Although the duration of the economic downturn remains to be seen, it will be significantly influenced by the actions of policy makers in the coming months. Gasoline prices have softened considerably since the highs seen early in the year, as shown in the chart below, but energy remains a key focus for the President-elect.

## US Gasoline Prices
The ailing economy has created challenges for many industries, but energy related businesses have tended to fare better than most. Oil companies have reported another quarter of strong profits (helped largely by high oil prices) and tanker companies have enjoyed record earnings so far in 2008. But with the precipitous drop in crude prices record profits may be hard to repeat. The coming change in leadership in the White House is projected to have decidedly mixed effects on the oil and shipping businesses.

## New Energy for America
Although this year's presidential election was largely determined by the poor management of the economy, energy issues played a key role. The President-elect has some ideas for tackling energy issues. Some of the key points of the President-elect Obama energy plan are as follows:

## x Provide Short-Term Relief To American Families
The first action is an expected enactment of a Windfall Profits Tax on oil companies as a way to pass a "rebate" check to consumers. A policy such as this would ostensibly deter companies from making investments to increase supply as the upside economic potential is arbitrarily capped, further deepening our dependence on foreign oil. Crude oil prices have declined by 40% since July 2008 so it may prove to be more challenging to impose the tax in the current price environment.

## Venezuela within 10 Years
Actual achievement of this goal would certainly negatively impact shipping. The US currently relies on the Middle East and Venezuela for 3.7 million barrels per day of imported crude oil, according to the Energy Information Administration (EIA). As part of the plan the administration will promote so called "responsible" domestic production of oil and gas leases, however a "use it or lose it" attitude will be adopted toward the producing companies. This policy would implicitly force companies to develop oil and gas fields with little consideration for respective capital expenditure plans.
Increased fuel economy standards and tax credits for advanced vehicles are also being offered as methods for energy conservation.

## x Create Millions of New Green Jobs
Clean coal technology is being heralded as a panacea for greenhouse gas emissions and a way to create jobs. Developing the technology makes sense as coal is a plentiful natural resource in America, however developing new jobs could potentially mean cannibalizing other jobs in the energy sector as a result. Additionally, this technology needs to be developed. The Administration, according to senior advisors, is committed to staying the course on ethanol as well. Creating millions of jobs is no small task and it would be surprising if this actually comes about.

## x Reduce our Greenhouse Gas Emissions 80 Percent by 2050
The plan hopes to make the U.S. a leader on climate change by addressing the U.N. Framework Convention on Climate Change. A market-based cap-and-trade system would also be implemented.

## Visions of Grandeur
The reality is that alternatives to petroleum are not particularly economic when factoring the costs associated with bringing them into mainstream use. The current highly efficient gasoline market will make it difficult to succeed in these initiatives without passing on significant costs to the consumers.
Without tremendous subsidies, to the detriment of the taxpayers, it will be very difficult to make these projects come to fruition. On a practical basis, the petroleum industry is safe for many years to come and pet projects passed by legislators in the U.S. will end up stinging American pockets far more than $2.50 per gallon gasoline. While the U.S. consumer is certainly conditioned to demand low gasoline prices if the new President really wanted to reduce demand he should put substantial tax on gasoline - say $5.00 per gallon. That might actually bring about something more closely resembling energy independence (to the detriment of tanker owners!), but don't count on such an obvious solution to achieve that goal.
