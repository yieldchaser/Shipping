## Moving Target
In an effort to head off a potential collapse in the oil market, the Organization of Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC) has moved their November meeting ahead to next week. The market has already begun to speculate that the general softening in crude oil prices means a cut in production is afoot. Meanwhile, future demand picture for crude oil remains clouded by the uncertainty of economic recession in key consuming markets - particularly the United States. Member countries are to decide whether increasing, decreasing or leaving production levels unchanged will be most beneficial to market stabilization in the coming months. By controlling such a large degree of both upstream production and spare capacity in the marketplace, decisions made by OPEC can have a potentially widespread impact on crude oil supply and in turn prices.

## Finding Balance
OPEC countries are attempting to achieve the highest price yielded in the marketplace without destroying demand. Quite recently demand for crude oil has slackened in prime consuming regions such as the United States and Europe signaling prices were unsustainable to consumers. OPEC has acknowledged the shift in consumption and recently cut expectations for fourth quarter 2008 demand by 450,000 barrels per day.

## Outlook in Sep-07
As high oil prices have begun to impact demand worldwide, consumers have reacted by changing behavior, if only temporarily.
Many observers speculate that OPEC may cut production next week - even up to as much as 2 million barrels per day! Since OPEC is perceptive of consumer behavior and weakening demand, it seems unlikely that this deep a cut in supply will materialize.
OPEC can get more mileage from sustaining production and ultimately restoring consumer confidence that the market will be adequately supplied with crude oil in the future.
The psychological impact of a potential cut in supply has already helped spike the price of oil today - imagine the effects of a real cut in production. Given the uncertain health of the world economy, now more than ever, OPEC should be circumspect of action that could further erode demand.
