## What are the prospects for clean Aframaxes?
As we discussed in one of our Weekly Opinions last month (" Do You Want Coatings With That "), many shipowners that are interested in the Aframax tanker segment are choosing to build coated tankers (LR2s) to maintain the optionality to trade dirty or clean. In the first half of 2024, clean Aframax rates were very strong, albeit quite volatile. However, over the summer, rates for LR2s have fallen precipitously. In this week's Tanker Opinion, we will delve deeper into the reasons LR2 rates were so strong in recent years, why they have come off and what might be in store for the future for this segment.
LR2 tankers are typically used for the long-haul transportation of refined products. Their size gives them a competitive advantage relative to LR1s and particularly MRs. The two key commodities transported on LR2s are diesel/gasoil (40% of the total since 2021 and naphtha, 37%). Gasoline is a distant third with 12% of the total, followed by jet fuel/kerosine. Saudi Arabia and India are traditionally the largest exporters of diesel on LR2s. Combined, they account for almost 50% of the diesel/gasoil trade on LR2s since 2021. The UAE, South Korea, Taiwan and Kuwait are also meaningful exporters of diesel/gasoil on LR2s. The second largest product moved on LR2s is naphtha. Main exporters are the UAE, Qatar, Russia, Algeria and Kuwait. The naphtha is primarily destined for the petrochemical industry in countries in the Far East (South Korea, Japan, China, Taiwan and Singapore are the top 5). Given the geographical distribution of both the exporters and the importers, it is no surprise that moving 75,000 Metric Tons (MT) of naphtha on the Arabian Gulf to the Far East route is the benchmark route for LR2 tankers (TC1 on the Baltic Exchange).
A review of the key LR2 trading routes shows that the Pacific is the dominant export region. Some 80% of LR2 voyages originate in the Pacific Basin, versus only 20% in the Atlantic. Within Asia, it is the "Wider Arabian Sea" area (comprised of the West Coast of India, the Middle East Gulf, and the Red Sea/Gulf of Aden that make up the bulk of the exports (78% of the total). Most of the refined products moving on LR2s stay in the Pacific. South Korea, Singapore and Japan are the top destinations. Australia is another key importer of clean products on LR2s. In recent years, Australia has closed most of its refining capacity, becoming highly dependent on imports for their domestic needs. Given the distances involved, Australia is also an important driver of ton-mile demand. Within the Atlantic Basin, most of the LR2 imports flow to Northwest Europe, the Mediterranean and West Africa.
The war in Ukraine as well as the conflict in the Middle East have had an impact on LR2 product flows. Both events have scrambled trade flows and have had a major impact on ton-mile demand. Europe, for example sourced a significant share of clean petroleum products (mostly diesel) from Russia. This was primarily an MR trade. After the invasion of Ukraine, Europe switched to alternative sources, such as Kuwait, Saudi Arabia, the UAE as well as India and South Korea. This provided a massive boost to LR2 ton-mile demand, because the importers switched from MRs to the larger more economical Aframax sized vessels on these long-haul trades. LR2's received another boost at the end of 2023 when the Houthi's started to disrupt transits through the Red Sea and vessels were forced to take the long way around the Cape of Good Hope on their way from the Pacific to Europe. Both geopolitical events strongly supported LR2 freight rates.
However, during the summer, LR2 rates weakened significantly. Europe started to import more products from the U.S., reducing its utilization of LR2s and switching to MRs. Refining margins in the Far East have been under pressure, reducing naphtha demand from the petrochemical sector. Lastly, a growing number of very large crude carriers have "cleaned up" and are being employed in the clean trades, cannibalizing the LR2 market.
However, we do believe that freight rates for clean Aframaxes will stage a recovery during the winter. VLCCs will revert to their normal employment as higher demand for crude transportation will pull vessels back from the clean trades. At the same time, a seasonal recovery of Asian petrochemical demand is expected to boost demand for long-haul product trades.
