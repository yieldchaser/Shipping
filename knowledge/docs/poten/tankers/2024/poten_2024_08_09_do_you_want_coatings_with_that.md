## Summary
Owners choose to pay a premium for optionality
A comparison of the newbuilding orderbook for Aframax and LR2 tankers shows a strong preference for the coated or clean version of this versatile tanker segment. There are only 40 crude Aframaxes on order, representing a very modest 4.7% of the existing fleet of 854 tankers. The LR2 orderbook, on the other hand consists of 158 vessels, a massive 53.6% of the existing fleet (295 vessels).

## Owners choose to pay a premium for optionality
A comparison of the newbuilding orderbook for Aframax and LR2 tankers shows a strong preference for the coated or clean version of this versatile tanker segment. There are only 40 crude Aframaxes on order, representing a very modest 4.7% of the existing fleet of 854 tankers. The LR2 orderbook, on the other hand consists of 158 vessels, a massive 53.6% of the existing fleet (295 vessels). What is the reason for this dramatic shift from dirty to clean tankers? We will try to address some of main drivers in today's Tanker Opinion.
Let's start with some background information. Aframax crude oil tankers have been around for a long time. Aframaxes are typically defined as tankers with a deadweight tonnage capacity ranging from 80,000 - 120,000 which can carry around 600,000 barrels of oil (exact capacity depends on the particular dimensions of the ship and the API gravity of the crude oil). The term "Aframax" originates from the Average Freight Rate Assessment (AFRA) system, developed by Shell in 1954 to standardize shipping rates and contracts. Aframaxes are designed to navigate smaller ports that cannot accommodate larger tankers like Suezmaxes and VLCCs. Aframaxes are very popular in regions such as the Black Sea, Baltic, North Sea and Caribbean.
In recent years, Aframax demand has received a boost from several unrelated developments. Western sanctions imposed on Russia after their invasion of Ukraine has led to an increase in Aframax ton-mile demand as Russian crudes need to be moved over longer distances. Since the opening of the Trans Mountain Expansion pipeline on the West Coast of Canada, Aframax utilization in that area dramatically increased as well.
Coated or clean Aframaxes, known as Long Range 2 (LR2) tankers are a more recent phenomenon. These vessels, which are the same size as the Aframax crude oil tanker, have coated cargo tanks and advanced tank-cleaning systems, which allows them to switch between dirty and clean cargoes (although typically not on a voyage-by-voyage basis). LR2 tankers started to gain in popularity in the early 2000s, due to a growing demand for long-haul transportation of clean petroleum products, in particular from the Middle East to Asia and (to a lesser extend) Europe.
Aframaxes and LR2s used to operate in different markets and the competition between these sectors was limited. LR2s are more sophisticated and more expensive to build, so they tended to operate in the "premium" clean trades. As these vessels aged and the maintenance of the coatings, as well as the tank cleaning systems became more expensive, the LR2s would gradually transition to the dirty trades. As Chart 1 shows, that is still largely the case. The youngest LR2s (0-5 Yrs old) traded almost 60% in clean, while 50% of the vessels were still trading products when 6-10 Yrs old. However, by the time LR2s are over the age of 10 Yrs, they have predominantly switched to the dirty trades, with only 25% still engaged in the transportation of clean products.
Freight rates for clean and dirty Aframaxes are driven by different factors and don't necessarily move in tandem (see chart). Owners of LR2s have the flexibility to switch from clean to dirty (and back), which gives them the opportunity to takes advantage of longer-term rate differentials. However, there are costs involved in "cleaning up" an LR2 after it has been trading dirty, and it typically takes several voyages to make a complete switch, so owners don't do this unless they foresee a sustained period of outperformance.
It is understandable that owners opt to pay a few million more for a coated Aframax, giving them the flexibility to switch from clean to dirty trades. The last three years have shown the potential benefits of this choice. The dirty Aframax trades benefited significantly from the Russian sanctions since 2022, while the clean trades received a boost from the Red Sea/Suez Canal disruptions. Properly positioned LR2s have been able to take advantage of both.
