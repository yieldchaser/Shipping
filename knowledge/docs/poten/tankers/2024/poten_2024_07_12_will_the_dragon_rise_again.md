## Chinese Oil Demand Growth Slows Down
Chinese oil demand has been growing rapidly over the past decades as the country developed and became the manufacturing hub for the world. According to the IEA, Chinese oil demand increased from 4.6 million barrel per day (Mb/d) in 2000 to 16.6 Mb/d in 2023, growing from 6.0% of global demand to 16.3%. Over this period, 47% of global oil demand growth originated in China. However, Chinese oil demand in recent months has disappointed and many articles about oil price movements published recently mentioned market worries about Chinese oil demand developments as a reason for 'low' oil prices. As the Chinese oil market represents such a crucial part of tanker demand, especially for crude tankers, it is wise to keep an eye on Chinese demand prospects. This week we will take a look at the underlying dynamics.
The top graph shows the development of Chinese petroleum product demand as reported to JODI. It shows a strong recovery after the country lifted many Covid restrictions in late 2022, but more recently the growth has leveled off, although demand for the first quarter still exceeds demand during the same period in 2023 and similar patterns have not been unusual in the past.
The key question is how Chinese oil demand will develop in the future. Analysts are generally positive on the outlook for demand from the petrochemical industry. The IEA forecasts that feedstock demand for the petrochemical industry in China will increase by 1.3 Mb/d from 2023 to 2030 as the country will improve self-reliance in polymers. Jet fuel demand is also expected to increase as passenger numbers continue to improve, although oil demand growth is expected to also be affected by efficiency gains in modern aircraft. The popularity of Electric Vehicles (EV) in China will curtail gasoline demand growth. In 2023, 60% of global EV sales occurred in China and the IEA estimates that by 2030 30% of all cars on the road in China will be electric. Diesel and gasoil demand is driven by road transportation and from industrial uses such as construction. Housing construction has suffered from the property crisis as the inventory of unsold real estate has grown, but the government has announced several measures such as lower down payment requirements for mortgages and providing funding for local governments to acquire unsold inventory for conversion to social housing.
The major forecasting organizations all expect continued growth for China: IEA forecasts Chinese oil demand to increase by 0.5 Mb/d in 2024 and by 0.4 Mb/d in 2025. OPEC is more optimistic with growth of 0.7 Mb/d for 2024 and 0.4 MB/d for 2025 while the US EIA predicts 0.3 Mb/d growth for 2024 and 0.4 Mb/d for 2025. In the medium term, the IEA expects Chinese demand to continue growing until 2030, for a total growth of 2.5 Mb/d between 2023 and 2030. OPEC, which published their long-term outlook in October 2023, predicts 2.6 Mb/d of growth between 2022 and 2030 while the EIA forecasts (also in October 2023) 2.4 Mb/d of growth over the same period.
The second graph shows the Chinese crude oil imports by origin. The chart shows that crude oil import volumes have levelled off over the last 6 months. Imports from Russia have increased in the last two years, although for most of the period such imports went mainly to teapot refiners in Shandong province. Recently, these refiners have suffered from low utilization rates and thus lower imports due to weak refining margins.
Chinese crude imports will likely increase again once refineries come out of maintenance season. Vortexa recently reported that the Chinese government has asked oil companies to increase their oil purchases for the SPR by 8 million tons (~58.5 Mln bbl) by March 2025. They expect that Russian exports will be the main beneficiaries of the SPR requirement.
While Chinese oil demand and crude oil imports are currently under pressure, the outlook generally remains positive. The main oil forecasting agencies are consistent in their relatively optimistic outlook for the coming years. With a limited oil tanker orderbook for delivery until 2025/26, it will only take a small improvement in demand for tanker rates to move up again.
