## Latin American producer shows enormous potential
Every oil production outlook for 2020 and beyond needs to consider the enormous potential represented by Guyana, a small Latin American country with a total population of about 800,000 tucked in between Venezuela, Brazil and Suriname (all oil producers). By far the largest player in the country is ExxonMobil, with its partners Hess and China National Offshore Oil Corp. (CNOOC). In 2015 Exxon made a discovery offshore Guyana in the Stabroek exploration block and since then it has found so much more oil that Guyana could become one of the largest oil producers in the world on a per capita basis. Production and exports are projected to reach 750,000 barrels per day (b/d) in 2025 supported by up to five Floating Production Storage and Offloading vessels (FPSO's). Since the production is offshore in deep water, there is ample flexibility for these export barrels to be sold and shipped anywhere in the world, including the Americas, Europe and Asia. Depending on the destinations, Guyana crude oil exports could support all the major crude oil vessel classes.
The story of Guyana's oil is one of perseverance. After it gained independence from the United Kingdom in 1966, companies have been looking for oil. Its neighboring countries, in particular Venezuela and Brazil have significant oil reserves, and the expectation was the same for Guyana. However, for decades, oil explorers only drilled dry holes. According to their website, ExxonMobil initiated oil and gas exploration activities in Guyana in 2008. Other sources date Exxon's involvement in Guyana back to 1999. Plagued by border disputes with Venezuela and Suriname and after a collapse in the oil price in 2014, several international oil companies pulled out of Guyana. However, Exxon remained committed. It brought in new partners and in May 2015, it announced that it struck oil. Since then Guyana has become one of the most successful oil exploration sites in the world. Since its first discovery, ExxonMobil and its partners have made 13 discoveries in the Stabroek exploration block offshore Guyana. The company estimates that the gross recoverable resource for the Stabroek Block is more than 6 billion oil equivalent barrels.
Developing all the resources and bringing it to market will take time. The first (Liza-1) well was discovered in May 2015. The Liza Phase 1 project will start up in early 2020 using the Liza Destiny FPSO and produce 120,000 b/d. Lisa Phase 2, which will be developed using a second FPSO is expected to start in the middle of 2022 and could produce up to 220,000 b/d. A third phase of development in Guyana will use an FPSO vessel designed to produce approximately 180,000 b/d by 2023.
Two additional FPSOs can push the production up to 750,000 b/d by 2025.
Liza is a medium, low sulphur crude with an API gravity of 32.1 and a sulfur content of 0.51%. It is a general purpose crude, which should provide a good yield of middle distillates and gasoline. Because of its relatively high quality, Guyana's crude will flow easily from the reservoir and the low sulfur content means that the crude will be less corrosive and the cost of refining the oil will be lower.
For the tanker market, the prospects of Guyana crude oil exports are very attractive. The oil fields are being developed and operated by Exxon, one of the largest and most experienced operators. Furthermore the oil fields are located offshore in deep water, making them easily accessible for tankers of different sizes. As discussed previously, it is a high quality oil that can and will be marketed worldwide. There is no domestic market in Guyana, so a full 100% of production will be available for export.
At this point it is difficult to say which tanker segments will benefit the most from Guyana's crude oil. Oil hasn't started flowing yet and when ExxonMobil and its partners start marketing it, it will become clear what the key markets will be. Guyana's location and the quality of its crude could make both the America's and Europe attractive target markets, but it is likely that a significant portion of these new crude flows will find their way to the fastest growing oil markets in Asia, making VLCCs the most likely beneficiary.
