## The outlook for oil demand is not as bright
Until recently, the consensus view on crude prices, oil supply and demand as well as tanker rates, was that we would face challenges in the first half of the year, but that the outlook for the second half of the year and into 2020 would be much brighter. However, there are indications that there could be some risks on the horizon, in particular as it relates to oil demand. While we are not predicting gloom and doom, it is prudent to check some of these factors and see how they may impact the tanker market in the next 6-12 months.
Several investment banks have issued reports during the last two weeks with warnings about a slowdown in oil demand in some of the largest oil consumers in the world. According to Morgan Stanley, preliminary reports from OECD countries (the US, Japan, Korea and Australia) combined with non-OECD countries China, India, Brazil and Thailand, saw oil demand decline by 480 Kb/d year-on-year in March. In April, this group (representing almost 50% of global oil demand) returned to growth, primarily on the back of an apparent turnaround in China (although one should take early oil demand statistics from China with a grain of salt). Nevertheless, the two months combined shows almost no growth y-o-y for this group of countries.
The slowdown in oil demand growth coincided with rising prices. Brent crude oil went from $65/barrel at the beginning of March to $72/barrel at the end of April. These price increases were probably driven by the perception that the oil market was tightening as a result of falling OPEC supply, in particular from Iran and Venezuela. At the same time geopolitical tensions were rising. Since the middle of May, prices have come down rapidly. At the moment, WTI is hovering around $62/barrel, well below the early March prices. The recent price weakness has been caused by the perception that despite the challenges facing Iran, Venezuela and Russia (Druzhba pipeline contamination), the oil market remains well supplied. The focus of the oil market seems to have switched to the weak macro outlook. This negative market sentiment is reinforced by significant crude stock builds in the US.
So, where do we go from here? Is the world heading towards a significant slowdown in oil demand growth, which would not bode well for the tanker market, or is there an alternative narrative that is more positive and equally credible?
Demand growth in China and India may be more resilient than the pessimists expect despite the on-going trade wars. China is particularly vulnerable to oil price shocks given its rapidly growing oil dependence and especially in light of the recent US oil sanctions on China's allies Iran and Venezuela. Therefore, it is possible that China will use the lower oil prices to boost oil imports and add to its Strategic Petroleum reserves on national security grounds. This would support tanker ton-mile demand.
The growth in US crude oil stocks is having a big impact on market sentiment, but it is not necessarily an indication of lower oil demand. Prolonged and extensive seasonal refinery maintenance in the US in anticipation of the IMO 2020 change in bunker fuel specifications is an important factor as well. There has also been a slew of unplanned refinery outages this spring due to the extensive flooding in the US Midwest.
The development of oil prices from here will probably have a big impact on the oil and tanker market over the next 6-12 months. If prices weaken further, OPEC will likely decide to keep production cuts in place or even cut further. This would be detrimental for ton-mile demand and tanker rates. If oil prices stabilize or increase, a relaxation of the production cuts (official or unofficial) will put more oil on the water and support the tanker market going into the winter. On 1 January 2020, the tanker market would get another boost from the mandatory lowering of the Sulphur content of bunkers.
