## St. Croix refinery restart will impact crude and product flows
By the end of 2019, Limetree Bay Refining is planning to restart (part of) the old Hess, St. Croix refinery in the U.S. Virgin Islands. Limetree Bay is owned by a consortium of ArcLight Capital (79%), Freepoint Commodities (7%) and a syndicate of other investors. The initial processing capacity of the facility is 200,000 b/d. The original refinery, which was built by Amerada Hess in the 1960s, had a design capacity of approximately 700,000 b/d and was one of the largest refineries in the world at the time. The Caribbean was a major refining center in the 1960s and 1970s, when the oil majors built refineries in Curacao, Venezuela, the Bahamas, as well as Panama, Trinidad and Puerto Rico. Proximity to the oil resources of Venezuela and Trinidad (at the time), tax incentives as well as a large supply of local labor with relatively low wages all played a role. The Hess refinery enjoyed the advantages mentioned above, but also benefitted from its location in U.S. territory and derived significant benefits from a Federal entitlement program that was meant to mitigate the disadvantages of having to source crude oil in the open market. The Hess refinery was also exempt from the Jones Act, which meant that they could import crude oil from Alaska and export refined products to the continental U.S. on foreign flag vessels. From the 1970s until the early 1990s, Amerada Hess imported a significant portion of its crude oil from Alaska on foreign flag VLCCs. During that period, the U.S. East Coast was an important market for its products and Hess used a mix of U.S. Flag and foreign flag assets for its transportation. Some 50 years later, the Jones Act exemption is still in place even though St. Croix is a U.S. territory since America bought the Virgin Islands from Denmark in 1917. Limetree Bay will therefore have some of the same advantages as Hess used to have.
The current setup provides Limetree with a number of advantages. It has negotiated a tolling agreement with BP, where BP will provide a certain volume of crude to the refinery and will sell the refined product on the market. This arrangement limits the refinery's exposure to the market and gives their investors a certain fixed return. It is unclear where BP will source the crude for the refinery, but it has ample options in the region; offshore Gulf of Mexico as well as onshore shale oil from the Eagle Ford or Permian Basin.
The products from the refinery can be sold internationally as well as on the U.S. East Coast (including Florida), where Limetree can be competitive versus U.S. Gulf refiners due to its Jones Act exemption. The U.S. East Coast is short of gasoline and diesel, even more so after the 335,000 b/d PES refinery in Philadelphia shut down.
The refinery's central location makes it also an attractive option for product sales into Europe, West Africa and South America. The facility has ample storage and the terminal can accommodate a wide range of tankers, including MRs, LR1s and even LR2s and LR3s (Suezmax size product tankers).
Another benefit that Limetree has is that it is capable of producing IMO 2020 compliant fuels that can be sold regionally and globally. The refinery has significant desulfurization capacity as well as a coker unit to meet the needs of the global shipping community. It also has significant additional product storage capacity onsite that it can market to generate additional revenue.
Although the Limetree Bay refinery has a number of inherent advantages, they also face some challenges: they are competing with U.S. Gulf refiners that have an advantage because of lower fuel cost. Compared to refiners in Europe or the Mediterranean, they have a higher risk of disruption due to adverse weather like hurricanes.
The startup of the Limetree Bay facility should be a net positive for the shipping markets. Although it is not yet clear where they will source their crude and where they may sell their products, it is unavoidable that they will use seaborne transportation to do both. Given the proximity of both sources of crude as well as markets for products, we expect that Aframaxes, Suezmaxes as well as MR product tankers are the most likely beneficiaries of the startup of this facility.
