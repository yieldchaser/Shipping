## Summary
Bears in the China Shop
Recent figures indicating a cooling of Chinese growth have generated fresh concern over the health of the global economy. The tanker market in particular has been banking on a rally of non‐OECD petroleum demand, seeing projected increases in Chinese petroleum consumption as one of the few rays of light in this year's dark market. However, even in the face of possible moderation, shifting trade patterns and expansion of overall Chinese involvement in petroleum spot trade still leaves potential for development of ton mile demand in the coming years.

## Bears in the China Shop
Recent figures indicating a cooling of Chinese growth have generated fresh concern over the health of the global economy. The tanker market in particular has been banking on a rally of non‐OECD petroleum demand, seeing projected increases in Chinese petroleum consumption as one of the few rays of light in this year's dark market. However, even in the face of possible moderation, shifting trade patterns and expansion of overall Chinese involvement in petroleum spot trade still leaves potential for development of ton mile demand in the coming years.
In its latest Oil Market Report, the International Energy Agency (IEA) estimated that Chinese petroleum demand will average 9.6 mbd over 2011. This reflects a downward revision of 190 kbd since the beginning of the year, and annual growth at less than half of last year's pace, as seen in the chart below.

## Total Chinese Demand
y‐o‐y Growth Additionally, customs data released on Thursday show that China's September crude oil imports fell 12% year‐on‐year, and have remained below 5 million barrels per day for four consecutive months. Troubles in the United States and Europe do have the potential to negatively impact China's rate of growth, as Chinese petroleum demand is driven largely by manufacturing and export activity. Even so, the IEA now estimates that Chinese consumption is set to contribute to more than half of incremental worldwide petroleum demand in 2011, and is expected to increase by an additional 480 kbd during 2012.

## Unipec
PetroChina Day Harvest Non‐Chinese Charterers This growth has been driven largely by the activity of Chinese charterers, who are expected to account for an impressive 30% of total VLCC spot fixture volumes in 2011.
While China's growing domestic demand for petroleum products can account for much of this new activity, it is also attributable to increased involvement of Chinese entities in global petroleum trade. According to spot fixture data, Chinese charterers have lifted over 50 VLCC fuel oil cargoes year to date from the Caribbean to trading hubs in Southeast Asia. These long haul volumes have accounted for over 10% of total Chinese VLCC spot fixture activity reported since the beginning of 2011. While future volumes on this specific trade are likely to depend on arbitrage opportunities resulting from relative fuel oil pricing, trends seen over the past five years underscore the potential for increased Chinese market presence to direct the evolution of global trade patterns.
The growth in Chinese demand for ships in the Atlantic Basin has been particularly remarkable. It is estimated that total eastbound VLCC trade out of the Caribbean by Chinese charterers has grown 250% over the past five years, as seen in the graph on the following page.
