## Show Us the Money, and We'll Show You the Barrels
Despite the generally steady refined product demand climate in the United States, it is interesting to note resurgence in refining activity. Over the course of the summer, the market on the East Coast of the United States (PADD I) has seen a marked increase in both structural refining capacity and crude oil throughput. It is curious that as capacity increases in a steady demand environment domestically, beleaguered consumers of retail gasoline have seen little price relief.
Not terribly long ago it was thought by many that the sun was setting on the golden age of refining in the United States. The onset of the financial crisis brought with it the shuttering of nearly 700,000 barrels per day of refining capacity in the continental United States - and nearly 1 million barrels per day including Aruba. While it seemed many units were mothballed for good, a recovery in refining margins quickly changed the general psyche. Focusing on the East Coast in particular, the chart below shows a step up in overall capacity in April and then again in July and the resultant overall increase in crude oil runs.

With margins trending upwards on the East Coast, it is not surprising that incremental barrels are finding their way into the system at large. What is perhaps more noteworthy is where these barrels are ultimately being absorbed.

## Turning Out the Lights
Since not all refineries are created equal, an analysis of the specific crude oil preferences for each refinery reveals that the pricing dynamics of the crude oil market at large may be the master puppeteer pulling the strings for increasing throughput.
The chart below demonstrates the relative price between various crude oil grades. The capability and preference of a refinery to digest a particular grade of crude oil will impacts the product it can supply and margins it is able to generate. East Coast refineries represent a smorgasbord of interests. The shift in pricing power towards heavier grades has created an incentive for those that can process the material to increase their runs.

## Light Oil Discount
Net crude oil inputs to PADD I refineries today represent about 96% of the operable crude oil distillation capacity available at the start of 2011. Over the same time period, overall inputs have seen a 38% increase as compared to the 15% increase in capacity. Furthermore, increases in utilization can be expected in the near term if the heavy crude oil discount persists.

## Thirst from Abroad
With overall demand for petroleum product in the United States generally flat, considering ethanol inclusion, local customers do not generally appear hungry for the prospect of more supply. Instead, it seems foreign markets are lending support for additional refinery runs.
Although freight rates remain generally soft for all tanker sectors, activity reported in the market could suggest the emergence of new trades.
The chart below highlights the trends in the clean tanker markets with respect to excess local supply on the US Atlantic Coast. Reported spot fixture activity indicates higher exports to the Caribbean, UK Continent and Mediterranean on an annualized basis. Overall volumes for the year could be up as much as 40% year‐on‐year.
