Crude Tanker Comments
The VLCC market was a mixed bag this week. Early on, the Atlantic market leaned in
favour of charterers. Several failures in the previous week and Chinese vessels
ballasting west saw the position list creep up against limited cargo. However, by mid-
week, Atlantic owners were putting up resistance; Brazil-East held at WS 132 to WS
135, and WAF-East stayed near WS 140. The main driver behind this market sentiment
remains East of Suez. Earlier in the week, routes inside high-risk areas of the AG and
Red Sea saw eye-watering earnings, with rates reaching WS 510 to WS 515. Even safer
loading areas in the GOO, such as Fujairah and MAF, stayed firm at around WS 170.
However, a recent Houthi strike on an oil tanker in the Red Sea highlights just how
uncertain those headline numbers really are. If heightened security risks mean that
these routes are no longer viable for most owners, those high rates will have much
less impact on setting a floor for the Atlantic. Adding to the uncertainty, the market is
also closely watching news of a potential agreement between Iran and Oman
regarding a commercial shipping channel through the Strait of Hormuz, which raises
even more questions about future transit security and regional dynamics. For now,
however, strong Middle East returns continue to give Western owners solid leverage.
For Suezmaxes, the week started with Atlantic sentiment under pressure, as limited
WAFR enquiry allowed tonnage to grow and led TD20 to around WS 217.5. CPC
continued to trade at a high premium, a reflection of the ongoing security concerns
and limited number of owners wanting to call Black Sea. As the week progressed,
Atlantic rates corrected sharply, with eastern ballasters flooding the market and
charterers picking friendly faces off the list.
CPC firmed throughout the week, reaching WS 535 for Med, although reports of
loading suspensions added some uncertainty around the premium pricing. In the USG,
a tightening list and a strong WTI demand into Europe helped rates find a floor at WS
160 after a 30-point drop for TA, with charterers increasingly reaching for tonnage
given the favourable economics. By week’s end, the Atlantic appeared to have found a
bottom, with TD20 at WS 175. Charterers returned to the market once lower rates
stabilised, while CPC received support and continues to trade as a two-tier market.
The Med Aframax market faced pressure this week as ample tonnage continued to
outweigh demand, resulting in steady rate corrections and soft sentiment. While
activity levels improved and the position list gradually tightened, downward pressure
on earnings persisted due to the large vessel overhang. By midweek, stronger enquiry
helped trim the front end of the tonnage list further, leading to signs that rates had
found a floor and raising the possibility of a rebound should activity be sustained.
However, limited fresh business at the end of the week left rates flat, and charterers
can continue to lower them. TD19 ends the week at WS 240.
The North Sea Aframax market began the week showing signs of stability, with rates
appearing to have found a floor. However, sentiment gradually weakened as local
activity remained subdued, the position list grew, and pressure from softer
surrounding markets weighed on fundamentals. Initial support from a firmer US Gulf
market faded as momentum there slowed, further reducing confidence. With limited
enquiry and vessel availability increasing, charterers gained leverage and were
increasingly expected to test rates lower. By the end of the week, the overall tone had
softened, with rates still under pressure. TD7 sits at WS 212 on Friday.
Product Tanker Comments
This week in the AG LR2 market has been a “tale of two halves”. It began with a few
failures, taking some of the fledgling energy which started at the back end of last
week. There were then renewed attempts to make Arabian Gulf loadings, but these
deals also failed, which is unsurprising when considering the significant logistical and
safety concerns in play regarding the Strait of Hormuz along with the massive prices
asked in a response to the greater risk and to the tighter position list. In the “new
normal” market of Indian Ocean loadings, rates for STS Oman-East moved up five
points on the week to hit WS 190. USD 5 Mn was one of the early week failures for STS
Oman-West, and this is being retested again now with similar levels being likely in
play. The Red Sea has had a dormant period recently but was retested yesterday, with
USD 3.6 Mn being the new Westbound rate. It is a slight decrease from last done, but
with the dearth of enquiry, a much weaker X-Med Aframax market, and northbound
loading in the Red Sea being the safest option, lower numbers begin to make sense.
AG LR1s spent another week in limbo, fully at the mercy of continued geopolitical
tensions. Most public activity was squeezed into one day, on which several naphtha
stems were covered at WS 195 (and at WS 190). Westbound demand has been more
present this week, with USD 3 Mn publicly agreed for an STS Sohar-UKC run. Short-
haul business has not been particularly active, and freight levels have not been
pushed out into the market, but some strong competition could be expected for these
sorts of runs so that owners can remain in the area as AGW rates firm. All in all, there
is no great sign that the war in the Middle East will conclude soon, so market activity
can only remain pinned down for now.
There has been a total change of pace for the LR market in North Asia this week. There
has been more enquiry for both sizebands, much of it initiated by increased Chinese
exports. Several LR2 stems emerged in the market for Australia- and Westbound runs
and backhauls. So far, rates have been unimpressive; USD 4.75 Mn was reported on
subjects for Korea-UKC, with a Singapore option at USD 950,000, but as more cargoes
emerge, we expect owners to push rates north of these levels. The LR1 sector has also
seen an increase in activity, with Chinese exports featuring strongly. There has been
little reported in terms of fixing numbers, but sentiment is firming, and we could well
see the market tick up next week.
Med MR activity has been very subdued this week, with just five or six stems visible
on the surface. As a result, rates have come under pressure, with the lowest tests
sitting at WS 130 TA, WS 150 UKC, and WS 170 Med. Sentiment remains bearish
heading into the weekend and into next week. A softer USG market is expected to
attract fewer ballasters, which could leave the Med with a better-supplied tonnage
list towards the end of the second decade, adding further downward pressure to
rates.
Handysize activity was limited, too. The West Med has seen the better share of
enquiry, with cargoes continuing to trickle in. Combined with a tight tonnage list, this
has helped keep levels broadly stable at around WS 225 X-Med. Central and East Med
have remained better-supplied throughout the week, and the latest test is rumoured
to have been agreed at WS 210 this afternoon. With the window moving further into
the second decade and additional tonnage becoming available against limited
requirements, more pressure could mount here. Overall, sentiment is quiet to softer
heading into the weekend.
