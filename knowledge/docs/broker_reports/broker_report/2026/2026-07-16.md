The mystery list is piling up again, much like last week. This implies that the majority

equilibrium. The list has now tightened slightly but unless charterers have very

there are a reasonable number of vessels currently on berth in the West, as well as
tonnage arriving from the East for the forward window. In isolation, Suezmaxes
appear steady. However, there are reports that East-bound enquiries have reignited in
the Atlantic in response to heightened tensions in the Middle East, while the
Aframaxes are firming in the West - this is bringing Suezmaxes closer to being
competitive, albeit not quite there yet.
North Sea
All of the western Aframax markets are active and firm putting upward pressure on
rates. North Sea rates have jumped 30+ points so far this week and the pressure
looks likely to remain on charterers for the balance of the month stems. The list has
also been depleted with early tonnage heading out of the area both to US and
Mediterranean with owners looking to lock in longer voyages at high daily returns.
Next natural North Sea stems now in the 28-30 window.
Mediterranean
Who would have said that TD19 will be printed at over WS 300 in the middle of
summer. Expensive Suezmaxes, fuel inquiries and a bit of forward fixing have pushed
rates probably at all-time high for the month of July. Charterer are trying to find viable
solutions while owners from surrounding areas are ready to ballast into the
Mediterranean with the dates pushed slowly even to August.
