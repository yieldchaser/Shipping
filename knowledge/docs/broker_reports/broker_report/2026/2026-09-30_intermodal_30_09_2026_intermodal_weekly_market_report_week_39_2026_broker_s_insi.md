Week 39 | Tuesday 29th September 2026  Market Insight
By Nikos Tagoulis, Head of Research Department
The European gas market is heading into the winter heating sea-
son against a challenging backdrop, shaped by relatively low in-
ventories, constrained Middle Eastern LNG availability in view of
the phase-out of Russian gas. EU gas storage currently stands at
around 71%, compared with a five-year seasonal average of about
86%, leaving the market with a considerably thinner buffer head-
ing into winter. The phase -out will advance from 1 January 2027,
when long -term Russian LNG imports are due to be prohibited,
followed by remaining pipeline imports at the latest 1 November
2027. At the same time, the US is taking a growing share of Eu-
rope’s LNG supply, accounting for around 58% of EU imports in
2025 and about 62% so far in 2026, according to LSEG.
That exposure has been amplified by persistent strain in global
LNG supply, as Middle East disruptions curtail Qatari exports and
QatarEnergy extends force majeure on deliveries to several term
buyers into the winter period. In parallel, the company has turned
to US LNG to cover the resulting shortfall, buying replacement
cargoes and discussing further purchases to honour its contractu-
al commitments. Prolonged weakness in Qatari output is there-
fore tightening the market from both sides: fewer volumes are
available, while QatarEnergy ’s own buying is adding fresh de-
mand for the very flexible cargoes on which Europe relies.
US LNG has consequently become important not only to Europe ’s
own gas balance, but also as a source of flexibility when disrup-
tions emerge. The gradual removal of Russian gas from the Euro-
pean energy mix is increasing the call on alternative LNG suppli-
ers. In Asia, however, current competition for uncommitted vol-
umes has been less intense than the tight market might suggest.
With spot LNG expensive and coal offering a cheaper option for
power generation, the more price -sensitive Asian importers have
adopted a wait -and-see approach, limiting purchases in the hope
that prices will ease, although some markets, such as South Ko-
rea, still need to replenish inventories. This hesitancy is giving
European buyers more room to attract flexible cargoes as they
seek to rebuild storage.
As Europe becomes more reliant on US LNG, energy security is
taking on greater relevance within the broader transatlantic
framework. While it is unlikely to determine policy on its own, the
growing importance of US supply adds another consideration to
Europe’s strategic calculus, particularly as potential friction with
Washington could carry higher economic and energy -security
costs. LNG is therefore becoming not only a commercial issue, but
also a more significant component of Europe-US strategic ties.
At the same time, Europe is looking to broaden its LNG supply
base over the longer term, with Canada emerging as a potential
new source. The proposed Ksi Lisims LNG project in British Co-
lumbia, with initial export capacity of around 6 mtpa targeted for
2029, has already attracted interest from German buyers includ-
ing SEFE and Uniper, while France has also signalled interest in
future Canadian LNG supply. With deliveries expected to build
from the early 2030s, these developments underline Europe ’s
broader effort to diversify its LNG portfolio and reduce concentra-
tion over time.
For LNG shipping, the key factor will be the arbitrage between
Europe and Asia. A sufficiently wide Asian premium to offset the
higher costs of the longer voyage would encourage more US car-
goes to head east, supporting tonne -mile demand, while stronger
European pricing would favour shorter -haul Atlantic trades. At
the same time, the phase -out of Russian LNG imports could pro-
vide additional support to ton -mile demand as Europe replaces
nearby Russian volumes with cargoes sourced from more distant
suppliers. With Europe needing to rebuild storage and price -
sensitive Asian buyers holding back amid elevated prices, more
US cargoes could be drawn to the Continent, particularly if alter-
native supply remains constrained.
In these market conditions, Europe increasingly depends on its
ability to compete for flexible LNG cargoes amid constrained sup-
ply. Low inventories, reduced Qatari availability and the phase -out
of Russian volumes are reinforcing the role of US LNG in the Euro-
pean balance, while Asian importers could return to the market
more actively during the winter heating season, or should prices
retreat. With LNG crucial to Europe ’s energy security, growing
reliance on US supply increases concentration risk, while also
adding a new strategic dimension to transatlantic diplomacy.

Last week, ship recycling markets saw mixed trends in Subcon-
tinent, with India and Pakistan showing weak sentiment, while
Bangladesh witnessed stable demand.
Alang market softened, while appetite for fresh tonnage, espe-
cially for larger units, remains active. Local market fundamen-
tals continue to weaken, as local plate price and USD dropped
slightly, close to previous week ’s levels. Specialized tonnage
continues to be the center of attention, as conventional vessels
activity remains subdued. On the economics side, tighter do-
mestic liquidity conditions due to the liquidity mop up by RBI are
likely to appreciate local currency and increase scrap prices in
the months ahead.
In Bangladesh, the market shows stability, as offers remain un-
changed and demand has recovered. Domestic plate prices de-
creased, while US dollar strengthened. The limited availability of
attractive recycling tonnage seems to not affect significantly
Chattogram, as yards feed on previously secured vessels. On
the policy side, potential EU restrictions on ferrous scrap ex-
ports may indirectly improve demand for domestic recycling,
although the direct EU exposure is limited. Raised concerns are
also evident over the sharp increase in domestic fuel prices,
which could lead to increased operating costs and potentially
slowing the recovery in steel demand.
At Gadani, the market conditions continue to weigh on recyclers ’
sentiment and buying appetite. Weaker demand pushes local
plate prices and currency down, however still at supportive lev-
els. Buying interest remains selective, as most facilities are cur-
rently occupied, following additional vessel arrivals. Further
pressure is added to recyclers, as availability of alternative steel
products due to renewed steel -product supply from Iran and
favorable tax incentives on imported scrap supports steel sup-
ply.
Aliaga’s market sentiment modestly improved with firmer scrap
markets and better, though seasonal, steel demand. Recycling
tonnage supply remained moderate, pending greater clarity on
market movements.
