Crude Tanker Comments
This week, the VLCC market has quietened considerably from last week’s exceptional
activity and record rates. East of Suez has seen the biggest slowdown; charterers have
taken a step back, and there have been delays to Gulf of Oman STS operations. Rates
have softened, and Fujairah-East was last seen at WS 792.5, with talks of WS 750. STS
activity is shifting towards India’s west coast, where there are fewer vessels and more
facilities available. This should allow for more efficient movement, though the
transition is expected to be gradual. Middle East crude demand remains strong, with
most stems going to China. Security remains an important consideration for the
developing STS network, and Iran has warned that the conflict could extend into the
Indian Ocean, adding another risk to operations moving towards WCI.
Uncertainty around the Strait of Hormuz remains a key variable for both crude flows
and freight. In the Atlantic, VLCC activity has all but ground to a halt as charterers
continue to find better value in Suezmaxes. That said, one ECM-WCI VLCC fixture was
reported at USD 47.75 Mn, softer than some may have expected, but as the laycan
worked particularly well for the vessel and the discharge position is so close to the AG,
the voyage was very attractive. As such, it should be treated as an opportunistic fixture
rather than a benchmark. VLCC tonnage remains tight and Middle East crude demand
is still strong. Without fresh East of Suez cargo, rates may keep easing, but any
meaningful increase in activity could quickly test the limited available tonnage.
The Suezmax market had an interesting week, with the Atlantic very firm at the start
before easing back as activity remained relatively quiet and tonnage supply
replenished. TD20 was assessed around WS 490 on Monday before bottoming at WS
435 by Friday, while CPC kept trading sideways at WS 500. USG also softened from WS
400 to WS 360, although the firmer Aframax market provided some support, with
USG-TA ending the week at WS 362.5.
East of Suez remained the standout, with Fujairah-East reportedly reaching WS 800,
though it sat at WS 690 on Friday. Despite the Atlantic correction, owners remain
reluctant to reposition tonnage in the East going into Q4, keeping a significant
premium in place. Friday’s slight stand-off and more positive Brazil fixture suggest that
enquiry may be starting to turn, with owners looking for a fresh influx next week to
firm the Atlantic.
The Mediterranean Aframax market was complex at the beginning of the week, with
several outstanding end-month cargoes letting owners extend Friday's momentum
and push rates higher. WS 630 was repeated multiple times, tempting owners to make
a move rather than risk missing dates. Midweek, rates held firmly above WS 600 but
proved highly date- and voyage-dependent, with short voyages bringing a significant
premium. Despite a quieter second half of the week, sentiment stayed firm, supported
by a rising TD25. Things were quieter yesterday, but the US market might entice
charterers to act. By Friday, TD19 had settled at WS 645.
The North Sea Aframax market began the week with one direction in view, as
developments in surrounding markets left owners certain of firmer rates. A tight
tonnage list and extremely elevated Med levels suggested the next fixture would test
substantially higher, and by Tuesday, the region had caught up, with a WS 400 deal
secured. Several prompt cargoes were covered, helping support what is close to a
record rate. Sentiment remains firm, and TD7 ends the week at WS 425.
Product Tanker Comments
While the possibility of cargoes being worked under the radar can never be
discounted, on the surface, it seems that this week has been a fraction calmer than
last in terms of overall deal count for AG LR2s. Charterers have been looking to funnel
cargo into the LR1 segment, which has rallied hard; still, freight levels greatly
improved on the week for the LR2 deals which did filter out. The 75 x WS 330 seen last
week for STS Sohar-Japan has risen considerably to WS 470. Westbound has also
moved substantially, with USD 9.5 Mn on subs for a WCI-UKCONT via Bab-el-Mandeb
agreed, USD 2 Mn above last done.
The lifts can partly be explained by a very tight list largely caused by the extremely
busy Far East markets taking substantial tonnage. Another factor was the lack of
vessels from the West given the very strong DPP markets, and Yanbu lifting has shown
signs of life and taken its own share of supply. Yanbu-UK Cont saw a less-alarming rise
of USD 200,000, but its current rate of USD 6.65 Mn is historically sky-high. Strong
markets await, with little sign of position list replenishment just yet.
LR1 owners have seen freight go from good to great without showing any signs of
slowing down. Excluding the period shortly after this war broke out, TC5 has not been
this high since a short-lived spike in May 2020. WS 450 is the current level, and it is
expected to reach WS 500 soon. Westbound business has also progressed, but less so.
USD 6.5 Mn for STS Vadinar-UK Cont via Bab-el-Mandeb was agreed but failed; still,
market ideas now sit closer to USD 7.5 Mn for the same route, and USD 8.25 Mn to
travel via Cape of Good Hope. One LR1 Duqm stem was covered by a Med-based LR2
own programme vessel, evidence of the tightness of the list. Outstanding demand is a
bit patchy, but all the sizes in the AG seem to be on a snowballing pathway which is
showing no signs of slowing down.
Replacement business continued to redefine the upper end of the Southeast Asia MR
market, with TC7 exceeding WS 500, while lucrative prompt short-haul employment is
letting owners remain patient on forward cargoes. Potential ballasters from South
Africa offer limited comfort, particularly with the AG market extremely firm. With
surrounding markets competing aggressively for tonnage and few natural
replacements, Singapore appears equally well-positioned to firm further, and owners
are likely to continue testing progressively higher freight levels.
Med MRs have continued to benefit from strong surrounding markets this week, with
owners still needing to be coaxed into loading ex-Med and not ballasting. This has
aided in keeping the list limited, especially with naphtha still the main focus of
enquiry. At the time of writing, such a stem has replaced at WS 170 TA, WS 220 UK
Cont, WS 260 Med, and USD 3.95 Mn Japan, though these levels feel inflated and the
next vanilla run will likely settle lower.
Med Handy activity was moderate this week, but with tonnage short inside end-
month, owners have been able to capitalise sharply, with 30 x WS 270 last subbed for
X-Med – about 70 points up from Monday. In the last 24 hours, fresh business has
been scarce, and come Monday, tonnage should be there for the next window.
Owners will return bullish but will need to see an active first decade of October. In
Northwest Europe, tonnage is now extremely tight on the front end, and the fixing
window is being pushed out until 5 October. Next-done TC2 can be expected to be
above WS 170, with owners firmly bullish and a sentiment that levels will continue to
firm further with paper also trading up.
