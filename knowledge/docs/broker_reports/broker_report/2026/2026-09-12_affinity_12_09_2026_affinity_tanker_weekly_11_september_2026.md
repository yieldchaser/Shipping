Crude Tanker Comments
This week the VLCC market has been hectic, with strong activity and rates skyrocketing
across the board. A surge in demand for prompt crude has coincided with
exceptionally tight tonnage. With growing disruption in the Middle East – over 15
vessels have been stuck around the Strait of Hormuz recently, and the Houthis are
advancing with their eyes on the Bab-el-Mandeb strait – charterers seem increasingly
keen to secure barrels now rather than risk the delay. This has led rates to soar, and
East of Suez has been the focal point, with Fujairah-East climbing from WS 265 to WS
550 between Monday and Friday. A shocking WS 1,350 for AG-East is rumoured,
though it should not be used as a benchmark.
The Atlantic has recovered from last week’s peak, but rates have kept rising, including
Brazil-East. USG-East has also firmed, and several voyages failed earlier in the week at
up to USD 29.5 Mn, but today there are rumours of a ship on subs at USD 34.5 Mn. The
list remains especially thin, and many of the unusually high number of Brazilian
barrels are going to China, boosting tonne-miles. Looking ahead, the combination of
scarce tonnage, strong demand, and geopolitical risk leaves little obvious reason for
owners to discount. With cargoes still left to cover and chaos in the Middle East, the
cards remain firmly in owners’ hands.
The week ended on a very firm note for Suezmaxes, with a significant increase in
activity and a strong VLCC market bringing further upside to the sector. The week
started with a stand-off between owners and charterers, but increasing enquiry and
several VLCC split stems quickly tightened the tonnage list, with rates pushing higher
across the board. WAFR was particularly strong, with WAFR-East beginning at WS 260
and ending with an agreement at WS 480. CPC also continued firming, rising from WS
320 to WS 450.
The firming Aframax USG market provided additional support to Suezmaxes; USG-TA
began at WS 275 and ended in the low WS 400s. Rates East of Suez also surged, with
Fujairah-East rising from the mid- WS 300s to WS 525. With a healthy number of
cargoes outstanding, owners have the upper hand, and momentum looks likely to
carry into next week.
This week, the Mediterranean Aframax market strengthened steadily after a surprise
jump to WS 260 ex-Sidi late last week. Monday and Tuesday saw rates searching for
direction, with owners attempting to build momentum while charterers worked to
contain gains. By midweek, strong activity and a long cargo list allowed owners to push
rates higher. On Thursday, despite charterers' efforts to resist further increases, firm
sentiment across tanker markets supported higher levels, particularly for prompt
cargoes. Overall, the week ended with clearly firmer sentiment, and TD19 is currently
freighted at WS 390.
The North Sea Aframax market remained subdued for most of the week, with limited
activity and rates largely repeating last done levels. Early sentiment was flat, as
insufficient local demand prevented any meaningful upward movement and market
direction depended heavily on developments in the US By midweek, activity remained
slow despite strength in the Mediterranean, keeping rates stable. However, by Friday,
firmer surrounding markets including TD25 encouraged owners to consider ballasting
out, increasing the likelihood that charterers will need to raise rates to retain tonnage
locally. TD7 sits at WS 260 at close on Friday.
Product Tanker Comments
It was thought that this week would be busy for AG LRs after little demand last week,
and this was proven correct, with 11 deals reported. The list has tightened up well,
but good timing from charterers led to a drop in rates for those deals which were
initially secured. The going rate for Westbound via the Suez Canal had been around
USD 6.5 Mn, but this fell to between USD 6 Mn and USD 6.15 Mn at the beginning of
the week. Clean-up Aframaxes have also began taking ULSD westbound. Naphtha runs
have been agreed at between 75KT x WS 230 and 75KT x WS 237.5.
The main headline news for the AG LR2 segment is the USD 16.5 Mn for AG-West via
Cape of Good Hope, a staggering number involving two parties engaging in significant
risk in the growing danger in the Middle East. The list ends lean, and there is a sense
that more oil is to come from the AG and Indian Ocean loading areas in the short
term. Recent levels are likely a floor as next week unravels.
The AG LR1 segment has a tight list, but vessel demand was a bit inside tonnage
supply this week. As a result, last week’s figure of 55KT x WS 295 for a naphtha voyage
slumped marginally to WS 285, done at this rate on at least two occasions. More
exciting was an AG-East export at 55KT x WS 810, which equates to about USD 10.1
Mn for Japan. Next week, LR1s will find themselves in the same position as LR2s will –
a tight list and a feeling that the combination of surging crude exports and greater
willingness to run ships through dangerous waters will lead to more CPP cargo flow.
There has been some activity for the LRs in North Asia this week, which is, perhaps,
unsurprising given how high freight rates are for the MRs. There have been reports of
three LR2s fixed or on subs from North Asia to Singapore with East Africa optionality,
and charterers have made use of some newbuilds to bring their dollar-per-tonne rates
down. The overall outlook for LRs in this area is positive as long as the bull run on the
MRs lasts.
It has been a positive week for the MR market in Singapore. Short-haul regional
voyages and cargos into SAFR and Australia stripped out a lot of the prompt tonnage
at the front end of the week, giving owners the chance to push rates up. Some huge
numbers have been fixed for short-haul cargoes, and TC7 hit WS 410 (though WS 425
was paid for a prompter stem).
Looking into next week, the tonnage list should see some rejuvenation; however, it is
likely that covering prompt cargos will still be a headache for charterers. Still, looking
farther down the track, it is expected that some of the many ships fixed into EAFR and
SAFR begin to reappear, and some of these might well make their way back to SE Asia,
especially if there is any dropoff in the WCI market. Similarly to the situation in North
Asia, there is a feeling that this market is close to its ceiling
MRs in NWE have remained steady at WS 100 for TC2, with owners looking to
reposition to the USG. The tonnage list remains long, and there is potential to firm,
with ARA-Med now at 37KT x WS 170 as owners look to maintain their position out of
the weaker Med market. Long-haul voyages with consistently positive TCE earnings
are owners’ preference, and they have softened to WS 150 for ARA-WAFR and WS 140
for ARA-Brazil. Unlike its neighbour, TC6 rose slightly this week, beginning at WS 174
and ending at WS 186.
