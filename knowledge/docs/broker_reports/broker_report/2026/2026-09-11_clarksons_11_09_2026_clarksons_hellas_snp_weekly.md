Desk Talk
As spot rates remain on a strong trend across both Wet and Dry spaces, we ran through another week
where we observed all-time-high tanker, and very strong dry cargo tonnage prices , while appetite for
secondhand vessels is continuing to absorb the elevated price levels.
On the tanker side, rising crude and product prices have pushed VLCC earnings on the traditional route
and global MR averages to $300,000/day and $37,200/day, respectively. At the same time, increased
availability of Middle Eastern crude, driven by shuttling and STS operations, combined with heightened
refining activity in Asia, has pushed regional MR rates above $55,000/day. Conversely, on MRs, earnings
in the Atlantic basin are expected to rebound, supported by increased tonnage demand from the
Middle East. Strong earnings continue to underpin demand for older tonnage, particularly for vessels
offering prompt delivery in the Middle East and Far  East. Against this backdrop, Korean -built VLCC,
NISSOS HERACLEA (ABT 313,525 DWT, 2009, HD HYUNDAI HI (HHI)) , was concluded at USD 112m basis
prompt delivery in Fujairah. The transaction represents approximately a 50% increase to pre -conflict
selling prices for Korean -built VLCCs, despite the  then already very strong market.  Now, with the IEA
having pushed back its assumption for a normalization of Hormuz flows from 2H2026 into 2027, we may
expect the positive tanker market consensus to prove more enduring than previously anticipated,
providing further support to S&P sentiment.
While all eyes remain on the tanker market, the Dry sector continues to hold firm, with particularly strong
Capesize rates and asset prices keeping the segment at elevated levels and supporting the broader
positive market consensus. Although the Capesize market saw a slight decrease in earnings d -o-d, the
week closed on a positive note compared to the previous week, with Capesizes remaining the standout
performer. Meanwhile, we are observing notable activity in the geared segments, particularly in
modern eco Ultramaxes and 15–20-year-old Supramaxes and Handysizes. We are also seeing renewed
interest in the Post -Panamax segment, which appears to be among the least inflated sectors in terms
of asset price appreciation. Notably, Post-Panamax prices for comparable tonnage remain broadly at
last year’s levels.
