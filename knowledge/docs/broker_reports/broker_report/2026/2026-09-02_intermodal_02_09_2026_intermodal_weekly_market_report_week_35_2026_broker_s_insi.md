Market Insight By Nikos Tagoulis, Senior Analyst
The EUA market maintained its upward trend in August, despite the quieter trading conditions and lower liquidity that typically characterize the holiday period. The advance extended the recov- ery that began in late March and left the EU ETS on a constructive footing heading into autumn. The Front Dec-26 EUA contract av- eraged €82.36 /ton during August and at the time of writing read €83.30 /ton, around 6.5% above the 2026 y-t-d average. Prices remained within a relatively narrow range during the month, and the contract continued to trade above its 50-, 100- and 200-day moving averages.
The European gas market has contributed to the firming of EUAs. Prices at the Dutch TTF hub rose significantly as geopolitical ten- sions leading to Qatari volumes off market, low European storage levels and competition from Asian buyers for LNG cargoes height- ened concerns over winter supply.
Although the correlation between front-month TTF gas prices and EUAs fluctuated in August, it has strengthened recently, suggest- ing that prevailing gas-market dynamics are supporting EUA de- mand. The principal transmission channel is fuel-switching eco- nomics: higher gas prices make coal more competitive with gas for power generation, encouraging more carbon-intensive output and increasing utilities’ demand for allowances.
Furthermore, recent economic signals add to this supportive backdrop, pointing to firm industrial demand. The August euro- zone manufacturing PMI reached its strongest level in more than four years, accompanied by stronger new orders, a recovery in exports and the first increase in factory employment after more than three years. Together, these developments strengthen the outlook for EUA demand.
Despite the positive backdrop, traders remain cautious. Invest- ment funds have reduced their net-long exposure in recent weeks, while commercial participants have continued to increase their forward exposure, reflecting hedging strategies built around expectations of further EUA price increases. The divergence indi- cates that commercial buying remains driven by risk management even as speculative interest weakens.
