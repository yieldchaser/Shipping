Crude Tanker Comments
The week ends with the VLCC market on firm footing, owing mainly to a large uptick in
activity, most of which has been under the radar. The mystery list is the longest it has
been for a while, with over 30 ships disappearing, but a few deals have come out
which showcase the direction of travel. Rates out of Brazil bottomed on Tuesday, with
WS 129.2 being recorded twice for China-bound voyages on a popular market quote.
Since then, the Atlantic list has continued to contract, and next done looks likely to be
in the mid-to-high WS 130s.
Information ex-USG has been limited, with rates and many deals kept under wraps,
but there has been clear activity, and the list prior 20 September is extremely tight. It
remains somewhat on a knife edge as to whether these deals will fix fully, but with the
Suezmax and Aframax markets also looking firm, rates are expected to continue ticking
up for now. WAFR has been quiet on the surface, with just one deal being reported at
WS 135 on older tonnage, but TD15 can be placed in the low-to-mid WS 140s today, a
few points above the BITR print. East of Suez remains active and continues to edge up.
Overall, the outlook is positive, though rates have only firmed gradually as ships
continue to disappear.
For Suezmaxes, the Atlantic has strengthened throughout this week, with both a
tightening list and high level of activity across the sea boosting rates. Last week ended
with a Brazil-UKC fixture printing at WS 177.5, which put TD20 at the WS 182.5 mark. A
flood of end-August cargoes has pushed TD20 to around WS 230, but this is on the
back of an Angola run which discounts to Nigeria and was done with an elderly ship;
other cargoes are in a standoff and owners expect rates to surge beyond these levels.
Guyana and the USG have also paid up. WS 200 is on subs for USG-TA, up nearly 30
points on last done, and Guyana is paying around 145KT x WS 245 – a jump of almost
100 points. CPC rates pushed up from their extraordinary highs to a record WS 575
paid for TD6; however, since laycan dates have shifted to September, the market has
likely topped as owners willing to call CPC have prioritised doing this business and are
willing to ballast ships from further afield to chase the earnings here.
The Mediterranean Aframax market softened through the first half of the week as an
oversupplied tonnage list and limited activity continued to weigh on sentiment. Rates
came under significant pressure on Monday amid minimal demand and a growing
number of available vessels. Although a few ships were absorbed on Tuesday and
support from a stronger US market provided some encouragement, vessel supply still
comfortably exceeded cargo demand. By Wednesday, however, activity in the
Mediterranean had started picking up, helping to reduce some excess tonnage and
suggesting that the market may have hit the floor. TD19 ends the week at WS 225,
bouncing back from Wednesday’s WS 205.
The North Sea Aframax market was under pressure for most of the week, reflecting
weak local fundamentals and an abundance of available tonnage following a very
quiet previous week. Rates gradually softened as charterers capitalised on oversupply,
although there was increasing questioning as to how much further values could fall
given strengthening conditions elsewhere. A busy US Gulf market and rising TD25
activity continued to attract ballasting tonnage westbound, helping support rates. By
midweek, sentiment suggested the market was approaching a floor, and by Thursday
rates appeared to have stabilised, with last-done levels repeated and external support
from the US market allowing for a steady outlook. TD7 sits at WS 200 on Friday.
Product Tanker Comments
On Monday and Tuesday, there was significant demand for AG LR2s, and with the
position list still very much on the leaner side, large improvements were made. There
have mostly been distillates in play, which moved on considerably from the USD 4 Mn
for a non-approved ship last week to the reliable rumour that USD 6.2 Mn is now on
subs for STS Oman or India-UKC via the Suez Canal. Naphtha has been traded less, but
this has also moved on well, with a 20-point improvement leading to WS 210 being
confirmed for 75,000 T. The vessel list is lean going into next week, so the outlook is
firm from the owners’ side.
AG LR1s had potential this week with a much leaner tonnage list, but the market has
barely moved at all. There has been a lot more activity taking place in the shadows
this week, but those fixtures with full details available have seemingly traded
sideways. Naphtha runs heading east have all been agreed at WS 195 throughout the
week. Westbound rates have not been tested publicly, but an agreement of USD 3.3
Mn could be estimated via Bab el-Mandeb. Red Sea activity has suffered in light of
recent Houthi attacks on Saudi business, so it is no surprise that nothing is moving
East through Bab el-Mandeb. Looking into next week, it would take little fresh
demand to kick the LR1s into a firmer market, but for now, it could remain flat.
It has been a challenging week for NWE MR owners, with a very slow start and a
significant oversupply of available tonnage weighing on the market. Freight levels
across all NWE routes have remained under pressure, with TC2 now last done at WS
105 and the floor yet to be established. Differentials over TC2 have continued to
narrow, with ARA-Brazil now tested down to WS 150 and ARA-UKC at WS 140. Activity
picked up on Thursday and today, helping to absorb some prompt tonnage from the
market. In addition, with sentiment in the USG firm again, five vessels have ballasted
that way, and more are considering their options. This should begin to disperse some
of the current tonnage surplus in NWE and help to start balance tonnage supply.
Med MR owners also had little luck, having to revise ideas sharply to secure cargoes in
a competitive tonnage space. Levels have therefore corrected across the board, with
last done Med-TA now down 25 points to 37KT x WS 110 on a unit whose last cargo
was palm oil. Short-hauls have also suffered, with UKC and X-Med last tested at WS
137.5 and WS 140, respectively. Whilst a rebound in TC14 has already begun to attract
WAFR and West Med ballasters, the outlook for next week still feels bearish
considering current volumes. What remains is a healthy pool of tonnage, which will of
course see further replenishment over the weekend. The market will eventually reach
a point where earnings cannot justify lower rates; however, charterers have a good
chance of squeezing out a bit more in the near term.
The week began slowly for NWE Handysizes. The few cargoes which did emerge
mostly looked at both 30,000 T and 37,000 T options, as the MR market remained
heavily oversupplied and was more economical. As a result, TC23 started the week at
around WS 220 but has fallen to WS 175. Still, an increase in activity during the latter
part of the week helped shorten the list. It was also a slow week for Med Handies,
which saw levels correct from 30KT x WS 200 to 30KT x WS 175, and the next test
should fall closer to the WS 170 mark. Whilst the list has reduced, Monday will bring a
bulky front-end, and owners will need to act quickly to establish the bottom. Earnings
are approaching lows such that owners cannot justify moving tonnage, so although
fundamentally the market could easily soften further, a floor will have to be found.
