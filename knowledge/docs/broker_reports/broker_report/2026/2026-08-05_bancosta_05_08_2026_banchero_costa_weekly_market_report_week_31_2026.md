SALE & PURCHASE 12NEWBUILDING ORDERS Newbuilding activity remained healthy this week, with contracting led by strong demand across the container and dry bulk sectors, while fresh orders were also reported for crude and product tankers, as well as a PCTC. Container activity was dominated by feeder and mid-sized vessels, whereas the dry bulk market saw a series of Ultramax orders alongside larger Newcastlemax commitments.In the dry bulk sector, Turkish owner Aruna Shipping reportedly placed an order for 6x 64,500 dwt Ultramax bulk carriers at Yangzhou Guoyu Shipbuilding, with each vessel valued at approximately USD 35 million and deliveries scheduled between mid-2028 and late-2030. The contract also includes options for four additional units. Greek owner W Marine is also understood to have booked 3x 64,800 dwt Ultramaxes at Jiangsu Dajin Heavy Industry for around USD 33.5 million each, with deliveries throughout 2028. Elsewhere, Greek owner JME Navigation reportedly contracted 1x 64,000 dwt Ultramax at New Dayang
Shipbuilding for approximately USD 35 million, while Cape Shipping S.A. is understood to have confirmed an order for 3x 181,000 dwt Capesize bulk carriers at Hengli Heavy Industry, priced at around USD 76 million each for delivery in late 2027.Tanker ordering also remained active. South Korean owner Pan Ocean reportedly returned to Qingdao Beihai for 2x 319,000 dwt VLCCs, with each vessel valued at approximately USD 122 million. The ammonia-ready vessels are scheduled for delivery in 2030. In the products segment, Greek owner Stealth Maritime is understood to have ordered 2x 50,000 dwt MR tankers at Hyundai Mipo, with deliveries due in 2027 at a reported price of USD 52.5 million each.The container sector once again generated the highest level of activity. UK-based Zodiac Maritime reportedly placed an order for 8x 6,400 teu containerships at Hengli Heavy Industry, with each vessel priced at around USD 80 million for
delivery in 2028. Chinese owner Zhenghe Trunk Line also returned to the yard for 6x 4,600 teu feeder containerships, reportedly costing USD 68.3 million each and intended for services between China and the Baltic Sea. Meanwhile, Norwegian owner Songa Box is understood to have ordered 2x 1,300 teu feeder vessels at Hubei Guangji Green Energy Shipbuilding, with options for a further four units.Elsewhere, Cyprus-based Sallaum Lines reportedly booked 1x 8,600 ceu PCTC at China Merchants Heavy Industry, with an option for one additional vessel. The LNG dual-fuel design will also be capable of future ammonia conversion, with delivery scheduled for 2029.

DEMOLITION SALESThe recycling market found its voice this week, breaking a month-long drought with the first reported basin sale: the chemical tanker Stolt Kikyo, at USD 455 per LDT delivered Alang. With Hormuz functioning again — fourteen commodity vessels transited on Wednesday — owners at last have a working exit route, and the tone turned quietly constructive despite mixed freight, the Baltic Dry Index recovering to 2,673 after a

Pakistan held USD 443–448 per LDT with plate near USD 714 per tonne, yet Gadani reported no new candidates for a second week. Turkey remained uncompetitive at USD 262–264 per LDT.
