The tanker market remains in the spotlight , now with Saudi Arabia ’s East-West pipel ine shutdown
amplifying the impact of the disruption of the Strait of Hormuz even more. Wednesday’s developments
suggested that a restart of the pipeline is likely, with around half of its total 7 mb/d capacity expected
to be restored in the near term , while a  full return to operations is considered realistic by October.  At
the same time , shuttling / STS ope rations that free up trapped cargo which is loaded  at the Gulf of
Oman continue to  be preferred by shipowners over performing a crossing of the Strai t. Against this
backdrop, oil buyers are locking in unprecedented refining profits, and shipowners are benefiting from
freight rates that have hit all -time highs. For tanker S&P this suggests ever-increasing asset prices and
tonnage demand, with the recent spike being especially notable for larger-sized crude carriers ranging
from Aframax to VLCC.
On the dry side, activity remains healthy, with the elevated earnings, asset prices and S&P transaction
volume showing no signs of easing back. Although Capesize earnings have pulled back from the highs
of early September's rally, they remain firm and continue to drive the market's positive momentum .
Overall, the dry bulk sector is underpinned by an exceptionally strong grain trade, and with the
expected seasonal peak in Guinean bauxite exports approaching alongside softer coal demand, the
market looks well poised heading into the fourth quarter.  Enquiry levels reflect broad -based interest
spanning the full size range, from Handysize up to Capesize tonnage, generally limited to units up to 10
years old.
