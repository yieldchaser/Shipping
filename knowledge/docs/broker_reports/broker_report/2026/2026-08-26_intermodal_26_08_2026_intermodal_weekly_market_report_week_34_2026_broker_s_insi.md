By Nikos Tagoulis, Senior Analyst
Months after taking effect, the Jones Act waiver continues to bol-
ster the US coastal trade. The exemption has now been extended
for a further 90 days, with the latest renewal since August 17,
2026, under a stricter framework than previously, reflecting polit-
ical debate in Washington over this century-old protectionist law
governing US cabotage, first introduced in 1920. The law has long
divided US maritime industry and policy makers. Supporters cite
domestic shipbuilding, seafaring employment and defence readi-
ness, while critics point to market distortion, higher freight costs
and a coastwise-eligible fleet insufficient to meet demand.
Under the revised framework, priority is preserved for coastwise -
qualified vessels through a MARAD availability survey, with for-
eign-flagged tonnage admitted only where none is available. Ap-
plicants must provide vessel, voyage and cargo details to the rele-
vant US authorities, which assess domestic vessel availability and
eligibility. Operators must also report the completed voyage with-
in 10 days.
The extension follows a substantial increase in waterborne US
flows. According to the U.S. Energy Information Administration,
combined shipments by tanker and barge from PADD 3 (US Gulf
Coast) to PADD 5 (U.S. West Coast) reached 190k bpd in April,
compared with less than 30k bpd in January and February, and
remained elevated in May. The composition of volumes changed
as well. A trade previously dominated by renewable diesel broad-
ened to include gasoline components, finished gasoline, jet fuel
and crude oil.
The shift is also evident on the East Coast, where flows from the
US Gulf to the East Coast (PADD 1) rose as well, reaching a record
high of 1.2m bpd in April, mainly comprising gasoline blending
components and distillate fuels. Moreover, crude shipments to
the East Coast rose in May to a record 180k bpd. This increase is
also partly attributed to Florida ’s high dependence on US Gulf
refineries for its fuel needs, making access to coastal tonnage
critical for the region’s fuel security.
The commercial implications are visible. By excluding foreign -
flagged vessels from coastwise trade, the Jones Act prevents the
wider international fleet from responding to US domestic trans-
portation demand. When coastwise -qualified tonnage is unavail-
able, cargoes may face higher freight costs, delays or alternative
routing.
The latest waiver therefore provides some relief, while preserving
priority for eligible tonnage, also allows international vessels to
meet part of the demand. This activates previously latent trans-
portation demand, as cargoes that may have been uneconomic or
difficult to move under the Jones Act can now be served by for-
eign tonnage. The volumes remain relatively small, and some Gulf
Coast barrels may displace imports into other US regions. The net
impact on international tanker demand therefore depends not
only on the additional coastal movements, but also on the car-
goes they replace.
The ton-mile contribution also varies significantly by route. USG -
East Coast voyages are relatively short, allowing vessels to turn
quickly, while West Coast cargoes involve longer Panama Canal
transits and potentially substantial repositioning, generating
more ton -miles and tying up vessels for longer. For the tanker
market, the additional demand is likely to continue benefiting
product tankers, particularly MRs, while the increase in crude
movements provides additional cargo opportunities for Aframax-
es. Yet the waiver may matter less for the tanker demand and
volumes it directly creates than for the additional employment
option it gives international vessels in US Gulf. Even modest vol-
umes can affect the market at the margin if a domestic cargo pro-
vides an alternative to an export stem, changing the opportunity
cost of fixing a prompt ship elsewhere. Its impact may therefore
emerge through vessel positioning and availability in the Atlantic
Basin rather than through growth in tanker demand. In this sense,
the waiver temporarily affects the deployment map for interna-
tional tankers in the US Gulf, broadening the deployment choices.
With the waiver still temporary and approvals granted on a voy-
age-by-voyage basis, the additional demand remains policy -
driven. However, if foreign tonnage continues to fill capacity gaps
and intensify competition, U.S. policymakers may move toward a
broader reassessment of how the Jones Act is implemented.

India's recycling market has gathered momentum, with buying
interest broadening from niche tonnage into more mainstream
candidates even as Alang's pricing continues to lag that of re-
gional rivals. Firmer domestic steel plate values have added to
the improved tone, offering further support to bids. Persistent
rupee depreciation against the dollar, compounded by inflation-
ary pressure, is eroding the purchasing capacity of local yards
and tempering expectations for how far the current improve-
ment can be carried.
Gadani carried the previous week's fundamentals forward, with
keen buying appetite meeting a thin list of recycling candidates
and prices continuing to firm, leaving Pakistan the most com-
petitive destination in the Subcontinent. With demand outstrip-
ping the limited tonnage on offer, competition for the units
reaching the market has pushed bidding to increasingly asser-
tive levels. The fall in Iranian steel imports has lent further sup-
port, alongside a constructive domestic backdrop in which mill
demand remains strong and plate values are increasing.
In Bangladesh, the market retains a broadly positive tone, with
active demand focused on mid - and smaller-sized dry bulk can-
didates. However, buyers remain cautious, monitoring the steel
market's direction, currently sluggish with declining prices as
monsoon-related adverse weather has impacted market mobil-
ity. On the economy front, signals are encouraging. Foreign re-
serves have accumulated to their highest level in recent years,
while inflation eased m -o-m in July, adding a optimistic note to
the broader outlook.
Turkey's market remains subdued, with currency weakness lim-
iting buyers' purchasing power and consequently demand. Can-
didates list continues to be underpinned by vessels moving un-
der EU regulation, though optimism persists for a further inflow
of tonnage ahead. On a positive note, the local steel market pre-
sented improving signs.
