## Market Report Content
Fearnleys
Weekly Report
Week 34 - August 19, 2026
info@hellenicshippingnews.com
01Tankers
VLCC
Suezmax
We’re in “stop the press” territory now with VLCC rates galloping in all areas in tandem
with the Suez- and Aframax segments. In the Atlantic we saw a stonking WS 197.5
fixture Brazil/China yesterday, up from a WS 182.5 just done a bit earlier! An
unconfirmed report suggests WS 210 has been done West Africa/East, but whether
true or not tallies with the last Brazil rate, if not higher. Fujairah-Oman/East cargoes
have followed suit sniffing on the WS 200 mark or above. Position lists are thinning by
the hour both east and west of Suez so today’s “expensive” fixture might very well
look “cheap” tomorrow. Time and tide wait for no man.
On the one hand we have prompt replacements & 1-5 window cargoes working
Suezmax, VLCC levels also in runaway mode with TD15 fixing circa 260KT x WS 200
Aframax
Rates
and their USG list almost devoid of tonnage within the next 30-day. Thus expectations
of strong West Africa demand to carry the Suezmax market in the 2nd decade could
sustain this spike. On the other, we are fully priced out of the USG market with 2
enquiries withdrawing yesterday & Aframaxes USD 2.5/BBL cheaper, and basis latest
cargo counts ex West Africa we may be down to our final 6 stems in the 1st decade
once present enquiry is cleaned out.
The list has 8 natural options FOC up to the 5th, 4 discharging presently, 3 at disport
waiting and overall 12 natural ETS within the next 5 days/22 natural ETS within the
next week. It's not hugely extensive & CPC players are not going to change their tune
whilst TD6 is paying in the WS 500s either. Given the confidence a low-VLCC count
should give owners in the 2nd decade, even if 1st decade demand falls off a little
towards the end of the week, we are not anticipating owners to suddenly become
pushovers.
North Sea
The North Sea list has been greatly reduced with vessels heading to the US. The
amount of vessels in ballast to the US so far in August is the largest amount we have
seen in the same period in any previous month. The North Sea market has remained
steady even with the clearing of the list holding around the WS 200 level. Dates are
starting to push towards the end of the month which should see some increase in
activity and itineraries will be important for B/L dates which may allow owners to
chase a slight premium.
Mediterranean
Activity in the Mediterranean has been consistent with a few replacement
requirements also coming into the mix. The US has attracted vessels from the West
Mediterranean along with some compromised vessels has kept the tonnage available
to charterers interesting. Rates have firmed around WS 15 points from last week and
heading to the end August/early September fixing window we may still see some
more firming.

This week markets are showing slight pressure with limited fixing today on Capesize
across all segments, however markets are consistent on C3 Brazil/China with the main
decline seen in Pacific C5. FFAs show slight positive sentiment in the long term
although above average laden to ballast ratio of vessels heading toward the Atlantic
gives a slight bearish outlook in the near future. This week sentiment has had a softer
tone in the Pacific and today saw a decline on paper. Earlier fixing concluded around
and just below the USD 14 range for West Australia/Qingdao. Today, two miners
reported fixing at USD 13.4 yesterday and latest bids are mixed and around USD 13 to
USD 14 for early September laycans. In the Atlantic, levels have remained consistent
however South Brazil and West Africa to China remains subdued with limited bids and
offers reported today. C3 Brazil/China sees bids and offer reported in low USD 36
levels with last done in high USD 34s on C3 for West Africa and another reported in
the USD 37 range on Friday for a Newcastlemax.
Panamax sentiment has turned more cautious this week, with the index giving back
part of its recent gains and both basins trading with a softer bias. The Pacific has led
the correction: prompt tonnage has accumulated across the North and South while
fresh NoPac grain and Australian and Indo coal enquiry has been slow to absorb it,
leaving charterers in control and the market still in search of a floor. The Atlantic has
held up rather better, with ECSA fronthaul remaining the firmer leg and Continent
tonnage still reasonably tight, although bid/offer have widened and fixing volumes are
thin as charterers wait for the north to settle. Period discussion remains active across
both Kamsarmax and Panamax, but owners are resisting the softer nearby curve and
bids have yet to close the gap, so relatively little is concluding. We would expect a
sideways-to-softer tone into next week, with any recovery contingent on a fresh
injection of grain and coal cargo in the Pacific; in the meantime the Atlantic should
continue to offer owners the better alternative.

regions and only modest gains in the Atlantic. The Continent and Mediterranean
continue to face pressure due to ample tonnage and limited fresh cargo input, while

has yet to translate into significant rate gains.
