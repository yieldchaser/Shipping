MARKET OVERVIEW
South Asian energy processors are actively overhauling their crude procurement tactics by chartering
tonnage to lift cargoes directly from within the Persian Gulf rather than paying elevated delivered rates. In
response to softening domestic maritime crew advisories and growing resistance toward alternative
Atlantic origins, major Indian refiners have transitioned toward purchasing regional stems on a direct
loading basis, awarding tenders to specialised international fleet operators willing to navigate the
contested Strait of Hormuz. This pivot has allowed buyers to capture substantial savings, even as
operational and security risks across the waterway remain an ongoing reality.
The primary incentive driving this is an extraordinary pricing campaign from Iraq , who face severe
competitive disadvantages due to their deeper geographic position. To compensate buyers for exorbitant
war-risk insurance premiums and chartering rates, Iraq state authorities have lowered official selling
prices to historic depths, offering medium and heavy grades at unprecedented discounts of up to US$37
per barrel below regional benchmarks. With limited capacity to transport, Ba ghdad is using these historic
rebates to protect market share against neighbouring producers who enjoy direct pipeline access to
alternative coastlines. While these markdowns have lured back adventurous buyers, the broader trade
remains constrained by a shortage of vessels willing to take on PG stems.
Meanwhile, operational bottlenecks have surfaced further east at China’s premier ship- refuelling center in
Zhoushan, where commercial vessels face extensive bunkering queues stretching up to two weeks instead
of the customary 48 hours. These mounting delays stem from an acute shortage of marine residual fuel,
as regional domestic refineries have heavily prioritized gasoline and diesel output to capture lucrative
light-distillate margins at the expense of lower-value heavy residues. Spot premiums for compliant VLSFO
have consequently surged to levels more than a quarter higher than competing hubs across Southeast
Asia, eroding the port’s price competitiveness. Seasonal industrial slowdowns and holiday factory pauses
are expected to provide short-term relief to the congestion.
While in the Middle East, Iran is facing mounting economic and military pressure seven months into the
conflict with the US. Inflation is nearing 90%, the rial has weakened sharply, and Iran reportedly loaded no
crude for export in September as the US blockade tightened. Meanwhile,  Middle East crude flows through
the Strait of Hormuz have recovered close to pre-war levels, reducing some of Tehran’s earlier leverage.
However, maritime risks remain elevated, highlighted by a crude tanker being struck by an unidentified
projectile off Oman. With additional US naval forces heading to the region, shipping, insurance and oil
markets remain exposed to renewed escalation.

Alang, India: Alang navigated a subdued trading environment in the opening week of October, as the
combination of national holidays and acute vessel shortages brought deal -making to a virtual standstill.
Domestic plate values managed a modest recovery following late September's sharp decline, but this
improvement was partially offset by a softer rupee, which continues to elevate buying costs for dollar-
denominated ships. With the candidate pool remaining sparse, open market pricing spreads have
narrowed, leading Alang’s shipbreakers to become noticeably more selective in their bidding. While plot
operators are eager to secure tonnage to maintain cutting schedules, the lack of candidate flow and
competitive pressure from neighbouring beaches have encouraged buyers to exercise discipline.
This restraint is set against an increasingly watchful macroeconomic climate across the broader Indian
economy as financial markets look ahead to next week’s Reserve Bank of India monetary policy meeting.
With annual consumer inflation firming toward 4.8% and global energy prices remaining buoyant, financial
analysts widely anticipate that the central bank may consider raising the benchmark repo rate from 5.25%
to 5.50% to anchor domestic price expectations and defend currency stability. Even with potential
borrowing adjustments on the horizon, India's underlying industrial momentum remains  exceptionally
sturdy, evidenced by an 8% expansion in industrial production and strong domestic infrastructure outlays
that continue to provide a firm floor under rerollable scrap consumption.

Chattogram, Bangladesh: Chattogram made a spirited push into the final quarter, with firm sentiment
and competitive bidding propelling Bangladesh back to the top of the board. Even though actual
transaction volumes remained relatively muted, local buying interest has noticeably s harpened. This
renewed appetite is being driven primarily by operational necessity; with significant cutting capacity
opening across local yards, buyers are eager to secure tonnage and are increasingly prepared to sweeten
their indications.
Fresh data from Bangladesh Bank shows that overseas worker remittances reached US$8.59 billion during
the first quarter of the fiscal year, marking an impressive 13.3% y-o-y growth that continues to reinforce the
country’s foreign currency liquidity.
Gross foreign exchange reserves have remained well anchored above US$36 billion, comfortably standing
near US$22 billion under the IMF’s BPM6 benchmark, providing the banking sector with the stability
required to facilitate Letters of Credit without foreign exchange bottlenecks.

Gaddani, Pakistan: Gadani ceded its regional pricing lead as appetite cooled. Having secured several
high-value vessels during earlier purchasing rounds, buyers have largely satisfied their immediate
inventory requirements, leading to more selective and restrained bidding. While general inquiry remains
visible, the scarcity of workable tonnage has made meaningful deal  making difficult to conclude. Rather
than chasing the market upward, buyers are taking a measured step back to digest existing tonnage and
monitor local scrap absorption.
Official data released this week showed that headline annual consumer inflation eased to 10.3% in
September, providing welcome relief to domestic markets as food price pressures moderated. External
financial stability also touched fresh milestones, with the State Bank of Pakistan reporting that its official
foreign currency reserves reached a high of US$21.44 billion by late September, lifting the nation's total
liquid foreign reserves to US$26.77 billion. With the central bank maintaining a steady benchmark policy
rate of 11.5% and commercial lenders facing minimal foreign exchange constraints when opening Letters
of Credit, Pakistan remain on solid financial footing.

Aliaga, Turkey: Aliaga experienced a subtle positive shift, with indications inching upward in tandem with
the traditional seasonal firming. Buyers have shown more flexibility on price to secure available inventory,
responding to a modest pickup in regional manufacturing inquiries and firmer scrap demand across
domestic mills.
Türkiye’s financial market anticipate next week's official inflation release, with consensus forecasts
projecting annual consumer price growth to ease toward 30.1% from August's 31.51%. To keep disinflation on
course against persistent global energy volatility, the Central Bank of the Republic of Türkiye continues to
maintain its decisive monetary policy stance with the benchmark interest rate anchored at 37%.

India: Containerised shredded scrap prices extended their decline this week as currency depreciation,
ample domestic substitutes, and elevated ocean freight dampened buying appetite. Import flows faced
additional hurdles from compliance complexities surrounding new PSIC requirements, though a UK -origin
steel turnings cargo concluded at US$340/t CNF Mundra. Overall sentiment remained suppressed, with
regional cross-market comparisons showing Bangladeshi bids for US HMS topping out near US $370/t CFR.
Pakistan: Import trading operated as a buyer's market, with mills largely staying sidelined while suppliers
carried unsold cargoes on the water. Confirmed bookings for shredded scrap materialized at US$418/t CFR
Qasim, alongside a second deal reported at US$420/t CFR subject to vessel space. Compounding the slow
demand, local mills continued to face operational uncertainty following court -issued temporary stay
orders in Islamabad and Peshawar against controversial power-bill sales taxes.
Bangladesh: Imported offers held firm despite cautious demand, evidenced by an Australian PNS booking
at US$405/t CFR Chattogram and Chilean HMS 90:10 bids around US $390/t CFR. Sellers actively pushed for
higher realizations amid mounting logistics costs, quoting Malaysian and Philippine PNS at US $415–420/t
CFR and bulk HMS near US$400/t CFR. However, mills resisted these elevated replacement values, leaving
both Australian HMS 90:10 and US West Coast bulk offers at US$400/t CFR unworkable.
Turkiye: Deep-sea import benchmarks held stable d-o-d amid quiet trading, with mills on the sidelines
and sellers maintaining an optimistic near-term outlook. Tradable values for Eurozone HMS 80:20 hovered
near US$395/t CFR, shortsea parcels stood at US$375–385/t CFR, and US premium grades clustered near
US$404/t CFR against broader indications of US$400–405/t CFR. While firm freight and tight Black Sea
semi-finished supplies lend support, anticipated October price hikes from Western suppliers may soon
challenge current levels.

Commodities Focus
Steel rebar futures climbed to roughly CNY 3,109 per ton, touching a one -week high after the World Steel
Association reported that worldwide crude steel output contracted by 1.2% y-o- y in August to 144.2 million
tons and slipped 3.3% m-o-m from July’s 149.2 million tons, with China’s monthly drop of 2.3 million tons
accounting for almost half of that global slowdown. Although China retains its role as the dominant
producer by supplying 51.7% of the world's steel, its domestic mills continue to battle weak builder  demand
and compressed profit margins, prompting the China Iron and Steel Association to urge steelmakers to
curb runs and draw down stock.
Iron ore futures echoed this modest stabilization, lifting toward CNY 710 per ton from eight -week lows as
factory activity in China returned to growth during September alongside government credit programs
targeted at real estate, technology, and civil works. Conversely, copper futures retreated toward US$6.5
per pound to reach two-week lows, as market participation thinned before China’s October 1–7 holiday
and official figures showed national industrial profit expansion decelerating to 15.7% for the first eight
months of the year, down from the 17.6% pace seen through July. The red metal’s near-term outlook
