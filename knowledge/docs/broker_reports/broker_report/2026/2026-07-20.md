MARKET OVERVIEW
The global sectors are monitoring shifting economic indicators out of East Asia, where China’s central
bank is moving to implement stronger counter-cyclical and cross-cyclical measures to bolster its
domestic economy. Following official data showing that second-quarter GDP growth moderated to 4.3%
falling below the national target and marking the softest expansion in several seasons , monetary
authorities have indicated they will deploy a flexible suite of financial tools, including reserve requirement
ratio cuts and reverse repos, to support liquidity. While aggregate financing and new loans for June both
missed initial forecasts, central banking officials characterised credit expansion for the first half of the year
as reasonable, arguing that a combination of loan and bond financing offers a more accurate metric of
capital flowing into the real economy going forward. To counter external speculation regarding currency
management, the bank emphasised that the yuan would continue to fluctuate naturally based on market
forces, even as the country officially emerges from a three-year deflationary cycle with nominal growth
outpacing real expansion.
Simultaneously, the fragile diplomatic detente in the Middle East has collapsed, plunging the Strait of
Hormuz back into a high-intensity conflict and driving global energy benchmarks higher. Following the
official end of the brief maritime ceasefire and the reinstatement of a US naval blockade on Iranian ports,
military forces have significantly widened the scope of their operations. In a major escalation, an unladen
tanker sailing under a Curacao flag was targeted and disabled by military aircraft deep wi thin the Persian
Gulf while attempting to transit toward Iran's primary export terminal at Kharg Island. With regional
authorities warning that the strategic waterway will remain entirely closed to international shipping unless
foreign forces respect their unilateral traffic rules and proposed service fees, local diplomatic channels
have stalled, raising fears of a protracted, destructive standoff.
The swift return of the blockade and the subsequent uptick in regional hostilities have severely disrupted
the temporary recovery of global energy trade. Visible commercial transits through the strait have
experienced a sharp contraction, with a moving average of oil flows dropping to 3.9 million BPD  over the
past week as shipowners refuse to expose their assets to a combination of drifting sea mines, missile
strikes, and heavy drone activity. The operational environment grew even more restrictive following the
suspension of loading operations at a major northern Gulf export hub after an active tanker was struck by
an aerial drone while taking on cargo. While some vessel operators continue to utili se clandestine "dark"
transits with deactivated transponders to shuttle crude out of the region, markets are preparing for a
semi-permanent period of periodic supply disruptions, warning that global benchmark prices are carrying
a structural premium of up to US$15 a barrel to absorb the ongoing geopolitical risk.

Despite ongoing market uncertainties, dry bulk recorded a positive opening half of the year, as tight vessel
availability kept average returns across all vessel sizes well above long- term averages. This supply
squeeze was fuelled by longer travel distances, bunker fuelling bottlenecks tied to the Strait of Hormuz
conflict, and a packed shipyard drydocking schedule for ageing vessels built during the late 2000s
shipping boom, which helped keep Capesize earnings highly lucrative at over US $30,000 per day.
However, a weaker outlook for the rest of the year persists due to a tight fertiliser supply evidenced by an
8% drop in global shipments following lower Persian Gulf exports coupled with extreme heat threatening
upcoming September harvests. On the trading floor, the benchmark Baltic Dry Index snapped a winning
streak on Friday, dipping 3.53% w-o-w to 2,840 points. Capesize index also slipped 11.9% to 4,097 points at

cushion the decline.

Market Insights
Alang, India: Alang experienced a modest gain this week, driven by a tightening of immediate inventory.
The flow slowed to a crawl due to a combination of seasonal monsoon disruptions and a backlog of
incoming tonnage waiting further out at sea. With fewer vessels actively beaching and cutting operations
operating at a reduced pace, the localised scarcity of heavy steel plates artificially elevated trading
sentiments.
Beneath the surface of this price correction, the underlying foundation of the market remains soft.
Consumer-level demand for finished steel products is heavily depressed across domestic infrastructure
and manufacturing sectors, keeping rolling mills highly selective and conservative in their buying patterns.
While Alang’s ship recyclers are deeply eager to acquire fresh tonnage to replenish their yards, they are
facing steep uphill battles. Competing recycling destinations across South Asia continue to put forward
aggressive, higher priced bids, leaving Indian yards struggling to secure commercial tonnage at workable
margins.
While the IMF has projected India to lead major economies with a 6.4% GDP growth rate for the fiscal year,
escalating geopolitical shocks are testing local stability. The resurgence of conflict in the Middle East has
triggered a severe global energy spike, driving Brent crude higher and heavily inflating India’s energy
import bill.

SUB-CONTINENT & TURKEY SCRAP MARKETS
India: Imported ferrous scrap prices edged up slightly amid geopolitical tensions and higher freight, but
buying remained stalled by a wide bid-offer gap. UK-origin shredded was offered at US$395/t CFR West
Coast India against bids at US$390/t CFR, while a 500-ton cargo of Costa Rica origin HMS 60:40 was
booked at US$298/t CFR Chennai. No fresh trades emerged for containerised shredded scrap, as sellers
prioritised higher paying neighbouring markets.
Pakistan: Imported shredded scrap trading held stable as local mills limited procurement to immediate
needs despite gradually firming seaborne offers. Selective deals for UK/EU -origin shredded scrap were
reported in the US$390–US$392/t CFR Qasim range, representing steady consumption at lower levels.
However, fresh indicators rising to US$398–US$400/t CFR have kept broader buying momentum restricted
as mills remain highly cautious.
Bangladesh: The seaborne scrap market remained subdued as mills limited imports, completely resisting
bulk US-origin HMS offers at US$400/t CFR Chattogram and Singapore-origin PNS at US$420/t CFR.
Cautious buying capped large scale bookings, though smaller-volume deals were concluded for
Malaysia-origin GI bundles at US$325/t CFR and Brazil-origin HMS 80:20 at US$345/t CFR. UK-origin
shredded offers hovered tightly near US$398–US$400/t CFR with HMS indicated at $360/t CFR.
Turkiye: Deep-sea import scrap prices held flat as sluggish finished steel demand and severely squeezed
operating margins continued to limit local buying interest. Benchmark US -origin HMS 80:20 was indicated
between US$370/t and US$372/t CFR, reflecting quiet and highly stable day-on-day trading. Many US
exporters remained reluctant to offer at these compressed levels due to elevated freight costs, keeping
fresh regional transactions to a minimum.

Commodities Focus
Iron ore futures sustained values over CNY 750 per ton, hovering close to monthly peaks. This price
resilience comes as BHP reported a 3% annual decline in quarterly production to 68.1 million tons due to a
strategic focus on alternative copper and potash projects, alongside a looming labour strike at Port
Hedland after wage talks collapsed. While stronger shipments from Rio Tinto suggested a stable global
picture, Chinese authorities added to near-term anxieties by restricting certain steelmakers from
accepting lower-grade Australian iron ore imports.

production falling 9.7% year-on-year to 381 million tons in June following a tragic mine incident in Shanxi
that temporarily idled over 300,000 tons of daily capacity.
Despite this first-half contraction of 1.7% to 2.37 billion tons, total annual coal output is still anticipated to hit
a record 4.87 billion tons to meet rising domestic electricity demands.
