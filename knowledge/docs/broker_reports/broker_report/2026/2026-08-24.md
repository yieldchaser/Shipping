MARKET OVERVIEW
International traders are adopting innovative logistical strategies to bypass mounting bottlenecks and
record-breaking transit costs at the Panama Canal. With drought conditions linked to an intensifying
weather pattern lowering local water reserves and prompting authorities to restrict daily passage
allocations, the cost to secure immediate transit slots for larger gas carriers surged to unprecedented
heights of US$4.6 million. To avoid these exorbitant expenses, major energy corporations are increasingly
deploying cargo transfers off the Pacific entrance near Balboa. Under this model, smaller vessels that
navigate the canal's conventional locks transfer fuel loads directly to larger carriers positioned offshore,
which then commence the transpacific journey to key Asian centers.
In East Asia, the corporate landscape witnessed the historic conclusion to a multi-year debt crisis as
judicial authorities handed down a life sentence to the founder of Evergrande alongside several senior
executives. The landmark ruling follows extensive state investigations into widespread financial
misstatements, unauthorised fundraising, and concealed debt obligations that ultimately precipitated the
group's default and subsequent liquidation. While massive corporate penalties have been levied and
extensive asset seizures ordered, the formal resolution provides a measure of closure for broader markets.
For global maritime, the development marks a defining end to an era of unbridled real estate expansion
that previously fuelled substantial bulk commodity demand, reinforcing a broader transition toward more
measured industrial growth across the region.
Meanwhile, U.S. energy markets are bracing for a temporary tightening of regional feedstock supply just as
domestic processing activity reaches multi-year highs. Refineries operating across the central United
States, which have been running at peak capacity to capitalise on historic distillate margins, face
imminent delivery reductions due to scheduled maintenance across Canadian oil sands facilities next
month. With regional storage hubs in Alberta hovering near historic seasonal lows, the anticipated
curtailment of hundreds of thousands of daily barrels will leave processing plants with minimal buffer
stocks.
This supply squeeze, coupled with renewed geopolitical friction in overseas maritime corridors, has
pushed regional crude benchmarks and fuel premiums higher, setting the stage for raised operational
expenses as refiners compete for available Atlantic and inland barrels.

On the trading floor, the benchmark Baltic Dry Index fell to 2,841 points, a slight 0.77% weekly decline when
compared against Week 33’s standing. Segment performance showed clear structural divergence
throughout the week, with Capesize rates maintaining a firm presence to help offset downward
momentum in the Panamax sector—which drifted to 2,103 points amid ample tonnage and quieter grain
demand—while the Supramax segment demonstrated steady support near 1,640 points (up from last

Segment Highlights
Capesize: Capesize softened overall this week as C5 rates fell 11.5% due to slower Western Australian iron
ore volumes and the return of open vessel capacity following the resolution of weather disruptions. In
contrast, steady long-haul cargo volumes from Brazil helped maintain stability despite quiet fixture
activity across the wider Atlantic. Reflecting this regional correction, spot earnings moved lower across
most key routes, bringing Pacific r/v down to US$37,250’s with T/A trips at US$40,575 per day.
Panamax / Kamsarmax: Panamax softened this week as spot rates slipped, weighed down by a decline in
Brazilian grain exports alongside a drop in Indonesian coal volumes. Although Australian grain enquiries
helped absorb some regional tonnage, expanding vessel availability in the North Atlantic and a cautious
tone across both basins kept charterers in control and widened the gap between bids and offers.
Consequently, spot returns faced a broad correction across key routes, bringing T/A rates down to
US$19,760’s and Pacific r/v to US$16,200’s per day.
Supramax / Ultramax: Supramax held broadly steady, rising as solid long-haul demand from the USG and
NOPAC helped offset persistent weakness in Southeast Asia. While an influx of North American stems
absorbed vessel capacity and supported the Atlantic, ample tonnage and limited fresh enquiry across the
Continent and Mediterranean continued to cap further upside potential. Reflecting these balanced
regional conditions, spot returns edged slightly higher across major routes, lifting T/A rates to US$27,360’s
and Pacific r/v to US$15,600’s.
Handysize: Handysize saw another subdued session, with the Continent and Mediterranean remaining
soft due to limited cargo volumes while the USG and South Atlantic traded broadly stable. Asia proved to
be the strongest performing region, supported by tighter prompt tonnage availability and steady cargo
flows despite relatively limited fixing activity. Reflecting modest gains across key trades, spot rates moved
upward across the board, lifting T/A rates to US$15,800’s per day with Inter-Pacific routes to US$15,725’s.

VLCC segment is witnessing another historic surge in freight rates as access through the Arabian Gulf
narrows to a trickle. Daily earnings on the benchmark Middle East-to-China route have broken past the
US$500,000 mark, with single-voyage lump-sum fixtures reaching a staggering US$31 million, as
demonstrated by the recent booking of the Mongolia Prosperity. To maintain exports, producers are relying
heavily on shuttle operations, with Saudi Aramco recently loading three tankers at Ras Tanura for prompt
S-T-S transfers off Fujairah. At the same time, major Chinese state shippers like COSCO and CMES have
pulled more than 100 tankers from entering the waterway entirely, choosing instead to collect outside the
chokepoint in Oman and the UAE. This has left global fleet deployment heavily skewed, with 592 empty
tankers currently ballasting across international waters against just 372 actively loaded vessels.
This freight spike reflects a severe deterioration in physical security, marked by seven vessel attacks in the
passage this month alone and the recent halting of the tanker Amara near Qeshm Island. Daily crude
movements through the Strait of Hormuz have fallen to roughly 2 million BPD, down from 4.8 million in July
and representing barely 11% of the 18 million barrels moved daily prior to the conflict. The broader Middle
Eastern export volume has halved to 9.5 million BPD compared to 21 million in 2025, with Iranian shipments
shrinking to 294,000 BPD. With China's refinery throughput inching up 0.3% month-on-month to 12.5 million
BPD —supported by a 25 to 28 million barrel draw on domestic inventories—and the IEA projecting a full-
year global supply decline of 4.3 million BPD leading to a 1.27 million BPD deficit, the market is bracing for
an enduring physical shortage across both crude and refined products.
Segment Highlights
VLCC: After a brief dip, Middle East VLCC rates surged to WS570 as heightened tensions—reignited by the
U.S. decisions—prompted shipowners to aggressively raise quotes. With position lists rapidly thinning on
both sides of Suez, rates across, including Brazil, West Africa, and Fujairah-Oman, are galloping higher in a
fast-moving, owner-favourable market. 260,000mt WAFR/China also saw rates jump to WS209.
Suezmax: West African market surged, propelled by shrinking prompt tonnage, a rush of late-August
stems, and runaway VLCC rates that pushed indices past WS328 for Nigeria/UKC. Rates are poised for
further strength on the back of owner leverage and firm second-decade demand, though dwindling first-
decade stems and being priced out of the USG by cheaper Aframaxes may temper the rally.
Aframax: Aframax are poised to maintain their firm momentum this week as steady Australian regional
crude flows tighten tonnage lists, with owners continuing to demand geopolitical risk premiums. Across
the Mediterranean, rates have gained around 23 points on consistent inquiry and prompt replacement
needs with Ceyhan/Lavera closing WS250, pointing toward further firming as vessels are drawn away
toward the U.S. market into late August and early September.
LR1 / LR2: Middle East LR2 closed lower as renewed tanker attacks in the Strait triggered delays, STS transfer
cancellations, and a sharp drop in fresh cargo inquiries. TC1 MEG/Japan gained momentum closing higher
at WS536. In the LR1 segment, rates also shifted upwards with TC5 seeing gains settling at WS565.

Market Insights
Alang, India: Alang saw a welcome boost in domestic pricing, with local ship steel plate and melting scrap
values strengthened noticeably m-o-m. The common plate price increased from INR 38,400/MT to INR
40,500/MT, a rise of INR 2,100/MT or about 5.5%. Similarly, ship melting scrap prices improved from INR
33,300/MT to INR 34,500/MT, gaining INR 1,200/MT or about 3.6%
.
The month-on-month improvement reflects firmer domestic steel sentiment and stronger recycler appetite, with
the sharper increase in higher-grade 8 ANI material indicating improved demand for reusable ship steel.
Supported by a steady currency exchange rate, providing local buyers with much needed margin relief.
While overall sentiment remains upbeat and end buyers continue to display an active appetite for fresh
tonnage, Alang faces persistent headwinds in the international bidding arena.
Substantial price premiums offered by competing yards in Pakistan and Bangladesh continue to make
conventional dry bulk carriers and container vessels difficult for Indian recyclers to secure. However, Alang
remains uniquely positioned at both ends of the market, serving as the only destination for sanctioned
tonnage, while also attracting highly sophisticated vessels requiring stringent, fully compliant green
recycling standards.
This resilience unfolds against a reassuringly solid national macroeconomic backdrop. In its latest policy
assessment, the Reserve Bank of India kept its benchmark repo rate steady at 5.25% while upgrading
India's full-year economic growth projection to 6.7%, underscoring the underlying strength of domestic
manufacturing and private consumption. Newly released government data also confirmed that July
consumer price inflation landed at a manageable 4.45%, keeping price pressures comfortably within the
central bank's target boundaries.

Gaddani, Pakistan: Gadani maintained its market-leading position across the subcontinent, with local
buyers sustaining an aggressive and highly competitive purchasing spree. Supported by an ongoing
domestic billet shortage and seasonal supply bottlenecks from monsoon weather, local steel plate prices
firmed to 203,000 Pakistani rupees/MT, the highest standing quote in the region. This moved in tandem
with rising vessel price indications, where Pakistani recyclers have shown greater determination and
bidding power than their counterparts in India and Bangladesh.
The sudden spike in offered prices was largely a reflection of mounting frustration and urgency among
certain recyclers. Some yards were prepared to pay aggressive premiums to secure tonnage and
demonstrate their operational capabilities as part of the process of obtaining or maintaining HKC
certification, while others were driven by taxation and internal accounting considerations, requiring
vessels to complete transactions and clear outstanding audit-related matters.
Fresh data released by the State Bank of Pakistan revealed that the country's total liquid foreign exchange
reserves climbed past US$22.5 billion in mid-August, with central bank holdings rising to US$17.08 billion.
Supported by an unchanged benchmark policy rate of 11.5% and a steady national economic growth
outlook projected between 3.5% and 4.5%, this expanding foreign currency cushion is keeping the rupee
stable and providing local commercial banks with the dollar liquidity needed to smoothly open LCs.

SUB-CONTINENT & TURKEY SCRAP MARKETS
India: Imported containerised shredded scrap strengthened alongside domestic steel to US$395–400/t
CFR, while HMS 80:20 stood near US$340/t CFR and Australia-origin HMS hovered at US$340-345/t CFR
Chennai. Despite firmer offers, procurement stayed measured as an unfavourable exchange rate and
competitive domestic scrap kept Nhava Sheva HMS bids low at US$310–315/t CFR. Consequently, mills are
actively adjusting their metallic mix toward DRI and pellets to avoid holding high-cost imported
inventories amid sluggish finished-steel sales.
Pakistan: Procurement momentum in Pakistan remained slow as comfortable mill inventories, persistent
monsoon rains, and tepid finished steel consumption reduced buying urgency. Exporters maintained
EU/UK shredded offers at US$415–420/t CFR Port Qasim, facing pushback from domestic mills holding bids
firmly around US$410–412/t CFR. This persistent bid-offer spread restricted fresh bookings, leaving
benchmark shredded assessments flat at US$415/t CFR Port Qasim.
Bangladesh: Import activity in Chattogram remained subdued and selective, weighed down by heavy
monsoon disruptions, slow construction progress, and strict shipment criteria across Asian origins. Deep-
sea HMS 80:20 was indicated at US$370-375/t CFR, while shredded scrap spanned US$390–400/t CFR and
premium Singapore PNS held higher at US$410–415/t CFR. With mills actively avoiding large-volume
commitments to mitigate price risk, seaborne purchases remain confined strictly to immediate
operational requirements
Turkiye: Deep-sea import values held stable, with benchmark US-origin HMS 80:20 assessed at US$375/t
CFR Turkiye as mills showed little urgency after covering their August requirements. European sellers
targeted US$370/t CFR or higher, supported by low Rhine water levels, Black Sea disruptions, and US freight
rates of US$35–36/t. However, persistent weakness in downstream rebar demand continues to prevent
Turkish steelmakers from accepting higher scrap levels for late-September bookings.

Commodities Focus
Chinese coking coal futures in Dalian jumped 15% this week to reach 1,583.50 yuan (US$235) per ton, their
highest valuation since October 2024, as an estimated 50 to 60 million tons of annual capacity in Shanxi
remains offline following extensive safety audits triggered by a major mining incident that initially
shuttered more than 130 million tons of capacity.
In the base metals market, an acute physical squeeze pushed global copper near record peaks as traders
aggressively redirected metal into the United States ahead of potential import tariffs, driving prompt cash
contracts as much as US$545/MT over three-month futures—the widest backwardation since 2021—and
depleting London Metal Exchange inventories by nearly half since mid-May before stabilizing at 207,800
tons with three-month prices settling at US$14,157.50/MT after an earlier 1.7% advance, alongside slight
upticks in aluminum of 0.4% and zinc of 0.3%.
Iron ore futures also slipped toward CNY 700 per ton to hover around 14-month lows, weighed down by
record contractions in Chinese new yuan lending that signal ongoing stagnation across property and
infrastructure development, even as blast furnace operating rates across domestic mills ticked up slightly
by 0.32% points to 82.64%.
