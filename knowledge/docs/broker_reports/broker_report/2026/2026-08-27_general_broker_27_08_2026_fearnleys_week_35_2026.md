## VLCC
No material change in the VLCC market, still strong by any measure. However, there are signs which could suggest it’s levelling out, which would not be too surprising. Risk/reward does start to play a part at these historically high levels – how much to push? Is another couple of points worth losing out and risk waiting? Daily returns up to and into USD 200k/day also means it “costs” the same to idle and wait to load. In the words of the late Dolly Parton, may she rest in peace: "The way I see it, if you want the rainbow, you gotta put up with the rain."

## Suezmax
There has been reasonable activity to start the week. However, the market remains very much oversupplied, with 17 FOC and 7 ETS 5 day window ETA West Africa by 10 September. On the flip side, there are plenty of cargoes to move in the second decade
from both Guyana and West Africa. For the time being, however, charterers do not appear to be under any pressure to work much beyond two weeks forward from these regions. In addition, the Aframax market stateside appears to be heavily oversupplied, with Aframaxes pricing Suezmaxes out of USG/TA moves on a USD/bbl basis, which further incentivises charterers to sit on their hands.

## Aframax
North Sea After the large number of vessels we have seen ballast towards the US the North Sea list has been left with a reduced number of natural candidates for North Sea business. Dates have pushed into early September with rates picking. The flow of tonnage leaving for the US is currently less as that market has softened on the back of vessels arriving but with the Mediterranean also pushing up tonnage will continue to move out. First decade North Sea stems are a little on the light side which may limit the firming of rates.
Mediterranean Activity in the Mediterranean remains consistent with dates pushing into September and cargoes from certain ports pushing out to the 5th. Like the North Sea the Mediterranean had seen vessels leaving for the US which has helped owners push for increases in rate levels. List remain interesting with some tonnage compromised due to trading history.

## Capesize
This week on Capesize we saw strong demand from Pacific C5 as three majors were seen in the market alongside a healthy showing of supportive operators. Recent bids seen at mid to high USD 15 levels and today a Newcastlemax fixed at low USD 15 C5 equivalent. Ballasters are looking to get ahead of yet another Typhoon forecasted to hit the coast of southeast China (near Fujian) around late Thursday to early Friday and recent fixtures ex West Australia have been reported in high USD 14s. Atlantic C3 Brazil/China has also seen healthy activity with rumors expecting even higher levels for October. With a flurry of fixtures yesterday, C3 ex Brazil reported levels for 2h September laycans in mid/high USD 37s with one stem reported at USD 38 for late September. Offers today reported in high USD 37 and mid USD 38s for early October
laycans ex Brazil. The North Atlantic shows some fronthaul and transatlantic demand ex Seven Islands for later September dates and tonnage remains healthy in the North. A recent fixture ex Seven Islands to India was reported in low USD 42 for end September.

## Panamax
The Panamax market has firmed steadily through the week, with the index posting consecutive gains and sentiment improving across both basins. The Pacific has been the clearer engine: NoPac and Australian grain and coal demand has absorbed the prompt tonnage that had been weighing on the market, and owners are now comfortable holding their levels while charterers chase. In the Atlantic the North has been the steadier of the two, supported by a healthy coal and minerals programme into the Continent that has kept fronthaul well bid, while the South Atlantic has traded more sideways as the market looks toward the next grain window and owners show little urgency to discount. Period enquiry has picked up noticeably, with a wider range of owners and operators willing to engage on one-year business, although bids continue to sit modestly behind owners' ideas. With paper firmer and tonnage tightening into September, we expect the tone to remain sideways-to-firmer in the near term.

## Supramax
The Supramax market remained largely subdued and stable. The Atlantic was mixed, with the Mediterranean gradually softening while the Continent saw some fresh enquiry and the US Gulf and South Atlantic remained broadly balanced. In the Pacific, activity was stronger in the NOPAC and parts of Southeast Asia, while Indonesian coal activity was relatively limited. The Handysize market showed a modestly firmer tone, supported by improving cargos in East Coast South America, tighter prompt tonnage in parts of the Pacific and better sentiment in the US Gulf. While the US Gulf was still soft in the short term despite expectations of improvement ahead.
