MARKET OVERVIEW
Daily merchant vessel crossings through the Strait of Hormuz have begun to recover from recent lows,
supported in part by active naval escort operations aimed at safeguarding energy exports. Recent
tracking data highlights a notable uptick in transit volumes, marked by the open departure of Qatar’s first
LNG shipment in several weeks alongside specialised gas carriers navigating with dark transponders.
While security risks in the Persian Gulf remain fluid following recent military exchanges, daily energy
outflows have stabilised near six and a half million barrels. This gradual rebound in cargo activity has been
accompanied by exceptionally high freight valuations, with charterers provisionally committing nearly half
a million dollars per day to secure tankers for upcoming loadings destined for East Asia.
Further west, commercial operators are navigating a fragmented security environment across the Red
Sea, balancing persistent regional threats against urgent supply commitments. Vessels calling at Saudi
Arabia’s western loading facilities continue to approach the area despite ongoing hostility near southern
maritime chokepoints. To mitigate exposure, a growing share of Asian crude buyers is taking delivery at
Egyptian Mediterranean terminals, relying on northern pipelines to transfer Middle Eastern crude away
from active conflict zones.
However, fresh safety concerns have emerged near northern canal approaches following unexplained
security incidents involving gas carriers at Egyptian ports, driving additional tankers to hold position
offshore while waiting for operational clarity.
On the macroeconomic front, global financial markets experienced notable volatility as equity indices
posted sharp single-day declines following central bank decisions to maintain elevated interest rates.
Investor sentiment has been further strained by rising energy costs, with benchmark American crude
climbing toward eighty-five dollars a barrel amid concerns over potential military escalation. Monetary
policymakers remain divided, as persistent inflation pressures above target levels have heightened
expectations of future rate hikes.
Historical market patterns suggest that while short-term equity pullbacks are common after sudden
market adjustments, broader financial conditions typically stabilise over multi-month horizons as energy
supply chains adapt to ongoing geopolitical friction.

DRY BULK | BDI: 2,732 ▼0.40% (w-o-w)
The dry bulk freight market experienced a general softening, as highlighted by BDI falling to 2,732 points.
Geopolitical and environmental factors continue to heavily influence market dynamics, with ongoing
uncertainty surrounding the Strait of Hormuz—shaping broader supply-demand expectations alongside
projected El Niño weather patterns that could tighten Panama Canal transits and increase sailing
distances. Looking at the individual vessel segments, the Capesize index regained slightly to 4,296 points,
while the Supramax index dropped 5.02% to 1,609 points.
Bucking the downward trend across the rest of the market, the Panamax index registered gains by rising
3.11% on Friday to 2,087 points. Despite these near-term index adjustments, underlying market
fundamentals for 2026 remain relatively supportive, as expanding grain and coal trade flows are expected
to keep demand growth ahead of the 1.5% to 2.5% fleet supply growth.
Segment Highlights
Capesize: Capesize saw a general softening across major basins this week with limited fresh West
Australian iron ore enquiry. Long-haul Atlantic routes out of Brazil and West Africa also encountered
downside pressure due to lower oil prices and quiet overall demand, with shipowners discounting prompt
tonnage to secure employment. As a result, market returns edged lower across most key routes, bringing
Pacific R/V down to US$31,543 per day, with T/A trips holding a slight gain at US$44,247 per day.
Panamax / Kamsarmax: Panamax market softened over the course of the week as the BPI fell 10% amidst
a seasonal summer lull, driven by a drop in Brazilian grain exports alongside reduced coal shipments from
Indonesia and Australia. Thin Atlantic enquiry forced owners to discount prompt tonnage to secure
employment, which dragged T/A rates down to US$19,625 per day. Meanwhile, a late-week improvement
in NOPAC grain enquiry helped steady the East off recent lows, leaving Pacific R/V at US$14,732 per day.
Supramax / Ultramax: Supramax extended its recent downward trend, weighed down by limited fresh
cargo enquiry, expanding tonnage availability in the NOPAC, and spillover competition from softer
Panamax rates. While the Mediterranean and Black Sea regions retained underlying support due to tighter
local vessel lists, a shortage of cargoes across the USG pushed F/H trips to US$28,221 per day. Sluggish
Asian demand and falling Indonesian coal exports further dampened the Eastern basin, dragging
Pacific/India runs to US$17,355 per day and Indonesian R/V to US$14,118 per day.
Handysize: Handysize market traded largely unchanged this week, driven primarily by vessel positioning
rather than any notable shift in cargo demand as a quiet Pacific basin kept fixing activity subdued. While
the Continent and Mediterranean regions maintained a balanced but quiet tone, the wider Atlantic basin
softened further as a growing tonnage list outpaced incoming cargo volume. This expanding vessel
availability weighed on Atlantic returns, dragging T/A rates down to US$15,965 per day while Inter-Pacific
runs edged up slightly to US$15,233 per day.

Shipowners are increasingly redirecting empty tankers toward Egypt’s Mediterranean terminal at Sidi Kerir
to collect Saudi crude, as heightened caution increases over sailing near Houthi-threatened waters in the
southern Red Sea. At least eight VLCCs are scheduled to arrive at the Egyptian port by mid-August, as
Saudi Aramco expands sales along this northern corridor via the Suez-Mediterranean (SUMED) pipeline.
Rising insurance restrictions for Saudi calls have prompted several fleet managers—including South
Korea's Sinokor Group—to load at Sidi Kerir instead. At the same time, a cluster of ten empty Saudi tankers
remains positioned near the Gulf of Aden, while visible traffic through the Strait of Hormuz remains
extremely low apart from occasional dark transits with transponders deactivated.
The wider oil sector is facing a growing mismatch between soft futures pricing and increasingly strained
physical supply. Memories of 2022, when Brent crude reached nearly US$140 per barrel before trade routes
adjusted, initially encouraged traders to believe the market could easily absorb Middle Eastern disruptions.
This optimism has kept benchmark prices below US$90 per barrel during recent lulls in military strikes, but
physical indicators paint a far tighter picture. Refining crack spreads have hit record levels as global
supplies of gasoline, diesel, and jet fuel continue to shrink, while crude inventories and the U.S. Strategic
Petroleum Reserve experience steady draws. As global demand outpaces real-world fuel production, the
market's heavy reliance on supply-chain flexibility is being tested by dwindling physical reserves.

VLCC: VLCC maintains a firm outlook as persistent MEG geopolitical risks and transit challenges in Hormuz
and the Bab el-Mandeb drive Far East charterers to secure tonnage early and seek alternative options like

Suezmax: West African market faces a slight dipped as ample supply of prompt vessels and Eastern
ballasters head towards the Cape kept rates under pressure despite steady cargo activity. Nigeria/UKC
trips closed at WS222. However, further rate drops are limited by solid demand from the Atlantic, as well as
risk-premium pricing on Black Sea CPC routes as CPC/Augusta trips gain some 130 points to WS440.
Aframax: Middle East segment remain steady as short-haul chartering demand continues despite
shippers taking a cautious, wait-and-see approach amid regional risks. Meanwhile, the Mediterranean
sector faces a growing tonnage list and rate correction, as Ceyhan/Lavera route fell to WS320.
LR1 / LR2: MEG LR2 market slipped slightly as route instability in the Persian Gulf and Red Sea continue to
keep rates in the higher mark. TC1 MEG/Japan fell to WS497.  Rates are expected to remain firm, bolstered

demand. In the MEG, rates continue to soar slightly with TC17 E. Africa trips settling at WS515 this week.

CONTAINERS
The global container shipping market continued its gradual downward correction this week as the SCFI
index fell to 3,062.95 points, signalling a stabilising adjustment rather than a sharp downturn. Reflecting
this broader trend across major trade lanes, the KCCI index dropped 81 points, to settle at 4,123, as the
initial surge from early summer peak-season shipments continued to taper off. Across the Americas and
South America, spot rates on both the U.S. East and West Coasts dipped further under the weight of
waning peak-season demand and shipper resistance to elevated pricing, though MSC’s new Panama
Canal surcharge underscores the persistent, high-cost operational structure underpinning these routes.
Middle East route demonstrated a clear counter-cyclical rebound, rising for a second consecutive week as
escalating security concerns surrounding the Strait of Hormuz prompted carriers to reintroduce risk
premiums, emergency fuel surcharges, and contingency fees. Looking ahead, while the influx of extra
vessel loaders will likely maintain short-term downward pressure on deep-sea trades, the floor for global
freight levels remains insulated by carrier capacity management—including routine 10% blank sailings—
and ongoing vessel diversions away from the Red Sea.

Market Insights
Alang, India: Alang observed a slight lift in buyer sentiment this week, driven by buyers actively seeking
fresh tonnage after losing several recent deals to competing yards in Chattogram and Gadani. Domestic
steel plate pricing saw a mid-week rally from 38,500 Indian rupees to 39,000 rupees per MT before
retracing back to 38,500 rupees, though favourable currency shifts helped push it to about US$404/ton.
This price firming was largely powered by tight physical plate inventories due to monsoon schedule rather
than a surge in steel mill demand, which remains relatively subdued. Nevertheless, with seasonal rainfall
deficits now fully covered, local recyclers increasingly believe the market has hit its floor and is primed for
a September rebound.
Following a prolonged lull, the Alang market witnessed a notable revival over the past two months, with
more than one million tons arriving each month. Tankers dominated the recycling deals and were
concluded at relatively discounted prices due to their specialised nature, providing much needed activity
and keeping the recycling industry busy.
This optimism coincides with broader indicators showing fundamental resilience across the Indian
economy, where industrial output quickened sharply to a near two-year high of 7.3% in June, powered by a
7.8% expansion in manufacturing. The Reserve Bank of India has maintained its positive GDP growth
projection of 6.6% for the fiscal year, reassuring markets that recent supply side inflationary pressures
stemming from global energy volatility remain manageable. Supported by robust domestic capital
expenditure and steady monsoon coverage, these economic fundamentals provide buyers with a
somewhat stable long-term backdrop as temporary seasonal disruptions clear.

SUB-CONTINENT & TURKEY SCRAP MARKETS
India: Imported ferrous scrap trading remains severely muted due to ongoing three-month monsoon
disruptions, leaving buyers reliant strictly on hand-to-mouth, negotiated transactions. Unviable import
economics persist as UK/EU-origin asking prices stay above US$345–350/t CFR and premium yards quote
US$355–360/t CFR, far exceeding local workable HMS 80:20 levels of US$320–330/t CFR. Meanwhile,
containerised shredded scrap is indicated at US$365–370/t CFR and Australia-origin HMS at US$335–
340/t CFR Chennai, keeping overall seaborne interest minimal.
Pakistan: The Pakistani seaborne scrap market maintained a firm stance, supported by constrained
regional supply and steady, selective mill demand. Highlighted transactions include UK-origin
containerised shredded scrap traded at US$418–419/t CFR Port Qasim on 29 July, outpacing domestic
shredded values resting around US$402/t. This ongoing price gap underscores the premium Pakistani
buyers remain willing to pay for quality imported raw materials amid tight global availability.
Bangladesh: Import activity in Chattogram remains subdued as local steel mills continue to resist
elevated offer levels, keeping fresh deal flow severely constrained. Broad bids for bulk HMS hover near
US$385/t CFR against seller targets of US$390–395/t CFR, while US-origin HMS remains the most
competitive option near US$380/t CFR. In contrast, higher-priced offers for Singapore HMS at US$400/t
CFR, alongside Japanese H2 at US$395–400/t CFR and HS at US$425/t CFR, have failed to attract
meaningful buyer engagement.
Turkiye: Deep-sea import values held stable on 30 July as trading slowed during the European summer
holiday period, dragging mill bids down to US$365/t CFR for UK/EU-origin HMS 80:20 and US$380/t CFR for
US cargoes. Despite quiet procurement, scrap prices found strong underlying support from severe supply
chain disruptions in continental Europe, where low water levels on the Rhine severely restricted inland
barge transport. These ongoing logistics bottlenecks have curtailed export cargo availability from German
hinterlands, effectively preventing a further downward correction in seaborne offers.

Commodities Focus
As July comes to a close, global industrial commodities face contrasting trajectories with macroeconomic
policy decisions and persistent domestic demand challenges in major consuming regions. Iron ore futures
slipped toward CNY 720 per ton, hitting their lowest valuation since June 2025 as financial losses among
Chinese steelmakers in Tangshan expanded beyond CNY 100 per ton, severely dampening raw material
purchasing. This weakness extended directly to the finished steel market, where rebar futures fell to near
CNY 3,010 per ton—their weakest point since October 2025—under the strain of a prolonged real estate
slowdown in China, a sector that typically drives about one-third of national steel consumption.
Market sentiment across bulk metals remained cautious as investors anticipated key policy signals from
Beijing's Politburo meeting, where officials are expected to prioritise current fiscal implementation over
aggressive new stimulus packages, while Australian supply remained plentiful after Western Australia's
Pilbara Ports reported shipping 759.4 million tons of iron ore out of more than 800 million tons of total
cargo handled during the 2025–2026 financial year.
