The shippers of cargo cannot exercise any control over the master and crew of a vessel, after the goods are once placed on board, and in this case the pits, do not appear even to have chartered tbis vessel for their own exclusive use, as no charter-party has been produced, and the bills of lading, for the two parcels of copper shipped at Hondeklip Bay and Port Nolloth, which were signed by the captain and put in evidence, are in the usual form which is used when goods are put on board a general ship. The captain and crew are, therefore, in no sense, as it appears to me, the agents of the shippers of the goods. On the contrary, the captain and owner of the ship stand in a totally diffe- rent situation, for being carriers of the goods for hire, they incur all the liabilities which attach to ordinary carriers by sea, subject, of course, to the provisions contained in the bills of lading.
