In February, the barque E., anchored in the Sloyne, in charge of a pilot, broke her sheer and drifted across the port bow and chain of the P., at anchor about two cables' length distant. The pilot and most of the crew jumped on board the P. After the vessels had been in contact three-quarters of an hour, the tide ebbed and the wind blowing from an opposite direction, caused a lumpy sea. The tug W. then came up, got hold of the E., and held her for an hour, without, however, getting her clear. Blue lights were burnt on board the P., and the tug N. came up in answer thereto, made fast to the E., and got her clear and to a safe anchorage. The Eva (Liverpool Pass. Co.), M. M.E.
