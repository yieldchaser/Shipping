A further question sometimes arises whether the owners may not have clothed the master with apparent authority so as to be precluded from disputing his autho- rity. Ibid. 78. The liability of an owner to pay for repairs and equipments ordered by the master, depends not upon the ground of ownership but upon the ground of a contract made with the vendor by a per- son who was the owner's agent, for the purpose of ordering such repairs and equipments. No contract of a master can bind the owner, unless such authority has been actually given to him, or unless the owner has by word or deed held out the master as his master, and thereby in- duced the vendor to supply the articles upon the credit of the owner.
