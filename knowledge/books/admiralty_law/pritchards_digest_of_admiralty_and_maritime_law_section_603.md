In November, the brigantine A. 0., from Lagos for London, with cereals, was descried about two miles off by the steamer B., which was waiting to enter the harbour of St. Helier. It was blowing a gale. The A. C. was drifting towards the rocks about half to leeward of her, and could not save herself by anchoring. The B. bore up to her, and with great difficulty making fast to her, towed her to within a mile of the harbour when the hawser parted. The A., on the 5th November, near Hasborough light, suddenly struck the ground, there being increasing gales and a high sea. For safety the sails were cut away, and a great part of the cargo thrown overboard. The water rose in the vessel under the half-deck, and the pumps could not remove it. On the following morning, the vessel being in great danger, a signal of distress was hoisted ; three fishing smacks put off to her assistance, and on the 9th carried her in safety into the Humber. This action was brought against the cargo, the vessel having been con- demned and sold, and one-third of the value paid to the salvors. The Apollo, S. G. April 27, 1847. V. 1,3502.— A. ^400 640.
