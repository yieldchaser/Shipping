In April, the steamer C. IX., from Copenhagen to London, with general cargo and passengers, had broken down about 130 miles from Harwich, having lost her propeller. She was burning rockets for assistance. The Danish paddle steamer E., from Esbjerg for Harwich, with cattle and butter, when in lat. 53-28 and long. 4-10, sighted the C. IX. about three miles off, and came up with her. The E. agreed to tow the 0. IX. to Harwich, whence a tug could take her to London. A hawser was made fast, and the E. commenced towing the 0. IX. The wind changed to a fresh breeze from S.8.W., and the tow-rope chafed. Next evening Lowestoft was sighted. The weather was hazy, with rain, and a pilot was taken on board each vessel. At midnight of the next day the two ships were brought to anchor off the Cork lightship. The E. lay by the C. IX. during the night, and at daylight both vessels proceeded into Harwich harbour.
