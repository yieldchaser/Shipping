The master has, by virtue of his employment, not merely those powers ■which are necessary for the navigation of the ship and the conduct of the adventure to a safe termination, but also a power, where such termination becomes hopeless, and no prospect remains of bringing the vessel home, to do the best for all con- cerned, and therefore to dispose of her for their benefit. It is a case of neces- sity, when nothing better can be done for the master's employers. Hunter v. Parker, 7 M. & W. 322 ; The Lord Cochrane, 2 W. Eob. 335. 398. The existence of a necessity suffi- cient to justify the master in the sale of the ship abroad depends upon the circum- stances of the case. It certainly does not arise where a ship is capable of repair, and where means exist by hypothecation or otherwise of paying for those repairs.
