In December, the Norwegian brig P., during a very heavy gale, and in a high sea, was aground on Castle Point and had a distress signal hoisted at the gaff end. A pilot, with a volunteer crew of three hands, went to her assistance. The boat was one hour getting to the ship, owing to the tempestuous and dangerous state of the weather. The brig had taken the ground fore and aft, and was striking heavily. The pilot took charge of her, and with his men separated the chains, which were secured round her mainmast, and one of his men, at great personal risk, put the buoy over the bow to buoy the chains. The tug Da. came up and towed for three hours, partly alone and partly with the tug Do. During the towing the hawser of the tug Da. parted, but another was taken to the Da. from the brig by the pilot boat. The Do. was engaged about five hours, and took the brig to the docks with the aid of the tug W., which came up after she was got off. £70 to the pilot boat, £75 to the Da., £40 to the Do., and £3 to the W.
