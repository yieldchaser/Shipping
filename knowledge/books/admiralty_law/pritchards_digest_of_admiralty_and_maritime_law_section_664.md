The schooner P., fifty-eight tons, laden with iron, met with great stress of weather, and laboured heavily, making a great deal of water. She was brought to anchor ; the wind increased to a gale, and she parted from her starboard anchor. The remaining part of the chain was shackled upon the inner part of the larboard chain, and veered out to the end. She then slipped from her anchor, and accepted the assistance of the P., of forty-six tons, with five or six hands, which took her to Whit- stable. The schooner was in imminent danger, and it was indispensable for her safety that she should be taken to Whitstable, there being no other port near. The Primrose, S. G. May 1, 1849.
