Having this right, it has been determined that, at an intermediate port, the master may, if the ship- per wishes his goods, give them up to him, and demand his full freight, or, if this is not paid, he may carry them on, and thus earn it. This grows out of the entirety of the contract. It is however another question, and one it seems to us susceptible of argu- ment, whether the master is obliged, at the intermediate port, to give up the goods on the tender of full freight. There can be no question as to his power to deliver them np ; but we are now considering whether he is so far bound to do this, that in default thereof he will be liable as a wrongdoer.
