The British frigate T., with bullion, struck upon a rock on the coast of Brazil., and was driven into a very dangerous cove, and sunk. The hull went to pieces, leaving the treasure upon the bottom in five and six fathoms water. The admiral on the station and the captains and crews of four sloops of war were engaged for eighteen months in recovering the treasure. The service was attended with great skill, labour, and danger, and four lives were lost. The J. was sunk in deep water and ten feet in the sand, near Margate, Several attempts by the agent of the underwriters to raise the vessel failed. A company of salvors made a second attempt, which also failed. They then purchased a vessel of 248 tons, fitted machinery on board at considerable expense, laboured a month, and raised the vessel, when the gear gave way and she again sank. About three months afterwards they raised her again, but her top separated from her bottom. They, however, saved all her valuable cargo of brandy. There were eight principal and thirty-eight subordinate salvors. The award included £2,300 for expenses, and salvors were to pay out of the award £850 to forty other men who had picked up and brought in some goods. The Jubilee, 3 Hagg. 43, in note. The screw steamer 8., of 1,500 tons, from Alexandria, with cotton, for Liverpool, in a very heavy sea, wind W.N.W., grounded in November on the Libra Plats, at the entrance of the Mersey, and hoisted signals for assistance. The steam-tugs U. K. and H. succeeded in getting her off. Soon afterwards she touched on the Little Burbo Plat. The steam-tug E. then came up, and the three tugs got her into the channel. The steam-tug Belief then came up, and, with her assist- ance, the S. was prevented from getting on Taylor's Bank. The four tugs then towed the 8. up the Mersey and docked her. The 8. was in considerable danger, and the tugs suffered some damage, and several of their hawsers were destroyed. £3,000 to the U. K., £2,800 to the E., £2,500 to the H., £2,000 to the Belief.
