In December, the steamer A. had her machinery disabled in the Bay of Biscay, and was sighted under sail by the steamer Alfonso, which, in answer to signals made after she had passed the A., turned back and agreed to tow the A. to Corunna. The wind was strong and squally, with a heavy cross swell from N.E. and N.W., and the A. was rolling heavily. Two hawsers were made fast, and the wind being S.S.E., both vessels laboured and rolled heavily, shipping large quantities of water. After twenty-four hours towage, during which the hawser parted once, nearly fouling the Alfonso's propeller, the A. was safely anchored in Corunna harbour. The Angelica, S. G. April 17, 1885, p. 250. V. 7,000/.— A. ,£550 525. In December, the barque C, 328 tons register, with petroleum oil, from New York for London, brought up by her port anchor and sixty fathoms of chain in Margate Boads. Next day, the wind having increased, she let go her starboard anchor with forty-five fathoms of chain. The gale increased so much, that she was forced to slip her anchors, and was nearing the Long Nose Bock when the tug A. came up, towed her to the Downs, and held her there ; whilst the master of the barque went in a lugger to Eamsgate to fetch anchors and chains. During his absence the barque's pilot deemed it better to take her into Dover, and with the aid of the steam-tug P., the tug A. took her in there ; and then returned and towed the lugger which had been to Bamsgate to Dover Harbour. In December, the steamer G-., about eight miles from St. Agnes Lighthouse, Scilly, was discovered by the steamer C, from Cardiff to Constantinople, entirely disabled, the tubes of her condenser being choked. Two coir ropes were made fast, and the G. was towed, steering very wildly and sheering in an increasing gale and sea, to off Falmouth Harbour, where two tugs were engaged at the cost of £25 to take her into the harbour. The C. incurred expenses by detention, and otherwise to the amount of £9 12s. The Gainford, M. M. B. March 9, 1883, p. 302. V. 7,663/.— A. ^550 527. In November, the barque M. in the North Sea, in very bad weather, was found in a sinking condition by the smack F. W., value £900, which took the crew of the M. on board. The F. W. lay by her till night, when the E. E. and the Mermaid fishing smacks came up and stood by. Next morning two or three hands boarded the M. and navigated her as far as Lowestoft, from whence the tug C. towed her to Harwich. The pumps had to be kept going all the time, and the services involved much risk. £500 to the smacks, £50 to the tug. The Marequita, M. M. B. March 22, 1883, p. 365.
