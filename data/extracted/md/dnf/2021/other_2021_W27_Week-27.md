Buying interest picked up again in the second-hand bulkcarrier 
market with 24 deals closed the past week. Attention remains 
focused on small-size segments, although with some interest again 
for Panamax and Capesize ships.
The small-class vessels sold were a total of 17 with 2 Handysize, 
10 Supramax, 4 Ultramax and one Handymax while there were 3 
sales recorded for Panamax vessels, 2 Capesizes, and one for Post 
Panamax and Kamsarmax ships. As to the age range, the most 
demanded was again 5-10 years old, with 12 transactions. 
However, three S&P sales were reported for ships within the 15-
20 years range.
Activity in the S&P market rebounded after two weeks of rather 
low attention. The need for small size tonnage continues to lead 
the market, however strong and consistent Panamax rates 
continutes to attract investors into that segment. Similarly, 
although not so consistently, Capesize's high freight rate levels 
continue to spark some interest in second-hand ships.
Sale of the week: The Greek company Seanergy Maritime Corp. 
sold the 2001-built LEADERSHIP to buyers from the Far East for 
US$11 M. LEADERSHIP is a capesize vessel sailing under the 
flag of Bahamas. Built by Japanese Imabari Shipbuilding Co., Ltd. 
the vessel has a carrying capacity of 171,199 DWT. Her length 
overall (LOA) is 288.93 meters, and her width is 45 meters.

The past week has seen interesting deals come to completion with 2 vessels with a combined 
LDT of 26, 267LDT sold to ship breakers. 25 years old, Oshima Shipbuiding Ltd built bulker 
MV K. Phoenix was sold to unnamed buyers at USD540/LDT. The vessel which is currently on 
voyage to Korea from Gladstone will be delivered to shipbreaker later this month. Likewise, 
MV Karunia also built in Japan by Marugame yard was sold for USD585/LDT to shipbreakers 
in Bangladesh. The 28 year old ship had been owned and operated by Indonesia’s Jaya Samudra 
Karunia Shipping. 
Scrap prices have continued an upward trajectory even supported by volumes of tonnage on 
offer and firm steel demand. Bangladesh prices were up 0.7% to USD573 while fierce rivals 
Pakistan barely edged out Chattogram buyers on the price front with a 0.5% increase in LDT 
rates from last week to USD574/LDT.
The price rally in the demolition market has masked the poor performance of the market 
segment in the past quarter. Cash buyers would continue to offer higher prices in hope they 
could lure shipowners to part with their vessels which in fairness does no t look likely with a 
burgeoning profit been coveted by owners.

Chinese shipyard Chengxi has bounced back from the recent lows 
seen in the Chinese shipbuilding market with CSS announcing
it had met the years target. There were three newbuildings contracts 
reported this week all involving Chengix shipyard. The yard has

IRON ORE
WESTERN AUSTRALIA’S JUNE IRON ORE FINISH ON A HIGH DESPITE DISRUPTIONS
A strong end to BHP's financial year underpinned a 5pc above-average export performance by the four largest producers in the Pilbara 
region of Western Australia (WA) in the week to 3 July, despite ongoing underperformance at Rio Tinto's 45mn t/yr East Intercourse 
Island (EII) berths. The four largest WA producers — Rio Tinto, BHP, Fortescue Metals and Roy Hill — loaded vessels with a 
combined 17.97mn deadweight tonnes (dwt) of capacity in the latest week, which was 5pc above average. Shipments were in line with 
the previous week, with gains by BHP and Rio Tinto offset by an easing in exports by Fortescue Metals. Rio Tinto loaded vessels with 
6.76mn dwt capacity in the week to 3 July, up from 6.05mn dwt the previous week and the highest level since mid-May when it closed 
its EII berths for maintenance.
Rio restarted loading at EII on 26 June with a vessel departing on 28 June. Only four vessels have departed EII since the maintenance, 
implying that the berth is still operating below-usual rates. Rio's total shipments were 2pc above its annual rolling average of 6.62mn 
dwt/week, as it used the latent capacity in its rail and port systems to push up exports out of its other berths at Dampier and through 
Cape Lambert. BHP shipped 12pc above average in the most recent week at 6.52mn dwt, up from 6.48mn dwt the previous week, as it 
pushed production ahead of the end of its financial year on 30 June. Initial estimates suggest that the firm shipped around 285mn t in 
2020-21, which is at the top of its guidance of 276mn-286mn t. Fortescue's shipments eased in the week to 3 July, after a strong run in 
May-June, to 3.3mn dwt from 3.96mn dwt the previous week and 7pc below its weekly average of 3.55mn dwt. Fortescue exported 
around 47.9mn t in April-June, according to initial estimates, which would beat the record quarter of 47.3mn t achieved a year earlier 
and put it on track to export around 181mn t in the year to 30 June, near the top of its guidance of 178mn-182mn t.
Roy Hill shipped 1.39mn dwt, down from 1.42mn dwt in the week to 26 June and 22pc above its rolling average of 1.15mn dwt. The 
deadweight tonnage is the maximum capacity of the vessel and overestimates actual shipments by around 5pc. China was listed as the 
destination for 76pc of shipments in the latest week, up from 75pc a week earlier. After including shipments with unconfirmed 
destinations — most of which are probably headed to China — the percentage was 80pc, in line with a week earlier and below the 
average of around 82pc. Argus assessed the ICX iron ore price at $220.35/dmt cfr Qingdao on a 62pc Fe basis yesterday, up from 
$209.30/dmt on 1 June but down from a high of $235.55/dmt on 12 May. Yesterday's assessment increased from $167.45/t on 1 April 
and $159.90/t on 31 December.
Source: Argus Media
BRAZIL SECOND QUARTER IRON EXPORTS UP 13.6% Y-O-Y
Brazilian Iron exports are on a strong run data show. Higher shipments to Europe and the Middle East drove a recovery of Brazil's iron 
ore exports in the second quarter compared to a year earlier, when demand in both regions was heavily affected by the Covid-19 
pandemic, while shipments to China and Japan also rose. Brazil's second-quarter exports rose by 13.6pc on the year to 85.84mn t, 
Brazilian customs data show, after shipments to Europe almost doubled compared to a year earlier to 9.21mn t as the region recovered 
from the impact of the Covid-19 pandemic, while June shipments to Europe rose by 34pc on the month to 3.58mn t. Second-quarter 
shipments to the Middle East increased by 70pc on an annual basis to 6.68mn t, while June shipments rose by 20pc on a monthly basis 
to 2.42mn t. 
Second-quarter shipments to China inched up by 2.7pc to 55.6mn t, accounting for almost two thirds of Brazil's exports, while 
shipments to Malaysia, the second-largest single destination for Brazil's iron ore, fell by 25.4pc to 5.14mn t, with the country going 
into lockdown from 1 June. Second-quarter exports to Japan rose by 32.9pc to 3.27mn t with the country's recovery from the pandemic, 
but shipments slipped by 9pc on the month to 981,746t in June. Brazil's June exports grew by 26.9pc on a monthly basis to 33.68mn t, 
driven largely by a 42pc monthly rise in shipments to China to 23.38mn t, while Australia's shipments to China also rose in late June. 
But the expectation that Chinese steel mills will cut production in the second half of the year to meet Beijing's targets for lower 
production has since dampened buying interest.
Source: Argus Media

AUSTRALIA’S NEWCASTLE PORT QUEUE NEAR 10-YEAR HIGH
The vessel queue at Australia's largest coal export port of Newcastle in New South Wales blew out to near a 10-year high in June, as 
the port struggled to offset throughput disruptions caused by the closure of a shiploader amid growing demand. The average queue at 
Newcastle was 42.8 in June, up from a year-to-date average of 22.2 for 2021 and an average of 11.2 in the same period of 2020. The 
average turnaround time at the port increased to 10.86 days in June from an average of 5.35 days for January-June and from 3.81 days 
in the same period last year, as congestion builds at the port. There were 40 vessels waiting as of today. The Port Waratah Coal 
Services (PWCS) terminals at Newcastle shipped 9.26mn t of coal in June, up from 8.41mn t in May and from 9.09mn t in June 2020, 
but down on the 9.76mn t shipped in June 2019 prior to trade disruptions caused by Covid-19 lockdowns. 
PWCS managed to boost shipments to 10.96mn t in December to compensate for [the closure of a shiploader at the neighbouring 
Newcastle Coal Infrastructure Group (NCIG) terminal] (NCIG) but has been unable to sustain this higher level of shipping. NCIG has 
shipped around 3.4mn t a month since its shiploader was damaged in November, which is around 1.25mn t/month less than it achieved 
on average in the eight months prior to the incident. The lengthy vessel queue is a combination of the lower throughput at the port and 
firmer demand from nations outside of China for Australian coal, which has helped drive up coal prices and seen the premium for high-
grade coal increase to record-high levels.
Sources: Argus Media
QINHUANGDAO COAL STOCKS DIP CHINA APPROVES COAL MINES FOR MONGOLIA
Coal inventories at two of China's key coal trans-shipment ports of Qinhuangdao and Caofeidian fell to multi-month lows as inbound 
deliveries fell steeply against a backdrop of rising offtake due to strong summer cooling demand. Stockpiles at Qinhuangdao were at 
4.05mn t yesterday, down from 4.68mn t a week earlier and the lowest since 23 April, according to data from coal industry association 
the CCTD. Caofeidian port had stocks of 2.98mn t of coal yesterday, down from 3.23mn t a week earlier and the lowest since 8 
February. The steep declines in stockpiles come at a time when China is facing a warmer-than-average summer that has bolstered 
demand for thermal coal to support air conditioning consumption. The power load in the coastal province of Zhejiang hit its highest so 
far this year on 5 July at 91.48mn kW on firm summer demand, according to the local utility.
Inbound deliveries at both ports fell sharply, suggesting difficulties with logistics or production. Inbound deliveries to Qinhuangdao in 
the week to 7 July were at 2.51mn t, down from 2.9mn t in the previous week and the lowest since the week that ended on 27 April 
2020, according to the CCTD. Caofeidian received 1.08mn t of coal in the week to yesterday, down from 1.23mn t a week earlier. 
China's mine safety authority the NMSA said earlier this week that it will not compromise on safety standards at coal mines, which 
could curb output. At least 30 open-cast coal mines in Inner Mongolia have received approval for operations to resume in a bid by the 
government to cool thermal coal prices. The overall capacity of these mines is unclear. But the onset of heavy summer rainfall that has 
been forecast by China's meteorological administration to be concentrated in northern China where Inner Mongolia and other key-
producing regions are situated may impede any significant production increase.
Argus last assessed the Chinese NAR 5,500 kcal/kg market at 962.92 yuan/t ($148.80/t) fob Qinhuangdao on 2 July, which is 
significantly above the government-set upper limit of Yn600/t. Offtake levels from both ports rose as more utilities grappled with 
rising electricity demand. Offtake levels at  Qinhuangdao port were at 3.14mn t in the week to 7 July, up from 2.86mn t a week earlier, 
according to CCTD data. Offtake at Caofeidian port was at 1.48mn t in the week to yesterday, up from 1.32mn t a week earlier. The 
vessel queue at Qinhuangdao port in the week to yesterday averaged 40.9/d, according to Argus calculations based on CCTD data, 
down from 48.9/d a week earlier.
Source: Argus Media

BRAZIL SOYBEAN EXPORTS TO HIT NEW RECORD AS CHINA’S DOMESTIC PRODUCTION DIPS
According to the USDA’s Global Agricultural Information Network (GAIN) report, Chinese domestic soybean production is estimated 
to drop one million tonnes to a total of 17.5 million tonnes. The report noted many producers are switching from soybean to corn based 
on China’s current near six-year high corn prices. A slight growth in feed demand has pushed China’s soybean imports up two million 
tonnes, to 102 million tonnes for the 2021-22 marketing year. 
China has imported over 64 million tonnes, up 6.2 million tonnes, or 10.6% from the past season in the first months of the marketing 
year. Meanwhile, The USDA report expects the Brazilian Soybean exports for the 2021-2022 marketing year to hit a record 94 million 
tonnes. Brazilian soybean exports have been strong demand, available supplies, and a favourable exchange rate.
Source: World Grain
BRAZIL CORN EXPORTS DOWN 70% AS POOR RAINFALL THREATENS OUTPUT  
Brazil lowered its corn crop outlook for a third month in a row, citing drier-than-normal weather. The 2020-21 corn harvest is on track 
to reach 93.4mn metric tonnes (t), down by 3pc from the 96.4mn estimated last month, the national supply company Conab said. The 
new estimate would also represent a decrease of 9pc over the 102.6mn t produced in the 2019-20 corn crop. The decrease is a 
consequence of unfavorable weather conditions, despite a 7pc increase in planted acreage over the previous season.
Conab estimated the winter corn crop at 66.9mn t, down from 69.9mn previously predicted, citing a 4pc decrease in expected 
productivity. The downgrade for the corn harvest is the main reason Brazil grains and oilseed production in the current season is now 
expected to reach 260.8mn t, down by 0.5pc from the 262.1mn t estimated last month. The new estimate would still be a record and 
represents an increase of 1.5pc over the 257mn t produced in the 2019-20 harvest.
Source: Argus Media
UKRAINE WHEAT CROP HARVEST TO HIT NEW RECORD IN 2021-2022  — AGRITEL
Ukraine's wheat exports quickened significantly in the week to 9 July, reflecting high stocks at the start of the season, and amid 
expectations of a record crop. Ukrainian exporters shipped 132,000t of wheat in the seven days to 9 July, taking exports since the start 
of the 2021-22 marketing year in July to 178,000t, agriculture ministry data show. At the same point last year, just 13,000t of wheat 
had been shipped. The increase follows higher wheat stocks at the start of the 2021-22 season — Ukraine only shipped 16.6mn t of the 
17.5mn t projected for export in 2020-21.
Ukraine is also expected to harvest a record wheat crop this year of 30.5mn t, up by 21pc on 2020-21, according to estimates from 
Argus agriculture consulting arm Agritel. As a result, Ukrainian wheat exports could exceed 20mn t this marketing season. Ukraine's 
corn exports rose to 314,000t during the week ending 2 July, bringing total exports this season to 333,000t. This is 106pc up on the 
year. There have been no barley exports this season, against 39,000t a year ago, despite the start of harvesting. Producers have now 
harvested more than 1mn t of new-crop barley, according to the agriculture ministry. Total grain exports from Ukraine this marketing 
year now stand at 518,000t, up from 216,000t at the same point last year.
Source: Argus Media
