Buying interest remains at rather low levels with only 14 
deals closed the past week. Attention continues to be 
focused on small-size segments, although with some 
interest for Panamax and Capesize ships.
The small-class vessels sold were a total of 10 with 6 
Handysize and 4 Supramax, while there were 4 sales 
recorded for Panamax vessels and 1 for a Capesize ship.
As to the age range, the most demanded was again 5-10 and 
10-15 years old, both with 6 transactions. However, a S&P 
sale was reported for a two ships within the 15-20 years 
range, both Panamax vessels.
Activity in the S&P market softened after Week 24 and has 
remained at the same level since then. The need for small-
size tonnage continues to lead the market, however strong 
and consistent Panamax rates have brought back attention 
to that segment. Meanwhile, transactions of Capesize 
vessels declined after another week of mildly softened 
rates.  
Sale of the week: The Chinese Minsheng Financial Leasing 
Co., Ltd. sold the 2010-built SPRINGBANK to Greek 
buyers for US$26.5 M. SPRINGBANK is a capesize vessel 
sailing under the flag of Liberia. Built by Chinese New 
Times Shipbuilding, the vessel has a carrying capacity of 
177,065 DWT. Her length overall (LOA) is 291.74 meters, 
and her width is 45.05 meters.

The month of July of begins with the sale of 37-year-old handy, Tabernacle Prince. The 
Ocean Shipmanagement owned vessel has been reported sold to Bangladesh recyclers 
this week for an undisclosed amount. The Japan built cement carrier has since arrived 
the beach at Chittagong. 
LDT rates are pushing new heights, Bangladesh prices were up 0.5% to USD569/LDT, 
Pakistan however offered the best rate this week at USD571/LDT. LDT rates have kept 
on rising after receding briefly last month. A dearth of tonnage despite firm steel 
demand has kept prices elevated. Nonetheless industrial activities are slowing down 
owing to stricter lockdown restrictions in the Indian subcontinent. 
Poor vaccine roll out in the subcontinent has led to the emergence of rapidly infectious 
new covid variant forcing lockdowns which has severely impacted shipbreakers. 
Although India has gone past the peak, Bangladesh is in the midst of a 1-week total 
lockdown as the country’s daily covid infection and death rates sky rocket with less 
than 10% of the population vaccinated.

IRON ORE
AUSTRALIA'S MAY IRON ORE EXPORTS HIT FIVE-MONTH HIGH
Australia's iron ore exports rose to a five-month high in May boosted by higher shipments to China and Japan. Australia's May 
shipments were the highest since the 77.63mn t exported in December, according to data from by the Australian Bureau of Statistics 
(ABS) and processed by GTT. Fines shipments marked the highest volume since the 59.2mn t shipped in December. The rise in fines 
were boosted by a 9.3pc gain in shipments to China to 45.52mn t from 41.66mn t in April but down by 5.9pc from the 48.4mn t 
shipped in May 2020. The higher fines shipments were partially offset by a 25pc slide in exports to South Korea, falling to 2.88mn t in 
May from 3.85mn t in April and relatively flat with the 2.9mn t shipped in May 2020.
Lump shipments increased by 12.2pc from April and relatively flat with a year earlier. Lump shipments to China rose by 17pc to 
12.65mn t in May from 10.8mn t in April and were 13.9pc lower at 14.69mn t in May 2020. Lump shipments to Japan rose by 3.9pc to 
2.39mn t from 2.3mn t in April and more than double from 1.17mn t in May 2020. The value of iron ore export receipts hit a record 
A$16.59bn ($12.87bn) in May, exceeding the previous record of A$14.12bn set in  arch and were up more than 18pc on the A$14bn 
received in April and up by 74pc from the A$9.54bn received in May 2020.
Export receipts were underpinned by values in May above the record average price received for a calendar year of $151/t in 2011. The 
average price received for fines was $160/t in May and $197.46/t for lump. This is based on a US-Australia exchange rate of $0.7759 
used by the ABS for May.
Brazil’s iron-ore exports reached 33.68-million tonnes in June, the highest volume in nine months, as major producers like Vale 
shipped more in response to higher international prices, official government data showed on Thursday. June exports were up 12.2% 
compared with the same month last year, the data showed. Compared to the previous month,June exports were up 26.3%. Commodities 
prices have been rising globally, and iron-ore prices have been bolstered by strong demand from China.

COAL
AUSTRALIAN THERMAL COAL EXPORTS AT 4-MONTH HIGH IN MAY
Australian thermal coal exports rose to a four-month high in May despite the absence of shipments to China for a fifth consecutive month. Exports to India refreshed 
a record high in successive months and exports to Vietnam, once an emerging growth market for Australia, declined by more than 40pc in January-May
from a year earlier, official data showed. Australian thermal coal exports were 11.3pc higher at 16.7mn t in May from 15mn t in April, which was revised lower from 
the original estimate of 15.24mn t and 4.2pc above the 16.02mn t in May 2020. 
May exports were the highest since the 16.73mn t shipped in January, according to May trade data from the Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) supplied through 
GTT. Shipments to India rose to a record 2.36mn t in May, inching past the previous record of 2.31mn t in April that was revised down from the original estimate of 
2.46mn t for April, and was well above the 75,000t shipped in May 2020, the ABS data showed.
Japan remains by far Australia's largest thermal coal customer despite exports falling to a 10-year low of 5.02mn t, the lowest level since the 4.51mn t shipped in 
May 2011. May 2011 was also the last time that monthly shipments to Japan stood under 5mn t. May 2021 volumes were 6.7pc below the 5.38mn t shipped in April 
and 1.21pc down from 5.08mn t exported in May 2020. Exports to Vietnam, which had been a growth market prior to the Covid-19 pandemic, have declined by 42pc 
in January-May to 3.4mn t from 5.94mn t a year earlier.
The Argus Newcastle 6,000 kcal/kg price averaged $97.26/t fob Newcastle in May, which was the highest monthly average price since January 2019 when it 
averaged $98.37/t and stood above the average of $91.32/t fob Newcastle in April and above $50.54/t in May 2020. Australia's average thermal coal export price was 
$73.07/t in April, up from $71.43/t in March and from $62.45/t in April 2020, according to the ABS. 
Source: Argus Media
CHINA'S QINHUANGDAO COAL STOCKS INCH UP ON DISRUPTIONS
Coal inventories at China's main coal transshipment port of Qinhuangdao edged up slightly from last week, as offtake levels fell sharply because of disruptions
to port activity caused by bad weather and the run-up to the 100th anniversary of the Chinese communist party. But many parts of south China are grappling
with "extreme humidity" starting from today, according to the country's meteorological administration, potentially boosting coal consumption for air-conditioning 
use. Coal stocks at the eight key coastal Chinese provinces can last only around 13-14 days, based on forecasts by market participants, potentially indicating a tightly 
supplied market ahead.
Coal stocks at Qinhuangdao were 4.68mn t yesterday, up slightly from 4.65mn t a week earlier, according to data from coal industry association the CCTD. The
increase was largely because offtake levels fell to 2.86mn t for the week to yesterday compared with 3.51mn t for the previous week. Inbound deliveries for
the week to yesterday were 2.9mn t, down from 3.07mn t for the previous week. Its vessel queue averaged 48.9/d for the week to yesterday, steady from 47.57/d the
previous week, according to Argus calculations based on CCTD data.
Coal logistics, production, as well as port activity in China were partially suspended because of the communist party anniversary. This comes after many coal mines 
were ordered to cease operations last week to minimise anymining-related accidents that could potentially overshadow the celebrations. These disruptions have 
compounded a supply shortfall in the short term. Prices of Chinese NAR 5,500 kcal/kg were assessed at 1,002.92 yuan/t ($154.91/t) fob Qinhuangdao on 25 June, the 
highest since Argus started assessing this market in 2009.
Coal production will be boosted after the celebrations that will result in a sharp drop in prices, China's main economic planning agency the NDRC said last weekend. 
But more China-based market participants are sceptical, pointing out that similar pledges have done little to significantly ease the current supply tightness. Electricity 
consumption in Yulin district, a key coal-producing part of Shaanxi province, is forecast to surge to a daily high of 90GWh from mid-July to early August, an 
increase of 65.14pc from a year earlier, according to the local branch of the State Grid Corporation of China. Such a surge in electricity consumption will likely 
reduce available,stocks of coal for other regions, potentially creating a domestic supply crunch.
Stocks at the other key coal transshipment port of Caofeidian were 3.55mn t on 29 June, down from 3.71mn t on 23 June, according to CCTD data. CCTD has not 
provided updated data for Caofeidian. Its offtake was 1.6mn t for the week to 29 June, down from 1.87mn t for the week to 23 June. Inbound deliveries were 1.43mn 
t for the week to 29 June, up from 1.39mn t for the week to 23 June.
Source:Argus Media

GRAINS
BRAZIL WHEAT PRODUCTION REBOUNDS AS ESTIMATED AT 6.25 MILLION TONNES
Wheat production in the 2020-21 marketing year rebounded compared to the previous year that faced adverse weather. The USDA maintains 
its wheat production estimate at 6.25 million tonnes. 
Despite lowering its estimate, the USDA expects Brazilian wheat production in the 2021-22 marketing year to reach a record high. The 
country is anticipated to see a wheat production decrease of 200,000 tonnes in the 2021-22 marketing year bringing the total to 6.85 million 
tonnes. Delayed planting and dry conditions are impacting the commodity’s production.
The USDA’s Brazilian wheat import forecast for the 2020-21 marketing year remains unchanged at 6.5 million tonnes as the devalued 
Brazilian real has made dollar-dominated imports more expensive. However, diminishing wheat stocks are expected to fuel wheat imports in 
the 2021-22 marketing year to 6.7 million tonnes. 
Source: World Grain
BRAZIL CORN EXPORTS DOWN 70% AS POOR RAINFALL THREATENS OUTPUT
Brazil's corn exports fell by 70pc in June from a year earlier amid delays in planting and dry weather. Exports declined to about 92,200 metric 
tonnes (t) in June from 312,200t in the same month a year earlier, according to data from the economy ministry released today. The low 
volumes highlight delays in planting of the seasonal second corn crop and lower output compared to 2020 as below-normal rainfall in key 
producing areas threatened output, partly offsetting a larger planted area for the second crop.
Harvest of the second crop has reached only 9.8pc, 6.3 pc less than same week last year according to data from the national supply company, 
Conab. Despite the year-on-year drop, June shipments are up about five times from exports of 13,919t in May. Brazil domestic corn output 
has been underwhelming with demand from the country’s animal feed makers up as they struggle to meet up production. Feed makers have 
had to import 191,000 tonnes of corn Argentina almost double the volume imported in 2020.
Source: Argus Media
SOYBEAN FUTURES UP AS STOCKS HIT 6-YEAR LOW
The CBOT soybean futures surged on Wednesday after the U.S. Department of Agriculture surprised traders with lower-than-expected 
plantings estimates and inventory data. The agency said soybean plantings were 87.555 million, compared to analysts’ expectations for 
88.955 million. The U.S. estimates fuelled global supply concerns as inventories are slim and growing areas in North and South America are 
grappling with unfavourable weather. “We needed higher acres to give us a buffer and it went the wrong way,” said Don Roose, president of 
Iowa-based brokerage U.S. Commodities.
Soybean futures were up 77 cents at $13.90 a bushel. The gains were a turnaround from losses across the markets before the USDA released 
its plantings and stocks data. “There is simply no margin of error for bad weather, and we do have bad weather,” said Arlan Suderman, chief 
commodities economist for broker StoneX. In its quarterly stocks report, the USDA reported Soybean stocks at a six-year low of 767 million. 
Analysts had predicted soybean stocks of 787 million. For soybean stocks, the United States is “already on the ventilator at 20 million 
bushels less than we thought,” Roose said. 
Source: Reuters
