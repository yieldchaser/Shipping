Another soft week for the secondhand bulkcarrier market
in terms of buying activity. With only 11 sales reported,
once again owners seem to have shied away from S&P
transactions. Still, there was some interest primarily
focused on small vessels.
The small-class vessels sold were a total 7 with 5 
Handysize, 1 Supramax and 1 Ultramax. On the other
hand, only one sale was recorded for a Panamax and a 
Kamsarmax and two transactions for Capesize ships. As to 
the age range, two out of the eleven transactions were for 
ships 10-15 years old, seven were 5-10 years old and one 
sale each for ships over 20 years and under five years old.
The interest in secondhand ships continues at rather low 
levels compared to what we witnessed earlier this year. 
Shipowners await the next development in the freight 
market while Capesize rates recover and Panamax's remain 
high. With one of the lowest count of sales of 2021 
recorded the past week, the smaller segments continue to 
lead sales as freight levels remain at high levels.
Sale of the week: The Korean company Bokwang Shipping 
sold the 2013-built Orient Glory to Greek buyers for US$15 
M. Orient Glory is a handysize vessel sailing under the flag 
of Korea. Built by Korean Samho Shipbuilding, the vessel 
has a carrying capacity of 32419 DWT. Her length overall 
(LOA) is 180.82 meters, and her width is 30 meters.

There were no trades involving dry bulk vessels in the demolition market in the past week. Market players 
have been cautious in bidding for tonnage with prices rising to record highs above USD600/LDT. Scrap 
buyers have been in constant worry of price increase with end buyers reluctant to bear more price increase 
with a high inventory in stock. All major markets increased prices this week. Bangladesh Scrap prices have 
rebounded this week by 1.0% to USD603/LDT after falling 0.5% last week. 
India prices were up 2.9% from USD577/LDT to USD594/LDT. Buyers in the country have put in a blitz of 
bids after being out of busineess during recent Covid spike. Chattogram buyers have been active in the 
market despite growing concerns over covid infections in the country. Economic activities are slowing 
returning after the lockdown was eased on pressure from business leaders.
Leading buyers in Gaddani offered limited price increase this week similar to their recent offerings. Pakistan 
prices were up 0.2% to USD587/LDT adding only USD1/LDT a sign that the market in Pakistan is reacting 
to new tax guidelines. Pakistan tax authorities have increased threshold for minimum value of steel products 
for tax purposes, leading to a hike of USD35/mt of steel plate. Demand remains firm but buyers are reluctant 
to pay more than necessary. Turkey remains at USD280 LDT for the umpteenth week, with buyers there 
favouring smaller vessels of other ship class.

IRON ORE
IRON FUTURES DOWN FOR THE SECOND CONSECUTIVE WEEK AS CISA PLEDGES REDUCED STEEL OUTPUT
China’s iron ore imports have fallen for the fourth consecutive month by volume, in the latest sign that Beijing’s restrictions on steel output are 
sapping demand for iron ore. China’s demand for iron ore is also being clouded by growing concerns about the impact of its worst COVID-19 
outbreak since early last year, inflationary pressures and a slowing manufacturing sector on its economic recovery. While analysts attributed earlier 
decreases to supply issues from Australia and Brazil, they said on Monday that the latest trade data indicated the Chinese government’s directive to 
lower steel production was starting to filter through to iron ore volumes.
China’s latest trade data, released at the weekend, revealed that iron imports by volume fell to 88.51 million tonnes in July compared with 89.41 
million the previous month. China’s iron ore imports have been falling since March, although some of those reductions were due to supply 
constraints. China imported 102.1 million tonnes of iron ore in March, 98.56 million tonnes in April, 89.79 million tonnes in May and 89.41 million 
tonnes in June. Analysts said the latest data was a sign that government policies designed to lower teel output to meet Xi Jinping’s carbon emissions 
targets were starting to have an impact. Lower demand was coming at a time when exports from Australia and Brazil were increasing. “Steel mills in 
the Shanxi region have been ordered to cut production by 50 per cent so as to cap steel production below last year’s record high with officials 
pledging to aggressively enforce restrictions,” Westpac analysts wrote. 
Shipments from Brazil, Australia’s only other significant iron ore competitor, were also expected to rise as the South American country seeks to 
normalise production lost to a series of disruptions, before and during the pandemic. Westpac economist Robert Rennie said China’s cuts to steel 
production were part of the story. “Demand in the first half was super strong, so you would expect some slowing when steel production is capped for 
the year. Scrap imports are rising and scrap use for steel production will rise too, and domestic iron ore production has been rising too. Multiple 
factors are at work here,” he said. Traders in China have warned officials will be reluctant to build iron ore stockpiles given signals from the 
government about slow demand and growing concerns about China’s slowing manufacturing sector and the potential impact of the latest COVID-19 
outbreak in China on the economy. 
The China Iron and Steel Association said in a statement this week mills that are more polluted or consume high energy should lower steel 
production. It also pledged to ensure that 2021 steel output will fall on an annual basis. Capacity utilisation rates of blast furnaces at 247 mills across 
China recovered slightly to 85.89% this week, data from Mysteel consultancy showed, but still well below 95.16% the same period a year earlier.
The most-traded iron ore futures on the Dalian Commodity Exchange DCIOcv1 slumped as much as 4.2% to 814 yuan ($125.66) per tonne on 
Friday, before closing 0.9% at 842 yuan per tonne. For the week, iron ore futures declined 8.2%. Spot prices of iron ore with 62% iron content for 
delivery to China SH-CCN-IRNOR62 slipped $2 to $166 per tonne on Thursday, according to SteelHome consultancy.
Source: Reuters, Financial Review

COAL
AUSTRALIA’S NEWCASTLE COAL EXPORT STUMBLES AS SHIP QUEUE GROWS
The ship queue outside Australia's largest coal export facility at Newcastle has passed 40 vessels again, as the Newcastle Coal Infrastructure (NCIG) 
terminal struggles to return to normal operations following the return of shiploader 2. The NCIG shiploader, which has been out of operation since 
November, was reinstated on 20 July, with the potential of adding 1.25mn t/month in export capacity at the port and helping to clear the extremely 
high ship queues. But shipping data show that no ships have been loaded at the K10 berth, which is loaded by this shiploader, since 25 November, 
and loadings from the other two berths operated by NCIG — K9 and K8 — have not ramped up.
 
The port of Newcastle shipped 14.48mn t of coal in July, up from 13.21mn t in June, but this was underpinned by a ramp-up in shipments from the 
Port Waratah Coal Services terminals at Newcastle, which shipped 10.57mn t in July compared with 9.26mn t in June. These figures imply that 
NCIG, which does not release shipping figures, shipped 3.91mn t in July, down from 3.95mn t in June and 4.47mn t in July 2020. 
NCIG loaded 11 vessels in the first 13 days of August compared with 16 in the first 13 days of July and 17 in the first 13 days of June, despite the 
new shiploader officially being reinstated. NCIG maintains that it has been operating at full capacity of 66mn t/y since the shiploader was reinstated 
on 20 July, but the data do not bear this out. The ship queue outside Newcastle has increased to 42 ships today from 36 on 4 August and
back above the near 10-year high of 40 seen on 6 July. Demurrage at the port is affecting costs of shippers using the port, with Chinese-owned 
Yancoal highlighting these expenses as a major factor in raising its cost guidance for 2021 to A$62-64/t ($45-47/t) from A$60-62/t previously.
 
Argus last assessed high-grade Australian thermal coal at $166.81/t fob Newcastle for NAR 6,000 kcal/kg on 13 August, up from $120.58/t on 4 
June and a low of $46.18/t on 4 September. It assessed lower-grade coal at $97.83/t fob Newcastle for NAR 5,500 kcal/kg on 13 August, up from 
$70.10/t on 4 June and $35.04/t on 4 September. The heat-adjusted premium on a NAR 6,000 basis for higher-grade thermal coal was at a record 
$60.09/t on 13 August, up from $50.52/t on 31 July, $44.11/t on 4 June and $8.65/t at the end of August last year.
Source: Argus Media
SOUTH KOREA COAL IMPORTS UP 25% Y-O-Y, INCREASES IMPORT OF AUSTRALIAN COAL
South Korean thermal coal imports spiked to an all-time high in July amid surging overall power demand and nuclear outages. South Korean thermal 
coal imports rose by 2.3mn t or 25pc on the year to more than 11.6mn t in July, according to customs data released today, narrowly surpassing the 
previous high recorded in September 2017. The surge in imports came amid a spike in coal-fired generation last month as seasonal power demand 
surged on hot weather while nuclear outages raised reliance on fossil fuels. Daily mean temperatures in Seoul averaged 28.4°C last month, making it 
the hottest July for at least 11 years, with daily peak power demand rising by 18pc on the year to a likely record-high average of 81.2GW. At the 
same time, nuclear availability fell by 1.7GW on the year to 16.2GW, compounding the country's reliance on fossil fuels to meet electricity demand. 
The 1.4GW Shin Kori 4 reactor was unavailable for most of the month because of an outage, while four other reactors were offline for the whole 
month for planned maintenance. Generation from coal and gas rose strongly on the year in July to meet the spike in demand, with South Korean 
LNG imports climbing by 71pc to more than 4mn t and outpacing the 25pc growth in seaborne coal receipts. Power-sector fossil fuel demand is set 
to remain firm this month as well. South Korea's official weather office sees a 40-60pc chance of above-average temperatures through the remainder 
of the month and only a 10-20pc chance of unseasonably cool conditions.
The rise in South Korean imports in July was driven solely by Australia, as receipts from other key origins all slipped on the year. The country 
received 3.9mn t more Australian coal than a year earlier and 6.3mn t in total, which was probably a record high and surpassed the previous peak of 
5.1mn t recorded in December 2015. The increase offset declines from Russia, Indonesia, and the Americas, which fell by a combined 1.4mn t on 
the year to 5mn t. In the January-July period, South Korean imports were up nearly 1mn t on the previous year at 55.9mn t, but still down from 
62.2mn t in 2019. Receipts from Australia were up by 8.6mn t, with Indonesian and Russian supply down by 3.8mn t and 1mn t, respectively, and 
Americas inflows were flat. The customs data pegged the average value of South Korean bituminous coal imports — 106mn t of the total receipt — 
at an average of $93.26/t, which was nearly 60pc higher than a year earlier. The value of South Korean LNG imports rose by around a third on the 
year to around $9.56/mn Btu.
Source: Argus Media

USDA FORECAST LOWER WORLD WHEAT SUPPLY; US EXPORTS UNCHANGED
The US Department of Agriculture lowered its forecasts for 2021-22 US and world wheat ending stocks. Wheat futures posted wide gains in response to the new 
projections that lowered US and world supplies more than markets had anticipated. The USDA forecast the US carryover of wheat on June 1, 2022, at 627 million 
bushels, down 38 million bushels from the July projection and down 217 million bushels, or 26%, from 844 million bushels in 2021. The USDA forecast US all-
wheat production in 2021 at 1.697 billion bushels, down 49 million bushels from the July projection and down 129 million bushels, or 7%, from 1.826 billion 
bushels in 2020. The market was caught off guard as the lower all-wheat production projection was because of reductions to the hard red winter and white wheat 
estimates. The forecast for hard red spring wheat production was unchanged from July. US wheat exports in 2021-22 were forecast unchanged from July at 875 
million bushels, which was down 117 million bushels, or 12%, from 844 million bushels in 2020-21.
 
The USDA projected 2021-22 world wheat ending stocks at 279.06 million tonnes, down 12.62 million tonnes from July and down 9.77 million tonnes, or 3%, from 
288.83 million tonnes in 2020-21. The lower world endings stocks forecast was tied to a 15.49-million-tonne reduction in the world wheat production projection for 
2021-22 to 776.91 million tonnes. Standing out in the 2021-22 world wheat balance sheet were lower production forecasts for the United States, Canada and Russia, 
which more than offset a higher forecast for Australia. The USDA forecast the 2021 Canadian crop at 24 million tonnes, down 7.5 million from the July projection. 
The Russian crop was forecast at 72.5 million tonnes, down 12.5 million tonnes from the July outlook. The Australian crop was forecast at 30 million tonnes, up 1.5 
million tonnes from July.
Source: World Grain
USDA FORECAST 4% INCREASE IN US CORN PRODUCTION 
The US Department of Agriculture forecasts corn production up 4% from 2020, however forecasts are below the average of pre-report trade expectations. The USDA 
forecast 2021 corn production at 14,750,368,000 bushels, up from 14,182,479,000 bushels in 2020 and the second highest on record after the 2016 crop, if realized. 
The average of trade predictions was 15.004 billion bushels. The 2021 corn forecast was based on a harvested area projection at 84.495 million acres, unchanged 
from the USDA’s June forecast but up 2% from 2020, and an average yield based on Aug. 1 conditions of 174.6 bushels per acre, up 1.5% from 172 bushels per acre 
in 2020 and the third highest on record. 
Meanwhile JBS SA, the world’s largest meat packer, imported 30 shiploads of corn from Argentina into Brazil due to a crop failure in Brazil,
Reuters reported. JBS said purchases from suppliers outside of Brazil represent 25% of the corn it is using as feed, with volumes greater than 1
million tonnes. Imports from Argentina were more competitive because of an excellent crop. It believes Brazil will export 15 million tonnes less of corn and import 
at least 4 million tonnes, Reuters said. The government said the country will export 29.5 million tonnes of corn and import 2.3 million tonnes. Weather-related 
problems with Brazil's second corn crop have led to companies exiting their contracts on washout clauses, threatening exports, Reuters said. Brazil's second corn 
crop represents between 70% and 75% of all output in a given year and is the country's biggest corn crop.
Source: Reuters, World Grain
USDA CUTS FORECAST OF SOYBEAN HARVEST TO 4.339 BILLION BUSHELS
The USDA revised down its outlook for domestic soybean harvest view by 1.5% on Thursday as dry soils in key western growing areas cut into the potential for a 
bumper harvest. The lower-than-expected production from world’s second largest soybean producer will enhance concerns of tight global crop supplies and food 
inflation, potentially raising prices of livestock feed, vegetable oils used for cooking and fuel, as well as bread and pasta.
 
The U.S. Agriculture Department’s latest crop forecasts were well below the average of analysts’ forecast production of 4.375 billion bushels with yields estimated at 
50.4 for soybeans. “We need to grow a large crop and we can’t have the yield adjusted down too far,” said Don Roose, president of U.S. Commodities in West Des 
Moines, Iowa. “With the rest of the world short, we might have to ration supplies. World grain supplies are just tight so we’re counting on North America to make up 
the difference.” USDA forecast soybean harvest at 4.339 billion bushels on an average yield of 50.0 bushels per acre. Although a month ago, the July report also 
predicted a soybean harvest of 4.405 billion bushels and an average yield of 50.8 worsening dry conditions have necessitated a lower forecast.
Source: Reuters
