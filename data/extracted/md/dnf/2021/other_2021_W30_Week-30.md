Another soft week for the secondhand bulkcarrier market 
in terms of buying activity. With only 10 sales reported, 
once again owners seem to have shied away from S&P 
transactions. Still, there was some interest primarily 
focused on small vessels.
The small-class vessels sold were a total 8 of  with 3
Handysize, 1 Supramax and 4 Ultramax. On the other 
hand, only one sale was recorded for a Kamsarmax and 
another one for a Post Panamax ship. As to the age 
range, three out of the ten transactions were for ships 10-
15 years old, four were 5-10 years old and three for ships 
0-5 years old.
After weeks of intense activity earlier this year, the 
interest in secondhand ships continues to fluctuate as 
shipowners await the next development in the freight 
market. With one of the lowest count of sales of 2021 
recorded the past week, the smaller segments continue to 
lead sales as freight levels remain at high levels. 
Sale of the week: The Japanese company Marubeni 
Corp sold the 2010-built Glorious Wind to Greek buyers 
for US$20 M. Glorious Wind is a capesize vessel sailing 
under the flag of Panama. Built by Japanese Mizushima 
Works and Shipyard the vessel has a carrying capacity of 
83,410 DWT. Her length overall (LOA) is 229 meters, 
and her width is 32.24 meters.

Scrap buyers reported to have bought 2 dry bulk carriers this week. The deals involved the 42  year old self 
discharging RIN sold to Pakistani shipbreakers for an undisclosed sum and has since been beached. India 
scrap buyers similarly closed a deal for the Saga Shipholding owned Saga Tide. The Saga Tide built in 1991 
by prominent Japanese shipbuilder, Oshima Shipbuilding was sold at a high market rate of USD598/LDT. 
Alang buyers have stormed to the forefront of the demolition market this week as India’s local demand for 
steel recovers with steel mills and the economy beginning to bounce back from the devastating impact of the 
Delta Variant in the country. India scrap buyers have been competing at a high level for tonnage this week 
pushing the average demolition prices up 5% to USD560/LDT. 
In Chattogram, buyers are waiting for the easing of lockdown and resumption of steel mill operations to 
ramp up their participation in market activities. Nonetheless, Bangladeshi buyers remain the market leaders 
offering the best values for LDT at USD600/LDT up from last week by 2.7%. 
Meanwhile sentiment is that Pakistan is underwhelming with no price increases in the past 2 weeks. 
Increasing Covid-19 infections across the country has led to the imposition of lockdown in Karachi the 
country’s commercial capital, with potential impact on Gaddani which is about 50km away.

Shipowners are plunging into the newbuild market 
for new orders or activating the optional 
agreements for previous orders as an elevated 
freight market ensures that the cash flow statement 
is strong, and thereby presenting an opportunity for 
shipowners to embark on extensive fleet renewal 
programmes. Newbuild orders in the past couple of 
weeks have been at a level not seen in recent times.

Kamsarmax with China’s Jiangsu Hantong with

Taiwan’s Wisdom marine for 2 Handysize in a deal 
worth $116 million. The vessels are scheduled to be 
delivered to the Taipei based shipowner from last

Marine has similarly replicated the orders from

IRON ORE
IRON ORE PRICES PLUNGE ON CHINA’S STEEL CURBS, INCREASING SUPPLY
Iron ore prices hit a snag this week falling 10% the worst week the commodity has suffered in 18 months. According to Australia’s 
CommSec, Iron prices fell $14.50 to $180.50 on the first day of August. The market is undergoing correction as Chinese steel makers 
scale back production while at the same time Iron ore supply is on the increase. The main Chinese domestic iron ore benchmark, the 
Dalian Commodity Exchange contract DCIOcv1, dropped around 10% in the week to July 23, the worst weekly performance since 
February last year. The contract ended the week at 1,126 yuan ($173.77) a tonne and has now slid about 17% from its record high in 
May.
China’s moves to curb the price of iron ore has begun to yield results and with China the destination for 70% seaborne iron ore trade. 
The Chinese has capped steel output at 2020 levels, and this has been pursued aggressively. Steel manufacturers in Jiangsu, Yunnan 
and Fujian and Tangshan have either been told to cap output or warned of repercussions for over producing. This has led to an expected 
decrease in volumes of crude steel produced in June, down from May’s 99.45 million tonnes to 93.88 million tonnes. Nonetheless, steel 
output is up 11.8% for 1H 2021 compared to 1H 2020 at 563.33 million tonnes. Analyst at Reuters believe for China’s steel production 
to match 2020 levels, output for the remainder of 2021 would not exceed 502 million tonnes, 11% below 1H2021. This is considered a 
stretch as China’s reputation for prioritising economic growth. Already China’s iron ore imports are on track for a strong July, with 
commodity analysts Kpler and Refinitiv both predicting an outcome well north of 100 million tonnes. Kpler expects China’s July 
imports to be around 107.4 million tonnes, the highest since October last year, while Refinitiv is even more bullish, saying monthly 
arrivals may stretch as high as 112.5 million tonnes, although this includes 15 vessels expected to arrive on July 31, meaning their 
cargoes may not make it into July’s customs data.
However, if China does successfully restrict steel production in the second half, it will likely occur just as supply is starting to recover 
to closer to potential, as the top three exporters – Australia, Brazil and South Africa – appear set to ship more.  Australia, the top 
shipper of the steel-making ingredient, is on track for a modest July export volume, according to Refinitiv, but this follows on June’s 
exports of 78.04 million tonnes, which was the highest since December. Many of the June-loading cargoes will have reached their 
destination ports in Asia in July.
Australia’s iron ore exports are likely to recover in coming weeks with the conclusion of maintenance at a major port in Western 
Australia state, and fewer weather-related disruptions, although planned maintenance at another port may limit volumes. However, 
number two exporter Brazil is slated for a stellar July, with Refinitiv estimating exports of 34.92 million tonnes, the most since August 
2020 and up strongly from June’s 30.95 million. Third-ranked exporter South Africa, which similar to Brazil has been battling ongoing 
waves of coronavirus outbreaks, is also on track for a strong July with exports around 8.1 million tonnes, which would be the highest 
since Refinitiv started tracking cargoes in January 2015,
and almost double June’s 4.4 million. 
Source: Reuters, CommSec

COAL
HAMPTON ROADS COAL EXPORTS REACH 15-MONTH HIGH
 
Coal exports from Hampton Roads,   Virginia, climbed in June to their highest level in more than a year, reflecting extended gains in 
thermal and metallurgical markets. Terminals handled 2.57mn short tons (2.33mn metric   tonnes) of coal last month, according to 
estimates from the Virginia Maritime Association. The last time export loadings were higher was in March 2020, just as global 
business activity started to slow in the early stages of the Covid-19 pandemic. 
 
Exports from Hampton Roads were about 2pc   higher than they had been in May and 66pc above June 2020 levels. But they still 
lagged June  2019 by roughly 200,000st. For the first half of 2021, terminals at the port handled an estimated 14mn st of coal. That is 
about 185,000st below January-June 2020, reflecting that shipments in   the first quarter of this year were still lagging year-earlier 
levels. Hampton Roads' coal exports started to top year-earlier 2020 levels in April. Market conditions remain supportive of continued   
year-on-year gains through the end of 2021. 
Steady demand and tight global supply   have pushed US thermal coal prices up to their highest levels since at least 2018. Metallurgical 
coal prices are also at multi-year highs. But eastern US coal producers are somewhat   constrained in how much they can participate in 
the spot seaborne market. Thermal coal producers are   reluctant to significantly ramp up output given uncertainty over longer-term 
demand. Metallurgical coal capacity also has been slow to respond to growth in demand, Arch Resources   executives said on an 
earnings conference call yesterday. And railroad capacity for spot shipments from mines to ports has been limited by increased demand 
and difficulties in hiring new crews. 
 
In June, all three Hampton Roads' coal   terminals handled more coal than they had a year earlier. Dominion Terminal Associates 
(DTA), which is co-owned by Arch Resources and Alpha Metallurgical Resources, loaded just over 1mn st of coal, compared with 
770,589st in June 2020. Norfolk Southern's Lamberts Point terminal loaded   837,462st of coal for export last month, compared with 
703,704st a year earlier. And loadings at Kinder Morgan's Pier IX increased nearly 10-fold, to 730,466st.

CHINA BOOST US WHEAT PURCHASE DESPITE REPORTS OF CHINESE BUMPER WHEAT 
HARVEST
US wheat sales ramped up in the seven days ending 22 July, supported by Chinese grains demand from both the food and animal feed 
sectors. The US sold 515,200t of wheat for the 2021-22 marketing year (June–May) in the latest reporting week, up by 9pc on the week 
and 46pc higher than the prior four-week average. The increase was primarily driven by rising purchases by China, with 128,900t of 
Chinese demand switched to the US from unknown sources, data from the US Department of Agriculture (USDA) show.
China reported bumper 2021-22 wheat production — the harvest of which took place last month — of 134.34mn t, up by 2.6mn t, or 
2pc, from a year earlier, according to the National Bureau of Statistics, but the country continues to actively purchase wheat from the 
global market. China is likely to have strong demand for high-quality wheat this season, after a rainstorm caused by Typhoon In-Fa last 
week affected wheat quality in the major producing regions of the country.

BRAZIL’S SOYBEAN PLANTING AREA TO INCREASE FOR THE 15TH YEAR
Brazil the world’s largest producer and exporter of Soybean is set to see crop acreage expand for the 15th consecutive year according 
to a report by Datagro. Brazilian farmers are poised to increase area planted by 4% to 40.57 million hectares (100.2 million acres) in 
the 2021/2022 cycle, Datagro said. 
Meanwhile, Chicago soybean futures slid for a second straight session on Monday, and corn ticked lower, pressured by rains in parts of 
the U.S. Midwest. “Soybean prices in the futures market are easing a little but people are still cautious,” said one Singapore-based 
grains trader. “In the physical market there is not much change. I think the market wants more evidence of improved crop output before 
further reduction in prices.” The most-active soybean contract on the Chicago Board Of Trade (CBOT) dipped 0.3% to $13.45-1/2 a 
bushel by 0312 GMT, after closing down 2.1% on Friday. Traders await direction from the U.S. Department of Agriculture’s weekly 
crop condition ratings on Monday, and from private estimates of U.S. soy yields, ahead of the USDA’s big August 12 crop supply 
/demand report.
Source: Reuters
