# Breakwave Bi-Weekly Dry Bulk Report - October 13, 2026

**Date:** October 13, 2026  
**Publisher:** Breakwave Advisors | **Category:** Market Insights  
**Original URL:** [https://www.breakwaveadvisors.com/insights/10132026db-reporthy2026jjh8](https://www.breakwaveadvisors.com/insights/10132026db-reporthy2026jjh8)

•**Capesize Spot Rates Hit Two-month Lows**– Although Capesize spot rates have**declined by approximately $20,000**from their early-September multi-year highs, they remain comfortably above the $30,000 threshold, representing a 40% yearover-year increase. This recent retreat likely stems from iron ore transportation costs hitting a decade-high percentage of the commodity's price, which has subsequently**stifled inquiry from smaller, cost-constrained miners.**However, elevated futures trading at a premium to spot rates (contango structure) indicate widespread market optimism that this downturn is temporary. Given persistent global operational inefficiencies that continue to constrain vessel supply,**further significant declines****remain unlikely**in the near term. While the market's underlying confidence does not suggest this could be the precursor to a more meaningful structural shift rather than a standard correction, a neutral, wait-and-see stance is recommended until fourth-quarter demand and**ongoing geopolitical developments**provide clearer direction while the elevated orderbook looms as a longer-term headwind in a market that is characterized by significant optimism given the record high freight levels across all shipping.

•**Iron Ore Exports out of West Africa Slowly Increase**– Although Brazil remains the dominant supplier of Atlantic iron ore, a gradual expansion of West African export volumes is steadily capturing regional market share. Propelled by the ongoing ramp-up of the Simandou project,**West Africa is projected to secure an approximate 15% share**of regional iron ore Capesize exports in the third quarter. While near-term shifts are driven by transitory factors, including adverse weather and margin compression among smaller Brazilian miners, the data indicates an underlying structural transformation. Although a comprehensive reallocation of market share will require several years to materialize, this development will**influence the Capesize market for the foreseeable future,**even though the definitive impact on tonne-mile demand remains minimal due to relative shipping distances.

•**Our Long-term View**– The last few years have been characterized by increased geopolitical uncertainty. Going forward, we expect such events to continue to affect global trade and have a meaningful impact on effective vessel supply. Combined with the potential for a multi-year cyclical rebound in China’s economic activity following the recent economic turmoil, dry bulk shipping should experience higher volatility on top of a secular tightness driven by stable bulk commodity demand and rather steady but elevated fleet growth.
