Fearnleys Dry Bulk Weekly, Sep 30, 2026

We made a model consisting of factors like the global ballast/laden vessel ratio, West of Singapore ballasters, and a couple of other AIS indicators. Below the charts are tables showing where the model sits currently. It is only at extremes that these models give very clear suggestions about the near-term outlook.

Since last week, the models have turned more constructive, primarily because of the drop in West of Singapore ballasters seen over the last month. Bearish signals remain live until the middle of October.

The total DWT of West of Singapore ballasters is currently at the same level as last year. That would have been a bullish indication over the last few months, but a trend shift might be starting to emerge demand-wise. Over the last two weeks, total volumes in the South Atlantic were lower than in the corresponding weeks last year. Both bauxite and iron ore volumes slowed down, primarily because freight levels rose so high that it impacted the profitability of several mining companies negatively. Several iron ore miners in Brazil have announced production/export cuts recently.

Through week 39, shipment volume growth was 3.8%. Over the last 10 weeks, volumes increased by 2.6% compared to the same period last year.  DWT Supply growth remains at 5.2%.

Our comment from last week regarding fundamentals still applies... lower volume growth will not matter until it suddenly matters:

The lower volumes are reflected in the ballast/laden vessel ratio shown below. This ratio has diverged from earnings over the last month and a half. If the market keeps rising even though volume/ton-time vs supply growth suggests it shouldn't, what causes it to rise? Fear of not getting hold of physical commodities certainly explains part of it. This phenomenon is much more common in the Tanker segment, where freight can get excessively bid for that reason.
