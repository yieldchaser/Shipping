## Capesize/Newcastlemax

Near-term indicators still looks toppish, but underlying fundamentals remain strong. 

**Top left chart:** Blast furnace output fell slightly last week, whereas steel mill profitability increased. The latter suggests output will be stable around current levels in the coming weeks. 
**Top right chart:** Shipment volumes were 7.1% higher than last year through week 17. Still strongly positive, but down from the peak earlier this year. Supply growth is at 1.5%. 
**Bottom left chart:** The South Atlantic tightness indicator remains in toppish territory. 
**Bottom right chart:** The laden/ballast ratio is at 1.27, suggesting the market is in toppish territory.

## Panamax/Kamsarmax

No significant change to near-term indicators since last week. Fundamentals are finely balanced, with shipment volume growth and fleet growth both around 4%. 

**Top left chart:** Shipment volumes were up 4.1% year on year through week 16. The growth rate continues to decline, and now remains just slightly above the supply growth rate of 4%. 
**Top right chart:** Last week, we suggested an eventual market dip could be followed by year-to-date highs for P5. As it turned out, the market kept rising, without any correction. The coal futures curve slipped back into contango over the last week, which indicates P5 might soon peak. 
**Bottom left chart:** The Pacific tightness indicator turned more bearish over the last week. 
**Bottom right chart:** The SATL tightness indicator remains at similar levels to last week, suggesting the market will be stable.

## Supramax/Ultramax

The below indicators suggest further upside is limited. Most likely, the S11TC index will drift sideways in the high teens. 
 
**Top left chart:** The three-month change in the price of copper suggests upward momentum will peak this month. 
**Top right chart:** The chart compares the ballaster/laden vessel ratio against the S11TC index. A low ballaster/laden vessel ratio reflects a tighter market balance. 
**Bottom left chart:** The Ultramax spread over the Handysize index is quite high, which usually means further upside is limited in the near-term. 
**Bottom right chart:** Shipment volumes were up 5.7% year on year through week 17. Supply growth is at 5.2%.

## Handysize

The market could rise a bit further, but there are signs that the Ultramax market is peaking, so the Handysize market might be close to reaching a plateau. 

**Top left chart:** The ratio between Rio-Recalada / Skaw-Passero (HS3) and SEA / Singapore-Japan (HS5) is usually a good predictor of tops and bottoms of the HS3 route. A higher ratio suggests a market top, and a lower ratio suggests a market bottom. 
**Top right chart:** Through week 17, Handysize shipment volumes were 4.4% higher than the same period in 2025. 
**Bottom left chart:** The correlation between the S11TC and HS7TC is over 90%. The correlation is even higher when the S11TC index is shifted forward a few days, which means that movements in the S11TC index often lead the movements of the HS7TC. 
**Bottom right chart:** The laden/ballast ratio is an accurate measure of underlying fundamentals, shown by the high correlation it has with to HS7TC index.
