## Capesize/Newcastlemax

In our previous report last week, we wrote the following:

*Going forward, we still think it looks like the market is going to rally. China's imported iron ore consumption lead, iron ore prices and steel prices suggest strength in the coming 2 to 3 months. Further, the number of laden vessels in the Atlantic heading to the Pacific has increased significantly the last weeks, to levels comparable to early January and early September last year. 
Looking a bit further ahead, the recent drop in iron ore and steel prices suggest the market could be in for a negative surprise from late August through September.*

Indicators are telling the same story this week, as shown in the charts below. The most likely outcome seems to be a spike in July, then a downtrend through August and September.

## Panamax/Kamsarmax

In our previous report last week, we wrote the following:

*It looks like a rally is starting within the time period we have suggested. The South Atlantic vessel absorption has been significant, which almost always results in a rally. Within the next 2 or 3 weeks, more grain shipments from the Black Sea can be expected, which might also coincide with increased coal demand in Asia.
The market movement is tracking seasonality very closely this year. If this continues, the BPI5TC could be around 19.000 USD pd in a month or so.*

No change to the outlook this week:

- The top left chart shows the seasonality of Asian coal shipments, which points to higher volumes the coming 1-2 months. 
- The Black Sea grain volumes should start increasing within the next week or two. 
- Like in the Capesize segment, a significant share of the fleet is laden in the Atlantic, and headed towards the Pacific. 
- So, we expect the upturn to continue in the coming weeks.

## Supramax/Ultramax

In our previous report last week, we wrote the following:

*The May manufacturing PMIs for the Eurozone have now been released, and all reports pointed clearly to a demand recovery brewing. 
Otherwise, AIS data shows a further uptick in vessels heading to or in the South Atlantic. Like for the Panamax/Kamsarmax segment, some support is likely within 2 or 3 weeks from increased Black Sea grains shipments.*

To the positive readings from the Manufacturing PMIs, we can now add the recently released supply chain index from S&P global:

"Globally, reports of backlogs increasing because of staff shortages at suppliers of critical goods and inputs hit 
their highest in almost a year-and-a-half in May, suggesting capacity expansion is required to meet existing and 
future orders. Overall, this paints an optimistic picture for the outlook in H2 2024 for global supply chains."

The above, coupled with surging container market rates, could mean there will be some spillover from the container segment to the Handy/Supra segments. 
The BSI10TC have averaged close to 14000 USD per day so far this year. We expect a higher earnings average during the second half of the year.
