## Market Commentary

Negative sentiment dominates Handysize market all across the Atlantic despite a new grain season starting in Europe

After a noticeable growth last week, the North Atlantic market is now seeing a trade slowdown in Supramax and Handysize tonnage groups. “We definitely see fewer fresh cargo offers for Supras/ Ultras with July dates,” a local broker commented. For instance, rates for shipment of 50,000 t of soybeans or corn from New Orleans to China have stabilized at high $20s/t given 10000x/8000x l/d rates. At the same time, the Handysize tonnage list is rapidly increasing and charterers even manage to lower rates discussing Inter-Caribs and Intra-Gulf shipments.

Meanwhile, Supramax/Ultramax owners working in the South Atlantic manage to raise rates thanks to ongoing increase in cargo offer for shipments from both Brazil and Argentina, as well as from West Africa. Relevant freight levels have risen by $1k daily and $1-2/t on all routes. Meanwhile Handysize rates hold steady despite bullish owners' stance. The fact is that there is a relative cargo/tonnage balance for spot dates in the region, which is perfectly felt by charterers. Transportation of 25-30,000 t of wheat from Upriver to Algeria still costs low $40s/t.

The Baltic & Continent Supramax market looks firmer this week due to a slight increase in steel scrap shipments. Freight rates have even inched up by $1k daily and $0.5-1/t over the week. At the same time, the Handysize market picture remains depressive both in the Continent and in the neighboring Mediterranean basin: acute shortage of cargoes increasingly leads

to long idle time of vessels waiting for offers. The upcoming start of a new grain season has had no impact on the market so far; anyways, spot and 1H July offers are very few. Handysize rates are under strong pressure both in the Black Sea and the Continent, and owners are struggling to keep freights at previous levels.

Supramax/Ultramax owners manage to push rates further up in the Asia-Pacific region due to brisk shipments of Indonesian coal to China, as well as an increase in offer of steel for shipments from the Far East on back-haul routes (towards Med and W.Afr, so to speak). This week, rates have added $1-2k daily and $0.5-1.5/t. As for the Handysize/Handymax segment, exports of steel generally look quite decent, but shortage of other cargo offers ex SE Asia for 1H July laycans does not allow ship owners to raise freight levels. Players also note growing vessel count for 1H July dates.

The Indian Ocean market is still showing quite a large bid/offer gap as owners prefer to steer clear of PG ports. According to ISM, however, average fixing levels are steady. The market is supported by regular exports of South African cargoes, as well as by a limited tonnage list off ECI, with Indonesian coal exports pulling some tonnage. Meanwhile, the number of vessels open off PG/WCI has grown considerably.

### Supramax/Ultramax freight rates and dynamics on major routes

In the North Atlantic, the market cooling off may also lead to a slight rollback in freight rates as the regional tonnage list starts to overtake the cargo offer again. “The flat nature of the market is bound to shift. Remains to be seen if we get more cargoes released next week,” a broker mentioned. Meanwhile, the sentiment looks optimistic in the southern part of the basin, and ship owners have all chances to insist on further rate growth next week. “Market is getting stronger, saw charterers bidding DOP WAfr or Continent for T/A trips ex Brazil,” a broker mentioned.

The Mediterranean market should also see rising activity in the coming weeks, as the start of the new grain season certainly cannot go unnoticed. Gradually increasing barley and wheat exports from Ukraine, Russia and EU ports should help owners to raise rates by at least $2-3k daily and $2-3/t during July. In North Europe, Supramax rates are likely to stabilize at current levels, as there are frankly few preconditions for further activity upturn.

Meanwhile, Handysize/Handymax rates may decrease in APAC next week due to an uptrend on the tonnage side in SE Asia and lack of preconditions for trade recovery. “I guess the market will calm down next week,” a broker adds.
