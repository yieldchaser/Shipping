## Market Commentary

Sentiment starts worsening on Continent and Mediterranean Handysize markets, while USG area still dominated by uncertainty

Sentiment starts worsening on Continent and Mediterranean Handysize markets, while USG area still dominated by uncertainty

Last week's trends persist in the Atlantic Ocean. Thus, the ECSA Supramax/Ultramax market keeps firming up on transatlantic and front-haul routes due to an upturn in grain shipments, while the WAfr segment remains weak, especially for T/A trades. Rates for shipments from ECSA have added $0.5k daily and $0.5-1/t this week. Thus, rates for shipment of 50,000 t of corn or soybeans from Santos to N.China have inched up to $37-38/t. Meanwhile, the regional Handy sector has seen an inflow of ballasters from USG and USEC, which triggered a decline in rates by $0.5k daily and $0.5-1/t.

In the meantime, uncertainty persists in the USG . Decision on potential extra costs for calling US ports by Chinese-built and Chinese-owned vessels is still under discussion and there is no clarity on the market picture looking forward. At the moment, freight levels are stable amid the outflow of tonnage from the North Atlantic, a slowdown of ballaster inflow and growing number of cargo requests ex non-US ports.

The Handysize/Supramax shipping market has slowed down slightly in the Baltic & Continent after a surge of activity last week. The vessel count for April dates is gradually increasing, while support from the cargo side is moderate these days. “Very confusing market, with owners still asking high numbers,

but charterers not agreeing the same,” a broker commented. Indicative freights stay at previous levels. For instance, transportation of 25,000 t of steel scrap (sf 55-58`) from ARAG to Turkey still costs $29- 30/t bss 1/1 given 6000x/5000c l/d rates.

Ship owners working in the Black and Mediterranean Seas have suffered a major frustration this week. Encouraging news about Turkish lifting of duties on wheat and on 1 million t of corn did not lead to an actual increase in freight market activity. The surplus of open vessels remains quite noticeable, which allows charterers to keep rates unchanged. Rates for transportation of 25-30,000 t of wheat or corn from POC to Iskenderun or Alexandria are still hovering at $13.5-14.5/t, while those from CVB to Algeria amount to $18-18.5/t.

In the Asia-Pacific region , charterers have managed to stop the increase in Supramax/Ultramax rates thanks to a slowdown in exports of Far Eastern steel and Indonesian coal. Meanwhile, shipments of nickel ore from the Philippines remain stable. Handysize/ Handymax rates are also flat, while the offer of main cargoes ex Australia and SE Asian countries stays limited.

Ship owners working in the Indian Ocean have managed to raise rates by $1-2k daily and $1-2/t due to some upturn in shipments of main cargoes from PG, as well as thanks to brisker traffic of salt and ore

Next week, the North Atlantic market picture will largely depend on the US decision on introduction of port fees for Chinese-built ships. At the moment, market players refrain from discussing forward deals, which slows down the overall pace of trade and gradually puts pressure on the spot market. At the same time, owners working in the southern part of the basin will most likely have hard times standing their ground considering the soft WAfr market and the inflow of ballasters from distressed USG.

The Baltic & Continent market is likely to sag further next week. Demand for steel scrap has weakened in Turkey, while exports of grain and fertilizers remain moderate. “The number of Handies is slowly increasing; owners are still trying to push the market, but I think we have reached the break point and it will start to soften next week,” a European operator shared his expectations. In the Black Sea, most owners still expect at least a slight increase in grain shipments from Ukraine and Russia, with the recent upturn in export prices by $4-7/t giving hope for a rise in freight rates.

“APAC Handysize and Supramax/Ultramax sectors may cool down,” an Asian-based broker expects. “Market may be flattish in the Indian Ocean next week as the majority of cargoes stand covered and voyage bids are still not as good as TCT levels,” another broker suggests.
