## Market Commentary

Charterers successfully keep coaster freights on bottom despite slightly shorter tonnage list and higher bunker prices

The situation remains discouraging for small- tonnage owners even after the end of holidays in Muslim countries. Despite an almost 10% decline in spot tonnage list seen in the Black and Mediterranean Seas during the week, owners have failed to benefit from it given the total shortage of firm cargo offers. “Seems like owners try at least to repeat their last done levels, not dreaming of something better so far,” a Ukrainian broker commented. Freight rates have not changed much in both regions, staying at the bottom. Thus, shipment of 5-7,000 t of corn or soybeans from Ukraine to Egypt still costs $21-22/t, while rates for transportation of 7-8,000 t of fertilizers or coal from Novorossiysk to Iskenderun are hovering at low-mid $20s/t. Note that the approach of the new grain season has no impact even on July rates (at least, charterers are not ready to pay above spot levels).

Sea-river owners working ex Azov Sea ports are witnessing an even worse market picture. Local grain and coal offers remain literally sporadic, and TCEs are much farther from OPEX levels compared to the sea- going sector. Although deals for shipments of new crop grains in 1H July are increasingly being discussed, corresponding rates are still just $1-2/t higher than spot levels. For instance, spot shipment of 3-5,000 t of grains (sf up to 54`) from Rostov/Azov to Marmara costs $17-18/t, while transportation with 1H July dates is discussed at around $19/t.

Ship owners working in North Europe are still able to get the highest TCEs, but fundamentally the situation is almost the same as in the neighboring Mediterranean basin. The only difference is that the number of open vessels is generally unchanged in the area this week (tonnage list has dropped slightly in the Baltic Sea and equally increased in the Continent). Freight rates hold steady.

Note that the market situation is additionally aggravated by the emerged uptrend in bunker prices.

There are still no signs of market improvement in the next couple of weeks. Despite available stocks of old crop grains, traders do not hurry to sell them amid low export prices. As for Ukraine's new crop, it should be on par with the previous one in terms of volumes, but there may be problems with exports, as the EU has imposed quotas on Ukrainian wheat. There is still weak demand for square billets in Turkey and finished steel products in MENA and the EU. North African fertilizer exports may pick up slightly, but this will hardly have any impact on freights in the region given huge tonnage surplus.
