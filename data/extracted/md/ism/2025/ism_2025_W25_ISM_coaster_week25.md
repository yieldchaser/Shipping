## Market Commentary

### Increased offer of Ukrainian grain failed to lift coaster rates from bottom

The small-tonnage shipping market finally sees timid signs of upcoming improvement. Market participants unanimously report a gradual recovery of grain shipments from Ukraine, Russia and EU ports with late June-early July laycans. However, there are still plenty of open vessels (the number of 6-12k dwt ships open in the Black and Marmara Seas has even inched up over the week), which does not allow owners to raise rates from the bottom in the region yet. For instance, transportation of 6-7,000 t of soybeans or corn from POC to Iskenderun or Alexandria still costs $21-22/t. Only the rates for shipments from Azov Sea ports are an exception: corresponding levels have added $0.5-1/t this week. Thus, a couple of deals for shipment of 3-5,000 t of wheat from Azov/Rostov to Marmara with early July laycans are discussed at $18- 19/t. Meanwhile, freights are generally unchanged in the Mediterranean Sea, where cargo flow is still critically weak. Note that the military conflict between Israel and Iran is also making its own adjustments to the situation on the local market. Thus, Egypt has suspended fertilizer production due to disruptions in gas supplies, which is starting to affect production and exports (fertilizer supplies are one of the main market drivers in the region). Importers from the EU and Turkey are now forced to consider alternative suppliers, and not only from the Mediterranean. In terms of port infrastructure, Israeli ports have not stopped operations, but agents warn of possible delays due to the limited number of personnel.

In the current situation, TCEs keep sliding down given the continuing increase in bunker prices.

Meanwhile, depressive sentiment persists on the Baltic & Continent coaster market. “The market is low; there are some good niche cargoes, but any given cargo is paying low,” a European ship owner commented. The cargo/tonnage imbalance continues to sharpen. During the week, the number of open vessels has again increased by about 10%, reaching the maximum in the last 2 months. However, freight rates are holding steady for now. “Seems only growing bunker prices hold on rates decline,” a local broker mentioned.

Most brokers and owners expect the uptrend in grain exports from Ukraine, Russia and EU to continue in the next couple of weeks, which will help to raise rates by at least $2-3/t. According to ISM, the Mediterranean market can significantly hinder such development, as there are virtually no preconditions for brisker cargo flow in the area, while
