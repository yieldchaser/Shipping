## Market Commentary

The small-tonnage shipping market remains depressed, especially in the Black and Mediterranean Seas. The already difficult negotiations have only got more complicated amid growing bid/offer gap. The point is that ship owners are trying to raise rates at least slightly,

Acute shortage of firm grain offers keeps small-tonnage freights under pressure all across Europe

trying to convince - sometimes even themselves - in higher activity after holidays and trying to compensate for increased bunker prices, while grain traders cannot accept freights above December levels given low prices for wheat and corn in main importing countries. “Seems livelier cargo offer is just an illusion since the share of firm offers has even decreased,” a Ukrainian broker commented to ISM. “Most grain charterers circulate their cargoes with extremely low ideas or search for best offers from owners, otherwise they simply postpone shipments or exit the market,” another broker complains. There are plenty of open vessels in all parts of the Black and Mediterranean Seas, and some vessels have been idle for several days (often not because of complete lack of options, but because of an unsuitable freight rate, which seems rather strange to ISM, as an even more interesting rate will not cover the loss from several idle days). Over the week, rates for shipments from Ukraine have sagged by $0.5-1.5/t, while freights ex other Black Sea ports have dropped within $0.5/t. Thus, rates for shipment of 6,000 t of corn from Odesa or Izmail to Bari

have declined to $26-27/t, while transportation of 3-4,000 t of steel billets from Novorossiysk to Marmara already costs $17.5-18/t. In the Mediterranean Sea, owners struggle to keep rates at previous levels due in part to a slight upturn in fertilizer shipments from North African ports. Rates for shipment of 5-6,000 t of urea from Damietta to Ravenna are still hovering at mid-$20s/t.

Following a very short-term surge of shipments reported in the end of last week (upon the return of Russian exporters from Christmas holidays), demand for tonnage has now again stabilized in the Azov Sea. Freight rates stay virtually unchanged over the week, while current TCEs for short-haul trips allow to cover OPEX only in the best case, namely with minimal ballast leg and minimal downtime. In the Caspian Sea, rates ex Russian ports have literally collapsed ($7-8/t down) given the absence of grain sales to Iran. Tonnage demand in the region is supported only by relatively stable exports from Aktau.
