## Market Commentary

The Handysize and Supramax/Ultramax shipping markets have stabilized in all parts of the Atlantic Ocean after a several-week decline. Thus, some upturn in cargo traffic from ECSA for late August laycans, coupled with decreased vessel count in West Africa, allowed Supramax owners to keep rates stable. The Handysize segment is also flat due to lively grain shipments to WCSA. However, tonnage is building up for September laycans.

Ship owners working in USG are optimistic and are raising ideas, as the number of requests for late August dates has started to grow quite rapidly. Nevertheless, the regional tonnage list remains long, so owners are still unable to achieve an actual increase in freight levels.

For instance, transportation of up to 50,000 t of soybeans or corn from USG to N.China still costs low $50s/t, while rates for shipment of a similar lot from Brazil to N.China are hovering at very low $40s/t bss 1/1.

The Baltic & Continent Supramax/Ultramax market appears quite strong. Despite a slight decrease  in cargo offer during the week, still moderate tonnage list allows ship owners to confidently stand their ground. Note that charterers of cargoes ex Russian ports are often forced to compete for tonnage open in the Mediterranean Sea and accept rates noticeably higher than the market average (difficulties arise as to whether a vessel can be bunkered in Russian ports).

The introduction of additional diving inspections has also increased costs and aggravated the situation. The Handysize market looks noticeably softer than the larger tonnage group in the Continent and in the Black Sea. There is still a shortage of new cargoes in this segment, especially when it comes to firm grain offers, which are the only owners' hope considering the desperation of the metallurgical market. Freight rates have not changed much during the week, with only a barely noticeable growth reported on intermed routes ($0.25k daily and $0.25-0.5/t up). Thus, rates for shipment of 40-45,000 t of steel scrap (sf 55-58`) from ARAG to Turkiye have inched up to $23-24/t, while those for transportation of 25-30,000 t of wheat from Novorossiysk or Kavkaz roads to Alexandria have increased to $15-16/t.

In the Asia-Pacific region, Supramax/Ultramax owners gain confidence from increased B/H exports from SE Asian ports, some upturn in shipments of Indonesian coal and Philippine nickel ore, as well as decreased tonnage list in the northern part of the region. Nevertheless, corresponding rates have not increased significantly so far. No changes can be seen on the local Handysize/Handymax market either. “The market still appears to be calm; in particular, the number of steel orders ex China and S.Korea has declined,” a broker describes the situation.

### Supramax/Ultramax freight rates and dynamics on major routes

Despite some slight realignments in cargo volumes and tonnage across different sections of the region, the Indian Ocean Supramax/Ultramax market remains largely balanced. The number of open vessels has not changed much off PG/WCI, while exports of main cargoes have slowed down a bit. Note that the opposite picture is reported in South Africa: the vessel count has increased, while the offer of coal and other cargoes appears flat.

Rates for shipments from USG ports are likely to go up next week. New offers for late August laycans increasingly show up, and owners' sentiment stays positive looking into next week. Meanwhile, a gradual increase in the Handy tonnage list may, on the contrary, push freight levels down in the South Atlantic. At the same time, ECSA Supramax/Ultramax rates should remain stable, as the market is moving towards a relative cargo/tonnage balance.

Handysize owners will be trying to raise rates little by little in the Baltic & Continent as well as in  the Mediterranean Sea, counting on a further upturn in grain exports. Note that an increase in export prices for wheat and corn adds optimism in this regard.

The outlook for the Asia-Pacific Supramax/Ultramax market is fairly optimistic given the inflow of coal cargoes expected in 2H August. At the same time, the local Handysize/Handymax market demonstrates little-to-no preconditions for improvements, according to the market players.
