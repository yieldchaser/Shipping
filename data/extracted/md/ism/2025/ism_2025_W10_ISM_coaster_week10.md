## Market Commentary

Having improved slightly, the small-tonnage market sentiment has deteriorated again this week, partly due to the start of Ramadan and minimized activity in the Mediterranean Sea (primarily this applies to shipments of fertilizers and minerals from North African ports). In the Black Sea, ship owners

have not managed to build on their success either after a slight and sporadic increase in rates for shipments from Ukraine. Moreover, fixing has slowed down somewhat in this region. “Seems owners and charterers have different understanding of market direction, therefore we often see wider gaps in ideas,” a Ukrainian broker commented to ISM. Note that the tonnage side dynamics in both regions is also unfavorable for ship owners, as the vessel count has increased again after 3 weeks of gradual decline (from 236 to more than 260 ships of 2-12k dwt open from Gibraltar to the Kerch Strait); the most significant upturn is reported in the sector for 4-6k dwt fleet (almost 25%). “The only segment where we still see some shortage of spot vessels is the 3-4k dwt one in BlSea and Marmara,” another broker mentioned. At the

moment, shipment of 5-6,000 t of soybeans or corn from Izmail to the southern Adriatic costs high $20s/t, while transportation from Odesa or Chornomorsk to Mersin or Beirut costs $21-21.5/t. Rates for shipment of 5-6,000 t of urea from Damietta to SpanMed are still hovering at $24-25/t.

Meanwhile, the North European market is generally unhanged. Spot rates stay at levels pretty acceptable for owners in the Baltic Sea due to the relatively short vessel list: many ships are traditionally working with contract cargoes. The Continent market is still looking the most attractive for ship owners both in terms of cargo traffic and earnings.

Sea-river owners managed to raise rates in the Azov Sea by $1-2/t at the very end of last and start of this week thanks to difficult ice conditions and positional shortage of vessels due to delays. However, charterers are trying to push freights down again closer to the weekend.

There are still no serious preconditions for higher activity and freight rates on the coaster shipping market. Moreover, gradually improving weather conditions in the Azov and Baltic Seas will surely put pressure on freight rates. As for the main export markets, stabilization of grain prices and moderate demand in North Africa and Turkey

are not encouraging. Demand for finished steel products remains weak in Mediterranean countries, and so does urea demand in the EU.
