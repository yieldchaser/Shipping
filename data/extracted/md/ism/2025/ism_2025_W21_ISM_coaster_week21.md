## Market Commentary

### Сharterers' pressure on small-tonnage rates has even strengthened

The small-tonnage market never ceases to amaze players with negative developments. “When it seemed that the bottom had already been reached, we heard a knock from below,” a local broker laconically commented on the situation in the Azov and Black Seas. Despite the return of players from major industry events, activity has not perked up at all in the Black and Mediterranean Seas; moreover, some of the main cargo flows have even slowed down further (primarily this applies to Ukrainian and Russian grain). Note that owners working in the Mediterranean Sea struggle to keep rates stable due to a slight decrease in tonnage list, while rates have sagged by another $0.25- 0.5/t on most routes in the Black and Azov Seas. For instance, rates for shipment of 5-6,000 t of wheat from Constanta to the Italian Adriatic have inched down to $21-22/t. According to ISM estimates, the vessel count has grown by 25% in the Black Sea during the week, especially if we talk about 6-12k dwt ships. Similar to the Azov Sea, the Caspian Sea market is virtually dead.

The North European market is also softening, but it looks more explainable considering higher TCEs compared to the south. Lack of cargo offers is getting increasingly acute in the Baltic Sea, while the cargo offer looks slightly healthier in the Continent. Note, however, that the ratio is balanced by ballaster outflow from east to west seen during the second consecutive week. Ship owners are forced to make concessions: freight rates have dropped by another €1-3/t on all

key routes this week. Thus, transportation of 3-5,000 t of bulk fertilizers from Klaipeda to ARAG already costs low €20s/t.

As forecasted earlier, players should not count on any burst of activity in the Black Sea till the end of June. Only the start of the new grain season is likely to allow owners to raise rates slightly, while many of them are now literally focusing on survival.

ISM sources do not expect the situation to improve in North Europe either, as the local market is entering the summer lull and the pressure on rates will continue to grow.
