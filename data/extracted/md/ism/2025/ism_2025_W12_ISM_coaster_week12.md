## Market Commentary

Charterers successfully keep small-tonnage freights stable despite much stronger grain demand in Turkiye

The fact that Turkey issued the quota for imports of 1 million tons of corn with a reduced 5% import tax and lifted import taxes for wheat has undoubtedly become the main news - and for once a positive one - for the small-tonnage shipping market this week. However, no sudden impact of these decisions can be seen on the freight market. Grain offer has not increased in the Black Sea , while other cargoes still provide minimum support to the market. Over the week, only grain freight rates have risen within $0.25- 0.5/t in this region. Thus, rates for transportation of 5-7,000 t of corn or soybeans from Ukraine to Marmara have inched up to $18-19/t, while those towards the Italian Adriatic have increased to $27-28/t.

In the Azov Sea , some charterers have managed to achieve even slight rate cuts due to improving weather conditions and growing list of spot sea-river vessels. Shipment of 3,000 t of wheat or peas from Rostov to Marmara still costs $21-22/t.

The Mediterranean Sea market is still seeing slow cargo traffic. “Many owners in the Mediterranean try to ask for higher rates, but mostly because of better sentiment in the Continent and Black Sea, while the Mediterranean itself remains unexciting,” a Turkish broker commented to ISM. Note that despite stable freight rates, the estimated RV TCEs for coaster fleet are still growing slowly due to the continued decline in VLSFO prices.

Activity keeps slowly increasing in North Europe . “We see more cargoes amid upcoming Easter,” an EU broker mentioned. The vessel count has decreased by 10-15% in the region this week, and ship owners have managed to raise rates slightly in the North Sea (€0.5-1/t up).

The Azov and Black Sea markets should pick up next week as sales of Ukrainian corn and Russian wheat to Turkey will almost certainly gain pace. However, considering low export prices for grain, as well as weak metallurgical and coal markets, ship owners can hardly count on a significant rate growth. At the same time, the standoff between owners and charterers will be sharpening in the Mediterranean Sea in the coming weeks, as voyage costs of owners will significantly increase in the region starting May 1 as restrictions on sulfur content in marine fuel will come into force (in fact, it is about long-planned expansion of SECA zone and transition to MGO 0.1). In North Europe, owners will keep trying to strengthen their positions, which looks quite probable ahead of the Easter holidays (only rates ex freezing ports will be gradually sliding down with improvement of weather conditions).
