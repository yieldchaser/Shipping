## Market Commentary

Handy/Supra rates got frozen amid sluggish trade, especially in the USG and Continent/Med areas

The Supramax/Ultramax market activity looks sluggish in ECSA as charterers have taken a wait-and- see stance amid a gradually increasing tonnage list for late March laycans. “Charterers hope to see lower rates very soon,” a broker commented. Meanwhile, ship owners have managed to insist on a positional increase in rates for shipments from West Africa to Asia thanks to an increase in corresponding cargo traffic. Similar picture can be seen in the Handysize segment: pressure on freight levels is mounting amid increasing spot vessel count, while new requests for shipments on T/A routes rarely enter the market. Nevertheless, ship owners still manage to keep rates stable due in part to livelier shipments to WCSA. Thus, shipment of 25-30,000 t of wheat from Upriver to Algeria with 7000x/5000x l/d rates still costs $37-38/t, while transportation with 7000x/2500x l/d rates costs $40-41/t.

The USG market has stalled waiting for the US decision regarding port fees on Chinese-built ships calling US ports. If this measure is introduced, non Chinese-built ships will ask for a premium in case of shipments from US ports. Some ship owners have already included such probability when discussing deals with late March - early April laycans, while many prefer not to fix until the situation is clarified. Limited number of deals discussed and signed this week shows stable freight levels. Thus, ates for transportation of 45- 50,000 t of scrap (sf 52-55`) from USEC to Iskenderun are hovering at low $20s/t bss 1/1.

After some activity decline in the Continent and the Mediterranean Sea, players report no signs of recovery this week, and the market remains unexciting. In these circumstances, owners can hardly expect a resumption of positive rate dynamics, so they are focused on at least keeping the rates at last week's level.

“Market is firming up slowly,” a broker describes the situation in the Asia-Pacific Supramax/Ultramax sector. Relevant rates are showing just minor changes. Ship owners are benefiting from the recovery of Australian exports (grains and ore), as well as of Indonesian coal shipments to India (which cannot be said about exports of Indonesian coal to China).

No major rate shifts have been seen on the Handysize/Handymax market either. Among positive factors, however, ISM sources note an increased number of requests for transportation of Far Eastern steel. “Steel traffic is heating up, with rising orders for both Supramax and Handysize vessels,” a broker mentioned. Meanwhile, the cargo offer for Handysize shipments from Australia is looking limited at the moment, but competition among owners for said cargoes is not that intense either given the slim tonnage list in the area. For instance, the deal for shipment of 40,000 t of steels from China to Yemen bss 2/2 is discussed at a rate close to $40/t.

While iron ore traffic from ECI remains limited, owners prefer to take local vessels for shipments from Indonesia. Ore and salt traffic from WCI is quite stable, but exports of main cargoes from PG have slowed down slightly.

Next week, it will be problematic for ship owners to stand their ground in ECSA, as the regional tonnage list is so far growing faster than corresponding cargo side. If this trend continues, ship owners will be forced to make concessions. High level of uncertainty and nervousness in USG will surely be present next week. The proposal regarding Chinese-built ships will be accepted or rejected by the USG President only on March 24, and until then, trade is likely to be noticeably restrained in the region.

The situation is unlikely to change in North Europe and the Mediterranean basin in the coming weeks, as there are still no preconditions for brisker cargo traffic. The players' eyes are still fixed on the geopolitical situation and statements of the US administration. “Everybody is still in a waiting mood,” an EU broker commented. Demand for Black Sea grain remains healthy, but low prices are hampering trade greatly.

Players expect Supramax/Ultramax rates to gradually rise in the Asia-Pacific region, although sharp spikes are unlikely.
