## Market Commentary

The situation has improved noticeably on ECSA Supramax/Ultramax and Handysize markets. Ship owners have managed to raise transatlantic rates by $1-2k daily and $1-3/t thanks to an upturn in relevant cargo flow, coupled with strengthening of the SAfr market (vessels opened in WAfr are actively considering this region as an option for repositioning along with ECSA). Thus, rates for shipment of 25-30,000 t of wheat from Upriver to Algeria have increased to low $40s/t given 7000x/3000x l/d rates. At the same time, the front-haul segment remains stable due to limited demand for South American grains in Asia.

Meanwhile, the North Atlantic is seeing the rate decline on all routes. The recent clarification regarding Chinese-built and flagged vessels resulted in plenty of ships entering the market at one moment, while the US cargo offer has barely noticeably increased (if it has at all). Handysize rates have dropped most noticeably, by $1k daily and $1-2/t, on all routes. Supramax/Ultramax levels have decreased by $0.5-1k daily and $0.5-1/t. For instance, shipment of 25-30,000 t of wheat from New Orleans to ARAG now costs $22- 23/t (-$1/t over ther week)

Negative trends persist in North Europe and the Mediterranean area. Activity has again shrunk to a minimum in the second half of the week due to a major shipping conference in Geneva and the holiday weekend in European countries. “The market is slowly declining and it seems this will continue

next week as well; owners are reluctant to decrease freight rates and wait till the very last moment when eventually they take what is available on the market,” an EU operator commented to ISM. By the end of the week, freight levels have dropped within $0.5k daily and $0.5-1/t on some routes, with only voy-bss rates staying unchanged in the Mediterranean Sea, where all owners are forced to use gasoil or ULSFO since May 1. The Continent Supramax sector looks the most resilient these days amid a slight upturn in steel scrap shipments with May laycans. “Market appears still weak but not dropping anymore.” a local charterer mentioned.

In the Asia-Pacific region, last week's positive momentum allowed Supramax owners to raise rates by another $0.5-1k daily and $0.5-1/t. At the same time, coal exports from Indonesia to China have slowed down by the end of this week, as has back-haul cargo traffic from Indonesia and Australia. Speaking of the Handysize/Handymax segment, the offer of main cargoes has slightly decreased, while the tonnage list has got longer; nevertheless, relevant freight rates have not changed significantly over the week.

The cargo/tonnage ratio stays balanced in the Indian Ocean, with freight rates holding steady. The number of vessels open off PG/WCI has not changed much, while ships open off ECI are actively involved

in cabotage shipments and transportation to SE Asia. However, shipping volumes of ore from ECI to China have even shown a decrease.

Negative trends are likely to continue in the North Atlantic, as the vessel count noticeably exceeds demand and only a significant increase in shipments from USEC and USG can stabilize the market. Meanwhile, South Atlantic rates are likely to rise further thanks to lively shipments from Brazil and Argentina, as well as from WAfr and SAfr.

There are virtually no preconditions for brisker cargo traffic from European ports. The grain season is gradually nearing completion, with export potential being minimal for the next couple of months. Some players are putting hopes on the recovery of steel scrap shipments in May, but even this upturn will hardly be sufficient to change the situation dramatically.

The APAC market may see a gradual resumption of activity after the May holidays. In the Indian Ocean, exports of main cargoes and coal imports by India are expected to increase ahead of the monsoon season (some pre-monsoon activities with minor disruptions are possible already in 2H May).
