## Market Commentary

Following positive market trends seen in the neighboring Panamax tonnage group, the ECSA Supramax/Ultramax segment is also showing signs of improvement. Livelier F/H grain offer with late February and March laycans allowed ship owners to raise Supramax/

ECSA and SEAsia markets are the only glimmers of light for Handy/Supra owners

Ultramax front-haul rates by $0.5-1k daily and $1/t. Thus, rates for shipment of 50,000 t of corn or soybeans from Brazil to S.China have inched up to $35-36/t bss 1/1 given 10000x/8000x l/d rates. Meanwhile, the transatlantic market remains inactive and ship owners have so far managed only to stabilize rates after a protracted decline. The Local Handysize market also looks stable. “Owners remain resistant and hopeful about the market,” a broker mentioned. “Tonnage availability remains high on the market, preventing any positive changes” another broker added.

The sentiment has improved slightly on the most depressed Black Sea market, as well as in the neighboring Continent. Although it is obviously too early to mention any noticeable upturn in cargo flow, particularly as ship owners have not managed to raise freights from the bottom yet (except for a couple of specific routes, e.g. towards West Africa). There are still plenty of open vessels in the area.

“There are some more optimistic feelings expressed by owners, but it remains to be seen next week whether there will be some real change or not; owners were trying to get more, but eventually were accepting charterers' levels,” an EU-based Handysize operator commented to ISM. “Supramax market appeared to be picking up through the past two days but since FFA's dropped yesterday evening and looking at the tonnage list, I would think there was a bit of a push, but still an imbalance between available tonnage and orders,” a large scrap charterer mentioned. As of now, transportation of 30,000 t of corn from POC to EgyptMed still costs $12-13/t, while rates for shipment of 40-45,000 t of scrap from ARAG to TurkMed amount to $14.5-15/t given 8000x/7000c l/d ratesю

Meanwhile, the downtrend continues in both the Supramax and Handysize segments of the USG. “Despite the fact that the tariffs imposed to Canada and Mexico are on pause for now, the situation remains uncertain which also does not help the market,” a broker stated. New inbound ships coming to the area only exacerbate the cargo/tonnage imbalance amid slow cargo traffic. Spot rates have sagged by $0.5-2k daily and $0.5-2/t on all routes in the region.

### TCT rates dynamics, $/day

The Asia-Pacific Handysize/Handymax and Supramax/ Ultramax markets are seeing the post-holiday recovery of trade. Thus, players note an increased number of requests for transportation of steel from China and coal from SE Asia for 2H February laycans. Handysize/Handymax rates have remained relatively stable by the end of the week, while Supramax/Ultramax levels have risen by $1-2k daily and $0.5-1/t.

The Supramax/Ultramax market sentiment is getting bullish in the Indian Ocean as well, even despite steadily limited ore exports from ECI and growing number of vessels open off PG/WCI. Among positive factors for ship owners, players note a shorter tonnage list off S.Afr, as well as brisk salt exports from WCI. Similar to APAC, Indian Ocean rates have added $1-2k daily and $0.5-1/t.

*** Positive trends are likely to continue in the South Atlantic after February 10 as demand for Brazilian and Argentine grains is gradually increasing, especially in Asia. “With the Chinese New Year festivities coming to an end, it is expected that the market will continue this momentum,”

a broker mentioned. Meanwhile, the situation is likely to remain tense in the North Atlantic amid uncertainty over trade tariff policy in the USA. We should not rule out the possibility of further rate cuts.

Having felt the wind of change, in the short term ship owners will surely keep desperately trying to raise rates from the bottom both in North Europe and the Mediterranean basin. However, with the recent rise in grain export prices having had little-to-no impact on cargo traffic, the current slowdown in price growth certainly does not bode well for the grain trade in the region. “There are still mostly sub-sale offers around on voyage-basis for BlSea grains,” a Turkish ship owner complains. There is no reason to expect support from other major commodity markets, namely metals, chemicals, coal.

As for the Eastern Hemi, players expect further market recovery in both the Asia-Pacific region and the Indian Ocean. “We expect market sentiment to improve further as there are still many cargoes in PG/WCI,” a broker shares.
