## Market Commentary

The end of Easter holidays has brought virtually no activity boost to the small-tonnage shipping market of the Black and Mediterranean Seas. “We see only a bit more offers, but the number of vessels in spot positions has even increased, especially in BlSea and Marmara,” a Turkish broker commented to ISM. Thus, charterers' pressure on rates has only increased, especially in the 5-6k dwt segment. “With very few exceptions, there is no talk about any work at least at OPEX level now,” a Ukrainian owner desperately mentions. Note that owners working in the Mediterranean Sea are trying to push freights up or at least keep them stable due to the approaching transition to the use of more expensive fuel (May 1). In the Black Sea, rates for shipments from Ukraine have sagged by $0.5-0.75/t, those ex EU ports and Turkey - by $0.25-0.5/t. Thus, some charterers already target below $15/t for shipment of 5-6,000 t of corn from POC to Marmara and up to $20/t towards Alexandria, while owners are ready to fix at least with a couple of dollars on top.

In the Azov Sea, demand for sea-river tonnage is supported only by relatively regular shipments of coal, while exports of Russian wheat remain extremely slow. Owners of about 3k dwt vessels manage to keep rates stable (and sometimes even achieve slight gains), while the situation is considerably worse for 5-6k dwt owners, and many of them accept further rate cuts. “For instance, we are not able to find a 3k dwcc

vessel below $21/t for shipment of our grain cargo to Marmara, while there are a couple of 5k dwcc vessels being ready to fix at $18.5/t or even $18/t,” a large grain charterer shared with ISM.

North Europe is not showing a much more confident recovery from the holiday weekend. The Baltic Sea spot market is still weak, with minimal number of fresh cargoes. At the same time, the number of 2-12k dwt vessels open in the Baltic area has dropped by about 15% (due in part to outflow of tonnage to the Continent, which shows slightly higher activity). Freight rates stay at last week's levels in the basin.

Next week, we can hardly expect any noticeable improvement in North Europe, while another holiday weekend in many European countries (May 1) will again slow down the pace of trade. The cargo/ tonnage imbalance in the Black and Mediterranean Seas will hardly allow owners to raise rates to any acceptable level either.
