## Market Commentary

Of all small-tonnage markets, only the EMed sector showed some recovery after the Ramadan

Activity has grown somewhat on the small- tonnage shipping market this week, but only thanks to an upturn in shipments of fertilizers, minerals and steel products from the eastern Mediterranean ports. At the same time, despite ship owners' hopes, neither the end of Ramadan nor recent lifting of grain import duties in Turkey have not affected the Black Sea market activity. Local players only note quite lively shipments of steel scrap from Romania.

The number of vessels open for mid-2H April laycans has even increased slightly in the Black Sea, Marmara Sea, Aegean Sea and the eastern Mediterranean; particularly this applies to ships of 4-8k dwt, the number of which has risen by almost 15%, from 79 to 90 units. “So far it seems there are at least a few vessels for every size, sometimes even surprised by the amount of feedback,” key EMed charterers commented to ISM. Freights are virtually unchanged this week, while rates for shipments from Ukraine have inched down within $0.5-1/t (relevant traffic has slowed down this week, especially from the Danube ports). Thus, rates for shipment of 10-12,000 t of corn or soybeans from POC to EgyptMed have inched down to $18-18.5/t, while those for transportation of 5-6,000 t lots towards Mersin or Lebanon have sagged to $21-22/t. Meanwhile, shipment of 5-6,000 t of urea from Damietta to SpanMed costs $25-26/t ($0.5/t up over the week). In fact, cheaper bunker prices seem to be the only real improvement for owners, although we should not forget that owners will switch to

much more expensive MGO for trading within the Mediterranean in just 3 weeks.

Rates continue sliding down in the Azov Sea due to the absence of any increase in grain exports from Russian shallow-water ports, while the number of sea-river vessels has grown noticeably in the last three weeks.

The North European market is still seeing no excitement ahead of Easter holidays. Moreover, the Baltic Sea spot market is looking extremely weak, with only a relatively moderate tonnage list helping ship owners to restrain the rate decline. Despite a small number of actual deals in the Baltic area, indicative freights have still dropped by €0.5-1/t, but estimated TCEs are virtually flat given a considerable decrease in bunker prices ($50-70/t down over the week). Meanwhile, North Sea rates stay at last week's levels.

There are few preconditions for activity upturns in the coming weeks, although a slight positional growth is still possible ahead of Easter. Forecasts regarding the Black Sea are the most ambiguous. “Most probably we will see higher demand for Ukrainian and Russian corn and wheat from China,
