## Market Commentary

Adverse weather and war risks remain the only supporting factors for owners on shortsea market

Seems like unfavourable weather conditions all across Europe remain the major supporter of freight levels in shortsea markets. “The weather is a huge problem almost everywhere - freezing Baltic, stormy and rainy UK, same from north France to Marmara, not saying about severe problems in Ukraine and Russia - everywhere it's bad and ships are sheltering,” an EU charterer commented to ISM. «We were cancelled 4 times in Med due to delays, trying to load cargo towards the Continent,” an EU owners' broker complains.

Despite still sluggish cargo flow in the Black Sea, owners are keeping freights stable due to limited tonnage list (only the supply of 4-6k dwt vessels has increased). At the moment, shipment of 5-6,000 t of corn or soybeans from POC or Izmail to EgyptMed or Lebanon costs $32-33/t, while rates for transportation of 7-8,000 t of wheat from Constanta to the Italian Adriatic remain at mid-high $20s/t. Regular shipments of fertilizers, minerals, agricultural products, and steel products are supporting decent tonnage demand in the Mediterranean Sea, while the vessel count has dropped slightly in the region. Shipowners are trying to push rates up, but average market levels have not changed much, according to ISM estimates. Meanwhile, Azov Sea charterers are forced to accept further growth of sea-river rates, as it is extremely difficult to find a vessel even for mid- February (ships are staying idle for 2-3 weeks due to

extremely difficult ice conditions and inspections). Corresponding freight rates have added another $2- 4/t over the week, despite weak exports of grain and coal from Russia. For instance, rates for transportation of 3,000 t of wheat from Rostov/Azov to Marmara have inched up to mid $50s/t. Note that current levels are abnormally high for February: over the past 10 years, only February 2023 saw higher rates. Unfortunately for owners, the same cannot be said for RV TCEs; although they exceed OPEX by 40-50%, current TCEs are lower than levels seen in February 2018, 2021, 2022, and

In North Europe, shipowners even manage to raise freights (up €1-2/t over the week) amid a modest tonnage list in the Baltic Sea. The Continent has also seen higher rates this week (especially on the spot market), despite a 5-10% increase in the number of open vessels.

Unless trading activity perks up in the Mediterranean and Black Seas - and there are frankly few signs that it will -, the coaster market may start sagging in 2H February, as the average trip duration will be decreasing and the number of open vessels will be growing as weather conditions improve. In the Azov Sea, the already weak cargo flow will almost certainly decline further after February 15 due to the introduction of export quotas on grain, so any improvement in regional ice conditions will also immediately trigger the rate decline.
