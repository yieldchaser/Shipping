## Market Commentary

USG pullback weighs on Handy/Supra Atlantic market, while Asia faces holiday- driven slowdown

The USG Supramax/Ultramax market is cooling down after a rapid and significant growth seen last week. The cargo traffic has slowed down noticeably, partly due to the holiday weekend in China. Meanwhile, the influx of vessels into the region has gotten brisker amid the recent surge in activity. As a result, the cargo/tonnage imbalance is worsening almost daily. Charterers are aggressively pushing rates down, seeking to return to pre-rally levels. By the end of the week, USG freights have dropped by an average of $1-3k daily and $1-3/t across all routes. For instance, rates for transportation of 50,000 t of corn or soybeans from USG to N.China have inched down to $57-58/t. The Handy market is also seeing a trade slowdown, while the regional tonnage list is generally unchanged (around 45-50 ships are open for the next 20 days in the area). So far, shipowners have managed to keep rates stable, but sentiment has started to shift in favor of charterers.

The South Atlantic is also facing a slowdown in F/H Supramax cargo traffic. Meanwhile, requests for transatlantic shipments are coming in quite frequently. The ECSA market is receiving additional support from the fairly strong West African market. “The West African market is a bright spot in the Atlantic right now, as tightening tonnage and steady bauxite and mineral demand support firmer rates in the area,” a broker mentioned. Supramax/Ultramax rates have not changed significantly over the week. Meanwhile, positive trends prevail in the Handy segment. Despite

moderate cargo flows, limited tonnage list for prompt dates allowed shipowners to raise rates by $0.5-1k daily and $1-2/t on all routes. Thus, rates for transportation of 25-30,000 t of soybeans from Santos to ARAG have increased to $39-40/t.

The Continent market appears balanced, with rates staying at last week's levels. Many charterers are attempting to cut freight levels, but have not yet managed to change the situation in their favor, as the vessel count is not particularly large. Ice conditions remain challenging in the Baltic Sea, so ice-class vessels enjoy healthy demand, allowing shipowners to confidently dictate terms and achieve higher rates from week to week (another $1-2k daily and $0.5-2/t up this week).

In the Black Sea, ISM sources note regular shipments of Ukrainian and Russian grain, but offers of other cargoes (including grain from the EU) are extremely scarce. Still, Supramax owners have managed to raise rates from the bottom (up $0.5-1k daily and $0.5-1/t). At the same time, Handysize freight levels are flat.

Exports of Indonesian coal and Far Eastern steel in Supramax/Ultramax and Handysize lots have slowed down in the Asia-Pacific region given the expected withdrawal of major players during the Lunar New Year celebrations. However, freight rates have not changed significantly so far.

### Supramax/Ultramax freight rates and dynamics on major routes

Despite the stable tonnage supply off PG/WCI, Supramax/Ultramax owners working in the Indian Ocean have even managed to push rates up by $1-2k daily and $0.5-1/t this week given the recent upturn in manganese ore shipments from South Africa as well as increasing number of Ultramax ballasters from South Africa towards ECSA.

Next week, negative trends are likely to continue in the North Atlantic. “The next move will depend on whether fresh Atlantic cargo emerges once the holidays pass. If it does, the market is still capable of firming again,” a broker commented to ISM. In the South Atlantic, rates are more likely to rise, supported by the lively West African market.

Shipowners will still try to maintain their positions in North Europe, counting on an increase in scrap exports. At the same time, the Mediterranean basin shows no preconditions for an upturn in cargo traffic, while the gradual improvement of weather conditions in early March may put an end to owners' attempts to raise freight levels.

The Asia-Pacific market will remain under pressure during the Chinese New Year celebrations. Meanwhile, the outlook for the South African market is mixed. “The ballasting trend towards ECSA may continue but most of manganese ore cargoes have been covered. People will suffer,” a broker quips.
