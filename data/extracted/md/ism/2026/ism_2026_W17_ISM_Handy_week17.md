## Market Commentary

Atlantic and APAC Handy/Supra markets remain resilient, while Mediterranean stays under pressure

Positive trends are generally dominating the Atlantic Supramax/Ultramax shipping market, although trading activity has decreased slightly after April 20 compared to last week (largely due to the renewed geopolitical complications). At the moment, major players are once again opting to take a wait- and-see stance, especially when it comes to signing contracts with forward dates.

The North Atlantic segment is seeing limited vessel availability for spot dates, which allows shipowners to demand higher rates. By the end of the week, spot TCT rates have risen by $1-2k daily across all routes, while voyage freights have added $1-2/t. New grain requests are more frequently showing up in the South Atlantic, which applies to both spot and 2H May-June laycans. Note that large Ultramax units are currently enjoying high demand. Also, demand for transatlantic shipments is higher than for fronthaul trips, which has been reflected in rate dynamics. Thus, F/H rates are largely unchanged, while T/A TCT levels have risen by $1-2k daily across all routes, and corresponding voyage rates have increased by $1-2/t. For instance, rates for shipment of 50,000 t of corn from Santos to SpanMed have increased to $37/t, while those for transportation of 50,000 t of soybeans from New Orleans to N. China have inched up to $66-67/t.

Handy rates are so far holding steady in USG, but the tonnage list is growing quite rapidly. For instance, about 80 vessels are expected to enter the region over the next few weeks. Market participants note that requests for 2H May shipments are coming in more actively than spot offers. Meanwhile, demand for Handysize carriers is gradually increasing in the southern part of the basin amid a rise in May cargo offer. This week, corresponding rates have increased by $1k daily and $1-2/t across all trades.

Solid demand for Handysize and Supramax tonnage in North Europe , coupled with limited supply, allows shipowners to continue driving rates higher. Cargo volumes remain fairly strong in the region, driven by shipments of fertilizers, grains, steel scrap, and to a lesser extent, timber. As a result, freight levels have strengthened by $0.5-1.5k daily and $0.5- 2/t this week. It should be noted that positive trends in the Atlantic Ocean also play a significant role in the growth of North European rates. At the same time, even the overall positive Atlantic picture does not allow owners to push rates up in the Mediterranean basin . Moreover, charterers have managed to lower Handysize rates by another $0.25-0.5k daily and $0.5- 1/t amid weak cargo flows. In the Asia-Pacific region, Supramax/Ultramax owners continue to benefit from rising demand across the basin. Thus, shipowners have taken advantage of increased shipments of Indonesian coal, Philippine nickel ore, and Australian cargoes. The regional Handysize/Handymax market is supported by a tighter supply of tonnage, as well as the increased number of requests for steel shipments from the Far East.

Owners of Supramax/Ultramax tonnage working in the Indian Ocean barely manage to keep rates stable amid a persistent shortage of PG cargoes, coupled with signs of a slowdown in South African coal traffic.

Next week, market developments will depend in part on the situation in the Middle East. However, fundamentals still carry greater weight and have a stronger impact on freight rates. Thus, demand for vessels is projected to increase in USG, especially on transatlantic routes, closer to 2H May, while a temporary shortage of spot cargo may have a negative impact on freight levels in the near term. In the southern part of the basin, players also note an increased number of requests for May shipments; however, the influx of ballasters from the Mediterranean area may slightly dampen shipowners' expectations and slow the pace of rate growth.
