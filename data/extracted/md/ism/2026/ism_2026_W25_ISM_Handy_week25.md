## Market Commentary

### Rising Atlantic demand for Handy/Supra vessels supports rates despite geopolitical uncertainty

Atlantic Supramax/Ultramax and Handysize shipping markets have been, quite logically, affected by shifting geopolitical sentiments during the week. Following the peace agreement between the US and Iran, many market participants have taken a wait-and- see stance, monitoring developments to gain a clearer understanding of market conditions, as the situation began to change rapidly amid a collapse in oil prices and, consequently, in bunker fuel prices. And, as Friday's events have shown, market participants were right to take their time, as Iran has once again closed the Strait of Hormuz and the situation may escalate once more. “Bunker prices have plummeted, while owners are still holding out for last week's numbers; the market is searching for a new direction,” a broker mentioned.

The cargo offer has increased in the North Atlantic. Shipowners have managed to secure a significant rate upturn in some cases, which particularly applies to Ultramax time-charter deals (primarily on transatlantic routes). Meanwhile, contracts with July laycans – particularly with 2H July dates – are being negotiated at lower rates, as charterers have aggressively started pushing for lower freights in anticipation of a considerable fall in bunker prices (which has once again been looking highly doubtful by the end of the week). Anyways, spot TCT rates have risen by $2-3k daily by the end of the week; however, voyage-basis freights have remained largely unchanged, or have even dropped within $0.5-1/t on some routes. The USG Handy market has also firmed up slightly, driven by an increase in offer of grain, petcoke, coal, timber, scrap

and iron ore from the US and Canada. TCT levels have added $1k daily, while voyage rates have declined by $1-2/t. For instance, TCT rates for Supramax vessels bss APS dely USG redel Mediterranean have inched up to $26.5-27.5k daily, while those for 30-32k dwt Handy vessels have increased to $17-18k daily. Meanwhile, rates for shipment of 30,000 t of wheat from New Orleans to ARA have declined to low $30's/t bss 1/1.

The South Atlantic Supramax market remains fairly active. “Market is well supported on F/H, while tonnage count is thin, especially for early July,” а broker commented. Delays at West African ports due to rain are having a further impact on the local market, limiting the inflow of vessels into ECSA. TCT rates for shipments from the region's ports have remained unchanged (staying the highest in the entire hemisphere), while voyage rates have dropped by $1-2/t amid a fall in bunker fuel prices. The Handy market picture is favourable for shipowners thanks to a positional shortage of vessels open for spot dates. Note that sometimes shipowners are voicing ideas that are too high and seem somewhat detached from the reality dictated by objective market fundamentals. The local vessel count does not appear to be critically tight, and shipowners' inflated expectations may be slightly ahead of the curve in this situation. By the end of the week, TCT rates have risen by an average of $2-3k daily, while voyage freights have declined by around $1/t.

### Supramax/Ultramax freight rates and dynamics on major routes

there are still plenty of available vessels in the region. Handysize rates have strengthened by $1-2k daily and within $0.5/t this week. Supramax/Ultramax time- charter rates have risen by $0.5-1.5k daily, while voy-bss freights have remained largely flat. The Mediterranean and Black Seas are gradually getting impacted by the approaching new grain season. “We increasingly receive requests from grain traders to indicate Jul-Aug shipments on different routes, though there are still not many firm offers,” a local broker told ISM. Despite this slight upturn, the Atlantic demand is still the main driver in the area, as it absorbs available tonnage from the Mediterranean basin. Over the week, Supramax/ Ultramax rates have added $1.5-3k daily and $0.5- 1.5/t, while the Handysize segment has seen a much more modest upturn of $1-2k daily and $0.5/t. Rates for shipment of 30,000 t of wheat from POC to Algeria have inched up to $30-31/t.

By contrast, the situation has worsened for Supramax/Ultramax owners working in the Asia- Pacific region due to growing shortage of coal offers for shipments from Indonesia, as well as an increase in the number of vessels available in the region. Furthermore, market participants have started to note a decline in offer of Far Eastern steel. Owners' resistance in Southeast Asia is still largely supported by brisk exports of Philippine nickel ore. This week, time-charter rates have dropped by $0.5-1k daily, while freight rates have declined by $1-2.5/t. The Handysize/Handymax sector is also seeing a decline in offer of Far Eastern steel, although sentiment amongst shipowners remains generally positive given the reduction in the number of available vessels. Time- charter rates have grown within $1-1.5k daily, while voy-bss freights are largely flat due to a fall in bunker prices.

In the Indian Ocean, activity remains subdued, and the peace agreement between the US and Iran has expectedly done little to change the current situation so far, particularly given how quickly traffic through the Strait of Hormuz has come to a standstill once again. Exports of main cargoes from PG ports stay limited, while iron ore traffic from ECI to the Far East is slowing down (some coastal iron ore shipments do support tonnage demand to some extent). Shipowners' confidence is mainly bolstered by the decline in regions' tonnage list. Time-charter rates are holding largely steady this week, while freights have dropped by $0.5-1/t.

### TCT rates dynamics, $/day

Forecasts regarding the market's future development are mixed. The situation will largely depend on whether the U.S. and Iran reach a long- term peace agreement. If fuel prices keep going down, rising TCT rates will merely slow the decline in voyage rates (taking into account the active market in the North and South Atlantic). However, in case Iran ultimately decides to reject the U.S. proposal, the Strait remains closed, and oil prices stop falling, voyage rates will hardly decrease, especially given the upcoming start of the new grain season in EU countries and the Black Sea region. Furthermore, due to rains and delays at West African ports, the influx of ballasters to ECSA will probably remain low, which will only exert additional upward pressure on rates there.

The tendencies towards tonnage oversupply and shortage of coal offer in SE Asia may keep affecting the APAC Supramax/Ultramax market. Since the peace deal has been cancelled, the cargo traffic from PG will be bottlenecked further.
