## Market Commentary

Summer lull deepens across coaster markets despite the start of the new grain season

The Azov sea-river market remains at rock bottom. Although new-crop wheat shipments in 2H July are being negotiated slightly more actively, demand for spot tonnage remains extremely weak. Shipowners have so far been unable to push rates higher. “Rates towards Marmara or EMed remain more or less the same; only a few contracts for shipments towards Kavkaz roads are being discussed at higher rates,” a Turkish broker mentioned. Shipment of 3,000 t of wheat from Rostov to Marmara still costs only $24- 25/t. which hardly reaches even 1k daily in TCE bss RV. No major shifts can be seen in the Caspian Sea . Draft issues in the Volga-Caspian Canal persist, while freight rates are holding steady.

The coaster shipping market has entered its summer lull in North Europe with the start of the holiday season in European countries. Cargo volumes remain poor, while the tonnage list has grown by about 5% in the region over the week. However, bunker price volatility helped owners to avoid further rate cuts for now.

The situation for shipowners operaing on the North European market may worsen slightly in the short term, which will probably force them to make further concessions in case of decreasing cargo volumes. Despite the start of the new grain season, the Black Sea cargo traffic is expected to remain slack over the next couple of weeks, as demand for new-crop wheat so far remains sluggish in Mediterranean countries and Turkey. Local importers have most likely taken a wait-and-see stance, hoping for price reductions given rising harvest forecasts in Ukraine and Russia. As before, shipowners cannot expect any support from other cargoes (this applies to both the Black Sea and the neighbouring Mediterranean region).
