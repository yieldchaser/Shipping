## Market Commentary

Sea-going coaster market looks empty even after Eid-al-Adha and sees further pressure on rates, while sea-river freights reached bottom in Azov Sea

In early June, the Black Sea and Mediterranean coaster shipping markets are looking decidedly depressed, which, however, was quite expected. Even after the end of protracted holidays in Turkey and MENA countries, trade has not picked up in the basin. Moreover, brokers' estimates show that the number of spot cargo offers has dropped by about 30% over the week. Therefore, even considering the shorter list of available 2-12k dwt vessels (down from 200 to 182 units – at least, this is the number shown by owners), the market imbalance has only worsened, especially given that most cargo offers on the market are “non-firm”. “Honestly, when it comes to firm offers and somewhat workable freight rates, the market appears almost empty, while owners are desperately searching for cargo wherever they can,” an EU broker eloquently describes the current picture. “Even scrap shipments have almost stopped, though they were one of the most active cargoes just a couple of weeks ago,” another broker added. Note that some market players are absent due to Posidonia and the major annual grain conference in Kyiv. A further decline in VLSFO and MGO prices is perhaps the only positive news for owners, as it partially offsets the ongoing decline in freights. Thus, by the end of the week, rates have dropped by another $0.5-1.5/t in the Black Sea, and by $0.5-2/t in the Mediterranean Sea; however, TCEs have not declined as significantly. Thus, shipment of 5-6,000 t of corn or soybeans from POC to Alexandria, Iskenderun or Lebanon now costs $31-32/t, while rates for transportation of 5-6,000 t of wheat from CVB to the Italian Adriatic have inched down to $31.5-32.5/t. Rates for shipment of 5-6,000 t

of urea from Damietta to SpanMed have declined to $36-37/t. According to ISM estimates, owners are still able to cover OPEX in the case of a short ballast leg (up to 300-400 nautical miles); at the same time, long- haul round-voyage TCEs with the full ballast leg back are well below OPEX levels.

The situation is even worse in the neighboring Azov Sea where freight rates appear to have bottomed out; however, shipowners are no longer able to improve their positions in any way by relying on the previously cheap Rostov MGO. This week, freight rates are generally holding steady in the region, while RV TCEs for 3k dwt vessels operating on the Azov-Marmara route have fallen below even $0.9k daily. Sales of Russian wheat to Turkey are minimal, partly due to the ruble strengthening. “Many Russian-flagged vessels have been repositioned to the Caspian Sea, but this has not affected the Azov market so far,” a Turkish broker commented. In the Caspian Sea , rates for shipments ex river ports and Astrakhan have inched down by $0.5-1/t, even despite the ongoing draft issue in the Volga-Caspian Canal. In addition, Iran's demand for grain has clearly declined in recent weeks.

Over the week, freight rates have declined by €1-3/t depending on the route.

A further deterioration on the coaster shipping market appears highly likely, both in South and North Europe. Cargo volumes will hardly grow before the start of the new grain season, and no support is currently expected from other major cargoes, primarily steel products and fertilizers. The return of many players from Posidonia is not projected to have a significant impact on market dynamics. In addition, another major industry event, the Bergen Shipping Dinner, is scheduled for next week. According to ISM estimates, in the best-case scenario for shipowners, freight rates will stabilize at current levels; however, this development is extremely unlikely if the downtrend in bunker prices continues.
