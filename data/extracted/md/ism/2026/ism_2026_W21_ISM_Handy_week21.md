## Market Commentary

Handysize/Supramax market: USG strength contrasts with cooling ECSA and sluggish Continent & Mediterranean segments

Activity has slackened somewhat on the South Atlantic Supramax/Ultramax shipping market, with June vessel count gradually rising. “Momentum eased slightly from the rally we saw last week as fronthaul activity slowed down,” a broker commented. Note, however, that large Ultramax vessels continue to command a premium and are successfully competing with the Panamax fleet. By the end of the week, F/H rates have stayed at quite high levels, while T/A freights have added another $1-2k daily and $1-2/t amid a limited number of vessels willing to take such biz. Meanwhile, Handy owners are making concessions to charterers in order to secure cargo. Despite some growth in cargo offer for early June dates (particularly this applies to grain shipments from Argentina to Mediterranean ports), the influx of ballasters from WCSA, Med, and WAfr is putting strong pressure on rates, primarily affecting the small Handy segment. As a result, rates have dropped by an average of $1k daily and $1-2/t across all routes. For instance, rates for the shipment of 30,000 t of wheat from Upriver to Algeria have inched down to $56-57/t given 7000x/5000x l/d rates or to very high $50s/t given 7000x/2500x l/d rates.

By contrast, the North Atlantic is seeing an activity upturn. “Healthy numbers are reported across all key routes,” a broker mentioned. Demand remains strong for 1H June, while the number of available vessels is rapidly declining in the region. Market participants

note that the gradual cooling of the Panamax market will lead to increased competition on the USG-Continent/Med and USEC-India routes, which could curb potential growth of Smx/Umx rates. Nevertheless, market sentiment remains positive for now: “Sentiment feels well supported moving into the first half of June,” a broker commented. The region's Handysize segment is also seeing an increase in cargo offer for 1H June (especially on transatlantic routes and the USEC direction), which has a positive impact on rates, given the moderate tonnage list (approximately 45-50 vessels available in the region for late May-early June). Over the week, Handysize/ Supramax rates ex USG & USEC have risen by $1k daily and $1-2/t on average across all trades.

The Supramax/Ultramax market picture is mixed in North Europe. The region's cargo traffic is mainly driven by steel scrap traffic, with players noting a slight uptick in such shipments with late May laycans, which leads to increased tonnage demand for those dates and even allows to sign individual deals at rates exceeding last week's levels. At the same time, offers of other cargoes are few and far between. “The market is still a bit unclear – in theory it should drop, with fewer cargoes generally available; it remains positional, and I have to admit the Supra-Ultra tonnage list is also slightly shorter than last week,

The Handysize sector appears to be in a severe slump both in the Continent and in the neighboring Mediterranean basin , featuring just some sporadic cargo requests amid a significant tonnage surplus in both regions (a total of more than 170 Handysize vessels are available for late May and early June dates). Note that the introduction of an import ban on soft wheat varieties into Morocco added to the market pressure (traditionally, French grain accounted for about 55–65% of imports, and this cargo flow used to be one of the main drivers of the Continent market). This week, freight rates have sagged by another $0.5- 1k daily and $0.5-1.5/t in both basins. Thus, rates for the transportation of 25-30,000 t of corn from Ukraine to Marmara have inched down to $17-17.5/t, while those to EgyptMed have declined to low $22.5-23.5/t.

The sentiment has slackened a bit in the Asia- Pacific region given a slowdown in Supramax traffic of Far Eastern steel and a shortage of requests for transportation of Indonesian coal, especially towards India. However, freights have stayed at last week's levels, with rather regular exports of peripheral SE Asian cargoes and Far Eastern steel in Ultramax-lots. In the Handysize/Handymax segment, rates have risen by as much as $0.5-1k daily and $1-2/t following the last weeks' uptrend and considering tight tonnage availability in the APAC region.

Meanwhile, Indian Ocean freights are holding largely flat. Thus, charterers still do not hurry to sign deals in the PG/WCI area. Meanwhile, the South African sector is viewed as positional, with stable exports of coal to Pakistan and manganese ore. In the meantime, coal traffic to India has slowed down.

Next week, Smx/Umx rates are likely to stabilize in the South Atlantic due to an increase in the region's vessel count caused by an influx of ballasters, as well as a gradual softening of the Panamax segment. Expectations regarding developments in the North Atlantic generally favor shipowners; however, lower freights for Panamax tonnage may limit potential growth of Smx/Umx levels.

In the Mediterranean and North Europe, the market will most likely continue to be held back by extremely weak export activity. This applies primarily to grains, as the season is drawing to a close. The local metals market also looks sluggish. In addition, next week will see extended holidays in Muslim countries for Eid al-Adha, so activity will almost certainly drop to a minimum, and rates may fall slightly further.

Players may continue to adopt a cautious approach in the Southeast Asian Handysize/Ultramax segment. Owners' concerns may increase regarding shipments to India ahead of the upcoming monsoon season.
