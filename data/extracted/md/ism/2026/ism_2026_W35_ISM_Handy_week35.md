## Market Commentary

Handysize/Supramax sentiment keeps improving in USG, Continent and Asia- Pacific, while pressure is mounting in the Mediterranean and ECSA markets

In the USG, demand for Supramax/Ultramax tonnage is gradually picking up this week. While owners are attempting to push rates higher when negotiating fronthaul routes, this is off the table for transatlantic and short inter-Caribs trips. Competition for these cargoes remains quite fierce among owners, as most prefer to keep their carriers within the Atlantic while waiting for the US grain export season to kick off. Meanwhile, the USG Handy segment remains under pressure, though cargo flow is starting to recover. At the moment, shipment of 25-30,000 t of corn or soybeans from New Orleans to EgyptMed costs high $30s/t bss 1/1.

The ECSA Supramax/Ultramax market has seen a slowdown in activity following last week's surge. Nevertheless, rates are holding at levels comfortable for owners, and the market has entered a wait-and-see mode ahead of the next wave of cargo traffic upturn. At the same time, Handy rates stay depressed in the South Atlantic. Thus, rates for transportation of 25- 30,000 t of wheat from Upriver to Algeria still amount to high $40s/t in case of 5000 sshex dischrate or low $50s/t in case of 2500 sshex dischrate. Prospects for a future recovery directly depend on dynamics in neighboring regions, primarily the USG. “It remains to be seen if USG will attract vessels open in ECSA or not,” a local broker commented to ISM.

As expected, the number of new cargo offers from Baltic & Continent ports is gradually rising, while the surplus of tonnage is no longer seen in the region. Handysize owners are feeling increasingly confident, with freight levels up by $1-2k daily and $0.5-1/t over

the week. The local Supramax segment is looking more stable; notably, owners are being quite aggressive in their freight ideas for trips toward the EMed, as they are reluctant to reposition their vessels to that area. This logic holds up given that shipments from Russia and Ukraine remain virtually non-existent, despite available cargo offers. As before, only a handful of owners are willing to work ex local ports, demanding sky-high rates alongside other conditions (e.g., a 50% advance payment, or EWRI+crew bonus on owner's account, etc.).

Despite a strong outflow in recent weeks, prompt tonnage list continues to grow slowly in the eastern Mediterranean . The cargo offer from EU, Turkish, and North African ports remains severely insufficient to keep owners employed. In these circumstances, rates for so-called non-premium trades are sliding down further. Supramax rates have dropped by $1.5-3.5k daily and $1-4/t in the Black Sea and EMed, while Handysize levels have declined by $1-1.5k daily and $1-2/t. For instance, rates for shipment of 25-30,000 t of wheat from CVB to SpanMed have sagged by $2-2.5/ t, to $19-20/t. Note that the western Mediterranean market is far more resilient, supported both by steady local cargo volumes and rising tonnage demand from North European and USG charterers.

In the Asia-Pacific region, Supramax/Ultramax rates have gained $1-2k daily and $0.5-1/t this week thanks to brisker exports of NOPAC grains and Far Eastern steel. Besides, market players report healthy peripheral cargo shipments in SE Asia. On the supply side, typhoon-related disruptions have affected

Stagnation continues in the Indian Ocean Supramax/Ultramax sector due to limited traffic of iron ore from India and manganese ore from South Africa. There is virtually nothing positive to report regarding the long-suffering Strait of Hormuz and the PG market.

Demand is expected to keep strengthening in the North Atlantic in early September as the season of active grain exports ex Unites States approaches. In the southern part of the basin, by contrast, a further slowdown in activity and a decline in cargo volumes cannot ne ruled out. Nevertheless, despite this mixed outlook, positive sentiment prevails among shipowners and brokers. “Actively growing demand in the USG will stimulate the influx of vessels into this region, while in the south there will be a certain shortage of vessel supply, which might have a positive impact on rates in ECSA,” an operator mentioned.

Next developments in Northern Europe appear quite likely against the backdrop of ongoing increase in cargo volumes. In the Mediterranean region, only intensified vessel outflow can help owners at least stabilize rates.

As the weather-related disruption has already passed its peak in the Asia-Pacific region, some of the Supramax/Ultramax tonnage temporarily tied up by the typhoon is likely to be released back into the market soon..
