## Market Commentary

Atlantic Handysize/Supramax segment shows signs of recovery, while APAC market continues on the softening mode

The USG&USEC Supramax/Ultramax market has strengthened owing to brisker shipments of grain, petroleum coke and coal, especially in the Far East. Brokers note a rapid decline in spot fleet supply in the region, which has allowed owners to raise rates by $1- 2k daily and $1-2/t across all routes. South Atlantic is also dominated by positive trends. “We see a decent amount of demand and tonnage being cleared out,” a broker mentioned. “F/H rates saw some slight improvement, while TA is rather flat for now,” another broker added. Therefore, the week has ended with a $0.5k daily and $0.5-1/t rise in Supramax/Ultramax rates. For instance, rates for shipment of 50,000 t of corn or soybeans from New Orleans to N.China have inched up to $55/t.

As for the Handysize market, the cargo traffic is also on a gradual increase in both North and South Atlantic, but this is actually not enough to boost the freight rates in the segment. At the same time, the fleet supply for the first half of February is declining rapidly, especially in ECSA. In such circumstances, rates might start going up in the near future.

The North European Supramax/Ultramax shipping market enjoys certain enhancements too. Demand for Supramax/Ultramax vessels has risen thanks to more Russian fertilizers offered for such shipments. In addition, deliveries of coal, grain, and European scrap remain quite regular. So, shipowners have managed to step up rates by $1-3k daily and $1-3/t. The most noticeable rate hikes have been seen at

Russian ports, where charterers are even facing more difficulties in finding available ships. In the meantime, the Handysize market remains inactive, with excess tonnage leaving no chance for owners to raise rates.

Although the number of available Supramax vessels in the Far East has decreased, and exports of both NOPAC cargoes and Far Eastern steel on the backhaul routes have picked up, owners have failed to raise rates in the region. ### Supramax/Ultramax freight rates and dynamics on major routes

stagnation on the Handysize/Handymax market persists, with dull trading activity across the region.

Rates remain under pressure in the Indian Ocean . The cargo traffic ex PG/WCI remains flat, while the tonnage count has edged up slightly. Meanwhile, the tonnage list off ECI continues to outweigh iron ore enquiries.

North Atlantic rates may grow again after January 26 if the cargo inflow continue to grow. “Some believe that the market levels have reached their peak, but I still see good potential and room for improvement,” a broker mentioned. Shipowners are also optimistic about the situation in the southern part of the basin, as they expect increased volume of grain for spot and forward dates to boost freight rates in the area. “There are expectations for the market to improve as we get closer to February, but more demand is required to give the rates much needed push,” a shipowner mentioned to ISM.

Furthermore, shipowners will be trying to strengthen their positions in northern Europe, although there are few prerequisites for such cargo traffic from European ports to grow. Further increase in steel scrap and fertilizer exports looks unlikely. In the Black and Mediterranean Seas, owners can only hope for an outflow of the fleet to the Atlantic.

It remains questionable whether trade in the Supramax/Ultramax shipping sector will gain momentum in the Asia-Pacific region, as charterers in SE Asia are in no great hurry. As for the Handysize/ Handymax srgment, market participants do not expect a significant improvement even after the Lunar New Year.
