## Market Commentary

Coaster market: Continent & Baltic Sea market strengthens while Ukrainian Danube rally loses steam

Following the rally seen over the past few weeks, charterers have managed to halt the rise in freight rates ex Ukrainian Black Sea ports (even despite further growth of congestion while waiting to pass through the Sulina Canal, which now exceeds two weeks). In any case, it is currently impossible to objectively assess transportation costs from Reni and Izmail, since charterers are ready to pay $15-20/t more for vessels open inside the Danube or the Sulina than for those opening in the Black Sea. Similarly, charterers are willing to pay significantly more if the shipowner is willing to transit through Bystre. Note that shipments to the eastern Mediterranean have clearly slowed down, while traffic towards the Marmara Sea has picked up. “To be honest, this week is quieter than recent ones,” a Turkish shipowner commented to ISM. Rates for shipments to the Marmara Sea have seen a particularly significant upturn over the week (up $6- 8/t). For instance, rates for shipment of 3-7,000 t of wheat from Izmail or Reni to Marmara have increased to mid-high $80s/t, while those towards EgyptMed have inched up to $103-107/t. “We see more and more so called backhaul/return cargoes to the Danube, which are actually being fixed at not that high rates, since many owners are ready to take those cargoes in order to have an advantage for the next employment after the discharge,” a local broker added, sharing his thoughts on current market conditions. Freight rates for shipments from other Black Sea ports, as well as those in the neighboring Mediterranean Sea as a whole, have not seen any major shifts. Cargo volumes have grown slightly only in the eastern Mediterranean, while a surplus of tonnage persists throughout the

basin (the number of 2-12k dwt vessels available from Gibraltar to Kerch has dropped very slightly this week - from 291 to 281 units). By the end of the week, freights ex EU, Turkish, and MENA ports have added an average of $0.5/t; however, this only offsets the latest rise in bunker prices (particularly MGO); TCEs stay at rock bottom and are rarely able to cover OPEX, especially if the ballast leg exceeds 300-400 nautical miles.

The Azov Sea market is still nonexistent, and shipping out to the Black Sea remains prohibited. At the same time, freight rates from Rostov to Iran via the river continue to rise rapidly, as are rates from the Caspian and Volga ports to Iran (up $10-20/t over the week).

The market in the Baltic and North Seas continues to demonstrate seasonal growth in activity and freight rates. This trend is mainly supported by an increase in grain offer for shipments from European ports. Shipowners are successfully pushing rates up, although freights are often rising not as rapidly as owners had anticipated (up €1-3/t by the end of the week). Thus, the deal for spot shipment of 4,000 t of dwt cargo from Lithuania to ARAG is negotiated at €28-29/t.

Without a normalization of exports from Ukraine and Russia, there are frankly few signs of an upturn in the Black and Mediterranean Seas, since export outlook for European wheat is moderate, while the region's steel market will almost certainly remain weak in the near term; also, the uptick in urea shipments will hardly be sufficient to move rates up given the very long tonnage list. The North European market will most likely continue to strengthen, although a possible influx of tonnage from the Mediterranean region could significantly complicate the situation for shipowners.
