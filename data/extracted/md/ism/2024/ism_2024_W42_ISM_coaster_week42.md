## Market Commentary

The small-tonnage market activity continues to grow, primarily in the Black Sea, where Ukrainian grain remains the driver. The fact that ship owners increasingly refuse to call the ports of Big Odessa due to Russian missile attacks is an additional challenge

Small-tonnage owners succeed in getting higher rates in Black Sea, while sea-river owners are bearish in Azov Sea

for charterers from Ukraine. The shortage of 3-4k dwt vessels is especially acute these days. By the end of the week, rates for shipments from Ukraine's deep- water ports have risen by $3-4/t, those from the Danube ports by $2.5-3.5/t. Thus, shipment of 6-8,000 t of wheat or corn from Odesa or Chornomorsk to the Italian Adriatic already costs $33.5-34.5/t, while rates for transportation of 5-6,000 t of corn or soybeans from Izmail to Marmara are hovering at $23-25/t. The cargo traffic from other Black Sea countries has perked up, albeit just slightly. Still, owners have managed to raise corresponding freights by $0.5- 1.5/t. For instance, rates for transportation of 3-4,000 t of wheat from Varna to Marmara have inched up to $16/t, while those for shipment of 6-7,000 t of coal from Novorossiysk to TBS have risen to $16-16.5/t. By

the way, this fact once again confirms the validity of ISM analysts' and many brokers' opinion: the upturn in rates ex Ukrainian ports is mainly caused by improved market conditions and not by increased military risks; otherwise, rates from neighboring Romania and Bulgaria would by contrast have sagged due to sharpening competition for few cargoes from these countries.

In the Mediterranean Sea, Egyptian fertilizers remain the most popular cargo, shipments of which have got brisker during the week. However, charterers have managed to halt the rate growth in the basin so far.

In the Baltic Sea, demand for tonnage is mainly supported by grain shipments, although traffic of coal and timber cargoes has slightly recovered. However, the overall cargo flow in the region is still insufficient to let owners raise freight rates further (even though weather conditions are gradually deteriorating).

Only the Azov Sea market retains negative sentiment this week, although it was the most active and high market just 3-4 weeks ago. Sea-river rates have sagged by another $0.5-1.5/t by the end of the week. “Seems negative impetus from previous week is very strong, owners are still unable to stop rates decline even despite shorter list of open vessels,” a Turkish broker commented. Shipment of 3,000 t of wheat from Azov/Rostov to Marmara now costs $31- 32/t, while some traders already target even below $30/t.

Next week, owners will certainly try to push rates further up in the Black Sea, as well as resume the upturn in the Mediterranean area. However, it will be quite difficult, as export prices for Ukrainian wheat and corn have stabilized after a sharp rise and are even starting to slide down. In addition, there is frankly little support in other commodity groups: demand for urea will hardly grow further on the Mediterranean market, while the interest of Turkish buyers in square billets is also declining. The North European market may see higher freight levels due to the expected traditional worsening of navigation conditions (storms, winds and rain) and decreasing fleet turnover. Meanwhile, players share radically different forecasts regarding further developments in the Azov Sea. On the one hand, many traders are expecting further freight market weakening and are ready to come out with offers only in case of further rate cuts. On the other hand, this week has shown a trend towards a decline in late October sea-river tonnage list, which may turn the market again, some brokers believe.
