## Market Commentary

Negative sentiment is still dominating the small- tonnage shipping market, especially in the Black and Mediterranean Seas. Slow cargo traffic from regional ports does not allow ship owners to even think about any rate upturn, particularly considering an increase in tonnage

Coaster rates stay under pressure amid sharpening surplus of open tonnage in BlSea & Med

list. At the moment, about 180 vessels are open in the Black Sea for the next 10 days, more than 100 ships in the Marmara and Aegean Seas, and more than 150 vessels of 2-12k dwt in the eastern Mediterranean, which is almost 20% more compared to early February. «Good weather and shorter delays [at Sulina, Bosphorus, Constanta, Novorossiysk] affect number of open vessels, putting additional pressure on rates,» a broker commented. In these circumstances, ship owners barely manage to keep rates stable, although charterers do try to lower freights. Thus, rates for transportation of 5-6,000 t of wheat or corn from Reni or Izmail to EgyptMed are still hovering at $36- 37/t, while those for shipment from Constanta or Varna to the Italian Adriatic amount to $25.5-26.5/t bss 1/1.

Meanwhile, charterers have again managed to cut rates in the Azov Sea ($1-2/t down): sea-river owners are

forced to make concessions amid persisting lack of grain and coal cargoes. Shipment of 3-5,000 t of coal from Rostov bb to TBS now costs $40-41/t. However, given shorter downtime in the Kerch Strait, current TCEs remain quite comfortable for sea-river owners, as they exceed OPEX at least 1.5 times.

The North European market is showing mixed picture. The Continent area looks stable as tonnage demand is supported by regular shipments of steel products and other cargoes. Owners are feeling quite confident, and rates hold steady. More uncertainty can be seen in the Baltic Sea. Players report continuing delays in Russian ports due to icebreaker pilotage, but the cargo traffic is too weak to allow ship owners to raise rates. The cargo flow cannot be called brisk in the lower Baltic, but relevant rates are stable considering short tonnage list.

So far, there are no preconditions for higher activity and freight rates on the coaster market, with situation unlikely to change in this respect in the nearest weeks.

Ukr wheat, CFR Adriatic, $/t Freight rate, wheat, 5-6,000t, Reni - Bari, $/t % of freight costs in CFR price

The fact is that demand for grains will hardly grow in Mediterranean countries till the end of the current season, while ongoing decline in export prices for wheat and corn makes any growth of logistic expenses even less probable. Demand for coal remains low in Turkey. The segment for steel products is not showing any light at the end of the tunnel either. Urea and some other types of fertilizers remain the only hope for ship owners, as demand for these cargoes should grow in the EU countries in March-April. According to ISM, ship owners should not expect the rate upturn in the coming weeks (and possibly even months), but they are quite able to preserve the current - by the way, not the worst - levels.
