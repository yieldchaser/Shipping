## Market Commentary

The Black Sea and Adriatic markets remain the most depressed in the small-tonnage segment: the already low activity has decreased slightly further this week. The fact that downtime in Bosporus reached 3-4 days in the last couple of weeks due to adverse weather had almost no

impact on the vessel list in the Marmara and Black Seas as the relevant tonnage surplus has even sharpened. Freight rates ex Ukrainian ports have dropped by $1.5-3/t during the week, while those from Romania and Bulgaria have sagged within $0.5/t. Thus, rates for shipment of 5-6,000 t of wheat from Constanta or Varna to the southern Adriatic have inched down to $25-26/t, while those for transportation of 5-6,000 t of corn from Reni or Izmail to Alexandria have dropped to $34-35/t. The cargo flow looks a bit healthier in the eastern Mediterranean. Tonnage demand in the area is supported by regular - although not very active - shipments of steel products, scrap, fertilizers and minerals. However, local ship owners hardly manage to keep rates stable, particularly considering higher competition: «We need more and more vessels open in Marmara or even BlSea considering ballasting to EMed,» a Turkish broker commented to ISM.

Following a barely noticeable upturn seen last week, the Azov Sea market has started sliding down again. Players note increased roadside transshipment volumes, but weak demand for wheat and coal in Turkey forces sea- river owners to make concessions. Especially this applies to 5-6k dwt vessels as demand for such fleet is noticeably lower than for 3k dwt ships. Over the week, rates have sagged by $1-2/t in the Azov Sea. Shipment of 3-5,000 t of coal from Rostov bb to TBS costs very high $20s/t bss 1/1. However, relevant TCEs are still 70-80% higher than OPEX and remain significantly higher than corresponding levels ex Black Sea ports. In the Caspian Sea, activity continues to decline due to a slowdown of grain shipments from Russia to Iran. Freight rates from Astrakhan ports have dropped by $2-3/ton, while transportation costs from the ports of Kazakhstan and other countries of the region are generally flat.

The market is looking the most favorable - or, rather, the least pessimistic - in North Europe and the western Mediterranean, where ship owners confidently keep rates at quite comfortable levels. This is still mainly conditioned not by cargo traffic, which can hardly be called brisk, but primarily by weather issues and delays, which form a

All hopes of ship-owners and brokers are now put only on the upcoming Easter in European countries and possible slight upturn of cargo traffic ahead of holidays. Frankly speaking, there are few other preconditions for market improvements. Weather conditions will certainly improve in those regions where they remain adverse, which will affect the tonnage list. Among positive points on commodity markets, players only note higher prices for Black Sea corn. Export quotes for wheat, barley, as well as for finished and semi-finished steel products remain under pressure given low demand for all these cargoes from key importers in Turkey, North African countries and the EU. EU demand for Egyptian urea also looks weak; only urea sales from Central Asia via Black Sea ports have perked up, but this is largely unrelated to the coaster sector. ISM believes that in current circumstances, ship owners will manage to keep rates relatively stable in the next couple of weeks, but they will most likely have to make concessions after the Easter holidays.
