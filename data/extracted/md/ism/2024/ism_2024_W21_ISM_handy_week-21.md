## Market Commentary

Handysize freight rates hold steady in ECSA despite brisk ballaster inflow from neighboring regions. “A little more resistance is coming from owners as cargoes are there,” a broker shared with ISM. Meanwhile, the local Supramax/Ultramax market is sagging ($1-2k daily and

North Atlantic market remains under pressure, while SE Asia is the only area standing out for its brisk freight traffic

$1-2/t down on all routes) due to a noticeable shortage of cargo offers. “Decent input of cargoes is still below the inflow of ballasters from different regions,” another broker mentioned.

Negative sentiment prevails in both tonnage groups of the USG market. The cargo/tonnage imbalance forces Handysize and Supramax owners to accept further rate cuts ($1-2k daily and $1-2/t down on all routes). “Rates remain subdued as the tonnage list continues to grow,” a broker commented.

The acute shortage of fresh cargoes remains a key problem for owners working in the Continent and the Mediterranean Sea , especially in the Handysize segment. “We see about 70 Handies for the next 10 days only in Med/BlSea and almost zero spot cargoes,” an experienced EU-broker commented. BreakBulk industry event in the first half of the week had an additional impact on regional

trade (primarily in North Europe ). Over the week, freight rates have dropped by $0.5-1.5k daily and $0.5-2/t, while transportation costs from Ukraine have declined even more significantly, within $2.5/ton. Note that rates for shipments of grain and other cargoes from Ukraine are now only $4- 5/t higher compared to neighboring Romania or Bulgaria, being almost identical to levels ex Russian ports.

This week's holidays in Singapore had a negative impact on SE Asian market picture, but Handysize/ Handymax brokers and owners express restrained optimism, noticing a gradual increase in offer of Chinese steel and CIS coal. Freight rates hold steady this week. The cargo/tonnage ratio looks quite balanced in the regional Supramax/Ultramax segment. Exports of Indonesian coal to China have perked up slightly, while shipments to India have slowed down. Corresponding rates are flat.

In the Indian Ocean , Supramax/Ultramax owners are still keeping rates at previously reached levels despite the start of the monsoon season and slower shipments of ore from ECI as well as of coal from South Africa. Note, however, that owners prefer to ballast their fleet from the PG/WCI area.

### TCT rates dynamics, $/day

In the North Atlantic, Supramax/Ultramax owners will most likely manage to at least stabilize rates thanks to growing cargo offer for June laycans. “We see a lot of enquiry coming for June so we may see rates rebounding soon,” a broker mentioned. Meanwhile, despite steady exports of grain and other cargoes (particularly steel products), Supramax/Ultramax freight levels may sag in ECSA as ballaster inflow will remain quite strong in the next couple of weeks. “Sentiment to remain softish, which is also evident in still negative FFA values,” a broker mentioned.  Handy owners working in ECSA will most likely face hard time as well, as the cargo/tonnage imbalance is likely to persist in the near future. “Expectations for early June remain bearish,” a broker mentioned.

The situation is likely to remain dramatic for owners in both the Continent and the Mediterranean Sea until the start of a new grain season (i.e. at least till early July). Sales of old crop grain are extremely slow given the rise in grain prices, while demand for steel scrap is also low in Turkey.

A slight pick-up in exports of Egyptian and Black Sea urea will hardly have any impact on the overall market picture.

The Indian Ocean market may see a further decline in exports of main cargoes from India due to the monsoon season, which will certainly impact primarily the Supramax/ Ultramax rates. Meanwhile, positive expectations prevail on APAC Handysize/Handymax market. “Seems like there are more and more cargoes after the end of holidays in Singapore. We even expect higher rates in coming days and next week,” an Asian broker shares his expectations.
