## Market Commentary

Activity remains minimal on the small-tonnage freight market, which is quite traditional for the very beginning of January. Thus, the 1H-mid January tonnage list is gradually growing in the Black and Mediterranean Seas, with only Ukrainian grains being relatively actively

offered among all the cargoes. Black Sea freights have sagged by $0.5-2/t, Mediterranean ones within $0.5/t. Rates for shipment of 5-7,000 t of wheat from Reni or Izmail to Alexandria have inched down to low-mid $30s/t, while those for transportation of 10,000 t of corn from Odesa or Chornomorsk to SpanMed have sagged to mid-$40s/t. Charterers already target below $27/t for transportation of 6,000 t of urea from Damietta to SpanMed. Meanwhile, the North European market is still strongly affected by weather conditions: protracted storms in the North Sea and worsening ice conditions in the Baltic area negatively impact fleet turnover, which in turn leads to decreased spot tonnage list. As a result, owners have managed to raise rates by €1-2/t despite weak cargo flow. Thus, shipment of 4,000 t of fertilizers from Klaipeda to N.Spain now costs €44-45/t.

In the Azov Sea , most exporters are still on holidays, so only a relatively small number of open sea-river vessels allows ship owners to keep rates stable «Many vessels were fixed before New Year holidays and will open in 2H January, while the list of spot tonnage is quite short,» a Turkish broker commented. Freight rates have dropped by mere $3-5/t over the week. Note that current TCEs allow sea-river owners to earn at least 2.5 times more than OPEX, considering lower freight rates and still impressive queues in the Kerch Strait.

In the short term, ship owners will most likely manage to stabilize rates in the Black and Mediterranean Sea after the full resumption of trade. However, the market often faces a decline in 2H January, and this development is quite possible this year, as there are no preconditions for higher demand for steel, grain and other main cargoes in Turkey and Mediterranean countries. The North European market may firm up slightly further unless the tonnage list grows (which is quite unlikely, as the cargo offer will also increase upon the return of all major players).
