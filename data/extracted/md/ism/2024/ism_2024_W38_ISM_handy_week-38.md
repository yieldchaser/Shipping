The shortage of spot cargoes has also resulted in lower Supramax/Ultramax rates in the South Atlantic. “Charterers are either fixing quickly to lock in rates or waiting in hopes for lower levels,” a broker mentioned. Only limited inflow of ballasters from WAfr helps to restrain the rate decline (ECSA direction does not look like an attractive option for ship owners these days). Over the week, spot rates for shipments from regional ports have dropped by $0.5-1k daily and $0.5-1/t on all routes. Meanwhile, the Supramax/ Ultramax market activity is growing in the North Atlantic due to brisker exports of coal and grain from the USA, mainly towards the European countries. Transatlantic rates have risen by $0.5-1k daily and $0.5-1/t this week, while front-haul levels hold steady so far. Transportation

of 50,000 t of corn from New Orleans to N.China still costs $57/t.

This week, ship owners' sentiment has improved in the Continent and the Mediterranean Sea thanks to a slight recovery of grain shipments from Ukraine and EU ports, as well as of steel scrap traffic ex EU and Russia. At the same time, long list of open vessels in both regions does not allow ship owners to push rates up noticeably, although some charterers working in the Baltic & Continent even face positional shortage of Supramax vessels open for September dates. By the end of the week, freight levels have added $0.5-1k daily and $0.5-1/t only on some routes. “Most fixing levels are stable, only the bids are going up,” an EU broker commented to ISM.

In SE Asia , Supramax/Ultramax owners have still managed to raise rates by $1-2k daily and within $1/t amid an upturn in coal shipments from Indonesia. On the other hand, Supramax rates have declined within $1k daily and $0.5/t in the Indian Ocean given the shortage of iron ore offers ex ECI, as well as a further increase in tonnage list off WCI/PG/Red Sea.

As for the Handysize/Handymax segment, a slowdown in steel exports from the Far East (with the exception of relatively brisk back-haul trips) during protracted holidays in China allowed charterers to cut freight rates within $1k daily and $0.5/t.

The South Atlantic market sentiment is largely negative looking forward. “The outlook for next week is mitigated by oversupply and lack of support from the cargo side,” a broker mentioned. At the same time, players are more optimistic in the northern part of the basin, as they expect a noticeable increase in grain offer for shipments from the US in October and a further upturn in coal traffic. However, weather may bring some adjustments. “Water level in the Mississippi River is playing a decisive role as it might delay grain shipments forecasted for October,” a broker noted.

In the Black Sea and the Continent, all ship owners' hopes are traditionally linked to the grain season, which usually reaches its peak in October-November, especially with the start of new crop corn exports.

“Mid-Autumn festival has just come to an end but other holidays are coming in less than half a month (Autumn Golden Week), so charterers may want to hasten exports of their cargoes at this time window which is going to make FEast market stronger,” a broker shares his outlook for Asian market development.
