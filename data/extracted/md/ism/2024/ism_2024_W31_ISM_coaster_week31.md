## Market Commentary

The small-tonnage shipping market still sees virtually no encouraging news for ship owners, although we have already left behind a month of new grain season. Note that during 1.5 months, rates ex Ukrainian ports have grown - or rather pushed

### Azov Sea remains the only area with growing coaster rates

from the bottom - by $1-1.5/t on short-haul routes and by $2-2.5/t in long-haul directions; freight levels from other ports have risen within $1/t or have not changed at all. Extremely slow increase in exports of new crop wheat to Mediterranean countries remains the key problem on the market. “Since demand for Ukrainian and Russian grain is relatively brisk in Asian countries, exporters are pushing prices up, which puts even more pressure on much more complicated inter-Med trade in the coaster sector,” an experienced broker of grain traders commented to ISM. It is worth noting that fertilizer offer from Egyptian ports has once again fallen to a minimum, adding to an already difficult situation in the eastern Mediterranean. “Looks like the producers are asking for too much money and the actual market is waiting for a correction plus

some factories are still on maintenance, so the actual output right now is much less and they don't have pressure to sell,” a market participant mentioned. The tonnage list has declined somewhat on the market due to increased demand for sea-river fleet in the Azov Sea, but the general imbalance remains considerable both in the Black Sea and the Mediterranean area: more than 100 vessels of 2-4k dwt, about 150 units of 4-8k dwt and more than 40 ships of 8-12k dwt are open for 1H August from Gibraltar to the Kerch Strait. Rates for shipment of 5-6,000 t of wheat or corn from Izmail or Odesa to Marmara are still hovering at $16.5-17/t, while those for transportation of 5-6,000 t of steels from Marmara or Nemrut Bay to Tunisia are voiced at very high $10s/t.

Despite numerous forecasts, Azov Sea remains the only region where owners manage to raise freight rates little by little. The fact that the majority of wheat sales in the last month have been made to MENA countries, leading to an increase in trip duration; thus, some positional shortage of vessels can be seen in

Thus, shipment of 3,000 t of corn or peas or bran from Rostov or Azov to Marmara now costs $22.5-23/t. Current freight levels allow owners to at least cover the OPEX in case ballast trips are not too long.

Activity remains extremely weak on the North European coaster market. The summer lull continues in the basin. Players note only a slight increase in cargo offer towards the Mediterranean area (mainly Turkey), which is generating interest among Turkish ship owners who had previously gone north. The tonnage list is quite long in all parts of the basin, with

indicative rates staying at previous marks. However, seeking to ensure employment, ship owners are often ready to make concessions when discussing deals in highly competitive environment.

There are few preconditions for any market improvements for the time being. Ship owners cannot count on significant rate upturns till October.
