## Market Commentary

Another uneasy week on the small-tonnage shipping market has brought radically opposite emotions to owners of sea-river and sea-going tonnage.

Sea-river owners have to sharply moderate their appetites, while seagoing market keeps firming up

month, sea-river owners had to sharply moderate their appetites and agree to rate cuts in the Azov Sea. This is caused by a significant decline in wheat shipments after the Turkish government announced new requirements to local importers (now only 15% of total purchases can be made abroad, while at least 85% should be bought from TMO). By the end of the week, rates for grain transportation have sagged by $4-8/t in the Azov Sea, those for shipments of coal by $2-4/t. Thus, rates for shipment of 3,000 t of wheat from Rostov/Azov to Marmara have declined to $33- 34/t. Note that coal exporters took heart and are now able to compete for tonnage with grain traders as well as with charterers of other high-margin cargoes.

The market picture is particularly interesting in the Black Sea , where players note rather modest number of open spot vessels, especially those of 3-4k dwt ready to take cargoes ex Ukraine. “It is still not clear if all vessels have been really fixed during recent couple of weeks or many owners simply hide their positions, heating the market further, but it is certainly much more difficult to find a vessel these days,” a Ukrainian broker commented to ISM. The situation on the commodity market for corn is also providing significant support to freight rates for shipments from Ukraine: the increase in export prices seen during the last two weeks gives more opportunities to both suppliers and ship owners. Finally, some players consider another Russian missile and drone strikes on Ukraine's port infrastructure to be the third factor of growth, but ISM, together with brokers, believes that this factor is the least significant, which is indirectly confirmed by an upturn in freights ex neighboring Romania and Bulgaria. Over the week, rates for westward shipments from Ukraine have added $2-

3/t, while those to the eastern Mediterranean and Turkey have grown within $1.5/t. Freight levels ex Romania, Bulgaria and TBS have increased by $0.5- 1/t, while rates from Russia are virtually unchanged, affected by the trend shift in the neighboring Azov Sea. Thus, rates for transportation of 3-5,000 t of corn from Izmail or Reni to EC Greece have inched up to $26-27/t, while those for shipment of 5-6,000 t of wheat from CVB to the Italian Adriatic have increased to $22-23/t.

The tonnage list looks much longer in the Mediterranean Sea than in the Black Sea, but even local ship owners are trying to push freight rates up due to the impulse from neighboring region. However, charterers are standing their ground, so in fact we are first of all seeing increased bid/offer gap, while fixing levels have grown merely within $0.5/t.

The North European coaster market also continues to show gradual improvements, but existing cargo traffic is absolutely insufficient for any considerable rate upturn. Similar to the neighboring Mediterranean Sea, North European freights have inched up by €0.5/t on average this week.

It will be difficult for sea-river owners to stop the rate decline in the Azov Sea considering new Turkish regulations for imported wheat. Russian wheat shipments from shallow water ports are likely to stay minimal. Meanwhile, further rate growth is quite likely in the Black and Mediterranean Seas, especially if demand for new crop Ukrainian corn continues to strengthen in the Mediterranean countries. Increased prices and slightly brisker purchases of square billets by Turkey should also play into the hands of ship owners. In addition, sales of Egyptian urea have perked up on the chemical market, which should also support the Mediterranean sector. The North European market is again seeing increased number of COA trades, which may take some vessels from the spot market and lead to a further increase in freight rates.
