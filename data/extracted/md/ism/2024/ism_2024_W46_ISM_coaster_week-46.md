## Market Commentary

The Black Sea coaster shipping market continues to soften this week as grain offer for spot shipments from Ukraine - being the market driver - has decreased almost to a minimum, which is referred to both the Global Grain event in Geneva held in the middle of

the week and still extremely unfavorable conditions on the export market. “Having shown a fresh cargo offer from Ukraine, we received 7 vessel positions from ship owners within a couple of hours, which we actually haven't seen for a long time,” a Ukrainian broker commented. Shipments from the EU and Turkish ports remain sporadic, while the already moderate cargo traffic from Russia has also dropped to a minimum this week. There is a surplus of open vessels on the market, especially in the 7-12k dwt segment; more than 40 such ships are open for 2H November in the Black and Marmara Seas only, while the number of cargo offers is almost twice as small. Over the week, freight rates for shipments from Ukraine have sagged by another $0.5-1.5/t, while transportation costs from other ports of the basin have inched

down within $0.5/t. For instance, rates for shipment of 3-4,000 t of corn or soybeans from Izmail or Reni to Marmara have inched down to $21-22/t, while those for transportation of 6-7,000 t of corn from Odesa or Chornomorsk to the Italian Adriatic have declined to low $30s/t. The Mediterranean market picture looks a bit better due to some upturn in urea shipments from Egypt, which helped ship owners keep rates stable. In general, however, the situation remains pessimistic for local owners, especially in WMed, where ship owners increasingly consider ballasting to EMed or the Continent amid an acute cargo shortage.

The sentiment of sea-river owners stays negative in the Azov Sea amid slow exports of Russian wheat to Turkey. Only worsening weather conditions and upcoming ice campaign allow owners to resist further rate cuts. Only the owners working in the Caspian Sea manage to preserve positive momentum due to healthy grain demand in Iran. This week, rates have added another $1-2/t in this area.

The North European market still looks unexciting. “Seems ship owners can only feel nostalgia for good old days, when October-December was a period of undisputable increase in freight rates all across the Baltic Sea,” an EU broker commented. The cargo traffic is now supported only by shipments of agri products, pellets, as well as by timid steel exports. Ship owners struggle to keep rates unchanged.

*** Despite the fact that maximum activity and rising rates are quite typical for late fall, most players are not optimistic. Moreover, many agree that coaster owners will be facing problems even keeping rates stable in late November-early December. The fact is that CFR prices for wheat and corn remain low, preventing Ukrainian and European suppliers from selling. Ship owners may only benefit from some positional recovery upon the return of grain traders from Geneva. As for other cargoes, demand for square billets stays minimal in Turkey. Urea exports from North Africa are declining again after a recent surge: “Prices corrected sharply, so now demand has died again; people are trying to cover what they sold short, but afterwards sales will be difficult again,” a large fertilizer charterer commented to ISM. As for the Baltic and Azov Seas, only worsening weather conditions may push freights up in these regions.
