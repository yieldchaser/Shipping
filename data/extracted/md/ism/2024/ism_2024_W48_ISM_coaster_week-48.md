## Market Commentary

Despite the upcoming holidays, only the North European small-tonnage market is showing some signs of a traditional pre-Christmas rally. Spot cargo offer is gradually increasing in the region, while brisker shipments of contract cargoes are also taking part of tonnage from the market. Moreover, weather

conditions affect the region, reducing the availability of spot/prompt tonnage. In these circumstances, ship owners manage to raise rates further. Thus, freights ex EU ports have inched up by €0.5-2/t. Meanwhile, rates for shipments from Russian ports have grown much more significantly, by €2-5/t. Note that TCEs for vessels ready to work from Russia are now at least 2 times higher than those for shipments ex EU ports. Thus, charterers offer €27/t for transportation of 3-3,500 t of flax seeds from Estonia to Ghent, but owners' ideas are voiced at low €30s/t or even higher (gives the TCE closer to $1.5k daily vs. $2-2.2k daily bss RV for a 4 dwt vessel); owners are seeking to get about €50/t for shipment of 3-3,500 t of flax seeds from St. Petersburg to ARA ports (gives the TCE of about $5k daily bss RV for a 4 dwt vessel).

Meanwhile, there is virtually no good news for ship owners working in the Black and Mediterranean Seas. “Market still looks quite weak, which is definitely strange thing having on mind we are already quite significantly into the winter period; never been like this in the end of November, but still charterers are relatively easily getting the rates they want,” an experienced Croatian broker commented to ISM. It should be noted that a slight increase in offer of grain for transportation from Ukraine to Turkey and Greece has practically no impact on the overall negative market tone. “We see slightly less 5-10k dwt vessels open in BlSea and Marmara this week, but market is still overtonnaged, with lack of firm wheat and corn offers,” a Ukrainian broker notes. In addition, fertilizer shipments from Egypt have slowed down again, although they were previously supporting the market. By the end of the week, rates for shipments from Ukraine have dropped by another $0.5-1/t and stabilized, probably having reached their bottom at
