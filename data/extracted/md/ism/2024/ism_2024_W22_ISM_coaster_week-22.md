## Market Commentary

The situation remains critical for ship owners working on the small-tonnage shipping market given long list of open vessels in almost every section. As expected, a barely noticeable upturn in shipments of Egyptian fertilizers and Russian steel had no impact

on the overall market picture. Moreover, rates have sagged by another $0.5-2/t in the Mediterranean Sea by the end of the week. Thus, rates for shipment of 5-6,000 t of steels from Marmara to Algeria have already sagged to $27/t given 2,000x/800x l/d rates, while transportation costs of 10-12,000 t of urea from Arzew to Savona have inched down to $13-14/t.

Sea-river owners working from Russian Azov Sea ports also have to make concessions amid the shortage of spot grain and coal offers. Particularly acute shortage of cargoes is reported in the segment for 5-6k vessels: corresponding rates have decreased most noticeably, by $2-3/t (against the decline of $1- 2/t in the segment for about 3k dwt fleet).

The Black Sea market looks unchanged. Relevant freight rates seem to have reached their bottom last week; especially this applies to shipments from Ukraine. “Many Arabian owners of overaged vessels already avoid discussing grain shipments from Ukraine, preferring to switch to other businesses or stay in dead spot rather than accept further rate cuts,” a Ukrainian charterer commented. Thus, shipment of 5-7,000 t of corn or soybeans from Izmail or Odesa to EgyptMed still costs $21-22/t. Among positive news for owners, we can only mention a barely noticeable recovery of steel shipments to Turkey.

The North European market is gradually entering the typical stage of summer lull. By the end of the week, freights have dropped by €0.5-2/t in the area; note, however, that owners working in North Europe still manage to bring their earnings above the level of running costs, unlike those in the Mediterranean and especially the Black Sea.

There are virtually no preconditions for higher market activity. Even a possible further increase in Turkish demand for Black Sea square billets will hardly offset the impact of slow trade in the segments for grains and finished steel products. Moreover, next week many European players will be off the market, visiting Posidonia and plenty of shipbrokers' parties in Athens.
