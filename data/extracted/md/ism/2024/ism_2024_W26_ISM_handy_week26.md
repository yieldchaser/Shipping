## Market Commentary

The Handysize shipping market continues to soften in ECSA . Freight rates are sliding down amid sharpening cargo/tonnage imbalance in the region. «We see further softening, with limited enquiry and growing tonnage list,» a broker mentioned. By the end of the week, relevant

Handysize market is firming up in USG, Continent and Med, with Supra/ Ultra segment staying under pressure all across the board

rates have dropped by $1k daily and $1-2/t on all routes. Thus, rates for shipment of 25-30,000 t of soybeans or corn from Santos to ARA ports have sagged to $31.5-32.5/t. Meanwhile, ship owners working ex USG ports are still enjoying growing market. «The lack of tonnage continues to drive the strength out of the USG area,» a broker commented. Ship owners are asking for premiums for short trips, preferring to sign deals for longer transatlantic or front-haul shipments. By the end of the week, rates have added $0.5-1k daily and $1-2/t. In the Mediterranean Sea and the Continent, positive sentiment also prevails among Handysize/Handymax owners thanks to a gradual increase in grain offer for shipments from the EU, Ukraine and Russia in 2H July. However, trade has slowed down slightly closer to the end of the week given the Istanbul Shipbrokers dinner. Freight rates have risen within $2/t in the Mediterranean Sea and within $0.5/t in the Continent. Thus, rates for transportation of 25-30,000 t of wheat or corn from CVB to SpanMed are already hovering at $18- 19/t, while those from Ukraine at $24-25/t. The situation

with shipments from Russian Baltic Sea ports looks the most uncertain as corresponding rates vary significantly. Players refer this to somewhat limited tonnage list for such shipments; besides, the cargo traffic from this part of the basin looks relatively brisk these days (mainly due to fertilizers).

Meanwhile, gradually growing Supramax/Ultramax vessel list in the Atlantic Ocean allows charterers to confidently keep rates stable, and sometimes to cut freight levels (particularly this applies to front-haul shipments from ECSA: relevant rates have sagged by $0.5-1k daily and $1-2/t). In USG, ship owners still manage to keep spot rates unchanged due to regular shipments of petcoke and coal, while Mediterranean freight levels are stable thanks to shipments of construction cargoes and grain.

Thus, demand for Supramax/Ultramax fleet in the Asia-Pacific region is supported by relatively brisk traffic of Indonesian coal and various cargoes from the Far East.

The Handysize/Handymax market generally looks inactive; exports of main cargoes from Australia and shorter tonnage list in SE Asia are helping ship owners keep rates stable.

The Indian Ocean Supramax/Ultramax market is pressured by a slowdown of South African coal exports and ore shipments from ECI, as well as by an increase in vessel list off PG/WCI. However, offers of minerals from the PG as well as of salt from WCI are regularly voiced by charterers, helping to balance the market.

Despite the current negative trends in ECSA, players are optimistic and believe that there is still something to look forward to, as the ECSA grain season us underway, with plenty of cargoes to come in Q2 and Q3. «Many deals are discussed for late July - 1H August,» a broker mentioned. Meanwhile, freight rates may go down in the northern part of the basin. Charterers are actively lowering rates when discussing deals for 2H July. «The picture for 2H July is still a bit blurry, but charterers are waiting for rates to come down,» a broker mentioned.

In the Black Sea, tonnage demand will most likely be strengthening gradually with the start of the new grain season, but the situation is unlikely to improve significantly considering limited demand and lower export prices for wheat. The North European Supramax shipping market may even soften a little bit. Thus, deals with late July laycans are being discussed with operators at rates slightly below the current market levels.

In the eastern hemisphere, the Supramax/Ultramax market outlook does not look very rosy. A gradual slowdown of Indonesian coal shipments cannot be ruled out in the Asia-Pacific region. As for the Indian Ocean, the monsoon may hinder imports to Bangladesh, while the tonnage list may grow off PG/WCI.

«Handysize market looks quiet overall this week. Maybe next week it will be more resilient,» a broker shares some wishful thinking.
