## Market Commentary

Thus, rates for shipment of 25-30,000 t of wheat from Upriver to Algeria have inched down to $40/t given 7,000x/3,000x l/d rates.

Despite increased cargo offer for Supramax/Ultramax shipments from the USA with 2H November laycans, the surplus of vessels remains significant in the North Atlantic , leading to a drop in rates ex USG by $1-3k daily and $1- 3/t. Spot Handysize tonnage list has not grown since last week, but demand is extremely low, and freight levels have declined by $1k daily and $1-2/t on all routes. For instance, transportation of 25-30,000 t of fertilizers from Tampa to N.Brazil now costs $21/t.

A major industry event in Hamburg has had a negative impact on the already low fixing activity in the

Continent and the Mediterranean Sea . The 2H November vessel count looks excessive in both regions, while worsening picture on grain and scrap commodity markets (a decline in CIF grain prices in North African and Asian countries, as well as in scrap quotes in Turkey) is putting additional pressure on trade and, consequently, on the freight market. Freight rates on key

routes have dropped by $0.5-1k daily and $0.25-1.5/t over the week. Thus, most charterers are not ready to pay more than $20/t for shipment of 25-30,000 t of corn from POC to EgyptMed or Iskenderun.

As for the eastern hemisphere, negative trends persist on the Indian Ocean Supramax/Ultramax market as well. Shipments of main cargoes from PG/WCI ports stay limited even after the end of Diwali celebrations in India. In addition, exports of South African manganese ore have slowed down noticeably: “There are almost no cargoes for November laycans,” a broker dramatizes. The number of vessels open both off PG/WCI and S.Afr has grown, while freight rates have decreased by $1-2k daily and $0.5-1.5/t this week.

### TCT rates dynamics, $/day

The Asia - Pacific market has also softened amid a further decline in shipments of Far Eastern steel and Indonesian coal. “There are still few enquiries on the market overall,” a broker commented on SE Asian Handysize/ Handymax sector. Freight rates have sagged by $1k daily and $0.5/t on average in the basin.

Next week, negative trends will most likely continue to prevail in all parts of the western hemisphere. “Without a further influx of fresh requests, the outlook is negative for next week,” a broker mentioned. The fact is that market fundamentals are not favorable for positive outlook: grain, metallurgical and chemical markets are seeing a decrease in prices for the vast majority of cargoes given limited demand in importing countries. Only positive trends in the Capesize segment can be called a supportive factor for ship owners, as they may become a harbinger of improvement in other tonnage groups. In addition, activity is likely to perk up slightly in North Europe and the Mediterranean area with the return of players from the Eisbeinessen.

The Indian Ocean Supramax/Ultramax market is likely to stay under pressure considering negative prospects for South African manganese ore traffic for the most of November and no signs of improvement in the PG/WCI area.
