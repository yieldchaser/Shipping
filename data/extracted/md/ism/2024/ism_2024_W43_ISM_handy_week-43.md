## Market Commentary

This week, USG can be called a hotspot, which supports all neighboring freight sections with its positive impetus. Growing number of grain and petcoke requests for Supramax/Ultramax shipments from USA to Asia allowed owners to raise rates by $0.5-1k daily and $1-

1.5/t on front-haul routes. Thus, shipment of 50,000 t of corn or soybeans from New Orleans to N.China now costs high $50s/t. At the same time, transatlantic freight levels hold steady. Positive trends also prevail in the USG Handy segment, but improvements are mainly positional and caused by a temporary lack of ships in spot positions. Thus, owners with vessels in spot positions have managed to get a minor premium for being lucky. Over the week, spot rates for shipments from regional ports have risen by $0.5k daily and $1/t.

Handysize rates are climbing up in ECSA as well amid the decline in number of vessels claiming cargoes from Brazil and Argentina (including due to the recovery of cargo traffic from WAfr, which absorbs part of ballasters). By the end of the week, spot freight rates have added $1k daily and $1-2/t. Rates for transportation of 25-30,000 t of corn or soybeans from Santos to ARAG are already hovering at $31-32/t. Meanwhile, the situation remains tense in the

Supramax/Ultramax segment, as most spot requests have already been covered and mainly offers for late November shipments emerge these days. However, freight levels are stable this week.

Both Handysize and Supramax markets look rather versatile and positional in the Mediterranean Sea and the Continent. On one hand, the cargo flow does not look very active in both regions, especially as for this period of time. On the other hand, the tonnage list is also moderate, which makes charterers be in tone. “Generally, the market is still fairly flat, with more than enough open tonnage, but owners are trying to play tough until they become spot,” an EU broker commented. During the week, freight rates have not changed much on key routes, while the number of deals has decreased slightly. Note that strong USG market plays into the hands of ship owners as charterers of US cargoes are increasingly competing for vessels open in the Western Mediterranean and the Continent, which supports freight levels. This week, rates on some routes have grown by $0.5k daily and $0.5/t. For instance, rates for shipment of 25-30,000 t of scrap (sf 55-60`) from ARAG to Nemrut Bay have inched up to $26-27/t.

Handysize/Handymax rates are so far largely unchanged in the Asia-Pacific region , but tonnage is gradually building up off Australia. At the same time, the offer of Australian cargoes, NOPAC grain and Far Eastern steel has declined. Meanwhile, charterers have managed to push Supramax/Ultramax rates down by another $1- 2k daily and $1-1.5/t amid a slowdown in exports of Indonesian coal and Far Eastern steel. “Far Eastern steel market is not as active as last week so there is a slight drop in rates. Since Supra rates from ECSA are also dropping, BH trips are not so attractive,” a local broker commented to ISM.

Supramax/Ultramax owners working in the Indian Ocean have managed to resist more successfully thanks to lively cargo traffic from South Africa. At the same time, PG/ WCI's main cargo exports are steadily limited.

The Atlantic market outlook is positive into next week. Market fundamentals are slowly but steadily moving to the side of ship owners. The USG market will surely remain the driving force and ECSA will be increasingly supported by WAfr cargo traffic: “More Port Kamsar and Guinea activity will make the area hot for the coming weeks,” a broker mentioned.

Meanwhile, there are few preconditions for higher activity and rates in the Mediterranean Sea and the Continent, especially considering the gradual decline in prices for Black Sea corn and wheat, which nevertheless does not allow local exporters to compete with Brazilian corn suppliers on the Mediterranean market and Australian wheat exporters on the Asian market. “Don't see much hope for the Handies, not enough cargoes to cover overflow,” an EU broker shared his expectations of the Continent market. The local market may be supported only by the possible tonnage outflow to the Atlantic Ocean (especially in the Supramax segment).

Players expect steel exports from the Far East to stay limited amid weaker demand outlook, which will continue to put pressure on SE Asian and Far Eastern freight markets. Ship owners located in the Indian Ocean will be having more problems standing their ground due to the upcoming Diwali holiday in India.
