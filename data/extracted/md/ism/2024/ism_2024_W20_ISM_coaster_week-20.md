## Market Commentary

The small-tonnage shipping market is still seeing no light at the end of the tunnel. Particularly this applies to the Black Sea and Mediterranean markets. Ship owners increasingly discuss laying off their vessels, but the vast majority of them decide to be patient, expecting market

conditions to improve in 1.5-2 months with the start of the new grain season. “Many owners increasingly discuss partial loading with smaller lots, others consider long ballast legs or staying idle waiting for cargoes,” a Turkish broker commented to ISM. In the Mediterranean Sea , the already slow cargo traffic has even declined slightly further over the week due to a decrease in fertilizer shipments (which used to support the local market at least slightly). Freight rates have sagged by another $0.5-1/t in both basins. Thus, rates for shipment of 5-7,000 t of corn from Izmail or Odesa to EgyptMed have inched down to $22- 23/t, while those for transportation of 10-12,000 t of wheat from Constanta or Varna to S.Adriatic have declined to $18-19/t bss 1/1. Despite the fact that Ukrainian grain is still the most popular cargo in the region, estimated TCEs for shipments from Ukraine increasingly turn out to be lower compared to shipments from EU, Russian or Turkish ports. According to ISM estimates, even in case of minimal

ballast trip, current freight rates ex Ukrainian ports are not even close to OPEX levels, while owners are still able to cover the OPEX working with cargoes from other Black Sea and Mediterranean ports.

Ship owners working in North Europe are also suffering mounting pressure. The cargo/tonnage imbalance is worsening, weighing on freight rates. Freights for shipments on all key routes have dropped by another €0.5-1.5/t. Shipment of 3-4,000 t of steels from St-Petersburg to ARA ports now costs €41-42/t. However, local owners are still feeling better in terms of earnings compared to the neighboring Mediterranean Sea.

The Azov Sea market is the only one showing a freight rate growth this week ($1-2/t up). According to ISM, this came as a bit of a surprise to most market participants and may be caused by some positional increase in activity after a series of holidays in Russia.
