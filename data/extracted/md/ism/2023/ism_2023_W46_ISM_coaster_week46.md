## Market Commentary

Negative sentiment continues to dominate the small-tonnage shipping markets of the Black and Mediterranean Seas due to persisting noticeable tonnage surplus. However, ship owners have finally managed to stop the decline in rates ex Danube ports, as well as

Mediterranean small-tonnage market remains under pressure, while rates inched up in Azov and Baltic Seas

ex Black Sea ports of EU, Turkey and Russia. «Seems the market has switched from downward trend to so called sideways trend this week,» a broker focused on Danube shipments commented to ISM. Note that the gap in fixing rates on same routes from Ukraine (both Danube and deep-water ports) sometimes exceeds $10/t, which can only indicate that the market is very positional, and its current state is very differently perceived by various charterers. Meanwhile, Mediterranean freight rates have dropped by another $0.5-2/t on all key routes as trade remains very limited in the basin, while the decrease in bunker fuel prices only adds to charterers' arguments during negotiations. At the moment, rates for shipment of 5-6,000 t of urea from Damietta to Ravenna are hovering at $27.5-28.5/t, while those for transportation of 5-6,000 t of steels from Marmara to Algeria at low $30s/t bss 1/1.

In North Europe, owners continue to aggressively push for rate hikes after a slight increase in 1H November. The paradox is that the market still does not show any hints of activity growth, and weather is the only reason for such owners' behavior as it negatively affects fleet turnover and leads to positional shortage of vessels. «Owners asking higher numbers to minimize the weather risk, but I am not sure if they always succeed to get higher rates at the end,» an EU broker commented. Based on the fixtures seen by ISM, rates have actually not changed much over the week.

Meanwhile, sea-river owners have managed to build on their success and insist on another rate upturn in the Azov Sea given some excitement that arose around shipments of Russian wheat. The matter is that many exporters of durum wheat stepped up shipments ahead of the export ban scheduled for December 1. Many ship owners have brilliantly taken advantage of the situation and managed to get rates more than $10/t higher than levels seen in early November. On average, grain transportation costs have added $7-9/t during the week. Following the general trend, rates for shipments of coal and other cargoes have also risen by $4-5/t. Shipment

of 3-5,000 t of wheat from Rostov to Marmara now costs $59/t.

In the short-term, there are still almost no preconditions for improvements in the Black and Mediterranean Seas. Demand for square billets has stabilized in Turkey after a barely noticeable increase, while demand for Turkish finished steel products remains minimal in MENA countries and Europe. Despite ample offer of Egyptian urea and gradual price cuts, importers' interest remains low. «Going forward, if the situation in Med region remains as it is, rates will even get under stronger pressure,» a Turkish broker commented. The market growth will hardly be protracted in the Azov Sea. Sea-river owners can at best count to raise rates slightly further next week, after which freights are very likely to sag in early December (even despite the start of ice campaign). In North Europe, ship owners will manage to raise rates somewhat if the cargo traffic perks up at least slightly ahead of Christmas holidays.
