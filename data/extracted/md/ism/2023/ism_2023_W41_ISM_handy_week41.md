Positive sentiment still prevails on Atlantic Handy/Supra market, with Continent looking like a hotspot

At the same time, the transatlantic segment is stable amid insufficient number of cargo offers. Thus, shipment of up to 50,000 t of corn from New Orleans to Alexandria still costs $30/t. Meanwhile, the local Handysize market has firmed up slightly thanks to ongoing shortage of fleet in USEC as well as some increase in cargo offer ex USG for late October laycans.

Despite the fact that spot grain offers increasingly enter the ECSA market, charterers have been slow to voice ideas, waiting for a clearer market picture. «Demand for end October dates ex ECSA has started to pick up, but not much fixing activity,» a broker mentioned. By the end of the week, Smx/Umx transportation costs ex regional ports have not changed much. Rates for transportation of 50,000 t of corn or soybeans from Santos to S.China are hovering at $43-44/t. Meanwhile, positive trends are reported in the

Handy segment. Further market strengthening in N.Brazil allowed ship owners to raise spot rates in the whole region (including those for shipments from S.Brazil and Argentina). This week, spot rates have increased by $1k daily and $1-2/t.

The Baltic & Continent Supramax/Ultramax market has strengthened much more noticeably than that in the Atlantic Ocean. Some excitement is again seen in the region. Note that some charterers have even switched from Supramax shipments to large Handy lots. As a result, rates have risen by $2-3k daily and $1-4/t. Thus, rates for shipment of 40-45,000 t of scrap (sf 55-60`) from Ghent to Turkey have increased by $2-3/t, to $23/t. However, many experienced charterers are skeptical about current situation: «Always the same positional story when operators have to perform booked cargoes and not many ships around are pushing the market up; once the operators have covered (mostly with a big loss in their calculation), the market will ease again». The North European Handysize sector looks much weaker. The regional tonnage list clearly exceeds demand. By contrast, negative trends generally prevail in the Black and Mediterranean Seas despite brisk offer of Ukrainian grain, especially in the Handysize segment. The fact is that grain shipments from EU ports continue to decline, while other cargoes are extremely scarce (except for Russian grain and Turkish construction cargoes). Rates on non-premium routes have dropped by $0.5-1k daily and $0.25-1/t during the week. At the same time, ship owners have managed to stop the decline in voyage rates for transportation from Ukraine, while time-charter levels are often $5-8k daily lower than time-charter equivalents, which certainly pleases most operators.

No major trade surge can be seen in the Asia-Pacific region after holidays in China. Similar to the neighboring Panamax/Kamsarmax tonnage group, Supramax shipments of Indonesian coal to India remain limited, while traffic to China has grown very insignificantly. Rates remain unchanged. Meanwhile, brisk exports of Australian cargoes remain the key driver on the SE Asian Handysize market. Note that in contrast to the Black Sea market, the

difference between TCT rates and actual TC-equivalents has decreased (often to less than $1k daily).

Despite a slight decrease in exports of main cargoes from PG/WCI, freight rates are generally flat in the Indian Ocean. Market participants explain this primarily by healthy demand for fleet from South African coal charterers.

ECSA rates are likely to go up next week as the cargo offer for October and November laycans continues to

grow. «There are many cargoes for November, though operators are not ready to offer discounts vs. spot market, which creates positive sentiment for the coming weeks,» a broker mentioned. The North Atlantic market is also expected to strengthen further as demand looks healthy looking forward, especially on F/H routes.

ISM sources share mixed opinions about short- term developments in North Europe. «All a bit unclear and very hard to predict what Supra rates will do in the next two weeks, but once again I feel it pushed up to high too fast and new sales will not be concluded on the trading side at these high freight rates,» an experienced EU charterer commented. At the same time, players do not rule out a slight strengthening of the Handysize segment as charterers are increasingly reducing or splitting Supra cargo lots. On the other hand, vessels opening in the western Mediterranean and not ready to deal with Ukrainian exports are increasingly competing for shipments from the Continent, which casts doubt on a possible rate upturn. In the Black Sea, ship owners can hardly count on any rate growth without another surge of Ukrainian grain shipments because the situation is unfavorable on the main commodity markets. First of all, this applies to the market for semi-finished and finished steel products: demand for Turkish and Russian steel is extremely weak. As for the grain market, prices continue to decline amid tough competition, which is also unlikely to push transportation costs of traders up.

In Asia-Pacific region, weak demand for Indonesian coal in India may continue to be the main stumbling block for ship owners. However, many of them are counting on increased activity in NOPAC. The matter is that Mississippi River draft is decreasing, which may force traders to reorient cargo flows through NOPAC ports.
