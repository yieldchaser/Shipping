## Market Commentary

Exports of US grain and petcoke continue to gain momentum, keeping the USG market the main and in fact the only driver of the whole western hemisphere. Supramax/Ultramax tonnage list for November dates remains limited in the area, with the shortage of Handysize

Handy and Supra/Ultra markets keep softening in all sections except USG, which remains the only hotspot

fleet mainly seen in USEC (similar to last month). «Supramax segments are busy mainly on front-haul routes, while Handy vessels are performing mostly transatlantic and inter-Caribs shipments,» a broker commented. By the end of the week, spot Supramax rates have risen by $1-2k daily and $1-2/t, while Handy levels have inched up by $0.5k daily and $1/t. Rates for shipment of 50,000 t of corn from New Orleans to Alexandria have already inched up to $34- 35/t. while those for transportation of 50,000 t of petcoke from Houston to Pipavav have risen to $54/t.

Meanwhile, the ECSA Supramax market is weakening amid increased vessel list for November dates in the region and worsening market conditions in SAfr. Ship owners struggled to keep rates stable, citing the outflow of carriers from NCSA and WAfr to USG, but they still have to make concessions. «We have yet to see more optimistic fixtures, positivity is fragile,» a broker mentioned. Over the week, spot rates have decreased by $0.5-1k daily and

$1/t. The Handy segment has also softened noticeably amid sharpening cargo/tonnage imbalance. Activity has decreased in N.Brazil and stopped supporting the owners. This week, Handysize rates have dropped by $2k daily and $2-3/t. Thus, the transportation cost of 25-30,000 t of wheat from Upriver to Bejaia has decreased to $37/t given 7,000x/5,000x l/d rates.

In the Black and Mediterranean Seas , as well as in the Continent, this week has been marked by major industry events (Global Grain in Geneva and Eisbein in Hamburg). Trade has slowed down noticeably in both regions, especially in the Black Sea, where, among other things, the Russian missile attack on a vessel moving along the Ukrainian grain corridor caused a lot of confusion in the market. Ship owners are now trying to get extra premium for shipments from Ukraine, quoting them $2-3/t higher than last week (citing $100-200k increase in EWRI and crew bonus), but traders are not ready to accept any freight rate increase, seeing the overall negative market tone. «Black Sea market disappeared basically, EMed shows very low activity,» an EU-based operator commented to ISM. Handysize rates in the Black Sea and eastern Mediterranean have sagged by $2-3k daily and $1-3/t over

the week, while Supramax levels have dropped slightly less significantly (within $1.5k daily and $2/t). At the same time, Supramax rates have even inched up in the western Mediterranean and are now $4-5k daily above levels seen in EMed. «WMed rates staying much higher thanks to hot USG market,» an operator explained. The same USG market has also supported the stabilization of Supramax/ Ultramax rates in the Continent . In addition, shipments of European steel scrap to Turkey have perked up slightly. At the same time, Handysize rates have sagged by another $0.5-1k daily and $0.5-1/t amid low tonnage demand in the Baltic and Continent. For instance, shipment of 25,000 t of scrap (sf 55-58`) from Avonmouth to Turkey now costs $27/t.

Despite quite brisk exports of Indonesian coal, Handysize and Supramax owners are still unable to stop the rate decline in SE Asia given the inflow of vessels from neighboring regions (in particular, from stagnating Far East, as well as from NOPAC and Indian Ocean). Rates in SE Asia have sagged within $1/t and $1-2k daily.

### Speaking of the Indian Ocean section, relevant freight

rates also continue sliding down amid weakening exports of South African coal and major PG cargoes. Meanwhile, the tonnage list remains ample off both coasts of India. Time-charter rates have dropped by $1-3k daily, while transportation costs of main cargoes have declined by $1- 2.5/t.

Next week, the South Atlantic market will most probably be staying under pressure as the cargo/tonnage ratio for 2H November dates is clearly on the charterers' side. However, the situation may change closer to late

November as grain requests with December laycans increasingly enter the market, giving optimism to ship owners looking forward. Meanwhile, the rate growth may slow down in the USG amid a noticeable increase in ballaster inflow from other regions (especially from the western Mediterranean).

Activity is likely to grow slightly in North Europe and the Mediterranean Sea next week upon the return of multiple players from the Global Grain and Eisbein. However, considering a significant surplus of tonnage, ship owners can hardly expect anything more than just stabilization of rates. There are no preconditions for any significant growth of cargo traffic. European grain remains unattractive for importers, while Ukrainian exports via deep-water ports are in doubt after the tragic incident; demand for Russian and Turkish finished and semi-finished steel products is minimal. Ship owners can only hope for increasing demand for steel scrap in Turkey.

In the Eastern Hemi, prospects for Supramax/ Ultramax owners are not very optimistic either. Particularly this is due to the upcoming Diwali celebrations in India starting November 12, which will last almost the whole next week. In APAC, Indonesian coal exports will support tonnage demand as China has yet to replenish stocks, and Indonesian coal prices look the most attractive (even compared to domestic market prices). However, this cargo flow alone will hardly be sufficient to allow ship owners to keep rates stable in the whole basin considering long tonnage list and the inflow of ballasters from all neighboring sections.
