## Market Commentary

Despite the fact that half a month has passed since the start of the new grain season, the sentiment is not improving on the small-tonnage shipping market. Over the past 8-9 days, activity increased only in the segment for grain transportation ex Danube ports (to Spain, Italy,

Danube grain is the only swift cargo flow on extremely dull small-tonnage market

Morocco), but the market has begun to slow down again by the mid-week. «There are some delays with grain supplies to ports, therefore some shortage of cargoes occurred for mid-July and 2H July dates,» local brokers commented to ISM. The offer of grain cargoes for transportation from EU ports remains minimal, while grain shipments from Russian ports have declined slightly this week due to very low demand from Turkey and North Africa. Transportation volumes of steel products from Russian and Turkish ports remain minimal, while the offer of steel products ex EU and Egyptian ports has grown insignificantly (if it has at all). In the Mediterranean Sea, tonnage demand is still supported mainly by shipments of minerals and fertilizers. The surplus of available seagoing vessels remains significant in the region, while the list of open sea-river tonnage has even got longer during the week. Freight rates have sagged by $0.5-2/t in both Black and Azov Seas. Rates for shipment of 5-6,000 t of wheat or corn from Reni or Izmail to Egypt have

inched down to $39-40/t, while those for transportation of 3,000 t of wheat from Azov to Marmara have sagged to $30-31/t.

Meanwhile, Mediterranean freight levels are virtually unchanged. Transportation of 5-6,000 t of urea from Damietta to SpanMed still costs $26-27/t.  Note that current TCEs for 3-12k dwt fleet are hovering close to OPEX levels in the Black and Mediterranean Seas; moreover, they are about 2.5 times lower compared to mid-July 2021 and 2022, but quite in line with July 2018-2020 earnings. The situation is slightly better for sea-river owners, who earn at least 20% more than OPEX, but even in this case TCEs are 1.5-2 times lower than in July 2021 and 2022.

In the Baltic and North Seas, coaster market expectedly remains quiet in summer, while the vacation season is gaining momentum in Europe. The tonnage list is also excessive in the basin, although the surplus is not as significant as in the south. Freight rates have not changed much this week.

Shipments from the Danube ports will most likely resume perking up unless any clear prospects for the resumption of the Grain Initiative emerge. Note that demand for new crop European grain may not grow much in the next couple of weeks as importers count on further price cuts. Nevertheless, in the medium term (by August), barley and wheat shipments from the EU and Russian ports will surely gain pace, and freight rates should finally bottom out. There is no other encouraging news for ship owners. In spite of all attempts of Russian square billet producers to increase sales to Turkey, importers insist on even more noticeable discounts as Turkish domestic prices are sagging. Demand for Turkish and European coils is weak in MENA and Europe, with prices slowly going down. The ongoing vacation season in Europe will surely not be the factor that supports brisker activity on the market.
