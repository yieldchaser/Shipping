## Market Commentary

The South Atlantic Supramax/Ultramax and Handysize markets have stabilized after last week's decline. Trade is gradually recovering after a long holiday weekend in the regional countries. New April and May requests enter the market regularly enough to allow ship owners to keep rates stable. Note that demand for Ultramax fleet

USG rates have not changed much over the week. Despite some increase of spot vessel list, the regional cargo traffic has also perked up (especially on transatlantic routes), balancing the market. Note that the Handysize period activity has grown as well.

In the Black and Mediterranean Seas , the cargo flow has increased very slightly (if it has at all), and has again shrunk by the end of the week given the Orthodox Easter in several countries of the region. Ukrainian and European grains remain the market drivers, although many offers from Ukraine are not firm (bss sub sale), including due to maximum tensions around inspection queue at JCC, which threatens the corridor functioning as a whole. Similar to the Atlantic Ocean, freight rates stay virtually unchanged

in the region this week; only those towards ECSA have sagged a bit.

The Baltic & Continent market has shown the most noticeable upturn this week. First of all, players report increased number of requests for transportation of fertilizers ex Russian ports. This again led to some excitement in this part of the basin and allowed ship owners to raise rates in the Baltic Sea by $1-2/t and $1.5-3k daily. Tonnage demand has grown slightly also in the Continent. «Many scrap stems for mid-late April dates have been booked by operators in March, therefore many of them are on the market for TCT-in deals,» a broker commented to ISM. Rates in the Continent have added $0.5-1k daily and $0.5- 1.5/t over the week.

Meanwhile, the downtrend continues in the Eastern Hemi. Indian Ocean rates have dropped by another $1-2k daily and $0.5-1.5/t on all routes, except shipments of coal and ore from South Africa. Note, however, that Supramax owners working with construction cargoes ex Persian Gulf are still able to earn the most money in the entire Eastern Hemi.

In SE Asia , time-charter rates bss Indo RV dely Singapore increasingly fall below $10k daily ($2.5k daily down since last week), while the decline is less rapid in the Far East ($1k daily down) as there are still quite a few uncovered requests for shipments of steel products and other general cargoes with April laycans. Handysize rates bss inner Pacific/SE Asia RV have dropped by $2k daily and $1-2/t, while back-haul TCT rates have sagged by mere $0.5-1k daily.

### TCT rates dynamics, $/day

In the short term, South Atlantic rates may start growing as the number of grain requests with late April- May laycans is gradually increasing. «Outlook is bullish as ECSA is expected to have a record crop of grains, a large part of which will have to be shipped soon,» a broker shared with ISM. Players are also optimistic about developments in the USG: «USG looks increasingly supported, with healthy levels of fresh demand coming into the market,» a broker said. The Black Sea market is not expected to perk up even after the Easter holidays. Demand for Black Sea grains will probably not increase in Mediterranean and Asian countries until the end of the season. In addition, the new requirements voiced by the Russian government put a big question mark over the further functioning of the Ukrainian grain corridor, which so far draws away a large number of vessels opening in the Mediterranean area. The excitement is likely to ease in North Europe, and rates will bottom amid very small number of new steel scrap offers and steadily slow exports of European grain.

The SE Asian market is likely to remain under pressure even if coal traffic from Indonesia gains pace as the tonnage list is quite long in the region, while the inflow of ballasters from the Far East and eastern India has also increased. Note that on April 11 Australia announced that it had taken steps to open the Chinese market for Australian barley, which had been blocked over the past three years by extremely high duties from China. China agreed to review the duties in the coming months and if grain trade between the countries resumes, Supramax/ Ultramax owners will get additional support in the APAC.
