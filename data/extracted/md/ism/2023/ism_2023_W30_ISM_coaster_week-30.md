## Market Commentary

The Black Sea small-tonnage market trends look very diverse and dynamically changing. Late last week and at the very start of this one, many grain traders were still wary of entering the market with firm offers ex Ukrainian Danube ports amid threats from Russia, and several deals

### Coaster owners still unable to raise rates anywhere except Danube ports

were cancelled. However, already on Tuesday-Wednesday the market has been flooded with new offers, and queues to enter Sulina have started to grow rapidly (currently about 100 vessels are already waiting for passage), allowing ship owners to raise rates aggressively. «Seems most owners in fact don't care much about higher war risks or avoid working ex Danube, but just use this to ask significantly higher rates,» a charterer complained to ISM. Freight rates for shipments from Danube ports have risen by $3-4/t at the end of the week. Rates for shipment of 5-6,000 t of wheat or corn from Izmail or Reni to Egypt have inched up to $44-45/t. As for other regions, despite tonnage absorption by the Danube market, there are still plenty of vessels open spot due in part to the fact that many ship owners are really not ready to work ex Danube. The cargo traffic from the Black Sea ports of EU, Turkey and Russia remains extremely weak; only steel scrap offers from Romania and Bulgaria rather regularly enter the market.

Freight rates remain steadily low. In the Mediterranean Sea , demand for fleet is still supported only by shipments of fertilizers, agri products and minerals, as well as by traffic of Egyptian steel products. Ship Owners are still unable to raise rates from the bottom despite an upturn in bunker prices of $20-30/t over the week (-$40-80 daily in terms of TCE). Thus, transportation of 5-6,000 t of phosphates from EgyptMed to Marmara costs mere $13/t, while rates for shipment of 5-6,000 t of steel from Marmara to Algeria are still hovering at high $20s/t. The Azov Sea cargo traffic remains dull given limited demand for grain and coal from Turkey. Nevertheless, ship owners have managed to raise rates slightly ($1-2/t up).

The North European market remains depressed, with an acute shortage of new cargo offers. As before, potential rate collapse is restrained only by relatively short vessel list in the area. However, freight rates ex Russian ports have still inched down within €1/t, though they remain premium compared to shipments from other countries of the region.

Next week, the Black Sea market is likely to show a further increase in freight rates for shipments ex Danube ports as the cargo flow and queues will surely continue to grow (unless there is some provocation from Russia). Probably, the decline in tonnage list will eventually push up transportation costs from other Black Sea ports as well. The cargo flow ex Mediterranean ports is likely to grow slowly (partly due to the recovery of grain shipments from the Adriatic Sea and Greece), but many Mediterranean charterers are counting on an inflow of vessels from the Black Sea, especially if the situation around the Danube ports escalates. Sea-river owners working in the Azov Sea may hardly expect further rate growth given the absence of preconditions for higher demand for Russian wheat in Turkey. The North European market will be slack in the next couple of weeks, especially as the vacation season reaches its peak in Europe.
