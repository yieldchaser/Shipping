## Market Commentary

Following the softening of the Panamax sector, the ECSA Supramax/Ultramax market has also weakened amid excessive tonnage list for late July laycans. Handysize rates are also under pressure given increased ballaster inflow from West Africa and USG/USEC. «Fresh requests for

Handy market in Mediterranean and Supra/Ultra segment in SE Asia are the only showing signs of improvement

late July dates remain insufficient to absorb tonnage,» a broker concluded. TCT rates have decreased by $1k daily on average, while voyage-basis freights have dropped by $1-2/t. Thus, rates for shipment of 25-30,000 t of soybeans from Santos to Rotterdam have inched down to $27/t given 8000 sshex bends l/d rates.

Meanwhile, the cargo/tonnage ratio looks balanced in the USG Supramax segment as lively shipments of petcoke continue to support tonnage demand in the region. The Handy market sentiment has even improved slightly amid the outlined shortage of vessels in prompt positions. «There are some fixtures agreed certainly higher than last done levels,» an operator commented to ISM earlier in the week. Closer to weekend, however, rates have again inched down to previous levels. As a result,

transportation costs ex regional ports have remained virtually unchanged over the week.

Russia's withdrawal from the Grain Initiative has undoubtedly been the key event of the Mediterranean market this week. Nevertheless, it had not particularly affected Handysize and Supramax market sentiment, as cancellations mainly apply to Panamax/Kamsarmax fleet. Moreover, Handysize owners have managed to raise rates for the second consecutive week ($0.5-1k daily and $0.5- 1/t up) thanks to a gradual recovery of grain shipments with August laycans, as well as a decrease in tonnage list. «Despite the fact that market looks less active again since yesterday (Wednesday, July 19), owners have succeeded to 'scratch out' some slight rate upturns,» a broker commented. Transportation of 25-30,000 t of wheat or corn from Constanta to SpanMed now costs $13- 13.5/t. Supramax/Ultramax owners can boast that they have finally managed to stop the rate decline, but their attempts to lift freight levels from the bottom are not very successful despite the upturn in transatlantic cargo traffic. Note that Supramax rates are still lower than Handysize rates on most routes (first of all, on inter-Med routes).

### TCT rates dynamics, $/day

Similar to the Mediterranean area, Supramax owners have failed to raise rates in the Baltic & Continent, although the number of open vessels has decreased slightly and players note a barely noticeable growth of steel scrap and grain shipments. Handysize/Handymax rates are also stable in the basin.

In SE Asia and the Far East, Supramax time-charter rates on key routes have risen by $0.5-1k daily, while transportation costs of coal and other cargoes have added $0.5-1/t on average. This increase is primarily referred to brisker exports of Indonesian coal to China, as well as longer vessel delays in southern China due to the typhoon. Shipments of NOPAC grain and FE Russian coal remain slow, while traffic of Chinese steel products has even decreased slightly. Meanwhile, the slowdown in Handysize shipments of key cargoes from Australia, as well as of timber from New Zealand, allowed charterers to reduce relevant rates within $1/t.

extremely limited exports of South African coal and PG construction cargoes. Ship owners are struggling to keep rates from falling.

Despite unprecedentedly active exports of Brazilian soybeans and corn, owners will hardly manage to resume the rate upturn in the South Atlantic next week as the 1H August tonnage list looks clearly excessive in the area; this also applies to the neighboring Panamax segment, which will put additional pressure. In the USG, ship owners

should hope for increased exports of other cargoes from the USA besides petcoke. First of all, players are talking about grain and steel scrap. «It will require another wave of fresh demand next week to maintain the current levels,» a broker mentioned. However, it is still difficult to say how likely it looks to step up shipments of wheat and corn, not only from the U.S., but also from the EU. On the one hand, realizing the dubious prospects of Ukrainian grain exports, importers are switching to alternative suppliers, while on the other hand, most importers are not ready to buy at current prices even considering the fact that quotes have started to roll back after an increase seen at the start of the week. In the Black Sea, Russian grain exports are expected to gain pace, but this may turn out insufficient to allow ship owners to push rates up, especially given the closure of the grain corridor from Ukraine, which used to absorb a lot of vessels opening in the Mediterranean Sea. Negative expectations prevail in Asia as well. Ship owners will face problems even keeping rates at previous levels only due to Indonesian coal shipments, while there are few preconditions for an upturn in traffic of steel products or other cargoes from the Far East.
