Additional concerns come from the fact that Handysize and small Handy owners increasingly compete for 8-12,000 t cargo lots (including shipments from the Danube ports, where charterers are increasingly enlarging stems to 7-10,000 t). This week, freight rates have sagged by $1-2/t in the Black Sea , dropping below the OPEX level in terms of RV TCEs. Thus, rates for shipment of 5-6,000 t of wheat or corn from Reni to the Adriatic Sea have decreased to $40-41, while those for transportation of 7-8,000 t of a similar grain cargo from Constanta to Egypt to $21.5-22/t. Meanwhile, freights are almost unchanged in the Mediterranean Sea ($0.5/t down only on some routes). Charterers are ready to pay €31/t for shipment of 5-6,000 t of steel profiles from Seville to Marmara bss 1/1. The shortage of cargoes in the Baltic and North Seas is even greater than in South Europe; corresponding rates have declined by another €0.5-1/t.

In the Azov Sea , owners of sea-river tonnage continue to escalate tensions and speculate on the approach of the new grain season. Gradual recovery of grain exports from the Volga and Caspian ports plays into owners' hands. In fact, however, activity remains low on the Azov Sea spot market, and rates have stabilized after a slight upturn last week. Meanwhile, freights have risen by $5-10/t in the Caspian Sea and Volga ports. Transportation of 3,000 t of grain from Astrakhan to Iran costs low $60s/t.

There are still no preconditions for a trend shift in the Black and Mediterranean Seas in the next couple of weeks. The cargo traffic recovery in the Mediterranean Sea is likely to be positional; even if this trend persists, it will hardly help ship owners to raise rates given such a significant surplus of available tonnage. Metallurgical and coal markets may show any improvements no earlier than July, when the grain season starts gaining pace in the EU and Russia.
