## Market Commentary

Trade has weakened on the Black and Mediterranean coaster markets closer to Wednesday, which is quite logical considering upcoming Easter holidays in Europe. First of all, players note decreased shipments of grain and other cargoes from the EU. Traffic of Russian steel products has also declined this week. Nevertheless, we

Small-tonnage market looks quieter despite still regular shipments of Danube grain and Egyptian fertilizers

should note a surge of wheat shipments from Ukrainian Danube ports to the Marmara Sea, Izmir and Egypt seen at the very beginning of the week, which allowed ship owners to prevent any significant rate drop in the basin. A barely noticeable downturn of $0.5-1/t is reported only in rates ex the Danube ports, but this is primarily referred to the reduction of actual downtimes at the Sulina entrance (down to just a couple of days). In the Mediterranean Sea, tonnage demand is supported mainly by shipments of fertilizers from Egypt: relevant freight rates have risen by another $0.5-1/t due to positional shortage of spot vessels in that area.

The already low activity has decreased a bit in the Baltic and North Seas . Charterers do not hurry to sign new deals, expecting a confident rate decline after the holidays (quite typical development for second half of spring).

Meanwhile, freight rates stay under serious pressure in the Azov Sea due to continuing slowdown of grain shipments ex Russian shallow ports caused by ongoing decline in relevant export prices. «The market is definitely sliding down, but not so fast at all - many were expecting a sharp fall,» a broker commented. Note that sea-river owners are still competing only for Azov Sea cargoes, as even with a rate drop of another $2-4/t this week, earnings remain at least 30% higher than in the neighboring Black Sea.

The market may recover slightly after April 10 when the European players will get back to work after Easter holidays. Nevertheless, the situation on key commodity markets does not promise any serious improvements so far. Moreover, Turkish importers of finished and semi-finished steel products (including those of Russian production) have covered their needs and are now taking a breather. The ongoing downtrend on the global wheat market still negatively affects trade, while there are persistent rumors

that a possible return of grain import duties in Turkey in mid-April could seriously exacerbate the situation on the freight market. Exporters of Egyptian urea also report a gradual weakening of importers demand. All the above factors give grounds to forecast if not the collapse, then the decline in small-tonnage freight rates in 2H April. Many players expect traders to try their best to clear out stocks of old crop grain in Europe before the new season, but shipments will hardly gain pace until mid-May. In the Baltic Sea, rates ex freezing ports will most likely sag even more noticeably than in other regions amid weather improvement and cancellation of all ice restrictions.
