## Market Commentary

The Supramax/Ultramax shipping market continues to recover all across the Atlantic in the second half of August. Market is getting strong support from a steady strengthening of the neighboring Panamax segment and positive trends on the FFA market. Positive sentiment also

Supra/Ultra and Handy owners feel more confident all across the Atlantic following bullish Panamax segment

Over the week, Supramax spot rates in ECSA have grown by $1-2k daily and $1-3/t on all routes thanks to lively grain exports from Brazil and Argentina. «The market is firming up as fresh cargo replenishment remains healthy,» a broker mentioned. Thus, rates for transportation of 50,000 t of soybeans from Santos to S.China have inched up to $36.5/t given 8,000 sshex bends l/d rates. The situation is not so clear in the Handy segment. The tonnage list remains excessive in southern Brazil, while owners of spot vessels in N.Brazil are able to raise rates ($0.5-1k daily and $1-1.5/t up).

In USG, Supramax/Ultramax rates are also growing steadily, especially on front-haul routes. «Some fresh demand is entering the market, as rates for front-haul trips are significantly higher than last done,» a broker commented. Unstable waiting time issue in the Panama Canal forces ship owners to insist on premiums when discussing front-haul deals ($2k daily and $2-2.5/t up).

Transatlantic shipping rates have risen slightly less considerably, namely within $1k daily and $1-1.5/t. Thus, shipment of 50,000 t of petcoke (sf 47-48`) from USG to EC India now costs $36/t bss 1/1. The Handy market has also strengthened a bit due to increased volumes of scrap shipments from USEC, as well as brisker cargo traffic from USG to WCSA. By the end of the week, rates ex USG/USEC have added $1k daily and $1-2/t.

In North Europe, grain and steel scrap shipments perked up somewhat at the start of the week, while the number of open carriers inched down. In this situation, ship owners managed to raise corresponding rates by $0.5-1.5k daily and $0.5-1/t. However, the market picture has started to worsen already in the mid-week. «There are fewer ships at the moment, which is pushing the market up, but the cargoes started to disappear, got fixed actually and closer to weekend it is calm again,» an EU broker commented to ISM. It should be noted that owners are again quite aggressively pushing rates up when discussing shipments from Russia, especially by Supramax fleet (this is largely due to the fact that Russian coal charterers are ready to pay very high rates for Panamax vessels).

Ship owners continue raising rates also in the Black and Mediterranean Seas; particularly this applies to the

Supramax segment. At the same time, the cargo traffic is growing very slowly in the basin (if it is growing at all), and only due to grain shipments from Romania and Bulgaria. The number of open vessels has not changed much in the region during the week and remains excessive (about 55 carriers of 20-45k dwt and less than 30 Supramax/ Ultramax ships will open in the basin for the next three weeks). Nevertheless, Handysize owners have managed to raise rates by another $0.5k daily and $0.5-1/t, while Supramax levels have risen within $1.5k daily and $1-2/t. Rates for shipment of 25-30,000 t of wheat from Constanta or Varna to SpanMed have inched up to $15.5-16/t.

In APAC, the Supramax/Ultramax and Handysize markets has also strengthened, not least due to typhoon- related vessel delays in the Far East, as well as a slight increase in late August cargo offer in SE Asia (particularly of coal for shipments from Indonesia to India). «As Panamax market has shot up a lot, many charterers are shifting to Supramax lots,» an Indian broker claims. Time-charter rates have grown by $1-3k daily during the week, while transportation costs of main cargoes have added $1-2/t. Note that ship owners now prefer to fix vessels under TC- trips as corresponding rates exceed most voy-bss freights by $1-2k daily. Short period rates have also increased in the

region, especially in the Handysize segment ($2-2.5k daily up since the beginning of August).

Indian Ocean Supramax/Ultramax rates have grown slightly less significantly than in APAC ($1k daily and within $1/t up). Tonnage demand has perked up slightly due to brisker exports of South African coal and positive trends in the Atlantic Ocean. On the other hand, Indian Independence Day and the Parsi New Year negatively affected the pace of trade.

*** The sentiment of Supramax/Ultramax owners working in the Atlantic Ocean is likely to remain positive

next week. The ECSA market will most probably stay active in terms of grain shipments on all routes, with continuing support from growing Panamax segment. The expected upturn in grain shipments from USA to Asia together with continuing (although reduced) delays in the Panama Canal will allow ship owners to further raise rates in USG. The Handy segment also promises to be stronger: «Prevailing sentiment is that the market will strengthen in late August and a strong autumn is anticipated,» a broker mentioned.

In the short term, rates are likely to stabilize in North Europe. So far there are no preconditions for a further increase of regional cargo traffic. Russian fertilizer cargoes have been covered till late August – early September. A significant recovery of steel scrap shipments is also unlikely given muted demand in Turkey. «We expect in the next two weeks the market to calm down,» an operator commented to ISM. Only grain shipments will remain relatively brisk as forward requests increasingly enter the market.

In the Black and Mediterranean Seas, a further rate growth is possible only in case of positive trends in the neighboring Continent, as well as in the Atlantic Ocean. Tonnage demand will hardly strengthen noticeably in the Black Sea given the blockade of Ukrainian ports. The volumes of grain exports and transit through EU ports are already close to the limit. Many players expect a significant upturn in Russian wheat shipments considering the ruble weakening, but the actual picture is different so far: domestic sales have stalled, with increasingly circulating rumors about export duty increase and unofficial price limit.

The Asian Handysize/Supramax market will continue to strengthen only in case of a further increase in coal exports from Indonesia, South Africa and Australia, as there are few other preconditions. Competition between steel suppliers has sharpened, leading to lower prices and in turn is unlikely to support the freight rate growth.
