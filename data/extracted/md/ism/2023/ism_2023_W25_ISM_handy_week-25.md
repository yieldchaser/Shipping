## Market Commentary

The Supramax/Ultramax and Handysize freight market sentiment has started to improve in some freight sections this week. First of all, this applies to the ECSA market which is believed to be the main driver of future recovery. The already healthy offer of grains for shipments

from Brazil with 1H July laycans has increased even more. «More orders entered the market and it has finally affected the sentiment,» an operator commented to ISM. Front- haul TCT rates for Supramax fleet have risen by $1-2k daily, while grain transportation costs to Asia have added $1- 2/t. T/A rates have stayed at last week's levels. Demand for Handysize fleet is also gradually increasing largely due to the emerging trend of Upriver draft reduction. Ship owners increasingly decide to transfer their vessels from northern Brazil to the south of the country. However, Handysize freight rates have not changed much yet.

Meanwhile, negative trends still persist in the USG. Despite healthy exports of petcoke, the shortage of grain offers from the US continues to put pressure on freight rates ex regional ports. By the end of the week, rates

have decreased by $1-2k daily and $1-2/t. The Handysize segment looks even weaker. Brisker ballaster outflow towards ECSA did not help ship owners to keep rates stable ($1k daily and $1-2/t down). Rates for transportation of 25-30,000 t of wheat from New Orleans to Alexandria have decreased to $31/t.

The market of the Black and Mediterranean Seas remains the most depressing. «There is literally no market before the new grain season begins; spot/prompt market is empty until Jul 15,» an experienced EU operator said. Spot rates remain at the bottom. However, grain offers from the EU and Russian ports for 2H July and August laycans are gradually entering the market, with relevant rates voiced $2-3k daily and $2-3/t above spot levels. For instance, transportation of 25-30,000 t of wheat or corn from Constanta to SpanMed with spot laycans costs $12-12.5/t on average, while deals for late July-August shipments are already discussed at $14-14.5/t.

The early July tonnage list has decreased slightly in the Baltic & Continent , but the cargo flow remains too slow to allow rate bottoming out. Moreover, a lot of players assure that many ship owners simply keep their tonnage

off-market, trying to resuscitate the market. Grain and fertilizer offers are sporadic, and the previously announced recovery of steel scrap exports is not reported either.

Note that a hike in bunker prices ($20-40/t up over the last couple of weeks) is another problem for operators in both hemispheres.

In SE Asia , Supramax/Ultramax owners have managed to change the trend due to increased coal shipments from Indonesia, mainly towards Vietnam. Corresponding rates have risen within $1-2k daily and $0.5-1.5/t. Handysize time-charter rates remain unchanged, while voy-bss levels have added another $0.25-0.5/t amid an upturn in bunker prices. The cargo offer has grown also in the Far East , allowing ship owners to raise rates by $1k daily and $0.25-1/t.

The Indian Ocean market picture is looking mixed. Ship owners are forced to make further concessions negotiating deals for shipments ex PG amid continuing

surplus of tonnage. Time-charter rates have sagged by $0.5- 1k daily, while voy-bss freights are virtually unchanged due to a considerable increase in bunker prices in the region (almost $50/t up for VLSFO bss Fujairah since mid-June). By contrast, after a prolonged decline, freight rates ex South Africa have risen by $1k daily and $1-2/t this week due to a slight recovery in spot coal shipments, as well as amid a slowdown in ballaster inflow from ECI (which is logical given the strengthening of the SE Asian market).

Next week, ship owners will be having trouble maintaining positive momentum in ECSA. Rates are likely to stabilize on the positions reached. A further rate growth requires positive trends in the neighboring Panamax segment, as well as a further upturn in cargo traffic from Brazil, which is doubtful. In the USG, rates are likely to sag further, as the cargo/tonnage ratio will remain imbalanced in the near future. «The market still needs a significant increase in demand to change the negative trend,» a broker mentioned.

In the Black Sea, as well as in North Europe, freight rates have almost certainly reached their bottom and will hardly go down further, although next week will most likely be very weak due to the Eid al-Fitr holiday in North Africa and Turkey. As for 1H July, ship owners will surely be aggressively trying to push rates up considering the start of a new grain season and a gradual inflow of grain offers for 2H July and August shipments voiced at higher levels. Note that demand for Russian and European wheat is gradually rising in Asian countries. On the other hand, increasingly circulating rumors about Russia's withdrawal from the grain deal put a big question to future exports from Ukraine, and hence one of the main cargo flows in the Black Sea region.

In the near term, players expect a slowdown in shipments of construction cargoes from the Persian Gulf due to holidays and the start of the monsoon season in the main importing country, Bangladesh. Meanwhile, the upward trend cannot be ruled out in SE Asia due to increased demand for coal from China amid rising temperatures in the country and higher electricity consumption.
