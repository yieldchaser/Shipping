## Market Commentary

Considering the upcoming Christmas and New Year holActivity is sagging on the Supramax/Ultramax and Handysize freight markets all across the Atlantic. The spot market is looking extremely soft ahead of holidays in USA and Europe: most cargo offers are already covered,

ECSA remains the only section with relatively steady Handy/Supra rates all across the Atlantic

especially in the Supramax/Ultramax segment. Spot rates for such shipments from USG ports have dropped by $2k daily and $2-3/t on F/H routes, while transatlantic levels have declined by $3k daily and $3-4/t. Thus, rates for shipment of 50,000 t of corn or soybeans from New Orleans to S.China have inched down to low-mid $70s/t. Handysize freight rates hold steady as Intercaribs trade remains brisk.

In ECSA , limited cargo offer is balanced by moderate vessel list, which allows ship owners to keep spot rates stable; this applies to both Handysize and Supramax segments. However, deals with 2H January laycans are negotiated below spot levels.

The market looks uneventful in the Black and Mediterranean Seas as well as in the Baltic & Continent.

The cargo offer has noticeably decreased, especially in the segments for European scrap and grain, as well as fertilizers. «So market definitely coming of, the question remains whether this is only because Christmas approaching and a couple of vessels left open between the holidays or whether the tendency in general is downwards,» an EU scrap charterer wondering. At the start of the week, ship owners still managed to sign spot deals at previous rates, but later on several contracts with January laycans were already concluded at much lower levels. Rates for shipments from North Europe towards EMed have dropped the most significantly ($4-6k daily and $2-5/t down over the week), while transatlantic and short-haul freights have shown a more modest downturn ($1-3k daily and $0.5-2/t down). Thus, transportation of 25-30,000 t of wheat from Rouen to Algeria costs $26/t (compared to $31/t a week ago), while rates for shipment of 25-30,000 t of urea from Damietta to Brazil have inched down to $31/t ($1.5/t down over the week). Note that only front-haul rates remain high these days: due to sharpening military-political situation in the Red Sea, many owners are reluctantly considering front- haul shipments. In addition, EWRI rates while passing the Red Sea have risen to an average of 1% of H&M.

Despite a slight recovery in grain shipments from NOPAC ports early in the week, Supramax/Ultramax rates have eventually stayed at last week's levels in the Asia- Pacific region due to limited exports of Australian and Indonesian coal. As for back-haul shipments, some owners are seeking to get even bit higher rates. «Some owners get more cautious to pass the Suez Canal or they do not consider trips via Suez Canal at all now,» a broker notes. However, no increase in fixing rates has been noted yet.

The Handysize market looks balanced. The cargo offer looks limited in the Asia-Pacific region and cargoes are mostly covered, but there are not many vessels in the region either.

In the Indian Ocean , Supramax/Ultramax owners also manage to keep rates steadily high thanks to lively ore exports from both coasts of India. Besides, the PG/WCI area has few available vessels for late December-early January dates (many continue to leave in ballast to ECSA), while offers of salt and sulfur to the Far East emerge regularly.

Next week is expected to be quite calm in the western hemisphere. «We expect next week to be quite calm with the holidays kicking in,» a broker mentioned. Upon the return of major players from holidays, the market is likely to soften on all routes as signs of weakening can already be seen all across the Atlantic. In the Asia-Pacific region, pace of trade and freight rates may inch down as well.

Note that from January 1, some innovations are expected for operations via the EU ports, namely: Emission Trading Systems (ETS) - applicable as of January 1, 2024 - which sets an annual absolute limit on emissions of certain greenhouse gases (GHG) and requires the purchase of allowances for emissions. Therefore, the inclusion of shipping in the EU ETS puts a price on GHG emissions. As expected, all owners who will fall under the regulation (applicable for vessels over 5,000 GT from January 2024) will try to compensate the additional charges by raising voyage-basis rates. «Owners have to pay it but of course they will either include cost in the voyage rate (same as bunkers) or on TC they will ask for reimbursement from charterers; in the bulk sector I think all charterers have accepted that they will pay for it,» a large EU charterer commented. According to players' expectations, the new regulations will result in additional costs of $1-4/t of dwt cargo, depending on the route. According to ISM, however, the after-holiday freight market softening may be more significant than the amount of compensation for these new expenses as it happened 3 years ago with the mandatory use of VLSFO, when voyage-basis rates did not increase (contrary to forecasts of many players and experts), but ship owners' earnings dropped significantly.
