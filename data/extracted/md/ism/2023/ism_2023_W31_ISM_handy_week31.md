## Market Commentary

Despite positive developments in the neighboring Panamax shipping segment, negative sentiment is again dominating the Atlantic Supramax/Ultramax and Handysize markets. In ECSA, a slight activity surge and positional improvements on T/A routes seen last week have

Handy and Supra/Ultra owners failed to catch positive momentum from large-tonnage market

almost faded away and the market is back to its normal. The inflow of ballasters from other regions puts additional pressure on the market, but ship owners show resistance and do not agree to work at lower bids yet, being primarily supported by a slight recovery of Argentine corn exports. «Argentina is adding corn to the market for August loading slots,» a broker mentioned. Thus, transportation of 30,000 t of corn from Upriver to Alexandria costs $33-34/t bss 1/1 given standard 7,000x/5,000x l/d rates.

Meanwhile, the USG Supramax/Ultramax and Handysize markets are declining across the board. The shortage of August cargo requests does not allow absorbing available Supramax/Ultramax tonnage in the area, while the Handy segment suffers additional pressure from brisk ballaster inflow from USEC, where activity is

extremely low these days. By the end of the week, rates have sagged by $0.5-1k daily and $1-3/t in the region. Thus, rates for shipment of up to 45,000 t of steel scrap (sf 52-55`) from USEC to Iskenderun have inched down to $19-19.5/t.

The situation has improved slightly for Handysize and Supramax owners working in the Black and Mediterranean Seas due to the decline in August tonnage list, but extremely slow cargo traffic still does not allow ship owners to push rates up. Increased spread in rates is the only change noted by brokers. «I would say market looks more volatile this week, with rates varying much, which is a matter of timing; but Black Sea market is not improving for sure,» an EU broker commented. Shipment of 25-30,000 t of wheat from Constanta or Varna to SpanMed with 8,000 sshex bends l/d rates is estimated by ISM at $14-15/t.

In the Baltic & Continent, the freight market activity is supported only by fertilizer shipments from Russian ports, as well as by traffic of EU grain and scrap. Unlike the Mediterranean basin, the regional vessel list is not getting shorter. Ship owners hardly manage to keep rates stable, with any strengthening out of question so far.

### TCT rates dynamics, $/day

Fundamentally, the market picture has not changed much in SE Asia and Far East this week. Adverse weather conditions remain the key driver in these regions, while shipments of Indonesian and Russian coal, Chinese steel and fertilizers, as well as of NOPAC grain remain limited. In the Indian Ocean, tonnage demand is supported primarily by shipments of limestone from WCI/PG, ore and rice from ECI, and coal from South Africa. However, the local tonnage list is excessive, and charterers are confidently keeping rates stable. Rates for transportation of 50,000 t of bulk gypsum or limestone from Oman or the UAE to WC India still do not exceed $10/t.

Next week, the situation will hardly improve for ship owners working in the South Atlantic, as August cargo offer and vessel list remain imbalanced in the region. On the other hand, if Panamax market continues to strengthen, it should give at least a little support to Supramax/Ultramax

owners. In addition, ship owners are putting hopes on a further upturn in corn shipments from Argentina. In the North Atlantic, the market will almost certainly weaken further, with owners hoping only for brisker petcoke exports. «Hopefully, August will bring the inflow of cargoes as the market needs to break this downward trend,» a broker mentioned.

In the Black Sea, ISM sources note increasingly circulating rumors about the possible resumption of negotiations on the Grain Initiative. Many players are inclined to believe that this is nearly the only chance for

the market growth. «We don't think market can improve without Ukrainian exports, even in case of further ballaster outflow,» an operator commented to ISM.

Typhoon Khanun is forecasted to make a U-turn and head towards Japan, keeping the SE Asian and Far Eastern markets uncertain next week. If weather conditions improve, it will be more difficult for ship owners to keep rates at previous levels, considering that there are very few preconditions for an increase in exports of Chinese steel and NOPAC grains, while coal offers will hardly be sufficient.
