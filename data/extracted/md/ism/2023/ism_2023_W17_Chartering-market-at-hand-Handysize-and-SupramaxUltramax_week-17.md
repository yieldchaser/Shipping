## Market Commentary

South Atlantic Supramax/Ultramax rates stay at healthy levels as new grain requests with May laycans quite regularly enter the market, balancing the list of available tonnage in the region. «The market is active, with rates holding firm as demand remains solid,» a broker said to ISM. The Handysize market has even started to grow amid

Handy/Supra owners forced to accept lower rates in all sections except ECSA and PG

an acute shortage of such vessels open for spot dates (especially this applies to large Handy fleet). By the end of the week, Handysize rates have risen by $2-3k daily and $3-4/t in the region. Thus, rates for shipment of 25-30,000 t of wheat from Upriver to Algeria have increased to around $40-41/t in case of a good discharge rate of 5,000 sshex, and to mid-$40s/t in case of a 3000 sshex disch.

The USG market has stopped growing as the number of open Supramax/Ultramax vessels is gradually increasing in the region. The Handysize segment is still showing positive trends thanks to relatively brisk cargo traffic on transatlantic routes ($1k daily and $1-2/t up). Rates for shipment of up to 50,000 t of corn from the USG to S.China hold steady at $50-51/t bss 1/1.

The already dull trade has slowed down almost to the minimum in the Black and Mediterranean Seas this week. The most acute shortage of cargoes is seen in the segment for grain, which is the main cargo in the basin. The fact is that most Ukrainian exporters have put their trade on pause, waiting for a decision regarding further functioning of the grain corridor (recall that the current agreement ends on May 18, and more than 70 vessels are still waiting for loading). Grain shipments from the EU and Russian ports are also rather dull. Nevertheless, ship owners successfully avoid significant rate falls despite the fact that more than 60 vessels of 23-39k dwt are reportedly open within the Mediterranean Sea for the next 2 weeks. During the week, Handysize rates have dropped by $1- 1.5k daily and $1-2/t. In the Supramax/Ultramax segment, freight levels are relatively stable due to the recovery of shipments of construction cargoes, minerals and coal.

The Baltic & Continent market is still driven by Russian cargoes, namely fertilizers and coal, less often steel products. Freight rates remain quite high in this part of the basin, especially those for spot dates. Meanwhile, the cargo traffic is noticeably slower in the lower Baltic

and the Continent, while the number of open vessels is growing gradually. Time-charter rates on most routes hold steady, although sometimes players note the decline of $0.5-1k daily and $0.5-1/t. Transportation of 25-30,000 t of fertilizers from Ust-Luga to Brazil costs low $40s/t bss 1/1.

The Asia-Pacific region is dominated by negative sentiment this week. In SE Asia, Supramax/Ultramax rates have shown a decrease virtually on all routes ($1k daily and $0.25-1/t down). Only rates for Indo RVs bss redel China remain unchanged as demand for tonnage to ship Indonesian coal to China looks quite healthy ahead of the next protracted holidays in the country.

In the Far East, players note a slowdown in shipments of NOPAC grains and Chinese steel products. Relevant Supramax rates have sagged by $1k daily and $0.5-1/t. The situation looks slightly better for Handysize owners due to steady shipments on back-haul routes.

Supramax/Ultramax owners have managed to stabilize rates in the Indian Ocean thanks to an activity upturn in the Persian Gulf closer to mid-week. At the same time, transportation costs of coal and ore from South Africa have started to fall ($1.5k daily and $0.5-1/t down) amid the shortage of new requests and tonnage oversupply. Demand for Handysize fleet in the basin is supported mainly by offer of Indian steel products, but these volumes are insufficient to support rates (as a result, another $1k daily and $0.5-1.5/t drop over the week).

Next week, ship owners may manage to insist on a rate hike in the South Atlantic. «Positive trend is expected as the grain export season reaches its peak,» a broker shared with ISM. Meanwhile, the USG Supramax market may weaken: «Market might soften slightly as tonnage supply continues to increase for mid-May dates, while demand remains flat,» a broker shared his expectations with us. The USG Handysize segment may also see a trend shift as most spot requests are already covered, while new offers for mid-May shipments less and less frequently enter the market: «The cargo list is getting shorter, which could result in a rate drop during the coming week,» another broker commented. Owners working in the Black and Mediterranean Seas will be facing great difficulties keeping rates stable in case of further uncertainty around the extension of the so-called Grain Initiative. Even the intensified outflow of large-tonnage vessels closer to Gibraltar will hardly help ship owners in this case. In the short term, there are no preconditions for market strengthening in North Europe. Turkish importers are still showing minimal demand for European steel scrap, while exports of European grains are gradually slowing down. Players expect the increase in Continent tonnage list (due in part to the inflow of fleet from the eastern Mediterranean) to put further pressure on rates. Players expect the SE Asian Supramax market to pick up after the protracted Labor Day holiday in China comes to an end. The Handysize segment is projected to show livelier traffic of barite/feldspar from India to the Persian Gulf, which, combined with still good demand for steel products, could help ship owners raise rates in May.
