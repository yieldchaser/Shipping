## Market Commentary

The market sentiment is suffering the most from the decline in cargo flow ex Danube ports , because a further reduction of queues at the Sulina entrance contributes to the rapid growth of regional tonnage list. «We see several vessels open in BlSea and Marmara being ready to ballast to EMed,» an experienced fertilizer charterer commented. Freight rates for transportation from the Danube ports have decreased by another $7-9/t, those ex deep-water ports of Ukraine by $5-6/t, from other Black Sea ports within $0.5- 1/t. Rates for shipment of 5-7,000 t of wheat from Reni or Izmail to EgyptMed have already decreased to $43-44/t, while those for transportation from Constanta to the Italian Adriatic have dropped to $27-28/t. At the same time, ship owners are desperately holding rates in the Marmara and Mediterranean Seas: «It's like owners really prefer to sit spot prior committing to less and under a certain level

they say 'no',» - an EU broker commented. Transportation of 5-6,000 t of urea from Damietta to SpanMed still costs $35-36/t. According to ISM, Mediterranean freight rates are holding steady largely due to the fact that alternative Black Sea freights were initially (in September-October) bringing $0.2-0.3k daily more in TCE to ship owners, while shipments from Ukrainian ports were bringing twice as much; now the markets have virtually leveled off (although work from Ukrainian ports is still able to bring owners of 5-6k dwt fleet up to $3k daily bss RV against $2.2-2.6k daily in case of shipments from other ports).

The North European market sentiment is not improving either. The offer of main cargoes is rather modest in the Baltic Sea, with no significant problems in fleet search; the North Sea market is looking only a bit more active. Bunker prices and weather conditions in the basin remain the key factors that somehow affect freights. So far rates stay at late October levels.

Even the hottest Caspian Sea market has started to cool down this week, reaching its peak on Monday- Tuesday. Activity has stabilized closer to weekend; the offer of grain ex Volga ports has even started to decrease, allowing charterers to stabilize freight rates.

*** In the short term, there are still no preconditions for any improvements in the Black and Mediterranean Seas. The overwhelming part of grain exports is carried out by large-tonnage fleet, which is supported by the geography of Russian, Ukrainian and European grain sales. No changes can be seen on the metallurgical market: demand for imported coils, billets and rebar remains extremely weak in Turkey, North African countries and Europe. Demand for Egyptian urea is supported mainly by India, which can hardly provide any support to the small-tonnage market. In North Europe, the forecasted worsening of weather conditions and decreased fleet turnover may lead to a slight rate upturn.
