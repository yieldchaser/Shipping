## Market Commentary

The small-tonnage freight market is falling rapidly in the Azov-Black Sea basin and the Mediterranean region in early May. Most players agree that this collapse is a result of decreased demand for grain cargoes from Turkey after the implementation of duties. The market is still showing an acute shortage of steel offers from Russia and Turkey.

Small-tonnage market softens in North Europe, being literally in free fall in the south

The 1H May tonnage list is confidently growing, and this applies to both sea-going and sea-river vessels. Azov Sea freight rates have decreased most noticeably over the week ($6-11/t down), which is quite logical considering that the share of grain shipments traditionally accounts for about 80% in this area. As for the sea-going segment, the collapse of the Azov Sea market had its strongest impact on rates from Russian Black Sea ports, which have dropped by $4-5/t. Freights from the ports of Ukraine, Turkey, North Africa and EU countries have sagged within $2.5/t. Thus, rates for shipment of 5-6,000 t of wheat or corn from Reni or Izmail to Marmara are already hovering at $39-40/t, while those for transportation of 5-6,000 t of steel billets from Novorossiysk to Maramra at $18-19/t. In the Mediterranean Sea, tonnage demand is still mainly supported by shipments of fertilizers, minerals and steel products from Egypt.

Note that a slight decline in bunker prices ($30- 40/t down for VLSFO and MGO over the week) is the only positive development for ship owners in this difficult situation. However, this drop is unable to compensate even 30% of owners' earnings losses caused by decreased freight rates. At the same time, ship owners working in the Mediterranean region are still able to earn 20-40% above OPEX, which, though much worse than in early May 2022 and 2021, is much better compared to the vast majority of seasons over the past 15 years.

The North European market is also low on activity. Already dull trade has slowed down further amid holidays in many countries of the region. «Baltic/Continent seems to be moving towards typical summer market as more open tonnages around push freight rates lower,» a local broker commented. Freight rates have dropped by €0.5- 1/t in the basin. At the moment, transportation of 3-5,000 t of fertilizers from Klaipeda to ARAG costs low €20s/t.

Any improvements can hardly be expected on the coaster market in May. Many players still count on an upturn in exports of old crop residues from the EU ports, as well as on brisker exports of Ukrainian grain via the Danube ports given further tensions over the grain corridor functioning. However, even this will hardly be sufficient to level out the increase in tonnage list considering the absence of any prospects for the recovery of metallurgical and coal exports so far. By the way, the regular inflow of vessels from Asia is also pushing vessel list up. «In the last 2 months we have seen over 10 new purchased coasters in FE looking for cargoes towards Med/BlSea,» a broker commented to ISM.
