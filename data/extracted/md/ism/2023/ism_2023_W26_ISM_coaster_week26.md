## Market Commentary

An already dull trade has expectedly slowed down in the Azov-Black Sea basin and the Mediterranean Sea amid long holidays in Turkey and MENA countries (Eid-al- Adha). However, ship owners still try to use the upcoming grain season as an argument and aggressively raise ideas,

Rates ex Danube ports bottoming out despite overall market lull as many players out for holidays and industry events

We should admit that many charterers working in the Black Sea are accepting higher freight levels. First of all, this applies to grain exporters from Danube ports: relevant rates have added $2-3/t this week. Shipments from Ukrainian ports of Reni and Izmail remain the most active on the market: «Although shipments towards EMed have slowed down this week, offers to EU countries, especially Spain, look brisker,» a local broker commented to ISM. Rates for shipment of 5-6,000 t of wheat or corn from Reni to the Italian Adriatic have increased to $44-45/t, while those to SpanMed to high $40s/t bss 1/1. Transportation costs from the ports of Romania, Bulgaria, Georgia, Turkey and Russia have not changed much over the week. Shipment of 3-4,000 t of steel from Bartin to Algeria still costs $35-36/t.

Sea-river freight rates for shipments ex Russian Azov Sea ports have inched down by $0.5-1.5/t after a sharp upturn in 2H June. At the same time, transportation with laycans after July 10 is quoted $4-5/t above spot levels. Thus, rates for shipment of 3,000 t of wheat from Rostov to Marmara in early July are hovering at $29-31/t, while those with mid-July laycans are voiced at $34-36/t.

In the Mediterranean Sea, charterers have managed to cut rates by another $0.5-1/t amid very considerable tonnage surplus. There is almost nothing to mention on the market apart from infrequent shipments of fertilizers, minerals, construction cargoes and grain.

Negative trends prevail also in North Europe amid steadily weak cargo flow, while freight rates remain unchanged.

Rus wheat, CFR Marmara, $/t Freight rate, wheat, 3,000t, Rostov - Marmara, $/t % of freight costs in CFR price

Next week, activity will most likely grow in the Mediterranean and Black Seas after players from Muslim countries return from holidays. «We will certainly see more bids from charterers on Tuesday-Wednesday, and owners will try to raise rates respectively, but closer to the end of next week we may see that charterers can hardly lift subs with such freights - market will probably start sliding down again, searching its fair and logic level,» an experienced EU broker focused on Danube and EU grains shared his expectations with ISM. A steady rate upturn looks much more likely in the Azov Sea considering large stocks of old crop grains and serious volume of new harvest in Russia. If Turkish demand for wheat strengthens, sea-river rates are likely to resume growing in the Azov Sea. A gradual increase in offer of European grain may support ship owners in North Europe, but any noticeable rate increase can hardly be expected without a recovery in traffic of steel products.
