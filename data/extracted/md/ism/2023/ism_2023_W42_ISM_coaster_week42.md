## Market Commentary

The negative sentiment that has been dominating the small-tonnage market of the Black and Mediterranean Seas in the last few weeks has only strengthened. This is primarily caused by a noticeable decline in cargo traffic from the Danube ports as a result of reorientation of

Activity slackens on coaster market, with much fewer grain offers ex Danube ports

many exporters to shipments from deep-water ports, as well as due to pressure from state control authorities on small grain exporters (in the form of inspections). «Seems in fact Ukraine is trying to make most charterers switch to working from deep-water ports,» an experienced broker commented. At the same time, a gradual upturn in shipments from Odessa and Chornomorsk only partially compensates for the lack of Danube cargoes, as charterers here are increasingly shipping larger cargo lots. Besides, switching to grain exports from the Danube to deep-water ports inevitably leads to improvement of average small- tonnage fleet turnover in the Black Sea and Mediterranean regions, and thus to increased number of opening vessels and pressure on rates. The situation is additionally aggravated by still limited offer of grain for shipments from the ports of Romania, Bulgaria and Turkey, extremely weak traffic of steel products from Russian ports, as well as of fertilizers and steel from the eastern Mediterranean.

«Only grain shipments from Turkey and the Adriatic ports continue to keep the Mediterranean market afloat,» a local broker commented. Despite the rise in bunker prices, freight rates on most routes have dropped within $1/t. Thus, transportation of 6-7,000 t of wheat from Reni or Izmail to Alexandria already costs very low $60s/t, while rates for shipment of 4-5,000 t of wheat from Constanta to Sicily have dropped to $29/t.

In the Azov Sea, demand for sea-river fleet continues to weaken due to a further slowdown of grain exports from Russia to Turkey. Amid the decline in average idle time in the Kerch Strait to 3-4 days, freight rates have dropped by another $2-3/t in the region, while TCEs have stayed virtually unchanged (again, only due to shorter queues). Shipment of 3-5,000 t of coal from Rostov to TBS now costs $41-42/t.

Activity remains limited also on the North European coaster market: regional cargo flow is supported only by regular shipments of grain and steel products, seldom - fertilizers. At the same time, ship owners still manage to

push rates up (up €0.5-1/t), primarily due to worsening weather conditions and deterioration of fleet turnover (charterers have started to face positional shortage of vessels for spot shipments).

In the nearest future, ship owners can hardly expect any market improvements. The commodity market for steel products remains depressed in the former CIS countries and Europe, while other commodity segments do not look much better either. «Urea cargo flow is super dead in Med and BlSea, nothing is moving it. We don't expect anything new for the next 7 days,» a large fertilizers trader commented. All hopes of players are put only on grains, which are hardly enough to change the trend on the whole coaster market, especially if the tonnage list continues to grow in the Mediterranean region. Activity will hardly show any noticeable growth in North Europe as well, although we cannot rule out some upturn in November amid approaching Christmas holidays, which is quite traditional for this period.
