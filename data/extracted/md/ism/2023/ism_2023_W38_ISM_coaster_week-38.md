## Market Commentary

The small-tonnage shipping market has shown slightly reduced cargo offer both in the Black Sea and Mediterranean area, including the most active Danube market . Among positive events, players note the vessel (of slightly more than 3k dwt) that was successfully loaded

and leaved the deep-water port of Ukraine, but few ship owners are ready to seriously discuss such shipments (although long-awaited offers of steel products are entering the market). The market activity is relatively stable in the Baltic and North Seas . Ship owners still manage to keep freight rates stable in all mentioned regions, and often even insist on a slight growth (by $0.5-1/t) due to positive tone in the neighboring Handysize sector, as well as relatively short tonnage list for late September- early October laycans (less than 100 ships of 2-12k dwt are open in the Black and Marmara Seas and about 160 in the Mediterranean Sea). «Many charterers are still bewitched by ship owners, being persuaded in the so called seasonality factor (as we reach October) and even in higher BDI as a sign of stronger sentiment even on coaster market,» an experienced Turkish broker commented sarcastically. Also, owners are very often (and successfully) using the factor of higher bunker prices as an argument

(+$40-50/t during a month for VLSFO and MGO). «It is all bunker driven, it's easy for owners to hide behind the increase,» a large Mediterranean charterer commented. At the same time, according to ISM information, bunker prices have not changed over the last week, and the whole monthly increase is de facto equivalent to $0.1-0.2k daily in time-charter equivalent or $0.3-0.7/t in freight terms (for dwt cargoes). Rates for transportation of 5-7,000 t of wheat from Reni or Izmail to Alexandria have inched up to $59-60/t given 7 days of waiting time in Sulina on owner's acct. Shipment of 4-5,000 t of steel from Nemrut Bay to N.Adriatic now costs low $30s/t bss 1/1.

At the same time, the Azov Sea market trend has sharply changed to negative this week: after 4 weeks of growth, sea-river rates have significantly fallen just within a couple of days due to both the decline in Russian grain exports (caused by lower prices in Turkey) and increased spot tonnage list. «We see dozens of vessels have finally passed the Kerch Strait simultaneously and now become open for late September dates,» a broker shared his thoughts to us. It should be noted that many charterers are now holding their cargoes in hope for further freight

By contrast, the Caspian Sea market is growing little by little due to an increase in Russian and Kazakh grain shipments to Iran. During the week, rates from Astrakhan, Aktau and Turkmenbashi have risen by $0.5-2/t, while those from Russian river ports have added $3-4/t.

The cargo offer from Ukraine's deep water ports may start growing on the Black Sea freight market after September 25, which will most likely negatively affect the small-tonnage market picture. The point is that in this case many traders will start shifting to large cargo lots, thus reducing the cargo flow via the Danube ports and contributing to decreased queues and increased tonnage list in the basin. Probable decrease in export prices for wheat and corn will most likely not contribute to the freight rate growth as well, despite the approaching period of traditionally high October-November market. On the other hand, ship owners who will be ready to work from Ukraine will surely be able to get super profit. However, it is still too early to talk about the establishment of cargo traffic from the ports of Greater Odessa (and, possibly, Mykolaiv), as attempts of Russian attacks on Ukrainian port infrastructure are possible at any moment. Meanwhile, there are no preconditions for further rate hikes in the Mediterranean Sea. Demand for Turkish steel products remains weak in the region, while trade in the segment for Egyptian urea has again declined after a barely noticeable recovery as buyers insist on price cuts. In North Europe, ship owners are still putting hopes mainly on exports of

agri products, as sales of other cargoes - fertilizers, steel and coal - are inactive. The downtrend is likely to continue in the Azov Sea as the local market got greatly overheated in 1H September.
