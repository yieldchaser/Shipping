## Market Commentary

In early September, the small-tonnage shipping market continues to see a gradual decline in tonnage list, especially in the Marmara, Black and Aegean Seas, where just over 90 vessels of 2-12,000 dwt are open for September dates, down 10-15% from a couple of weeks

ago. «Obviously, we now see the so-called cumulative effect of huge congestions and delays at Sulina,» an experienced Turkish broker explains, claiming that the Danube market continues to absorb vessels. So does the Azov sea-river market, which adds some boosting effect not only directly to rates ex Russian shallow ports, but also to those from deep-sea ports. By the end of the week, rates from Ukrainian ports have risen by $0.5-2/t, those from Romania, Bulgaria, Turkey, Georgia and Russia by $0.5-1/t. Thus, transportation of 5-7,000 t of wheat from Reni or Izmail to Alexandria now costs high $50s/t (given 7 days of waiting time at Sulina on owner's acct), while rates for shipment of 3-4,000 t of wheat from Constanta to Cyprus have increased to $29-29.5/t bss 1/1.

Transportation costs from Azov Sea ports have grown much more significantly - by $8-11/t - as charterers

are forced to accept further rate upturns amid increasing exports of Russian wheat. Rates for shipment of 3-5,000 t of wheat from Rostov to Marmara are hovering at $61-63/t, while some traders are even ready to pay mid-$60s/t.

The Mediterranean Sea market also continues to strengthen, although the number of open ships has dropped less significantly that in the Marmara and Black Seas. At the moment, there are up to 80 vessels open for the next three weeks in the Eastern Mediterranean (excluding the Aegean Sea), about 20 ships in the Adriatic Sea, and about 40 vessels of 2-12k dwt in the western Mediterranean. The cargo traffic has perked up somewhat in the basin (primarily due to a further increase in grain shipments, as well as a slight recovery of steel transportation), which allowed ship owners to raise rates by $0.5-2/t.

The tonnage list has decreased also in North Europe, with around 80 ships opening in the Continent and Baltic for September dates. Grain shipments are also gaining pace in the area, while transportation of other main cargoes, mainly from Baltic Sea ports, has also perked up slightly. Freight rates on most routes have added €0.5-1/t.

In mid-September, the coaster shipping market will most likely continue to grow both in the North and South Europe, but these forecasts are conditioned only by an increase in European and Russian grain sales. Given the absence of any progress in the resumption of the Grain Initiative, the Danube ports will probably continue to operate at maximum capacity, which will continue to take a huge amount of tonnage due to downtime (unless the shelling of Ukrainian port infrastructure by Russia leads to critical consequences). Worsening weather conditions, typical for the autumn period (especially on the Baltic market), as well as further growth of small Handy and Handysize rates, will have an additional upward effect. Note that some charterers, checking the market for October, are already facing very aggressive ideas of ship owners, which exceed spot rates by $5-7/t. However, this is quite traditional behavior of ship owners during this time of the year and is not yet a necessary precursor of an actual freight rate growth, especially considering rather unfavorable situation on other commodity markets. Thus, the steel market picture remains dismal: not only is demand for coils and squares low in Turkey and North

African countries, but major exporters are still losing out to Asian suppliers. Demand for Egyptian urea also remains weak despite lower prices, while sales of fertilizers from the former CIS countries have picked up slightly
