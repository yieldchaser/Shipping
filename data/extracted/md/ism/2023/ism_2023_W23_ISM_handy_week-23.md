## Market Commentary

Supramax and Handysize owners are still unable to stop the rate decline in almost all sections of the Western Hemi.

Rates for Handy/Supra shipments down again all across the Atlantic, apparently touching bottom

is seen in the Black and Mediterranean Seas, where 5-10 vessels are competing for one cargo. During the week, inter-Med and T/A time-charter rates have dropped by another $0.5-1k daily, while transportation costs of grain, minerals and other cargoes have decreased within $1/t. F/H rates have shown a more considerable downturn: sometimes time-charter rates and TCEs are almost at the same level as inter-Med rates as many ship owners are trying to leave the eastern Mediterranean at all costs. As of now, shipment of 25-30,000 t of wheat or corn from Bulgaria to Egypt costs $12-12.5/t on average.

In the South Atlantic, the surplus of Supramax/ Ultramax tonnage also continues to grow due in part to the ongoing inflow of ballasters from the Indian Ocean, as owners cannot find suitable cargoes ex South African

ports. «Sentiment remains soft due to strong competition still existing among owners with tonnage open in South Atlantic, WAfr and those heading SAfr,» a broker mentioned. By the end of the week, front-haul Supramax/ Ultramax rates have decreased by $1-2k daily and $1-2/t. Transatlantic levels have dropped more significantly ($2-3k daily and $2-4/t down). Thus, rates for transportation of up to 50,000 t of corn from Santos to Alexandria have declined to $23/t. The Handysize segment is also weakening ($2-3k daily and $3-4/t down) for the same reason of excessive tonnage list, with the only difference that ballasters from USG and western Mediterranean dominate here. Despite tonnage outflow, rates are still rapidly declining in USG amid the shortage of cargoes. Over the week, spot rates have sagged by $2-3k daily and $2-4/t. Players working in the Baltic & Continent report only sporadic requests for transportation of Russian fertilizers and EU grains. Corresponding rates have dropped by $1-2k daily and $1- 3/t this week. Note that there is virtually no gap in time- charter rates via Russian and other ports of the region any more, although there are still charterers of Russian cargoes ready to pay well above the market average.

Only rates for shipments from South Africa continue to sag ($1k daily and $1-2/t down) amid slow coal exports and plenty of vessels open along EAfr and ballasting from ECI.

In SE Asia, Supramax rates have also stabilized closer to mid-week thanks to a recovery in shipments of Indonesian coal with 2H June laycans. At the moment, rates for shipment of 40-45,000 t of coal from Samarinda to South China are hovering at $6.5-7/t bss 1/1 given high l/d rates. Meanwhile, Handysize rates continue sliding down given the shortage of cargo offers. In the Far East, lack of steel requests is forcing ship owners to accept further rate cuts ($1-3k daily and $0.5-2/t down).

Negative trends are expected to continue in the Atlantic Ocean next week. «Looking ahead, the sentiment is bearish and market participants do not expect any positive news to push rates up,» a broker commented. For sure, the tonnage list will remain excessive in the South Atlantic so even despite quite brisk cargo flow, it will be difficult for ship owners to at least stop the rate decline. Any trend shift should hardly be expected in the northern part of the basin either: «The outlook for the region looks uninspiring, with a lot of prompt ships opening and very low cargo availability for the rest of the month,» an ISM source mentioned. In the Black Sea, ship owners put hopes only on the new grain season, which starts in July, so we

should not expect improvements in the next couple of weeks. The North European market is likely to show some upturn in steel scrap shipments amid a slight recovery of Turkish buyers, but this will hardly be sufficient to at least stop the rate drop. Despite sporadic growth of cargo flow (from Indonesia, the Persian Gulf), owners working in the APAC and Indian Ocean will generally be facing difficulties pushing rates up. The fact is that coal imports by India and China are likely to remain limited in the coming weeks amid still quite high coal stocks in both countries.
