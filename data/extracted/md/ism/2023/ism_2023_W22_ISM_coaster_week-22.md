## Market Commentary

The situation keeps worsening for coaster owners. This week's softening has been rather rapid, especially in the western part of the Black Sea and the Marmara Sea. Only Egyptian fertilizers and minerals, as well as Danube grains are still quite regularly shipped on the market. The

Small-tonnage rates stay under pressure both in North and South Europe amid sharpening cargo/tonnage imbalance

shortage of other cargoes leads to a further increase in tonnage list in both the Black Sea and the Mediterranean region. Owners are now ready to compete for literally any cargoes, increasingly competing for cargo lots that are 1.5-2 times smaller than vessel's dwt. Moreover, owners of Handysize fleet are actively showing interest in shipments of 10-15,000 t stems. Transportation costs ex Danube ports are sliding down literally every day (another $4-5/t down over the week). «Danube ports lost their charm, TCE levels are not good now compared to shipments from other ports of the basin,» a ship owner commented to ISM. Thus, rates for shipment of 5-6,000 t of wheat or corn from Reni or Izmail to Alexandria have declined to $43-44/t. Rates for shipments from Romanian, Bulgarian, Mediterranean, Baltic and North Sea ports have inched down by $1-1.5/t. Only rates ex Russian Black Sea and Azov Sea ports hold steady (corresponding freights reached their bottom a couple of weeks ago). Owners of sea-river tonnage even

tried to raise rates ex Azov Sea ports late last week and early this week, but unsuccessfully. Transportation of 3-5,000 t of wheat from Rostov to Marmara still costs $25-26/t.

There are not many preconditions for an upturn in small-tonnage cargo traffic. Some players are still hoping for the recovery of old crop grain shipments and increased demand for wheat and steel products in Turkey (as political certainty was set in the country), but anyway, owners globally suffer from lower export prices for almost all major cargoes, particularly for grains, urea, steel products, coal, some kinds of minerals. In these circumstances, traders will hardly be able to pay more than they do now. According to ISM, further freight rate decline is quite possible in the next couple of weeks considering huge vessel list and further softening of the Handysize segment. The situation may change only when the new grain season starts in July
