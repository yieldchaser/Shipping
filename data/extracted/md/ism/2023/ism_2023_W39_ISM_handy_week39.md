## Market Commentary

The Supramax/Ultramax and Handysize shipping markets have started to cool down in almost all the freight sections, partly due to the start of prolonged holidays in China and South Korea.

Pressure mounts on Supramax/Ultramax and Handy market as spot demand is weakening gradually almost in all freight sections

In ECSA and USG, charterers are actively trying to lower rates amid some decline in grain offer for October laycans, especially on transatlantic routes. Although brisker shipments from West Africa provide strong support to owners of vessels opening in the South Atlantic, the surplus of vessels is getting increasingly clear on the market. Nevertheless, Supramax owners still manage to keep rates at last week's peaks. The same applies to the USG market, where ship owners manage to keep Supramax rates stable due to steady shipments of petcoke, as well as a slight recovery of grain offer for transportation from the USA to China. Shipment of 50,000 t of petcoke from Houston to Pipavav still costs $47-48/t. An acute shortage of vessels in USEC allows Handysize owners to raise corresponding rates, while the outlined balance in USG allows keeping freights stable. Meanwhile, USG Handysize rates have sagged by $1k daily and $1-2/t over the week.

Wheat / corn, 50,000t wheat, Chornomorsk - Cigading / Siwandan (10000x/7000x), $/t

Baltic & Continent. Following a protracted growth, rates have started to decline on all key routes ($1-3k daily and $2-3/t down this week). The cargo traffic has slowed down noticeably this week, while the tonnage list is growing literally every day. «Continent market is coming off quite heavily. There is hardly any prompt cargo,» a charterer commented. «The ones asking high money are still open. Ships missing cancelling have been canceled, while last week were getting extension,» an operator added. It should be noted that there is still a gap in ideas for shipments ex Russian ports. Turkish ship owners are often dumping when discussing such shipments, while European owners still ask for quite high premiums.

Earlier in the week, Supramax owners working in the Black and Mediterranean Seas were still able to preserve last week's positive momentum, but closer to mid-week the market started to cool down as well. There is no further growth in the Handysize segment either. «It seems market has topped for now,» an EU operator commented. Spot freights remain unchanged, while rates for forward dates (December-January) are noticeably lower than spot levels ($1.5-2.5k daily lower for December-January dates and $5-7k daily lower for January dates). Note, however, that excitement around Ukrainian exports remains, the overwhelming number of offers comes towards China and Spain. It should be noted that while the segment for shipments from Ukraine is in the stage of searching for levels, the bid/offer gap often reaches $20/t and more. EWRI and so called crew bonus also vary greatly. Besides, many ship owners are not ready to consider offers from Ukraine, expecting to see upcoming successful calls in the next couple of weeks. Shipment of 30,000 t of wheat from Chornomorsk to SpanMed is estimated by ISM at high $60s/t, while transportation of 60-65,000 t of corn to S.China costs low $80s/t bss 1/1.

The APAC Supramax/Ultramax market has also started to soften ahead of the Golden Week in China and South Korea. The north of the region is affected by slower grain shipments from NOPAC ports, while the south suffers decreased traffic of Indonesian coal. The sentiment

is worsening also in the Indian Ocean, as the lull in SE Asia leads to longer vessel list off Indian coasts (particularly this applies to Supramax tonnage, while the surplus of Handysize fleet is much less noticeable). Moreover, players report slightly decreased coal offer from South Africa and fixing activity ex PG. Time-charter rates have sagged by $2- 3k daily in all parts of the Eastern Hemi, with transportation costs of main cargoes down by $1-3/t.

Next week, trade may perk up in the South Atlantic as new requests for soybean and corn shipments for mid-

October laycans start entering the market more actively. Besides, owners of vessels open in N.Brazil (especially Handysize) are actively considering the USG area as an option for repositioning as ships open in USG are being actively absorbed by the USEC market.

In the short term, owners working in the Continent will be facing difficulties resuming the rate growth even despite a slight recovery of European steel scrap imports by Turkey. The fact is that given tough competition with Black Sea suppliers (including Ukraine forced to dumping), it will be extremely difficult for European grain exporters to increase volumes. Meanwhile, the growth of October tonnage list looks quite stable so far. In the Black Sea, much will still depend on the success of Ukrainian shipments. If Russian terrorism can be avoided or successfully resisted, demand for tonnage in the area will surely be high throughout October.

Next week, trade will most likely continue slowing down in the Asia-Pacific region amid the Golden Week in China and South Korea.
