# New Building

In tankers this week, Dalian announced contracting four firm plus two optional LNG dual fuel 300k dwt VLCCs with an unnamed owner, reported to be clients of Capital, with the firm vessels set to deliver from their Tianjin facility throughout 2027. DH Shipbuilding announced contracts for three firm 154k dwt Shuttle Tankers for an unnamed owner, reported to be clients of Maran, with the first vessel expected to deliver in November 2026 and the second and third vessel in 1Q 2027. NYK Stolt announced contracting six firm 38k dwt Stainless Steel Chemical tankers at Nantong Xiangyu, with the first vessel expected to deliver in 4Q 2026, two vessels in 2027, two vessels in 2028 and the final vessel in 2029. Finally, GEFO announced a large order of ten firm 3.8k dwt Stainless Steel chemical tankers at Nantong Xiangyu, with the vessels set to deliver through 2026, 2027 and 2028. They will hold LNG ready class notations.

In dry bulk, Hayfin announced contracting two firm 100k dwt Coal Carriers at Oshima, with both vessels set to deliver with 2026. Huayan Star Shipping ordered two firm 65k dwt Bulk carriers for domestic trade at Haitong Offshore, with the vessels slated for delivery in December 2026 and March 2027.

In Gas Carriers, Samsung announced contracts for fifteen firm 174k CBM LNG Carriers with Middle Eastern interests, reported to be QatarEnergy. The vessels will deliver from 2H 2026, throughout 2027 and into 2028, with the final vessel delivering in October 2028.

Finally, in Ro-Ro’s, Smyril Line announced contracting two firm 3,300LM Ro-Ro’s at Yantai Raffles, with both vessels slated for delivery in 2026.

# Recycling

## Dragons Den!

As we enter Chinese New Year (Year of the Dragon), the recycling market certainly does not resemble the reputed attributes of the dragon – the market is most certainly not feeling endlessly energetic and full of vitality. There is now more demand for ships from Bangladesh and India, however the Indian market is falling by the wayside with no intent of trying to compete with their counterparts and only green recycling tonnage looks set to head to the Alang shores, if and when they become available.

The major disruption to the shipping industry in general, caused by the Red Sea and Russian/Ukraine conflicts and limited Panama Canal transits, is driving freight rate spikes and charter gains in both the wet/dry and container sectors, the latter especially bouncing back to rates seen during the Covid period. It remains to be seen exactly how long the recent impact from the Red Sea disruption will continue, but the potential recycling looks set to be ‘pushed back’ again towards 2025 whilst these elevated freight rates persist.
