# Desk Talk

Tanker rates have hit record highs this week. VLCC earnings are now averaging over USD 600,000 p/d globally, with rates near Hormuz reaching as high as USD 800,000-1.5 million p/d. This is being driven by a surge in oil shuttled through the Strait via ship-to-ship transfers, now above 7.5 million barrels a day, as Saudi Arabia routes more crude through the Gulf. Freight costs have climbed to 15% of the value of a barrel of oil, far above the historical norm of 2%. Product tankers (Aframax, LR2, MR) are rising too, on stronger flows out of Asia and the US Gulf. Rates could ease if talks between the US and Iran, taking place alongside this week's UN General Assembly, lead to Iran reopening the Strait.

A possible US ban on diesel exports is also being seen as a risk for the product tanker market. On Dry bulk, Capesize rates held around USD 52,500 p/d, and Panama Canal dry bulk traffic is improving as the grain season boosts demand, and water levels recover.

On the Tanker side, buying interest remains focused on prompt tonnage, MR2 up to VLCC, in the Singapore-AG range, with vessels around 20 years old being the priority.

In the Dry bulk segment, enquiries are centered on Capesizes around 15 years old, whilst Far Eastern Buyers are sourcing Supramax and Panamax tonnage in the 15-20 year bracket.

## Dry Cargo

On the dry bulk side, negotiations remain ongoing across several segments, but no concluded sales can be reported this week.

## Tanker

On the Suezmax segment, the ECO-M/E unit **SPEEDWAY** (ABT 158K DWT, 2017, HYUNDAI SAMHO) was sold for USD 99 m, while on the LR2 side, the **EUROINTEGRITY** (ABT 105K DWT, 2009, HHI) was concluded at USD 60m.
