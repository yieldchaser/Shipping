# Desk Talk

Crude export volumes through the Strait of Hormuz have recovered to near pre-crisis levels, supported by using shuttle tankers and ship-to-ship (STS) transfers, with Aramco reportedly considering this approach as a longer-term solution. Under the revised arrangement, responsibility shifts from buyers, who traditionally secured tankers for loading at Saudi ports, to exporters, who assume the risk of transporting crude through Hormuz to the Gulf of Oman. Buyers then take delivery and arrange onward transportation using a separate vessel. This shift suggests a greater vessel requirement per barrel transported: before the crisis, moving 1 mb/d of crude from the Middle East to the Far East was estimated to require an average of 24 dedicated VLCCs, while under the shuttle model, this requirement increases to 25.5 VLCCs, equivalent to a 6% rise.

On the S&P tanker side, as crude carrier tonnage remains historically high in demand, we are now also noticing an increased spike in interest in product tankers, namely LRs and MRs, albeit the possible US diesel export ban. China has also reportedly resumed its October product exports after a brief pause during its Golden Week, further supporting the segment. More specifically, Buyers are actively looking mainly for non-eco product tankers of around 15 years of age that can give prompt delivery. Asset prices continue to climb across all tanker segments all the way up-to VLCC.

On the dry side, we are noticing activity on modern eco Kamsarmaxes, especially Japanese-built units, as well as a focus on Capesizes, as earnings continue to hold firm. In the geared space, Handysize vessels have attracted notable interest this week, with non-eco 15-year-old tonnage appearing to be Buyers’ main focus.

## Dry Cargo

In the Kamsarmax sector, the scrubber-fitted **WORLD DIANA** (ABT 81,190 DWT, 2020, OSHIMA SHIPBUILDING) was sold at USD 41m.

In the Panamax sector, **AH GLOBE** (ABT 74,432 DWT, 2005, HUDONG ZHONGHUA) changed hands at USD 11.5m, basis delivery within 2026.

Finally, following an invitation for bids earlier this week, Japanese Owners sold the Handysize **LOCOMOTION** (ABT 38,046 DWT, 2013, SHIMANAMI SHIPYARD, C:4X30.5T) at a price in the high USD 19m's.

## Tanker

On Tankers, SFL has concluded sale of three scrubber-fitted Suezmaxes, namely **MARLIN SANTORINI**, **MARLIN SICILY** and **MARLIN SHIKOKU** (ABT 156K DWT, 2019, NTS, SCRUBBER) to clients of Trafigura at undisclosed levels.

The same Buyers and Sellers also engaged in a transaction involving four LR2 units, the scrubber-fitted sisters **SFL PANTHER**, **SFL PUMA, SFL LION** and **SFL TIGER** (ABT 115K DWT, 2015/2015, GUANGZHOU LONGXUE, SCRUBBER), with price again undisclosed.
