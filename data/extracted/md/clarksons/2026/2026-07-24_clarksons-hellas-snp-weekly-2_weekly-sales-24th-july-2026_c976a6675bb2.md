# Desk Talk

With the conflict in the Middle East Gulf escalating further, this week’s incidents mark the first Houthi attacks on vessels within the Red Sea in over 6 months, and more specifically on two Saudi-linked tankers, a 2003 built LR2 and a 2007 built VLCC. Moreover, a review of the crude tankers and VLGCs that have departed Yanbu recently shows that only about half of the vessels are taking the risk of exiting the Red Sea through the Gulf of Aden or the Suez Canal, a sign which is consistent with the overall uncertainty in the region.

Amid the escalated tensions at the Strait of Hormuz, two scenarios can be pointed out to have bolstered the oil market: If all current Yanbu crude exports destined for Asia were rerouted via the Suez Canal and then through Cape of Good Hope, global crude tonne-mile demand could theoretically increase. Under an alternative scenario in which Yanbu exports are redirected primarily to Europe, displacing U.S.-to-Europe crude flows, with those U.S. volumes instead moving to Asia, global crude tonne-mile demand could increase as well.

The S&P wet market has in turn been once again positively influenced by the increased freight demand, with the observed tanker appetite being reflected in enquiry count, and the increasing asset prices. Out of all enquiries, 15–20-year-old VLCCs, Suezmaxes and Aframaxes stand out, while product tankers spanning a wide age range are also receiving interest. There is a number of modern eco MR2s that are also receiving attention and are being negotiated at firm levels.

On the dry side, freight market has strengthened, driven by a sharp increase in Capesize earnings, supporting stronger demand and sentiment in the larger vessel segment. At the same time, demand remains healthy across all vessel sizes, with the geared segment, particularly Ultramax and Supramax vessels, attracting the majority of the attention. For comparison, we are hearing of a 53K 2011 Chinese Supramax being sold in the low USD 15m's, whilst four months ago, a same aged sister vessel achieved a price of USD 12m, basis surveys due delivery.

## Dry Cargo

In the Ultramax segment, c/o OBE Ships Maritime have acquired the **SEACON TOKYO** (ABT 67K DWT, 2023, TSUNEISHI ZHOUSHAN) for USD 41.6m. Meanwhile, the two sister vessels **BENJAMIN & BRITTA OLDENDORFF** (ABT 62K DWT, 2020, OSHIMA) have been sold to c/o Meghna Shipping at USD 37.5m each.

On the Supramax front, Japanese Owners have concluded the sale of the two sister vessels **UNITED & VENUS HALO** (ABT 56K DWT, 2012, I.H.I. KURE) achieving USD 19m each.

## Tanker

Although no new tanker sales can be reported, several negotiations are understood to be ongoing this week.
