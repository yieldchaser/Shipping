# Desk Talk

In the tanker space, geopolitical risk remains high after seven tankers were reportedly hit in Hormuz. Talk of lifting US sanctions is unlikely to offer much relief, since the 170 sanctioned VLCCs average 22 years of age and only 25 hold valid class certificates. On the freight side, VLCC spot earnings corrected by almost 25% this week to $541,000/day for eco-scrubber units. The weakness started in the Atlantic, where US crude bookings to Asia for November are running at roughly half of last month's volume. Forward rates have nonetheless held firm, with 2027 US Gulf–China FFAs at $213,000/day, and the market remains tight. Product tankers continue to strengthen as two-thirds of the LR2 fleet has switched into dirty trades, further tightening clean supply.

On the wet S&P front, prices are climbing week on week, and sellers' ideas keep shifting as owners weigh selling against trading their ships. Despite these elevated levels, several players are actively negotiating, a clear sign of confidence that current earnings justify today's prices. The firm sentiment has carried over to MR2s and LR1s, with increased activity on both dirty and clean units. Prompt tonnage continues to command significant premiums, with VLCC resale at $245m, up from $145m at mid-year, and a 10-year-old MR at $44m, above its 2024 peak.

The dry bulk market has had a volatile week. Capesize spot rates fell 25% last week but rebounded 3.6% today to $30,500/day on stronger Australia–China iron ore freight. December FFAs are trading at $40,300/day, some 32% above spot, signaling expectations of a year-end recovery. This is supported by a 17% rebound in global iron ore exports last week, led by Brazil. More broadly, fundamentals remain positive, underpinned by longer-haul iron ore and bauxite trades from Guinea's Simandou project, resilient coal demand and a limited orderbook. Weaker global growth and grain demand remain the key risks.

In the dry S&P market, vintage Supramaxes and Panamaxes continue to attract increased interest, particularly from Far Eastern buyers and for units able to offer prompt delivery in the region. Capesize demand has eased momentarily on the back of softer short-term rates, though asset prices remain stable, supported by positive long-term expectations.

## Dry Cargo

On the Post-Panamax segment, Japanese Owners have concluded the sale of the **MARITIME KING** (ABT 96K DWT, 2011, IMABARI) at USD 19m.

Moving to the geared segment, the Ultramax **LIBRA CONFIDENCE** (ABT 63K DWT, 2019, NEW DAYANG) was sold for USD 33.5m via online platform. Meanwhile, the c/o Ugland Marine have sold the Supramax **KRISTINITA** (ABT 58K DWT, 2011, TSUNEISHI ZHOUSHAN) at levels around USD 20.5m.

On the Handysize segment, c/o Wisdom Marine have sold the 2 x sister vessels **PAIWAN WISDOM** and **DAIWAN WISDOM** (ABT 32K DWT, 2010, HAKODATE) enbloc to US-based Buyers for USD 23.5m.

## Tanker

On the product tankers segment, c/o Odfjell have sold their 2 x Korean built MR2 **BOW ELM** and **BOW LIND** (ABT 46K DWT, 2011, SLS) at xs USD 62m enbloc basis surveys passed.
