# Desk Talk

The main discussion over the past few months has consistently been the significant increase in Hormuz crude oil flows and the associated surge in tanker earnings. Following the onset of the US-Iran crisis, cargo volumes shipped from the MEG to the East initially remained relatively modest amid the threats posed. However, shuttle volumes through the strait subsequently increased significantly, reaching an estimated average of 10.5 mb/d in October. This provided a small subset of the tanker fleet with the opportunity to become considerably more productive, as vessels only needed to carry cargoes over short distances for ship-to-ship transfers.

Today, after a largely uninterrupted 6-month-rally of upward Tanker momentum, and in the wake of the Democrats’ election victory, we are noticing a drop in tanker equity valuations reflecting a substantial decline in freight rates. It’s important to underline that a lower war premium on oil prices does not immediately translate to normal shipping conditions, as long as the existing disruption remains unresolved. Even if the risk of further escalation declines, shipping conditions may remain abnormal because of security concerns, longer routes, shuttle voyages, ship-to-ship transfers and waiting times.

Despite the recent geopolitical developments, asset values remain firm and S&P activity continues to be healthy overall. In the tanker market, it remains to be seen what implications the election outcome may have and how lasting these effects will be. In the dry bulk segment, we observe a slightly subdued buying appetite in the Capesize sector. On a more positive note, however, demand for Panamax vessels from Far Eastern buyers stands out as particularly strong while geared tonnage remains in high demand across both basins, as reflected in firm earnings and asset prices.
