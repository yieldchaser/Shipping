# Desk Talk

Market sentiment this week continued to be shaped by escalating security risk across two flashpoints. Hormuz transits stayed near record lows, with attacks continuing, and the Black Sea saw a fresh spike in hostilities.

On Tankers, Suezmax demand remained strong, spanning from the US Gulf, West Africa, the Mediterranean and now Yanbu, while VLCCs are also seeing active chartering activity across multiple loading regions, including inside Hormuz itself. Ship-to-ship activity off Oman and Fujairah continues to build as exporters find ways to move crude despite ongoing Strait disruption, and there are early signs that some inner-Gulf export activity is cautiously resuming, though still limited to risk-tolerant players.

Crucially, market strength is no longer seen as purely a spot-market phenomenon, but FFAs have also moved up meaningfully, signaling that the market expects elevated earnings, and a substantial Hormuz risk premium to persist well into next year. This shift in sentiment, from a short-term disruption story to a structurally longer-term outlook is a key support for asset values, reinforcing Owners' conviction on firm pricing.

Beyond tankers, attention is also turning to a strengthening El Niño and its potential to disrupt the Panama Canal later this year, with early restrictions already being announced. Dry bulk sentiment remains firm, with Capesize rates assessed at USD 40,200/day, supported by rising Chinese iron ore imports (up to 105.3 million tons in the January-July period from 99.6 million tons a year earlier) as domestic production declines.

Tanker enquiries this week remained concentrated on larger tonnage, with VLCCs offering prompt delivery near the AG standing out. On the dry side, enquiries were more evenly distributed across all segments, with a particular buying interest towards Capesizes.

## Dry Cargo

In the Ultramax space, the Greek-owned eco-type M/E **AMARYLLIS** (ABT 63K DWT, 2013, SINOPACIFIC DAYANG, CR: 4X36T) was sold at a price in the region of USD 24m to Chinese interests. By comparison, around mid-June, the **ULTRA SAKA** (ABT 63K DWT, 2013, CHENGXI, CR: 4X30T) was sold for USD 23.25m

Elsewhere in the Supramax sector, C/O Byzantine Maritime have concluded the sale of their **MARIANNA** (ABT 56K DWT, 2010, I.H.I. (YOKOHAMA), CR: 4X30T) which achieved a price of around USD 17m. Additionally, the one-year older **LILA MUNDRA** (ABT 57K DWT, 2009, STX DALIAN, CR: 4X30T) was sold by Lila Global at USD 12.5m.
