## Desk Commentary

### Desk Talk

#### Tankers

Tankers are closing the week stronger after some volatility in rates, with eco VLCC and MR earnings rebounding 15.5% and 17.6%, respectively, from last week's close. At the same time, the Strait of Hormuz has become more uneasy with several tankers reportedly attacked, while market players engaging with trades in the region enjoying lucrative returns. Aframax to VLCC tonnage that can give prompt delivery remains scarce and highly sought after, although we now also observe rekindled buying appetite for product tankers, ranging from MR1 to LR1.

S&P activity has remained on an overall strong course as we have entered September month. Freight rates maintain very high numbers across both dry and wet spaces, which continue to underpin asset prices and S&P appetite, albeit the uncertainty associated with the ongoing geopolitical disturbances in the Black Sea and the Middle East.

More specifically, this Friday, Capesize spot rates reached $58,000 per day, representing new multi-year highs, namely since December 2023 when they briefly reached $60,000. To capitalize on the firm earnings particularly driven by the Atlantic basin, Cape / Nuke buyers are coming on the market, with the sweet spot appearing to be units at ~10-20 years of age. For reference a 15-year-old Japan built Cape is priced in the region of high USD 30's m today representing roughly a 70% increase in value over a same aged ship in December 2023. Moreover, a healthy number of enquiries for both Eco and Non- Eco ~10-15-year-old Panamaxes and Kamsarmaxes has been observed with a slight preference toward Japan builds.

### Dry Cargo S&P

In the Supramax segment, the FLC HAPPINESS (ABT 57K DWT, 2009, TAIZHOU KOUAN) was sold to Chinese interests for USD 12.9m, while the scrubber-fitted COLUMBIA RIVER (ABT 56K DWT, 2006, MITSUI TAMANO, SCRUBBER) was also concluded to Chinese interests for low USD 13m.
