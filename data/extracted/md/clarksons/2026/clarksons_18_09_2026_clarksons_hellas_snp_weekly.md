## Desk Commentary

### Desk Talk

#### Tankers

The tanker market remains in the spotlight, now with Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline shutdown amplifying the impact of the disruption of the Strait of Hormuz even more. Wednesday's developments suggested that a restart of the pipeline is likely, with around half of its total 7 mb/d capacity expected to be restored in the near term, while a full return to operations is considered realistic by October. At the same time, shuttling / STS operations that free up trapped cargo which is loaded at the Gulf of Oman continue to be preferred by shipowners over performing a crossing of the Strait. Against this backdrop, oil buyers are locking in unprecedented refining profits, and shipowners are benefiting from freight rates that have hit all-time highs. For tanker S&P this suggests ever-increasing asset prices and tonnage demand, with the recent spike being especially notable for larger-sized crude carriers ranging from Aframax to VLCC.

#### Dry Cargo

On the dry side, activity remains healthy, with the elevated earnings, asset prices and S&P transaction volume showing no signs of easing back. Although Capesize earnings have pulled back from the highs of early September's rally, they remain firm and continue to drive the market's positive momentum. Overall, the dry bulk sector is underpinned by an exceptionally strong grain trade, and with the expected seasonal peak in Guinean bauxite exports approaching alongside softer coal demand, the market looks well poised heading into the fourth quarter. Enquiry levels reflect broad-based interest spanning the full size range, from Handysize up to Capesize tonnage, generally limited to units up to 10 years old.

### Dry Cargo S&P

In the Capesize segment, the HIGHLAND (ABT 174K DWT, 2006, SHANGHAI WAIGAOQIAO) was sold for USD 25m to Chinese interests. Increased activity was recorded on the Kamsarmax side, where the scrubber-fitted BW JAPAN (ABT 82K DWT, 2019, TSUNEISHI CEBU) was acquired by c/o Great Eastern Shipping Company for low-mid USD 38m basis delivery November 2026 – January 2027 range, whilst the OSAKA STAR (ABT 85K DWT, 2016, SASEBO) was committed at close to USD 34m. The CK VENTURE (ABT 82K DWT, 2012, DALIAN) achieved levels around USD 19m. On the Panamax segment, Far Eastern interests bought the MELIA (ABT 76K DWT, 2005, TSUNEISHI JAPAN) for USD 11.5m, whilst the vintage DOLPHIN 76 (ABT 74K DWT, 2002, NAMURA) was bought by Chinese interests for USD 7.85m. In the Supramax sector, the SKY KNIGHT (ABT 58K DWT, 2012, ONISHI) after inviting offers earlier this month, was sold for USD 21.5m.

### Dry Cargo S&P

The LUZON (ABT 56K DWT, 2010, MITSUI TAMANO) was sold in the low USD 18m range. Lastly, the semi-OHBS Handysize CRIMSON WYOMING (ABT 33K DWT, 2015, KANDA ZOSENHSO) after inviting offers earlier this month, was committed to Greek interests for USD 18m.

### Tanker S&P

On Tankers, the LR2 PS AMALFI (ABT 108,958, 2010, HUDONG-ZHONGHUA) was concluded at levels of xs USD 45m.
