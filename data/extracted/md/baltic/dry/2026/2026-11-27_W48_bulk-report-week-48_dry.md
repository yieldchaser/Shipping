# Bulk Report – Week 48

**Date:** 27 November 2026  
**Publisher:** Baltic Exchange | **Category:** Dry Bulk  
**Original URL:** [http://www.balticexchange.com/en/data-services/WeeklyRoundup/dry/news/2026/bulk-report-week-48.html](http://www.balticexchange.com/en/data-services/WeeklyRoundup/dry/news/2026/bulk-report-week-48.html)

### Capesize

The market enjoyed a positive week, with momentum building as the Pacific continued to lead the advance, underpinned by consistent miner participation and solid operator demand.

The BCI 182 5TC climbed from $43,346 on Monday, peaking at $46,250 on Thursday before easing to finish the week at $45,558, reflecting a broadly improving market sentiment. In the Pacific, C5 strengthened from the mid-$15s to the high-$16s by Thursday. Although the initial surge moderated as the week progressed, continued enquiry and increasingly firm owner expectations saw fixtures move into the mid-$16s, with an early December cargo achieving the high $16s, before rates eased back towards $16 by Friday’s close.

The Atlantic presented a more divided picture. The North Atlantic gained traction as the week progressed, with improving sentiment and firmer Transatlantic fixtures lending further impetus. The South Atlantic, however, remained notably subdued, with limited enquiry and a persistent gap between bids and offers restricting activity. C3 edged higher into the mid-$35s, although gains remained modest given the lack of real momentum in the basin.

Overall, the market finished the week below its midweek highs but comfortably ahead of Monday’s opening levels, with the Pacific remaining the principal driver and the North Atlantic providing additional support, while the South Atlantic continued to lag.

### Panamax-Kamsarmax

The Panamax market strengthened throughout the week, with consistent gains across both basins.

In the Atlantic, brokers reported sustained December demand with tightening prompt tonnage and weather-related delays supporting firmer rates, particularly in the north. East Coast South America also remained well supported, with healthy fronthaul enquiry maintaining competition for available vessels. A 75,000-dwt open West Africa fixed an East Coast South America fronthaul at $21,750 plus $475,000 ballast bonus, whilst an 82,000-dwt open East Coast India fixed around $25,750 for a similar trip.

In the Pacific, renewed North Pacific grain enquiry and improving Australian demand helped absorb available tonnage, encouraging owners to raise their expectations. An 82,000-dwt fixed a North Pacific grain round at $19,000, while Australian business included an 82,000-dwt fixing at $21,500 to South China and another 82,000-dwt at $22,000 for a similar trade. Indonesian business remained selective, with owners initially reluctant to commit to shorter trips, although improving Pacific sentiment helped lift fixing levels as the week progressed. A 76,000-dwt fixed an Indonesian trip to South Korea at $19,000, while other business to South China was reported at similar levels. Period activity remained evident, with an 82,000-dwt fixing for 11 to 13 months at $17,500.

### Ultramax/Supramax

It has been a week of consolidation, with little change in the overall market levels compared to last week.

The US Gulf again saw some strong fixtures, despite many predicting the positional squeeze last week would be short lived, with a 63,000-dwt fixed from New Orleans to Egypt with coal at $42,500 and a similar type achieving $39,000 for a trip India with petcoke. The South Atlantic was balanced with little movement evident, seeing a 63,000-dwt fixed Santos to the Far East at $19,500 + $950,000 ballast bonus, and the strength on the Continent was maintained with another 63,000-dwt fixed for scrap from Immingham to the Egyptian Mediterranean at $34,250. The Mediterranean was subdued with market levels dropping in minimal trading. North Asia bottomed out and a few better fixtures began to emerge with a 63,000-dwt fixed from South Korea for a NoPac round voyage at $23,500. However, further south, rates were capped by vessels ballasting from the Indian Ocean as that area remained starved of fresh enquiry and owners were forced to look at other markets. This was evidenced by a 63,000-dwt open South Africa fixing at $27,000 for a trip via East Coast South America to China with grains, and a 61,000-dwt open Bangladesh fixing delivery passing Singapore via Indonesia to China at $20,000. With spot rates stabilising in the North, short period interest returned with a 63,000-dwt reported at $22,250 and a 61,000-dwt fixed at close to $22,000 for 3 to 5 months, whereas longer period trades were concluded on an index-linked basis.

### Handysize

The Handysize market saw generally quiet trading over the reporting period, with regional trends remaining mixed. The Continent and Mediterranean remained broadly stable, with fresh interest early in the period giving way to limited activity as participants attended events in Geneva. Reported business included a 37,000-dwt vessel open in Belfast on 25 November, fixed via Harghshamn to Iskenderun with scrap at $32,000.

The US Gulf retained a firm tone, supported by healthy demand and tightening tonnage availability ahead of Thanksgiving. In contrast, the South Atlantic lost some momentum as prompt cargo availability eased and tonnage lists began to lengthen, leaving rates around or slightly below previous levels. A 36,000-dwt vessel open in Sao Sebastiao on 25 November was fixed from Recalada to North Brazil with grains at $30,000. Activity across Asia was limited, and sentiment varied by location. Reports highlighted healthy steel demand from China and active trading on North Pacific to Southeast Asia routes. A 38,000-dwt vessel open in Kaohsiung on 26 November was fixed for a trip to the US Gulf at around the $19,000s.
