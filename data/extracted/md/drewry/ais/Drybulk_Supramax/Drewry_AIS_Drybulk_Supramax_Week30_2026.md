## Market Commentary

### Summer slowdown weighs on Atlantic while Asia remains supported

- The Supramax tonne-miles index edged down 3% to reach 117.7 in week 30, as the summer holiday season reduced new cargo enquiries across most regions. Softer spot rates reflected limited early-to-mid-August cargoes, particularly in the US Gulf, where greater vessel availability forced owners to compete for vessel employment.

- The US Gulf faced the sharpest correction as a lack of new cargoes and a growing list of vessels weighed on fronthaul and transatlantic rates. The Continent also softened amid weaker Turkish scrap and fertiliser demand, while the East Mediterranean remained comparatively resilient, supported by new grain cargoes and stronger premiums for owners willing to trade via the Red Sea.

- The Pacific lost some momentum as enquiries from North Asia eased and vessel availability increased across Southeast Asia and East Coast India. However, steady Chinese demand for Indonesian coal, continued South African cargoes in the Indian Ocean, and improving Australian export activity helped limit further downside.
