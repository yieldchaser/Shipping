### Resilient tonne-mile demand offset rising vessel availability.

1 content_summary

* Aframax utilisation eased slightly to 90.3% last week as vessel availability increased, particularly in the Atlantic Basin where rising ballast inventories and softer underway tonnage pointed to a more comfortable prompt vessel position. Nevertheless, tonne-mile demand remained relatively firm at 94.5, suggesting the decline was driven more by supply-side factors than weaker crude trade flows.

* East of Suez Aframax fundamentals remained comparatively stronger, with rising underway tonnage and a higher proportion of laden vessels pointing to robust cargo employment across Middle Eastern and Asian trade routes. In contrast, softer cargo absorption and increasing vessel availability in the Atlantic Basin contributed to a gradual easing of market tightness.

* The increase in laden anchor days, particularly around Indian and Pacific import hubs, highlights continued operational delays and discharge waiting times. These inefficiencies reduced effective fleet productivity and helped absorb additional vessel supply entering the market, limiting downside pressure on fundamentals.
