## Market Commentary

### Atlantic softness weighs on Handysize rates as Pacific holds firm

- The tonne-miles index declined slightly by 0.8 percentage points to 117, in tandem with the falling Baltic benchmark HS7TC, which fell 494 points WoW to $16,466/day. In the Atlantic, the US Gulf and South Atlantic lost momentum as more vessel availability and limited fresh enquiries widened the supply-demand imbalance, placing downward pressure on rates.

- The Pacific market continued to outperform the Atlantic, supported by sufficient cargo demand to absorb increasing tonnage. Although Australian grain remained limited and Southeast Asia saw a gradual build-up in vessel availability, steady activity in the Far East and continued fresh cargoes from the Indian Ocean helped prevent a sharper contraction.

- Market participants continued to monitor Typhoon Bavi for potential supply disruptions across the Pacific. At the same time, growing vessel availability in Southeast Asia suggests that a stronger August cargo will be required to prevent further rate erosion, while improving cargo visibility in the Atlantic will be essential to absorb oversupply and restore market balance.
