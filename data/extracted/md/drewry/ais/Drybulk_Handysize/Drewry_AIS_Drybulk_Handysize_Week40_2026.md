## Market Commentary

### Higher fleet utilisation and tonne-mile demand support Handysize rates despite easing congestion

- Handysize utilisation increased further, reflecting stronger fleet employment. The improvement coincided with firmer regional and minor-bulk activity, while other Handysize cargoes such as grain, fertilisers, steel products and forest products continued to support vessel deployment.

- The tonne-mile index rose WoW, strengthening the underlying demand picture. Longer-haul grain sourcing and changing trade routes could provide additional support to Handysize shipping demand over the remaining weeks of 2026.

- Laden congestion declined sharply across regions, including China, releasing capacity and improving vessel supply. This may partly offset the impact of firmer demand on freight rates in the region.

Terms
Define
1
Handysize vessels
Small Dry Bulk vessels, and are in the size range 10,000-40,000 dwt.
2
Trading status
Status of vessels has been derived primarily from the navigation field within the AIS message. Vessels which do not have IMO number have not been included in the fleet.
Furthermore, vessels that have not returned AIS message for at least 30 days, have been excluded from the analysis of fleet status while vessels that have not returned an
AIS message for up to 30 days are assumed to have the same trading status as when last seen.
3
Laden dwt miles
Average distance travelled per week across the laden target fleet multiplied by the average vessel deadweight (or capacity)
4
Bunker price
Price of the VLSFO at Singapore Port
5
% Laden
Share of laden vessels in total fleet excluding vessels under repair
6
East
East to Suez Canal, comprising China, Pacific, Indian Ocean and Oceania
7
West
West of Suez Canal, comprising Atlantic region
8
HS4_38
Spot rates from Baltic Exchange on the US Gulf or north coast South America to Skaw-Passero route
9
HS7_38
Spot rates from Baltic Exchange for North China/South Korea/Japan to South East Asia route
10
At anchor
Vessels with navigation status ‘At Anchor’ in the waiting zone near port area
11
Moored
Vessels moored in port for loading or discharging
12
Underway
Vessels currently at sea and are currently underway towards their destination
