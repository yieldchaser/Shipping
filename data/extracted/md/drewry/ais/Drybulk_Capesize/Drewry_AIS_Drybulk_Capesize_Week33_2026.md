## Market Commentary

### Pacific correction offsets the resilient Atlantic region in Capesize market

- The Capesize utilisation fell to 92.1% following a 12% decline in Baltic benchmark C5TC rates over the last two weeks. The Pacific region was the main source of weakness as Chinese ports reopened following Typhoon Dolphin, allowing previously stuck vessels to return to the market. With vessel availability increasing faster than cargo demand, spot rates on Australia-China C5 fell sharply from above $16/tonne to around $14/tonne by the end of the week.

- The reopening of Chinese ports released vessels delayed by Typhoon Dolphin, easing Pacific congestion and increasing available tonnage. Greater vessel availability intensified competition for cargoes, with some owners increasingly ballasting toward the Atlantic, where C3 remained more stable. This is likely to keep Pacific rates under pressure in the near term, while slower speeds and repositioning flows may support Atlantic availability.

- South Brazil and West Africa–China routes continued to provide support, with C3 holding around $36/tonne having fewer ballasting vessels, and healthy first-half September demand helped offset softer sentiment elsewhere.
