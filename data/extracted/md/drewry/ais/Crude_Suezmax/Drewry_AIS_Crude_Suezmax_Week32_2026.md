## Rising congestion offsets weaker tonne-mile demand in the Suezmax segment

1 content_summary

* Suezmax utilisation stood at 90%, down by 1.8 percentage points WoW and 1.2 percentage points MoM. The tonne-mile index declined from 103.2 to 100.7 WoW and by 10.6% YoY. The weaker tonne-mile environment suggests softer long-haul trade demand compared with last year.

* Supply tightened further as global tonnage at anchor rose by 2.2 mdwt and laden anchor days increased 22.2%, reducing prompt vessel availability. Congestion remained most pronounced in the Atlantic basin and Indian ocean, while regional anchorage trends diverged with anchored capacity falling 8.9% MoM in the west but rising 12% in the east, pointing to ongoing fleet repositioning.

* Vessel speeds remained broadly stable despite bunker prices surging 33.4% MoM to $845 per tonne. Ballast speeds declined mostly in both basins, while laden speeds remained largely unchanged. Although the share of laden vessels fell by 5.1 percentage points in the east and 7.3 percentage points in the west, high utilisation, rising anchorage activity and persistent regional congestion continue to offset weaker tonne-mile growth, keeping Suezmax market supportive.
