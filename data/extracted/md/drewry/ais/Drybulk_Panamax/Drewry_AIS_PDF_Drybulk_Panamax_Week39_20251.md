## Panamax laden vessel queues surge at Chinese ports
<sup>1 content_summary</sup>

* In late September, congestion worsened at China’s key dry bulk ports, Ningbo, Qingdao, Caofeidian and Rizhao, after Super Typhoon Ragasa disrupted transport links in the south. The storm delayed vessel arrivals and triggered ripple effects that spread congestion further north and east.

* The disruption coincided with a surge in iron ore, coal and soybean shipments from Australia, Indonesia and Brazil. This influx of Panamax vessels placed significant pressure on berth availability, leaving many ships anchored offshore waiting for discharge slots.

* While weather conditions have now improved, congestion remains heavy at Ningbo, Qingdao and Shanghai, where terminals are still managing high volumes of cargo. Backlogs are being addressed, but Panamax vessels carrying coal and grain are still facing prolonged waiting times

* In the coming one to two weeks, waiting times are expected to ease as port operations normalise and capacity expands. However, continued strong flows of bulk shipments from Brazil and Indonesia mean queues are unlikely to clear completely in the short term.
