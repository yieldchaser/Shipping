## 1 content_summary

* Panamax tonne-mile index remained broadly stable in Week 43

* The Panamax tonne-mile index remained broadly stable in Week 43, as an increase in vessels waiting at anchor MoM in the West led to higher congestion, effectively tightening supply and supporting freight rates, reflected in the rise of the Baltic P5TC benchmark.

* China’s suspension of US soybean imports amid trade tensions has disrupted seasonal shipping flows, with no cargoes arriving so far this season. Typically, 12 million tonnes move from the US to China between October and November, generating around 120 billion tonne-miles, roughly 12% of the total Panamax demand. The absence of this trade could remove 180–190 Panamax voyages leading to falling laden congestion in China.

* At the same time, increased Indonesian and Australian coal shipments into India have added to laden congestion in the Indian Ocean, partially offsetting the demand loss.

* Loading activity strengthened in the East and Indian Ocean, with the share of laden vessels rising to 60% and spot rates in the East spiking nearly 170% since the start of the year, reflecting robust regional demand.
