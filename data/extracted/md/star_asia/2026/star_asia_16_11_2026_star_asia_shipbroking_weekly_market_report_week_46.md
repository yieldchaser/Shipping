## Page 1

The US dollar is wrapping up its strongest weekly performance in over a month, climbing 0.6% on the Bloomberg Dollar Spot Index to reach levels last seen in May 2025. This momentum stems from consecutive monthly consumer inflation readings that exceeded forecasts, alongside robust employment figures that have pushed Treasury yields sharply higher and prompted traders to price in the possibility of a more aggressive half-point interest rate increase from the Federal Reserve in December. As the greenback strengthens against major peers, even pushing the Japanese yen past the closely watched US$160 threshold, international shipping participants face heightened currency conversion pressures and elevated financing costs across dollar-denominated freight and bunker markets. On the legislative front, Washington has moved to cushion domestic energy costs by extending a temporary waiver of the century-old Jones Act for another 90 days just ahead of its scheduled expiration. The renewed exemption permits foreign flagged vessels to transport crude oil, refined fuels, fertilisers, and other essential commodities between US ports, a measure designed to relieve persistent supply chain bottlenecks linked to ongoing Middle East hostilities and Russian refinery damage. Since the broader waiver was first introduced in March, foreign tonnage has completed roughly 338 coastal voyages and moved an additional 90 million barrels of product, with California serving as the primary recipient. Although recent adjustments now require case-by-case availability reviews through the US Maritime Administration, narrowing active utilisation, the extension provides immediate logistical flexibility for energy shippers while drawing continued criticism from domestic shipbuilders concerned about longterm fleet protections. Meanwhile, physical oil supply out of the Persian Gulf showed notable resilience in October as key regional producers ramped up output despite ongoing transit hazards. According to International Energy Agency data, Iraq boosted production by 540,000 barrels per day to reach 4.6 million barrels daily, its highest mark since September 2019, while Saudi Arabia raised output by 1.1 million barrels per day to 10 million, and the United Arab Emirates maintained steady production near its 4.4 million BPD ceiling. By leaning on discreet shuttle voyages through the Strait of Hormuz and expanding overland bypass routes, Gulf exporters lifted overall regional shipments by 950,000 barrels per day to 18.4 million. Even though total outbound volumes remain roughly 26% below pre-war baselines, this operational adaptation continues to supply global tanker routes and moderate immediate crude supply anxieties.

# DRY BULK | BDI: 2,980 ▲6.24% (w-o-w)

Dry bulk market registered a strong performance this week, with the benchmark Baltic Dry Index advancing 100-plus points from last week on Friday, to reach 2,980 points. Even as freight sentiment climbed, the broader maritime community remained on high alert following a sharp escalation of hostilities around the Strait of Hormuz over the past three days, where precision strikes by American aircraft disabled the 1999 built, St Kitts and Nevis-flagged bulk carrier Huda in the Gulf of Oman for violating the naval blockade, while separate attacks and mine incidents left three tankers damaged or ablaze and forced the rescue of 22 Arab seafarers. Vessel transit conditions improved in the Western Hemisphere, where healthier water levels enabled the Panama Canal Authority to raise maximum authorised draughts in the Neo-Panamax locks to 15.24 meters (50 feet) and expand daily capacity to 34 slots by adding a Super category berth in the Panamax locks, bringing total allocations to 24 Panamax and 10 Neo Panamax crossings across upcoming auction and special competition booking periods.

Balanced Asian tonnage should maintain a stable rate floor.

# CONTAINERS

The container shipping market experienced a downward shift in pricing this week, with the latest SCFI standing at 3,633.45 points on November 12th following a 53.9-point retreat. This reflects a typical post- peak seasonal slowdown in bookings colliding with a noticeable recovery in effective vessel capacity across major east-west corridors. On transpacific lanes, spot rates to both the U.S. West and East Coasts eased as port congestion moderated and ocean carriers scaled back scheduled void sailings, releasing regular vessel supply back into the market just as retail cargo requirements tapered off. Similarly, Northern European routes saw downward pressure outweigh early November rate hike attempts, largely driven by sluggish underlying demand and the accelerating restoration of Suez Canal transits; according to Drewry, 60% of deployed Asia-Europe capacity was utilising the Suez corridor by early November, though the simultaneous arrival of these vessels alongside ships returning from the Cape of Good Hope raises the risk of renewed vessel bunching and port congestion across European terminals. In contrast, Mediterranean and Middle Eastern corridors demonstrated considerably firmer pricing fundamentals, supported by targeted carrier rate defence and persistent regional operational friction. While softening across the U.S. East Coast and the Middle East capped broader composite gains on the KCCI, Middle Eastern spot rates remained solidly underpinned by ongoing geopolitical hazards and severe berthing delays at alternative regional gateways.

# COMMODITIES, BUNKERS & RATES

## Commodities Focus

In the ferrous sector, iron ore futures on the Dalian Commodity Exchange retreated 1.83% to close at 671.0 yuan per metric ton, dragged down by growing supply pressure and cautious, needs-based procurement across Chinese steelmakers. Downstream finished steel markets mirrored this quiet tone, with Chinese steel rebar futures dipping 0.46% to 3,024 yuan per ton, extending a monthly slide of 1.95%, even as longer-haul trade patterns continue to shift toward emerging West African mining hubs like Guinea's Simandou and Japanese hot-rolled shipments recorded a 2.8% monthly uptick. Across the regional secondary scrap markets, subdued construction turnover and liquidity hurdles have kept buyers selective.

In the energy and base metals segments, markets are digesting evolving geopolitical signals alongside shifting inventory dynamics. Thermal coal prices remain elevated near recent 24-month highs around US$152.45 per ton, marking a 38.06% annual increase, as Asia-Pacific utilities continue rebalancing generation toward coal to offset constrained LNG supplies, with China's full-year seaborne thermal coal imports projected around a central case of 295 million tons following 24.72 million tons of arrivals in October.
