# WEEKLY MARKET REPORT

# MARKET OVERVIEW

The cascading effects of ongoing Middle Eastern maritime disruptions are generating unprecedented congestion across alternative global trade arteries, most notably along the Panama Canal. With primary transit corridors through the Persian Gulf and the southern Red Sea heavily constrained, international operators moving containerised freight, energy, and chemical commodities have increasingly pivoted toward Pacific-to-Atlantic routings. This has triggered severe logistical bottlenecks, stretching waiting times for unbooked large-capacity vessels to well over a week. To bypass these extensive delays, commercial operators are committing historic sums in private queue auctions, highlighted this week by an eye-catching US$4 million bid by a major container ship to secure immediate northbound transit. Furthermore, the backlog is due to scheduled maintenance outages and draft restrictions stemming from El Niño-driven seasonal dry spells, which continue to limit daily throughput and elevate operational expenses across global supply chains. At the same time, European energy security is facing acute scrutiny as prolonged conflicts severely throttle standard LNG exports from the Persian Gulf. With regional storage facilities currently standing at only 59% of capacity, marking the lowest seasonal buffer recorded in over fifteen years, the continent faces an increasingly narrow window to accumulate necessary heating and power reserves ahead of peak winter consumption. The near-total curtailment of output from premier Middle Eastern export terminals has triggered fierce competition between Asian and European buyers for available Atlantic and spot cargoes. Moreover, distorted market pricing structures, where immediate summer delivery contracts have traded at substantial premiums over forward winter deliveries, have actively discouraged commercial traders from injecting gas into storage facilities, leaving government administrators to weigh the financial and market risks of state backed purchasing interventions. Against this backdrop of supply chain tension, broader macroeconomic indicators have injected fresh uncertainty into international financial markets. Major equity indices retreated from recent highs following data that revealed notable decelerations in consumer sentiment and retail activity, dampening corporate outlooks despite easing expectations for further interest rate hikes. While cooling domestic demand and moderating inflation metrics may encourage central banks to pause their monetary tightening campaigns, investors remain cautious regarding the underlying health of global economic demand. Coupled with the persistent threat of renewed international trade sanctions and elevated energy benchmarks, market participants are bracing for an extended period of margin pressure as industrial sectors adapt to costlier freight routes and volatile commodity supply baselines.

Saudi Arabia is actively re-establishing loading operations at its Persian Gulf export terminals while balancing ongoing security threats across both primary regional maritime corridors. Satellite imagery recently captured a VLCC at the Ju'aymah moorings within the Ras Tanura complex, the first recorded supertanker at the facility in nearly a month alongside a Suezmax vessel at the nearby sea island terminal. This activity coincides with Saudi Arabia reporting a July production increase of over 1 million BPD to OPEC, though total output remains significantly below pre-war baselines. However, tracking flows have grown exceptionally complex as most ships operate with transponders deactivated. Concurrently, export activity at the Red Sea port of Yanbu has slowed following Houthi threats, forcing a shift in logistics where northbound shipments through the Suez Canal and the SUMED pipeline to Sidi Kerir reached 2.2 million BPD last week, effectively replacing the more dangerous southern route through the Bab el-Mandeb Strait. With security concerns intensifying, total commercial transits through the Strait of Hormuz have plunged to just 5 to 10 vessels daily far below the pre-war norm of roughly 140 ships-confirming Tehran's de facto operational influence over the chokepoints. In response to these persistent vulnerabilities, Western and regional planners are advancing strategic infrastructure alternatives designed to bypass both the Strait of Hormuz and the Bab el-Mandeb Strait. The newly announced US$5 billion MERA Oil consortium is finalising a site for a 200,000 BPD refinery and deepwater export hub outside the Persian Gulf on the Gulf of Oman or Arabian Sea, targeted for completion by late 2029. This project aligns with a broader wartime redesign of the India-Middle East-Europe Economic Corridor (IMEC) anchored in Oman, as well as proposals for cross-peninsula land routes toward the Mediterranean.

# CONTAINERS

Container market returned to an upward trajectory this week as the SCFI index rose about 100 points, to reach 3,355.24 points. This gain was driven primarily by rate rebounds across the Americas, South America, and the Middle East, supported by carrier GRIs, peak-season surcharges, and strict capacity management amidst compounding supply constraints. Middle East routes continued to rise due to navigation risks in the Strait, combined with severe landside bottlenecks. Heavy cargo concentration at alternative hubs like Jeddah has caused acute port congestion and overloaded inland networks, reducing vessel turnaround times and prompting carriers such as Maersk to shift Gulf bound transshipments toward alternative gateways like Salalah and Khor Fakkan.

**Gaddani, Pakistan: Gadani maintained a firm footing this week, with local recyclers continuing to show**

strong appetite for additional tonnage amid robust domestic demand. Offered prices have seen a sudden spike, driven by a combination of taxation-related considerations, the renewed HKC certification process, and increasingly aggressive buying from a handful of recyclers eager to secure tonnage. The strength in ship-recycling prices is particularly notable considering that prime-quality HRC (Hot Rolled Coil) is currently being imported at around US$560/ton, which may ultimately limit the sustainability of the present upward momentum. While local recyclers and steel re-rollers continue to navigate evolving tax and compliance measures, most notably the revenue authorities' recent introduction of an electricity consumption based sales tax for the domestic steel sector, underlying market sentiment has refused to waver. A major catalyst behind this persistent purchasing appetite is the drastic reduction in overland steel and scrap imports from neighbouring Iran, which has created an acute structural shortage of meltable material across the country. Accordingly, domestic steel mills are leaning far more heavily on shipbreakers to provide essential raw feedstock, considerably strengthening yard operators' willingness to compete aggressively and actively bid for any available candidate vessels. This coincides with a welcome easing in national economic pressures. Official data revealed that Pakistan's headline inflation slowed significantly to 9.2% in July, down from 11.1% in June, primarily driven by softer housing and transport costs. With the Asian Development Bank maintaining the country's annual economic growth projection at 3.7%, improving price stability is helping cushion heavy industries against earlier cost spikes.
