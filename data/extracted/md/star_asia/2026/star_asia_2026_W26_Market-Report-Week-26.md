## MARKET OVERVIEW

The fragile confidence sustaining the recovery of Middle Eastern trade corridors has been severely shaken following a kinetic attack on a commercial vessel within the Strait of Hormuz. A Singapore-flagged, container ship operated by a major Taiwanese line sustained structural damage to its bridge windows after being struck on its starboard side by an unidentified projectile. The vessel was navigating a globally recommended southern transit route near the Omani coast when the incident occurred, though it managed to complete its exit safely into the Gulf of Oman without any reported casualties or environmental damage. This development marks a sudden disruption to the temporary period of relative calm established by the recent interim peace agreement, which had initially prompted a swift and significant increase in daily commercial crossings. Tensions in the Gulf escalated on Saturday after Bahrain said it had been targeted by Iranian drones, while a tanker was reportedly struck by an unidentified projectile in the Strait of Hormuz. The incidents came as Tehran and Washington accused each other of breaching a fragile ceasefire. Iran said it had attacked US sites in the Persian Gulf in retaliation for American strikes on Iranian missile storage and radar facilities on Friday. Washington said those strikes followed an Iranian drone attack on a container ship in Hormuz a day earlier. Bahrain, which hosts the US Navy's Fifth Fleet, said several Iranian drones targeted the country early Saturday. Meanwhile, maritime authorities raised the security threat level in Hormuz to "substantial." The renewed violence threatens to undermine a recent 60-day truce aimed at restoring shipping through the vital energy corridor and advancing nuclear talks in exchange for sanctions relief. In immediate response to the strike, the IMO has suspended its newly launched voluntary evacuation framework, which was designed to safely shepherd hundreds of stranded merchant vessels and thousands of mariners out of the locked region. Maritime safety agencies have advised passing crews to transit with extreme caution as security coordinators work to reconfirm necessary safety guarantees with coastal states. The situation is further complicated by conflicting regional directives, as local administrative bodies have explicitly warned that ships operating outside their unilaterally designated northern channels will no longer receive safe passage guarantees or standard insurance coverage.

# DRY BULK | BDI: 2,524 ▲+7.27% (w-o-w)

The dry bulk market slowed further during Week 26, as the benchmark Baltic Dry Index (BDI) fell by 200 points w-o-w on Friday, to reach 2,524 points. When evaluated on a weekly basis against the previous week's close of 2,659 points, this represents a decline of approximately 7.2% for the main shipping index. Looking closely at the individual vessel categories from the prior report, the Capesize segment faced continued downward pressure with its index sliding down to 3,640 points due to a lack of fresh long-haul fixtures from Brazil, offsetting temporary gains in the Pacific. Meanwhile, the BPI registered a regional split, as robust grain and coal flows in the Atlantic provided a much-needed floor against an oversupply of available ballast ships in the East, bringing its index to 2,110 points.

## Segment Highlights

**Capesize: Capesize closed slightly lower this week as strong downward pressure from weak Atlantic and**

Brazilian long-haul routes offset a brief, fixture rebound on the W. Australia to China run. Market caution remains high as near-month and third-quarter FFA contracts continue to trade at a discount to spot rates, reflecting scepticism over demand sustainability and looming tonnage pressure. Regional spot sentiment finished on a softer note with Pacific R/V dropping to US$29,800 per day, and T/A trips settling at US$38,680's, while F/H provided a lone bright spot by ticking up to US$68,850's.

**Panamax / Kamsarmax: Panamax remained deeply divided this week as steady South American grain**

and U.S. EC coal volumes successfully filled the Atlantic, while an oversupply of ballast vessels and dropping Indonesian cargo weighed heavily on the Pacific. This acute regional imbalance in vessel supply left the futures market sceptical of a short-term rebound. Despite these supply pressures, slight gains emerged across key routes, lifting T/A rates to US$18,150 per day and Pacific R/V to US$15,150's, while Indonesian R/V crawled to US$13,500's.

**Supramax / Ultramax: Supramax saw a generally robust but cautious tone this week, as a strong**

mismatch between prompt vessel positioning and cargo timing in the Atlantic helped offset a slight loss of momentum. Surging grain exports from Brazil, coupled with a tight supply of available ships, successfully pushed T/A rates up to US$29,600 per day and F/H routes to US$30,950's. Despite steady fixture activity on the Indonesia/India and SEAsia lanes, wider bid-offer gaps and the potential influx of ballast vessels nudged Pacific R/V down to US$17,190's per day.

**Handysize: Handies maintained a solid footing this week, anchored by healthy cargo demand and highly**

resilient sentiment sweeping across the South Atlantic and USG. While trading conditions across the Continent and Mediterranean remained largely unchanged, a balanced supply-demand picture in Asia ensured consistent cargo flows and a highly stable market environment. This steady fundamental backdrop safely pushed Inter-Pacific routes to US$16,550's per day, and CIS R/V settled at US$15,000's.

# CONTAINERS

The container shipping market maintained its strong upward momentum this week as the SCFI index climbed to 3,121.69 points. This steady growth reflects a market supported by early summer peak season demand, structural ship shortages in South America, and the full implementation of June GRIs and peak season surcharges rather than an unguided surge. Geopolitical factors and vessel constraints continue to push up primary trade lanes, particularly across the transpacific corridor, where front-loaded volumes- driven by importers rushing to beat upcoming July tariff deadlines-have led to severe space shortages and notable spot rate increases to the U.S. West Coast. Carriers are actively prioritising these highly lucrative American routes. Meanwhile, the Middle East route experienced its first downward adjustment since the regional crisis, slipping as risk premiums eased slightly on emerging transit negotiations; however, this represents a short-term premium correction rather than true route normalization, as substantial operational overheads like emergency fuel surcharges, elevated war risk insurance premiums, and security costs remain completely unchanged.

## Market Insights

**Alang, India: Alang saw a weaker week, with purchasing prices correcting downward in response to softer**

domestic economic fundamentals. Despite currently operating at largely unprofitable levels, most recyclers are continuing to actively acquire incoming tonnage primarily to maintain essential business continuity and keep their facilities running. While the persistent labour shortages that severely disrupted operations over recent months have finally been resolved, extreme seasonal heat conditions continue to heavily restrict and slow down daily production volumes. With India currently positioned as the lowest-priced destination on the subcontinent, local yard owners are maintaining a steady but cautious posture as heavy July monsoon rains begin to dictate the physical pace of vessel beaching.

**Chattogram, Bangladesh: Chattogram saw a visible decline, with weak domestic steel and falling scrap**

prices subduing buyer demand. Despite these quiet conditions and ongoing seasonal monsoon constraints, the market marked some sales were reported on speculation in a falling market. Going forward, time will tell whether this speculative deal turns out to be a logical deal or not. As bunkers and shipping premiums continue to deflate, owners of older tonnage have far less incentive to keep them at sea, leaving well-funded Bangladeshi yards perfectly positioned to absorb this emerging wave of vessels once the heavy seasonal rains begin to clear. Overall sentiment showed signs of weakness as supply pressure eased and a large number of ships lined up to head for the beaches of Chattogram. Experts believe prices are bound for a major correction to align with the underlying domestic fundamentals.
