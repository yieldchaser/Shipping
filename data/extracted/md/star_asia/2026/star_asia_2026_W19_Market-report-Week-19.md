WEEKLY MARKET REPORT

WEEK 19 · MAY 9TH, 2026

MARKET OVERVIEW

The US is awaiting Iran's response to President Donald Trump's latest proposal to end the 10-week conflict, after renewed clashes in the Strait of Hormuz strained a fragile month-long ceasefire. The proposal, sent on Wednesday, calls for Iran to reopen the waterway while the US lifts its blockade on Iranian ports over the next month. Tehran said the plan remains "under review," offering no timeline for a decision. Markets remain focused on whether the ceasefire can hold, with Brent crude settling near $101 per barrel despite a weekly decline of around 6%. Tensions escalated after US forces struck two empty Iranian oil tankers attempting to enter Iranian ports, an action Tehran called a ceasefire violation. Qatar continues to play a mediation role, while China and other major powers are pressing for the Strait's reopening. With roughly a fifth of global oil and LNG flows normally transiting Hormuz, any renewed escalation would keep energy and freight markets on edge. The current market is in a "wait and see" hold, as any long-term recovery in the Persian Gulf is expected to be gradual given the reported damage to regional infrastructure. For now, most commercial traffic remains stalled, with only a few state-owned tankers reportedly attempting to navigate the Strait of Hormuz by turning off their tracking signals to avoid detection. In China, the shipping sector is managing a complex transition as the second quarter moves forward. Despite the broader geopolitical turbulence, China's manufacturing and industrial sectors have shown resilience, with recent private surveys indicating an unexpected uptick in firm activity. However, the exportoriented container market is facing its own set of internal pressures, as carriers aggressively implement blank sailings and rollovers to maintain rate stability in the face of rising operational costs.

14-day lead time for refuelling at Asian hubs

The economic consequences of this prolonged deadlock are now being felt across the global supply chain, with the cost of shipping fuel becoming a primary driver of market volatility. S&P Global warn that the current 14-day lead time for refuelling at Asian hubs could soon become a global standard if the disruption continues, leading to significant delays and higher operational expenses for all vessel types. As we look toward the remainder of the second quarter, the industry is bracing for a period of "operational stress" as global fuel inventories, particularly in developed markets, reach critical lows. Major shipping hubs are beginning to feel the strain of depleted product stocks, with some, warning that a significant supply gap could emerge by June if international transits do not resume in a credible way.

# DRY BULK | BDI: 2,978 +9.08% (w-o-w)

The dry bulk sector demonstrated significant strength this Friday, as the Baltic Exchange's main freight index climbed to reach 2,978 points, its highest level since early December 2025. This upward momentum was primarily fuelled by the larger vessel categories, with the Capesize index jumping to a five-month peak of 4,955 points, while the Panamax index rose to 2,233 points.

Despite these healthy levels, the market is currently witnessing a notable collapse in volatility to levels not seen in years, largely mirroring the steady, range-bound behaviour of iron ore prices. However, this period of relative tranquillity may be deceptive; while supply constraints are keeping rates elevated for now, intensifying global economic risks and potential shocks in emerging Asian markets suggest the sector may face challenges in remaining insulated from broader macroeconomic headwinds.

# Segment Highlights

Capesize: Robust surge across all segments, with Pacific R/V jumping to US$47,075 as West Australia/China routes lead the sector's gains. In the Atlantic, limited prompt tonnage and healthy mineral flows have pushed T/A rates to US$47,400's. Sentiment remains positive as the market outperforms paper expectations.

Panamax / Kamsarmax: A pickup in grain activity from South America has effectively cleared the Atlantic tonnage list, with F/H routes hitting US$26,300. Despite Golden Week, Pacific remains resilient with tight availability and steady mineral demand from Australia and Indonesia, pushing R/V averages to US$21,099.

Supramax / Ultramax: The Supramax market remains largely steady, with T/A earnings rising to US$23,400's as localised demand in the USG offsets a softer Mediterranean market. In the Pacific, tighter vessel availability and stable cargo flows have supported a modest rise in R/V rates to US$17,350.

Handysize: The Inter-Pacific route closed at US$14,800 as tight tonnage in Asia helps offset a slower start to the month. While the broader market remains cautious, improved demand in the USG and S. Atlantic has supported a modest rise in T/A earnings to US$13,950's. Activity has lost some momentum following recent holidays, but steady demand suggests the sector is maintaining a firm floor despite limited cargo flows.

# TANKERS | BDTI: 2,629

-4.47%

# (w-o-w)

Total open interest in Brent futures has plunged to its lowest level since August, reflecting a "risk-taking desert" where traders are retreating from unpredictable, binary outcomes. With the Strait of Hormuz blockade now the longest on record, algorithmic traders and hedgers have become the primary market participants, as the high cost of volatility makes traditional flat-price betting nearly untradeable for many fundamental investors.

In the tanker segment, a meaningful divide has emerged between the Middle East Gulf and the Atlantic basin. Western shipowners remain especially cautious about committing tonnage through the Strait, leading to a westward shift as vessels move out of the East in high volumes. This has led to a surge in tonnage availability across the Atlantic, putting pressure on freight rates for routes from West Africa and the USG to China.

# Segment Highlights

VLCC: The persistent blockade and a lack of meaningful volume are placing significant downward pressure on VLCC rates, with earnings across the Pacific and Atlantic basins softening this week. Despite this decline, daily returns continue to hover near US$100,000, though the short-term outlook remains bearish as uncertainty continues to distort market activity.

Suezmax: Suezmax rates have softened significantly, with West Africa to UKC earnings falling 20% as the market awaits the next release of CPC stems. Despite this, the demand outlook remains optimistic in the USG, where favourable economics allow Suezmaxes to outperform Aframaxes.

Aframax: Aframax rates in the Mediterranean have softened to US\$94,200's as owners contends with a more balanced tonnage list and cooling regional demand. In the North Sea, stagnant activity and a build-up of available vessels over the long weekend are expected to test rates further, particularly as the USG market remains under pressure.

LR1 / LR2: LR2 rates in the Middle East have remained steady, with TC1 holding at WS550. Conversely, the Med/East route and the LR1 sector are facing downward pressure, with the TC15 index daily earnings retreating to $93,600.

MR: USG saw rates plummet this week, with the TC14 to the UKC losing over 180 points and wiping 60% off daily earnings. MEG remains an exception, with TC17 index holding steady at exceptionally high levels near WS737.

# SUB-CONTNENT & TURKEY SCRAP MARKETS

India: Market activity remained subdued as a significant bid-offer gap persists, with Brazil-origin HMS offered at US$375/t against bids of US$365/t and UK-origin shredded quoted near US$395/t CFR. Buying interest is severely limited by poor mill margins and a clear preference for domestic scrap, keeping USorigin turnings workable only at the lower US$337-$340/t CFR range. Pakistan: The market maintained a firm stance supported by selective transactions, including a notable 1,000 t sale of Malaysia-origin LMS bundles finalised at US$365/t CFR Qasim. While sentiment remains steady, buyers are cautiously monitoring the landscape as UK-origin shredded offers are currently indicated between US$426/t and US$428/t CFR. Bangladesh: Trading participation has weakened despite a baseline of buying interest, leaving the market largely stable with UK-origin HMS offers held between US$390/t and US$400/t CFR. Indicative pricing for shredded scrap to Chattogram remains near US$410/t CFR, as the region grapples with a general slowdown in overall transactional volume. Turkey: Deep-sea sentiment remains firm due to limited US-origin cargo availability, with premium HMS 80:20 assessed at $412/t CFR and tradable values reaching up to US$416/t. Despite pressure from weak rebar demand, dock levels for UK HMS 80:20 are holding at EUR 300-310/t, providing a sturdy floor for the current pricing structure.

# COMMODITIES, BUNKERS & RATES

Commodities Focus

Aluminium prices have surged 3.8% to US$3,315 per ton due to Middle Eastern supply shocks that could take a year to resolve. Simultaneously, iron ore futures have reached a peak above CNY 815 per ton, supported by a seven-week decline in steel inventories and renewed purchasing activity. While copper holds steady near US$6 per pound on long-term electrification demand, coal prices recently hit a high of US$119 per ton as energy security needs outweigh the transition to cleaner sources. This shift is further intensified by the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, which has disrupted 20% of global liquefied natural gas supplies.
