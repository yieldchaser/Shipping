## MARKET OVERVIEW

The global sectors are monitoring shifting economic indicators out of East Asia, where China's central bank is moving to implement stronger counter-cyclical and cross-cyclical measures to bolster its domestic economy. Following official data showing that second-quarter GDP growth moderated to 4.3% falling below the national target and marking the softest expansion in several seasons, monetary authorities have indicated they will deploy a flexible suite of financial tools, including reserve requirement ratio cuts and reverse repos, to support liquidity. While aggregate financing and new loans for June both missed initial forecasts, central banking officials characterised credit expansion for the first half of the year as reasonable, arguing that a combination of loan and bond financing offers a more accurate metric of capital flowing into the real economy going forward. To counter external speculation regarding currency management, the bank emphasised that the yuan would continue to fluctuate naturally based on market forces, even as the country officially emerges from a three-year deflationary cycle with nominal growth outpacing real expansion. Simultaneously, the fragile diplomatic detente in the Middle East has collapsed, plunging the Strait of Hormuz back into a high-intensity conflict and driving global energy benchmarks higher. Following the official end of the brief maritime ceasefire and the reinstatement of a US naval blockade on Iranian ports, military forces have significantly widened the scope of their operations. In a major escalation, an unladen tanker sailing under a Curacao flag was targeted and disabled by military aircraft deep within the Persian Gulf while attempting to transit toward Iran's primary export terminal at Kharg Island. With regional authorities warning that the strategic waterway will remain entirely closed to international shipping unless foreign forces respect their unilateral traffic rules and proposed service fees, local diplomatic channels have stalled, raising fears of a protracted, destructive standoff. The swift return of the blockade and the subsequent uptick in regional hostilities have severely disrupted the temporary recovery of global energy trade. Visible commercial transits through the strait have experienced a sharp contraction, with a moving average of oil flows dropping to 3.9 million BPD over the past week as shipowners refuse to expose their assets to a combination of drifting sea mines, missile strikes, and heavy drone activity. The operational environment grew even more restrictive following the suspension of loading operations at a major northern Gulf export hub after an active tanker was struck by an aerial drone while taking on cargo. While some vessel operators continue to utilise clandestine "dark" transits with deactivated transponders to shuttle crude out of the region, markets are preparing for a semi-permanent period of periodic supply disruptions, warning that global benchmark prices are carrying a structural premium of up to US$15 a barrel to absorb the ongoing geopolitical risk.

# DRY BULK | BDI: 2,840 ▼-3.53% (w-o-w)

Despite ongoing market uncertainties, dry bulk recorded a positive opening half of the year, as tight vessel availability kept average returns across all vessel sizes well above long-term averages. This supply squeeze was fuelled by longer travel distances, bunker fuelling bottlenecks tied to the Strait of Hormuz conflict, and a packed shipyard drydocking schedule for ageing vessels built during the late 2000s shipping boom, which helped keep Capesize earnings highly lucrative at over US$30,000 per day. However, a weaker outlook for the rest of the year persists due to a tight fertiliser supply evidenced by an 8% drop in global shipments following lower Persian Gulf exports coupled with extreme heat threatening upcoming September harvests. On the trading floor, the benchmark Baltic Dry Index snapped a winning streak on Friday, dipping 3.53% w-o-w to 2,840 points. Capesize index also slipped 11.9% to 4,097 points at week's closing. The Panamax index rose 0.18% to 2,257 points (up from 2,253 points last week), while the Supramax index climbed 1.41% to reach 1,730 points, its highest level since August 2022.

## Segment Highlights

**Capesize: Capesize saw firm upward momentum early in the week in the Pacific. This bullish sentiment**

quickly spread to long-haul Atlantic routes, with West Africa/China F/H trading at US$76,250's and T/A trips rising to US$48,425's per day, even as a spike in bunker prices and easing post-midweek activity began to cap the overall advance. Despite a slightly softer, flat tone in the Pacific toward the end of the week, the concentrated fixture activity successfully pushed Brazil R/V to US$40,875's per day.

**Panamax / Kamsarmax: Panamax held a firm but consolidating tone this week as a concentration of**

China-bound fixtures, anticipated U.S. soybean purchases, and typhoon-related schedule delays successfully eased prompt tonnage pressure across both basins. Robust NOPAC grain demand and Australian coal flows drew prompt ships south, providing strong support to Pacific R/V at US$17,525's and Indonesian R/V at US$16,250's per day. Meanwhile, the Atlantic basin maintained steady floor support from active USG F/H activity, keeping T/A at US$21,200's per day.

**Supramax / Ultramax: Supramax climbed higher in the Atlantic this week, propelled by tight available**

vessel supply and firm, consistent cargo demands out of the USG and Mediterranean. This pushed T/A rates up to US$27,670's per day and drove F/H routes to a strong US$29,260's. In the Pacific, basin softened slightly as reduced coal shipments bound for China and a growing list of available tonnage dragged Pacific R/V to US$17,700's per day, though mounting vessel congestion at Indonesian coal ports helped cushion the decline.

**Handysize: Handies remained highly active this week, supported by a healthy mix of spot and short-**

period fixtures across both basins, with the Atlantic generating most of the cargo flow. Tonnage circulation remained steady as consistent grain, coal, fertiliser, and petcoke trades kept vessels moving and provided reliable short-haul employment opportunities in Asia. This dynamic helped push T/A rates up to US$16,950's, while Pacific R/V edged up to US$15,000's while Inter-Pacific trips settled at US$15,600's/day.

# CONTAINERS

The container shipping market experienced a notable downward correction this week as the SCFI index fell again this week, marking a broad softening across deep-sea lanes including Europe, the Mediterranean, the Americas, and South America. This decline is largely a correction following months of rapid pricing gains, a trend further amplified by growing shipper resistance to aggressive early-July surcharges and the strategic introduction of extra vessel capacity by ocean carriers. SCFI Shanghai - N. Europe rate also fell 4% to US$3,215/TEU. Global freight rates remain elevated due to severe European port congestion, record- high charter rates, and Maersk’s highly watched, small-scale trial to return a Mediterranean service via the Suez Canal, which has introduced a psychological shift regarding capacity constraints.

**Chattogram, Bangladesh: Chattogram experienced a visible slowdown over the past week as severe**

monsoon conditions shifted from a predictable seasonal shift into a major operational hurdle. Torrential downpours and extensive flooding disrupted daily operations, making it incredibly difficult for local yards to maintain steady cutting schedules or manage production. Yard operators are focusing on handling immediate weather related challenges at their sites rather than pursuing new vessels to add to their pipelines.

The continued scarcity of vessels available for recycling has prompted some recyclers to explore alternative sources of raw material. In order to keep their mills operational and sustain business activity, several recyclers have begun importing steel plates from China for conversion into wire rods. This has emerged as a viable interim option for the industry while the supply of recycling tonnage remains limited. On a positive note, the country managed a modest economic expansion of 3.7% for the recently concluded fiscal year, supported by a healthy recovery in large-scale manufacturing and a steadier foreign exchange reserve position.
