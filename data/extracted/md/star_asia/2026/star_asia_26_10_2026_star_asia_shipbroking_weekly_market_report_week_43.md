## MARKET OVERVIEW

Russian seaborne crude loadings experienced a noticeable lull heading into mid-October, largely driven by a temporary operational pause at the Baltic terminal of Primorsk. Marine traffic data and satellite observations pointed to several days of empty berths as local maintenance work brought loading activity to a brief standstill, pulling national outbound volumes down to their lowest weekly levels since early spring. While shipments from the Black Sea hub of Novorossiysk held up well and activity across the Baltic showed signs of life by the weekend, the interruption dragged both four-week export averages and overall weekly revenues down to multi-week lows. Even with strong realised pricing for Pacific grades like ESPO cushioning the blow, weaker volumes and softer Urals benchmarks combined to put a clear dent in Moscow's immediate export earnings. Beneath these short-term terminal bottlenecks lies a more intricate tug-of-war between refining disruptions and genuine supply constraints. Ukrainian drone strikes against domestic processing sites resumed over the past week, leaving a primary crude distillation unit damaged at Ryazan and causing an industrial fire in western Siberia. While damaging local refineries often forces unrefined crude directly into the seaborne export pipeline, Russia's ability to ramp up foreign deliveries is increasingly running into an upstream ceiling. Nationwide crude extraction slipped in September to roughly eight and a half million barrels daily, the lowest monthly mark recorded in over six years, marking a tenth straight month of declining production that naturally puts a tighter cap on how much crude can make its way to the water. At the same time, commercial players are closely watching how evolving political signals from Washington might reshape the flow of barrels into Asia. Recent American policy manoeuvres, including the shelving of proposed congressional sanctions and discussions aimed at steering shadow vessels back into the commercial mainstream, have noticeably eased tariff anxieties for key buyers in India. Although a sizable volume of oil at sea remains tied up in mid-voyage lightering zones off Singapore and the Russian Far East, major consumers across India and China continue to absorb the lion's share of available supply. With geopolitical talks lingering in the background and buyers keeping a close eye on delivery schedules, market participants are watching to see whether this brief lull in shipments quickly gives way to a fresh surge of Asian bound liftings.

# DRY BULK | BDI: 2,660 ▲5.93% (w-o-w)

The dry bulk sector demonstrated encouraging resilience, led by an upbeat performance across the larger vessel categories that helped lift the Baltic Exchange's dry bulk freight index for a third consecutive session by 3.4% to reach 2,660 points. Leading this upward drive, the Capesize index climbed for a third straight day with a surge to 3,791 points, finding support from positive longer-term fundamentals even as West African iron ore shipments from the advancing Simandou development are anticipated to claim approximately 15% of regional Capesize exports during the third quarter without immediately altering tonne-mile dynamics. Smaller segments experienced steady to divergent outcomes, with the Supramax index edging up to 1,887 points, while the Panamax sector faced moderate downward pressure, declining to settle at 2,109 points. A steadying tone for dry bulk, buoyed by the renewed strength of heavy-cargo carriers and selective support across the broader fleet.

## Segment Highlights

**Capesize:** Capesize experienced some weakness this week as overall rates dropped amid expanding ballast tonnage, though late-week paper improvements sparked a solid rebound in West Australia and Brazil. Firm Australian coal enquiry and healthy C5 demand should help establish a near-term floor.

**Panamax / Kamsarmax:** Panamax softened further this week as sluggish USG grain appetite and Atlantic tonnage accumulation outweighed firmer fixtures and typhoon-related prompt demand in the Pacific. Reflecting this disparity, Transpacific and South Asian runs held relatively stable while longer-haul business suffered, leaving Indonesian R/V slightly higher at US$19,850's and Fronthaul returns slipping to US$30,000's per day. Australian coal flows and weather disruptions around Indonesia should lend ongoing support to the East, but discounted November FFA paper will maintain downward pressure in the Atlantic.

**Supramax / Ultramax:** Supramax drew steady support from the East this week, where tighter prompt vessel availability around Indonesia and route-specific stem concentrations lifted NOPAC and Indo-India trades by over 4%. Atlantic activity showed mixed momentum as mild USG gains were offset by softer West African F/H, settling Pacific-India earnings up at US$21,325's while B/H runs eased marginally to US$16,905's.

**Handysize:** Handysize displayed a mixed tone this week, with quiet trading and subdued inquiry in the USG alongside continuing rate improvements across the South Atlantic. Balanced fundamentals in the Mediterranean and a tight tonnage supply against steady inquiries in the Pacific helped insulate spot levels, nudging Inter-Pacific returns up to US$17,095's while T/A rates softened to US$18,400's. Healthy South Atlantic demand should maintain a dependable floor.

# TANKERS | BDTI: 7,152 ▼-7.44% (w-o-w)

Beyond baseline voyage tariffs, unprecedented operational friction and regulatory clampdowns are reshaping buyer behavior toward short-haul certainty. Delays in STS transfers outside the Strait of Hormuz, exacerbated by drone strikes, have propelled demurrage charges to US$1 million daily-adding approximately US$0.50 per barrel for each idle day on a laden VLCC-while war-risk insurance premiums have soared to 8% of vessel value, adding another US$9 to US$10 million. These extreme logistics costs have even driven Canadian Trans Mountain pipeline export freight on smaller Aframaxes up to US$30 per barrel, mirroring larger carrier classes and prompting Chinese state firms to quickly secure all Novemberloading Russian ESPO stems ahead of independent refiners. Maritime regulatory enforcement has expanded under Operation Economic Outcast, with the U.S. Treasury blacklisting 9 vessels alongside service facilitators, including the law firm Karnakis & Karnakis, fleet manager Roxette Maritime Shipping, Amigoz Singapore, and Dubai-based Whales Shipping Line for enabling illicit commodity flows. The tanker landscape continues to fragment into shorter, highly localized voyages under historic freight costs.

# CONTAINERS

Global container shipping market maintained a steady, range-bound posture this week as the SCFI index edged up to close at 3,681.34 points. In Europe and the Mediterranean, rates posted modest gains as ocean carriers actively defended spot baselines ahead of upcoming 2027 contract renewals, supported by MSC introducing fresh Freight All Kinds rate benchmarks effective October 19th; however, the accelerating transition of mainline services back to the Suez Canal is progressively shortening sailing distances and expanding effective capacity, prompting BIMCO to caution that container fleet growth in 2027 will comfortably outpace cargo volume expansion as Cape of Good Hope diversions unwind. Regional Middle Eastern trades registered a slight rate increase, reflecting persistent maritime risks alongside ongoing logistics adaptations designed to navigate the embattled Strait of Hormuz. Meanwhile, Iraq’s national maritime carrier marked a historic development by deploying the 902 TEU Basrah on an eight-day shuttle linking Jebel Ali with Umm Qasr-representing the state line's first scheduled Gulf operation since the mid-1960s-to establish a dedicated maritime conduit for stranded volumes. This direct marine shuttle serves to complement, rather than replace, the extensive multimodal networks developed during the regional disruption, which have seen Jordan’s Red Sea gateway of Aqaba handle 37,182 Iraq-bound transit containers during the first seven months of 2026-a 933% surge over the 3,599 containers moved during the same period last year-alongside integrated overland truck connections arranged by Maersk, CMA CGM, and Hapag-Lloyd to keep commercial imports moving.

## Commodities Focus

Copper led base metals lower this week as a selloff in AI-related stocks weakened sentiment and raised concerns that slower spending on data centres and power grids could dampen demand. Supply disruptions drew less attention, despite threatened strike action at Canada's Highland Valley copper mine and mounting labour tensions across South America. Nickel held up better as traders weighed prospects of tighter Indonesian supply. Aluminium traded just above US$3,000 per tonne in London, near multi-month lows, as strong Asian supply outweighed persistent Gulf disruptions.
