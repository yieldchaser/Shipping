## MARKET OVERVIEW

The international energy trade is navigating fresh logistical bottlenecks across the Western Hemisphere as a dramatic escalation in tanker freight rates has stalled export agreements for Venezuelan crude scheduled for upcoming delivery. As transportation expenses have more than doubled over the past month, the delivered cost of heavy, sulphurous barrels into the USG Coast has lost its competitive edge against piped Canadian alternatives. This pricing dispute has complicated negotiations between major commodity intermediaries, who are wary of committing substantial upfront capital into dedicated payment facilities while regional refinery outages in the Midwest and Texas have temporarily curbed processing appetite for heavy grades. With primary buyers hesitating, unsold volume is beginning to accumulate in regional storage, creating an opening for non-traditional trading houses with sufficient balance-sheet strength to negotiate access to these displaced stems. Across continental Europe, inland transportation faces unprecedented operational strain as water levels along the Rhine River have dropped to their lowest recorded marks in nearly a century and a half. This has severely restricted the intake capacity of shallow-draft barges, compelling operators to cut cargo loads by nearly a third to avoid grounding while triggering a rapid surge in clean product barging tariffs and further squeezing hydroelectric power availability across the continent. While meteorological forecasts hint at potential relief from early autumn rain systems, the prolonged dry cycle highlights an increasingly structural climate risk for European industrial supply chains, which are struggling to distribute coal and heating distillates ahead of peak winter consumption. In contrast, container shipping equities across the Asia-Pacific region have surged to the forefront of financial markets, outperforming traditional technology shares as sustained geopolitical dislocations drive box rates higher. Ongoing security diversions around vital Middle Eastern conduits, combined with cargo rushes to beat potential trade tariff deadlines and holiday factory closures, have propelled benchmark container freight indices to multi-month highs. Regional port efficiency has been further tested by lateseason typhoon disruptions, creating widespread terminal backlogs that effectively remove vessel capacity and support exceptional quarterly earnings projections for major liner operators. While market analysts caution that any genuine diplomatic breakthrough along Middle Eastern transit lanes could quickly soften war-risk premiums and ease vessel tightness, the present issues continue to provide strong fundamental support for the container sector.

# DRY BULK | BDI: 3,426 ▲1.66% (w-o-w)

Dry bulk sector continues to navigate a remarkable paradox, where exceptional charter earnings persist alongside an anticipated 1% contraction in total seaborne trade for 2026-a rare downturn seen only four times over the past four decades. This resilient earning environment remains heavily insulated by widespread geopolitical flashpoints and strategic chokepoint constraints. The Baltic Dry Index held near its mid-month highs, edging slightly from last week on Friday to close at 3,426 points. Large bulkers experienced a minor pause as the Capesize index slipped midweek before regaining to 5,784 points yet still posted a 0.28% weekly gain over the previous week. Meanwhile, the midsized and smaller segments maintained steady upward momentum; the Panamax index climbed 6.93% to 2,407 points-while the Supramax index nudged up to 1,786 points, cementing a 1% rise above last week.

## Segment Highlights

**Capesize:** Capesize showed regional divergence this week as tonnage tightness in the Atlantic pushed C3 rates up, whereas a surplus of prompt Pacific capacity dragged C5 routes down. Reflecting these split conditions, spot earnings remained strong across key lanes, with T/A rates advancing to US$56,300's and Pacific R/V holding firm at US$51,700's. Rates are expected to remain soft, as persistent Pacific overcapacity and forward Q4 paper trading at a discount are expected to curb upside potential despite steady Atlantic support.

**Panamax / Kamsarmax:** Panamax reversed its recent softer trend as a revival in Atlantic grain stems and tightening North Continent prompt tonnage supported owner sentiment across both basins. While steady NOPAC grain and Australian coal flows provided constructive demand in the East, an expanding list of ballast vessels and sharp weekly drops in Chinese coal shipments capped more substantial Pacific gains. Key spot route averages edged higher, with F/H rates rising to $30,388 per day and Pacific R/V advancing to $20,754 per day.

**Supramax / Ultramax:** Supramax remain unchanged, led by a slight gain in the Pacific as healthy cargo inflows from SE Asia successfully absorbed the influx of ballast tonnage. In the Atlantic, an acute shortage of prompt tonnage in the South lent firm downside defence and kept overall trading momentum somewhat mixed. Spot benchmarks registered modest shifts, with Pacific r/v advancing to US$17,850's per day while F/H returns edged down to US$32,550's per day.

**Handysize:** Handysize market maintained a firm and stable trajectory this week, continuing to draw steady support across key trading basins. Transatlantic activity showed renewed positive momentum to push rates up to US$18,025's per day. Meanwhile, consistent cargo flows across Asia provided reliable backing to regional sentiment, lifting Inter-Pacific earnings to US$16,600's per day.

# TANKERS | BDTI: 5,366 ▲+9.53% (w-o-w)

Oil benchmark prices eased sharply earlier this week following announcements that Saudi Arabia had resumed operations along its critical East-West pipeline after strikes forced a temporary shutdown, though physical export data reveals that Aramco had not loaded crude from the Red Sea terminal of Yanbu since September 16. Before that disruption, the conduit had been channelling roughly 4 million BPD westward to avoid Gulf bottlenecks. Its temporary outage accelerated an already burgeoning transhipment network in the Gulf of Oman, where smaller shuttle tankers ferry barrels out of the Persian Gulf to load onto larger vessels anchored outside the chokepoint. This operational pivot has propelled freight economics into uncharted territory, with shipping costs expanding to represent as much as 25% of the total landed value of crude delivered from the Middle East to China. Spot rates on the benchmark TD3C route from Ras Tanura to Ningbo surged to a historic high of over US$1.2 million per day, translating into unprecedented freight expenditures of roughly US$30 per barrel for VLCC, while the Baltic Exchange's TD34 Gulf of Oman-to-China index climbed to nearly US$871,000. At the same time, the concentration of idling tankers conducting transfers in the Gulf of Oman has raised acute navigational and safety concerns as seasonal winds strengthen.

**Suezmax:** West African rates held firm around the WS435 mark, heavily underpinned by active split chartering and demand from the USG. In the Black Sea, the market saw rates remain elevated around WS500.

**LR1 / LR2:** MEG LR2 rates are maintaining extraordinary strength, closing at WS875 mark, propelled by robust cargo demand through late September despite transit constraints at the Strait of Hormuz. In the LR1, MEG/Japan also saw rates climbed to WS890.

**MR:** Far East rates closed higher this week, lifted by strong spillover demand from an exceptionally tight LR market. Sustained cargo volumes from China alongside steady long-haul requirements to Australia are expected to keep regional sentiment bullish. MEG/E. # CONTAINERS

The global container shipping market maintained a steady posture this week as the SCFI rose to reach 3,686.62 points. While U.S. West Coast rates benefited from brisk intake and cargo diversions from the East Coast, the market was bolstered by tight vessel availability linked to ongoing Panama Canal transit constraints and vessel delays originating from adverse weather and port congestion in China. Southeast Asian routes also experienced an acute lift ahead of the holidays. Looking at medium-term structural fundamentals, BIMCO highlighted that although network disruptions and an active head-haul market continue to absorb capacity in 2026, an expanding fleet threatens to tip the industry into a severe supply-demand imbalance by 2027. Global container volumes grew by 5.1% over the first seven months of the year, as vessel demand temporarily outpaced general cargo growth. As carriers continue restoring regular sailings through the Suez Canal, BIMCO projects that a full unwinding of Cape of Good Hope diversions could reduce vessel demand by up to 10% against available space.

# COMMODITIES, BUNKERS & RATES

## Commodities Focus

Copper futures pulled back below US$6.70/pound on Thursday, retreating from recent record territory as strong American economic indicators lifted the U.S. dollar and fueled projections for additional Federal Reserve interest rate hikes. While currency strength made base metals more expensive for global buyers, downside movement was cushioned by acute supply challenges, highlighted by BHP's operational pause at the colossal Escondida mine in Chile following a worker fatality, alongside projections by Sprott Asset Management that global mined output could shrink this year for the first time since 2017 after mine disruptions in Indonesia and the Democratic Republic of Congo removed approximately 600,000 tons of expected annual volume. UK aluminium futures also slipped beneath US$3,250/ton to touch a three-week low, pressured by the advancing dollar, smelter restarts, and an outbound flow of metal from China-where August aluminium shipments climbed 17.2% y-o-y, which helped counterbalance an ongoing 44% y-o-y output drop across the Gulf Cooperation Council stemming from impaired transit through the Strait of Hormuz, an area that historically accounted for roughly 10% of global supplies. Meanwhile, Chinese iron ore futures hovered near one-month lows around CNY 710 per ton, weighed down by weak manufacturing and construction demand as well as abundant seaborne supply, which saw Australian shipments exceed 21 million tons w-o-w and combined stockpiles across seven major export ports in Australia and Brazil rise by 258,000 tons to 11.95 million tons. Domestic steel producers continue to suffer compounding margin losses from costly Shanxi coking coal, though the prospect of pre-holiday inventory building ahead of the week-long Golden Week celebrations in early October has provided a temporary floor for ferrous prices.
