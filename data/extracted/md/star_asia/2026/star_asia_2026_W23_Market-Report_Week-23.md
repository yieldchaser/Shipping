WEEKLY MARKET REPORT

WEEK 23 · JUNE 5TH, 2026

MARKET OVERVIEW

This week saw Posidonia 2026 arrive on the sunny shores of Athens. With over 40,000 attending the exhibitions and various parties around the islands. It was a biennial event that attracted maritime professionals from all over the world. Key topics that dominated the talks are of AI in maritime and of course, the Strait of Hormuz. This week, commercial transits through the Strait of Hormuz have slowed to an absolute minimum as intermediate peace talks between the United States and Iran stall over geopolitical conditions. Daily shiptracking data indicate that recent activity has been limited to just a handful of domestic or heavily insulated vessels, with zero commercial transits observed on Friday morning. The underlying friction remains a significant gap in diplomatic demands, with Tehran tying the reopening of the crucial waterway to broader regional ceasefires. Compounding the gridlock, naval forces enforcing the regional port blockade have rerouted or interdicted well over a hundred merchant vessels to maintain regulatory compliance. Meanwhile, independent satellite intelligence has identified multiple crude tankers anchored just outside the strait, utilising dark-fleet tactics and transponder deactivations to bypass escalating enforcement efforts. Evaluating real-time cargo flows has become increasingly difficult for market analysts due to widespread satellite interference and deliberate tracking disruptions across the high-risk corridor. It has become common practice for regional operators to deactivate AIS when entering the hot zone, with tracking signals often remaining dark for up to two weeks until vessels reach East Asian waters. This reduction in transparency, coupled with the heavy naval presence, continues to skew operational data and inflate regional war-risk insurance premiums. While a select group of independent shipowners continues to navigate the area to secure high freight premiums, mainstream operators are choosing to maintain an extended buffer, leaving the trade corridor highly constrained. This operational impasse has ignited a fierce debate within international shipowner associations regarding potential compromise solutions to reopen the waterway. While some have recently suggested that paying localised transit fees might be preferable to enduring indefinite stagnation and exorbitant insurance costs, the official industry consensus remains firmly opposed to any financial concessions. Leading maritime bodies, including the Union of Greek Shipowners, clarified their position at a major industry forum in Athens this week, completely rejecting the implementation of unilateral passage tariffs.

# DRY BULK | BDI: 2,981 ▼-7.54% (w-o-w)

The dry bulk shipping market encountered some downward pressure end week, with the Baltic Exchange's main freight index falling for a fourth straight session on Friday by 7.5% to hit a one-week low of 2,981 points. This softer trend was heavily influenced by the larger vessel segments, as the Capesize index slumped to 4,893 points, while the Panamax index shed to settle at 2,236 points. In contrast, smaller vessels showed resilience against the broader decline, with the Supramax index bucking the trend by gaining 19 points to reach 1,588 points. Despite these short-term index fluctuations, the overall market continues to showcase solid fundamental strength and insulation from the sharper volatility seen in other commodity sectors. Stable cargo movements, such as steady bauxite shipments from Guinea and fresh iron ore volumes coming out of West Africa's Simandou development, continue to support a balanced market and generate healthy tonmile demand in the Atlantic. While long-term macroeconomic concerns linger regarding how high energy prices might impact economic activity in the second half of the year, the current landscape remains anchored by reliable underlying transportation demand and a stable freight futures curve.

# TANKERS | BDTI: 2,110 ▲+2.03% (w-o-w)

Oil prices softened slightly as market participants reacted to a new diplomatic proposal aimed at defusing Middle Eastern tensions. At Posidonia this week, Greek shipowners revealed they have strategically positioned a substantial number of large crude carriers near India and East Africa, just a few days' sailing distance from the Persian Gulf. While leaving these vessels idle represents a financial drain compared to active Western routes, owners are betting on an immediate surge in lucrative freight rates should a diplomatic breakthrough suddenly reopen the blocked waterway. While mainstream fleets still avoid the area due to drifting sea mines, a few bold operators maintain minimal trade flows via deactivated transponders or direct government deals involving localized transit fees. Paying these structural tolls has sparked fierce debate among independent shipowners; some view formalized tariffs as an acceptable alternative to prolonged closures, while others argue it sets a dangerous precedent in international waters. Concurrently, ongoing aerial strikes on Russian downstream infrastructure-most recently targeting a refinery in the Krasnodar region-have forced unrefined crude directly into export channels. This disruption has unintentionally driven Black Sea seaborne loadings to a four-year high, boosting weekly export values and creating a dense secondary supply chain for tanker fleets to manage.

Segment Highlights

VLCC: VLCC market experienced a quiet week as industry participants traveled to Athens for Posidonia, keeping rates flat with the Middle East-to-China TD3C route holding steady at WS402.5. Although fresh US blacklisting of Iranian shadow fleets has tightened vessel availability, President Trump's rejection of the US-Iran draft MOU is expected to maintain geopolitical tensions and cap demand, keeping the near-term outlook stable but slow. Suezmax: Suezmax rates in West Africa faced a sharp decline as a post-holiday gap in chartering allowed ballasting VLCCs to aggressively absorb available European-bound cargo, pushing the TD20 route down 5 points to WS147.5. However, rates in the Black Sea held steady near WS219, as high off-market activity and charterers rushing to secure simple fixtures ahead of the Posidonia event balanced out weak supply fundamentals. Aframax: The Mediterranean and USG surged dramatically this week as a rush of fresh business, replacement fixtures, and pre-Posidonia positioning drove the cross-Med TD19 route up nearly 50 points to WS228 and the TD26 Gulf run up 54 points to WS254. The Middle East sector remains flat and listless, weighed down by stagnant cargo inflows and weak Asian margins from the ongoing regional war. LR1 / LR2: Middle East LR market continues to face a downward trend as the prolonged blockade of the Strait of Hormuz has severely cut off regional oil product shipments and reduced operating rates at Asian refineries. This disruption has caused cargo inflows to plummet even in lower-risk zones like Oman and the West Coast of India, pulling the LR1 TC5 index down another 6 points to WS540. MR: Far East MR market remains flat as a drought in product export cargoes, caused by poor global refining margins and lower refinery operating rates, continues to increase available vessel capacity. Rates are firmer elsewhere, with the Middle East TC17 route holding steady at WS730 and the USG TC14 surging 29 points to WS259.

Market Insights

The recycling markets closed the week with the same imbalance that has shaped trading for much of the quarter: firm appetite at the yards set against a thin supply of available candidates. Demand-side fundamentals across the sub-continent remain constructive, but the limited release of tonnage has kept reported sale activity muted, with most concluded business confined to smaller and specialist units. Currency movements were the week's most notable feature. The Indian Rupee and Pakistani Rupee both firmed against the US Dollar, easing some of the import cost pressure that had weighed on local steel economics, while the Bangladeshi Taka held within its established band and the Turkish Lira drifted marginally weaker. Local steel plate prices softened modestly in India and Bangladesh and held flat in Pakistan, leaving ship recyclers cautious on offered levels even as competition for any genuine candidate remained keen. Ship recycling markets expect the supply-constrained pattern to extend through the remainder of June. Firm freight earnings and an easing geopolitical backdrop give owners little incentive to release tonnage, while the monsoon will increasingly govern the cadence of beaching across the sub-continent. Pricing should stay broadly firm, with Bangladesh and Pakistan leading and India close behind on the back of a recovering Rupee. Alang, India: Alang markers remained steady through the week. The recovering Rupee was the dominant theme. From 01 January to 05 June 2026, a total of 45 vessels were beached, amounting to 435,694 MT. Activity was strongest in April, with 14 vessels totaling 179,373 MT, while February also remained firm with 16 vessels totaling 119,589 MT. In sharp contrast, May recorded a historic low, with only one vessel beached for just 8,320 MT. This marked the weakest monthly performance of the period and highlighted a significant slowdown in recycling activity. The lack of fresh tonnage suggests that sellers remained hesitant, while buyers and yards adopted a cautious approach amid uncertain local steel prices, currency volatility, and subdued market sentiment. Early June has seen one vessel beached so far, totalling 33,003 MT as a few more larger tankers on their way, but stronger fresh sales will be required for the market to recover from May's unusually weak momentum, finding a firmer tone to import economics and supporting buyers' willingness to commit, even as local plate prices drifted lower over consecutive sessions. India remains the most keenly priced of the sub-continent destinations, and the breadth of compliant capacity at the yards keeps it the default home for owners seeking a transparent, well-documented recycling route.

Gaddani, Pakistan: A strengthening Rupee and disciplined local conditions kept buyer sentiment positive, with recyclers actively seeking units against a backdrop of scarce supply. Local plate prices were unchanged on the week, and Pakistan's absolute pricing stayed among the firmest in the basin, narrowing the customary gap to the higher priced Bangladeshi yards. Pakistan's competitive standing has consolidated over the quarter, helped by currency resilience and a shortage of available ships that has concentrated demand. A sustained normalisation of regional shipping routes could, over a multi-month horizon, erode some of the proximity advantage Gadani has enjoyed, but that is a question for later quarters. For now, the yards remain well-positioned, with the absence of tonnage the only cause of concern.

Aliaga, Turkey: Ship recycling markets remained subdued, with limited fresh activity and cautious sentiment. The Lira drifted marginally weaker, and offered levels stayed well below the sub-continent across all vessel types, leaving Turkey structurally uncompetitive for mainstream tonnage on a per LDT basis.

Turkey's relevance continues to lie in the EU-regulated and specialist segments, where regulatory compliance and proximity to European owners outweigh headline price. We expect that niche role to persist regardless of how the broader recycling cycle develops, with Aliaga effectively operating in a separate market from the sub-continent yards.

# SUB-CONTINENT & TURKEY SCRAP MARKETS

India: The imported ferrous scrap avenue remains soft as domestic mills continue to draw down local inventories and clear existing seaborne arrivals rather than issue fresh orders. While international traders face inventory liquidation pressures, their asking prices for UK/Brazil-origin HMS 80:20 at US$345/t CFR and UK shredded scrap at US$400-$405/t CFR have met severe buyer resistance. Refracted by tight local margins, Indian buyers have capped workable indications near US$365/t CFR for shredded material and US$340/t CFR for Australia-origin HMS 80:20, keeping broader trading volumes heavily restricted. Pakistan: Imported scrap values remained subdued as a persistent bid-offer gap continues to stall fresh procurement and limit localised booking momentum. Buyers are aggressively holding their ground by targeting containerised shredded scrap within a tight range of US$415-$420/t CFR Qasim. Although alternative raw material options have surfaced, including Malaysia-origin GI bundles at US$430/t CFR and standard HMS bundles at US$408/t CFR, actual transactions have been few and far between due to defensive milling sentiment. Bangladesh: The seaborne arena in Chattogram remains distinctly muted, with local steel mills consistently favouring domestic scrap over expensive imports due to superior cost competitiveness. This cautious approach is reinforced by healthy local scrap availability in Dhaka, trading at BDT 49,000- 51,000/t (US$399-US$415/t), which has successfully suppressed the urgency for imported volumes. Consequently, elevated PNS offers from Singapore and indicative UK-origin shredded prices at US$410- $415/t CFR have failed to stimulate any major transactional activity. Turkey: Deep-sea imported scrap prices maintained a stable day-on-day profile on 5 June, though overall trading activity remains slow as participants remain firmly on the sidelines. US-origin HMS 80:20 offers have consolidated around US$407/t CFR, even though some optimistic suppliers continue to hold out for tradable benchmarks nearer to US$410/t CFR. Meanwhile, European exporters are presenting EU-origin HMS 80:20 cargoes within a steady bracket of US$397-$398/t CFR, keeping the short-term outlook flat but steady.
