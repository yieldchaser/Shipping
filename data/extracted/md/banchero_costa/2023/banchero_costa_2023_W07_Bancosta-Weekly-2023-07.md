# Banchero Costa Weekly Market Report - Week 7, 2023

## Weekly Macro Insight: China Coal Imports

After a slow start in the first quarter, global coal trade has really picked up pace last year, and is now fully back to pre-Covid levels. In the full 12 months of 2022, total global seaborne coal loadings increased by +5.8% y-o-y to 1204.8 mln t (excluding cabotage), from 1138.3 mln t in the full 12 months of 2021, although still below the 1275.6 mln t in Jan-Dec 2019. As already mentioned, the worst was at start of the year, and the trend in recent months has been increasingly positive.

In 1Q 2022, global loadings were down -4.8% y-o-y to just 257.4 mln t, and down -20.3% from 1Q 2019. In 2Q 2022, coal loadings were a strong +8.5% y-o-y at 313.8 mln t, and down -4.1% from 2Q 2019. In 3Q 2022, shipments increased again to 317.2 mln t, up +6.3% y-o-y, and just -0.7% from 3Q 2019. In 4Q 2022, loadings were 316.5 mln t, up +12.9% y-o-y from 4Q 2021, and -0.6% from 4Q 2019. In Jan-Dec 2022, exports from Indonesia increased by +21.1% y-o-y to 388.8 mln t, whilst from Australia down -5.0% y-o-y to 340.4 mln t. Seaborne coal imports into the European Union surged by +34.0% y-o-y to 116.6 mln t in Jan-Dec 2022, whilst imports to India increased by +13.6% y-o-y to 203.8 mln t, and imports to China declined by -3.2% y-o-y to 234.7 mln t. **Mainland China** is currently the world's largest seaborne importer of coal (including both thermal and coking), accounting for 19.8% of the global seaborne coal market in 2022. It was ahead of India, which accounts for 17.2% of coal trade and Japan with a 14.6% market share.

Total seaborne coal imports into China in the 12 months of 2022 reached 234.9 mln tonnes, according to Refinitiv vessel tracking data. This was down -3.3% y-o-y from the 242.8 mln tonnes of 2021, and -1.6% from the 238.7 mln t in 2020, and also -10.4% below the record 262.1 mln tonnes imported in 2019. The year started poorly but finished strongly. In 1Q 2022, China imported just 38.7 mln t of coal (-35.2% y-o-y), in 2Q 2022 they climbed to 49.3 mln t (-16.5% y-o-y), in 3Q 2022 moved up further to 72.1 mln t (+10.7% y-o-y), and in 4Q 2022 improved further to 74.8 mln t (+26.8% y-o-y). The main coal import terminals in Mainland China are: Meizhou in Fujian (14.8 mln t discharged in Jan-Dec 2022), Guangzhou in Guangdong (13.3 mln t), Caofeidian in Hebei (11.3 mln t), Fangcheng in Guangxi (10.7 mln t), Haimen in Guangdong (9.2 mln t), Qinzhou (8.4 mln t), Yuhuan (7.2 mln t), Shanghai (6.6 mln t), Bayuquan (6.3 mln t), Tianjin (6.0 mln t), Shanwei (5.6 mln t), Rizhao (5.4 mln t), Jingtang (5.1 mln t), Taicang (5.1 mln t), Zhuhai (4.9 mln t), Raoping (4.8 mln t), Zhanjiang (4.7 mln t).

The majority (60%) of coal volumes into China are loaded on Panamax or Post-Panamax vessels, with 30% on Handy or Supramax vessels, and just 10% on Capesize or VLOC tonnage. In terms of trading patterns, things are changing quite a bit. Indonesia is still by far the top supplier of coal to China, accounting for 60.9% of China's imports in 2022. Arrivals from Indonesia however declined by -1.9% y-o-y to 143.0 mln t in the full 12 months of 2022. The second largest supplier of coal to China is Russia, accounting for a 23.9% share of Chinese imports. Shipments from Russia to China increased by +35.5% y-o-y to 56.0 mln t in Jan-Dec 2022, from 41.4 mln tonnes in the same period of 2021. On a monthly basis, volumes from Russia to China reached an all-time record of 7.4 mln tonnes in July 2022 (+81.8% y-o-y) and then declined to a still impressive 5.6 mln tonnes in December 2022 (+78.8% y-o-y).

Imports from Russia have surged last year as Europe suspended purchasing from the country. Shipments from the USA to China declined by -62.9% y-o-y to 4.5 mln t in Jan-Dec 2022, from the record 12.0 mln tonnes in 2021. Volumes from South Africa have also declined by -38.6% y-o-y to 8.5 mln t in 2022, from 13.8 mln t in 2021.

# CAPESIZE MARKET

## ATLANTIC AND PACIFIC BASIN

The negative sentiment persisted and the Capesize 5TC average fell to $2,246/d last week. The market remained quite depressed with very little signs of recovery at least for the coming future. Current levels are the lowest since early June 2020. Taking into account a possible situation with idling tonnage, a recovery is not expected on the short term. The current market conditions are largely due to a lack of cargoes and very low activity in the Atlantic basin. There are no fresh cargoes from West Australia, some players believe that the floor may have been reached. The Pacific was a just a bit busy with all miners in the market during the week. Nevertheless, the C5 route ended up the week recording levels of $6.05/mt with Rio Tinto that fixed 2 Capesize ships to move their 170,000 mt +/- 10% iron ore from Dampier to Qingdao at $6.05/mt for a laycan 6-8 March.

In the Atlantic basin, very little activity reported from Brazil, with some fixtures reported for end March laydays at a level around $16.25/mt. The oversupply kept putting pressure on current freight rates. Some backhaul cargoes from Canada and Colombia to Far East were reported at negative TCE pushing the market further down. Out of Brazil, CSN fixed a Capesize ship to move their 170,000 mt +/- 10% iron ore stem from Itaguai to Qingdao for 28-31 March laycan at $16.25/mt. From South Africa, Solebay was looking for a Capesize to move their cargo of 170,000 mt +/- 10% magnetite from Richards Bay (option Maputo loading) to Qingdao, laycan 10-16 March and indicative offers were seen at low/mid $11s/mt.

# PANAMAX MARKET

## ATLANTIC BASIN

Despite activity slightly increasing, rates remained downbeat due to the number of ballasters desperately looking for cargoes to fix at a reasonable rates. In N Atlantic a modern Kamsarmax open in Flushing was reported fixed close to $14,000/d for a trip via US EC to India and an LME open Hamburg was fixed at $12,750/d dop for the same direction and an option to redeliver Spore/Jpn range at $13,750/d. NCSAm and ECSAm were the most active regions and top specs ballasting Kamsarmax achieved $14,500/d + 450,000 bb for a trip via NCSAm and redely F East. Large grain houses have been bidding Panamax stems ECSAm-China around $31/32/mt depending on dates and vessels' spec. Activity in BSea remained constant and an eco Kamsarmax achieved $14,500/d + 500,000 bb basis delivery Port Said for a trip via Ukraine BSea to F East with grains.

## PACIFIC BASIN

Another negative week in the Pacific market. Despite higher activity from S America was absorbing a part of the ballasters coming from F East, the severe tonnage oversupply in the Pacific region was still too large and impacting negatively rates: the level of demand coming from Indonesia, Australia and NoPac was not enough. For what concern coal exports from Indonesia, standard Panamax/Kamsarmax were reported fixed around $5/6,000/d basis dely S China; good specs, eco vessels managed to get $1/2,000/d more. Out of Australia, the standard round coal to China was paying $6/7,000/d sub vessel specs and position. Grain cargoes were paying a bit more due to required specs; for example an 81,000 dwt built 2018 was reported at $9,000/d basis dely Zhoushan. On NoPac charterers were fixing standard ships in the $6/7,000/d and good specs candidates around $8/9,000/d for grain cargoes.

# SUPRAMAX & HANDYSIZE MARKET

## US GULF / NORTH AMERICA

On larger units rates finally rebounded, several fixtures were reported and the tonnage list decreased considerably. TransAtlantic RV was fixed on Supramax around $11,000/d and on Ultramax units around $12,500/d. On Fronthauls rates rose to $13,500/14,000/d for Supramax and around $15,500/d for Ultramax. On Handysize vessels rates remained stable, trip to Med was assessed at $7,500/d and around $5,000/d for CrossCaribbs or to ECSAm.

## EAST COAST SOUTH AMERICA

Rates slightly increased for all sizes. On Handies there were rumors that a 38,000 dwt was fixed on subs at $14,000/d for a tct with grains to WCSAm basis dely aps S Brazil. A 40,000 dwt was fixed basis dely ECSAm for a tct via Santos to Morocco with sugar at $10,500/d. On larger units very little was whispered and Tess58 type was assessed around $10,000/d basis dely aps ECSAm to Med with grains, unchanged from the previous week.

## NORTH EUROPE / CONTINENT

Slow activity in N Sea kept Handy rates stable at $7,000/d for Cont-Med and at $10,000/d for Cont-F East. Supramax rates were softer and Cont-Med was fixed around $6,500/d while fronthaul remained at $12,000/d. An Ultramax was rumored at $8,000/d from Cont to Turkey with scrap.

## BLACK SEA / MEDITERRANEAN

Despite the positive performance of FFAs during the week, the market in BSea remained stable once again and Supramax recorded even a downturn on some routes. The trip to F East basis dely Canakkale remained stable for all the sizes: Handies $12,000/d, Supramax $12,500/d and Ultramax $13,000/d. CrossMed (including dely Canakkale via BSea to Med) recorded minor variations, slightly positive for Handies and slightly negative for larger units; everything on the route was fixed around $8,000/d The rate via Black Sea to Cont remained around $8/8,500/d, but Supramax units lost something compared to Handies. TransAtlantic trips via Med to USG and ECSAm remained stable for Handies and improved a tick for Tess58: $6,500/d and $5,000/d for 35,000 dwt and around $8,000/d for 58,000 dwt.

# SUPRAMAX & HANDYSIZE MARKET

## SOUTH AFRICA / INDIAN OCEAN

Rates appeared to be steady in the area and more fixtures were recorded. CrossMeG on Supramax was fixed multiple times around $10,000/d basis dely sop MEG. On the usual trade of aggregates to Bangladesh Supramax were fixing around $13/14,000/d basis dely loadport in MEG. It was strange to hear that 56/57,000 dwt were fixed around $13,500/d for loading S Oman with one vessel ballasting from Tanzania and another from Mozambique showing that the market in the MEG region held better than neighbouring areas. EC India held around $7,000/d for Supramax to China with iron ore. The market in S Africa dropped and Ultramax were fixed at $10,000/d + 100,000 bb basis dely aps RBCT for trips to India and $12,000/d + 80,000 bb to F East. Towards the very end of the week a Supramax was rumored at $10,000/d basis dely aps S Afr to Pakistan/WC India.

## FAR EAST / PACIFIC

The market remained quite stable with some positive signs for Supramax units. On Chinese destinations, a 55,000 dwt with dely Indo was reported fixed at $9,000/d for a trip via Indo to China, a 58,000 dwt with dely Indo achieved $12,500/d for a trip via Australia to China and a 56,000 dwt with dely S China was done at $6,000/d for a trip via Indo to China. A 56,000 dwt dely Vietnam was fixed at $8,500/d for a trip to Bangladesh with clinker and a 63,000 with dely N China was rumored $6,000/d for a trip to USG.

# CRUDE TANKER MARKET

**VLCC** rates moved up substantially to WS68 for 270,000 mt for MEG-China and around WS67 for 260,000 mt WAfr-China. These are fresh 2023 highs. The **Suezmax** market in WAfr was active for the first decade of March and Petrogal fixed 130,000 mt to Sines off 7/3 at WS130. In the Americas rates for 70,000 mt USG-UKCM jumped up to WS217.5 level. East of Suez the market was pretty much steady around WS187.5 level for 80,000 mt from the MEG. Still 2 (vs 2) VLCC and 1 (vs 1) Suezmax waiting off China laden for more than 2 weeks. Delays at Turkish Straits 11 days northbound and 7 days southbound.

China's thermal coal prices hit their lowest levels in a year this week on rising inventories as domestic mine production is recovering faster than demand, analysts and traders said. High inventories in the world's top coal consumer are capping its appetite for imports, pressuring global prices.

The spot price of iron ore is being pushed higher in a pincer move of stronger Chinese demand and lower supply from the world's two biggest exporters of the steel raw material. Global miner BHP Group reported a steeper-than-expected 32% fall in first-half profit owing to a drop in iron ore prices, sending its shares down, although it flagged a brightening outlook in China, its biggest customer. China's strict zero-COVID-19 policy curtailed economic activity and dented demand over the past year, driving iron ore prices down from lofty levels, while miners wrestled with surging costs and a tight domestic labour market.

## **India's soymeal exports to soar as drought trims Argentine supply**

Soymeal exports from India are increasing sharply as drought has hit output from top exporter Argentina, with 500,000 tonnes likely to be shipped from the South Asian country in February and March combined, three exporters said. The drop in Argentina's output has driven up the South American producer's prices, making Indian soymeal more competitive.

## **India sets up panel to assess impact of higher temperatures on wheat crop**

India has set up a panel of officials to assess the impact of rising temperatures on the wheat crop, government officials said on Monday, as the weather office warned that above normal temperatures would prevail in key producing states. ## **Russia, Turkey leaders may talk Black Sea grain deal soon -RIA**

Turkish and Russian leaders may soon discuss a U.N.-backed initiative that has enabled the export of grain from Ukrainian ports, but there is no date set yet, a source familiar with the negotiations on the deal told RIA Novosti on Monday. Broader negotiations on extending the Black Sea Grain Initiative that has allowed grain to be exported from three Ukrainian ports are expected this week, Ukraine said.

## **Ukraine grain exports down 28.7% so far in 2022/23 season, exceed 30 mln tonnes**

Ukraine grain exports are down 28.7% at 30.3 million tonnes in the 2022/23 season so far, hit by a smaller harvest and logistical difficulties caused by the Russian invasion, agriculture ministry data showed on Monday. The volume so far in the July to June season included about 10.8 million tonnes of wheat, 17.4 million tonnes of corn and about 2 million tonnes of barley.

## **Brazil farmers harvest 25% of soybean-planted area**

Brazilian farmers have harvested 25% of the soybean area planted for 2022/23 through last Thursday, agribusiness consultancy AgRural said on Monday, as work in the fields advances quickly in top grain producing state Mato Grosso. Harvesting was up eight percentage points from the previous week, said AgRural, while at the same time last year 33% of the Brazilian soy fields had been reaped

Brazilian grain growers are hoarding soybeans as domestic prices drop, according to the board chairman of Brazil's biggest farmer cooperative Coamo, based in the south of the country. In an interview on Friday, Jose Aroldo Gallassini said this year's forward soybean sales are estimated at 5% of total projected output of 6 million tonnes by Coamo, compared with the 25% to 30% range last year at this time.

## **Argentina soy harvest outlook to shrink due to heat wave and drought, exchange says**

Argentina's Buenos Aires grains exchange said on Thursday it would have to cut its harvest estimate for the country's beleaguered 2022/2023 soy crop, citing the combined impact of a recent heat wave and a prolonged drought.
