# COMMENT MARKET REPORT – WEEK 31/2026

# INDIA'S CRUDE OIL IMPORTS

After a marginal decline in 2024, things picked up in 2025, with volumes increasing by +1.8% y-o-y. In Jan-Jun 2026, however, global crude oil loadings declined by -6.1% y-o-y to 1,016.4 mln tonnes, excluding all cabotage trade, according to vessels tracking data from LSEG. Exports from the Arabian Gulf plunged by -30.7% y-o-y to 299.7 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026, and accounted for 29.5% of global seaborne crude trade, down sharply from the historical norm of about 40%, reflecting the ongoing war in the Persian Gulf. Exports from Russian ports (including oil of Kazakh origin) edged up by +3.5% y-o-y to 117.1 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026, or 11.5% of global trade. From South America, exports surged by +31.5% y-o-y to 137.6 mln t. From the USA, exports increased by +20.5% y-o-y to 107.3 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026.

In terms of demand (measured by arrival at discharge ports), the top seaborne importer of crude oil in Jan-Jun 2026 was Mainland China, accounting for 20.5% of global trade. Volumes into China decreased by -14.2% y-o-y to 209.5 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026, from 244.1 mln t in Jan-Jun 2025. Imports into the EU27 up by +1.7% y-o-y to 227.6 mln t, accounting for 22.3% of global trade. To South Korea, imports declined by -18.5% y-o-y to 56.7 mln t. To Japan, imports fell by -22.5% y-o-y to 44.1 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026. Imports into the USA up by +8.9% y-o-y to 62.8 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026. **India** is now again the world's fourth largest seaborne importer of crude oil, after the EU, China, and ASEAN. India accounted for 11.7% of global crude oil trade in Jan-Jun 2026. Seaborne imports to India increased by +2.4% y-o-y in Jan-Dec 2023 to 228.1 mln t, and then by +2.5% y-o-y in Jan-Dec 2024 to 233.4 mln t, and then by +2.5% y-o-y in Jan-Dec 2025 to 239.4 mln t.

In Jan-Jun 2026, crude imports into India edged down -0.3% y-o-y to 119.4 mln t, from 119.7 mln t in Jan-Jun 2025. About 48% of crude oil volumes discharged in India in Jan-Jun 2026 were carried in VLCCs, about 30% were carried in Suezmaxes, and about 22% in Aframaxes. Top crude discharge ports in India in Jan-Jun 2026 were Jamnagar (31.6 mln tonnes of crude oil in Jan-Jun 2026), Vadinar (25.3 mln t), Paradip (16.4 mln t), Mundra (10.9 mln t), Mumbai (9.1 mln t), Cochin (7.7 mln t), Visakhapatnam (6.2 mln t), New Mangalore (5.9 mln t), Chennai (4.7 mln t).

In terms of sources of the shipments, there has been understandably a bit of politically driven reshuffling in recent years. Seaborne imports from Russian ports (which include both oil of Russian origin and oil of non-Russian origin such as Kazakh oil) rose from just 4.1 mln t in 2021 to a peak of 82.0 mln t in 2024, before easing to 79.5 mln t in 2025. In Jan-Jun 2026 they edged up by +1.8% y-o-y to 41.7 mln t. Russian ports remained the second largest source of seaborne oil to India, accounting for 35.0% of volumes in Jan-Jun 2026, now only modestly behind the Arabian Gulf with 39.5%, with West Africa in third place at 8.3% and the USA at 3.6%. Shipments from the Arabian Gulf to India fell by -13.3% y-o-y in Jan-Jun 2026 to 47.2 mln t, but the picture varied widely by country. Saudi Arabia remained India's largest Gulf supplier at 15.1 mln t, or 12.6% of total Indian imports, broadly flat at -2.0% y-o-y. The UAE jumped by +37.9% y-o-y to 13.4 mln t (11.2%), overtaking Iraq to become India's second largest Gulf supplier, while Oman rose sharply to 4.1 mln t, from just 0.7 mln t a year earlier. Iraqi volumes, by contrast, collapsed by -59.3% y-o-y to 9.8 mln t, while Kuwait fell by -32.6% to 2.4 mln t, with smaller contributions from Qatar (1.9 mln t) and Iran (0.6 mln t). Shipments from West Africa to India surged by +31.5% y-o-y in Jan-Jun 2026 to 9.9 mln t. Imports from the USA decreased by -35.2% y-o-y in Jan-Jun 2026 to 4.3 mln t.

## **Vale sees iron ore market resilient despite lower prices in China**

Brazilian miner Vale, one of the world's largest iron ore producers, said on Friday that market fundamentals for the commodity remain resilient despite prices in China hitting a one-year low this week. The situation in China is "more balanced than domestic indicators suggest," Vale's executive vice president for commercial and development, Rogerio Nogueira, told a call with analysts. Demand is also improving outside China, he added.

## **Indonesia's aluminium exports more than **doubled** in June, trade platform data shows**

Indonesia's exports of primary and unwrought alloyed aluminium in June more than doubled compared to the same month of last year, data platform Trade Data Monitor showed. Indonesia shipped 105,619 metric tons of aluminium in June, a surge of 127% from the same month last year, data released late on Monday showed.

## **Rio Tinto signals no rush to revive Glencore deal as standstill ends**

A freeze on Rio Tinto approaching Glencore for a takeover expires this week, but people who have been briefed by top executives don't expect any fresh tie-up talks for now as CEO Simon Trott focuses on cost cuts and asset sales. Trott launched a simplification strategy to collapse Rio Tinto into three core businesses and concentrate on its most profitable assets after he took the top job at the world's second-biggest listed miner a year ago.

## **Chinese state traders make large US soybean purchases**

The U.S. Department of Agriculture on Monday confirmed Chinese purchases of nearly half a million tons of soybeans, after traders said earlier that Chinese state trading companies had bought 14-16 cargoes on Friday totalling one million tons. On Friday, Reuters reported that Chinese government buyers purchased at least eight cargoes of U.S. soybeans for shipment from U.S. Gulf Coast terminals and six cargoes for shipment from Pacific Northwest ports in October and November, citing two U.S. traders with knowledge of the deals.

## **Argentina's July agricultural export revenue down 28%**

Exports from Argentina's agricultural sector totaled $2.918 billion in July, the CIARA-CEC grains processors and exporters chamber said on Monday, down 28% from the same month last year. The chamber noted that July 2025 had above-average sales for due to temporary cuts to export duties in Argentina - the world's largest exporter of soybean oil and meal, as well as a major corn and wheat supplier.

## **Three Russian Black Sea terminals restrict grain intake by truck amid shipping risks, sources say**

Three major Russian Black Sea grain export terminals have restricted grain deliveries by truck amid rising shipping risks in the Black Sea and increased flows following restrictions in the Sea of Azov, five industry sources told Reuters. Russia, the world's largest wheat exporter, restricted shipping through the Sea of Azov this month after Ukrainian drones attacked several Russian grain vessels on the route, which handles about a quarter of the country's grain exports.

## **Russian grain lobby says Kyiv's attacks threaten Black Sea exports, global food security**

Russia's main grain lobby group warned on Friday that Ukrainian drone attacks on Russian ships and ports could shut down grain exports via the Black Sea in the near future, pushing up prices and causing hunger in Africa and the Middle East. Russia, the world's largest wheat exporter, and Ukraine, also a major agricultural exporter, have been attacking each other's agricultural export facilities and commercial vessels in the Black Sea area in recent weeks.

## **India's January to June rice exports climb 5% as non-basmati shipments rise, sources say**

India's rice exports rose 5% in the first half of 2026 from a year earlier, as higher shipments of non-basmati rice offset a drop in basmati exports after the U.S.-Israeli war on Iran disrupted trade with key Gulf markets, two government officials said. India, which accounts for more than 40% of world rice exports, usually ships more than the combined exports of the next three biggest suppliers — Thailand, Vietnam and Pakistan.

## **Global sugar market deficit to widen in new season, says broker**

The global sugar market is projected to show a larger deficit than previously forecast in the new season that starts in October, broker and analyst StoneX said on Tuesday. StoneX revised its deficit estimate to 1.7 million metric tons, from a previous projection of 0.55 million tons, saying that a hot and dry summer will cut European production while drier weather from the El Nino weather pattern will hurt output in India and Thailand. ## **OPEC+ agrees September oil hike, completing rollback of voluntary cuts**

OPEC+ approved an oil production quota increase on Sunday of around 188,000 barrels per day from September, the producer group said, in a move that completes the unwinding of a layer of voluntary output cuts. Due to export disruptions from the Gulf, Russia and Kazakhstan caused by the Iran and Ukraine wars, successive monthly OPEC+ hikes over most of this year have remained largely on paper with little impact on the market.

## **US commercial crude stocks fall to lowest since 2018 on higher exports, refinery processing, EIA says**

U.S. commercial oil inventory drew more than expected last week as refineries stepped up processing and exports edged higher, the Energy Information Administration said on Wednesday, as the war between the United States and Iran escalated. Crude inventories, excluding stocks in the U.S. government's Strategic Petroleum Reserve, fell by 7.2 million barrels to 404.5 million barrels, in the week ended July 24, EIA said, the lowest level since 2018. That compared with analysts' expectations in a Reuters poll for a 1.3 million-barrel draw.

## **Shell more than doubles its profit in Q2, beating expectations**

Shell's adjusted earnings, its definition of net profit, reached $9.84 billion in the second quarter, it said, beating expectations and more than doubling its profit from the same time last year. Higher oil and gas prices, stronger LNG and oil trading and improved chemicals margins helped support the British major's earnings, offsetting lower sales volumes from disruptions to its Qatar operations during the conflict in the Middle East.

## **Trump blasts Exxon, Chevron for making 'too much money,' demands lower gasoline prices**

U.S. President Donald Trump accused ExxonMobil and Chevron on Monday of making "too much money" off higher fuel prices and said the oil giants should "give some of that back to the public," a notable break from his usual alliance with the industry. "I don't like it," Trump told reporters on Monday, three days after the companies reported blowout second-quarter earnings as the ongoing war in Iran keeps oil prices high.

## **US oil exports in July fall to lowest level in eight months, data shows**

U.S. oil exports in July fell to 3.66 million barrels per day, the lowest level in eight months, ship tracking data showed, as a short-lived peace deal between the U.S. and Iran in June briefly flooded markets with Middle Eastern oil and diminished demand for American crude abroad. The U.S. had cinched the spot as the world's top oil exporter this year after the Iran war sharply cut supplies of Middle Eastern crude and forced Asian and European countries to turn to the U.S. to fill the gap.

## **Oil companies signing agreements in Venezuela, but delays persist**

Venezuelan officials and oil executives have moved ahead with the migration of dozens of joint ventures to new terms approved in a sweeping government reform, but delays remain in finalizing the agreements needed to greenlight the projects, four sources involved in the preparations said. Venezuela's oil ministry had set July 28 as the deadline to complete the migration of all existing projects, but the numerous permits and agreements linked to core contracts have created obstacles.

## **Drone strike in Egypt sparks security concerns about Suez oil exports**

A drone strike that damaged two gas tankers in Egyptian waters has renewed energy security concerns about the nearby Suez Canal and a related pipeline, a vital export route for Saudi Arabian oil since the beginning of the Iran war. Even as Iran and its Houthi allies have fired on tankers transiting the Strait of Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb, Egypt's Suez Canal and Sumed pipeline continued to offer safe, northbound export routes for Saudi Red Sea energy cargoes.

## **US LNG exports stall in July despite higher LNG prices**

U.S. exports of liquefied natural gas slipped to 10.48 million metric tons (MT) in July from 10.6 MT in June, according to preliminary data from financial firm LSEG.

# CAPESIZE MARKET

## ATLANTIC AND PACIFIC BASIN

The Capesize market opened the week on a softer footing before staging a recovery into month-end, as an early correction driven by easing bunker prices gave way to firmer freight derivative (FFA) values and a firmer Atlantic. Rates finished mixed to higher across the major iron ore routes. The week began with a downward correction. The Western Australia–Qingdao route (170,000 mt iron ore) eased to USD 12.50/wmt on 27 July as softening bunker prices prompted owners to refresh offers cautiously, then slipped a further 25 cents/wmt to USD 12.25/wmt on 28 July amid sluggish, deadlocked exchanges despite healthy cargo volumes. Sentiment steadied thereafter: with FFA values firming and the Pacific cargo list lengthening, the route closed at USD 13.00/wmt on 31 July — a 70-cent/wmt gain on the day and its highest of the week — as bullish confidence returned and Dampier–Qingdao fixtures were concluded in the high-USD 12s to USD 13/wmt range. The Atlantic traced a similar path. The Tubarao–Qingdao route corrected from USD 34.25/wmt on 27 July to USD 33.55/wmt on 28 July as fixtures dried up, before rebounding on firmer sentiment and a lengthening South Atlantic cargo list to close at USD 34.10/wmt on 31 July.

Owners held offers in excess of USD 35/wmt for early-September laycans, while fronthaul demand stayed firm with North Atlantic fixtures reported from Port Cartier. The South Africa route (Saldanha Bay–Qingdao) softened to USD 24.55/wmt mid-week before recovering to close unchanged on the week at USD 24.95/wmt on 31 July. Overall, the market ended July on a firm note, with resurgent confidence, sustained tonnage demand and a firmer overnight Atlantic underpinning sentiment into the new month.

# PANAMAX MARKET

## ATLANTIC BASIN

The Atlantic maintained a firm, balanced tone all week, ECSA again accounting for most reported activity. Grain cargoes to the Far East remained the principal source of employment, lending steady support to owners despite charterers continuing to test lower levels. Fresh fixtures were not especially numerous, yet consistent cargo flow prevented any meaningful softening in rates. On ECSA/Far East business, modern Kamsarmaxes opening India generally fixed USD 20,000-21,000/day. An 82,000 dwt built 2007 opening Karaikal secured USD 20,000/day, an 82,000 dwt built 2020 opening Kandla USD 20,250/day, whilst a newer 82,000 dwt built 2019 was reported at USD 20,750/day, confirming charterers' continued premium for modern eco tonnage. Older units remained active, several 76,000–82,000 dwt vessels concluding between USD 18,500–19,500/day, illustrating the clear age differential currently evident. Overall, most ECSA grain business concluded within a relatively narrow range, highlighting a stable freight environment. Elsewhere in the Atlantic, activity was more selective but yielded stronger returns. A modern 82,000 dwt opening Rotterdam fixed USEC/India USD 34,000/day, another securing USD 33,000/day for USEC/Far East coal.

Colombian coal cargoes remained well supported, a modern Kamsarmax reportedly fixing just under USD 30,000/day, confirming the premium still available on Atlantic coal against South Atlantic grain.

## PACIFIC BASIN

The Asia-Pacific Panamax market described a V-shaped week, softening in the first half before recovering into the close. Rates fell on Monday 27 July amid a slow start, and again on 28 July as high tonnage availability weighed across regions, marking a mid-week trough. Participants subsequently suggested the Pacific may have found its floor, with improving cargo volumes and a reviving east coast South America market lending support. Sentiment turned more constructive on 30–31 July as freight derivative rates surged overnight and owners adopted a firmer stance, although a persistently lengthy tonnage list capped the extent of the recovery. On the key Australian coking coal routes, Hay Point–Qingdao dipped to USD 17.40/mt on 28 July before rebounding to USD 17.95/mt by 31 July, marginally above the USD 17.85/mt of the preceding Friday. Hay Point–Paradip traced the same path, closing at USD 20.50/mt. The Indonesian thermal coal trade was softer overall, Samarinda–Guangzhou easing to USD 8.75/mt from USD 8.90/mt a week earlier.

Fresh Pacific fixtures stayed limited, with charterers maintaining pressure on owners. Bunker prices were volatile but broadly softer.

## SUPRAMAX & HANDYSIZE MARKET

## **US GULF / NORTH AMERICA**

The USG market was broadly stable during the week, demand remaining strong. On the Supra/Ultra, rates varied: a 63,000 dwt reportedly fixed USD 38,000 APS USG for a TCT with bulk petcoke to India, 55 days WOG, whilst a 56,000 dwt fixed around USD 21,000 basis delivery APS USG for one TCT with grains to Singapore/Japan range, 80 days WOG. Trans-Atlantic saw little reported, most demand concentrating on fronthaul. Handies rates were softer week on week: a well-described 36,000 dwt fixed voyage at an equivalent USD 17,000 APS for grains to the central Mediterranean, 40 days WOG, and a 38,000 dwt fixed around USD 19,000 for one TCT with grains to the Continent, 40 days WOG. Fronthaul demand was limited.

## **EAST COAST SOUTH AMERICA**

The ECSA market continues its downward trend, in line with the general softening across both the Supramax and Handysize segments. On Supramax, West Africa saw a unit fixed via ECSA to China at USD 25,000/day and another to the Continent at USD 20,000/day. On Handysize, a 40,000 dwt built 2023 fixed basis delivery Recalada 29–31 July to the Far East at USD 23,000/day, whilst another unit open ECSA achieved USD 20,500/day to the Continent. Overall, the ECSA market remains in a downward phase, alternating days of relative stability with more pronounced falls.

## **NORTH EUROPE / CONTINENT**

The market maintained a stable-to-firm sentiment overall, although the Continent saw little scrap enquiry until later in the week. On the handies, trips to ECSA and USG/USEC remained the usual discounting directions for owners looking to reposition, holding steady at around USD 8,500/day and USD 11,000/day respectively. Several fixtures were reported on outbound and trans-Atlantic runs: a 35,000 dwt open Casablanca fixed a trip via Safi, redelivery Abidjan, at USD 15,500/day; a 39,000 dwt open Brake was reported fixed APS Sluiskil for a trip to WCSA with fertilisers at USD 15,250/day; whilst a 32,000 dwt open Santander was fixed basis delivery APS Rouen to Atlantic Colombia with grains at USD 9,500/day. On the larger units, levels remained generally firm, with Supramax fronthaul trips from the Continent to Singapore/Japan evaluated at around USD 26,250/day, showing solid support on long-haul business. To conclude, trans-Atlantic repositioning trips from the Continent to the USG held steady in the mid-teens, hovering around USD 16,500/day.

## **BLACK SEA / MEDITERRANEAN**

The Black Sea and Mediterranean market continued its descent this week, with Ukraine and Russia called by almost nobody. Vessels cancelled for Ukrainian cargoes remained spot, discounting to take cargoes out of Romania and Bulgaria, which are not many as yet. End July and August are normally peak season, but this year no short-term increase appears likely. Handy intra-Mediterranean rates now stand at USD 12,000/day basis passing Çanakkale. # SUPRAMAX & HANDYSIZE MARKET

## MEG / INDIAN OCEAN

Indian Ocean Supramax and Ultramax sentiment softened over 27–31 July, with growing tonnage availability along the Indian coasts and limited fresh cargo keeping a lid on rates. Owners nonetheless resisted lowering fixing ideas, particularly for trips towards India, while bunker prices proved volatile through the week. Out of the Persian Gulf, the limestone trade drifted lower before steadying. Freight to move 50,000 mt of limestone from Mina Saqr to Paradip on the east coast of India eased to USD 17.25/mt by 31 July, from USD 17.35/mt on 27 July, having dipped to USD 17.10/mt mid-week. Firmer enquiry was evident for larger stems: a 55,000 mt (plus/minus 10%) limestone cargo from Dibba in the UAE to the east coast of India for 29 July–10 August laycan was fixed at USD 27/mt. From South Africa, the coal trade was largely rangebound amid muted fixing activity. Freight to carry 50,000 mt of coal from Richards Bay Coal Terminal to Port Qasim slipped to USD 22.05/mt on 31 July, from USD 22.40/mt on 27 July, with no fresh fixtures reported during the week.

Newbuilding activity remained healthy this week, with contracting led by strong demand across the container and dry bulk sectors, while fresh orders were also reported for crude and product tankers, as well as a PCTC. Container activity was dominated by feeder and mid-sized vessels, whereas the dry bulk market saw a series of Ultramax orders alongside larger Newcastlemax commitments. In the dry bulk sector, Turkish owner Aruna Shipping reportedly placed an order for 6x 64,500 dwt Ultramax bulk carriers at Yangzhou Guoyu Shipbuilding, with each vessel valued at approximately USD 35 million and deliveries scheduled between mid-2028 and late-2030. The contract also includes options for four additional units. Greek owner W Marine is also understood to have booked 3x 64,800 dwt Ultramaxes at Jiangsu Dajin Heavy Industry for around USD 33.5 million each, with deliveries throughout 2028. Elsewhere, Greek owner JME Navigation reportedly contracted 1x 64,000 dwt Ultramax at New Dayang Shipbuilding for approximately USD 35 million, while Cape Shipping S.A. is understood to have confirmed an order for 3x 181,000 dwt Capesize bulk carriers at Hengli Heavy Industry, priced at around USD 76 million each for delivery in late 2027.

Tanker ordering also remained active. South Korean owner Pan Ocean reportedly returned to Qingdao Beihai for 2x 319,000 dwt VLCCs, with each vessel valued at approximately USD 122 million. The ammonia-ready vessels are scheduled for delivery in 2030. In the products segment, Greek owner Stealth Maritime is understood to have ordered 2x 50,000 dwt MR tankers at Hyundai Mipo, with deliveries due in 2027 at a reported price of USD 52.5 million each. Elsewhere, Cyprus-based Sallaum Lines reportedly booked 1x 8,600 ceu PCTC at China Merchants Heavy Industry, with an option for one additional vessel. The LNG dual-fuel design will also be capable of future ammonia conversion, with delivery scheduled for 2029.
