# Banchero Costa Weekly Market Report - Week 39, 2026

## Weekly Macro Insight: China Soybean Imports

Soybeans are one of the most important dry bulk commodities, and account for around 3 percent of all seaborne dry bulk trade.

Total seaborne exports of soybeans in calendar (Jan-Dec) 2025 increased by +4.6% year-on-year to 159.9 mln tonnes, according to vessel tracking data from AXS Marine.

So far this year, volumes have continued to grow at a healthy pace.

In Jan-Aug 2026, global loadings increased by +6.4% y-o-y to 122.6 mln t, from 115.2 mln t in Jan-Aug 2025.

This was above the 103.7 mln tonnes loaded in Jan-Aug 2023 and 108.5 mln t in Jan-Aug 2024.

The largest exporter of soybeans by far is Brazil, which accounted for 77.8% of global soybean exports by volume in Jan-Aug 2026.

In Jan-Aug 2026, total shipments from Brazil increased by +6.5% y-o-y to 95.3 mln tonnes, up from 89.5 mln tonnes in Jan-Aug 2025.

In second place are the United States, which accounted for 15.7% of global soybean exports this year.

In Jan-Aug 2026, soybean exports from the USA increased by +30.9% y-o-y to 19.3 mln tonnes, recovering from a -10.8% y-o-y decline recorded in Jan-Aug 2025.

In third place is Argentina, whose exports typically peak from May onwards, with a 2.8% share so far in 2026, followed by Uruguay with 1.3% and Canada with 1.0%.

In Jan-Aug 2026, Argentina exported just 3.4 mln tonnes of soybeans, down -43.1% y-o-y from the 6.0 mln t in Jan-Aug 2025.

In terms of imports, the top destination for soybeans so far in 2026 is still Mainland China, accounting for 65.0% of global seaborne imports.

Imports to China increased by +2.0% y-o-y in Jan-Dec 2025 to 107.6 mln tonnes.

In Jan-Aug 2026, Chinese imports increased by +4.0% y-o-y to 75.5 mln tonnes.

Imports to the European Union declined by -6.2% y-o-y in Jan-Dec 2025 to 13.5 mln t.

In Jan-Aug 2026, however, European imports recovered by +5.2% y-o-y to 9.7 mln tonnes.

The EU now accounts for 8.3% of global seaborne soybean imports.

Let's now focus a bit more on China. Top soybean discharge ports in China in Jan-Dec 2025 were: Rizhao (9.6 mln t in Jan-Dec 2025), Machong (7.4 mln t), Tianjin (7.2 mln t), Zhangjiagang (7.0 mln t), Gaogang (5.4 mln t), Fangcheng (4.2 mln t), Nantong (4.2 mln t), Dongjiakou (4.0 mln t), Ningbo (3.6 mln t).

93.1% of soybean imports into China in 2025 were loaded on Panamaxes (including Kamsarmax), 6.3% on Supramaxes, 0.3% on Post-Panamaxes.

In Jan-Aug 2026, arrivals of US soybeans into China were broadly stable, down -1.1% y-o-y to 12.3 mln tonnes, following a -10.8% y-o-y decline in Jan-Aug 2025.

This was also below the 13.9 mln t delivered from the USA to China in Jan-Aug 2024.

The USA now accounts for 16.2% of China's imports so far this year.

Brazil accounted for 75.5% of China's soybean imports in Jan-Aug 2026, with volumes from Brazil to China essentially flat at +0.4% y-o-y in that period, at 57.0 mln tonnes.

It is important to consider the seasonality of soybean crops.

Brazilian exports are shipped mostly in the Spring, from February to July, and will appear in China's import statistics from March to August.

USA exports, on the other hand, normally peak in the fourth quarter of the year, and should be the most active trade in the winter months.

With the peak Brazilian season now largely reflected in the Jan-Aug 2026 import figures, attention turns to the upcoming US export season in the fourth quarter, which will indicate whether China returns more actively to the US crop this year.

# COMMODITY NEWS – DRY BULK

## **Indonesia's nickel production curbs leave market unconvinced**

Indonesia is learning that building a dominant position in the nickel market is the easy part. Leveraging its influence over supply to control prices is proving much trickier. The Southeast Asian country now accounts for over 60% of global output of a metal used by both stainless steel makers and electric vehicle battery manufacturers.

## **China to cut tariffs on US farm goods, but list excludes soybeans**

China is set to cut tariffs on a broad range of US agricultural goods, from corn and wheat to meat and dairy, but top import item soybeans were excluded from a tariff-reduction list its commerce ministry issued. Markets have been waiting for news on Chinese tariff cuts on US farm goods following last week's Washington summit of leaders Xi Jinping and Donald Trump.

## **China private crushers buy Australian canola, first deals since 2020**

A private Chinese crusher bought 68,000 metric tons of new-crop Australian canola for March shipment, after a similar cargo purchased by another private processor arrived earlier this month, trade sources said, in the first such deals since trade was disrupted in 2020. "Old crop trade was dominated by COFCO but there some interest from private sector in new-crop Australian canola," said one Asia-based trader.

## **Wheat buyers brace for higher costs as Russia-Ukraine war drags on**

Global wheat buyers holding out to replenish dwindling stocks face painfully higher costs when they return to the market in the coming weeks, with world prices soaring as the Russia-Ukraine war shows no signs of abating. Top wheat importers in Asia, the Middle East and Africa that were counting on the

Black Sea region for wheat have been finding it hard to secure supplies, as attacks on vessels and port infrastructure have brought cargo movements to a near standstill since July.

## **Russia can restart 80% of Black Sea grain export terminals if attacks stop**

Russia could quickly restart up to 80% of its grain terminal capacity in the Black Sea and Sea of Azov if diplomatic efforts to halt mutual attacks by Russia and Ukraine result in a ceasefire, a Reuters analysis of industry data showed on Friday. But three terminals accounting for about 20% of Russia's grain export infrastructure in the area have suffered heavy damage and could take months to repair, the analysis showed.

## **Russia has fewer wheat buyers as Black Sea trade stalls, data shows**

Fewer countries are buying Russian wheat after attacks by Russia and Ukraine in the Black Sea have kept a key export route closed since July, data from the Russian Grain Union lobby group showed on Wednesday. The data showed that only eight countries bought wheat from Russia, the world's biggest wheat exporter, in September, down from 26 a year earlier. In August, the number of buyers fell to 16 from 43 in the same month of 2025.

## **Ukraine looks to Baltic ports for exports, much like Russia**

Ukraine is exploring the use of Baltic Sea ports for grain exports, the agriculture ministry said on Wednesday, following a similar move by Russia as both sides seek alternatives to Black Sea routes disrupted by their war. Sources have told Reuters that Russian companies are repurposing fertiliser, coal, and other cargo terminals at the country's Baltic and Arctic ports to handle grain exports after Ukrainian drone attacks disrupted shipments through the Black Sea.

## **France loads first wheat for Yemen in years amid Black Sea disruption**

A vessel is loading around 60,000 metric tons of wheat in France to send to Yemen, LSEG data showed on Friday, a first such shipment in four years that comes as importers seek alternatives to disrupted Black Sea supplies. A bulk carrier is due to load 20,000 tons at Dunkirk after already taking 40,000 tons at Rouen along the north coast of France, LSEG's shipping data showed.

## **Argentina set for near-record farm dollar windfall as global supply shocks lift grain exports**

Argentina is on track for a near-record farm export windfall next year as strong crop prices, a bumper harvest and supply disruptions in rival exporting regions boost grain shipments and hard-currency inflows, the Rosario grains exchange said in a report on Friday. The farm sector could bring in about $40 billion in export dollars in 2027, which would be the second-highest annual total on record behind only 2022, according to the report.

## **US harvest delays send soy processors scrambling for supplies**

Soybean processors in the western US Midwest are offering hefty premiums for immediate deliveries of the oilseed as persistent rains delay the early harvest, tightening supplies and forcing some plants to scale back production, according to interviews with grain merchandisers and publicly reported company data. The scramble for soybeans has sent cash prices soaring, creating an unexpected windfall for farmers able to harvest their crops and get them to market, or sell off the last of their old crops.

## **Saudi Arabia restarts East-West oil pipeline, sources say**

Saudi Arabia has restarted operations at its East-West Pipeline and could resume exports from the Red Sea port of Yanbu later on Tuesday, three sources briefed on the matter said, as signs mounted of an increase in Middle Eastern oil flows. The resumption of supplies on Tuesday helped to drive selling on global oil markets. Global oil benchmark Brent crude fell by more than $2 a barrel towards $97, its lowest since September 8.

## **Mideast oil exports rebound in September as Saudi Arabia boosts shipments**

Crude oil exports from key Middle East producers rebounded in September to 12.8 million barrels per day, the highest since the US-Israeli war with Iran started in February, data from Kpler showed, as Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates boosted exports. The rebound came following a recovery in exports via the Strait of Hormuz, which were set to hit about 7.4 million bpd this month, as Saudi Arabia diverted oil exports from the Red Sea port of Yanbu following attacks that damaged its East-West pipeline, the preliminary data showed.

## **Gulf of Oman ship-to-ship oil transfers reach limit as Saudi exports surge**

Ship-to-ship transfers in the Gulf of Oman for Middle Eastern oil from inside the Strait of Hormuz have reached their limits after Saudi Arabia diverted exports from the Red Sea, adding to shipments from other producers, trade sources and analysts said. The increasing amount of Saudi exports this month requires more supertankers to shuttle crude through Hormuz to conduct cargo transfers, they said. That has reduced ship availability, raising shipping costs and increasing the time cargoes are on the water before reaching refineries.

## ****Saudi oil export strategy hits new hurdle as Red Sea insurance costs soar****

The cost of insuring an oil tanker loaded from Saudi Arabia's main Red Sea port has tripled in recent weeks, industry sources told Reuters, adding to the major crude producer's struggles to find a viable channel for its exports. Saudi Arabia used its East-West pipeline to divert around 4 million barrels per day - 4% of global supply - to the Red Sea after Iran constrained Gulf exports via the Strait of Hormuz from March in response to US and Israeli air strikes.

## **US crude inventories rise, fuel stocks fall as refining slips, EIA says**

US crude stocks rose last week while fuel inventories fell following a drop in refining activity in the Midwest, the Energy Information Administration said on Wednesday. Crude inventories rose by 3 million barrels to 426.4 million barrels in the week ended September 18, the EIA said, compared with analysts' expectations in a Reuters poll for a 641,000-barrel draw.

## **Trump supports diesel export ban as fuel prices hit records**

US President Donald Trump said on Tuesday he backed a ban on diesel exports, as Republican candidates in some of the tightest election races in November called for the measure as a way to curb record fuel prices. Diesel prices have shot to record highs in the US and Europe as wars in Iran and Ukraine sharply cut exports from some of the biggest producers such as Russia, Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates.

## ****US Energy Secretary Wright says diesel export ban won't work****

US Energy Secretary Chris Wright on Wednesday said a US ban on diesel exports would not work and could push up gasoline and jet fuel prices, a stance at odds with President Donald Trump, who is backing the idea. Diesel prices have surged to record highs due to the US-Israeli war with Iran and the conflict in Ukraine, angering farmers and other users of the fuel ahead of the midterm elections in November that will decide control of Congress.

## ****AI challenges fuel trading's old guard: Russell****

The Iran conflict has put a spotlight on the somewhat murky world of refined fuel trading as the world battles constrained supply and record-high prices. But it is AI that may bring the trading of diesel, gasoline and other products out of the shadows for good. Global biofuels output is expected to surge nearly 70% by 2030 from 2025 levels as major producing countries push ahead with raising blending mandates in response to the energy crisis triggered by the Iran war, a study published on Tuesday found. The study was co-authored by London-based think tank Chatham House and non-profit certification group the Forest Stewardship Council.

## **Global gas market pricing prolonged tightness due to Iran war, IGU executive says**

Global gas markets are increasingly pricing in tight supplies well beyond winter as the Iran war disrupts Gulf liquefied natural gas exports and complicates Europe's efforts to rebuild gas inventories, a top industry executive said. The outlook comes as Europe competes with Asia for LNG cargoes while attempting to refill storage sites ahead of winter, amid continued uncertainty over the conflict's impact on exports from Qatar, one of the world's largest LNG suppliers.

## **EU warns of energy price crisis, asks countries to consider curbing demand, letter shows**

The European Union is facing an energy price crisis as fallout from the Iran war roils oil and gas markets, the bloc's energy chief said on Friday in a letter seen by Reuters that urged countries to consider measures to curb natural gas demand. "We are facing a price crisis linking to a supply crisis," EU Energy Commissioner Dan Jorgensen said in a letter to EU countries' energy ministers.

## **High European gas prices push power producers back to coal**

Soaring gas prices are driving European utilities back to coal, with power generation from the most polluting fuel likely to jump by a quarter in the next six months to offset a similar plunge in gas-fired generation, analysts said. The Iran-US war has choked liquefied natural gas shipments through the Strait of Hormuz, sending benchmark European gas prices above €80 per megawatt hour this month, their highest in three years.

## **Russia cuts natural gas output, export forecasts**

Russia's economy ministry expects the country to produce and export less natural gas this year than previously forecast, according to a draft government document seen by Reuters, as Europe prepares to wind up its final Russian gas purchases. Earlier this month, Reuters reported that Russia had also downgraded its 2026 oil output forecast to a 17-year low and revised the fuel export outlook for this year and next due to the war with Ukraine.

## **India orders captive coal plants to maximise power output on surging demand**

India has ordered more than 100 captive coal-fired power plants to operate at maximum capacity from October 1 through year-end to meet what it expects will be a rise in electricity demand. The federal power ministry's order, invoked under emergency provisions of the Electricity Act, applies to plants with installed capacity of at least 50 megawatts.

## **India sees coal remaining power mainstay through 2035-36**

India expects coal to supply about half its electricity through 2035-36 as surging power demand forces the country to rely on thermal generation alongside a rapid expansion of renewable energy, senior power ministry officials said on Thursday. "Thermal is here to stay for a long time," said Aadhar Raj, joint secretary in India's Ministry of Power, at an industry event by the Federation of Indian Chambers of Commerce and Industry.

## **Lithuania plans key substation near Russia, fortified against explosive drones**

Lithuania is planning defences against military drones at a key future power grid substation linking the Baltic states with western Europe in an area that intelligence sources say Russia could target with a false flag attack. The construction plans include fencing strong enough to resist an explosives-laden truck, sensors searching for possible tunnels dug by hostile actors, and concrete "sarcophagi" for transformers to withstand any drone attack, Litgrid CEO Andrius Semeskevicius told Reuters.

## **Zelenskiy says he discussed energy truce, efforts to end Russia war with Trump**

Ukrainian leader Volodymyr Zelenskiy said on Tuesday he discussed with US President Donald Trump efforts to end the 4-year-old war with Russia, including a potential bilateral ceasefire on energy-related targets. Speaking to the media after a 40-minute meeting with Trump at UN headquarters in New York, Zelenskiy said Kyiv was ready for any format of an energy ceasefire with Moscow, but there had been no discussion of Ukraine unilaterally halting its attacks on Russian oil refineries.

# CAPESIZE MARKET

## ATLANTIC AND PACIFIC BASIN

The Capesize market firmed early before retreating from midweek, all three principal iron ore routes closing lower as surging bunker prices and a succession of Asian public holidays sapped liquidity.

Japanese participants were absent from 21 to 23 September, with Chinese and South Korean markets closed later in the week.

The Western Australia–Qingdao route (170,000 mt iron ore, plus/minus 10%) gained 20 cents/wmt to USD 16.80/wmt on 21 September on strong freight derivative (FFA) gains, owners resisting lower offer ideas against a wide bid-offer spread, and held there on 22 September, when a mining major fixed several Capesizes from Dampier to Qingdao at around mid-USD 16/wmt for 8–10 October laycan and another took tonnage from Port Hedland at high-USD 16/wmt.

The route then fell 45 cents/wmt to USD 16.35/wmt on 23 September as sentiment weakened, a further 35 cents/wmt to USD 16.00/wmt on 24 September amid surging bunker prices — Dampier–Qingdao fixed at USD 15.95/wmt for 12–14 October — and 35 cents/wmt to close at USD 15.65/wmt on 25 September, the Mid-Autumn holiday leaving exchanges sparse.

Dampier–Qingdao was reported at about USD 15.60/wmt for 13–15 October.

The route ended 95 cents/wmt below its 18 September level.

Tubarao–Qingdao firmed 10 cents/wmt to USD 42.85/wmt on 21 September and 50 cents/wmt to USD 43.35/wmt on 22 September as owners pushed offers higher on thin South Atlantic cargo volumes, one fixture being reported at mid-USD 43/wmt for mid-October onward laycan.

Confidence then eroded, the route shedding 45 cents/wmt, 40 cents/wmt and 30 cents/wmt to close at USD 42.20/wmt, down 55 cents/wmt on the week, amid a prolonged dearth of fresh orders and an absence of firm bids.

Transatlantic enquiry remained ample, while a Newcastlemax was fixed Sept-Îles–Qingdao at around USD 47.00/wmt for 19–26 October laycan.

Saldanha Bay–Qingdao rose to USD 32.00/wmt by 22 September before easing over the remaining sessions to close at USD 31.25/wmt, down 35 cents/wmt.

# PANAMAX MARKET

## ATLANTIC BASIN

Atlantic sentiment strengthened further, with ECSA fronthaul continuing to attract firm demand and North Atlantic business achieving increasingly strong levels.

Modern Kamsarmaxes were particularly well positioned, grain requirements supporting both South Atlantic fronthaul and transatlantic employment, whilst South Africa/India remained another attractive alternative.

In ECSA, an 82,000 dwt 2017-built from Haldia fixed Singapore/Japan grains at around USD 25,000–25,500/day, whilst an 82,000 dwt 2021-built achieved USD 23,000/day plus USD 1.3m ballast bonus for similar employment.

An 82,000 dwt 2020-built from the Sunda Strait was also reported at USD 22,500/day, whilst older tonnage remained closer to the USD 20,000 mark.

North Atlantic rates were notably stronger: an 82,000 dwt 2025-built from ECSA fixed redelivery Skaw-Gibraltar at USD 35,500/day, matching another ECSA-positioned 79,000 dwt 2010-built at the same level for Skaw-Passero.

## BLACK SEA / MEDITERRANEAN

Last week almost the whole Black Sea was added to the High Risk Areas, so P&I began charging Owners extra premiums for calling Constanza, Varna and Bourgas too, whence most grain has lately moved; this pushed area rates up and led some Owners to stop calling Black Sea ports.

Despite the notable number of cargoes, Owners are waiting until the last minute to fix, to benefit from the improving market.

## DEMOLITION SALES

The status quo continues across the Indian Sub-Continent demolition markets which remains starved of fresh tonnage. Despite strong improvements in scrap prices post monsoon season - charter markets across all sector continue to keep Owners happy and looking anywhere but the sub continent as their next destination. More locally, the Pakistan market once again keeps the lead in pricing, although has cooled a touch since the highs of August. The Bangladesh market has steadily rebuilt appetite and is likely in a position to compete with its neighbors in Gadani especially on tonnage coming from the East. While India remains well behind on conventional units the local buyers continues to absorb specialist Vessels such as reefers, gas carriers, green ships and non-ferrous-rich tonnage. It is also worth to note, the industry continues to make progress in satisfying European officials as support continues towards the proposed addition of two Indian recycling facilities to the European List, although final approval remains pending.
