# COMMENT MARKET REPORT – WEEK 26/2026

# CHINA'S COAL IMPORTS

In Jan-May 2026, global seaborne coal loadings increased by +2.3% y-o-y to 527.6 mln t (excluding cabotage), based on vessel tracking data from AXS Marine. In Jan-May 2026, exports from Indonesia declined by -2.6% y-o-y to 185.9 mln tonnes, whilst from Australia were up by +8.3% y-o-y to 138.5 mln t. From Russia exports increased by +4.2% y-o-y to 72.4 mln t in Jan-May 2026, from the USA by +8.4% y-o-y to 35.9 mln t, from South Africa +6.1% y-o-y to 29.6 mln t. Shipments from Colombia were up by +11.3% y-o-y to 20.8 mln t in Jan-May 2026, from Canada down by -6.1% y-o-y to 18.6 mln t, and from Mozambique down by -8.9% y-o-y to 7.4 mln t. Seaborne coal imports into Mainland China declined by -14.0% y-o-y to 126.2 mln t in Jan-May 2026. Imports to India declined by -6.7% y-o-y to 96.5 mln t, to Japan increased by +5.0% y-o-y to 64.8 mln t in Jan-May 2026, to South Korea up by +22.0% y-o-y to 46.5 mln t.

To the EU imports were broadly flat at -0.5% y-o-y to 26.2 mln t in Jan-May 2026, whilst to Vietnam increased +4.4% y-o-y to 30.8 mln t. Imports to Malaysia declined by -3.9% y-o-y to 15.4 mln t, and to Bangladesh increased by +5.0% y-o-y to 7.5 mln t. **Mainland China** is currently the world's largest seaborne importer of coal (both thermal and coking), accounting for 24.0% of the global seaborne market in Jan-May 2026. It is ahead of India, which accounts for 18.4% of coal trade and Japan with a 12.3% market share. Total seaborne coal imports into China in the 12 months of 2025 reached 379.7 mln tonnes, according to AXS vessel tracking. This was down -12.7% y-o-y from the 435.1 mln t of 2024, which in turn was up +14.5% from the 379.9 mln t in 2023. In Jan–May 2026, imports into China declined by -14.0% y-o-y to 126.2 mln t, compared to 146.7 mln t in Jan-May 2025.

In Jan-May 2026, most coal volumes into China were loaded on Panamax vessels (59.4%), with 20.0% on Supramax and Ultramax vessels, 7.4% on Post-Panamaxes and 10.1% on Capesize tonnage. Top discharge ports for coal imports into China in Jan-May 2026 were Machong (9.3 mln tonnes of coal), Qinzhou (7.4 mln t), Fangcheng (7.0 mln t), Gaolan (5.9 mln t), Meizhou (4.8 mln t), Guangzhou (4.5 mln t), Haimen (4.3 mln t), Shanghai (4.1 mln t), Caofeidian (3.8 mln t), Yuhuan (3.3 mln t), Chaozhou (3.1 mln t), Tangshan (3.1 mln t). Indonesia is still by far the top supplier of coal to China accounting for 56.2% of China's imports in Jan-May 2026. Arrivals from Indonesia to China declined by -4.2% y-o-y to 70.9 mln tonnes in Jan-May 2026 compared to 74.0 mln t in Jan-May 2025. Australia is the second largest supplier of coal into China with a share of 19.8% in Jan-May 2026. In Jan-May 2026, China imported 25.0 mln t of coal from Australia, down -17.1% y-o-y from 30.2 mln t in Jan-May 2025.

The third largest supplier of coal to China is Russia, accounting for a 17.7% share of Chinese imports in Jan-May 2026. Coal shipments from Russia to China declined by -18.3% y-o-y to 22.3 mln t in Jan–May 2026, from 27.3 mln t in Jan-May 2025. Coal volumes from Canada to China declined by -38.1% y-o-y in Jan-May 2026 to 3.2 mln t. From the Philippines, volumes declined by -36.9% y-o-y in Jan-May 2026 to 2.4 mln t. From the USA they declined by -70.4% y-o-y to 0.7 mln tonnes in Jan-May 2026.

## **Asia's thermal coal imports jump on China, Japan, S.Korea buying**

Asia's imports of seaborne thermal coal are rebounding as China boosts purchases to compensate for soft domestic output and Japan and South Korea seek energy security amid the fallout from the U.S.-Israeli war on Iran. The top-energy-consuming region's imports are on track to reach 77.37 million metric tons in June, the most in six months and up from May's 68.39 million, according to data compiled by commodity analysts Kpler.

## **China's coal power on the rise again in 2026, reversing first-in-a-decade decline**

China's coal-fired power generation is set to rebound this year from its first fall in a decade, analysts said, due to the impact of El Nino and the Iran war and as renewable sources of energy have failed to keep pace with demand. The world's biggest power consumer increased its usage of thermal power by 3.4% year-on-year in the first five months of the year to 2.53 trillion kilowatt-hours (kWh), statistics bureau data showed last week.

## **India will monitor Chinese steel imports before deciding on further curbs, source says**

India will monitor steel imports for at least two more months before considering whether further measures are needed to curb the flow of shipments primarily from China, a source with direct knowledge of the matter told Reuters. In December, New Delhi had imposed a three-year import tariff on select products to curb cheap shipments from China.

## **India seeks EU scrap export relief as curbs threaten trade pact gains**

India has asked the European Union for relief from planned curbs on metal scrap exports as steel and aluminium makers warn the move could tighten supplies, raise costs and undermine gains from a trade pact, industry and government officials said. From May 2027, the EU's revised waste shipment regulation is set to bar exports of non-hazardous waste to countries outside the OECD group of mostly developed economies, unless the EU approves them by November 2026.

## **UK raises planned steel import limits to soothe businesses' cost concerns**

Britain on Thursday said it would allow more tariff-free imports of steel than previously planned when it introduces new measures to protect its domestic industry next month, after businesses said initial government proposals were too restrictive. Steelmakers have struggled to survive in the birthplace of the Industrial Revolution after decades of decline driven by deindustrialisation and, more recently, high energy costs and a glut of cheap steel from countries like China.

## **Chinese copper smelters baulk at spot-indexed ore pricing proposal**

China's copper smelters are pushing back against a proposal from miner Antofagasta to link prices of raw material concentrate under term contracts to the spot market fearing the move will hit their already weak finances, industry sources say. Under their annual supply contracts, miners traditionally pay smelters fixed treatment and refining charges (TC/RCs) to process copper concentrate into refined metal. The charges, which reflect supply and demand in the copper market, make up a sizeable portion of the smelters' revenue.

## **Argus lifts Russian wheat crop forecast to highest since record 2022/23**

Argus raised its forecast for Russian wheat production in 2026/27 to 91.2 million metric tons, the highest since the record 2022/23 harvest, after a virtual crop tour showed stronger winter wheat prospects. The 2026/27 Argus forecast has a range of 88.5 million to 93.9 million tons, the commodity data firm said. That compares with an April estimate of 88.7 million tons, a 2025/26 harvest of 90.4 million tons and a record 96 million tons in 2022/23.

## **IGC raises 2026/27 world corn crop forecast**

The International Grains Council on Thursday raised its forecast for 2026/27 global corn (maize) production, mainly reflecting improved prospects in Argentina and India. The intergovernmental body, in a monthly update, lifted its 2026/27 world corn crop forecast by 10 million metric tons to 1.310 billion tons, though this would still trail the prior season's 1.339 billion.

## **France fears 30% plunge in maize harvest as crops wilt in heatwave**

Maize production in France could plunge by around 30% this year as potential damage from a record heatwave comes on top of a sharp decline in planting, the agriculture ministry said on Thursday. Maize (corn) is one of the most exposed crops to the extreme heat in France, the European Union's largest grain producer, and prices on Euronext have surged 9% since the start of last week. ## **Middle East producers push on with oil and LNG loadings despite ship attacks**

Middle East producers are pushing ahead with oil and liquefied natural gas loading despite fresh ship attacks in the Strait of Hormuz and renewed strikes between the U.S. and Iran in recent days, shipping data showed. Energy shipping in the strait slowed after attacks on a container ship on Thursday and an oil tanker on Saturday sparked tit-for-tat strikes, threatening Washington and Iran's interim peace deal.

## **Crude shipments through Hormuz at highest since Iran war began**

**Crude shipments through Hormuz** rose this week to their highest level since the U.S.-Israeli conflict with Iran began in February after a ceasefire deal reopened the waterway, data showed on Thursday. Concerns about how long the strait would stay open also boosted trade.

## **Iraq's push for bigger OPEC quota driven by revenue pressures, new oil investment**

An economic crisis caused by the Iran war and a fresh surge of investment by oil majors are driving Iraq's aggressive push for a higher OPEC production quota, potentially placing it on a collision course with the producer bloc. The pressure from Baghdad adds to the challenges confronting the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries, still reeling from the conflict's fallout and the shock departure of the United Arab Emirates after nearly 60 years as a member.

## **Saudi Aramco resumes oil loading at Ras Tanura after 4-month halt**

Saudi Aramco resumed oil loading on Friday at its Ras Tanura terminal in the Gulf after a near four-month halt, shipping data from LSEG showed, in a sign that Middle Eastern producers are pushing forward with plans to boost exports despite a ship attack in the Strait of Hormuz. Middle Eastern producers had been ramping up oil and gas output and exports in the lead-up to the interim deal between the United States and Iran to halt the war and reopen the strait where a fifth of the world's oil and liquefied natural gas supplies used to pass.

## **Global physical crude markets dive as Middle East ramps up supply**

Physical crude oil cargoes are selling at steep discounts around the world, changing trade flows as markets come under pressure from fast-rising Middle Eastern supply with Iran set to boost sales following a temporary reprieve from U.S. sanctions. The discounts, the biggest in years in some cases, follow the signing of a 60-day interim deal between the U.S. and Iran aimed at ending the war that began on February 28.

## **Oil markets start to signal near-term oversupply as tankers exit Strait of Hormuz**

Prices of Brent crude oil for second-month delivery traded higher on Wednesday than prices for prompt delivery for the first time since the Iran war started in late February, signaling increased near-term supply. Brent, the global benchmark, traded 12 cents higher for contracts delivering in September versus those with August delivery, implying the market is pricing in ample prompt supply but fewer barrels in the coming months.

## **Russia eyes diesel export ban, fuel imports amid Ukrainian strikes; Crimea restricts public life**

Russia is considering a diesel export ban, Deputy Prime Minister Alexander Novak said on Tuesday, while a newspaper reported on possible fuel imports to tackle shortages, especially in Crimea, which tightened restrictions on public services and activities. Russian President Vladimir Putin also made his first comments about Ukraine's recent strikes on civilian infrastructure, including on Moscow's oil refinery, saying they were a ploy to destabilise society.

## **Saudi Arabia may cut August oil prices sharply for Asia as supply improves**

Saudi Arabia is likely to sharply cut its official selling prices (OSPs) for crude oil to Asia in August to a four-month low, a Reuters survey showed, after spot crude markets tumbled on rising Middle Eastern supplies. The August OSP for flagship Arab Light crude may slide to a premium of $1.5 to $3 a barrel above the average Dubai and Oman quotes, four industry sources said in the survey, $6.5 to $8 a barrel lower than the July OSP.

# CAPESIZE MARKET

## ATLANTIC AND PACIFIC BASIN

The week opened on a mixed note on Monday 22 June, with residual positive sentiment from the previous Friday fizzling out during Asian trading hours as offer levels in the Pacific softened and concerns over bunker price volatility dampened market direction. Iron ore and coal inquiries from Western Australian miners were sparse, whilst exchanges out of the South Atlantic — despite a reasonable availability of cargoes — remained subdued owing to thin bid and offer refreshment. The Western Australia–Qingdao route was assessed at USD 11.80/wmt, Tubarao–Qingdao at USD 31.95/wmt, and Saldanha Bay– Qingdao at USD 22.95/wmt, with the Cape T4 Index closing the session at USD 30,036/day. Through Tuesday and Wednesday (23–24 June), freight rates edged further lower. Pacific tonnage demand remained thin amid a lack of fresh iron ore orders, with sentiment subdued despite a modest uptick in coal cargo availability. A developing typhoon Mekkhala sweeping across the East China Sea had limited impact on vessel itineraries. On the South Atlantic, cargo volumes initially firmed, with H1 July laycan charterers exhibiting greater urgency to fix at prevailing levels before activity eased as requirements were cleared.

Ballaster supply continued to build for H2 July dates, suggesting a backwardated structure across the July loading window. The market deteriorated sharply on Thursday and Friday (25–26 June), with FFA losses dragging physical assessments significantly lower. Prompt tonnage demand was lackluster across both basins, bids and offer levels were not refreshed at prevailing rates, and concluded fixtures reflected materially weaker sentiment. By the week's close on 26 June, the Western Australia–Qingdao route had fallen to USD 10.20/wmt, Tubarao–Qingdao to USD 27.75/wmt, and Saldanha Bay–Qingdao to USD 20.45/wmt. The Cape T4 Index closed at USD 22,938/day — a weekly decline of approximately 7,100 points, or roughly 24%.

# PANAMAX MARKET

## ATLANTIC BASIN

Atlantic activity remained relatively steady throughout the week, with East Coast South America continuing to generate the bulk of the reported business. Freight levels were broadly unchanged from the previous week, although prompt Atlantic positions and transatlantic employment continued to command healthy premiums. Early in the week, an 81,000-dwt Kamsarmax (built 2012) was fixed basis aps East Coast South America for a voyage to Singapore/Japan at USD 20,500 per day plus USD 1.05 million ballast bonus. A modern 82,000-dwt Kamsarmax (built 2023) opening Tuticorin secured USD 24,500 per day for an East Coast South America to Far East trip, while an 82,000-dwt vessel (built 2022) opening Dangjin was reported fixed for the same route at USD 21,250 per day. A Panamax (built 2012) opening Ennore was also committed for East Coast South America/Far East employment, although the rate remained undisclosed. Transatlantic demand continued to underpin the Atlantic market. An 82,000-dwt Kamsarmax (built 2019) was reportedly fixed basis aps Santos for an East Coast South America transatlantic voyage at USD 32,000 per day, while a modern 82,000-dwt vessel (built 2023) opening Pecem achieved USD 31,000 per day for a North Coast South America transatlantic grains trip.

Additional North Coast South America business also emerged, with the Lacerta reportedly fixed for a grains voyage from North Coast South America to Ireland. Towards the end of the week, fresh enquiry for July arrivals into East Coast South America remained evident. An 82,000-dwt Kamsarmax (built 2012), sailing from Mumbai, fixed at USD 20,000 per day for an East Coast South America fronthaul voyage, while a 75,000-dwt Panamax (built 2013) opening Tuticorin reportedly secured around USD 20,400 per day for similar employment. Another 77,000-dwt Panamax (built 2012) ballasting from Navlakhi was fixed at USD 19,000 per day, and a modern 82,000-dwt Kamsarmax (built 2017) was reported fixed at USD 19,500 per day while positioning towards East Coast South America.

## PACIFIC BASIN

The Pacific market remained generally balanced this week, with a steady flow of coal and grain cargoes providing support across the basin. Whilst activity was sufficient to keep prompt tonnage moving, rate levels showed little overall improvement, with sentiment remaining mixed depending on loading area and destination. In Indonesia, coal demand remained relatively stable, although earnings continued to vary across the main discharge regions. A 2012-built 75,423 dwt vessel fixed at USD 16,500 for an Indonesia/South China coal voyage, while a 2009-built 76,619 dwt vessel secured USD 13,750 for Indonesia/India. Elsewhere, a 2006-built 73,600 dwt vessel reportedly fixed around USD 14,000 for an Indonesia/South Korea trip. Overall, rates remained broadly unchanged, with limited upward momentum. In the NOPAC region, grain cargoes continued to underpin activity despite a relatively quiet flow of fresh enquiries. A 2016-built 81,895 dwt vessel fixed at USD 20,000 for a NOPAC/Singapore-Japan grain voyage, while a 2013-built 81,896 dwt scrubber-fitted vessel secured USD 14,000 for a NOPAC round voyage. Overall, sentiment remained steady.

On the Australian market, business continued to provide the strongest support within the Pacific basin, driven by coal and iron ore exports into North Asia. A 2021-built 87,296 dwt scrubber-fitted vessel fixed at USD 19,000 for an East Coast Australia/Japan voyage, while a 2019-built 81,783 dwt vessel secured USD 17,250 for an Australia/Singapore-Japan coal trip. Elsewhere, a 2012-built 95,666 dwt vessel fixed at USD 15,500 for a West Coast Australia/China iron ore voyage.

# SUPRAMAX & HANDYSIZE MARKET

## **US GULF / NORTH AMERICA**

The US Gulf market had a fairly steady week on Supramax and Ultramax tonnage, with rates slowing slightly. On the transatlantic side, a 63,000-dwt vessel open East Coast Mexico fixed a trip to West Mediterranean in the very low 30s, basis aps. Similar levels were recorded from the US East Coast, where an Ultramax fixed a trip to the Continent with wood pellets at USD 32,500 per day. On Supramaxes, a 58,000-dwt vessel was fixed, then failed, at USD 28,500 per day for grains to Spain, while the petcoke route to India was concluded in the mid-30s on nice Ultramax vessels. On Handies, transatlantic business to the Continent was fixed in the low 20s on standard Handysize vessels.

## **EAST COAST SOUTH AMERICA**

The Baltic indices show continued upward movement, though at a slower pace than last week. For Supramax, the West Africa trip via ECSA to Skaw-Passero climbed from USD21,482 on Monday to USD21,814 by Friday, whilst the trip to North China rose from USD27,207 to USD27,343. Handies saw an even firmer push, with the Rio de Janeiro-Recalada to Skaw-Passero route jumping from USD24,375 to USD25,106. Fixture activity confirms the growth, with Handies easily securing USD24,000–26,000 for transatlantic trips out of ECSA; fixtures were also reported at USD12,750 for Casablanca/Continent and USD10,000 for Jorf Lasfar/ECSA. Front-haul and positioning rates are holding up well too, with a 61,000-dwt vessel achieving USD26,750 for a trip via ECSA to the Mediterranean. The market remains tight and firmly in owners' favour.

## **NORTH EUROPE / CONTINENT**

The market remained stable again and in line with the previous week around the Continent/Baltic area, with a highly positional market continuing to affect the basin. On Handies, trips to premium destinations such as the Mediterranean and West Africa held in the mid-teens (USD 13,500/14,500 depending on specifications). Repositioning routes kept discounting, with Continent/East Coast South America estimated in the USD 8,000/9,000 range, and trips to the US Gulf/US East Coast hovering between USD 9,000 and the very low teens. Front haul on Handies was estimated in the mid-high teens. On larger units, the market remained firm despite a quiet week for fresh orders. Front haul was estimated in the mid-20s depending on specifications (Ultramaxes asking a touch more), with owners now reconsidering premium levels due to a slowdown in the Pacific basin. Scrap runs to the East Mediterranean were still paying some premium, with Supramaxes/Ultramaxes holding in the mid-20s basis ARAG/Skaw, while transatlantic trips to the US Gulf were discounting in the low-mid teens (USD 12,000/14,000) for repositioning there.

In this sense, a 63,000-dwt vessel fixed a scrap run to the East Mediterranean at USD 23,000.
