# **CHINA BAUXITE IMPORTS**

Bauxite is one of the most important dry bulk commodities, accounting for 4.4 percent of all seaborne dry bulk trade in 2025, rising to 4.9 percent so far in 2026.

Total seaborne exports of bauxite increased in Jan-Dec 2025 by +20.3% year-on-year to a record 246.4 mln tonnes, according to vessel tracking data from AXS Marine.

This followed on the +13.6% y-o-y growth recorded in 2024, and the +5.2% y-o-y growth in 2023.

In Jan-Oct 2026, global loadings increased by a further +11.7% y-o-y to 226.2 mln tonnes, from 202.6 mln t in Jan-Oct 2025.

The largest exporter of bauxite by far is Guinea, which accounted for 72.5% of global bauxite exports in Jan-Dec 2025, rising to 78.2% in Jan-Oct 2026.

In Jan-Oct 2026, shipments from Guinea increased by +21.9% y-o-y to 176.9 mln tonnes, from 145.2 mln t in Jan-Oct 2025.

Guinea’s exports in the first ten months of 2026 alone already exceeded the 102.8 mln tonnes it exported in the whole of 2022.

In second place is Australia, which accounted for 14.9% of global bauxite exports in Jan-Oct 2026.

In Jan-Oct 2026, bauxite exports from Australia declined by -11.9% y-o-y to 33.7 mln tonnes.

This followed a +2.4% y-o-y increase in Australian exports in 2025, and a +12.8% y-o-y jump in 2024.

Far in third place is Brazil, with just a 2.1% share in Jan-Oct 2026.

Exports from Brazil declined by -7.9% y-o-y in Jan-Oct 2026 to 4.8 mln t, after surging +27.2% y-o-y in 2025.

A recent development is Guyana, whose bauxite exports surged by +280% y-o-y in 2025 to 2.9 mln t, although volumes have since flattened, down -3.3% y-o-y to 2.4 mln t in Jan-Oct 2026.

Indonesian exports are still down at zero, due to their policies in recent years to ban exports of unrefined ores and push for investment in smelters in the country.

Let’s remember that as recently as in 2022 Indonesia exported 18.2 mln tonnes of bauxite, amounting to 11% of global bauxite exports.

In terms of imports, by far the top destination for bauxite so far in 2026 is Mainland China, accounting for 88.6% of global bauxite imports.

Imports to China increased by +13.2% y-o-y in Jan-Oct 2026 to 201.8 mln tonnes, from 178.3 mln t in Jan-Oct 2025.

This followed a +23.9% y-o-y increase in Jan-Dec 2025 to 213.1 mln tonnes, which was over 50 percent above the 139.2 mln t imported by China in Jan-Dec 2022.

Outside China, the largest importer was the European Union, accounting for 4.0% of global bauxite imports in Jan-Oct 2026.

Imports to the EU increased by +3.3% y-o-y in Jan-Oct 2026 to 9.1 mln t, continuing the recovery from the recent trough of 7.6 mln t imported by the EU in full-year 2023.

In third place was India, accounting for a 3.2% share and 7.4 mln t in Jan-Oct 2026, a surge of +82.3% y-o-y.

The United Arab Emirates were in fourth place with 2.2 mln tonnes in Jan-Oct 2026, down -51.0% y-o-y, a 0.9% share of global imports.

Let’s now focus specifically on China, which as we mentioned above is the world’s largest importer of bauxite with an 88.6% share.

Top bauxite discharging ports in China in Jan-Oct 2026 were:

Yantai New West Port (34.3 mln t in Jan-Oct 2026), Fangcheng (29.1 mln t), Yantai (16.9 mln t), Huanghua (16.7 mln t), Caofeidian (14.7 mln t), Longkou (14.6 mln t), Qingdao (13.7 mln t), Rizhao (13.6 mln t), Tianjin (8.2 mln t).

77.3% of bauxite imported into China in Jan-Oct 2026 was loaded on Capesizes, 5.2% on VLOCs, 11.7% on Post-Panamaxes or Baby-Capes, 3.4% on Panamaxes/Kamsarmaxes, 2.4% on Supramaxes.

Obviously, the main source of bauxite for China is Guinea, accounting for 80.7% of China’s bauxite imports in Jan-Oct 2026.

Shipments from Guinea to China increased by +21.9% y-o-y in Jan-Oct 2026 to 162.9 mln tonnes.

This follows a +32.8% y-o-y increase in 2025, a +13.6% y-o-y increase in 2024, and +35.4% y-o-y in 2023.

In second place as a source was Australia, which accounted for 16.1% of China’s imports in Jan-Oct 2026.

In Jan-Oct 2026, shipments to China from Australia declined by -3.1% y-o-y to 32.4 mln tonnes.

A new entry since 2025 is Guyana, which shipped 2.6 mln tonnes to China in 2025 and 1.9 mln t in Jan-Oct 2026.

# COMMODITY NEWS – DRY BULK

**ArcelorMittal beats profit expectations as European market strengthens**

ArcelorMittal, the world's second-largest steelmaker, reported quarterly earnings above market expectations on Thursday, partially thanks to higher steel shipments in Europe. The Luxembourg-based company reported earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation of $2.02 billion for the third quarter, beating analysts' average forecast of $1.96 billion in an LSEG poll.

**US September copper imports fall 3.1% from August**

US copper imports in September dipped 3.1% from August but were still almost double levels of a year earlier, official data showed on Wednesday, as metal continued to flow to the country ahead of possible tariffs. The US imported 194,284 metric tons of refined copper and copper alloys in September, down from 200,519 tons in August and a record 225,094 tons in July. Imports were 102,279 tons in September 2025.

**Falling sulphuric acid prices deepen China's copper smelter squeeze**

Chinese copper smelters are facing mounting pressure as falling sulphuric acid prices erode a key source of profits that had helped offset losses from processing copper concentrate at negative fees, industry sources said. The double blow for China's smelters, which produce more than half of the world's refined copper, has prompted some operators to extend maintenance outages this quarter.

**India approves $297 million incentive policy for lithium, nickel processing**

India's cabinet approved on Wednesday an incentive policy worth 28.14 billion rupees ($297 million) to boost domestic processing of lithium and nickel, the government said in a statement, in a move aimed at reducing the country's reliance on supply chains dominated by China. India has a high import dependence on critical minerals such as lithium, and tighter conditions following export controls by China create potential supply-chain vulnerability, the government had said in March.

**China's slow buying of US farm goods tests $17 billion trade pledge**

Chinese buying of US farm goods other than soybeans is on track for a seven-year low this year despite an agreement to purchase billions of dollars in imports, a Reuters analysis of customs data showed, underscoring long-running uncertainties about the deal. In May, the White House said Beijing agreed to buy $17 billion of US agricultural goods annually from 2026, with this year's target prorated.

**USDA attache sees China 2026-27 soybean imports at 110 million metric tons**

China is projected to import 110 million metric tons (MMT) of soybeans in the 2026-27 marketing year, the US Department of Agriculture's attache in Beijing said in a report dated Wednesday. The unofficial forecast in the attache's report, issued by the USDA's Foreign Agricultural Service, is below the USDA's official projection for China to import 115 million tons of soybeans in 2026-27.

**China says US farm purchases hinge on tariff-cut deal**

China is open to buying more US farm goods, but only if the two sides implement their reciprocal tariff-cut agreement, the commerce ministry said on Thursday, signalling tariff concessions remain central to Beijing's aims. Following a September summit between Presidents Xi Jinping and Donald Trump in Washington, the two sides proposed a $60 billion package of reciprocal tariff cuts, including roughly $17 billion worth of US farm goods that China imported in 2024, though the plan has yet to be finalised.

**Brazil's soy production, exports to fall in 2026/27, says Rabobank**

Brazil's soybean crop and exports are expected to decline in 2026/27 as farmers in the world's largest producer and exporter face tighter margins amid high borrowing costs, while Chinese demand is seen remaining broadly stable, Rabobank said on Wednesday. Production is forecast at 178 million metric tons, unchanged from Rabobank's July estimate but down 4 million tons from the previous season.

**Ukraine grain transportation for exports rises 26% m/m in October, rail operator says**

Ukraine's grain transportation for exports rose by 26% month-on-month to 1.08 million metric tons in October, Ukrainian state railway operator Ukraliznytsia said on Thursday. The operator said the volume included 215,010 tons for Danube river ports and 854,330 tons headed to the western land border.

# COMMODITY NEWS – CRUDE OIL & PRODUCTS

**Freed from OPEC quotas, UAE says oil exports have risen as it hosts industry event**

Six months after leaving OPEC, the United Arab Emirates on Monday began hosting ADIPEC, one of the world's largest energy conferences, where it told an audience of industry executives it had increased oil exports in spite of the Iran war. The UAE was for 59 years a pivotal member of the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries. It said in April it would leave the group, weeks into the US-Israeli war with Iran.

**Kuwait lifts October oil output to highest since start of Iran war**

Kuwait raised its crude oil production to 2.1 million barrels per day in October, the highest level since the outbreak of the Iran war and an increase of over 300,000 bpd from September, a person with direct knowledge of the matter told Reuters. The rise came despite a record number of attacks on shipping in the Strait of Hormuz, adding to signs that Gulf oil producers are maintaining or increasing output as they adapt to the disruption risks.

**Saudi Arabia cuts December Asia oil prices to fresh 6-year low**

Saudi Aramco cut its December price for its Arab Light grade for Asian customers by $2 a barrel versus November on Friday, to a discount of $6.50 a barrel to the Oman/Dubai average, the lowest since May 2020 during the pandemic. The producer also cut December prices across all grades for Northwest Europe and the Mediterranean by $1 a barrel while keeping prices unchanged in the US, a pricing document showed.

**As high oil prices curb demand, fresh signs of glut appear**

The global oil market is showing signs of a return to excess supply as demand drops due to high prices, analysts and traders said, despite the lack of a resolution to the Iran war, which has disrupted exports from the Middle East since March. The market was widely expected at the start of 2026 to be in a surplus, but the outbreak of the Iran war in late February led to what the International Energy Agency called the world's biggest ever supply disruption and cut Middle East exports, overturning the surplus projections.

**Gulf oil flows dip in October from post-Iran war high, data shows**

Gulf oil exports dipped in October from September's levels, which were the highest since the Iran war started, data from two ship-tracking companies showed, although both said the figures could be revised upwards as more details on shipments emerge. Faced with intensifying attacks on shipping around the Strait of Hormuz, which reached a record in October, tankers have also stepped up efforts to avoid detection, making Gulf oil flows harder to track and raising the likelihood of further revisions to preliminary export data.

**Soaring freight rates give US refiners edge as crude discounts widen**

The Iran conflict has sent tanker rates soaring, forcing sellers to deepen discounts on heavy crude grades and giving US Gulf Coast refiners a cost advantage over Asian rivals, executives said after reporting bumper third-quarter profits. Security threats around the Strait of Hormuz, a key maritime chokepoint through which about one-fifth of global oil and liquefied natural gas supplies passed before the war started, have disrupted tanker traffic, while higher war-risk insurance premiums and longer voyages have driven up shipping costs.

**Middle East crude oil premiums tumble to pre-war levels amid ample supply**

Spot premiums for Middle Eastern crude benchmarks returned to levels seen before the Iran war this week as ample supplies weighed on the market, while cooling refining margins in Asia slowed buying interest. Middle Eastern producers ramped up crude exports in September and October, which J.P.Morgan analysts said has created a surplus, despite rising attacks on vessels transiting the Strait of Hormuz.

**US crude, fuel inventories surge as demand hits 1-year high, EIA says**

US crude, gasoline and distillate inventories all fell last week as demand hit its highest level in a year, the Energy Information Administration said on Wednesday. Crude inventories fell by 865,000 barrels to 409.8 million barrels in the week ended October 30, the EIA said, compared with analysts' expectations in a Reuters poll for a 877,000-barrel rise.

**Oil services firms face major cost pressure from Iran crisis, execs say**

The Iran conflict is causing the oil services industry to increase its cost assessment for projects due to rising shipment, fuel and security costs, which are affecting its Middle Eastern contract performance, executives said at a conference in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday. The Iran war, now in its ninth month, has damaged infrastructure and forced staff to evacuate in several countries. Threats to shipping via the Strait of Hormuz have made equipment deliveries difficult, increasing the cost of both labour and insurance.

# COMMODITY NEWS – NATURAL GAS & POWER

**Warm Asian winter could ease Europe's LNG squeeze**

Europe's gas prices have hit multi-year highs as traders fret over low storage levels. But unusually warm weather forecasts across Asia may offer some relief this winter, as less demand for heating in the Far East may free up LNG supplies for Europe. Forecasts point to significantly above-normal temperatures across several of East Asia's largest gas-consuming economies between December and March, due largely to a strong El Niño weather pattern that tends to result in milder and wetter conditions across much of Asia.

**Qatar's US LNG bet signals a new energy order**

Turning to your biggest rival is rarely a sign of confidence. Yet Qatar's decision to sign long-term contracts for US supplies suggests the world's second-largest LNG exporter is increasingly concerned that instability in the Gulf could undermine its ambitious expansion plans. QatarEnergy last week signed a 20-year agreement to buy 3 million metric tons per year (mtpa) of liquefied natural gas from Woodside Energy's facilities in Louisiana.

**Germany lags EU neighbours with gas storage refill as winter starts**

Germany, which holds more than a fifth of the European Union's gas storage capacity, has entered winter with its lowest gas stocks on record, far below the EU average, as the Iran conflict inflates prices and traders see little profit in stockpiling. Low stocks and a four-year high in gas prices have stoked supply fears in Europe's biggest economy and beyond, with the memory of the 2022 energy crisis, when Moscow stopped a decade-long supply relationship with the continent, still fresh.

**Goldman Sachs raises 2027 Europe gas price forecasts on MidEast supply, winter risks**

Goldman Sachs has raised its forecasts for 2027 European natural gas prices, saying the outlook remains highly uncertain due to risks surrounding Middle East liquefied natural gas supply and winter weather conditions. The bank lifted its first-quarter 2027 European natural gas price (TTF) forecast to €74 per megawatt-hour (MWh) from €44 previously, while raising its full-year 2027 forecast to €51/MWh from €32/MWh.

**EU grid investment to keep rising toward 2030, ACER says**

The European Union Agency for the Cooperation of Energy Regulators (ACER) said electricity grid investment is expected to keep rising towards 2030, while delays and congestion could push up costs for consumers. Of the 18 countries that gave estimates for the report, 15 expect electricity grid investment in 2030 to exceed 2025 levels.

**Most EU citizens back an urgent transition to greener fuel, survey shows**

Three quarters of European Union citizens say moving away from fossil fuels is urgent and nearly nine in ten want politicians to prioritise renewable energy sources, a survey of 23,000 people by the European Investment Bank showed on Thursday. The findings, shared exclusively with Reuters, found popular support for rapid action ahead of the COP31 climate talks next week, even as governments are under pressure to deal with wars, popular protests and strained finances.

**Iraq's Khor Mor gas field suspends output after drone attack, operator says**

Production was halted on Sunday at Iraq's Khor Mor gas field, one of the largest in the Kurdistan region, following a "suspected" drone attack, joint field operator Dana Gas said in a statement. The company based in the United Arab Emirates said in Monday's statement it was assessing the damage after dousing a fire sparked by the attack, with no injuries to staff.

**Emerging economies set to match advanced nations' renewable capacity additions, IEA says**

Emerging and developing economies outside China are expected to add as much new renewable energy capacity as advanced economies for the first time over the next five years, driven by the current energy crisis, the International Energy Agency said on Friday. Gas and oil prices have soared this year as the US-Iran war disrupts traffic through the Strait of Hormuz, a key route for global oil and gas trade.

**China's Q3 emissions post biggest drop since pandemic as Hormuz crisis curbs fossil fuel use**

China's carbon dioxide emissions from energy and industry fell 3.1% year-on-year in the third quarter of 2026, the biggest quarterly drop since the pandemic, as the world's biggest emitter used less fossil fuel, according to an analysis on Friday by the Helsinki-based Centre for Research on Energy and Clean Air (CREA). China cut its oil use by 4% during the quarter as the crisis in the Strait of Hormuz curbed energy shipments, said the CREA analysis, which was carried out for UK-based Carbon Brief.

# CAPESIZE MARKET

## ATLANTIC AND PACIFIC BASIN

The Capesize market lost ground in the Pacific while the Atlantic held broadly flat, a sharp three-session correction in bunker prices leaving participants cautious.

The 2 November assessments are not among the available files; the levels implied by the 3 November movements — USD 13.80/wmt, USD 36.65/wmt and USD 27.50/wmt respectively — are used here.

The Western Australia–Qingdao route (170,000 mt iron ore, plus/minus 10%) fell 55 cents/wmt to USD 13.25/wmt on 3 November, ample Pacific tonnage outweighing demand after owners had proved reluctant to ballast at elevated bunker prices; a mining major fixed several Capesizes from Dampier to Qingdao at about USD 13.25/wmt for 18–20 November laycan.

Confidence turned late on 4 November as derivative values rose and owners lifted offers, taking the route 35 cents/wmt higher to USD 13.60/wmt, with Port Hedland–Qingdao heard at mid-high USD 13/wmt.

It gave back 30 cents/wmt to USD 13.30/wmt on 5 November as plunging bunker prices kept charterers cautious — several Dampier–Qingdao ships fixed at around USD 13.30/wmt for 22–24 November — then added 15 cents/wmt to close at USD 13.45/wmt on 6 November on thin exchanges, participants having covered ahead of a Singapore public holiday.

The route finished USD 1.15/wmt below its 30 October level.

Tubarao–Qingdao eased 20 cents/wmt to USD 36.45/wmt on 3 November, recovered 5 cents/wmt and then held at USD 36.50/wmt through the remaining sessions, down 30 cents/wmt from 30 October.

South Atlantic activity nonetheless heated up sharply overnight on 4 November, with a flurry of Brazilian fixtures: Tubarao–Qingdao at USD 36.00/wmt for 8 December onward, twice at USD 37.00/wmt for 1–6 December and at USD 36.75/wmt for 27 November onward.

North Atlantic fronthaul and transatlantic rates stayed supported, a Capesize fixing Ponta da Madeira–Rotterdam at around USD 16.75/wmt for 1–10 December laycan.

Saldanha Bay–Qingdao slipped to USD 27.10/wmt by 4 November before gaining 65 cents/wmt to USD 27.75/wmt, where it closed, unchanged from 30 October.

# **PANAMAX MARKET**

## ATLANTIC BASIN

The Atlantic market is showing a more balanced picture, with North American coal and grain routes continuing to attract interest, whilst ECSA remains active but with a noticeable spread in achieved rates. Owners are still finding opportunities for premium European redelivery, although the latest fixtures suggest that rates remain highly dependent on vessel position and specific trading patterns.

ECSA activity included an 82,000 dwt vessel fixed for grains with redelivery Skaw-Gibraltar at USD 29,400/day APS, one of the stronger reported levels.

Another 82,000 dwt unit achieved USD 20,500/day for grains to Singapore-Japan, whilst a further fixture to the Far East was reported at USD 19,500/day.

The difference highlights the premium attached to European redelivery compared with conventional fronthaul employment. On the North Atlantic side, an 82,000 dwt vessel fixed USEC/South Korea coal at USD 26,250/day, whilst USEC/India business was reported at USD 25,000/day.

US Gulf/ Singapore- Japan employment was also reported at USD 24,000/day, whilst a Newport News/Gangavaram coal cargo was quoted at USD 47.45/tonne fio.

## PACIFIC BASIN

The Pacific remained fairly active through the week, although the overall picture still showed some pressure on rates, particularly in Indonesia.

Australia continued to attract a reasonable number of fixtures, whilst NOPAC grain business remained relatively stable.

Indonesian activity was mainly on South China and regional employment, rates ranging from USD 14,000 to USD 21,800.

Older and smaller units fixed at the lower end, with better levels for South Korea and Singapore-Japan: an 81,500 dwt fixed USD 21,800 for a South Korea coal run and a 75,700 dwt achieved USD 20,500 for China.

NOPAC grain activity remained relatively steady, rates mostly around USD 19,250–21,250 for trips to Singapore-Japan: an 82,000 dwt fixed USD 21,250 for a Singapore-Japan grain trip, a 75,000 dwt achieved USD 19,500 for Japan and another 81,000 dwt fixed USD 19,250 for a Singapore-Japan run, whilst on coal an 85,000 dwt achieved USD 24,000 for a Singapore-Japan trip, indicating better levels.

Australia remained one of the more active Pacific areas, with a good number of fixtures from both coasts. East Coast business was generally concluded between USD 17,500 and USD 24,750: an 84,500 dwt fixed USD 23,000 for a South China coal run and an 82,500 dwt achieved USD 24,750 on the same route, whilst a further 82,000 dwt fixed USD 26,000 for an Australian coal trip, with the scrubber benefit going to Charterers. ## NORTH EUROPE / CONTINENT

The Continent showed mixed dynamics this week: Supra/Ultra activity reduced noticeably against the previous strong weeks, even if scrap rate levels remained elevated, whilst Handysize gained extra support from fresh enquiry and tightening prompt tonnage supply.

On Handysize, sentiment stayed very healthy on both regional and transatlantic routes: outbound grain saw a 39k (2015) open Dublin fix DOP via Liepaja to Algeria USD 23,000/day, whilst on repositioning and southbound legs a 32k (2009) open Casablanca secured USD 15,000/day DOP with fertilisers to Brazil.

# **DEMOLITION SALES**

Ship recycling prices held steady across all four main destinations during the week, despite a less supportive backdrop.

Bangladesh remained the strongest market, with indications of around USD 515 per LDT for bulkers, USD 535 per LDT for tankers and USD 545 per LDT for containerships.

Pakistan followed at USD 505–535 per LDT, India at USD 470–500 per LDT and Turkey at USD 290–320 per LDT.

Local steel prices softened.

Alang plate fell to INR 40,700 per tonne and Gadani plate eased to PKR 183,000 per tonne, while Chattogram was unchanged at BDT 66,000 per tonne, although a firmer US Dollar put pressure on the Taka.

Meanwhile, the Baltic Dry Index recovered to 2,740 points on 5 November, giving owners of older tonnage reasons to keep trading.

On 4 November, the European Parliament’s ENVI Committee voted to object to adding Indian yards to the EU approved list, although a plenary vote and Member State approval are still pending.

No fresh sales were reported. The 44,411 LDT tanker ERA arrived at Chattogram, while previously sold units were delivered at Alang and Gadani.

Buying appetite remains healthy, particularly in Bangladesh, but the shortage of willing sellers continues to hold back activity.
