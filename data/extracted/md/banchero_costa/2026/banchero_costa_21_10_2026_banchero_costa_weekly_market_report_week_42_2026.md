# Banchero Costa Weekly Market Report - Week 42, 2026

## Weekly Macro Insight: Brazil Crude Oil Exports

After a slight decline in 2024, when global crude oil loadings decreased by -0.2% y-o-y, things picked up in 2025, with full-year volumes increasing by +1.8% y-o-y.

In Jan-Sep 2026, global crude oil loadings decreased by -6.1% y-o-y to 1,547.6 mln tonnes, excluding all cabotage trade, according to vessels tracking data from LSEG.

Exports from the Arabian Gulf were down by -29.1% y-o-y to 464.5 mln t in Jan-Sep 2026, accounting for 30.0% of global seaborne crude trade, down from 39.8% in the same period of last year, reflecting the disruption from the ongoing war in the Persian Gulf.

Exports from Russian ports (including oil of Kazakh origin shipped via the CPC terminal) increased by +8.3% y-o-y to 187.1 mln t in Jan-Sep 2026, or 12.1% of global trade.

From South America, exports surged by +33.1% y-o-y to 212.3 mln t. From the USA, exports went up by +10.5% y-o-y to 150.5 mln tonnes in Jan-Sep 2026.

From West Africa, exports decreased by -10.2% y-o-y to 118.7 mln t. From ASEAN exports rose by +8.3% y-o-y to 86.2 mln t in Jan-Sep 2026 (this inevitably includes trans-shipped cargoes).

In terms of demand (measured by arrival at discharge ports), global seaborne crude imports totalled 1,549.2 mln t in Jan-Sep 2026, down -5.4% y-o-y.

The top single-country seaborne importer of crude oil in Jan-Sep 2026 was Mainland China, accounting for

18.8% of global trade, although the EU27 as a bloc was a larger buyer. Volumes into China declined by -20.7% y-o-y to 291.8 mln t in Jan-Sep 2026, from 367.7 mln t in Jan-Sep 2025.

Imports into the EU27 increased by +1.3% y-o-y to 350.9 mln t, accounting for 22.6% of global trade. To ASEAN, imports decreased by -8.0% y-o-y to 198.7 mln t. To South Korea, imports declined by -10.7% y-o-y to 92.0 mln t.

Imports into the USA increased by +4.0% y-o-y to 96.8 mln t in Jan-Sep 2026. To Japan, imports declined by -13.5% y-o-y to 71.1 mln t.

Brazil is fast emerging as a significant exporter of crude oil, with volumes increasing rapidly in recent years.

In Jan-Dec 2023, volumes surged by +27.2% y-o-y to 88.2 mln tonnes. In Jan-Dec 2024, exports increased by a further +4.3% y-o-y to 92.0 mln t. In Jan-Dec 2025, exports rose by a further +18.7% y-o-y to 109.2 mln t.

In 2025, Brazil accounted for 4.9% of global seaborne crude oil exports.

2026 so far has been even stronger, with exports in Jan-Sep 2026 up by +22.8% y-o-y to 97.1 mln tonnes, up from 79.1 mln t in Jan-Sep 2025.

About 30 percent of international crude exports from Brazil in Jan-Sep 2026 were loaded at Acu, about 24 percent from Angra Dos Reis, about 19 percent from Santos, about 3 percent from Peregrino FPSO, about 2 percent from Sao Sebastiao, about 2 percent from Madre Deus, about 2 percent from Itaguai.

The vast majority of crude oil cargoes loaded in Brazil, 68 percent in Jan-Sep 2026, are loaded on VLCCs. About 25 percent of volumes are loaded on Suezmaxes, and 5 percent is loaded on Aframaxes.

In terms of destinations for crude oil shipments from Brazil, the major ones are in Asia, which results in a very strong contribution to shipping tonnemiles.

The number one destination is Mainland China, accounting for 39 percent of Brazilian shipments so far this year. In Jan-Sep 2026, Brazil exported 37.5 mln tonnes of crude oil to China, up +13.4% y-o-y.

Direction Europe, about 18.1 mln tonnes (18.6 percent of the total) were shipped from Brazil to the European Union in Jan-Sep 2026, up +13.7% y-o-y from the 15.9 mln tonnes in Jan-Sep 2025. Of these, 5.7 mln t were shipped to the Netherlands, 4.6 mln t to Spain, 4.1 mln t to Portugal.

Shipments to the rest of South America surged by +56.3% y-o-y to 10.2 mln tonnes, including 6.8 mln t to Uruguay and 2.5 mln t to Chile.

To India volumes surged +150.3% y-o-y to 6.1 mln tonnes.

To South East Asia, volumes increased by +22.6% y-o-y in Jan-Sep 2026 to 9.0 mln tonnes, from 7.3 mln t in Jan-Sep 2025. Of these, 4.0 mln t were shipped to Singapore, and 2.1 mln t to Malaysia.

Exports to the United States were essentially flat at -1.4% y-o-y to 6.7 mln t, after declining by -16.0% y-o-y in the same period of last year.

The Capesize market fell steeply in a thoroughly bearish week, abundant tonnage in both basins and successive declines in freight derivative (FFA) values tilting the market firmly in charterers' favour.
