# COMMENT MARKET REPORT – WEEK 19/2026

# UNITED STATES CRUDE OIL EXPORTS

After a marginal decline in 2024, when global crude oil loadings decreased by -0.2% y-o-y, things picked up in 2025, with full-year volumes increasing by +1.8% y-o-y. In Jan-Apr 2026, however, global crude oil loadings declined by -3.3% y-o-y to 690.1 mln tonnes, excluding all cabotage trade, according to vessels tracking data from LSEG. Exports from the Arabian Gulf plunged by -23.6% y-o-y to 218.8 mln t in Jan-Apr 2026, and accounted for 31.7% of global seaborne crude trade, down sharply from the historical norm of about 40%, reflecting the ongoing war in the Persian Gulf. Exports from Russian ports (including oil of Kazakh origin) declined by -1.5% y-o-y to 73.8 mln t in Jan-Apr 2026, or 10.7% of global trade. From South America, exports surged by +29.1% y-o-y to 89.8 mln t. From the USA, exports increased by +8.9% y-o-y to 66.4 mln t in Jan-Apr 2026. From West Africa, exports decreased by -8.0% y-o-y to 52.5 mln t.

From ASEAN exports jumped by +30.9% y-o-y to 42.7 mln t in Jan-Apr 2026 (this inevitably includes trans-shipped Iranian and Russian cargoes). In terms of demand (measured by arrival at discharge ports), the top seaborne importer of crude oil in Jan-Apr 2026 was Mainland China, accounting for 23.1% of global trade. Volumes into China increased marginally by +1.0% y-o-y to 161.5 mln t in Jan-Apr 2026, from 159.9 mln t in Jan-Apr 2025. Imports into the EU27 increased by +2.0% y-o-y to 150.6 mln t, accounting for 21.6% of global trade. To ASEAN, imports decreased by -7.9% y-o-y to 85.0 mln t. Imports to India declined by -2.9% y-o-y to 79.0 mln t in Jan-Apr 2026. To South Korea, imports declined by -15.6% y-o-y to 37.0 mln t. To Japan, imports fell by -26.6% y-o-y to 28.5 mln t in Jan-Apr 2026. Imports into the USA increased by +22.1% y-o-y to 43.3 mln t in Jan-Apr 2026. The United States have been arguably the biggest winner from the sanction regime imposed on Russia and from OPEC's attempt to support oil prices through cutting production quotas.

Exports from the USA sharply increased in recent years, to the point that it is now the third largest exporter in the world, after Saudi and Russia. The USA now account for 8.2% of global crude oil loadings. Crude oil exports from the USA increased by +22.9% y-o-y in 2022 and by +19.5% y-o-y in 2023. 2024, however, was disappointing, with exports declining marginally by -0.3% y-o-y to 197.4 mln tonnes. 2025 was even more disappointing, with full-year exports declining by -7.8% y-o-y to 182.0 mln tonnes, the lowest since 2022. In Jan-Apr 2026, however, exports rebounded by +8.9% y-o-y to 66.4 mln tonnes, buoyed by a particularly strong April (21.9 mln t), likely as buyers sought alternatives to disrupted Arabian Gulf supply. About 53 percent of international crude exports from the USA in Jan-Apr 2026 were loaded in Corpus Christi, about 18 percent from Houston, about 8 percent from Galveston, about 7 percent from Bayport, about 3% from Beaumont, about 2% from LOOP.

Given <u>persisting</u> infrastructure limitations, only 39 percent of crude oil volumes loaded at USA ports in Jan-Apr 2026 were loaded on VLCCs, down from 51 percent previously. Most VLCC cargoes are loaded in Corpus Christi, Galveston and LOOP. About 28 percent of volumes are loaded on Suezmaxes, and 31 percent is loaded on Aframaxes. In terms of destinations for the shipments, it is quite diversified, with about 43% to Europe, 36% to Asia, and the rest to the Americas and Africa. Direction Europe, about 24.4 mln tonnes (36.8 percent of the total) were shipped from the USA to the European Union in Jan-Apr 2026, up sharply by +28.5% y-o-y, whilst about 3.7 mln t (5.6% of the total) went to the UK. Specifically, 7.9 mln tonnes were shipped in Jan-Apr 2026 to the Netherlands, 4.6 mln t to France, 2.9 mln t to Spain, 2.8 mln t to Italy, 1.8 mln t to Germany, 1.4 mln t to Sweden, 0.8 mln t to Denmark.

Direction Asia, about 8.7 mln t were shipped from the USA to S. Korea in Jan-Apr 2026, up +12.9% y-o-y, and 2.3 mln t to India, down -53.6% y-o-y. To Mainland China volumes were essentially flat at -2.1% y-o-y in Jan-Apr 2026 at 2.1 mln tonnes, very far from the 9.8 mln t in Jan-Apr 2023.

# COMMODITY NEWS – DRY BULK

**Iran war gives small boost to thermal coal as LNG shortages make fuel switching attractive.** Seaborne thermal coal prices in Asia have rallied modestly since the war began, with the Newcastle benchmark ending at $130.81/ton on May 1, up 12.6% from pre-war levels. High-quality Australian coal has outperformed mid- and lower-grade Indonesian product. Japan imported 7.89 million tons in April, down from 9.10 million in March; South Korea imported 5.70 million tons, down from 5.81 million. Spot LNG at $17.80/mmBtu is now above the coal-switching threshold for Japan. (Reuters, 5 May) **Russian wheat export prices stable; May shipments forecast to halve from April.** FOB wheat with 12.5% protein content held at $238.50/ton. IKAR estimates May exports at no more than 2.5 million tons, roughly half of April's 3.8-4.0 million. The Russian agriculture ministry confirmed 97% of crops in good or satisfactory condition but noted a significant delay in spring sowing. (Reuters, 5 May) **Oklahoma winter wheat crop projected at just 47.8 million bushels.** Field assessments showed average yields of 23.11 bushels per acre, sharply below the 10-year state average of 94.5 million bushels. Western Oklahoma and western Texas remain critically dry. Winter wheat in Texas was rated 56% very poor to poor. USDA's first official 2026 winter wheat production forecast is due May 12. (Reuters, 6 May) **Algeria buys 390,000-420,000 tons of milling wheat in international tender.** Purchases were reported at $268-270/ton c&f, with most bought at the $270 level. Traders expected Black Sea origins, especially Romania and Bulgaria, to be the main source. Shipment was sought in two periods from Europe: July 1-15 and July 16-31. Algeria's previous tender in March secured about 690,000 tons at $272/ton. (Reuters, 7 May) **Brazil's soybean area to grow only marginally in 2026/27 due to El Nino risks.** Argus analyst Nathalia Giannetti said higher production costs, rising fertiliser prices and the risk of a strong El Nino are reducing incentives for expansion. A strong El Nino would be more damaging for Brazil's soy output than La Nina, risking drought in the Center-West grain-producing region. (Reuters, 7 May) **India sees no need to curb sugar exports despite lower output.** The government concluded after reviewing production data that there is no need to restrict shipments. Of 1.59 million tons cleared for export, about 530,000-540,000 have shipped and over 800,000 contracted. Sugar consumption has softened as the Iran conflict worsened cooking gas shortages, crimping demand from eateries and bulk buyers. (Reuters, 7 May) **Brazil's 2025/26 corn crop expected to drop 7% year-on-year to 140.5 million tons.** Agroconsult said the second corn crop, representing the bulk of production, is forecast at 112.1 million tons — down nearly 10%. Average productivity is expected to decline to 101.9 bags per hectare from 114.4 bags previously, with all major producing states except Sao Paulo set to show declines. (Reuters, 8 May) **IKAR cuts Russia's 2025/26 wheat export forecast to 44.5 million tons.** The downgrade from 46.0 million tons was linked to a strong rouble, which has gained over 4% against the dollar this year, making exports less profitable. IKAR head Dmitry Rylko also cited a seasonal slowdown toward the end of the marketing season. (Reuters, 8 May) **Indonesia and Philippines sign nickel cooperation agreement.** The nickel associations of both countries signed an MOU covering information exchange, joint downstream processing technology development, and human resources. Indonesia exported $9.73 billion in nickel products last year. The Philippines will supply nickel ore with the proper silicon-to-magnesium ratio needed by Indonesian smelters. (Reuters, 8 May) **China steel exports fall 9% in April year-on-year; YTD shipments lowest since 2023.** China shipped 9.5 million tons of steel in April, up 4% from March but down from last year's record pace. Exports in the first four months fell 9.7% to 34.2 million tons. Iron ore imports dipped 0.8% month-on-month to 103.9 million tons as shrinking steel margins curbed buying appetite. (Reuters, 11 May) **China soybean imports jump in April, year-to-date shipments ahead of 2025.** April imports reached 8.48 million tons, more than double March's 4.02 million and up 40% year-on-year. Analysts expect arrivals of over 10 million tons per month from April to June as US and Brazilian record crops reach Chinese ports. Year-to-date imports totalled 25.2 million tons versus 23.19 million in the same period last year. (Reuters, 11 May) **South Korea's NOFI buys 136,000 tons of corn in international tender.** Nonghyup Feed Inc purchased two consignments of 68,000 tons each from Mitsui and Cargill at around $269-270/ton c&f. Shipment origins include the US Pacific Northwest, US Gulf, South America and South Africa, with arrival sought around September. (Reuters, 11 May) Source: Reuters / LSEG

# CAPESIZE MARKET

## ATLANTIC AND PACIFIC BASIN

The Capesize market staged a strong mid-week rally before retreating towards the week's end, with rates rising sharply between 4 and 6 May, holding steady on 7 May, then easing on 8 May amid thinner cargo demand and sideways freight derivative markets. The week opened sluggishly on 4 May, with activity dampened by extended public holidays across major Asian markets and in the UK. Despite subdued conditions, a trio of Western Australian iron ore miners was heard seeking tonnages, lending support to physical demand. Assessed freight on the Western Australia to Qingdao route stood at USD 13.75/wmt, while Tubarao to Qingdao was assessed at USD 34.75/wmt and Saldanha Bay to Qingdao at USD 25.90/wmt. Activity accelerated markedly from 5 May, underpinned by a surge in forward freight agreement rates that prompted shipowners to resist charterers' lower bids and raise offer levels across both basins. Fresh iron ore requirements emerged among operators and traders in the Pacific, and the trio of Western Australian miners returned for a second and third consecutive session.

Multiple fixtures were concluded on the Western Australia to Qingdao route, with mining majors heard fixed from Port Hedland and Dampier to Qingdao at levels ranging from mid-USD 14s/wmt to around USD 15.55/wmt over the course of the week. Assessed freight peaked at USD 15.70/wmt on 6 May. In the South Atlantic, fixing activity rebounded strongly post the extended Labour Day holiday. A trading house fixed from Tubarao to Qingdao for a June 1-5 laycan at mid-USD 36/wmt on 6 May, with assessed freight peaking at USD 36.85/wmt on both 6 and 7 May. Out of South Africa, a ship operator was heard fixed from Richards Bay to Qingdao for a May 13-18 laycan at around high-USD 27/wmt on 7 May, with Saldanha Bay to Qingdao peaking at USD 27.25/wmt on the same day. The market softened on 8 May as cargo volumes thinned across both basins following frenzied mid-week activity. Assessed freight on Western Australia to Qingdao eased to USD 15.10/wmt, Tubarao to Qingdao fell to USD 35.45/wmt, and Saldanha Bay to Qingdao retreated to USD 26.55/wmt.

# PANAMAX MARKET

## ATLANTIC BASIN

Market sentiment improved progressively, with Atlantic fronthaul enquiry building momentum throughout the week, particularly on NCSA routes. Monday activity was limited; a 2008-built 76,000 dwt Panamax fixed at around USD 19,500 basis P6 fronthaul Far East. Tuesday saw a noticeable pickup. A 2014-built 81,000 dwt Kamsarmax fixed retro Karachi via ECSA, redelivery Singapore/Japan, at USD 22,000/day; a 2018-built 81,000 dwt fixed APS for an NCSA transatlantic run at around USD 27,000/day; India-based ECSA openings concluded between USD 20,000 and USD 21,000/day basis P6 equivalent; and a 2024-built 82,000 dwt secured period employment at around USD 24,000/day. Wednesday brought firmer levels across the basin. A 2012-built 83,000 dwt Kamsarmax opening Las Palmas fixed ECSA/Singapore-Japan grains at USD 28,000/day; a 2012-built 79,000 dwt fixed from Gibraltar for NCSA fronthaul at USD 25,000/day; and a 2012-built 82,000 dwt fixed two-leg at USD 24,000/day. Thursday maintained momentum. A 2013-built 82,000 dwt Kamsarmax fixed ECSA/Singapore-Japan at USD 22,000/day plus USD 1,200,000 ballast bonus; a 2019-built 82,000 dwt fixed from Haldia at USD 22,000/day on similar business.

Friday saw further strengthening. A 2019-built 82,000 dwt Kamsarmax opening Gibraltar achieved USD 29,400/day for NCSA/Singapore-Japan grains; a 2013-built 82,000 dwt fixed at USD 27,000/day on similar business; and a 2016-built 85,000 dwt secured USD 30,250/day for St. Lawrence/Far East grains, one of the strongest fixtures of the week.

## PACIFIC BASIN

The Pacific market held firm throughout the week, with rates edging higher across most loading areas as steady cargo flow absorbed prompt tonnage. Coal and grain demand supported the upward trend, whilst NOPAC levels held largely unchanged week on week. In Indonesia, firm coal activity and a tightening prompt tonnage list drove rate improvement on South China runs. A 2015-built 81,868 dwt fixed at USD 26,000/day for Indonesia/South East Asia coal; a 2011-built 75,980 dwt at USD 24,250/day and a 2014-built 77,095 dwt at USD 24,000/day for Indonesia/South China coal; a 2015-built 77,154 dwt at USD 20,000/day plus USD 175,000 ballast bonus; and a 2007-built 74,483 dwt at USD 20,000/day. NOPAC activity was limited, with grain demand holding rates in the high teens to low USD 20,000s/day. A 2013-built 81,964 dwt fixed at USD 19,500/day for NOPAC/Singapore-Japan grain, whilst a 2008-built 77,171 dwt secured USD 18,250/day for two to three laden legs Singapore-Japan period business. East Coast Australia remained healthy on steady coal and iron ore demand, with rates above USD 20,000/day.

A 2018-built 81,824 dwt fixed at USD 25,750/day for East Australia/India coal, whilst a 2010-built 82,194 dwt secured USD 24,000/day plus USD 250,000 ballast bonus for North Australia/China iron ore.

# SUPRAMAX & HANDYSIZE MARKET

## US GULF / NORTH AMERICA

The market held firm during the week. On Ultramax tonnage, transatlantic business on shallow-draft 63,000 dwt vessels was concluded at around USD 28,000/day for grains to the Eastern Mediterranean, with coal reported at broadly similar levels. Fronthaul rates also firmed modestly, with one Ultramax reportedly fixed on a voyage basis at around USD 28,000/day for a petcoke fronthaul cargo. Activity in the Handysize sector was limited. The only reported fixture was a 37,000 dwt vessel fixed at USD 16,000/day for coal to Morocco, on a short-duration trip of approximately 25 days, wog.

## EAST COAST SOUTH AMERICA

**EAST COAST SOUTH AMERICA** Rates in East Coast South America remained on an upward trend, with strong activity recorded across both smaller and larger vessel sizes. On Handysize tonnage, transatlantic rates from East Coast South America to the Continent were at mid-USD 20,000s/day. For Supramax tonnage, transatlantic rates from West Africa via East Coast South America to the Continent/Mediterranean were around USD 17,500/day, whilst fronthaul from West Africa via East Coast South America to China was around USD 21,500/day. On Ultramax tonnage, transatlantic rates from West Africa via East Coast South America to the Continent/Mediterranean were around USD 18,000/day, whilst fronthaul from West Africa via East Coast South America to China was around USD 22,000/day.

## NORTH EUROPE / CONTINENT

**NORTH EUROPE / CONTINENT** Reported fixtures remained limited despite growing sentiment, particularly for larger units. Tonnage was relatively tight, though a slow injection of fresh orders kept the market firm. On Supramax and Ultramax tonnage, scrap trades from the Arabian Gulf/Red Sea to the Eastern Mediterranean were estimated in the mid-USD 20,000s/day, with a modern 63,000 dwt vessel heard fixed slightly above this level via the Continent. Handysize tonnage did not follow suit; grain enquiry to Morocco for June and July failed to attract support as owners remained reluctant to fix forward. Russia-loading cargo offered a modest premium of a few thousand dollars. Estimated Handysize rates: Continent (ex-Russia)/West Africa low to mid-USD 10,000s/day; Baltic (Russia)/Morocco mid to high-USD 10,000s/day; Continent/Mediterranean low to mid-USD 10,000s/day; Continent/ECSA around USD 8,000/day; Continent/US Gulf around USD 10,000/day.

## BLACK SEA / MEDITERRANEAN

**BLACK SEA / MEDITERRANEAN** The Black Sea and Mediterranean market continued to soften during the week, with the exception of fronthaul business to the Far East. Prompt inter-Mediterranean cargo enquiry was largely absent, with activity concentrated towards the US Gulf and Far East, predominantly on Supramax tonnage. In the Handysize sector, inter-Mediterranean trips are at around USD 8,500/day basis delivery passing Çanakkale, or potentially below. Transatlantic rates to the US Gulf held steady to slightly weaker at USD 9,500/day, whilst East Coast South America trips softened to USD 7,000–7,250/day. For Supramax and Ultramax tonnage, inter-Mediterranean rates stand at USD 10,000/day, whilst transatlantic rates to the US Gulf and US East Coast have eased to USD 11,000/day. # SUPRAMAX & HANDYSIZE MARKET

## MEG / INDIAN OCEAN

The Arabian Gulf, South Africa, and Indian Ocean Supramax markets were subdued in the week of 4–8 May, pressured by regional holidays, rising tonnage supply, and weak cargo enquiry. In the Indian Ocean, no fresh fixtures were reported throughout the week. Assessed freight for 50,000 mt of limestone from Mina Saqr to Paradip opened at USD 18.00/mt on 4 May, peaked at USD 18.50/mt on 6 May, then retreated to USD 17.95/mt by 8 May. West coast India fundamentals were described as poor, with rates underpinned mainly by owners' resistance rather than demand. Late in the week, some vessel movement towards Arabian Sea limestone ports in Oman and the UAE offered modest support. Out of South Africa, assessed freight for 50,000 mt of coal from Richards Bay to Port Qasim fluctuated between USD 24.00/mt and USD 24.70/mt, closing at USD 24.10/mt on 8 May.
