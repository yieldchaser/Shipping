CHINA IRON ORE IMPORTS

2025 was another positive year for global seaborne iron ore trade. In Jan-Dec 2025, global loadings of iron ore increased by +3.5% y-o-y to 1,732.0 mln tonnes, based on AXS Marine vessel tracking data. 2026 has remained on a positive, albeit slower, note, with export volumes up by +1.0% y-o-y in Jan- Sep 2026 to 1,279.2 mln tonnes. Exports from Australia increased in Jan-Sep 2026 by +2.2% y-o-y to 713.0 mln tonnes. From Brazil, exports declined by -1.5% y-o-y in Jan-Sep 2026 to 284.3 mln t. From Canada there was a -8.0% y-o- y decline to 43.7 mln tonnes. From South Africa volumes were essentially flat, -0.1% y-o-y, at 41.0 mln t. India saw an increase of +3.2% y-o-y in Jan-Sep 2026 to 20.1 mln t. From Norway exports increased by +2.2% y-o-y to 16.0 mln t. Demand is still driven primarily by Mainland China, which accounts for 75% of imports. Iron ore imports into China increased by +4.1% y-o-y in Jan-Sep 2026 to 967.8 mln tonnes. Imports into Japan declined by -3.6% y-o-y to 69.2 mln t. To the EU, imports declined by -3.1% y-o-y to 53.1 mln t. Volumes into South Korea increased by +3.7% y-o-y to 55.3 mln t. Imports into Malaysia increased by

+6.2% y-o-y to 19.2 mln tonnes. To Vietnam volumes were up by +20.2% y-o-y to 20.8 mln t. To Oman, volumes were down -22.7% y-o-y to 8.3 mln t, to Saudi Arabia down by -70.6% y-o-y to 2.5 mln t, to Bahrain -78.8% y-o-y to 2.0 mln t.

Mainland China is by far the largest importer of iron ore in the world. China accounted for 74.6% of global iron ore imports in Jan-Dec 2025. Imports into China increased +4.0% y-o-y in Jan-Dec 2024 to 1,256.4 mln tonnes. They have further increased by +1.8% y-o-y in Jan-Dec 2025 to 1,279.4 mln t, which is a new all- time record. As already mentioned, in Jan-Sep 2026, imports into China increased by +4.1% y-o-y to 967.8 mln t. This was driven primarily by a very strong +11.3% y-o-y surge in 1Q26, whilst growth moderated to +1.1% y- o-y in 2Q26 and +0.5% y-o-y in 3Q26. The vast majority (71%) of iron ore volumes into China in Jan-Sep 2026 were loaded on Capesizes (130,000- 220,000 dwt), with a further 21% carried on VLOCs (over 220,000 dwt), whilst just 3% was carried on Post-Panamaxes, 2% on Panamaxes, and 3% on Supramaxes.
