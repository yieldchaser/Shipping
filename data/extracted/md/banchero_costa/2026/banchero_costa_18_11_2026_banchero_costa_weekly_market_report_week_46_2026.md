# Banchero Costa Weekly Market Report - Week 46, 2026

## Weekly Macro Insight: Arabian Gulf Crude Oil Exports

In Jan-Dec 2025, global crude oil loadings went up by +1.8% y-o-y to 2,221.1 mln tonnes, excluding all cabotage trade, according to vessels tracking data from LSEG.

This year started well, but the trend reversed from March onwards, and global crude oil loadings in Jan-Oct 2026 were down by -5.8% y-o-y to 1,738.8 mln tonnes.

Exports from the Arabian Gulf were down by -27.6% y-o-y to 531.3 mln t in Jan-Oct 2026, accounting for 30.6% of seaborne crude trade, down from 39.8% in the same period of last year.

From South America, exports increased by +32.5% y-o-y to 238.8 mln t, with a share of 13.7%.

Exports from Russian ports (including oil of Kazakh origin) increased by +6.7% y-o-y in Jan-Oct 2026 to 207.8 mln tonnes, or 12.0% of global trade.

From the USA, export volumes increased by +8.5% y-o-y to 166.4 mln t in Jan-Oct 2026, a 9.6% share.

From South East Asia exports increased by +2.7% y-o-y to 92.8 mln t in Jan-Oct 2026 (but this inevitably also reflects changes in the re-export of Russian origin volumes).

In terms of demand, the top single-country seaborne importer of crude oil in Jan-Oct 2026 was Mainland China, accounting for 18.9% of global trade, although the EU27 as a bloc was a larger buyer.

Volumes into China declined by -20.1% y-o-y to 328.4 mln t in Jan-Oct 2026.

Imports to the EU27 increased by +0.9% y-o-y to 387.8 mln t, accounting for 22.4% of global trade.

To ASEAN, imports declined by -9.2% y-o-y to 219.4 mln t (again this includes Russian volumes later re-exported elsewhere in Asia).

To India, volumes increased +1.5% y-o-y to 200.8 mln t in Jan-Oct 2026.

The **Arabian Gulf countries** (including Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman, Iraq and Iran) are the single largest seaborne exporter of crude oil in the world.

In 2025 they accounted for 39.8% of global seaborne crude oil exports.

In the 12 months of 2025, seaborne crude oil exports from AG countries increased by +1.7% to 883.1 mln tonnes, excluding cabotage, after declining by -0.8% y-o-y in 2024.

In Jan-Oct 2026, however, Arabian Gulf crude exports collapsed by -27.6% y-o-y to 531.3 mln tonnes, from 734.1 mln t in the same period of 2025, and their share of global seaborne crude trade fell to 30.6%, from 39.8% a year earlier.

About 85.8% of volumes loaded in the AG countries in Jan-Oct 2026 were carried in VLCCs, 11.0% were carried in Suezmaxes, and 3.1% in Aframaxes.

These proportions increased in recent years in favour of the largest tankers, as Suezmaxes and Afras were diverted to carry more Russian cargoes.

The ranking of export terminals has been transformed by the war in the Gulf, with loadings shifting decisively to terminals that can be reached without transiting the Strait of Hormuz.

The main crude oil export terminals in the AG are now: Fujairah (137.8 mln tonnes, or 25.9%, in Jan-Oct 2026), Yanbu (104.0 mln t, or 19.6%), Ras Tanura (59.2 mln t, or 11.1%), Basrah (52.6 mln t, or 9.9%), Sohar (49.8 mln t, or 9.4%), Mina al Fahal (36.7 mln t, or 6.9%), Mina al Ahmadi (14.3 mln t), Zirku Island (11.4 mln t), Das Island (6.1 mln t).

Ras Tanura, the largest terminal in the world a year ago, has been overtaken by Fujairah and Yanbu, both of which lie outside the Strait.

In terms of destinations for crude oil shipments from the AG, the focus in recent years has clearly shifted to Asia. In Jan-Oct 2026, the top destination was South East Asia, accounting for 21.6% of the total AG crude exports, narrowly ahead of Mainland China with 21.5%, followed by India with 14.6%, South Korea with 11.0%, Japan with 7.4%.

Direct shipments to the USA now account for just 1.8% of AG exports, and direct shipments to the European Union for just 2.2%.

Another 6.1% of AG exports are shipped to the Ain Sukhna Terminal in Egypt to feed the Sumed pipeline, and will eventually mostly end up in Europe.

In Jan-Oct 2026, the AG countries shipped 114.4 mln tonnes of crude to Mainland China, which represents a -39.4% y-o-y decline from 188.9 mln tonnes in Jan-Oct 2025.

Shipments to South East Asia fell by -27.8% y-o-y to 114.9 mln t, from 159.0 mln t in Jan-Oct 2025.

The AG also shipped 77.5 mln t this year to India, which represents a decline of -15.4% y-o-y from 91.6 mln t in the same period last year.

Volumes to South Korea declined by -28.4% y-o-y in Jan-Oct 2026 to 58.5 mln t.

Shipments from the AG to Japan more than halved, down -52.4% y-o-y in Jan-Oct 2026 to 39.4 mln tonnes.

# COMMODITY NEWS – DRY BULK

**China's iron ore imports at five-month low as thin margins dent appetite**

China's iron ore imports in October slid 3.8% from the month before to a five-month low, as persistently weak steel margins dampened buying appetite and global shipments fell. The world's largest iron ore consumer brought in 105.66 million metric tons of the key steelmaking ingredient last month, the lowest monthly level since May, data from the country's General Administration of Customs showed on Tuesday.

**China's October crude steel output falls on weak demand, thin margins**

China's crude steel output fell 3.8% in October from September to its lowest level so far this year, official data showed on Monday, weighed down by subdued home demand, falling exports and persistently thin margins. The world's largest steel producer churned out 70.83 million metric tons of the metal used in the construction and manufacturing sectors, down 1.6% year-on-year, data from the National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) showed.

**Australia wary of iron ore trade manipulation as China seeks more bargaining power**

Australia said any potential manipulation of trade in iron ore would be against international rules and a threat to government revenues, as China's centralisation of its buying increases its bargaining power in negotiating contracts. Resources Minister Madeleine King said the prospect of lower prices for Australia's biggest commodity export was concerning but the government was not looking to get involved in contract talks.

**Mexico says crackdown on Asian steel shipments is paying off as US tariff talks advance**

Mexico is working to block steel from China and other countries from being rerouted through Mexico into the US under North America's trade pact, a Mexican official said on Wednesday, as Mexico presses Washington for relief from steel, aluminum and auto tariffs. Luis Rosendo Gutierrez, Mexico's deputy economy minister for foreign trade, told journalists the government had tightened traceability measures and was guaranteeing that products were not reaching the United States by exploiting Mexico's trade preferences.

**China's Oct coal output falls for seventh month as safety checks curb supply**

China's coal production fell year-on-year for a seventh month in October, the statistics bureau said on Monday, extending a decline triggered by a greater focus on safety after a coal mine disaster in May. China produced 384.94 million metric tons of coal in October, down 5.8% on the year but nearly flat with September's 385.13 million tons, the National Bureau of Statistics data showed.

**Goldman Sachs flags risks to $15,400/t copper price forecast for 2027**

Goldman Sachs said on Thursday it sees downside risks to its 2027 copper price forecast of $15,400 a metric ton after the US midterm elections, citing AI-related positioning and lower US tariff expectations. "The main risk to our forecast stems from positioning rather than physical demand," Goldman Sachs said in a note, referring to AI-related market exposure.

**China October soybean imports fall on high inventories, weak crush margins**

China's soybean imports fell to their lowest level for October since 2023, as high crusher inventories, negative margins and low pork prices dampened buying appetite, analysts said. China imported 7.35 million metric tons of soybeans in October, down 22.5% from the record 9.48 million tons imported a year earlier and 38% lower than September, customs data showed on Tuesday.

**China raises 2025/26 soybean imports forecast in November**

China's agriculture ministry raised its 2025/26 soybean import estimate to 111.62 million metric tons and left its 2026/27 import forecast unchanged, according to its November outlook released on Tuesday. China's soybean harvest is nearly complete, with above-average protein content and good overall quality, the ministry said.

**Indian rice export rates gain on Africa demand, firm rupee**

Indian rice export prices rose this week, buoyed by modest demand from African buyers and a rupee that strengthened to its highest level in nearly seven months, while Thai and Vietnamese export rates also drifted higher. India's 5% broken parboiled variety was quoted at $385-$390 per ton, up from last week's $382-$386. Indian 5% broken white rice was priced at $390-$395.

**USDA lowers US corn crop estimate, cuts corn and soy stocks forecasts**

US farmers will harvest fewer bushels of corn than previously expected, the US Department of Agriculture said on Tuesday, lowering its crop estimate in a monthly report after raising its forecast in October. The agency also pared its forecast of remaining US corn stockpiles at the end of the current marketing year more than analysts expected, and cut its forecast for US soybean stocks, bucking trade expectations for an increase.

# COMMODITY NEWS – CRUDE OIL & PRODUCTS

**China's October crude oil imports rise to highest level since March**

China’s October crude oil imports rose to their highest level since March this year, official data showed on Tuesday, after the country sharply cut imports as the US-Israeli war on Iran disrupted supplies from the Middle East. The world's largest oil buyer imported 41.94 million metric tons of crude oil in October, or 9.88 million barrels per day (bpd), with the daily import rate up 9.4% from September, according to China’s customs data.

**OPEC oil output rises in October to 90% of pre-war rate, Reuters survey shows**

OPEC oil output rose in October, a Reuters survey found, led by gains in Saudi Arabia, Kuwait and Iraq despite continued constraints on exports due to the Iran war and a US blockade on Iran's shipments. Crude output by the 11-member Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries rose by 1.38 million barrels per day month-on-month to 22.87 million bpd, the survey found.

**Asia buys up African, Brazilian oil as shipping costs fall**

Asian buyers have been snapping up Atlantic Basin crude at lower prices after tanker freight rates on cargoes from Africa and Brazil slumped from all-time highs, traders, shipbrokers and analysts said. Unipec, the trading arm of the world's top refiner Sinopec, bought some African cargoes last week. Both it and South Korea's largest refiner SK Energy have chartered vessels at much lower rates to load West African grades in December, the people said.

**China's October oil throughput rate dips, data indicate crude stock build**

China's October refinery throughput rate fell slightly from a month earlier, official data showed, while Reuters calculations indicated that October saw the largest crude inventory build since March this year amid the US-Israeli war on Iran. The world's second-largest oil consumer processed 58.3 million metric tons of crude oil, or 13.73 million barrels per day (bpd), with the daily rate down 2% from September and 8.1% from a year earlier, according to data released by the National Bureau of Statistics.

**Goldman sees Brent easing to $78 a barrel by end-2027 as markets adapt**

Goldman Sachs said it expects Brent crude to average $88 a barrel next year and slip to $78 by December 2027 as a return to surplus weighs on markets, but in the near term it sees upside risks to prices due to shipping attacks in the Middle East. Goldman expects crude prices to normalize, with WTI to average $78 a barrel in 2027 and ease to $70 by end-2027.

**US crude, fuel inventories rise as demand falls and imports jump, EIA says**

US crude and fuel inventories rose last week, driven by softer refined products demand and a jump in oil imports, the Energy Information Administration said on Thursday. Crude inventories rose by 4.7 million barrels to 414.4 million barrels in the week ended November 6, the EIA said, compared with analysts' expectations in a Reuters poll for a 1.1 million-barrel draw.

**BP in talks to sell stake in Brazil oil discovery to Petrobras, sources say**

BP is in talks with Petrobras to sell a stake in its Bumerangue oil block off the coast of Brazil, two sources at the Brazilian state-run oil firm told Reuters, while cautioning that the potential deal was not considered a priority. The negotiations are ongoing, but the field's high carbon dioxide content remains an issue, according to the sources. "It seems BP doesn't know what to do with Bumerangue," said one of the sources.

**Alberta landowners' group tells farmers, ranchers to resist oil development**

An Alberta landowner advocacy organization is urging farmers and ranchers to "actively resist" new oil and gas development on their land, an unprecedented move highlighting growing tensions between industry and rural residents in Canada's main energy-producing province. Growing opposition to oil and gas development in a province that has long supported the oil industry could challenge Prime Minister Mark Carney's efforts to boost oil output, build new pipelines and attract new markets for Canadian energy.

**US crude production to hit record high for fourth straight year in 2026, EIA says**

US crude production is expected to hit a record high for the fourth straight year in 2026, the Energy Information Administration said on Tuesday. Production will reach 13.9 million barrels per day this year, up from 13.7 million bpd in 2025, it said. US crude output is then set to rise again in 2027, to 14.3 million bpd.

**Trump’s Iran war pause is as costly as new strikes**

As US President Donald Trump weighs his next steps in the Iran war, Gulf energy producers have adapted to a no-war, no-peace reality that is making their operations more expensive and risky. Even without renewed full-scale fighting, those additional costs could help put a floor under global oil prices. The question is how long this uneasy equilibrium can last.

# COMMODITY NEWS – NATURAL GAS & POWER

**Hormuz LNG traffic hits highest in October since Iran war began**

The monthly tally of laden liquefied natural gas (LNG) tankers exiting the Strait of Hormuz reached its highest in October since the US-Iran conflict disrupted shipping this year, vessel tracking data showed. Last month, 31 LNG tankers went through the key waterway, according to data from S&P Global Energy, of which 23 were loaded at Ras Laffan in Qatar and eight at Das Island in the United Arab Emirates.

**Europe draws LNG from Asia, but not fast enough**

Europe is starting to pull more liquefied natural gas away from Asia as its critically-low storage levels and fears of a cold snap trump the high spot price of the super-chilled fuel. Europe's LNG imports are on track to reach 10.11 million metric tons in November, the most since March and up from 8.99 million in October, according to data compiled by commodity analysts Kpler.

**US natural gas output and demand to hit record highs in 2026 and 2027, EIA says**

US natural gas supply and demand will both rise to record highs in 2026 and 2027, the US Energy Information Administration said in its Short-Term Energy Outlook (STEO) on Tuesday. The EIA projected dry gas production will rise from a record 107.6 billion cubic feet per day (bcfd) in 2025 to 113.0 bcfd in 2026 and 117.6 bcfd in 2027.

**Venture Global asks DOE for approval to re-export LNG from CP2 project**

Venture Global has asked the US Department of Energy for authorization to re-export liquefied natural gas from its CP2 LNG project in Louisiana as the company moves closer to bringing the first phase of the facility online, according to a notice from the DOE on Thursday. The second-largest LNG exporter in the US said it is seeking approval by January 15; it requested a blanket authorization to export previously imported LNG to countries that have free-trade agreements with the US as well as those that do not.

**Norway to buy carbon offsets from Indonesian power projects, Indonesia says**

Norway has agreed to buy up to 12 million metric tons of carbon credits generated by solar power projects developed by Indonesia's state-owned utility Perusahaan Listrik Negara, the Indonesian government said on Thursday. The carbon credits are valued at between $20 to $40 per ton of CO2e, the government said in a statement.

**Asia's nuclear pipeline marks a new challenge for fossil fuel demand**

Asia dominates the construction pipeline for both new nuclear power plants and renewable energy capacity, setting the stage for a potential erosion in fossil fuel use in power generation across the region. Asian nations are home to 73% of all new reactors currently being built, according to data from Global Energy Monitor (GEM), accounting for 66 gigawatts (GW) of the global total of 91 GW moving through construction.

**Europe's shrinking flexible power cushion leaves Germany exposed**

German power prices have hit their highest since 2022. While weak wind output and higher gas costs triggered the rally, the root cause is Europe's shrinking flexibility cushion. Germany's latest power shock reveals a challenge that is becoming central to Europe's broader energy transition. The issue is no longer how quickly the region can add renewable generation, but whether the power system can maintain sufficient flexibility when weather-dependent generation falls short.

**Power prices in largest US grid rise the most in recent months, Hitachi Energy data shows**

The largest US electric grid, which covers the world's biggest data center hub, had the biggest increase in power prices of any grid in the country in a recent six-month period, according to industry data released by Hitachi Energy on Thursday. Average day-ahead wholesale electricity prices in PJM Interconnection, which spans 13 Mid-Atlantic and Midwest US states, increased by about 24% to $52 per megawatt-hour in the period from April to September this year compared to the same period last year.

**US power demand to beat record highs in 2026 and 2027 as AI use surges, EIA says**

US electricity consumption will rise to record highs in 2026 and 2027 due to power-hungry AI data centers and electrification, the US Energy Information Administration said in its Short-Term Energy Outlook (STEO) on Tuesday. The EIA projected that power demand will rise from a record 4,216 billion kilowatt-hours (kWh) in 2025 to 4,306 billion kWh in 2026 and 4,370 billion kWh in 2027. If correct, 2026 would be the third year in a row of record demand.

# CAPESIZE MARKET

## ATLANTIC AND PACIFIC BASIN

The Capesize market diverged sharply, the Pacific rallying strongly from midweek as owners resisted lower offers while Brazil and South Africa retreated on plunging bunker prices.

The Western Australia–Qingdao route (170,000 mt iron ore, plus/minus 10%) firmed 5 cents/wmt to USD 13.50/wmt on 10 November amid subdued post-holiday trading, a mining major fixing Dampier–Qingdao between mid-USD 13/wmt and USD 13.60/wmt for 24–26 November laycan.

The route eased 45 cents/wmt to close at USD 15.05/wmt on 13 November on dull trading, with Dampier–Qingdao fixed at around USD 14.45/wmt for 28–30 November and Port Hedland–Qingdao at USD 14.60/wmt, finishing USD 1.60/wmt above its 6 November level.

Tubarao–Qingdao shed 10 cents/wmt to USD 36.40/wmt and 40 cents/wmt to USD 36.00/wmt as bunker prices plunged, although market sources noted daily hire rates remained attractive.

It was unchanged at an implied USD 36.00/wmt on 12 November before falling USD 1.00/wmt to close at USD 35.00/wmt, down USD 1.50/wmt, owners revising offers lower as sentiment softened; a Capesize was fixed on the route at around USD 35.00/wmt for 10–16 December laycan.

West African business was brisk, Nouadhibou–Qingdao heard at around mid-USD 36/wmt for 1–10 December, while the North Atlantic stayed firm, Ponta da Madeira–Rotterdam fixing at about USD 18.00/wmt.

# **PANAMAX MARKET**

## ATLANTIC BASIN

The Atlantic market maintained a steady tone, with ECSA grain activity providing the main source of employment and US Gulf long-haul business continuing to attract firm rates.

Although the latest fixtures do not point to a sharp upward move across the board, Owners are still securing respectable returns, particularly on voyages to the Far East and on longer-haul grain movements.

ECSA remained active, with several vessels fixed for Singapore-Japan grains.

Rates ranged from USD 18,500/day to USD 21,500/day, with newer tonnage generally achieving the upper end of the range.

An 82,000 dwt vessel fixed at USD 21,500/day, whilst other units of similar size secured USD 20,500–20,750/day.

Additional fixtures included an 82,000 dwt vessel at USD 20,000/day plus USD 1m ballast bonus and another at USD 19,000/day plus USD 900,000 ballast bonus.

These deals confirm continued demand for fronthaul employment, with positioning and vessel specifications influencing the final returns.

On the US Gulf side, a scrubber-fitted 82,000 dwt vessel fixed for China grains at USD 27,500/day, following an earlier indication at USD 27,350/day.

This remains a strong level for long-haul employment and provides positive support for the wider Atlantic market.

A further ECSA grain fixture from Aratu was reported at USD 18,750/day plus USD 875,000 ballast bonus.

## PACIFIC BASIN

The Pacific market remained fairly active throughout the week, with a good flow of fixtures reported across the main loading areas.

Activity was particularly concentrated in Indonesia and Australia, whilst NOPAC grain business continued to see steady demand and relatively firm levels.

Indonesian activity remained fairly active, rates showing a wide range depending on vessel size, age and destination.

South China business was mostly concluded at around USD 15,500–17,000 for older and smaller units, whilst better levels were achieved for Japan and India-bound employment: a 95,000 dwt fixed USD 22,000 plus USD 120,000 for a Japan coal trip and an 82,000 dwt achieved USD 22,000–23,500 for India, whilst other fixtures included a 76,000 dwt at USD 17,750 for South Korea and a 75,000 dwt at the same level for Vietnam.

NOPAC grain business remained relatively firm, rates ranging from USD 19,500 to USD 22,250 for Singapore-Japan and other Far East destinations: an 82,000 dwt fixed USD 22,250 for Singapore-Japan, including scrubber benefit to Charterers, another 82,000 dwt achieved USD 19,500 on the same route, a further 82,000 dwt fixed USD 20,000 for Vietnam and a 76,000 dwt achieved USD 19,500 for Japan.

East Coast Australia continued to see a reasonable flow of fixtures, rates generally ranging between USD 19,000 and USD 25,000 for Pacific destinations: an 82,000 dwt fixed USD 25,000 for a Singapore-Japan coal trip and an 84,000 dwt achieved USD 23,750 for the same destination, with other reported fixtures including an 82,000 dwt at USD 22,500 for Singapore-Japan and an 81,500 dwt at USD 21,500 for China.

West Coast activity included a 95,000 dwt fixed USD 22,500 for a Singapore-Japan bauxite trip and a 76,000 dwt achieving USD 20,000 for a Singapore-Japan grain run, whilst an 81,000 dwt fixed USD 21,000 for an East Australia/India coal trip.

# SUPRAMAX & HANDYSIZE MARKET

## US GULF / NORTH AMERICA

The US Gulf stayed firm and positive week on week, Ultramax TA rates pushing very high on East Med and USEC, fronthaul well supported and Handies firmer, particularly regional and NCSA.

Transatlantic strong: a 61k on subs Miss River/East Med petcoke USD 40,000; a 63k open Quebec on subs Chesapeake/UK-Cont wood pellets USD 35,000 and another on subs APS USG USD 27,000.

Fronthaul firm: a 64k on subs APS USG/India coal USD 36,000, a 63k USG/India petcoke USD 34,000; to the Far East a 64k (2026) open Puerto Moin rumoured Japan grains USD 31,000 APS and a 63k on subs APS USG grains around USD 29,000–30,000.

Period: a 64k ex Houston about 4 months worldwide USD 28,000. Handies firmed regionally and TA: a 42k (2026) open Miss River dely WWR/Colombia USD 24,000, a 36k open Ocho Rios SW Pass/NCSA grains USD 19,000, a 34k open Puerto Cabello APS VDC/USG alumina USD 25,000 and a 39k APS Pascagoula/UK-Cont grains USD 21,750.

## EAST COAST SOUTH AMERICA

Across ECSA the larger bulk segments continue to see tight conditions this week loading out of the south, where steady cargo flow meets a short list of open ships.

Northern rates for December are holding strong, on the tight list of ballasters and the strong Continent/US Gulf market conditions.

On the smaller vessels the market remains strong, maintaining slight growth on transatlantic, whilst a little decrease is seen on fronthauls on the lack of cargoes.

On runs from Santos to East Coast India and South East Asia, 63,000 dwt units saw bids and offers exchanged around USD 17,750/day plus USD 775,000 ballast bonus against USD 18,250/day plus USD 825,000.

From Upriver and Bahia Blanca to Chittagong, a 63,000 dwt unit quoted a voyage rate translating to roughly USD 18,250/day plus USD 820,000, whilst modern Ultramaxes are aiming higher, asking around USD 19,000/day plus USD 900,000 for standard ECSA fronthaul runs.
