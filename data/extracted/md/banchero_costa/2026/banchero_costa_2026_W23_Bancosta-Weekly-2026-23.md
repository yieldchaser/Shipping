# Banchero Costa Weekly Market Report - Week 23, 2026

## Weekly Macro Insight: Brazil Crude Oil Exports

After a marginal decline in 2024, when global crude oil loadings decreased by -0.2% y-o-y, things picked up in 2025, with full-year volumes increasing by +1.8% y-o-y. In Jan-May 2026, however, global crude oil loadings declined by -5.3% y-o-y to 851.1 mln tonnes, excluding all cabotage trade, according to vessels tracking data from LSEG. Exports from the Arabian Gulf plunged by -30.3% y-o-y to 249.5 mln t in Jan-May 2026, and accounted for 29.3% of global seaborne crude trade, down sharply from the historical norm of about 40%, reflecting the ongoing war in the Persian Gulf. Exports from Russian ports (including oil of Kazakh origin) edged up by +0.4% y-o-y to 95.1 mln t in Jan-May 2026, or 11.2% of global trade. From South America, exports surged by +35.0% y-o-y to 115.5 mln t. From the USA, exports increased by +18.3% y-o-y to 89.1 mln t in Jan-May 2026. From West Africa, exports decreased by -9.3% y-o-y to 66.3 mln t.

From ASEAN exports jumped by +20.3% y-o-y to 52.9 mln t in Jan-May 2026 (this inevitably includes trans-shipped Iranian, Russian cargoes). In terms of demand (measured by arrival at discharge ports), the top seaborne importer of crude oil in Jan-May 2026 was Mainland China, accounting for 21.7% of global trade. Volumes into China decreased by -6.6% y-o-y to 187.6 mln t in Jan-May 2026, from 200.8 mln t in Jan-May 2025. Imports into the EU27 increased by +2.5% y-o-y to 191.2 mln t, accounting for 22.2% of global trade. To ASEAN, imports decreased by -8.7% y-o-y to 106.7 mln t. Imports to India declined by -1.2% y-o-y to 99.6 mln t in Jan-May 2026. **Brazil** is fast emerging as a significant exporter of crude oil, with volumes increasing rapidly in recent years. In Jan-Dec 2023, volumes surged by +27.2% y-o-y to 88.2 mln tonnes. In Jan-Dec 2024, exports increased by a further +4.3% y-o-y to 92.0 mln t. In Jan-Dec 2025, exports rose by a further +18.7% y-o-y to 109.2 mln t.

In 2025, Brazil accounted for 4.9% of global seaborne crude oil exports. 2026 so far has been even stronger, with exports in Jan-May 2026 up by +25.1% y-o-y to 52.2 mln tonnes, up from 41.7 mln t in Jan-May 2025. About 32 percent of international crude exports from Brazil in Jan-May 2026 were loaded at Acu, about 24 percent from Angra Dos Reis, about 19 percent from Santos, about 4 percent from Peregrino FPSO, about 2 percent from Madre Deus, about 1 percent from Itaguai. The vast majority of crude oil cargoes loaded in Brazil, 71 percent in Jan-May 2026, are loaded on VLCCs. About 22 percent of volumes are loaded on Suezmaxes, and 4 percent is loaded on Aframaxes.

In terms of destinations for crude oil shipments from Brazil, the major ones are in Asia, which results in a very strong contribution to shipping tonne-miles. The number one destination is Mainland China, accounting for 43 percent of Brazilian shipments so far this year. In Jan-May 2026, Brazil exported 22.5 mln tonnes of crude oil to China, up +32.4% y-o-y. Direction Europe, about 7.5 mln tonnes (14.3 percent of the total) were shipped from Brazil to the European Union in Jan-May 2026, down -14.2% y-o-y from the 8.7 mln tonnes in Jan-May 2025. Of these, 2.1 mln t were shipped to Spain, 2.1 mln t to the Netherlands, 1.8 mln t to Portugal. Shipments to the rest of South America surged by +129.6% y-o-y to 6.1 mln tonnes, including 4.0 mln t to Uruguay and 1.5 mln t to Chile. To India volumes surged +163.1% y-o-y to 4.0 mln tonnes. To South East Asia, volumes decreased by -4.5% y-o-y in Jan-May 2026 to 3.7 mln tonnes, from 3.9 mln t in Jan-May 2025. Of these, 2.2 mln t were shipped to Singapore, and 0.8 mln t to Malaysia.

Exports to the United States corrected by -23.6% y-o-y to 2.6 mln t, after also declining by -27.2% y-o-y in the same period of last year.

# COMMODITY NEWS – DRY BULK

## **Morocco Suspends Wheat Import Duty from August**

Morocco will lift customs duties on soft wheat imports from 1 August 2026 following a dramatic recovery in domestic production. The country's harvest is estimated to have doubled year-on-year to approximately 9 million tonnes, prompting the USDA to forecast a 40 per cent fall in Moroccan grain imports to around 4 million tonnes in 2026/27. The policy appears intended to preserve consumer price competition alongside the improved domestic supply. For bulk carrier operators in the transatlantic grain trade, the outlook points to softer demand from one of North Africa's largest buyers, with reduced import volumes into Casablanca and other Moroccan ports expected over the second half of the year.

## **South Korea Covers Feed Corn Requirement**

The Korea Feed Association purchased 52,000 to 68,000 tonnes of corn at $260.98 per tonne cost and freight from Freepoint Commodities, with arrival expected in South Korea during September and October 2026. The cargo is expected to load from the United States Gulf or South American origins on a Panamax or Kamsarmax vessel. South Korea operates one of Asia's most active grain procurement programmes, driven by consistent demand from its poultry, swine and dairy industries. The purchase confirms buyers are continuing to cover forward positions, providing useful visibility for Panamax owners on Pacific basin cargo flows despite ongoing global uncertainty.

## **China Wheat Harvest Damaged by Excessive Rainfall**

Heavy rainfall during the harvest window has caused significant quality deterioration across China's central wheat provinces, with estimates suggesting up to 7 per cent of production in Henan and Hubei has been downgraded or is showing pre-harvest sprouting. These provinces account for a large share of China's milling wheat supply, making the quality losses disproportionately significant for the domestic flour industry. Analysts believe the damage could prompt additional imports of premium milling-grade wheat, at a time when global wheat prices have already risen approximately 20 per cent in 2026. Any increase in Chinese import volumes at scale would be a substantial positive for Panamax and Supramax demand on Pacific routes.

## **Egypt Approaches Record Domestic Wheat Procurement**

Egypt has procured 4.5 million tonnes of wheat from domestic farmers since the harvest season opened in mid-April 2026, putting the country on track to exceed its 5 million tonne procurement target according to the agriculture ministry. Officials described the season as unprecedented in delivery volumes, reflecting both a strong crop and effective government incentives. Egypt is also considering replacing its in-kind food subsidy scheme with cash transfers from July, a structural reform that could alter the composition of future import demand. For bulk carriers trading into Alexandria and Damietta, a strong domestic harvest typically reduces the scale of state import tenders in the second half of the year.

## **BHP Advances Biofuel Trial on Iron Ore Bulk Carrier**

Mining major BHP has deployed a biofuel blend commercially on the bulk carrier Berge Lyngor, operating on the iron ore route between Western Australia and China. The fuel is a 50/50 mixture of tallow biodiesel and used cooking oil, both second-generation feedstocks derived from animal processing and food-industry waste. BHP reports a greenhouse gas reduction of approximately 79 per cent compared with very low sulphur fuel oil. The use of waste-derived feedstocks avoids the land-use controversies associated with first-generation biofuels, a key barrier to wider maritime adoption. Charterers and owners across the dry bulk sector are watching the results closely, as the trial represents one of the most credible near-term decarbonisation pathways yet demonstrated at commercial scale.

## **Hundreds of Ships and Seafarers Stranded in the Arabian Gulf**

Several hundred commercial vessels and an estimated 20,000 seafarers remain unable to proceed owing to the Strait of Hormuz closure, with the human and financial costs compounding daily. V.Group chief executive Darren Wardle, speaking at the Posidonia conference in Athens, confirmed the company has 13 ships stranded in the Gulf, roughly half tankers. Before the conflict, approximately 125 vessels transited the strait each day, carrying oil, LNG, grain and dry bulk cargoes critical to global supply chains. Wardle stated that credible safe passage guarantees must be in place before commercial operators can responsibly resume transits, warning that sending crews through without such assurances is not an acceptable option given ongoing safety risks. ## **OPEC+ Approves Fourth Consecutive Output Quota Increase**

Seven core OPEC+ members agreed on Sunday to raise their collective output targets by a further 188,000 barrels per day from July 2026, the fourth consecutive monthly increase since the Strait of Hormuz closure began. Total quota additions since April now amount to nearly 600,000 bpd, but the increases remain largely nominal: actual group production has collapsed from 42.77 million bpd in February to just 33.19 million bpd in April, as Gulf members cannot export freely. Iraq's quota will rise by 26,000 bpd from July. Analysts noted that the continued unwinding of the group's 2023 output cut sets the stage for a potentially rapid market reversal when Hormuz reopens, with the risk of sentiment shifting abruptly from shortage to surplus.

## Iranian Oil Exports Fall to Six-Year Low

Iranian crude oil exports fell to their lowest level in six years during May 2026, with estimates placing shipments at between 209,000 and 260,000 barrels per day, down dramatically from 1.34 million bpd in April and approximately 1.9 million bpd in March. The collapse reflects the US naval blockade in effect since 13 April. Approximately 147 million barrels of Iranian crude are estimated to be held in floating storage globally, with a further 55 to 67 million barrels believed to be stranded in tankers inside the Arabian Gulf itself. The eventual release of this volume into the market, whenever political conditions allow, represents one of the most consequential supply-side variables for global crude price trajectories.

## Russia's Western Crude Exports Hit Eight-Month High

Russian crude oil exports from western loading ports reached an eight-month high in May 2026, rising approximately 15 per cent to 2.5 million barrels per day compared with 2.2 million bpd in April. The increase partly reflects the effect of Ukrainian drone strikes on Russian refinery infrastructure, which has curtailed domestic processing capacity and pushed crude that would normally be refined internally onto the export market. Key terminals contributing to the higher volumes include Primorsk, Ust-Luga and Novorossiysk on Russia's Baltic and Black Sea coasts. While the increase adds some marginal supply to global markets in the context of the Hormuz crisis, these flows predominantly move on shadow fleet vessels and face continuing exposure to Western sanctions enforcement activity.

## US Crude Stocks Draw Down, Exports Near Record

US commercial crude oil inventories fell by 8 million barrels to 433.7 million barrels in the latest weekly data, extending a destocking trend that has removed approximately 63.9 million barrels from storage since the end of February. US crude exports reached 5.9 million barrels per day, the second-highest volume ever recorded, as domestic producers and traders capitalise on elevated global prices driven by the Hormuz disruption. Brent crude was trading at $97.93 per barrel at the time of the report. Strategic Petroleum Reserve volumes also fell by 8 million barrels to their lowest level since January 2024, as the administration continues to use reserve releases as part of its market management strategy.

## Kuwait Outlines Six-to-Eight-Week Recovery Timeline

Kuwaiti officials have stated the country could restore approximately 70 per cent of its oil production capacity within six to eight weeks of the Strait of Hormuz reopening, with the remaining 30 per cent requiring a further month to bring online. Kuwait's domestic refinery infrastructure is expected to return to normal operating capacity within two to three weeks of resumed access. However, the IEA has cautioned that a best-case full recovery of regional flows could take six to eight months, while ADNOC in the UAE suggested complete recovery of regional transit operations could extend until mid-2027. The divergence in these estimates reflects genuine uncertainty about how quickly commercial shipping, insurance markets and commodity trade flows will normalise even after any formal reopening.

## Dangote Refinery Surpasses Nameplate Capacity

Nigeria's Dangote petroleum refinery surpassed its design nameplate capacity of 650,000 barrels per day during a test run, reaching 700,000 bpd, with the company targeting 1.4 million bpd within 30 months. The refinery has been exporting refined products to destinations including other African nations, Europe and the United States. Actual export volumes reached 353,000 bpd in April before easing to 285,000 bpd in May as operations stabilised. The refinery's growing output has significant implications for regional product trade flows in West Africa and could progressively displace imported refined cargoes that currently arrive on LR1 and LR2 product tankers from European and Middle Eastern refineries. # CAPESIZE MARKET

## ATLANTIC AND PACIFIC BASIN

The Capesize freight market opened the week on a firm footing, carrying over the late-May upswing, but sentiment deteriorated steadily and rates fell across all principal iron ore routes in both basins through to the close. Thin trading activity — accentuated by the Posidonia industry event in Athens drawing participants away from their desks — alongside mounting expectations of burgeoning Pacific tonnage supply were the dominant headwinds. The Western Australia–Qingdao route was assessed at USD 16.10/wmt on 02 June, already 70 cents/wmt below the late-May high amid subdued fixing and softer offer levels, with Tubarão–Qingdao at USD 37.55/wmt and Saldanha Bay–Qingdao at USD 28.10/wmt. Pacific enquiry persisted, as Western Australian mining majors sought tonnage for mid-to-late June laycans, though conviction was lacking. The downtrend gathered pace on 03 June, with the miners notably quiet: Western Australia–Qingdao shed a further 95 cents/wmt to USD 15.15/wmt, Tubarão–Qingdao softened to USD 36.75/wmt and Saldanha Bay–Qingdao to USD 27.80/wmt. On 04 June, deteriorating sentiment drove Western Australia–Qingdao down 60 cents/wmt to USD 14.55/wmt — a mining major fixing a Capesize from Dampier to Qingdao at USD 14.60/wmt for the 18–20 June laycan — whilst freight derivative values edged lower and Chinese port congestion remained elevated despite easing weather.

Tubarão–Qingdao held at USD 36.75/wmt and Saldanha Bay–Qingdao eased to USD 27.50/wmt. Tepid trading persisted into 05 June as participants wound down ahead of the weekend amid a dearth of Pacific cargoes. Western Australia–Qingdao slipped a further 65 cents/wmt to USD 13.90/wmt, with a Newcastlemax fixed from Port Walcott to Kaohsiung in the mid-USD 13s/wmt and a Capesize taken from Port Hedland to Qingdao at USD 13.80/wmt. Tubarão–Qingdao fell to USD 36.25/wmt and Saldanha Bay–Qingdao to USD 27.45/wmt, although firmer period interest emerged, with several Capesizes reportedly fixed for periods in excess of one year. Overall, the market closed the week sharply lower, the Pacific bearing the brunt: the Western Australia–Qingdao benchmark fell USD 2.20/wmt across the week amid a soft cargo book, ample prompt tonnage and a weakening forward curve.

# PANAMAX MARKET

## ATLANTIC BASIN

The week proved exceptionally quiet in terms of reported fixtures, largely due to the Posidonia exhibition in Greece, where many of the industry's leading shipowners, charterers and brokers had gathered. Market activity slowed considerably as a result, with only a limited number of transactions emerging whilst participants focused on meetings and negotiations rather than concluding spot business. By midweek, Atlantic fronthaul indications remained broadly stable despite the absence of fresh activity. An **82,000-dwt Kamsarmax [2012]** opening Hamburg and positioning towards Barcarena was said to be seeking around USD 27,500 per day for a long fronthaul voyage. Separately, an **82,000-dwt Kamsarmax [2013]** opening Fujairah and ballasting towards ECSA was fixed for an ECSA–Far East trip at levels reported in the high USD 19,000s per day on a P6 basis. Conditions remained subdued through Thursday, with very few fresh fixtures coming to light. Friday brought a modest uptick in reported activity. A **64,000-dwt Ultramax [2024]** opening Lagos was fixed from South Brazil for redelivery in the Mediterranean/Far East range at USD 19,000 per day plus a USD 900,000 ballast bonus.

In the Kamsarmax segment, an **82,000-dwt vessel [2022]** opening Gibraltar was fixed on a transatlantic run via the US Gulf and Red Sea with grains, redelivering Gibraltar at USD 21,000 per day. An **81,000-dwt Kamsarmax [2011]** was additionally reported fixed on a retro basis from Vizag at an equivalent rate of approximately USD 20,300 per day.

## PACIFIC BASIN

The Pacific market remained relatively firm across the Australian and NOPAC regions this week, though a lower volume of reported fixtures and softer enquiry weighed on sentiment towards the end of the period. Rates eased gradually, particularly in Indonesia where round-voyage levels softened from earlier highs. In Indonesia, coal cargoes to South China and the Philippines generated consistent enquiry through the first half, though sentiment began to soften latterly. A **75,381-dwt vessel [2013]** fixed at USD 21,000 for an Indonesia/South China coal round voyage; a **82,195-dwt Kamsarmax [2022]** secured USD 20,000 for the same route. A **75,038-dwt unit [2013]** concluded at USD 20,000 for Indonesia/Philippines coal, whilst an older **75,026-dwt vessel [2010]** fixed at USD 15,500 for Indonesia/ South China coal. NOPAC sentiment remained well supported, underpinned by steady grain demand and limited prompt tonnage availability, particularly for modern units. An **82,161-dwt Kamsarmax [2025]** fixed at USD 25,000 for a NOPAC grain trip redelivering Singapore–Japan; an **82,057-dwt vessel [2020]** secured USD 25,000 for NOPAC/Japan coal.

An **81,929-dwt unit [2026]** was reported on subjects for NOPAC grain business. On the East Coast Australia market, strong coal demand into Japan and North Asia tightened availability and supported firmer levels, with modern Kamsarmaxes regularly achieving mid-to-high USD 20,000s. An **84,820-dwt vessel [2019]** fixed at USD 27,000 for Australia/Japan coal, as did an **81,000-dwt unit [2015]** on the same route. An **81,487-dwt scrubber-fitted vessel [2020]** fixed at USD 22,500 for Australia/Singapore–Japan coal, matched by a **77,888-dwt unit [2014]** on a similar run.

# SUPRAMAX & HANDYSIZE MARKET

## US GULF / NORTH AMERICA

The US Gulf market remained firm throughout the week across both the Supramax/Ultramax and Handysize segments, though reported activity was limited, largely owing to the Posidonia event combined with other holidays. On Ultramaxes, a 63,000-dwt vessel was rumoured fixed at around USD 28,000 for a petcoke voyage to UK-Continent, duration 25 days WOG. A 61,000-dwt unit was fixed at USD 33,000 for a petcoke trip to Spain. On fronthaul, a 55,000-dwt vessel was reported fixed at around USD 23,000 for a grains trip redelivering Singapore-Japan range, whilst a 56,000-dwt unit was fixed at USD 28,000 for petcoke to India. In the Handysize segment, a 37,000-dwt vessel was reported fixed at USD 14,000 basis DOP Colombia for a trip to North Spain.

## EAST COAST SOUTH AMERICA

Rates in East Coast South America were marginally softer on Handysize tonnage, whilst larger units showed improved levels. Handysize transatlantic rates from East Coast South America to the Eastern Mediterranean were assessed at around USD 20,000 per day. Supramax transatlantic rates from West Africa via East Coast South America to the Continent/Mediterranean were assessed at around USD 18,000 per day, whilst fronthaul business from West Africa via East Coast South America was evaluated at around USD 23,500 per day. On Ultramaxes, transatlantic rates from West Africa via East Coast South America to the Continent/Mediterranean were at around USD 18,500 per day, whilst fronthaul trips from West Africa via East Coast South America to China were assessed at around USD 24,500 per day.

## NORTH EUROPE / CONTINENT

The market saw a modest uptick on fresh forward grain enquiries. August stems from North France to Portugal were rated around USD 15,500 TCE from charterers, against owners seeking USD 17,000. ECSA trips were discounting in the USD 7,000–8,000 range on Handies; the US Gulf estimated in the USD 9,000–10,000 range. Ultramaxes were marginally firmer. Scrap to the Eastern Mediterranean was estimated in the low USD 20,000s on Supramaxes and low-to-mid USD 20,000s on Ultramaxes, with owners reluctant given scarce return cargoes.

## SUPRAMAX & HANDYSIZE MARKET

## MEG / INDIAN OCEAN

Geared tonnage in the Indian Ocean traded quietly through the week, with the south-west monsoon curbing activity and limiting fresh inquiry. Sentiment held broadly stable, though owners maintained firm fixing ideas and resisted discounts, particularly on trips towards the Indian subcontinent. Ex-Persian Gulf, the benchmark 50,000 mt limestone run from Mina Saqr to Paradip firmed steadily, assessed at USD 19.45/mt on 2 June and climbing to USD 21.20/mt by 5 June — a net gain of USD 1.75/mt — underpinned by thin tonnage and limited fixing. Out of South Africa, the 50,000 mt coal route from Richards Bay (RBCT) to Port Qasim held broadly rangebound, peaking at USD 20.40/mt on 4 June before easing to USD 20.25/mt on 5 June, leaving it little changed week on week. Rates were supported by owners' reluctance to redeliver in the region during the monsoon. Late in the week a charterer emerged seeking coverage for a 55,000 mt RBCT/Chittagong–Mongla cargo, July 20–30 laycan. Overall, a stable-to-firmer week for Middle East-origin business, with African coal steady.
