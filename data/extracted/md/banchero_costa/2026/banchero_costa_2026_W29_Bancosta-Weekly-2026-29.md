# COMMENT MARKET REPORT – WEEK 29/2026

# SAUDI ARABIA CRUDE OIL EXPORTS

After a marginal decline in 2024, when global crude oil loadings decreased by -0.2% y-o-y, things picked up in 2025, with full-year volumes increasing by +1.8% y-o-y. In Jan-Jun 2026, however, global crude oil loadings declined by -6.1% y-o-y to 1,016.4 mln tonnes, excluding all cabotage trade, according to vessels tracking data from LSEG. Exports from the Arabian Gulf plunged by -30.7% y-o-y to 299.7 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026, and accounted for 29.5% of global seaborne crude trade, down sharply from the historical norm of about 40%, reflecting the ongoing war in the Persian Gulf. Exports from Russian ports (including oil of Kazakh origin) edged up by +3.5% y-o-y to 117.1 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026, or 11.5% of global trade. From South America, exports surged by +31.5% y-o-y to 137.6 mln t. From the USA, exports increased by +20.5% y-o-y to 107.3 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026. From West Africa, exports decreased by -10.4% y-o-y to 78.2 mln t.

From ASEAN exports jumped by +14.7% y-o-y to 59.8 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026 (this includes trans-shipped Iranian, Russian cargoes). In terms of demand (measured by arrival at discharge ports), the top seaborne importer of crude oil in Jan-Jun 2026 was Mainland China, accounting for 20.5% of global trade. Volumes into China decreased by -14.2% y-o-y to 209.5 mln t in Jan-Jun 2025. Imports into the EU27 up by +1.7% y-o-y to 227.6 mln t, accounting for 22.3% of global trade. To ASEAN, imports decreased by -9.7% y-o-y to 129.8 mln t. Imports to India declined by -0.3% y-o-y to 119.3 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026. To South Korea, imports declined by -18.5% y-o-y to 56.7 mln t. To Japan, imports fell by -22.5% y-o-y to 44.1 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026. Imports into the USA up by +8.9% y-o-y to 62.8 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026. **Saudi Arabia** is the single largest seaborne exporter of crude oil in the world, ahead of Russia, the USA and Iraq. In 2025 it accounted for 14.4% of global seaborne crude oil exports.

In the 12 months of 2025, Saudi seaborne crude oil exports declined by -0.4% to 319.4 mln tonnes, excluding cabotage, after declining by -4.6% y-o-y in 2024. Volumes were still above the 310.2 mln tonnes of 2021. In Jan-Jun 2026, Saudi crude exports declined a further -22.0% y-o-y to 123.5 mln tonnes, from 158.4 mln t in the same period of 2025. About 91.3% of volumes loaded in Saudi Arabia in Jan-Jun 2026 were carried in VLCCs, 6.2% were carried in Suezmaxes, and 2.4% in Aframaxes. These proportions increased in recent years in favour of the largest tankers, as Suezmaxes and Aframaxes were diverted to carry Russian cargoes. The main crude oil export terminals in Saudi Arabia are: Yanbu (67.1 mln tonnes, or 54.4%, in Jan-Jun 2026), Ras Tanura (53.3 mln t, or 43.2%), Ras al Khafji (3.0 mln t, or 2.4%).

In terms of destinations for crude oil shipments from Saudi Arabia, the focus in recent years has clearly shifted to Asia. In Jan-Jun 2026, the top destination was Mainland China, accounting for 21.3% of the total Saudi crude exports, followed by South Korea with 15.1%, ASEAN with 13.1%, India with 12.4%, Japan with 10.8%, Taiwan with 3.5%. Direct shipments to the USA now account for just 2.9% of Saudi exports, and direct shipments to the European Union for just 0.7%. Another 14.7% of Saudi crude exports are shipped to the Ain Sukhna Terminal in Egypt to feed the Sumed pipeline, and will eventually mostly end up in Europe. In Jan-Jun 2026, Saudi Arabia shipped 26.3 mln tonnes of crude to Mainland China, which represents a -29.6% y-o-y decline from 37.4 mln tonnes in Jan-Jun 2025. The second top destination was South Korea, with 18.7 mln t, down by -25.9% y-o-y from 25.2 mln t in Jan-Jun 2025. Shipments from Saudi to ASEAN declined by -9.6% y-o-y in Jan-Jun 2026 to 16.2 mln tonnes. Volumes to India declined by -2.7% y-o-y in Jan-Jun 2026 to 15.3 mln t.

Saudi Arabia also shipped 13.3 mln t this year to Japan, which represents a decline of -47.8% y-o-y from 25.5 mln t in the same period last year. Direct shipments to the EU increased by +79.3% y-o-y in Jan-Jun 2026 to 0.8 mln t, from 0.4 mln t in the same period last year.

BHP Group posted record iron ore output and higher realised prices, despite purchasing restrictions imposed by China Mineral Resources Group amid tense contract talks earlier this year. China's imports of refined copper hit a nine-month high in June, thanks to strong demand and declining domestic supply. The world's largest copper consumer brought in 281,307 metric tons of the metal in June, up 0.5% from the prior month and the highest for a single month since last September, data from the General Administration of Customs showed.

## **Ukraine's Black Sea ports lose a third of grain export capacity, farmers' union says**

Ukraine has lost about a third of its capacity to export grain via its vital Black Sea ports due to intensifying Russian missile and drone attacks, the country's main farmers' union said. More than four years into its war with Russia, agricultural exports like grains and vegetable oils remain Ukraine's biggest source of foreign currency earnings, with more than 90% shipped through three ports in the southern Odesa region.

## **EU grain exporters could see increased demand after Black Sea shipping attacks**

European Union countries could see more grain export demand if attacks on Black Sea ports and ships disrupt Russian and Ukrainian sales, traders and analysts said on Thursday. Wheat prices have surged this week, with U.S. futures reaching two-year highs and Euronext wheat rising to 17-month highs on concerns that attacks on shipping and port infrastructure could hinder exports from the two leading wheat suppliers at a time of tight markets.

## **Brazil's Conab raises 2025/26 grain crop forecast, sees record corn output**

Brazil's total grain crop is expected to reach 360.11 million metric tons in the 2025/26 season, national crop agency Conab said on Tuesday, raising its forecast from the 358.64 million tons projected in June. The new estimate, driven mainly by higher soybean and corn production, would mark a 2.2% increase from the previous season.

## **Russia says harvest shows good potential, farmers will get the fuel they need**

Russia is on track for a good 2026 harvest and the state will ensure that farmers receive the fuel they need to bring in the grain, Agriculture Minister Oksana Lut said on Friday, amid shortages caused by Ukrainian attacks on Russian oil refineries. Russia is the world's largest wheat exporter, making the size and quality of its harvest a key factor for global grain markets.

## **Indian rice export prices extend gain on higher reserve price, monsoon concerns**

Indian rice export prices rose for a third straight week, on a higher reserve price for the commodity sold under the government's Open Market Sale Scheme (OMSS) and concerns that weak monsoon rains could delay planting. India's 5% broken parboiled variety was quoted at $352-$357 per ton the week till Thursday, up from last week's $348-$352. Indian 5% broken white rice was priced at $353-$357.

## **US sugar users ask government to allow more low-tariff imports**

The U.S. Sweetener Users Association asked the U.S. government on Friday to allow more lower-tariff sugar imports, saying domestic supplies fell below the level considered by the administration as adequate. The association said the government can meet the request by reallocating low-tariff import quotas, known as TRQs, to countries able to export sugar to the U.S.

## **US tariffs on Brazil are a bitter pill for sugar and ethanol makers**

Brazil's ethanol and sugar producers on Thursday despaired at the imposition of new 25% tariffs on Brazilian goods by the U.S. government, lamenting a rollback in cooperation between the two countries. The European Commission will propose an overhaul of the EU's Emissions Trading System, allowing industries to emit CO2 longer while offering more financial support to invest in clean technologies in Europe. # CAPESIZE MARKET

## ATLANTIC AND PACIFIC BASIN

The Capesize market ended the week decisively lower across all major iron ore routes, as early-week firmness gave way to sustained losses driven by freight derivative (FFA) values that turned from rangebound to softer, building Pacific tonnage availability and easing sentiment. The week opened on a firmer note. The Western Australia–Qingdao route (170,000 mt iron ore) edged up to USD 13.50/wmt on 13 July, supported by persistent enquiry from the mining majors despite thin cargo volumes and disruption from Typhoon Bavi, which kept several Chinese ports closed. Elevated bunker prices lent additional support. However, as ports reopened and tonnage supply in the Pacific accumulated, the route corrected steadily — to USD 13.35, USD 13.25 and USD 12.40/wmt — before shedding a further 60 cents/wmt to close at USD 11.80/wmt on 17 July, a weekly loss of USD 1.70/wmt. Fixtures on the Dampier and Port Hedland to Qingdao runs confirmed the softening trend, concluding near USD 11.70–11.80/wmt by week's end. The Atlantic proved more resilient early on.

The Tubarao–Qingdao route surged to a weekly high of USD 34.75/wmt on 14 July amid firmer sentiment and owners' resistance for H1 August laycans. The rally faded thereafter as a charterer–owner standoff limited fresh fixtures, and the route retreated to USD 31.95/wmt by 17 July, down USD 1.05/wmt over the week. South Atlantic cargo volumes remained ample into the close. The South Africa route (Saldanha Bay–Qingdao) mirrored this pattern, peaking at USD 25.25/wmt on 14 July before easing to USD 23.90/wmt on 17 July. Overall, activity thinned as the week progressed, compounded by a South Korean public holiday on 17 July. With Pacific tonnage lengthening and the derivatives market under pressure, sentiment closed the week firmly on the back foot.

# PANAMAX MARKET

## ATLANTIC BASIN

The Atlantic market held a firm tone throughout the week, though the volume of reported fixtures remained relatively limited. ECSA continued to dominate activity, with grain cargoes lending consistent support to fronthaul employment into the Far East, whilst the North Atlantic saw only isolated business. Amongst the principal fixtures, a modern 83,000 dwt built 2024, retro Singapore, was fixed via ECSA with redelivery Singapore/Japan at USD 23,000 per day. An 82,000 dwt built 2015, opening Haldia, followed closely at USD 22,100 per day, whilst a further 82,000 dwt built 2022, likewise retro Singapore, secured USD 21,500 per day for the same route. Older tonnage continued to trade at a discount, an 80,000 dwt built 2011 being fixed from Krishnapatnam via ECSA to Singapore/Japan at USD 19,000 per day. In the North Atlantic, activity proved more selective. An 82,000 dwt built 2022, opening Gibraltar, was fixed via NCSA with redelivery Skaw–Gibraltar at USD 26,500 per day on a coal cargo, confirming that transatlantic business continues to command a premium over South Atlantic fronthaul voyages.

Overall, the market remained well supported, with modern Kamsarmaxes fixing around USD 22,000–23,000 per day for ECSA/Far East employment, whilst Atlantic transatlantic business achieved markedly stronger returns.

## PACIFIC BASIN

Asia-Pacific Panamax freight rates trended firmer, with the Pacific basin drawing consistent support from a firm East Coast South America (ECSA) market that kept ballasting west an attractive option and underpinned owners' bullish fixing ideas. Elevated tonnage levels and thin cargo replenishment capped momentum, yet vessel delays and port congestion from a recent typhoon tightened prompt availability early in the week. Freight derivative rates firmed at the start but softened from mid-week, while bunker prices spiked early — amid Middle East tensions — before retreating over the final two sessions. Trading thinned towards the week's end as charterers covered prompt requirements and retreated to the sidelines. Australian coal was cited as the principal driver for Pacific rates. The benchmark run for 75,000 mt (plus/minus 10%) of metallurgical coal from Hay Point to Paradip firmed steadily to USD 21.05/mt by 17 July, from USD 19.85/mt on 13 July, while Hay Point to Qingdao edged up to USD 18.40/mt from USD 17.95/mt.

Fixtures underpinned sentiment: Hay Point/Visakhapatnam was reported at USD 20.80/mt, USD 21.50/mt and USD 22/mt across successive laycans, while an Abbot Point/Visakhapatnam stem fixed at USD 20.35/mt late in the week. Further enquiry emerged ex Abbot Point and Gladstone. Out of Indonesia, the Samarinda– Guangzhou coal trade (65,000 mt) proved choppier, climbing to USD 9.60/mt by 15 July before easing to USD 9.15/mt on 17 July as fresh enquiry dried up. On the Indonesia–India leg, thermal coal from Banjarmasin to Krishnapatnam on India's east coast rose to a mid-week USD 11.60/mt before settling at USD 11.50/mt, with the Mundra run on the west coast at USD 12.75/mt. Several coal stems were worked towards south China and India — including Tanjung Bara, Taboneo and Tanjung Pemancingan liftings — though fresh fixtures remained scarce. Overall, the Pacific closed the week on firm but cooling sentiment, owners resisting discounts against the ballast alternative.

# SUPRAMAX & HANDYSIZE MARKET

## US GULF / NORTH AMERICA
**US GULF / NORTH AMERICA** The US Gulf market saw a slight decrease last week on lower activity in the area, though rates remain firm at present. The petcoke trade to India stays very active, with owners rating high USD 30,000s for a trip to India on Ultramaxes. On transatlantic routes, a 63,000-dwt fixed in the low USD 30,000s for ARAG, while a 61,000-dwt fixed at USD 31,000 for a trip to the West Mediterranean with grains. On handies, market rates remained in the low USD 20,000s for transatlantic on a standard Handysize.

## EAST COAST SOUTH AMERICA
**EAST COAST SOUTH AMERICA** The East Coast South America market appears to have slowed, though rates remain quite high. In the Handysize segment, Argentine coastal voyages fixed USD 27,500 to Chile, USD 22,500 to Brazil and USD 23,500 to Colombia East Coast. Ex Recalada, a 37,000-dwt fixed to Chile at USD 27,000. On longer runs, a 32,000-dwt open Rio Grande secured USD 20,000 to grain to Egypt. China with petcoke, a 38,000-dwt fixed Rio Grande/Vietnam at around USD 21,500, and Handysize voyages Argentina/China around USD 21,500. A 41,000-dwt fixed a fronthaul via ECSA at USD 28,000, while ex North Coast South America a 37,000-dwt secured East Mediterranean with scrap in the low USD 20,000s. Among larger sizes, Ultramaxes fixed around USD 32,000 APS Brazil for Via ECSA ex West Africa, Ultramaxes achieved around USD 28,000 to China and USD 23,000 to the Continent. Supramax transatlantic ECSA runs West Africa/Continent hovered around USD 22,500, with fronthauls to China at USD 27,500.

## NORTH EUROPE / CONTINENT
**NORTH EUROPE / CONTINENT** The Continent and Mediterranean held relatively steady, again with little information reported. On handies, trips to ECSA and USG/USEC remained the usual discounting destinations for owners looking to reposition. Rates to ECSA were estimated around the mid-USD 8,000s (USD 8,500/day), while transatlantic trips back to USG/USEC were around the USD 11,000/day mark. Towards other destinations, a modern 38,000-dwt open Gdansk fixed delivery APS North France for a trip with grains, redelivery West Africa, at USD 14,750/day, with Continent/Mediterranean trips currently covering around similar levels. To conclude, fronthaul on handies was estimated in the mid-to-high teens, with Russian loadings still paying some premium. On larger units, Ultramax fronthaul trips were evaluated around USD 26,000/27,000, showing that the long-haul business remains solid. Similar rates were heard for the usual scrap runs to the Mediterranean. Lastly, transatlantic repositioning trips from the Continent to USG remained at around USD 16,000/17,000/day.

## BLACK SEA / MEDITERRANEAN
**BLACK SEA / MEDITERRANEAN** The Black Sea and Mediterranean saw a very complicated week. In Ukraine, a series of attacks struck port infrastructure and vessels, also in Russian waters; Ukrainians claimed to have hit 116 Russian vessels with drones. Rates naturally rose, as underwriters immediately raised Additional War Risk premiums for the area and owners must pay crew bonuses, among other things. On this alone, handy voyage freights rose at least USD 3/pmt. Some vessels bound to Ukraine to load and discharge cargoes, including fertiliser, anchored outside Ukrainian territorial waters, as some P&I clubs suspended war risk cover for Ukrainian-port voyages. Handy rates for intra-Mediterranean climbed to USD 16,000/day. Transatlantic trips to US Gulf and ECSA improved, reaching USD 9,000/day for ECSA and USD 11,250 to US Gulf. US Gulf now stands at USD 16,500 for Ultramaxes and USD 15,500/16,000 for Supramaxes. # SUPRAMAX & HANDYSIZE MARKET

## MEG / INDIAN OCEAN

Indian Ocean Supramax and Ultramax sentiment held broadly firm over 13–17 July, with Middle East and African cargoes lending support amid otherwise thin fixing activity across the size segments. Out of the Persian Gulf, fixing information remained scant throughout the week, though rates on the limestone trade edged higher. The benchmark run to move 50,000 mt of limestone from Mina Saqr to Paradip on the east coast of India firmed to USD 17.05/mt by 17 July, from USD 16.55/mt on 13 July, having peaked at USD 17.25/mt mid-week. Fresh enquiry surfaced late in the week, with an Indian steel producer seeking to lift a 52,500 mt limestone cargo from Dibba in the UAE to Paradip for 25 July–10 August laycan. From South Africa, the market stayed well-supported on the back of a firm South Atlantic, despite an oversupply of tonnage. Freight to carry 50,000 mt of coal from Richards Bay Coal Terminal to Port Qasim climbed to USD 22.70/mt on 17 July, up from USD 21.75/mt. A 55,000 mt (plus/minus 10%) coal cargo from Richards Bay to Chittagong/Mongla was fixed in the high-USD 27/mt for 21–30 July laycan.

## FAR EAST

Pacific Basin Supramax and Ultramax freight rates ground higher across 13–17 July, though sentiment was tempered by lacklustre cargo demand and thin exchanges. Rates drew support from weather-related disruptions early in the week, which constrained tonnage supply, while surging bunker prices — on concerns over the closure of the Strait of Hormuz — lent further support before easing late in the week. # DEMOLITION SALES

The recycling market remained paused rather than broken this week, with no sales reported across the sub-continent. Two forces restrained tonnage: firmer freight — the Baltic Dry Index closed at 2,944, its highest since 5 June — kept ageing vessels trading, while a reinstated naval blockade of Iranian ports delayed the anticipated Gulf exit queue amid renewed war-risk premiums. Bangladesh topped the rankings but softened, with dry bulk indications of USD 448–453 per LDT; severe flooding around Chattogram slowed beaching to a near-halt, the next meaningful tide window opening 29 July. Pakistan held steady at USD 443–448 per LDT, Gadani plate flat near USD 700 per ton. India was steady at USD 418–423 per LDT, Alang offering the basin's deepest capacity and strongest compliance profile despite the weakest price; local plate firmed toward USD 394. Turkey remained structurally uncompetitive at USD 263–265 per LDT, its Aliaga market defined by EU regulation and Basel Convention compliance rather than price. Overall sentiment stayed cautious. Firmer earnings and blockade disruption continue to defer recycling candidates, leaving cash buyers with thin enquiry and yards awaiting dry ground.
