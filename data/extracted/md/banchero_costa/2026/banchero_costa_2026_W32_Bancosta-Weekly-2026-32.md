# COMMENT MARKET REPORT – WEEK 32/2026

# EUROPEAN UNION COAL IMPORTS

In Jan-Jul 2026, global seaborne coal loadings increased by +3.0% y-o-y to 756.8 mln t (excluding cabotage), based on vessel tracking data from AXS Marine. In Jan-Jul 2026, exports from Indonesia declined by -1.6% y-o-y to 261.8 mln tonnes, whilst from Australia were up by +4.5% y-o-y to 201.9 mln t. From Russia exports increased by +10.4% y-o-y to 112.6 mln t in Jan-Jul 2026, from the USA by +9.4% y-o-y to 49.3 mln t, from South Africa +10.7% y-o-y to 40.7 mln t. Shipments from Colombia were up by +9.3% y-o-y to 28.9 mln t in Jan-Jul 2026, from Canada down by -3.4% y-o-y to 27.0 mln t, and from Mozambique broadly flat at -0.8% y-o-y to 11.6 mln t. Seaborne coal imports into Mainland China declined by -3.7% y-o-y to 191.0 mln t in Jan-Jul 2026. Imports to India declined by -7.8% y-o-y to 133.6 mln t, to Japan increased by +4.8% y-o-y to 91.2 mln t in Jan-Jul 2026, to South Korea up by +20.3% y-o-y to 67.9 mln t, whilst to Vietnam increased +8.6% y-o-y to 44.0 mln t.

The European Union is now the sixth largest seaborne importer of coal in the world, after China, India, Japan, South Korea and Vietnam. In 2025, the EU accounted for 4.8% of global seaborne coal shipments, rising to 5.1% in Jan-Jul 2026. The EU's seaborne coal imports softened further in 2025, declining by -2.6% y-o-y to 63.7 mln tonnes, from 65.4 mln tonnes in 2024, as Europe progressively abandoned coal as a source of energy and embraced natural gas and renewables. Now, however, in Jan-Jul 2026, the EU imported 38.5 mln tonnes, up +5.5% y-o-y from 36.5 mln tonnes, interrupting that downtrend. Looking at individual member states, the Netherlands is by far the largest single point of entry, accounting for 47.5% of the EU's seaborne coal imports in Jan-Jul 2026 at 18.3 mln tonnes, up +17.5% y-o-y, although a large share of these volumes is transhipped onwards to inland European end-users via Rotterdam and Amsterdam rather than consumed domestically. Poland followed with 4.6 mln tonnes in Jan-Jul 2026, up +22.2% y-o-y, and Germany with 4.0 mln tonnes, down -6.3% y-o-y, after a +23.3% y-o-y increase to 7.5 mln t in 2025.

Italy recorded the sharpest fall amongst the majors, down -25.1% y-o-y to just 1.5 mln tonnes in Jan-Jul 2026, following a -17.2% y-o-y decline to 3.3 mln t in 2025. In terms of sources of the shipments, Europe was heavily dependant on Russia, but this drastically changed after the war in Ukraine — although Russian volumes have since been recovering. In 2024, 5.9% of the EU's seaborne coal imports were sourced from Russian ports, which also includes Kazakh coal shipped through Russian ports, rising to 7.5% in 2025. In Jan-Dec 2025, coal imports to the EU from Russian ports increased by +23.2% y-o-y to 4.8 mln tonnes. In Jan-Jul 2026, imports from Russian ports rose further, by +28.8% y-o-y to 3.8 mln t, or 9.9% of the EU's total coal imports.

The most important supplier to Europe in Jan-Jul 2026 was Australia, accounting for 27.5% of Europe's imports. In 2024, volumes from Australia stood at 19.3 mln t, but in 2025 they corrected down by -9.2% y-o-y to 17.5 mln t. In Jan-Jul 2026, coal volumes from Australia to the EU increased by +21.3% y-o-y to 10.6 mln t. The second largest supplier to Europe is the USA, accounting for 27.3% of the EU's imports in Jan-Jul 2026. In 2024, imports from the USA stood at 17.9 mln t, and in 2025 edged up +1.2% y-o-y to 18.1 mln t. In Jan-Jul 2026, volumes from the USA to the EU declined by -2.8% y-o-y to 10.5 mln t. In third place was Colombia, with a 13.8% share. In Jan-Jul 2026, volumes from Colombia to the EU declined by -3.3% y-o-y to 5.3 mln t.

# COMMODITY NEWS – DRY BULK

## **China July iron ore imports slide from June as thinning steel margins curb appetite**

China's iron ore imports in July slipped by 4% from the prior month, as some domestic steelmakers began equipment maintenance amid shrinking steel margins, curbing appetite for the key steelmaking ingredient. The world's largest consumer of the raw material brought in 108.09 million metric tons of iron ore in July, data from the General Administration of Customs showed on Friday.

## **More workers join strike at BHP's Port Hedland iron ore operations**

More workers joined a strike at BHP's Port Hedland operations in Western Australia on Sunday, a union spokesperson said, in the first major industrial action at the iron-ore export hub in a quarter-century. Sunday's 24-hour stoppage by the three unions of the Combined BHP Ports Unions, which represents some of BHP's workforce of more than 800 people at the port, followed a 24-hour ship-loading halt on Saturday.

## **Depletion of iron ore mines will underpin next decade's prices, Rio Tinto executive says**

Supply pressure stemming from the depletion of iron ore mines built earlier this century, such as those in Australia, is set to underpin the iron ore market and prices over the coming decade, a Rio Tinto executive said on Wednesday. Rio expects to invest more than $13 billion on new mines, plant and equipment in the Pilbara region from 2025 to 2027 while estimating that 800 million tonnes needs to be added globally across the next decade to maintain supply.

## **Surge in Black Sea attacks adds to strain on global commodity flows**

A surge in attacks on ships, ports and export terminals in the Black Sea is disrupting global supplies of grain and oil, turning the region into the latest strategic trade chokepoint to be hit by escalating conflict. The Black Sea is a vital route for shipments of grain, crude oil and refined products. Its waters are shared by Russia and Ukraine as well as Bulgaria, Georgia, Romania and Turkey.

## **Ukraine turns to alternative grain export routes, counts losses as Russia blocks ports**

Alternative grain export routes from Ukraine will reach required capacity at the end of August at the earliest, its agriculture minister said, warning they would cover only half of volumes handled by Black Sea ports disrupted by Russian attacks. Russia has intensified missile and drone attacks on foreign-flagged civilian vessels in the southern Odesa port hub, which handles most of Ukraine's grain shipments. The situation endangers exports of around half of this year's grain and oilseeds forecast total, Taras Vysotsky told Reuters.

## **Argentina strikes deal with maritime pilots, easing grain export bottleneck**

Argentina's government said on Tuesday it had reached a deal with maritime pilots to cut their fees by 20%, clearing the way for ship movements to resume after a stoppage that disrupted grain exports from one of the world's top agricultural suppliers. The agreement should ease congestion at ports along the Parana River, a crucial artery for exports of soy, corn and wheat, after business groups warned that dozens of vessels had been affected by the dispute.

## **China July soybean imports drop on sow herd size concerns**

China's July soybean imports fell 1.6% from a year earlier to 11.48 million metric tons, data from the General Administration of Customs showed on Friday. The country's oilseed import volume was down from a high base from last year's surge in buying of Brazilian beans amid the trade war with the U.S.

## **China buys at least 10 more cargoes of US soybeans, traders say**

China has bought at least another 10 cargoes of U.S. soybeans, three Asian traders said on Thursday, the latest in a flurry of purchases ahead of President Xi Jinping's expected U.S. visit next month. State-run buyer Sinograin purchased 10 to 15 cargoes on Wednesday, the traders said. At least 10 cargoes were for October-November shipment, one trader said.

## **India sugar prices hit record high on tight supplies, festival demand**

Indian sugar prices have jumped 10% over the past month to a record high and are expected to remain elevated for at least the next three months as supplies tighten and festival demand gathers pace, traders and industry officials told Reuters. Rising prices will add to retail inflation that breached the central bank's target for the first time in 17 months in June, and prompt New Delhi to take further measures to curb prices after banning exports and imposing stock limits last month. ## **Yemen's Houthis attack Saudi refinery after kingdom signs defence pact**

Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis have attacked Saudi Aramco's Jazan refinery, they said on Sunday, two days after the kingdom signed a defence pact with Turkey and Pakistan in response to growing regional instability from the U.S.-Israeli war on Iran. The alliance with Sunni Muslim U.S. allies, Turkey and Pakistan, is intended to strengthen collective deterrence against any act of aggression and stipulates that an armed attack against any of the three would be regarded as an attack on all.

## **Iran ties Hormuz reopening to US concessions on several demands**

Iran said it was nearing a final pact with Oman defining new shipping lanes between them through the Strait of Hormuz but repeated that the U.S. must meet other conditions, including compensation and an end to sanctions and military threats, before the strategic waterway is reopened. A U.S. official told Reuters on Friday that Iran and Oman were close to a deal that could pave the way for restoring safe passage through the strait, a major conduit for oil and gas shipping before Iran blocked Hormuz in response to U.S. and Israeli attacks on Tehran that began in late February.

## **Vessel traffic through Hormuz dwindles this week as markets watch Iran-Oman talks**

Shipping traffic through the Strait of Hormuz has dwindled to 33 vessels from Monday to Thursday this week, data showed, versus 50 in the week-ago period, as markets watched talks between Iran and Oman for signs of progress in reopening the key waterway. Four vessels transited the strait on Thursday, including a very large crude carrier, Nissos Kea, carrying about 2 million barrels of Basrah crude loaded in Iraq, Kpler data showed.

## **US is set to import most Middle East crude since Iran war began**

U.S. imports of Middle Eastern crude are set to hit about 600,000 barrels per day in August, the highest since the Iran war began, as a brief opening of the Strait of Hormuz and the rerouting of Saudi oil through the Suez Canal pushed barrels toward American ports, ship-tracking data showed. Nearly a dozen ships loaded with Middle Eastern crude were headed to U.S. ports as American refiners and traders moved swiftly to snap up barrels that exited the strait when a memorandum of understanding signed by U.S. and Iran in June helped release vessels from the maritime choke point.

## **Gulf oil exports steady in July, still 40% below pre-war mark**

Gulf countries' crude oil and condensate exports were largely steady in July and remained about 40% below pre-war levels, shipping data showed, with signs of a slowdown emerging in the second half of the month as fighting in the region intensified again. The relatively stable export levels have helped ease concerns about a sharper supply disruption and offset a drawdown in global oil inventories.

## **Sinopec steps up Russian oil imports to offset Mideast supply cuts, traders and tracker say**

China's state-owned Sinopec Corp, the world's biggest refiner, has ramped up purchases of Far East Russian oil to compensate for Middle East supplies diminished by the Iran war, according to multiple trade sources and ship tracking data. Sinopec's purchases of Russian oil, which is cheaper than rival grades from sources such as Brazil and West Africa, have helped it maintain relatively stable throughput and ship surplus fuel on strong export margins, despite China limiting overseas sales of fuel products from March to protect domestic supply amid the war-related trade disruptions.

## **Russian July seaborne oil product exports drop 33% m/m, data from sources shows**

Russia's seaborne oil products exports fell by a third in July month-on-month to about 3.9 million metric tons, reflecting a drop in fuel production following Ukrainian attacks on Russian refineries and export restrictions, according to market sources and LSEG data. Russia introduced a temporary ban on diesel exports last month to secure domestic supplies and later extended the measure until the end of August for producers, as fuel shortages in the local market drove wholesale prices higher.

## **Middle East buyers interested in LNG cargoes from Canada, Pacific Energy says**

Buyers in the Middle East are looking to secure liquefied natural gas from Canada as a hedge against geopolitical turmoil, the president of the company that majority-owns Canada's Woodfibre LNG project said. Ratnesh Bedi, president of Singapore-based Pacific Energy — which owns a 70% stake in the Woodfibre project currently under construction on Canada's Pacific Coast near Squamish, B.C. — said in an interview that his company is fielding inquiries from Middle Eastern buyers even though the project's 2.1 million tonnes per year of capacity is already fully contracted to BP. # CAPESIZE MARKET

## ATLANTIC AND PACIFIC BASIN

The Capesize market advanced strongly over the week, building on the late-July recovery as typhoon-related concerns and robust iron ore demand tightened tonnage supply across both basins. Rates surged through midweek before steadying, closing markedly higher on all major iron ore routes. The Pacific led the rally. The Western Australia–Qingdao route (170,000 mt iron ore) jumped USD 1.35/wmt to USD 14.35/wmt on 3 August as an approaching typhoon expected to affect central China raised vessel-delay concerns, with a rising number of mining majors seeking prompt tonnage. Momentum accelerated to USD 15.00/wmt on 4 August and a weekly high of USD 16.20/wmt on 5 August, driven by typhoon-induced scheduling inefficiencies and a shrinking fleet around the tight laycan window. The route then eased 30 cents/wmt to USD 15.90/wmt on 6 August and held there on 7 August as activity turned rangebound, for a net weekly gain of USD 1.55/wmt. The Atlantic firmed in tandem. The Tubarao–Qingdao route climbed from USD 34.60/wmt on 3 August to USD 36.10/wmt on 5 August amid a flurry of Brazil-to-Qingdao fixtures for early-September loading dates and firmer freight derivative (FFA) values.

It edged down to USD 36.00/wmt on 6 August and was unchanged on 7 August, up USD 1.40/wmt over the week, with offers heard around USD 37/wmt for mid-September dates. North Atlantic fronthaul activity was also brisk. The South Africa route (Saldanha Bay–Qingdao) tracked the trend, rising to a weekly high of USD 26.40/wmt on 5 August before easing to close at USD 26.25/wmt on 7 August, a net gain of 85 cents/wmt. Overall, the market held firm into the weekend. With typhoon disruption still in focus and tonnage fundamentals tight, sentiment remained well supported despite the midweek plateau.

# PANAMAX MARKET

## ATLANTIC BASIN

The Atlantic remained well supported through the week, with a steady flow of reported activity across both the South and North Atlantic. ECSA remained the main area of interest, particularly for fronthaul grain business into Singapore/Japan, whilst NCSA activity also showed strength, with transatlantic and Continent/Mediterranean employment commanding premium levels. Period interest remained healthy, especially for modern kamsarmax tonnage. In ECSA, an 82k dwt (2026) fixed delivery Singapore for ECSA/Far East at USD 24,500/day, whilst an 82k dwt (2022), retro New Mangalore, achieved USD 23,500/day for ECSA/Singapore-Japan grains. An 81k dwt (2014) fixed the same route from Singapore at around USD 20,000/day, with an 82k dwt (2007), retro Hazira, securing USD 19,500/day and an 81k dwt (2012) from Haldia reported at USD 18,500/day, showing the discount still applied to older units. NCSA also remained firm, with an 81k dwt (2016) fixed from NCSA to Continent/Gibraltar with grains at USD 35,000/day, whilst a similar-sized unit on NCSA/ECSA business achieved around USD 21,500/day plus a USD 550,000 ballast bonus.

In the North Atlantic, an 82k dwt (2023) fixed from Tilbury to Liverpool at USD 24,000/day and a 75k dwt (2009) from La Coruna achieved USD 25,000/day for USEC/Skaw-Gibraltar coal.

## PACIFIC BASIN

The Asia-Pacific Panamax market firmed over the week on largely sentiment-driven support, despite choppy day-to-day movements. After a slow start on Monday 3 August, rates advanced as a strengthening east coast South America market, rising freight derivative values and an approaching typhoon (Dolphin) tightened Pacific tonnage and left owners reluctant to discount. Tonnage levels fell week on week, reinforcing the firm tone, although a lengthy Indonesian tonnage list and lacklustre fresh cargo replenishment tempered gains. Rates eased on Friday 7 August as activity thinned into the close, though sentiment held optimistic, with more southerly-positioned ships expected to ballast towards ECSA. On the key Australian coking coal routes, Hay Point-Qingdao firmed to USD 18.35/mt by 7 August, up from USD 17.95/mt the preceding Friday, having peaked at USD 18.40/mt. Hay Point-Paradip closed at USD 20.80/mt, off a mid-week high of USD 21.20/mt. The Indonesian thermal coal trade also gained, Samarinda-Guangzhou rising to USD 9.15/mt from USD 8.75/mt a week earlier. Fixtures featured an 80,000 mt Dalrymple Bay-Visakhapatnam metallurgical coal cargo at USD 22.65/mt.

Bunker prices were volatile.

# SUPRAMAX & HANDYSIZE MARKET

## MEG / INDIAN OCEAN

Indian Ocean Supramax and Ultramax sentiment stayed under pressure over **3–7 August**, as insufficient demand, a lack of outbound activity from the Indian coasts and limited iron ore movement kept a lid on rates. Many participants adopted a wait-and-watch approach amid geopolitical uncertainty, while bunker prices swung sharply through the week. Out of the Persian Gulf, the limestone trade softened after a brief mid-week lift. Freight to move 50,000 mt of limestone from Mina Saqr to Paradip on the east coast of India eased to USD 16.85/mt by 7 August, from USD 17.10/mt on 3 August, having firmed to USD 17.25/mt on 4 August before retreating. Fixing information remained scant to muted throughout. From South Africa, the coal trade was choppy but broadly rangebound amid soft sentiment and thin activity. Freight to carry 50,000 mt of coal from Richards Bay Coal Terminal to Port Qasim slipped to USD 21.90/mt on 7 August, from USD 22/mt on 3 August, fluctuating within a USD 21.80–22.25/mt band.

Fresh enquiry emerged late in the week, with a 60,000 mt (plus/minus 10%) manganese ore stem from Port Elizabeth/Coega to China worked for 20–23 August laycan.

## FAR EAST

Pacific Basin Supramax and Ultramax freight rates came under pressure over **3–7 August**, as thin exchanges, a dearth of fresh enquiry and a sharply correcting South East Asian market weighed on sentiment. Some support was anticipated from Typhoon Dolphin, which participants expected to constrain tonnage availability in the Far East, while a wait-and-watch mood prevailed amid geopolitical uncertainty. Bunker prices swung sharply through the week. On the key Indonesia–China thermal coal trade, freight to move a 55,000 mt coal cargo from East Kalimantan to Guangzhou eased to USD 11.85/mt by 7 August, from USD 12.30/mt on 3 August, as Indonesian coal demand stayed lacklustre. Several stems were worked towards south China, including Muara Berau and Tanjung Kampeh liftings, though fresh fixtures were scarce and Australian enquiry showed only a modest pick-up. In the Handysize segment, Pacific rates were mixed to softer. Freight to carry 30,000 mt of alumina from Gladstone, East Coast Australia, to Lianyungang held at USD 35.60/mt on 7 August, having peaked at USD 36.35/mt on 4 August, while the Bunbury/Kwinana–Lianyungang run eased to USD 33.80/mt.

# NEWBUILDING ORDERS

Newbuilding activity remained firm this week, with 49 new orders reported despite the summer holiday period. China once again dominated contracting, while Greek owners remained particularly active. Tanker activity was led by strong interest in Aframax LR2 tonnage, with several owners adding modern units at Chinese yards, while containership ordering remained healthy across the feeder and larger segments. Dry bulk activity was concentrated at the upper end of the size range, with Newcastlemax and 210,000+ dwt vessels benefiting from long-term iron ore employment. In the tanker sector, Greek owner Centrofin Management reportedly placed an order for 6x 114,000 dwt Aframax LR2 tankers at Titan Wind, with the series estimated at around USD 420-480 million in total, equivalent to approximately USD 70-80 million per vessel. The IMO Tier III-compliant vessels will be capable of carrying both crude oil and refined petroleum products, with deliveries scheduled between 2028 and 2031. Greek owner Arcadia Shipmanagement also added 3x 114,000 dwt tankers at Hengli H.I., bringing its total number of orders at the yard to nine, with deliveries scheduled between 2028 and 2029.

Meanwhile, China Merchants Energy Shipping, through its Hong Kong subsidiary Hai Hong Shipping, ordered 5x 114,000 dwt Aframax LR2s at Dalian Shipbuilding Industry at approximately USD 73.6 million each, with deliveries between 2029 and 2031. The MR product tanker segment also remained active, with Danish owner Torm reportedly ordering 6x 50,000 dwt MR2 tankers at Zhoushan Changhong for around USD 46 million each, with deliveries from 2029 through 2031. Options for a further two vessels remain in place, although BRL notes that these are not formally confirmed. Greek owner Nautilus Management also placed its first tanker order, for 1x MR1 products carrier at Wuhu Shipyard, with an option for one additional unit. Pricing and further contractual details were not disclosed. The container sector continued to generate significant activity. Greek owner Minerva Marine reportedly returned to Hengli H.I. for a series of 3,100 teu containerships, representing a new design introduced by the yard. The number of vessels was not disclosed by the owner and BRL has estimated the order at 2 units plus 2 options.

The order follows Minerva's recent booking of 4x 6,000 teu containerships at Hengli's Dalian Shipyard. South Korean owner CK Line also ordered 2x 6,400 teu containerships at Huangpu Wenchong, with each vessel offering 1,150 reefer slots, while options remain for a further two units. In the dry bulk sector, activity was focused on larger tonnage. South Korean owner Polaris Shipping reportedly placed an order for 4x 210,000 dwt bulk carriers at Qingdao Beihai, priced at approximately USD 105 million each for delivery in 2030. The vessels are understood to be backed by long-term charters to Vale for Brazilian iron ore transportation and will feature ethanol fuelling. Norwegian owner New Yangtze Navigation also ordered 2x 212,000 dwt bulk carriers at Zhoushan Changhong International Shipyard for around USD 80 million each, with deliveries in 2029. Elsewhere, a Norwegian owner reportedly contracted 2x 211,000 dwt Newcastlemax bulk carriers at Tangshan Dajin Offshore Engineering for approximately USD 70 million each, with delivery scheduled in 2029.

Elsewhere, MSC Berhad reportedly ordered 1x 18,700 cu.m. LNG carrier at Hudong-Zhonghua, backed by a proposed ten-year charter to Malaysia LNG and intended for service between Malaysia and Sendai. Overall, the week's activity confirms continued appetite for modern, fuel-efficient tonnage, with owners across the tanker, container and dry bulk sectors continuing to commit to Chinese yards despite the seasonal slowdown.
