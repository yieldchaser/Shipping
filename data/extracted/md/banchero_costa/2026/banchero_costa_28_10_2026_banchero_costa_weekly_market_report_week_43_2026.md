# Banchero Costa Weekly Market Report - Week 43, 2026

## Weekly Macro Insight: Indonesian Coal Exports

In Jan-Sep 2026, global seaborne coal loadings increased by +1.1% y-o-y to 976.0 mln t (excluding cabotage), based on vessel tracking data from AXS Marine.
In Jan-Sep 2026, exports from Indonesia declined by -6.3% y-o-y to 332.6 mln tonnes, whilst from Australia were up by +3.9% y-o-y to 264.0 mln t.
From Russia exports increased by +10.0% y-o-y to 145.8 mln t in Jan-Sep 2026, from the USA increased by +10.4% y-o-y to 64.0 mln t, from S Africa by +9.4% y-o-y to 51.7 mln t.
Shipments from Colombia were up by +12.6% y-o-y to 39.1 mln t in Jan-Sep 2026, from Canada marginally down by -0.4% y-o-y to 34.7 mln t, and from Mozambique up by +1.5% y-o-y to 16.1 mln t.

Seaborne coal imports into Mainland China declined by -5.0% y-o-y to 250.7 mln t in Jan-Sep 2026.

Imports to India declined by -9.2% y-o-y to 166.1 mln t, to Japan increased by +1.9% y-o-y to 118.6 mln t in Jan-Sep 2026, to S Korea up by +14.0% y-o-y to 90.1 mln t.
To the EU imports were up by +4.3% y-o-y to 49.6 mln t in Jan-Sep 2026, whilst to Vietnam volumes increased by +8.4% y-o-y to 53.3 mln tonnes.
Imports to Malaysia declined by -1.4% y-o-y to 29.8 mln t, whilst to Bangladesh they increased by +16.5% y-o-y to 14.1 mln t.

**Indonesia** is the world’s largest seaborne exporter of coal,

accounting for 34.1% of the global seaborne coal market so far in 2026. Export volumes from Indonesia were relatively depressed during 2020 and 2021, due to disruption from Covid-19 and from government policies favouring domestic consumption, but bounced back to an all-time record high in 2022, and continued to grow further in 2023 and 2024, before correcting in 2025.

Total seaborne coal exports from Indonesia in 2023 reached 500.2 mln tonnes, +10.8% y-o-y, according to AXS Marine vessel tracking data.
In 2024, coal exports from Indonesia increased further by +7.4% y-o-y to 537.3 mln tonnes.

In 2025, however, exports from Indonesia declined by -8.0% y-o-y to 494.2 mln tonnes, and they were down a further -6.3% y-o-y in Jan-Sep 2026 to 332.6 mln t.
The vast majority of Indonesian coal exports are loaded in East Kalimantan and South Kalimantan (the island of Borneo), with some volumes also shipped from southern Sumatra island.

The main coal export terminals in Indonesia are:

Taboneo/Banjarmasin (58.0 mln t loaded in Jan-Sep 2026), Bunati (31.5 mln t), Balikpapan (30.3 mln t), Tanjung Bara (28.8 mt), Samarinda (24.6 mt), Muara Banyuasin (23.0 mt), Muara Pantai (21.4 mln t), Muara Berau (17.6 mt), Adang Bay (13.4 mt), Tanjung Pemancingan

(12.3 mt), Tarahan (9.3 mln t), Sangkulirang (8.5 mt), Kaliorang (8.5 mt), Tarakan Island (6.9 mt).

The majority (54.0%) of coal volumes from Indonesia in 2025 were loaded on Panamax vessels, with 27.3% on Supramax vessels, 9.8% on Post-Panamax, and just 7.0% on Capesize tonnage.

Mainland China continues to be the top importer of Indonesian seaborne coal exports, accounting for 37.7% of shipments in Jan-Sep 2026.
In Jan-Sep 2026, shipments from Indonesia to Mainland China declined by -6.8% y-o-y to 125.5 mln tonnes.
About 17.6% of exports, or 58.6 mln tonnes in Jan-Sep 2026, were shipped to India, representing a correction of -20.4% y-o-y.
Shipments to the Philippines, on the other hand, increased by +2.6% y-o-y in Jan-Sep 2026 to 28.6 mln tonnes, with the Philippines accounting for 8.6% of Indonesian exports.
Exports from Indonesia to Vietnam increased by +16.1% y-o-y in Jan-Sep 2026 to 21.6 mln t, with Vietnam accounting for 6.5%.
Malaysia imported 18.7 mln t of coal from Indonesia in Jan-Sep 2026, down -3.7% y-o-y.

Japan received 17.8 mln tonnes, down -4.0% y-o-y. Exports to South Korea declined by -19.9% y-o-y to 16.1 mln t. Shipments to Taiwan, CN, dropped by -26.0% y-o-y to 8.8 mln t in Jan-Sep 2026.

# China's state iron ore buyer agrees new annual supply contract with Rio Tinto

China's state iron ore buyer has agreed a new supply deal for the key steelmaking ingredient with Rio Tinto, people with knowledge of the matter said. China Mineral Resources Group (CMRG) verbally notified some steel mills of the agreement on Wednesday without giving further details, they said. CMRG will start on Thursday to collect information on volumes that each mill needs to buy from the miner, two of the people said.

# China's tug-of-war with Australian miners lurks beneath iron ore calm

But beneath the calm picture presented by imports of the main steel raw material is a tug-of-war between China, which buys about 75% of global seaborne supplies, and Australia's miners, which provide around two-thirds of China's needs. Beijing is attempting to gain more control of iron ore pricing, seeking to leverage its buying power to secure favourable terms. The problem is that Australian miners such as Rio Tinto, BHP Group and Fortescue have considerable market power of their own as China has no alternative sources that supply the volume of required iron ore.

# US tariff war is fueling Vietnam's coal boom

A years-long trade war between the United States and China has helped shift manufacturing investment, factory output and export production into Vietnam, driving the Southeast Asian nation's coal-fired electricity generation to record highs. Vietnam's coal plants generated 132.9 terawatt hours (TWh) of electricity during the first nine months of 2026, which is a 15% jump from the same months last

year and the highest for that period on record, according to data from Ember.

# India stays net finished steel importer in April-September, government data shows

India remained a net importer of finished steel in the first half of the fiscal year as imports grew faster than domestic production, government data showed on Friday. Finished steel imports jumped 23.8% from a year earlier to 4.14 million metric tons during April-September, exceeding exports of 3.55 million tons. China and South Korea together accounted for nearly 63% of imports, while the United Arab Emirates and Vietnam were India's largest export destinations.

# Nickel Industries says Indonesia cuts coming as Chinese producers weigh steep reductions

Australia-listed Nickel Industries says nickel producers in the Indonesia Morowali Industrial Park (IMIP), a key production base for the metal, will coordinate on output cuts as Chinese operators weigh steep reductions. "There will be coordinated cuts across all of the operations within the IMIP," Managing Director Justin Werner told analysts on an earnings call, without giving a percentage. "Certainly what we're seeing is the non-integrated producers ... are the ones that are going to suffer the hardest here."

# Latvia emerges as top-five country used to ship Russian grain

Latvia, a member of the European Union and NATO, has emerged as one of the top five countries used to ship Russian grain this marketing season despite strained political ties, as Moscow redirects exports from the Azov-Black Sea basin to Baltic ports. Some 431,700 metric tons of

Russian grain were shipped via Latvia between July and October, consultancy ProZerno said. The Latvian foreign ministry said in a statement that Russian grain imports have been banned in Latvia since 2024 and that while there is no transit ban in place yet, the government is working on that as either an EU or a national measure.

# China snaps up Australian canola as improved ties, lower tariffs fuel demand

Chinese oilseed buyers have purchased around 1 million metric tons of Australian canola in recent deals, as improved Beijing-Canberra ties, lower tariffs and stronger processing margins boost demand, three Asia-based traders told Reuters. The cargoes, for arrival in early 2027, were bought at about $695 to $705 per ton, including cost and freight (C&F) to China, said one of the sources with direct knowledge of the deals.

# Global sugar market supply deficit to grow sharply

The global sugar market supply deficit, or the gap between production and demand, was projected on Tuesday to grow to 6.28 million tons, 3 million tons more than a previous estimate, according to market analyst Green Pool Commodities. The analyst blamed poor production in major sugar-making regions like Brazil, India and the European Union for the revised outlook, as global agriculture struggles to cope with impacts from the El Niño weather anomaly.

# Russian energy officials urge Europe to reconsider ban on Russian gas imports

Senior Russian political and energy officials urged the European Union on Thursday to reconsider its plans to ban imports of Russian LNG, arguing the bloc's low storage levels had put Europe in a precarious position. The EU plans to halt imports of Russian gas completely by the start of next year in response to the war in Ukraine. However, those plans have been put in jeopardy due to unusually low gas stocks, while the European Court of Auditors said last month that the bloc was not investing enough to achieve its aims of fully replacing Russian energy with diversified fossil fuel supplies.

# UK approves long-delayed Jackdaw gas field on energy security grounds

Britain on Wednesday approved the Jackdaw North Sea gas field, ending years of legal and political wrangling as the Labour government seeks to balance its climate goals with energy security concerns. Energy minister Miatta Fahnbulleh said the project would begin supplying gas by the end of this month, helping ease energy costs that have surged during the Iran war. Jackdaw, at its planned peak production of 40,000 barrels of oil equivalent per day, would cover just under 4% of Britain's daily gas demand, according to Reuters calculations based on government data.

# Kinder Morgan to invest $4.7 billion in two gas pipeline projects as demand surges

US pipeline operator Kinder Morgan said on Wednesday it has approved a total of $4.7 billion as its share of investments in two major natural gas pipeline joint ventures, citing rising demand from power plants, data centers and LNG exporters. The projects helped lift Kinder Morgan's natural-gas project backlog to a record level, underscoring the

industry's efforts to expand infrastructure to serve the new demand. Kinder Morgan would invest about $3.1 billion in Southern Natural Gas' Southeast Connector project and $1.6 billion in Natural Gas Pipeline Company of America's (NGPL) Permian Link project.

# Europe's benchmark hits €90/MWh for first time since December 2022

Europe's benchmark gas contract traded above €90 per megawatt hour (MWh) for the first time since December 2022 on Tuesday amid increased global competition for supplies tightened by the US-Iran war ahead of winter. The Dutch front-month contract at the TTF hub, Europe's benchmark, hit an intraday high of €90.16/MWh, or $29.62 per million British thermal units, its highest level since December 22, 2022, ICE data showed.

# EU still committed to keeping oil, coal and gas in ground in Arctic

The European Union remains committed to "ensuring that oil, coal and gas stay in the ground, including in Arctic regions," according to a new Arctic strategy presented by the European Commission and EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas on Tuesday. "Due to its specific vulnerabilities, the Commission will work with partners to protect the Arctic environment and prevent all hydrocarbon reserve developments that pose a threat to the Arctic ecosystem," the document also says.

# Qatari electricity lifeline for Lebanon includes two power plants, LNG supply deal

Qatar plans to construct two power plants in Lebanon over the next three years and sign a 20-year liquefied natural gas supply deal under a proposal to revive the ailing Lebanese power sector, a document seen by Reuters showed. Lebanon's energy sector is at the centre of the country's financial and economic

malaise, contributing tens of billions of dollars to the national debt since the country's 1975-1990 civil war even as it failed to deliver 24/7 power.

# Northwest Europe gas storage may remain low for winter

Northwest European gas storage is likely to remain low for winter despite stronger Norwegian production unless Middle East LNG supplies pick up, Goldman Sachs said on Monday. Norwegian gas exports to NW Europe have climbed back to the 2022 highs, analysts at the bank said in a note.

# EQT to buy Range Resources in $13 billion deal to create top US gas producer

EQT said on Monday it will buy Range Resources in a $13 billion all-stock deal to create the largest US natural gas producer, betting on rising demand from LNG exports and power generation. The deal combines two major Appalachian Basin producers, giving EQT greater scale and infrastructure to serve utilities, LNG exporters and industrial customers.

# IEA flags concentration of clean energy supply chains as emerging challenge

The concentration of a small number of countries in clean energy supply chains is an emerging challenge for Southeast Asian countries, International Energy Agency Executive Director Fatih Birol said on Tuesday, amid fast-rising power demand in the region. "The age of electricity is coming. We will still use oil, still use natural gas including LNG, but electricity demand is growing very strongly," Birol told delegates at the Singapore International Energy Week conference.
