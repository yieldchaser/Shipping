## TÜRKIYE CRUDE OIL EXPORTS

After a slight decline in 2024, when global crude oil loadings decreased by -0.2% y-o-y, things picked up in 2025, with full-year volumes increasing by +1.8% y-o-y.

In Jan-Sep 2026, global crude oil loadings decreased by -6.1% y-o-y to 1,547.6 mln tonnes, excluding all cabotage trade, according to vessels tracking data from LSEG.

Exports from the Arabian Gulf were down by -29.1% y-o-y to 464.5 mln t in Jan-Sep 2026, accounting for 30.0% of global seaborne crude trade, down from 39.8% in the same period of last year, reflecting the disruption from the ongoing war in the Persian Gulf.

Exports from Russian ports (including oil of Kazakh origin shipped via the CPC terminal) increased by +8.3% y-o-y to 187.1 mln t in Jan-Sep 2026, or 12.1% of global trade.

From South America, exports surged by +33.1% y-o-y to 212.3 mln t. From the USA, exports went up by +10.5% y-o-y to 150.5 mln tonnes in Jan-Sep 2026.

From West Africa, exports decreased by -10.2% y-o-y to 118.7 mln t. From ASEAN exports rose by +8.3% y-o-y to 86.2 mln t in Jan-Sep 2026 (this inevitably includes trans-shipped cargoes).

In terms of demand (measured by arrival at discharge ports), global seaborne crude imports totalled 1,549.2 mln t in Jan-Sep 2026, down -5.4% y-o-y.

The top single-country seaborne importer of crude oil in Jan-Sep 2026 was Mainland China, accounting for 18.8% of global trade, although the EU27 as a bloc was a larger buyer.

Volumes into China declined by -20.7% y-o-y to 291.8 mln t in Jan-Sep 2026, from 367.7 mln t in Jan-Sep 2025.

Imports into the EU27 increased by +1.3% y-o-y to 350.9 mln t, accounting for 22.6% of global trade. To ASEAN, imports decreased by -8.0% y-o-y to 198.7 mln t. To South Korea, imports declined by -10.7% y-o-y to 92.0 mln t.

Imports into the USA increased by +4.0% y-o-y to 96.8 mln t in Jan-Sep 2026. To Japan, imports declined by -13.5% y-o-y to 71.1 mln t.

Türkiye is one of the largest exporters of crude oil outside the Arabian Gulf, and a major player in the Mediterranean oil market.

This is so despite the fact that Türkiye does not have any significant domestic oil reserves. Instead, it is a major export outlet for crude from Azerbaijan and from Northern Iraq.

In Jan-Sep 2026 exports from Türkiye rebounded by +31.4% y-o-y to 24.1 mln t.

The majority of crude shipped from Türkiye has always been Azeri BTC grade, sourced from the Baku-Tbilisi-Ceyhan pipeline. This is a 1768 kilometres long crude oil pipeline connecting the Azeri-Chirag-Gunashli oil field in the Caspian Sea to the Mediterranean Sea Coast.

In Jan-Sep 2026, 18.7 mln tonnes of the 24.1 mln tonnes loaded in Türkiye were Azeri BTC grade.

Most of the rest of Turkish exports was Kirkuk grade oil from Northern Iraq. Kirkuk oil was sourced from the 970 kilometres long Kirkuk-Ceyhan Oil Pipeline, also known as the Iraq-Türkiye Crude Oil Pipeline.

Türkiye stopped oil flows through the pipeline from northern Iraq on the 25th of March of 2023.

Officially, Türkiye began maintenance work on the pipeline, which, according to Turkish officials, passes through a seismically active area and was damaged by floods.

Kirkuk cargoes reappeared at Ceyhan in October 2025, after a gap of two and a half years, and accounted for 4.9 mln tonnes of Turkish loadings in Jan-Sep 2026, against nil in the same period of 2025.

Essentially all Turkish crude oil exports are loaded at the Ceyhan terminal, 98.4% of the total in Jan-Sep 2026.

About 29.9% of volumes loaded in Jan-Sep 2026 was carried in Suezmaxes, and about 68.4% was loaded on Aframaxes.

In terms of destinations for crude shipments from Türkiye, the EU27 is by far the top one, accounting for 79.3% of crude oil volumes exported from Türkiye in Jan-Sep 2026. In Jan-Sep 2026, Türkiye shipped 19.1 mln t to the EU27, +26.0% y-o-y.

# COMMODITY NEWS – DRY BULK

## **Asia's **seaborne thermal coal** market is shrinking very slowly**

The market for seaborne thermal coal in Asia is engaged in the slowest of races as a mild decline in demand competes with an equally gentle decline in supply in the coming years. This means that the longer-term outlook for prices will largely be driven by which of supply and demand dips at a slightly faster pace than the other.

## **India's SAIL flies in Mongolian coking coal as it seeks new supplies**

Steel Authority of India has airlifted coking coal from Mongolia for the first time, testing the landlocked nation as a potential new supplier as India seeks to reduce its dependence on Australia, two sources said. The state-backed steelmaker flew in a 1-metric-ton sample earlier this month and will assess whether the coal meets its steelmaking requirements, the sources said, declining to be identified because the development has not been made public.

## **India targets 600 million tons steel capacity by 2047**

India's new steel policy plans 600 million metric tons of steelmaking capacity by 2047 and is a roadmap for the next two decades, the country's steel secretary said on Tuesday, adding that it will be released to the public domain soon. The policy, a draft document of which was reviewed by Reuters, also lays out plans to cut sector emissions and shore up access to iron ore and coking coal — key raw materials — to meet anticipated demand.

## **BofA raises long-term copper forecast on tightening supply outlook**

Bank of America raised its long-term copper price forecast, citing tightening supply outside the US and persistent market tightness in China, while warning that elevated oil prices and the ongoing Iran conflict could continue to weigh on precious metals in the near term. The bank increased its 2031 copper price forecast by 20% to $13,577 per metric ton, driven by constrained copper availability outside the US due to continued inflows of copper into warehouse financing deals, and a tight domestic market in China.

## **China demands copper supply commitments for Anglo Teck merger approval**

China's antitrust regulator has asked Anglo American to commit to supply the country with a steady flow of copper concentrate as a condition for approving its proposed $54 billion merger with Canada's Teck Resources, three people aware of the development said. The demand reflects China's struggle to feed its vast copper smelting industry, which refines up to 60% of the world's copper cathodes but faces its worst feedstock shortage in decades.

## **Hindalco aims to kick-start an alumina pricing revolution**

What price alumina? The market for the raw material needed to produce aluminium is both huge and highly opaque despite the best efforts of both exchanges and price reporting agencies. The London Metal Exchange (LME) launched an alumina contract in 2019 but it hasn't traded since early 2020. The CME's product, trading since 2017, has seen only sporadic activity.

## **China's weak soybean demand dims prospects for US cargoes after tariff snub**

China's soybean buying is likely to decline in the months ahead due to weak animal feed demand and negative crush margins, leaving little room for US cargoes as they were excluded from proposed tariff relief after last week's Washington summit. China is set to lower tariffs on a broad range of US farm products, but soybeans, its biggest US agricultural import, were excluded from the tariff reduction list following the talks between Chinese President Xi Jinping and US President Donald Trump.

## **Weather, not price, to drive Brazil mills on sugar production**

Sugarcane processors in Brazil, the world's largest producer of sugar and second largest of ethanol, will pay more attention to the weather than to market prices to decide how much sugar or fuel to make from now to the end of the harvest in December. Wetter-than-normal weather in center-south Brazil, a characteristic of strong El Ninos, will likely prevent mills from boosting sugar production as some market players expected after raw sugar prices gained more than 21% last month.

## **ICE October raw sugar delivery seen at 1.45 million metric tons**

Deliveries of raw sugar on the expiry of the October contract on the ICE exchange were seen at 28,574 lots, or 1.45 million metric tons, according to preliminary information from two sugar traders on Wednesday, when the contract expired. Singapore-based commodities trader Wilmar International was said to be the sole receiver of all that sugar, while Brazilian commodities trader BTG Pactual Commodities was seen as the largest deliverer with 9,267 lots, or 470,790 tons.

## **Analysts raise 2026 oil forecasts on prolonged Gulf disruption**

Analysts have raised their 2026 oil price forecasts with benchmark Brent crude expected to average nearly $90 a barrel as disruption to Gulf exports offsets concerns over demand growth. A September survey of 30 economists and analysts forecast that Brent crude would average $89.05 a barrel in 2026 and U.S. crude $83.90 a barrel.

## **Gulf crude oil exports return to 2025 level, Goldman Sachs says**

Goldman Sachs estimates Gulf oil exports, including "dark exports" involving ships operating with their location transponders turned off, have recovered to **23.3 million** barrels per day over the last week, in line with their 2025 average, as exports doubled in September, it said in a note on Tuesday. It noted that the recovery was driven by increased exports through the Strait of Hormuz, including via ship-to-ship transfers.

## **Middle East crude oil exports exceed pre-war levels but tanker attacks increase**

Crude oil exports from the Middle East rose above pre-war levels in four of the seven days of the final week of September, shipping data showed on Monday, despite attacks on vessels passing through the Strait of Hormuz. Crude exports from the region exceeded pre-war levels on September **24** and between September **27** and **29**, rising to between **19.5 million** barrels per day and **22.5 million bpd**, provisional data from ship-tracking firm Kpler showed.

## **OPEC+ oil producers set to keep output targets steady at Sunday meeting, sources say**

OPEC+ oil-producing countries are likely to keep their oil production targets steady for November when they meet on Sunday, two sources with knowledge of the matter told Reuters. The online meeting of seven core OPEC+ members — Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Algeria, Kazakhstan and Oman — comes as Gulf members have been boosting exports that have been disrupted for months by the Iran war, although most are still pumping well below their output targets.

## **Russian oil supply to India tightens on reduced exports, strong Chinese demand**

Indian refiners expect Russian oil supplies to further tighten in October and November due to reduced exports and stiff competition from China, forcing the world's third-largest crude importer to buy more costly alternatives for the rest of the year, four trade sources said. India has been the top buyer of seaborne Russian Urals crude since 2022, when Moscow diverted its energy flows away from Europe at deep discounts.

## **Asia's gasoline margin skyrockets on outages, China export halt, traders say**

Asia's gasoline margin rose to a record high on Thursday as various outages at regional refineries and reports that Chinese refiners have halted fuel exports for October tightened the supply outlook, traders said. The refining profit, or crack, for gasoline rose to **$50.53** per barrel over Brent crude.

## **US tells France and Germany to release diesel stocks or face US export ban, sources say**

The Trump administration has told Germany and France to draw down emergency diesel inventories to help ease soaring global fuel prices or face a potential US diesel export ban, according to three people close to the discussions. The warning marks an escalation in pressure on Europe as US President Donald Trump considers a potential ban to help bring down US fuel prices ahead of November's midterm elections.

## **G7 countries agree on release of diesel and oil stocks after US pressure**

Group of Seven countries agreed on Friday to release 100 million barrels of diesel and crude oil from emergency reserves and pledged to refrain from energy export restrictions after pressure from US President Donald Trump. The US led a pressure campaign on the European Union to draw down emergency diesel inventories, with the Trump administration warning that failure to act could lead to a US ban on diesel exports. Such a ban could sharply reduce supplies to Europe, which has become increasingly reliant on US diesel, and potentially inflict high economic costs on the bloc.

## **White House weighs red-dyed diesel tax relief to lower fuel prices**

The White House is considering regulatory relief that would allow broader sales of red-dyed diesel as part of an effort to bring down soaring prices, a move that could allow some buyers to avoid the federal fuel tax, according to two people familiar with the discussions. The red-dye proposal has emerged from days of administration deliberations as one of the leading alternatives to a diesel export ban, which has been discussed as global supply disruptions drive prices to record levels.

## **September LNG shipments through Hormuz hit highest since start of US-Iran war**

The number of liquefied natural gas cargoes exiting the Strait of Hormuz rose in September to its highest monthly level since the US-Israeli war on Iran began, data from analytics firms, shows, despite persistent security worries and uncertainty. A total of 19 LNG cargoes were shipped through the key waterway in September, made up of 13 from Qatar and six from the United Arab Emirates, S&P Global Energy said.

## **Qatar extends force majeure notices on LNG supply to Edison, some Asian clients**

QatarEnergy has extended force majeure notices and will not be able to deliver liquefied natural gas cargoes to Italian utility Edison or some Asian clients as the Strait of Hormuz remains closed, Edison and trading sources said on Monday. Edison, one of the company's biggest customers in Europe, will not receive LNG cargoes until the beginning of December, the Italian group said on Monday, in the latest extension under a force majeure notification first issued in April following disruptions caused by the US-Iran war.

## **Alaska LNG could cost more than double Gulf Coast rivals, raising questions over economics**

US President Donald Trump's push to steer $54 billion of South Korean investment into Alaska LNG is putting a fresh focus on a 20 million-ton-a-year export project that would cost more than twice as much as a comparable US Gulf Coast plant. Trump unveiled the plan on Wednesday as part of a broader $200 billion South Korean package. Seoul quickly said it had not yet decided whether to fund Alaska LNG and would first review the project's commercial viability.

## ****Alaska LNG shipping savings justifies billions in investment, developer says****

Alaska LNG's developer said the project's access to stranded natural gas and shorter shipping routes to Asia will make it competitive with US Gulf Coast rivals, despite questions from some analysts about the project's massive infrastructure costs. When the project is completed, the cost of shipping gas to Asia from Alaska's North Slope would be at least 65% cheaper than shipping LNG from the Gulf Coast, Tim Fitzpatrick, communications director for project developer Glenfarne, said on Sunday in an email to Reuters.

## ****Trump unveils $200 billion South Korean investment in US; Seoul hedges on Alaska pipeline****

US President Donald Trump unveiled plans on Wednesday for South Korea to invest some $200 billion in US projects, but Seoul quickly suggested one key element, a $54 billion pipeline for an LNG project in Alaska, was not yet set in stone. The announcement from the White House amounted to the first set of major projects under a broader $350 billion strategic investment package South Korea made as part of a trade agreement with Washington last year. The package included $150 billion for shipbuilding and $200 billion for strategic investments.

## ****Alternative capital powers America's next wave of LNG and pipeline projects****

Alternative asset managers deploying cash from their insurance arms are reshaping how America's energy infrastructure is financed, emerging as major backers of LNG export projects and the pipelines moving crude and natural gas around the country. The influx of cash is dominated by a trio of the finance world's biggest names: Apollo Global Management, Blackstone and KKR.

## ****EU eyes methane law delay, IEA to discuss oil stock releases if needed****

The European Union is considering delaying its methane emissions rules for oil and gas imports, while International Energy Agency members may discuss whether to release strategic oil reserves, as concerns mount among European governments over rising energy prices and tight supplies this winter. EU Energy Commissioner Dan Jorgensen said the bloc was looking at delaying rules which, from January 1, 2027, will require foreign oil and gas producers that supply the EU to monitor and report their emissions of methane, a potent greenhouse gas.

## ****ICE launches new LNG, freight and UK power derivatives****

The Intercontinental Exchange announced the launch of new JKM LNG (Platts) options, LNG Balmo freight futures, and UK base electricity options, in an expansion of its global gas and power markets, the exchange said in a statement on Monday. The new JKM LNG options aim to give customers a way to manage Asian gas price risk at a specific point in time. They will complement ICE's existing JKM LNG (Platts) average price options, which are used to manage exposure that accumulates over the month.

# CAPESIZE MARKET

## ATLANTIC AND PACIFIC BASIN

The Capesize market fell heavily over the week, a severe freight derivative (FFA) sell-off and sharply lower bunker prices eroding confidence through the first three sessions.

The Pacific recovered late, but both Atlantic routes continued to slide, with Golden Week holidays in China thinning liquidity.

The Western Australia–Qingdao route (170,000 mt iron ore, plus/minus 10%) plunged USD 1.30/wmt to USD 14.35/wmt on 28 September, mining majors fixing Dampier– and Port Hedland–Qingdao at around USD 14.35–14.40/wmt for 14–16 October laycan.

It lost a further 65 cents/wmt to USD 13.70/wmt on 29 September as owners cut offers across the day, Dampier–Qingdao being taken at USD 13.80/wmt and Port Hedland at USD 13.70/wmt, and 25 cents/wmt to the week's low of USD 13.45/wmt on 30 September amid a pronounced bunching of tonnage arriving in Australia from 11 to 16 October and ballasters heading towards Singapore.

Sentiment then turned: with earlier positions covered, owners increasingly ballasting towards the South Atlantic and the paper sell-off exhausted, the route rebounded 60 cents/wmt to USD 14.05/wmt on 1 October and 20 cents/wmt to close at USD 14.25/wmt, down USD 1.40/wmt from 25 September.

Tubarao–Qingdao fell throughout, shedding USD 1.25/wmt to USD 40.95/wmt on 28 September as owners revised offers aggressively lower and quotations turned backwardated for mid-October to early-November loading, USD 1.50/wmt to USD 39.45/wmt on 29 September and USD 1.20/wmt to USD 38.25/wmt on 30 September.

Further losses of 15 cents/wmt and 40 cents/wmt left it at USD 37.70/wmt, down USD 4.50/wmt on the week, sentiment bearish on lower West African fixtures and a wide bid-offer gap for October–November split dates.

On fronthaul, Port Cartier–Qingdao was fixed at about USD 53.00/wmt for 15–24 October laycan, while Buchanan–Qingdao was reported at about USD 36.50/wmt.

Saldanha Bay–Qingdao declined in every session, by USD 1.00/wmt, 90 cents/wmt, 45 cents/wmt, 15 cents/wmt and 55 cents/wmt, to close at USD 28.20/wmt, down USD 3.05/wmt.

# PANAMAX MARKET

## ATLANTIC BASIN

The Atlantic closed the week on a strong note, with ECSA fronthaul continuing to command firm levels and North Atlantic business showing further signs of strength.

Grain demand remained an important driver in the South Atlantic, whilst coal and ore requirements provided good support across the wider basin.

In ECSA, an 82,000 dwt 2023-built from Jaigarh achieved around USD 26,250/day for Singapore/Japan grains, whilst an 82,000 dwt 2023-built from Krishnapatnam fixed at around USD 25,000/day.

An 82,000 dwt 2020-built from Haldia was also reported at USD 25,200/day, with older tonnage generally closer to USD 20,000–21,000/day.

A 75,000 dwt 2011-built from Abidjan fixed ECSA/Singapore-Japan at a strong USD 28,750/day.

North Atlantic levels remained particularly healthy: an 82,000 dwt 2020-built from Amsterdam was fixed fronthaul, whilst an 80,000 dwt 2020-built from Gibraltar achieved USD 25,000/day for Puerto Drummond/Skaw-Gibraltar coal.

Voyage activity was also supportive, with USG/North China grains indicated at around USD 77.00 fio and Baltimore/Rotterdam coal at around USD 22.00 fio.

## PACIFIC BASIN

The Pacific market remained relatively firm this week, with rates generally holding at healthy levels across the main Pacific areas.

Indonesia saw a stronger tone, whilst NOPAC remained steady.

Towards the end of the week, however, sentiment began to soften slightly, with momentum losing some strength.

Indonesia saw improved levels across the Pacific round voyage, with Indo/South China business ranging from USD 15,000 to USD 22,750: a 73,193 dwt 2001-built fixed USD 15,000 for Indo/China, whilst a 72,270 dwt 1999-built achieved USD 16,250 for Indo/South China.

A 75,331 dwt 2013-built fixed USD 18,000 for Indo/Malaysia, whilst a 75,347 dwt 2014-built achieved USD 24,250 for Indo/South China.

Further business included a 79,467 dwt 2012-built at USD 16,500 for Indo/South China and a 78,821 dwt 2009-built at USD 20,000 for Indo/South Korea.

Longer-haul Indonesian business remained at a premium, an 81,728 dwt 2014-built fixing USD 20,500 for Indo/South Korea.

NOPAC remained steady, with rates mainly around USD 19,000–22,000: an 80,410 dwt 2012-built fixed USD 17,300 for NoPac/Singapore-Japan grains, whilst a 78,092 dwt 2012-built achieved USD 22,000 on the same route.

An 81,945 dwt 2015-built fixed USD 21,000 and a 95,750 dwt 2013-built USD 20,000, both for NoPac/Singapore-Japan, with further business including an 82,023 dwt 2013-built at USD 21,750 and an 81,080 dwt 2016-built at USD 20,250 for NoPac/Singapore-Japan.

Australia was mainly driven by East Coast business, with no clear West Coast fixtures reported, rates generally in the low/mid-USD 20,000s: a 79,386 dwt 2010-built fixed USD 25,000 for EC Australia/India, a 76,195 dwt 2013-built achieved USD 20,000 for EC Australia/China and an 82,209 dwt 2023-built fixed USD 22,500 for EC Australia/Singapore-Japan coal.

# SUPRAMAX & HANDYSIZE MARKET

The US Gulf was firm, with positive momentum across both Ultramax and Supramax segments on solid transatlantic and long-haul demand, whilst Handies held steady with steady interest on long-haul trips. **NORTH EUROPE / CONTINENT**

## BLACK SEA / MEDITERRANEAN

**BLACK SEA / MEDITERRANEAN**

The Med and Black Sea is still stable and improving on some routes, a general lack of prompt tonnage hindering cargoes charterers are trying to bring to the spot market. ## DEMOLITION SALES

While the ongoing conflict in the Middle East shows little sign of any resolution, sentiment across the Indian Subcontinent demolition market remains positive. Strong freight rates across all sectors continue to starve the market of any real activity. In a change to the recent order, Bangladesh now takes the top spot in terms of pricing as local competition between Buyers heats up having lost tonnage to neighboring Pakistan in recent months. While the Indian market remains well behind on conventional units local buyers continue to absorb specialist Vessels including that of a more 'exotic' compliance nature. To update on last weeks comment around EU approval for certain Indian yards, there remains no official statement from the EU commission in regard to the update to the list of recycling facilities approved by the EU. With two of the Indian ship recyclers having been listed for possible inclusion, the previous indications from the EU was that time would be needed for them to obtain the response from all the member states.

A couple of sales to report this week including the U-Ming of Taiwan controlled vintage cement carrier, namely the MV ASIA CEMENT NO.3, 3,520 LDT been reported sold at USD 446/LT on an 'as is' Taiwan basis. And also the BW controlled large LNG Carrier, the 2007 blt LNG KANO reported committed into Alang at USD 530 LDT - it is understood the Vessel has cracked cargo tanks which takes it beyond repair despite her age.
