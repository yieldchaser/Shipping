## Key View

### Prices bounced modestly on the last session before the holiday, but hot metal fell a fourth straight week with the decline widening, and SMM already warns of a markedly sharper drop in early October

Today's prices and today's data point in opposite directions. On prices: the DCE most-traded contract reclaimed 700 at 702.5 yuan/mt (+0.50%), Qingdao port spot rose 2 yuan across the board except for flat IOCJ, and the port spot indices had the 65% up 2 yuan, the 61% up 1 and the 58% unchanged. On data: daily average hot metal across 242 mills fell to 2.3882 million mt (-6,100 mt WoW), with the blast furnace operating rate at 88.57% and capacity utilisation at 88.15%. Laying out the full path makes the direction much clearer: 2.4080 (Sep 2 peak) → 2.4028 → 2.3991 → 2.3943 → 2.3882 million mt — four consecutive weekly declines, 19,800 mt off the peak, with the last three drops widening from 3,700 to 4,800 to 6,100 mt. Demand is not bottoming; the contraction is accelerating. More important, SMM has now given explicit forward guidance: with mills loss-making, some report they will draw up maintenance plans in early October and overall hot metal output may fall markedly. Combined with maintenance losses projected to reach 1.6839 million mt next week (a further +55,100 mt), the first post-holiday hot metal print could well be the largest decline of this cycle. Margins move the same way: the average imported ore loss widened from 5.08 to 6.12 yuan/mt, and SMM expects further widening. One basis tension should be stated plainly, though — that table references Sep 29 spot and freight, whereas on Sep 30 port spot recovered and freight fell another 4–6%, both of which mechanically repair margins; the first print after the holiday could recover some ground. Two structural shifts are worth recording. First, freight's accelerating collapse: C3 has slid from 43.06 to 40.26 and C5 from 16.36 to 14.13, two-day falls of 6.5% and 13.6%. The support argument that high Brazilian freight was curbing shipments is now void, and the room for Brazilian supply to recover only grows. Second, coke supply is recovering: coke oven capacity utilisation rose to 75.0% this week (+2.3 ppt) and Shanxi to 72.7% (+2.8 ppt) — the practical precondition for the rumoured first round of price cuts, and currently the only thread pointing to demand repair. On balance, the pre-holiday bounce is sentiment repair and does not change a soft and choppy picture. The first trading week of October turns on two things: how large the hot metal decline actually is, and whether the coke cut lands. The first sets the slope of the decline; the second decides whether it has an end.

## Market Commentary · Imported Ore

### Futures & Spot

Broad gains on the final session before the holiday. The DCE most-traded I2701 settled at 702.5 yuan/mt, up 3.5 yuan (+0.50%) from Sep 29 and back above 700; SGX most-traded was USD 94.86/dmt (as of Sep 29, -0.47%). Qingdao port spot on SMM's basis rose 2 yuan/wmt across the board: PB Fines 665, Newman Fines 668, Mac Fines 667, BRBF 720, Newman Lump 860, SSF 527 and PB Lump 860 yuan/wmt; only IOCJ was unchanged, at 842 yuan/wmt. Basis note: the DCE change is computed on closing prices (699.0 → 702.5); SGX and the all five MMi indices are updated through Sep 30, while SGX and ocean freight are as of Sep 29, one day behind the spot basis.

### Driver

Both sides advanced a step. Bullish: talk of a first round of coke price cuts is still live, and coke oven capacity utilisation recovered to 75.0% this week (+2.3 ppt), with Shanxi at 72.7% (+2.8 ppt) — recovering coke supply is precisely what makes a price cut feasible; the two-day slide in freight is also pulling landed costs down. Bearish: hot metal fell for a fourth week with the decline widening, and SMM now warns that early-October maintenance planning will bring a markedly sharper fall. SMM's overall call is unchanged: iron ore lacks bullish support and prices should stay soft and choppy near term.

### Demand

The key numbers this issue. On Sep 30 SMM's 242-mill survey showed daily average hot metal at 2.3882 million mt (-6,100 mt WoW), the blast furnace operating rate at 88.57% (-0.19 ppt) and capacity utilisation at 88.15% (-0.18 ppt). Laid out in full, the path reads 2.4080 (Sep 2 peak) → 2.4028 → 2.3991 → 2.3943 → 2.3882 million mt: four consecutive weekly declines, 19,800 mt off the peak, with the last three drops of 3,700, 4,800 and 6,100 mt widening each week. Two furnaces entered maintenance (Hebei, Liaoning) against two restarts — a structural reshuffle. The real signal is in the outlook: with mills loss-making, some say they will draw up maintenance plans in early October and overall hot metal output may fall markedly. Maintenance losses were not updated this issue and still read 1.6288 million mt for this week (Sep 26–Oct 2) and 1.6839 million for next (Oct 3–9), per the Sep 28 survey.

### Inventory & Supply

No new weekly supply data this issue: arrivals in China remain 29.4625 million mt (-5.93% WoW) and global shipments 35.5121 million mt (+3.21%) from the Sep 28 survey, while port stocks stand at 145.45 million mt across 35 ports as of Sep 25 (+1.12 million mt WoW) and 102.594 million mt across 10 ports as of Sep 24 (+681,500 mt). Two supply-side items are new. First, Australia's big three published their October discounts, which diverge by product. Second, the Brazilian freight constraint has broken down further: C3 has slid from 43.06 to 40.26, so the earlier argument that high freight was curbing shipments from smaller miners no longer holds, and the room for Brazilian shipments to recover keeps opening up.

### Cost & Margin

SMM puts the average imported ore loss at 6.12 yuan/mt, up modestly from 5.08, mainly on the slight decline in spot prices, and expects losses on imported ore to widen further. Freight fell sharply again: C3 Brazil–China at USD 40.26/mt (-4.05%) and C5 Australia–China at USD 14.13/mt (-6.11%), both as of Sep 29 — two-day falls of 6.5% and 13.6%, with C5 now USD 3.64 below its Sep 14 level of 17.77. One tension between basis and mechanics is worth flagging: the table was published on the morning of Sep 30 and references Sep 29 costs and realisations, whereas on Sep 30 port spot rose 1–2 yuan and freight fell another 4–6% — both of which point to margin repair (the seaborne index rose USD 0.15/dmt the same day, which roughly offsets the port spot gain). SMM's “losses may widen further” is a forward call premised on soft ore prices; the first post-holiday print could well recover some ground.


## Market Commentary · Domestic Ore

### Prices

SMM's brief turned to eastern Liaoning today: local domestic ore prices showed no clear movement, with both sides trading cautiously. The SMM China Domestic Ore Composite Price Index stands at 831.59 yuan/mt (as of Sep 24, -0.70%), with no new print this issue. On a monthly basis, national iron ore concentrate output was 18.87 million mt in September 2026.

### Supply & Demand

Supply: sales are mostly against long-term contracts, and supply remains generally tight; holders' costs are high and their willingness to sell cheaply is thin, which supports prices. That said, some concentrators are struggling to move material and a few have accepted low bids under inventory pressure, a meaningful bearish drag. Demand: mill operations are relatively stable and demand resilience is still decent — a subtle contrast with the falling hot metal on the imported ore side, explained by domestic concentrate demand coming largely from comparatively steady regional contract buyers. Weekly data were not updated and still read a 58.1% mine capacity utilisation rate (-0.1 ppt) and 898,000 mt of concentrate output (-2,000 mt) as of Sep 25.

### Outlook

SMM expects local iron ore prices in eastern Liaoning to trade range-bound near term. Set against last issue's “soft and choppy” call for Tangshan, eastern Liaoning is holding up better on tight supply and a high share of contract volumes — regional divergence is widening. Watch after the holiday for whether individual concentrators' inventory pressure spills over.
