## Key View

### Seaborne prices and futures recover modestly, but domestic spot divergence keeps buying in focus

Prices recovered to some extent, but the rebound was not uniform. DCE most-traded closed at 672.5 yuan/mt, up 0.15%. The seaborne 61% and 65% indices rose 0.68% and 0.23% to USD 89.40 and 107.75/dmt, respectively, while all 12 brand quotations increased. Most port products rose 2 yuan/wmt, but Carajas Fines fell 8 yuan and PB Lump was unchanged. The domestic ore composite index declined to 796.81 yuan/mt, highlighting differences in support across markets. Costs offered a marginal cushion. C3 and C5 freight continued lower and SMM’s average import margin recovered to 6.01 yuan/mt. However, stronger import trading margins do not mean mills have greater buying capacity. Needs- based purchases and controlled arrivals persist; sustained improvement in transactions would provide a stronger test of the recovery than a single day’s price gain. Firmer quotations should be distinguished from a change in the demand trend. Improving end-user orders and mill margins could gradually generate sustained restocking and price support. If discretionary buying remains limited, port stocks and product-specific supply may constrain the rebound. A low-level, range-bound view remains appropriate while watching purchasing persistence, hot metal and inventory absorption rather than treating a one-day rise as a trend reversal.

## Market Commentary · Imported Ore

### Futures & Spot

DCE most-traded I2701 closed at 672.5 yuan/mt, up 0.15% from Oct 15. Qingdao PB Fines were 642, Newman Fines 645, Mac Fines 646, BRBF 692, Newman Lump 837 and SSF 504 yuan/wmt, each up 2 yuan; Carajas Fines fell 8 yuan to 817 while PB Lump held at 826. SGX official current-month FEFV26 was last priced at USD 91.00/dmt, timestamped Oct 16, 17:30:47 (UTC+8). SMM’s daily review reported reasonable trader willingness to offer, needs-based mill restocking and thin overall trading.

### Driver

Seaborne composite indices and brand quotations recovered and domestic futures edged higher, but port products did not all rise together, showing structural differences in the recovery. The rebound may reflect adjustments in offers and buying schedules; its durability requires confirmation from actual transactions. The seaborne 61% index rose more than the 65%, narrowing their spread from USD 18.70 to 18.35/dmt. A one-day spread change alone is insufficient to establish a shift in blending demand.

### Demand

SMM’s Oct 16 coke briefing described cautious, needs-based mill purchases and controlled arrivals. Cost relief has not translated broadly into discretionary restocking. The latest 242-mill survey showed daily hot metal output of 2.3520 million mt on Oct 14, down 1,200 mt from Oct 7. A narrower decline may ease concerns about further demand contraction, but a recovery still depends on orders, margins and maintenance implementation.

### Inventory & Supply

Ten-port stocks were 104.0212 million mt on Oct 15, up 1.0047 million mt from Sep 30, with increases in fines and lump and declines in concentrates and pellets. This comparison spans the holiday, and product trends may reflect both arrivals and mill collection patterns. Global shipments of 34.1393 million mt and Chinese arrivals of 27.9147 million mt in the week to Oct 9 both declined from the preceding survey week. Whether lower flows ease inventory pressure still depends on subsequent trading and collections.

### Cost & Margin

SMM’s Oct 16 imported ore cost-and-margin assessment put the period’s average import margin at 6.01 yuan/mt, up 4.91 yuan from 1.10. This measures import trading rather than steelmaking profitability. Oct 15 freight was USD 31.39/mt for C3 (-3.50%) and 11.60 for C5 (-8.23%), offering potential cost relief. Expectations for the next coke price move remain contested; changes not yet implemented should not be treated as realised cost improvements.


## Market Commentary · Domestic Ore

### Prices

The Domestic Ore Composite Price Index was 796.81 yuan/mt on Oct 16, down 24.77 yuan (-3.01%) from 821.58. Regional quotations and the national composite index measure different markets and should not be substituted for one another.

### Supply & Demand

SMM’s Oct 16 west Liaoning brief described quiet trading, cautious trader restocking and pressure on bids. Producers were reluctant to offer large concessions because of elevated earlier costs, tight ore feedstock and difficulty replenishing at low prices, preferring to wait. Mill losses and cost-control pressure continued to sustain strong buyer resistance.

### Outlook

SMM expects west Liaoning concentrate prices to remain soft and range-bound in the short term. Supply constraints coexist with pressure on bids, leaving stabilisation dependent on actual transactions. Improved mill profitability followed by stronger buying could provide better support for some resources.
