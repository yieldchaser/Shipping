## Key View

### Seaborne prices soften after a rebound; cost relief still needs sustained buying support

Renewed price weakness suggests that the previous seaborne rebound has not translated into sustained market support. DCE most-traded closed at 677.0 yuan/mt, down 0.88%, while the 61%, 58% and 65% port indices fell to 657, 619 and 821 yuan/wmt. The seaborne 61% index eased to USD 89.50/dmt and the 65% to 108.70, narrowing the grade spread to USD 19.20. Eleven of the 12 seaborne brands declined, leaving the recovery in mainstream fines fragile. Costs and supply flows offer some cushion. The second coke cut has taken effect and C3 and C5 freight continued to fall, while the latest shipments and arrivals both declined. However, the impact of cost relief on buying appetite still depends on finished-steel selling prices, end-user orders and mill margins. Lower supply flows must also be assessed against existing port stocks and collection rates rather than equated directly with tighter spot availability. The immediate focus should be the persistence of transactions and restocking. Buying limited to immediate needs could make one-day rebounds difficult to sustain and keep ore prices range-bound at low levels. A combination of margin repair and stronger orders could gradually generate additional purchases and support prices. For now, cost improvement should be distinguished from demand improvement, and gains in individual brands should not be treated as an overall trend reversal.

## Market Commentary · Imported Ore

### Futures & Spot

DCE most-traded I2701 closed at 677.0 yuan/mt, down 0.88% from Oct 12. Qingdao PB Fines were 645, Newman Fines 648, Mac Fines 649, BRBF 695, Newman Lump 840, SSF 507 and PB Lump 831 yuan/wmt, each down 3 yuan; Carajas Fines were unchanged at 831. The latest price for SGX official current-month contract FEFV26 was USD 90.65/dmt, timestamped Oct 13, 17:39:38 (UTC+8).

### Driver

Seaborne quotations softened again after the previous day’s broad recovery, alongside declines in domestic futures and composite port indices, showing that the price recovery lacks persistence. Lower costs may ease some pressure on mills, but end-user orders, finished-steel prices and actual purchases jointly determine

### Demand

SMM’s Oct 13 review cited projected hot metal output losses from blast furnace maintenance of 1.9011 million mt for Oct 10–16, up 78,600 mt WoW, highlighting the constraint from maintenance schedules. This covers a week still in progress rather than a realised decline in daily hot metal. Mill enquiries were reasonable but overall transactions remained limited, with purchases focused on immediate needs. The second coke cut has taken effect, but additional buying still depends on finished-steel margins and orders.

### Inventory & Supply

In the week to Oct 9, global shipments were 34.1393 million mt, down 5.02% WoW, and arrivals in China were 27.9147 million mt, down 4.05%. Brazil shipped 7.0226 million mt and Australia 19.5081 million mt. Lower supply flows may ease subsequent pressure, although existing port stocks and mill purchasing rates remain important to the balance. The latest available 10-port inventory was 103.0165 million mt as of Sep 30.

### Cost & Margin

Oct 12 freight was USD 33.26/mt for C3, down USD 0.42 (-1.25%) from the previous reading, and 13.92 for C5, down USD 0.47 (-3.27%). Lower transport costs may improve landed cost conditions for some cargoes. The second coke purchase-price cut offers mill cost relief but does not establish that mills have returned to profit; finished-steel selling prices and procurement costs must be assessed together. SMM’s current imported ore cost-and-margin assessment put the average import margin at 9.60 yuan/mt, down 5.77 yuan from 15.37; this measures import trading rather than steelmaking profitability.


## Market Commentary · Domestic Ore

### Prices

The latest Domestic Ore Composite Price Index was 821.58 yuan/mt on Oct 9, down 0.39% from the previous reading. Softer imported ore prices may continue to influence bargaining room for domestic concentrates.

### Supply & Demand

SMM’s Oct 12 Shandong brief reported 64% basic concentrate at 759 yuan/mt on a dry-basis, pre-tax ex-mine quotation payable by acceptance bill, down 12 yuan from the previous week. Mill collections returned to a normal pace, some miners saw slower sales and some contract suppliers had little inventory pressure. Region, grade and payment terms distinguish this market from the domestic composite index.

### Outlook

Cautious mill buying could slow mine destocking and price recovery. Conversely, gradual margin improvement from cost relief could support domestic concentrate demand. Near-term attention should remain on regional transactions and purchasing intensity rather than extrapolating individual local quotations to the national market.


## Market Factors

### Supportive Factors

- The latest global shipments and arrivals in China both declined.
- Lower C3 and C5 freight reduced transport costs for some cargoes.
- The second coke cut provides a cushion for mill costs.
- Regional resource differences may support some individual brands.

### Bearish Factors

- DCE closed 0.88% lower and all three port indices declined.
- Both seaborne indices and most brand quotations softened.
- End-user demand and finished-steel profitability continue to constrain buying appetite.
- The previous seaborne rebound did not persist, limiting evidence of a durable recovery.
