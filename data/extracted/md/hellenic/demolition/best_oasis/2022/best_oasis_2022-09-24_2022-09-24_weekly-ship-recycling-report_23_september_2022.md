# Best Oasis Weekly Ship Recycling Report - 2022-09-24

## Weekly Market Overview

Global economy is knocked off course by soaring inflation, energy crisis and threats of a potential nuclear war, resulting in a sharp decline in economic activities across many regions. There is a downward pressure on ferrous scrap prices across all major recycling destinations due to slower end-user industry demands, thereby dampening the sentiments for ship recyclers. With elongating trade routes and growing supply chain woes, ocean freight rates do not appear to be returning to the pre-pandemic levels anytime soon, making it difficult to predict when a steady inflow of tonnage will resume in the ship recycling industry.

Chinese steel mills have ramped up production this month, with their average daily crude steel output in the 10 days to Sept. 20 hitting a three- month high of 2.89 million tonnes. There is an improvement in domestic steel demand as the industry prepares for a week-long National Day holiday at the start of October. Analysts saw signs of increased construction activity in China, with real estate developer Evergrande's 668 projects having resumed construction while hopes of more stimulus to shore up China's COVID-hit economy also added to the buoyant mood.

The demand and price of copper has gone down by nearly a third since March due to a manufacturing recession that came out of an energy crisis However, miners and metal traders say that a massive shortfall will emerge for copper in just a couple of years, as demand for it goes up.

Global markets wobble as U.S. Federal Reserve's inflation fight inspires rate hikes at central banks worldwide. Fed officials acknowledge the possibility that such aggressive rate hikes might bring on a recession but highlighted that it will tolerate a recession to bring inflation down.

### Turkey

**Central Bank delivered another shocking rate cut**

- Major steel producers have raised their domestic and overseas scrap offers due to low material availability, high production rate and increased demand.
- Turkey's central bank delivered another surprise 100 basis-point rate cut on Thursday, sending the lira tumbling to an all-time low, even as inflation rose above 80% and as central banks globally race in the opposite direction and tighten policy. The policy direction has stunned investors and economists, who say the refusal to tighten policy is a result of political pressure from President Erdogan. The lira has lost more than 27% of its value to the USD year to date, and many economists predict a further fall upto 24/USD by March 2023.
