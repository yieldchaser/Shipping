## Market Overview

Highlights of the Week
Global economy is knocked off course by soaring inflation, energy crisis and threats of a potential nuclear war, resulting in a sharp decline in
economic activities across many regions. There is a downward pressure on ferrous scrap prices across all major recycling destinations due to slower
end-user industry demands, thereby dampening the sentiments for ship recyclers. With elongating trade routes and growing supply chain woes,
ocean freight rates do not appear to be returning to the pre-pandemic levels anytime soon, making it difficult to predict when a steady inflow of
tonnage will resume in the ship recycling industry.
Chinese steel mills have ramped up production this month, with their average daily crude steel output in the 10 days to Sept. 20 hitting a three-
month high of 2.89 million tonnes. There is an improvement in domestic steel demand as the industry prepares for a week-long National Day holiday
at the start of October. Analysts saw signs of increased construction activity in China, with real estate developer Evergrande's 668 projects having
resumed construction while hopes of more stimulus to shore up China's COVID-hit economy also added to the buoyant mood.
The demand and price of copper has gone down by nearly a third since March due to a manufacturing recession that came out of an energy crisis
However, miners and metal traders say that a massive shortfall will emerge for copper in just a couple of years, as demand for it goes up.
Global markets wobble as U.S. Federal Reserve's inflation fight inspires rate hikes at central banks worldwide. Fed officials acknowledge the
possibility that such aggressive rate hikes might bring on a recession but highlighted that it will tolerate a recession to bring inflation down.

The domestic scrap prices have softened this week on weakening end
user demand and rumors that vessels loaded with large quantities of
scrap are arriving at the West Coast of India. As Turkish scrap prices have
plummeted, the Indian market has become a more attractive destination
to send shiploads of scrap steel.
The Indian rupee touched an all-time low of 81.15 this week on the back
of US Treasury yields climbing to fresh multi-year highs and dollar
demand from importers. This would directly impact the business
environment in India as a stronger dollar would make everything
expensive for traders and companies.
