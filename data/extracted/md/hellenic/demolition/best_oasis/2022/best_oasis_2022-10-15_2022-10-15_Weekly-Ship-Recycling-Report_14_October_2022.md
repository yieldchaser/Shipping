# Best Oasis Weekly Ship Recycling Report - 2022-10-15

## Weekly Market Overview

Steel prices worldwide are under pressure due to the alarm bells ringing for a possible recessionary phase in most regions. In the ship recycling industry, even though the major recycling destinations are facing issues such as fluctuating currency exchange rates, restrictions on Letters of Credit issuance and dwindling demand, offer prices remain stable week-on-week due to the shortage of tonnage available for recycling. Among the subcontinent players, Bangladesh is leading the price board, closely followed by India and Pakistan.

European countries have begun implementing emergency measures to mitigate rising energy prices. The steel sector is among those energy- intensive industries that are particularly vulnerable to the ongoing energy crisis, thus impacting steel prices. Rising input costs, on top of eroding demand, have already caused a wave of curtailments that threaten the viability of Europe’s entire metal sector.

A significant consumer pullback is showing up in ocean shipping, with almost a 20% drop in ocean freight orders for the months of September and October. The decline in demand cuts across many products, resulting due to a combination of too much inventory coupled with a lack of clarity on consumer demand.

The International Monetary Fund has cut its growth forecast for 2023 and warned of a cost of living crisis as the global economy continues to be affected by the war in Ukraine, broadening inflation pressures and a slowdown in China. The fund maintained its global economic estimate for this year at 3.2 per cent but downgraded next year's forecast to 2.7 per cent. In short, the worst is yet to come, and for many people 2023 will feel like a recession,” said Pierre-Olivier Gourinchas, IMF chief economist.

### Turkey

**Weak market sentiments**

- The European Council has adopted the European Commission's proposal of an eight sanction package against Russia which seeks an import ban on steel products processed in third countries using steel originating from Russia. Negatively impacted by dumped steel exports from Russia, Turkey's exports to the EU will also be at risk in the coming period with these sanctions.
- President Erdogan welcomed Vladimir Putin’s proposal to turn Turkey into a regional hub for natural gas, potentially positioning the country as a key route for Russian flows to Europe.
- The Turkish Lira has remained relatively stable against the dollar over the last one month after falling by 28.36% against the USD since January 2022.
