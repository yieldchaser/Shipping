Reporting week: 02 JUNE - 05 JUNE 2026

# MARKET SUMMARY

## INDIA

The Indian market recorded a modest correction this week; however, this should not be viewed as a sign of stronger sentiment but rather a market adjustment following an earlier high-priced vessel sale.

A key development this week was the recyclers’ association directing yards to introduce loading charges, while also asking recyclers to restore stricter payment discipline, with mills expected to settle on a next-day basis or within a maximum credit period of 10 days, after payment cycles had loosened during recent geopolitical tensions.

While these measures are expected to improve liquidity for ship recyclers, the additional loading costs and tighter payment structure could place pressure on downstream buyers and mills.

As a result, the local market may face some short-term weakness in the coming week as participants adjust to the revised commercial framework and assess its impact on margins.

# BANGLADESH

The Bangladesh market softened slightly this week, with local prices easing on a dayto-day basis.

Despite the softer local market, interest remains visible for mid-sized assets, which continue to be the preferred tonnage for buyers.

Bangladesh remains the highest-paying recycling destination compared to the other major markets, supporting continued buyer interest.

# PAKISTAN

The Pakistan market remained firm this week, with buyers actively looking for vessels and overall sentiment staying strong.

Local market conditions continue to support prices, particularly due to a shortage of available goods.

This shortage has added strength to buyer sentiment and kept the market relatively positive compared to other destinations.

# TURKIYE

The Turkish market remained quiet over the past week, with limited activity and very few transactions reported.

Market sentiment continues to be cautious, while trading levels remain subdued.

Looking ahead, market direction remains difficult to predict due to domestic political uncertainty, regional geopolitical tensions, and continued depreciation of the Turkish Lira.
