Reporting week: 23 MAY - 01 JUNE 2026

# MARKET SUMMARY

This week, the shipping industry and broader maritime market attention remained focused on Posidonia in Greece, a key platform for industry engagement, market insights, and commercial discussions. Relevant developments and key takeaways from the event will be consolidated and shared in the upcoming weekly update.

# INDIA

The Indian market remained highly unpredictable this week, with prices holding steady and overall sentiment staying soft amid limited activity.

Buyer appetite remains firm nonetheless, supported by a steady flow of vessels arriving in India, including a notable share of dark fleet tonnage, while improved steel availability has traders looking to tighten recycling payment terms.

End prices remain the same whether recycling a standard vessel or a dark fleet unit; however, with dark fleet tonnage acquired at cheaper rates, conventional recyclers are finding it increasingly hard to compete.

On the policy front, the Government issued its first-ever ship recycling credit note under the new scheme, confirming the mechanism is now operational, a positive step towards strengthening India's competitiveness as a recycling destination over the longer term.

# BANGLADESH

The Bangladesh market remained largely the same as last week, with conditions broadly unchanged.

Following the Eid break, yards have resumed operations, though activity stays quiet for now, with no sales reported and overall buying reduced.

Rates have softened alongside the slower pace of business, keeping the market subdued at present.

That said, momentum is expected to build over the course of this week as operations settle back into rhythm.

# PAKISTAN

With the market closed for the Eid holidays over the past week, no sales were concluded and conditions held steady from before the break.

Buyers remain keen for fresh tonnage, though limited vessel availability continues to constrain purchasing.

On the regulatory front, Pakistan passed new ship recycling legislation this week advancing the Hong Kong Convention, introducing mandatory hazardous-material surveys and certification for vessels arriving for recycling - a positive step for the longer-term growth of the country's recycling sector.

# TURKIYE

The Turkish market has remained relatively quiet over the past week, with limited activity, few notable transactions, and cautious sentiment keeping trading levels subdued.

Despite the current lull, visits and inspections at Turkish yards by several major container operators offer some encouragement, raising hopes of larger recycling projects and a pick-up in activity going forward.

Forecasting remains difficult, however, as internal politics, regional tensions, and the continued depreciation of the Lira sustain a high level of uncertainty over future prices and market direction.
