Reporting week: 16 MAY - 22 MAY 2026

# MARKET SUMMARY

The EU Environmental Crime Directive (Directive (EU) 2024/1203) continues to draw significant attention within the recycling sector following the formal inclusion of unlawful ship recycling as a criminal offence. The directive is expected to increase compliance scrutiny on recycling destinations and further strengthen the importance of environmentally compliant recycling practices across the industry.

# INDIA

The Indian market softened further this week, with local prices coming off while the U.S. Dollar continued to strengthen, keeping overall sentiment under pressure.

Despite the weaker market, demand from buyers remains healthy due to the limited availability of recycling candidates. For non-sanctioned vessels, particularly units not associated with the dark fleet, there still remains a good chance of securing firm buyer interest.

One recycling yard has reportedly received U.S. approval to recycle sanctioned vessels, and we have also submitted our application, which is progressing through the approval process. We remain hopeful of receiving the necessary approval soon.

With the ASRY–Priya Blue joint venture now in place in Bahrain, the yard has already received its first vessel, a barge, for recycling. Please keep us posted on any suitable recycling opportunities for Bahrain.

There are also market discussions suggesting that authorities may consider requiring vessels to be Indian-flagged prior to recycling delivery. While nothing has been formally confirmed, the industry remains concerned that such a move could discourage owners from recycling in India and divert tonnage toward competing destinations.

# BANGLADESH

Bangladesh buyers remain keen to acquire tonnage, although prices have softened from previous levels.

Market participants are closely monitoring expectations of an increase in import duty on scrap vessels, anticipated during the first week of June, resulting in a more cautious approach from buyers on pricing levels.

Overall sentiment remains steady, although buyers are maintaining conservative numbers until further clarity emerges regarding the proposed duty adjustments.

# PAKISTAN

The Pakistan market remains largely unchanged this week, with sentiment continuing to hold firm.

Supply constraints continue to support the market, as limited scrap, HMS and shredded material is being received from the Middle East, while fresh vessel availability also remains limited.

Overall, the shortage of imported material and limited tonnage supply are keeping price ideas firm, although buying activity remains selective.

# TURKIYE

The recycling market in Turkiye remains relatively stable this week, although sentiment continues to be affected by the depreciation of the Turkish lira, which has limited buying appetite and kept activity for fresh tonnage subdued.

Most recycling yards, particularly EU-approved facilities, are operating at high capacity levels, resulting in limited availability for prompt deliveries and a cautious approach across the market.

Overall, market participants remain watchful of currency movements and yard capacity developments, with sentiment expected to stay steady but cautious in the near term.
