Reporting week: 25 JULY - 31 JULY 2026

# MARKET SUMMARY

## INDIA

The Alang ship recycling market showed some improvement in sentiment during the week, with buyers actively seeking tonnage after losing several recent opportunities to Chittagong and Gadani.

Although local market fundamentals remain relatively weak and overall activity is still below the levels seen in Bangladesh and Pakistan, some buying interest continues to be observed.

Recent sales involving dark fleet vessels have provided limited support to market activity, while buyers remain selective on available tonnage.

The Indian government's plans to develop port, ship repair, and shipbuilding infrastructure in and around Gujarat may provide support to the regional maritime economy over the longer term.

# BANGLADESH

The Bangladeshi ship recycling market remained firm during the week, with buying interest continuing to strengthen.

Chittagong recyclers maintained a strong appetite for tonnage despite a recent decline in local melting scrap and steel plate prices.

With the monsoon season nearing its end and an increasing number of HKC-compliant recycling yards becoming operational, demand for recycling tonnage is expected to rise further.

These positive market fundamentals are expected to support steady and gradually improving price levels in the coming weeks.

# PAKISTAN

Market conditions remained healthy during the week, supported by firm demand from local recyclers.

The continued absence of steel imports from Iran has helped sustain domestic demand and positive buyer sentiment.

Recyclers remain interested in available tonnage, with conditions expected to remain steady in the near term.

# TURKIYE

Conditions remained largely unchanged during the week, with no notable movement in vessel supply or buyer demand.

Activity continued at a steady pace, while overall sentiment remained stable.

Türkiye continues to attract owners seeking compliance with EU Ship Recycling Regulations, though this remains a relatively limited segment.

The significant price gap with recyclers in the Indian subcontinent continues to restrict competitiveness, with most conventional recycling candidates expected to move toward the higher-paying subcontinent markets.
