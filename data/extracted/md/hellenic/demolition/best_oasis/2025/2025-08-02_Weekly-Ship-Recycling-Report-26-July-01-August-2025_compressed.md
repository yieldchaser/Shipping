Reporting week: 26 July - 01 August 2025

# Highlights of the week

Ship recycling markets across South Asia and Türkiye remained quiet overall this week, with limited activity and sentiment still soft. Local pricing in most destinations held at last week’s levels, but the broader picture continues to show a lack of momentum, with buyers across the region maintaining a cautious approach. In India, the market stayed steady with no downward pressure on local prices. However, buyers have slowed their activity, and the overall mood has turned more watchful. The strong dollar and its impact on costs remain a concern, overshadowing otherwise firm pricing levels. Bangladesh showed no improvement either, with buyers staying on the sidelines and unwilling to move higher on price. Their reluctance to adjust expectations has left the market flat and momentum lacking, even as conditions remain unchanged week on week. Pakistan continued in the same weak tone as before. Prices are at last week’s levels, but sentiment is soft and there has been little sign of any buyer participation. The overall market there remains stagnant, with no indication of a shift in direction. Türkiye mirrored this subdued regional trend. Activity stayed slow, with no visible movement in the market. Prices held at last week’s levels, though pressure for a possible decline is gradually building given the weak demand environment. Taken together, the region continues to face a muted period, with buyers holding back and little urgency to move. Pricing has held steady for now, but without stronger demand or a shift in sentiment, the outlook remains soft across all key markets.

On the global front, renewed US tariffs have reignited trade tensions, adding further uncertainty to market sentiment worldwide.

# India

The market remains steady, but buyer activity is constrained by currency pressures.

The local market stayed unchanged compared to last week, with no downward pressure. Prices held steady, which is a positive sign for now.

The recent surge in the US dollar against the rupee, triggered by new tariff announcements from the US, has increased import costs and squeezed margins. This has made buyers more cautious despite stable local market conditions. As a result, buyer activity has slowed, reflecting concerns over profitability rather than weakness in domestic demand.

Going forward, stability in the exchange rate will be key. Unless currency pressure eases, margins will remain tight and buying momentum will likely stay muted.

# Bangladesh

Market activity stays muted, with buyers waiting and resisting higher price levels

The Bangladesh market has seen no activity this week, as buyers continue to remain on the sidelines. Rather than committing, they are carefully watching for the right opportunities before stepping back in.

Buyers have not raised their price levels and prefer to stay positioned at the lower end of the market. This cautious approach reflects their reluctance to commit at higher numbers, even as the market stays steady.

With this stance, overall momentum has stalled. Unless there is a shift in buyer sentiment or a change in market dynamics, activity is likely to remain muted in the coming weeks.
