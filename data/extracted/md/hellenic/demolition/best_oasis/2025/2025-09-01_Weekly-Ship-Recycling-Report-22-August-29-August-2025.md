Reporting week: 22 August - 29 August 2025

# Highlights of the week

Ship recycling markets across South Asia and Türkiye remained largely steady but subdued this week, with activity levels staying muted in most regions. In India, the market saw a slight softening after holding stable in recent weeks, while domestic steel sales remain slower than usual due to seasonal weather and the approaching festive period. In Bangladesh, overall conditions remain weak, with HKC compliance delays continuing to limit yard availability and restrict buying activity. Smaller buyers remain mostly inactive, and concluding deals for small vessels has become increasingly difficult, often resulting in lower pricing levels. Pakistan also experienced quiet market conditions, with limited buying interest and few fresh transactions. The recent floods in Punjab province have added further pressure to an already slow market, alongside sluggish demand and restricted participation. In Türkiye, sentiment remains largely unchanged from recent weeks. Although there are reports of increased steel mill activity, this has yet to reflect in local or import pricing, leaving the overall market direction uncertain for now.

China has recorded a 320% year-on-year surge in semi-finished steel exports, reaching 7.4 million tons between January and July 2025, with July alone contributing 1.5 million tons. The rise, driven by weaker domestic demand and a strategic push toward overseas markets, is providing countries like Indonesia, the Philippines, Turkey, Italy, and Saudi Arabia with more affordable raw materials. This export momentum could influence global steel prices, shift trade dynamics, and intensify competition among international producers.

# India

The market shows a mild correction; prices stabilize as buyer sentiment steadies.

After maintaining stable levels last week, the market has seen a slight softening, with prices now settling around ₹37,800 per metric ton for 8 ANI-grade steel scrap. Based on current trends, prices are expected to remain range-bound in the near term.

Buyer sentiment remains positive but has eased slightly compared to recent weeks, shifting from an aggressive stance to a more measured and steady approach.

On the domestic front, steel end-product sales have slowed somewhat, impacted by seasonal weather conditions and the upcoming festival period across India, which is temporarily affecting consumption patterns.

# Bangladesh

The market activity stays largely muted amid prolonged HKC delays.

The local market continues to remain subdued, with no meaningful upside expected in the near term.

The focus remains on HKC compliance, as the market awaits clarity on when additional yards will resume operations. The prolonged lack of development has kept smaller-sized buyers on the sidelines, limiting overall activity. In this environment, selling small vessels into Bangladesh is becoming increasingly challenging, with deals likely being concluded at significantly lower price levels.

Until there is progress on HKC implementation and yard readiness, the market is likely to stay muted, with restricted participation and low buying momentum.

# Pakistan

The market activity continues to stay subdued amid ongoing disruptions.

There have been no significant changes observed in the market this week, with overall conditions continuing to remain subdued and activity levels largely inactive.

Momentum has slowed further, impacted by the major floods in Punjab province, which have disrupted operations and weighed heavily on overall market sentiment.

Alongside the floods, factors such as muted buyer interest, slower demand, and a lack of fresh transactions are also contributing to the subdued activity levels.
