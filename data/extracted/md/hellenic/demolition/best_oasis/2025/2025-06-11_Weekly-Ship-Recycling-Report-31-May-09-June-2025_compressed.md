Reporting week: 31 May - 09 June 2025

# Highlights of the week

A belated Eid al-Adha Mubarak to all our readers. With markets across key recycling destinations closed during the holiday period, last week’s report was not issued on Friday. As operations now begin to resume, early market signals are beginning to emerge.

The global ship recycling market faced a broadly weaker trend this week, shaped by holidays, price corrections, and continued regulatory anticipation. In India, prices declined further under local pressure, and sentiment remains subdued. Bangladesh remained inactive due to Eid holidays, with the market expected to reopen mid-June. Pakistan also saw minimal activity, with a few buyers active ahead of the Hong Kong Convention deadline, but overall momentum stayed limited. Yard compliance efforts continue, though clarity remains lacking. In Türkiye, both import and domestic prices fell amid weak demand and post-holiday slowdown. As the 26 June HKC enforcement date nears, attention across all markets is shifting toward compliance readiness and post-holiday direction.

Global trade tensions are heating up again as sweeping U.S. tariffs introduced under President Trump begin to take effect, rattling confidence in supply chains and triggering concerns about growth. The move has already led to a downgrade in global economic forecasts, with the OECD warning of broad consequences if the situation escalates. Against this backdrop, senior officials from the U.S. and China are meeting in London this week for a new round of trade talks. While expectations are cautious, the stakes are high. From critical mineral exports to tariff rollbacks, the outcomes of these discussions could either open a path toward de-escalation or push markets further into uncertainty. What unfolds in London may well set the tone for global trade in the months ahead.

# India

Market drifts lower with no light at the end of the tunnel and sentiment fading.

The market has come off notably in the past week, with increased local issues adding pressure. Sentiment remains weak, and there appears to be little hope for a near-term recovery.

The only potential hope lies in a change in policy that would allow ship-recycled steel to be used in the production of TMT bars. As previously reported, the Steel Ministry had rejected this proposal, which has severely limited domestic demand and weighed on overall sentiment.

The Indian Ship Recyclers Association is currently working on the issue. If their efforts lead to any progress, it could help revive the market. Until then, the outlook remains weak.

# Bangladesh

Market remains quiet during Eid as attention turns to HKC and post-holiday trends.

The market has remained inactive this week, with operations largely on hold due to the Eid al-Adha holidays. The break, which began on 5th June, will continue until 15th June, effectively bringing the market to a standstill.

As the holiday period comes to an end, attention will turn to how the market reopens. In the coming days, we will begin to see whether any momentum returns and how pricing and sentiment develop heading into the second half of the month.

Additionally, with the Hong Kong Convention set to come into force on 26 June, the weeks ahead could prove significant. How Bangladesh aligns with compliance expectations and regulatory processes may influence both market confidence and volume going forward.

# Pakistan

The market remains slow with few deals as HKC deadline approaches.

The market remained slow this week, with only a few buyers active. As noted in our earlier updates, some purchases were concluded in recent weeks, mainly driven by the upcoming Hong Kong Convention deadline rather than any shift in sentiment. Activity was further subdued by the ongoing Eid holidays, keeping overall momentum low.

Around 8 to 10 yards are believed to have submitted plans for HKC compliance. However, according to local sources, only one or two may be in a position to meet the required standards.

With no official confirmation on yard approvals, the broader market remains on pause. Activity is expected to stay limited until more clarity emerges closer to the 26 June implementation.

# Türkiye

The market softens further amid weak sentiment and holiday slowdown.

Activity remained limited last week, largely due to the holiday period. Import prices softened by about USD 3, while the local market saw a sharper decline of around USD 5.

Sentiment remains weak, with no signs of recovery in buying interest. The continued pressure on both import and domestic pricing has raised concerns about further downward adjustments in the weeks ahead, especially if demand fails to pick up.

Türkiye’s economy grew 2.0% year-on-year in Q1 2025, down from 3.0% in the previous quarter. Seasonally and calendar-adjusted growth was 1.0% quarter-on-quarter. The slowdown reflects tighter monetary conditions, with full-year growth expected to ease slightly from 2024’s 3.2%.
