Reporting week: 10 May - 16 May 2025

# Highlights of the week

The global ship recycling market remained largely stagnant this week, with limited activity across key regions. In India, conditions were unchanged, with persistent liquidity issues and weak steel demand keeping sentiment subdued. Bangladesh also saw little movement, as buying interest stayed low amid rising regulatory uncertainty. Several vessels await clearance, but the lack of NOCs for non-HKC-compliant yards has stalled progress ahead of the convention's enforcement. Pakistan continued to remain inactive, with no improvement in demand. However, focus is slowly shifting to how the market will respond to upcoming compliance requirements. In Türkiye, prices held steady despite a soft market, though slight adjustments may emerge in the coming week.

Since last week’s heightened tensions between India and Pakistan, the situation has eased following the announcement of a ceasefire. The de-escalation is a welcome development, aligning with hopes for regional stability and continuity in cross-border engagement. The reduced military activity brings a measure of relief to the broader South Asian landscape, particularly in light of both nations’ economic significance.

In a new development, Germany has approved its first ship recycling yard, aligning with international standards and expanding Europe’s regulated dismantling capacity.

# Bangladesh

Market movement softens while NOC approvals remain out of reach for non-green yards.

The Bangladeshi recycling market remains quiet this week, with little buying interest and no notable sales activity, reflecting the soft local market and growing uncertainty around regulatory constraints.

Several vessels are currently in the pipeline, but with no NOCs being issued to nongreen, non-HKC-compliant yards, progress has stalled. Five units are reportedly waiting, though their prospects remain uncertain given the current regulatory environment.

With the HKC enforcement date drawing closer, the disconnect between available capacity and compliance continues to weigh on the market, keeping momentum low.

Bangladesh has sought an additional $762 million from the IMF, pushing total support to USD 4.1 billion as it navigates persistent economic pressures.

# Pakistan

The market shows no movement, with HKC now on the horizon and uncertainty lingering.

Market conditions remain unchanged and continues to stay on the sidelines, with no improvement in demand or activity.

With the Hong Kong Convention deadline approaching, this market remains one to watch. While there’s no immediate shift, the coming weeks could bring clarity on how recyclers plan to align with upcoming compliance requirements.

On the industry front, Pakistan and Russia have agreed to build a new steel mill in Karachi. The two countries had previously worked together in the 1970s when the Soviet Union helped Pakistan build Steel Mills.
