CONFUSING VALENTINES! 
The second week of February brought on a really confusing spin to the markets, with fundamentals 
across the board performing bizarrely (compared to last week’s performance), as after a volatile 
beginning to the month, the Baltic Exchange Dry Index and even oil futures found steady ground 
this week, while even the U.S. Dollar firmed against all ship recycling nation currencies except India 
(ain’t that a switch), as did local steel plate prices, which also saw India slip the car into reverse and 
backpedal nearly all of its recent gains. 
In an expanded view, the Baltic Exchange’s Dry Bulk Index fell 0.6% this week, pressured primarily 
by the larger-sized segments, as Capes fell nearly 2% and the Panamax index slipped a matching 
0.6%, Supras marched (improved) an impressive 1.8% of their own. Sliding into the barrel, we see 
WTI crude oil futures pull a drone maneuver and hover around (a lower) USD 62.8/barrel, as 
markets, investors, and traders collectively worry about the bacteria brewing under the U.S./Iranian 
nuclear peace carpet once again, and fears over ongoing supply being drastically affected could 
have a devastating effect on oil prices the world over. 
But for the ship recycling world, it’s likely to be even quieter as Chine enters its New Year holiday 
period, an already subdued (in terms of volumes, not price/demand) international ship recycling 
market is more than likely to be deprived of potential recycling candidates even further. The 
incoming Year of the Horse hopefully brings with it some better times for the industry, after what 
has been (and seemingly continues to be) a record low delivery of recycling tonnage over the last 
several years, and depreciating levels from the highs of USD 600/LDT down to well below USD 
400/LDT, have been a jolt to end-of-life residual values of ships and the books of owners/operators.
Recycling markets do seem to have stabilized within the region of the turbulence and for the time 
being, events are conspiring in favor of sub-continent markets that paint a brighter picture moving 
forward. The much anticipated (and long overdue) Bangladeshi elections were finally settled this 
week with an expected victory for the second largest party in the country (behind disgraced and 
banned former PM Sheikh Hasina’s party), as the BNP claimed power with over a 2/3 majority. The 
Pakistani market remains the firmest for another week as the cheap flood of Iranian imports has 
ceased for the time being, and Gadani steel plate prices continue to dominate industry levels. 
And with currency concerns consuming India’s sanity since the start of the year having stabilized 
this week as well (even showing signs of improvement) and Turkey on the far side slipping back 
into obscurity after a brief binge of RoRos, overall, it does seem that things couldn’t get any worse 
than they have, and levels may have reached a state of equilibrium for the year (unless something 
happens). The only thing left is for supply to deliver on its side of the bargain.

BNP IN! 
After what has seemed like an eternity (and then some) of a wait, encouraging political news finally 
emerged through from the beleaguered Bangladeshi market this week, as the interim student-led
regime finally made way for the country’s second largest party, the Bangladesh Nationalist Party 
(BNP), into the leadership seat with more than a 2/3 majority win during the nation’s recent General 
Elections.  
This (hopefully) means that the long-awaited (and quite frankly, desperately needed) domestic 
infrastructure projects can finally get off the drawing board and into the national eye, which should 
ignite domestic construction projects and resume demand for steel once again. While there is likely 
(more like hopefully, with Trump still at the helm of the global economic reins) to be some 
increased optimism in the market now that a far more progressive, business-friendly, and locally 
popular party is finally in charge, reality may have its own say on the matter. Time will tell, eh? 
Meanwhile, following a request by the government to follow all international HKC regulations,
this week also saw the BSBRA adopt the provisions of the International Ready for Recycling 
Certificate (IRRC), as is also the case now in Pakistan and India. This would mean that once the 
mandatory IHM Parts I, II, and III have been provided to the ship recycling yard by the Sellers, the 
relevant recycling yard must then produce a Ship Recycling Plan (SRP), which will then be 
presented to the class / flag state authorities (once available), in order to produce an IRRC — a fresh 
requirement for any vessels now arriving any sub-continent ship recycling destination. 
On the fundamentals side, all was not rosy, especially given that the Taka lost 17 basis points 
against the U.S. Dollar and closed the week out at BDT 122.25, on a trajectory that seems to indicate 
even more falls next week. Local steel plate prices have, meanwhile refused to leave their 
hammocks as they continue to flatline at nearly USD 494/ton for nearly 3 weeks now, indicating an
utter lack of trade with local steel mills, which itself presents a massive problem for Chattogram 
yards if they are unable to sell any of the recycled ship’s steel. 
But that certainly hasn’t stopped them from keeping an eye out for the right “type” of unit, as not 
only did local recyclers manage to reel in another collection of smaller LDT units including a 7K 
LDT bulker, it was the arrival of a 24K LDT unit that confirmed why Chattogram choppers 
willingly loosen their belts at the recycling buffets.  
As such, despite what has happened/might happen in the nation, the right type/LDT combination 
of unit will bring a Bangladeshi buyer out of the woodwork and strike a deal. 
Loose belts.

INDIA
CLIFF HANGER! 
The Alang market continues to leave the industry on a cliff-hanger, week after week, with vessel prices 
moving, fundamentals violently shaking the sanity of everyone’s innards, and a hungry landscape of 
ship recyclers who remain on the lookout for tonnage fueled by nothing more than a hefty serving of 
nervy sentiments and the gravity of hope. 
And even though India may be at the bottom of the ship recycling ranking board for yet another week, 
Alang recyclers have managed to remain quizzically competitive with their neighbors on any of the 
slim pickings of vessels that do sail onto the bidding tables, as is amply evident by the state of Alang’s 
anchorage this week, where well over 47K LDT is idling / has been delivered to its new owners, 
including 2 rare large LDT tankers — certainly a sight for hungry eyes. 
From Alang’s performance at the bidding tables to the reality on the beaches, one thing is for certain: 
the ongoing lack of tonnage is seeing the ship recycling fraternity (regardless of destination) reacting 
rather unexpectedly as they’re now seemingly making a dash for the winning line when the right type 
of unit is tabled. And it’s easy to see why. 
Fundamentals, meanwhile, continue to rubber-band the local mindset into frenzies after immediate 
declines, where even this week, we saw local steel plate prices systematically fell nearly USD 10/ton 
and reversed a majority of its recent gains, all while the Indian Rupee decided heading north was a 
better option and reclaimed another 15 basis points against the U.S. Dollar, to end the week at Rs. 90.6. 
Overall, the election victory for the BNP in Bangladesh may ease some of the tensions between India 
and Bangladesh, as a settled and elected party finally takes control, and former disgraced PM Sheikh 
Hasina, who has been sentenced to death in absentia, continues to live in exile in Delhi despite demands 
from the Bangladeshi government for her return to face the consequences of her trial, having fled 
Bangladesh in August 2024. 
Steel respite.

PAKISTAN
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
    
PAPA’S HUNGRY! 
Gadani continues to lead the way for yet another week despite an improving Bangladesh 
narrowing the gap (in the wake of the election victory for the BNP). Yet, the Gadani gang remained 
comfortable negotiating on (critically) few workable fresh candidates in order to satisfy what has 
been a corrosive demand fueled by the acidic lack of supply that is only expected to get worse now 
that the traditionally quieter Chinese New Year holidays are under way. Truth be told, it would 
have to be a cold day in Lucifer’s bathtub for a Chinese-owned unit to be delivered into Gadani, 
but stranger things have happened, as reality is weirder than fiction. 
And other than leading the recycling ranking charts, Pakistan has also been dominating sub-
continent currency and steel plate price performances over the last couple of quarters, given that 
not only has the Pakistani Rupee firmed to below PKR 280, but local steel plate prices even rose
recently and have remained atop the industry numbers. 
Moreover, this week’s performance wrench that affected all markets, managed to keep Pakistan
more or less in the clear, as it ended the week just a few basis points weaker at a still healthy PKR 
279.59. Local steel plate prices, too, remain even-keeled at an industry-fantastic PKR 594/ton —
USD 100/ton more than those from Bangladesh and about USD 190/ton above those from Alang. 
And these improvements, backed by news of numerous local market and private fixtures, saw 
Gadani’s waterfront light up with a series of decent LDT bulk carriers that collectively carried the 
total volume of tonnage at anchorage to nearly 30K LDT via 4 vessels this week. A massive upgrade
for local buyers compared to their silent operating mode over the last several months, where the 
occasional small LDT vessel would arrive locally, be delayed, and eventually beach. 
Finally, HKC infrastructure works also continue at pace in Gadani, with two ship recycling yards 
now having received their HKC accreditations and upgrades on remaining yards still ongoing and 
reportedly even making significant progress, as Pakistan may continue to dominate the rankings 
should the current trends persist.
