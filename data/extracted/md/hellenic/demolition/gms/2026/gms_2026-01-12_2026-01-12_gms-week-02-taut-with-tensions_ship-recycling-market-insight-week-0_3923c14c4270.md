TAUT WITH TENSIONS! 
If 2026 continues as it has started, we are more than likely in for another roller-coaster year full of 
geopolitical risks, tensions, aggravations, and increasingly frequent shocks that could affect the 
shipping and ship recycling industries in numerous — and as yet untold — ways. 
Trump’s kidnapping (let’s call it what it is) of President Maduro and his wife from Venezuela —
followed by U.S. claims that it will “run” the country when Trump can’t even handle the U.S. — has
already set the tone for the madness we can expect in 2026. Amping up the octogenarian crankiness, 
he also threatened to take Greenland as a U.S. territory this week, drawing swift condemnation from 
Russia, China, and even senior Congressional Republicans on Capitol Hill, who confirmed that 
Congress would never agree to such overtly aggressive actions on behalf of the U.S. The subsequent 
capture of a Russian-controlled oil tanker carrying Venezuelan oil sent further shocks through the 
industry, raising the question of which other wild move this administration is willing to inflict on 
the world. 
Markets reacted as they always do i.e. with chaos and volatility. The Baltic Exchange reported 
another bleak week of declines for the fifth straight Friday, falling about 1.7%, dragged down by 
capes (down 3%) and supramaxes (down 9 points), while panamaxes jumped about 0.7% by Friday’s 
close. Oil meanwhile managed to rise just about USD 59.50/barrel, extending its gain for a third 
consecutive session. 
Currencies went on a tear once again, with the U.S. Dollar inflicting pain in Turkey and India (once 
again), while retreating in the Pakistani and Bangladeshi markets. Last week’s reported increase in 
local steel plate prices was also met with declining levels, even though the weekly moves might 
suggest otherwise — as current pricing remains lower than where it stood two weeks ago. 
Trickling down to the ship recycling sector, while India has enjoyed a resurgent start to the year on 
the back of local steel plate prices that jumped nearly USD 30/Ton, this week saw local sentiment 
turning negative again as plate levels retreated just as rapidly. Pakistan and Bangladesh meanwhile
responded in kind with improved demand and pricing of their own as activity starts to seemingly 
heat up ahead of the inevitable slowdown for Chinese New Year at the end of January into February
and Far Eastern tonnage typically takes another break from Bangla waters. Local deliveries also 
showed marginal improvement, with just a couple of candidates making it to sub-continent 
anchorages this week. 
As such, after the end-of-2025 decline in pricing, owners with vintage tonnage and surveys due may 
look to test the market again as levels hover around the USD 400/LDT mark, which are still strong 
– all things considered.

TAILS UP! 
After the stark declines towards the tail-end of Q4 2025 followed by vanishing demand and 
collapsing local offerings that saw Chattogram choppers land at the bottom of the market rankings 
just last week, signs of life and recovery emerged locally this week 
However, with the wild performance of India’s fundamentals and dithering local sentiments there,
demand in Bangladesh started to firm, despite political uncertainty reportedly reaching a feverous
pitch amidst tensions and outbreaks of violence continue, with the nation’s critical elections now 
just about a month away. Moreover, following the recent death of the first female PM to run the 
country and the assassination of a youth leader — both within weeks — episodes of violent protest 
engulfed Bangladesh in the aftermath, leaving many hoping for a more serene and orderly election 
process ahead. 
Locally, the still-deflated level of supply saw just one fresh vessel arrive and make its way to 
delivery, alongside all of the units that arrived the week prior, leaving Chattogram’s anchorage 
dead as the week ended. This cross-border see-saw — which saw empty anchorages in Alang and 
Gadani just last week — may soon see Chattogram tie the competition with a likely empty 
anchorage of its own in the coming week(s). 
Several L/Cs were meanwhile held up over the recent tide due to a change in personnel at the 
Central Bank in Dhaka, with additional documentary requirements introduced since the change to 
push these L/Cs through. For the most part, however, the fact that Chattogram’s anchorage is now 
empty, indicates that local deliveries did manage to pull through. 
Fundamentals meanwhile remain frail, as the Taka gained a mere nine basis points against the U.S. 
Dollar and walked out the Friday door at BDT 122.26. Local steel plate prices, on the other hand, 
finally started trading once again and opened mid-week around USD 500/ton — not the best place 
to be, but still not as grim as India, which is nearly USD 100/ton lower and yet continues to outbid 
Bangladesh at the tables.  
Clearly, no one locally wants to eat on a full stomach. 
If local recyclers don’t make it back into the fray by end of Monday, it seems all will be quiet locally 
— at least until elections conclude.

INDIA
QUICK REVERSE! 
After what seemed to be some serious improvements in local steel plate prices post-New Year in India 
— with levels jumping nearly USD 30/ton last week, this week, they opened USD 6/ton lower than last 
and ended up wiping out all of the recent gains, sliding back down to USD 400/ton once again. Not 
wanting to be outdone, the Rupee chased the U.S. Dollar as it lost another 20 basis points this week 
and closed at Rs. 90.21. 
Given that Alang buyers tend to be a nervy bunch, sentiment has weakened, and end buyers have 
become more cautious about being boisterous with their offers, for fear of further falls ahead — 
particularly amid the global political and economic uncertainty besetting markets at present. At the 
same time, a USD 30/ton swing in the core pricing mechanism of recycling vessels is not something to 
be scoffed at. 
Mercifully, tonnage supply remains weak, so these expectedly lower indications are unlikely to lead 
anywhere in the interim. But even as supply remains thin, India did manage to crawl out of “empty 
anchorage” syndrome as at least one 2,500 LDT vessel greeted the waterfront last week, with another 
two reportedly waiting to come in. 
Notwithstanding, sustained performance — and a bit of courage — will be needed in Alang for 
recyclers to secure their share of any market tonnage that becomes available, especially as Pakistan and 
Bangladesh warm up to negotiating at the tables again, albeit with Bangladesh potentially in for a short 
nap until election day. 
Weakened.

PAKISTAN
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
    
INTO THE BREACH! 
Pakistan has been looking to get back into the action since the start of the year, with enquiries 
emerging from buyers whose yards are in the process of being upgraded to HKC standards. 
However, local offerings have (as has been the trend over the last 2.5 decades from this market) 
remained just behind those of India, and more aggressive posturing will be needed to secure 
tonnage and fill local plots again — especially given that local fundamentals remain a breath of 
fresh air compared to the competition, with both the PKR and local steel plate prices displaying far 
more stability across the region. 
Not only did local steel plate prices firm by about USD 6/ton this week, they also closed the week 
out at USD 585/ton — USD 185/ton higher than India and an USD 85/ton margin over Bangladesh. 
It certainly seems as though Iranian imports may have eased off the brakes for now, given Iran’s 
own domestic upheaval at present. At the same time, the Pakistani Rupee gained seven basis points 
against the U.S. Dollar and closed the week at PKR 280.12 — inching its way back toward the PKR 
279s. 
With so much financial artillery in Gadani’s possession, it was shocking to see the market end last 
year with just 202 total vessel s delivered locally. Seemingly this year — this poor waterfront 
performance seems likely to continue especially as HKC yards remain scarce and provisional 
DASR certifications (issued while HKC upgrade work continues) remain a barrier to fresh inflows. 
This has left the market barren at the waterfront, and for the second week in a row in 2026, Gadani’s 
anchorage ended the week on an empty note. 
Speaking of inflow, with the supply of tonnage expected to increase in 2026, Pakistan ship recyclers 
need to be ready — and willing — to step into the breach at meaningful numbers, with certified 
yards in place, in order to fill plots and secure their share of the recycling action.

Aliaga’s ship recycling sector seems to have gone into winter hibernation, with little noteworthy 
news emerging other than the ever-degrading Turkish lira, which lost another 23 basis points this 
week and closed at TRY 43.14 against the U.S. Dollar. 
While local deliveries have remained lower than those seen in Pakistan, it is not for a want of tonnage, 
but rather the need for a more logical application of EU laws so businesses do not suffocate under 
media pressure and NGO scrutiny. However, with the sub-continent now formally operating under 
the HKC, it remains to be seen whether any knock-on changes will be forthcoming for Aliaga in 2026.
If the last five years are any indication — and especially if the lira continues to slip — local offers will 
continue to fall, and EOL vessel values will ultimately fetch even less for the banks who have 
mortgaged assets out. What happens then?
