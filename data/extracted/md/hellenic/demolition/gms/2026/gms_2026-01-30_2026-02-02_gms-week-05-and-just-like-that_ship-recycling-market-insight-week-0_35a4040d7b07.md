### Editorial: AND JUST LIKE THAT…

… January 2026 joins the pages of history as being the purveyors of ongoing misery for the global ship recycling world as despite the odd wave of tonnage that has made us numb to the conflicting signals of the markets based on supply trends across the last 5 years, something eye-catching did change this week. And it would seem that the rising ripples from Trump’s train wreck of activity has posed global markets to setup a serious “Debbie Downer” for February 2026, especially if the current trends persist.

To start, we have noteworthy surges to report this week. Across the board, for most of the major fundamentals, except sub-continent steel plate prices that have become the perennial party poopers of the recycling world of late, the momentum was definitely beyond noticeable. The Baltic Exchange Dry Index, oil futures, and the U.S. Dollar collectively soared this week, creating ripples that would certainly be felt within the next 4 weeks. While the dry index soared 7.3% on the back of Capes pole- vaulting 12%, Panamaxes tripping up 1.6% in comparison, and Supras having a comparatively minor hiccup gain of 0.5%, oil futures impressively slid past USD 65/barrel and close the week out at USD 65.10 – a near 1% jump from last week.

And the U.S. Dollar’s performance across the ship recycling markets was reminiscent of Marilyn Monroe’s infamous wavy skirt subway photo, the wavy lines capturing just how volatile Trump has made global financial markets on the back of further tariff threats against Canada, nation states in Europe including France and Denmark, and even striking into the hearts of global ship recycling markets with Pakistan (surprisingly) taking the biggest of hits this week. Speaking of the ship recycling markets, they certainly seem to be having a global “up” moment, with India, Pakistan Bangladesh and Turkey all reportedly picking up some long overdue tonnage, although only India Bangladesh and Turkish anchorages are delivering the receipts to confirm these fixtures. India managed to even secure an LNG for strictly HKC recycling only and Turkey with its bevy of roros and “buffet-full bellies” confirming that all of the major ship recycling destinations collectively secured their share of available market vessels since the start of January 2026 (and in a long time).

## 3. Country Market Intelligence

### Bangladesh

- **Headline**: BACK TO BIZ?
- **Key Notes**: *Securing cape.* | *22 HKC yards.* | *Landmark elections.* | *Tick tock.*

After missing out on several vessels to sub-continent rivals (including an LNG to India and several panamax sized bulkers seemingly into Pakistan), it was certainly good to see Bangladeshi buyers get back in on the action at the bidding tables with the only reported capesize bulker acquisition of the week, and that reportedly too at decent levels, an oddity considering Bangladesh’s overstuffed and digestive state just last week.

And as purchases were grabbing the headlines at the waterfront, political headlines continue to grab the news inlands across the country, especially now with January passed and the nation’s landmark elections - the first in 2 years - standing just 2 weeks away. It has certainly been a long time coming for the country to finally find a modicum of political and economic stability, even if it potentially stands to be short-lived (just as the last election was), and given how much the nation has clearly struggled under the currently dysfunctional regime over the last 20 months, they are hopefully on their collective way out.

What has also been short-lived is Chattogram’s dance with an empty port position as clearly this week, several tankers of varying LDTs greeted the waterfront with one even being promptly delivered to its respective new owner already. News of the lowly local resale prices on some of the smaller tankers are emerging as well, with one reportedly achieving a meager USD 330/LT, hence the surprise at the reportedly firm price on this week’s reported acquisition.

Fundamentally, things look like they make solid pricing moves (at least in early February) a near uncertainty given the ongoing performance of local steel plate prices (currently dead on the floor for at least 2 weeks running), which are stranded at USD 487.24/ton. Add into the pricing cocktail, some spicy Tabasco of Bangladeshi Taka, which fell 1% this week and closed it out at BDT 122.95 against the U.S. Dollar, it seems like next week might be back to the pricing board for local buyers, before making any rash purchases in the immediate future.

Overall, with 22 yards (and counting) that now stand HKC approved, it has been encouraging to see some fierce bidding on HKC only tonnage (particularly LNGs) being offered to approved Chattogram yards. And his becomes even critical whenever the supply of green tonnage ramps up (tick tock reality, its 2026 already).

### India

- **Headline**: RABID RUPEE!
- **Key Notes**: *Belly jiggle.* | *Damp demand.* | *Cannot compete.* | *Triangular seesaw.*

The Indian Rupee has had a fundamental (pun intended) earthquake on an economic scale as it plummeted another 25 basis points by week’s end, and after briefly surging to Rs. 92.18 against the U.S. Dollar – currently still in excess of Rs. 93 for non-regulatory Dollar purchases (i.e. for regular people) – it closed shop for the weekend at Rs. 91.82.

Local steel plate prices added their own belly jiggle to this week’s entertainment as it fell to nearly USD 398/Ton before recovering to a – still lower than last week - level of about USD 401/Ton, dictating the destiny of upcoming offer levels in the coming week. And as is typical of the nervy Indian sentiment, amidst the perplexing historical lows seen in the performance of INR, of course we saw fears that the currency may even breach Rs. 100 against U.S. Dollar, ensuring future acquisitions get ever so dicier!

Ironically however, while on the one hand, this clearly placed a dampener on prevailing sentiments and demand, manifesting itself into the current state of local offerings that have ensured India remains at the bottom of the sub-continent pricing board, on the other hand, Alang was and has been the busiest of all anchorages as nearly 47K LDT via 6 vessels (including another large LDT tanker) earmarked the nation’s ship recycling waterfront this week.

But, as recycling nation currencies across the board have reacted negatively and in unison to the U.S. Dollar this week, could we see the same level of pullback from competing markets in the coming week(s) now that as Pakistan and Bangladesh have had their chance to stay notably ahead over these last couple of weeks? Crystall ball moment.

What is however certain, is that with another Pakistani yard not snagging a Class NK pedigree HKC approval in Gadani, the triangular see-saw of sub-continent ship recycling is about to get really interesting in the months and years ahead.

### Pakistan

- **Headline**: SECOND HKC UP!
- **Key Notes**: *Iranian halt.* | *Importing dry.* | *Right time.*

That’s not a “second hiccup” folks and you read that right. Gadani’s HKC recycling momentum has now welcomed a Class NK approved HKC certified ship recycling yard to its gradually growing collection, with Salams International now becoming the nation’s second ship recycling yard to earn this prestigious certification.

And while this will certainly challenge neighboring Alang’s purchasing prowess as time progresses, it truly welcomes Gadani’s infrastructure into the international arena and will hopefully generate greater confidence towards the nation’s industry.

Meanwhile, local steel plate prices, like those in Bangladesh, seem to be experiencing their own lights-out moment as they remained horizontal for the week, albeit at an industry-leading USD 587/ton. But after weeks of flaunting its stabilizing (and even firming) ability, it was the Pakistani Rupee that took home the attention of the industry, declining by about 1.4% and wrapping up the week at nearly PKR 282 once again.

In the interim (and reportedly), several decent sized bulker sales (largely handymax and panamax sized) have taken place to local buyers, as a result of Pakistan’s comparatively firmer offerings than India, and there is still a growing desire to import more vessels – particularly evident this week during a bidding contest over a capesize bulker for recycling.

Overall, slow and steady gains mixed in with better late than never is what the Gadani Gang seems to be prodigiously on, and with the timing of supply, the clock might just show the right time for Gadani soon enough.
