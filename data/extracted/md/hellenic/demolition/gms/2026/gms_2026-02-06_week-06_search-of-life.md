# Market Commentary

## SEARCH OF LIFE!

The weekly roller coaster that has become the global marketplace went through its regular gravitational drop this week, with the U.S. dollar taking the “cement-footed diving approach” in all ship-recycling destinations… except—you guessed it—Turkey!

The Baltic Exchange Dry Bulk Index fell 0.7% down to a two-week low, dragged by the Capesize index, which fell a massive 1.1% over the week. The Panamax index slipped a comparatively more acceptable 0.4%, while the Supramax index rose a modest 0.2% to even things out. Meanwhile, easing tensions between the U.S. and Iran alleviated concerns over potential supply disruptions erupting out of the Middle East (thanks, Donnie), resulting in oil falling below USD 62/barrel, yet it managed to crawl ahead and close Friday at USD 63.15/barrel.

As we slip past oil discussions and into the Indian sub-continent ship-recycling destinations, faint signs of life have certainly started to show (at least at the waterfronts) as we head further into February, after an inert start to the year produced some worrying reversals that seemed like the visiting mother-in-law who wasn’t planning on leaving (still feels like that way somehow). Local steel prices surged this week with all sub-continent destinations reporting major jumps across the week.

The shortage of recycling candidates meanwhile continues to flirt with a starving industry that is seeing stars and demand ramp up once again, following what has been a general theme over the years, accompanied by record-low sales due to continually firm freight markets on the back of some new global crisis that somehow never misses a beat to arrive for a paycheck. Older handy bulkers and LNGs have certainly been sold at the start of this year, and at some firming levels too, but tankers (for the most part) and containers have been absent over the last few years.

Bangladeshi elections are also on the horizon and the government has widened the local holiday period to ensure everyone has time to register their vote. All sub-continent recycling destinations meanwhile have had their own unique port positions to report for the week, with Chattogram leading the way once again. That can’t be happening, right? Turkey at the far end remains relatively quiet again, with no news of freshly proposed market units of late.

# Bangladesh

## ELECTION SPECULATION?

*Margin notes: Back in the picture. | Critical event. | More progressive? | Land of no return.*

After a worrying start to the year that saw local offerings placed firmly below the psychological barrier of USD 400/LDT—and Bangladesh even slipping to the bottom of the market rankings for a week—Chattogram seems to have slipped back into the picture this week, not only through local discussions but also with a local anchorage that remained busy, including local deliveries from last week that were promptly delivered when the tide broke this week, as well as the welcome arrival of a rare large LDT tanker.

The performance of domestic fundamentals certainly played its part with the Taka spiking 0.65% against the U.S. dollar this week and venturing off into the weekend at BDT 122.05. Local steel plate prices also shockingly woke up after two horizontal weeks (with no local trading being reported) and jumped nearly USD 9/ton clock out at almost USD 494/ton, giving local recyclers that much more of a buffer on the state of current purchases—because for the upcoming units? Who knows where the markets might stand then.

Meanwhile, shades of pre-election speculation are starting to take grip the nation as the critical election day is due to take place by mid-February, and the usual flurry of lobbying, rallying, and breakout instances of political violence continue to mar headlines across the nation. If, as is being hoped for, a more progressive pro-business party is elected to power, we could see a surge in local markets off the back of long-delayed infrastructure projects being resumed, with optimism finally returning to the nation as a result.

For now, however, as Chattogram continues to confuse with its weekly performance—where last week it felt like a “land of no return”—this week feels like a whole new personality breaking through the seams yet again. Seems like Mr. Hyde is out and about once again.

# India

## SECURE ME SOME!

*Margin notes: LNGs still. | Rupee concerns. | HKC advantage. | Week after week.*

Alang buyers have reportedly managed to secure some interesting market / private units of late (and this week), including another high-profile LNG and several smaller LDT units positioned in the region, whilst Pakistan and Bangladesh went for the larger dry bulk units in the parade.

Moreover, even though there was another arrival of a rare large LDT tanker, most of the deliveries (just like in Chattogram) were those from last week, taking the total LDT at Alang to just over 36K, for what has been a terrible few weeks thus far.

And while local steel plate prices have primarily been the driving force behind the disaster that has been Alang’s constant re-positioning on the market rankings of late, this week saw a whole new face as not only did local steel plate prices jump USD 14/ton to nearly USD 415/ton, but the U.S. dollar also lost a massive 1.32% against the Indian Rupee and closed shop for the week at INR 90.08, looking ready to break back into Rs. 89.X territory (hopefully next week).

On the HKC front, with nearly all recycling yards in Alang being HKC-certified—23 in Chattogram and two in Gadani (with more on the way)—India will still hold the advantage in this regard, even if (and whenever) competitors catch up and an abundance of tonnage forces sellers toward Alang. Only one of these will sadly be true for the foreseeable future.

For now, regardless of the performance of the competition and where India lands on the market rankings week after week, Alang will always be a home for a unit being proposed on the bidding tables.

# Pakistan

## LEADING WAY?

*Margin notes: Stunning reversal. | Dry bulk sales. | Good HKC news.*

After what can only be described as a stunning reversal of fortunes since the start of this year, Pakistan has surprisingly been leading the way in the sub-continent ship-recycling sector over the last few weeks, including this one, where several Panamax and handy bulker sales have reportedly taken place to hungry local recyclers. And from the looks of it, there is still further demand from a hungry few to import vessels at decent levels, still exceeding USD 400/LDT.

Like the rest of the industry, domestic fundamentals have been the trampoline bounce that Gadani recyclers have been desperately looking for, as local steel plate prices finally broke through their cemented coffins and surged nearly USD 8/ton, closing the week out at an industry leading USD 597/ton. Not wanting to be left behind, the Pakistani Rupee also seized the moment this week and surged 1% against the U.S. Dollar, registering a closing bell of PKR 279.14.

And what really snapped the camel’s back this week was the arrival of a near 7K LDT unit at Gadani’s anchorage, breaking what has been a tediously deserted 2026 for the nation’s waterfront thus far. Moreover, with so many rumors of private and market fixtures to the Gadani Gang over the last couple of weeks, the immediate future remains an exciting one to keep an eye on.

Finally, further good news enveloped the market at the start of this year, as the second HKC yard was approved in Gadani and announced in recent weeks. We are finally starting to see the requisite infrastructure being laid out across the shores of Gadani to ensure the survival of this industry in the decades ahead.

With Chattogram decidedly out of the running during election time and tonnage seemingly squeaking its way onto the bidding tables, it’s certainly an exciting time for the Gadani Gang after what feels like an eternity on the sidelines.

Seems like some serious anti-gravity UAP like performance from the Lira.
