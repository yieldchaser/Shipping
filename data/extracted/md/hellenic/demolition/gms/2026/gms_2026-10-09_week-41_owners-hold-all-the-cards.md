# Market Commentary

## OWNERS HOLD ALL THE CARDS

Last week, Chattogram reclaimed first place on the conventional recycling board. This week, its leading bid has been left looking for something to buy. Across the sub-continent, the familiar imbalance persists: end buyers are available, prices remain broadly intact, and the ships needed to turn both into transactions are still earning money elsewhere. The market has not lost its appetite. It has simply run short of willing sellers.

The Strait of Hormuz supplied another reminder that the recovery in Gulf shipping remains fragile. Renewed tanker attacks have disrupted crude movements and revived concerns over passage, security and insurance. Washington's announcement that it would refrain from further strikes against Iran before the November 3 midterm elections provided some immediate relief, although the U.S. blockade remains in place. The threat of further disruption has not disappeared, and shipowners continue to navigate a Gulf where commercial passage carries more than the usual risks.

Oil responded with another sharp reversal. Brent climbed above USD 105 per barrel on Thursday as attacks intensified, before retreating toward USD 103 on Friday following Washington's announcement. WTI traded around USD 90. The immediate fear of escalation may have eased, but the premium attached to moving energy through the Gulf remains firmly embedded. Higher bunker costs and altered trading patterns continue to complicate voyage economics, without necessarily encouraging owners to consider recycling.

Dry freight, meanwhile, has finally shown a more consistent retreat. The Baltic Dry Index eased from 3,148 on October 2 to 2,973 on Thursday, October 8, with Capesizes slipping to 4,512 while Panamaxes and Supramaxes held at 2,410 and 1,808 respectively. Larger bulkers have surrendered more ground, but the smaller segments remain comparatively resilient. Earnings are cooling, yet not sufficiently to trigger the broader retirement decisions that recyclers have been awaiting. A softer freight market is welcome. A sufficiently weak one remains elusive.

The macro picture offered little common direction. India's Reserve Bank raised its policy rate to 5.50%, reflecting persistent inflationary pressure, while Bangladesh's September CPI edged higher to 8.34%. Pakistan's inflation eased to 10.26%, and Turkey's fell below 30% to 29.73%. Domestic steel markets remain uneven, with Indian and Pakistani plate recording modest gains while Bangladesh continues to struggle with sluggish local demand. None of these developments has materially altered the underlying shortage of available recycling candidates.

At the beaches, previously committed vessels continue to arrive and deliver across Alang, Chattogram and Gadani, providing work for the yards without meaningfully replenishing the next sales cycle. Bangladesh retains its conventional pricing lead, Pakistan remains competitive without August's urgency, and India continues to seek stronger value from specialist and non-ferrous-rich units. Turkey has moved modestly lower. Across all four destinations, buying interest remains considerably healthier than fresh transactional activity.

The final quarter is therefore developing into a contest of patience. Recyclers have the capacity and willingness to buy, but owners still have freight earnings, second-hand alternatives and time on their side. Unless those alternatives weaken materially, the long-awaited increase in recycling supply may continue slipping toward 2027. The yards are ready, the bids are waiting, and the decision remains firmly with the owners. For now, they hold all the cards.

# Bangladesh

## CHATTOGRAM'S BID OUTRUNS SUPPLY

*Margin notes: Appetite Holds. | Steel Slumbers. | Dryness Beckons.*

Chattogram enters another week at the top of the conventional recycling board, although the stronger buying position has yet to translate into a meaningful increase in fresh transactions. Demand for suitable dry, tanker and container tonnage remains healthy, with several recyclers prepared to compete for workable candidates. The challenge continues to be availability rather than appetite. Freight earnings and second-hand values are keeping potential sellers on the sidelines, leaving buyers with little opportunity to convert their interest into concluded business.

The domestic steel market has offered little encouragement. Demand for finished products remains subdued, with local mills and stockists continuing to exercise caution. Plate prices remained unchanged at BDT 64,000 per ton, approximately USD 522.24 per ton, throughout the week. September inflation edged higher to 8.34% from 8.26% in August, adding to the pressure on an already cautious domestic market. Despite these headwinds, vessel indications remain firm at USD 510/LDT for dry bulk, USD 530/LDT for tankers and USD 540/LDT for containers. The resilience of these levels reflects continuing yard requirements and the scarcity of available candidates rather than any improvement in underlying steel fundamentals.

Physical activity has continued, with the 2,398 LDT tanker Petrova delivered on October 9, followed by the 911 LDT general cargo vessel Pacific Leader on October 6. The 11,304 LDT Ro-Ro vessel Haku was delivered October 3, while Merc and Phil also completed deliveries earlier in the month. These movements have kept the waterfront occupied, although much of the tonnage was secured during earlier buying periods.

Attention is gradually turning toward the approaching dry season, when improved operating conditions could support local steel demand and yard productivity. Whether that seasonal improvement translates into stronger purchasing activity will depend on both domestic consumption and the willingness of owners to release tonnage. For now, Chattogram has the strongest conventional bid in the sub-continent. What it lacks is a steady supply of ships against which to deploy it.

# India

## ALANG CHARTS ITS OWN COURSE

*Margin notes: Specialists First. | Plate Improves. | Value Prevails.*

Alang continues to navigate a different commercial course from its sub-continent competitors. With Bangladesh and Pakistan maintaining stronger conventional indications, Indian recyclers remain selective in their pursuit of standard dry and tanker candidates. Their greater interest continues to lie in LNG carriers, reefers, passenger vessels and other specialised units carrying attractive non-ferrous content. These ships command valuations beyond the ordinary steel-based pricing structure and offer Alang opportunities that are not always available to competing destinations.

Domestic steel has provided some support, despite a modest retreat toward the end of the week. Alang plate advanced from INR 42,700 per ton on October 3 to INR 42,900 during the week before settling at INR 42,600 on Friday, approximately USD 440.90 per ton. This remains above last week's closing level and provides a slightly stronger foundation for recyclers. The Reserve Bank of India's decision to raise its policy repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50%, however, introduces a further consideration for financing and domestic steel consumption. Stronger plate values help, but higher borrowing costs and competition from neighbouring destinations continue to restrain conventional purchasing.

Activity at Alang has continued with several deliveries, including the 1,226 LDT fishing vessel EVS on October 9, the 10,068 LDT oil tanker Right on October 7, and the 9,886 LDT bulker Enisey on October 6. The yards are receiving tonnage, but the market for fresh candidates remains selective. Conventional GMS indications consequently hold at USD 465/LDT for dry bulk, USD 485/LDT for tankers and USD 495/LDT for containers. India's competitive advantage remains firmly vessel-specific. While its neighbours contest the conventional market at higher levels, Alang continues to find its better opportunities in ships where material composition, specialist handling requirements and compliance considerations carry greater weight than the standard price board. The conventional competition remains difficult. The specialist market offers a course of its own.

# Pakistan

## GADANI WAITS WITHOUT WAVERING

*Margin notes: Demand Endures. | Steel Edges. | Patience Prevails.*

Gadani remains firmly positioned behind Bangladesh on the conventional recycling board, with buying interest continuing despite the scarcity of fresh candidates. The urgency that characterised August has moderated as previously secured vessels have gradually entered the yards, easing immediate requirements. Nevertheless, recyclers remain receptive to suitable tonnage and are prepared to compete when opportunities arise. The absence of new business reflects the shortage of available ships more than any meaningful withdrawal of demand.

Domestic steel has shown a modest improvement, with plate advancing from PKR 195,000 per ton to PKR 196,000 on October 9, equivalent to approximately USD 705.80 per ton. The Pakistani Rupee remains relatively stable against the Dollar, while September inflation eased to 10.26% from 11.1% in August. Although these developments provide some support, local steel demand continues to face a cautious environment, limiting the scope for a stronger recovery in vessel pricing. GMS indications remain unchanged at USD 500/LDT for dry bulk, USD 520/LDT for tankers and USD 530/LDT for containers.

The 5,688 LDT bulker Lina F was delivered on October 2, while the 7,381 LDT Portland 11 and 5,143 LDT Maestro remain among the more recent arrivals at Gadani. These units continue to provide physical activity, although fresh buying opportunities remain few and far between.

Pakistan enters the remainder of October with a more measured but still competitive approach. Recyclers have little incentive to pursue marginal opportunities at inflated levels while domestic steel consumption remains uncertain. Yet suitable ships can still attract meaningful attention when owners are prepared to negotiate. Gadani may no longer be setting the pace, but its appetite has not vanished. The buyers remain in place. The next move belongs to the sellers.

# Turkey

## ALIAGA TRIMS ITS SAILS

*Margin notes: Prices Retreat. | Inflation Eases. | Niche Holds.*

Aliaga has edged lower this week as softer steel and scrap-market sentiment weighs on recycling indications. Dry bulk levels have eased to USD 290/LDT, tankers to USD 300-310/LDT and containers to USD 320/LDT, reversing part of the modest improvement seen during September. Domestic mills remain cautious in their purchasing, with weak finished-steel demand offering little incentive to chase imported scrap or higher-priced recycling candidates.

Turkey's annual inflation fell to 29.73% in September from 31.51% in August, dropping below 30% for the first time in several years. The improvement provides some macroeconomic relief, although persistent currency weakness and elevated financing costs continue to challenge purchasing power. The Turkish Lira remains under pressure against the Dollar, limiting the benefit of lower domestic inflation for internationally priced vessel transactions.

Aliaga continues to operate within its established commercial niche, where geography, proximity to European markets, vessel suitability and applicable environmental requirements can outweigh the substantial pricing gap with the sub-continent. The latest adjustment reflects weaker domestic fundamentals rather than a fundamental change in Turkey's position within global recycling. The board has been trimmed. The market's distinctive role remains intact.
