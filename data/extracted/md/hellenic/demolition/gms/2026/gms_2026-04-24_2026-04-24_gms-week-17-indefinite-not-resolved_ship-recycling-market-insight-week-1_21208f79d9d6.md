INDEFINITE, NOT RESOLVED 
 
The Week 16 view that a temporary memorandum had narrowed the diplomatic off-ramp has 
already been overtaken. On April 22, President Trump extended the US-Iran ceasefire indefinitely 
after Pakistan-hosted talks failed to convene. Within hours, Iran attacked three commercial vessels 
in the Strait of Hormuz and claimed seizure of two, following a US Navy interdiction of an Iranian-
flagged cargo vessel earlier in the week. The US naval blockade of Iranian ports remains fully in 
force, while the Pentagon now estimates mine clearance in Hormuz will take approximately six 
months. More than thirty countries are assembling a multinational task force at an RAF base outside 
London, though deployment remains contingent on a durable ceasefire. The extension is indefinite. 
The resolution is not. 
Energy markets have responded accordingly. Brent rallied about 5 percent to USD 94.90 per barrel 
on April 20 on seizure headlines, with WTI at USD 88.44. The prior week’s slide on IEA demand 
concerns has reversed as geopolitical risk reasserts itself, and the recent inventory draw now reflects 
tightening supply rather than weakening demand. 
Freight continues to diverge. The Baltic Dry Index closed at 2,673 on April 23, up about 150 points 
from 2,523 a week earlier, extending a twelve-session rally before easing marginally. Capesize 
earnings are at a four-month high on sustained Brazil to China iron ore flows, with Panamax and 
Supramax also firmer. The expected Q1 tonnage release has now been deferred for a third 
consecutive week as owners continue to favour trading over recycling. 
Currencies reflect re-embedded risk. USD to INR has weakened to 93.80 after partial RBI unwinding, 
approaching the March 27 low of 94.86. USD to BDT remains rangebound at 122.70 to 123.00, USD 
to PKR has edged to 279.70, and the Turkish lira has hit a fresh low near 44.92. The Dollar Index has 
softened to about 98.2. This does not alter sub-continent pricing dynamics. It reinforces them. 
With five weeks remaining before the monsoon window closes, Bangladesh’s LC pipeline is 
clearing, Pakistan’s Gulf proximity advantage is strengthening, and India retains its HKC 
compliance edge. Aliaga remains structurally uncompetitive outside EU tonnage. Across all 
destinations, the constraint remains unchanged. Owners are not releasing tonnage, and the 
indefinite ceasefire offers no incentive to do so. 
The Week 16 question of whether supply returns before the monsoon or rolls into backlog has been 
provisionally answered in favour of backlog. Week 17 closes with the ceasefire indefinite, the 
blockade intact, mines six months from clearance, and the beaching window narrowing by the day.

PIPELINE CLEARS, WINDOW TIGHTENS 
Bangladesh enters Week 17 with its Letter of Credit pipeline clearing at its fastest pace since the 
post-Eid reopening. The central bank approvals that defined Q1 friction have now moved 
meaningfully faster over the past ten days, and Chattogram cash buyers report turnaround on 
qualified files materially shorter than the February benchmark. Bangladesh Bank is holding its 
policy rate at 10%, with foreign exchange reserves reported at USD 34.12 billion as of March, and 
the macro stability is sufficient to sustain the current transaction environment without fresh 
constraints. The USD/BDT rate has held in its 122.70 to 123.00 band through the week, preserving 
the direct flow-through of local steel strength into USD offer capacity that continues to distinguish 
Chattogram from Alang and Aliaga. 
Local steel plate prices are holding in the BDT 69,400 to 71,000 per ton band, translating to a USD 
equivalent of approximately USD 571 to USD 584 per ton. The modest softening at the Week 16 
close has not extended, and the plate market appears to be absorbing the firmer freight complex 
without passing it through to offer levels. Chattogram's position as the sub-continent's highest 
bidder remains unchallenged on dry bulk and tanker tonnage across the LDT spectrum. The 
compliance shadow cast by the two OFAC-sanctioned VLCCs at OPL Chattogram persists into its 
sixth week, and the heightened due diligence culture across the cash buyer community is now 
effectively embedded as an operating practice rather than an episodic response. 
Bangladesh's March 2026 CPI print at 8.71%, down from 9.13% in February, with non-food inflation 
holding at 9.09%, continues to provide the central bank the flexibility it needs on LC approvals 
without introducing fresh macro noise. The monsoon window has tightened to approximately five 
weeks. The fresh escalation in the Gulf has reinforced owners' inclination to keep tonnage trading 
rather than beach it, and the Week 16 expectation of supply release has now fully given way to the 
Week 17 expectation of Q1 overhang rolling into a Q2 backlog. Bangladesh has the financial 
positioning, the compliance pathway, the pricing, and the urgency. It does not have the ships. The 
problem is unchanged and worsening. 
Monsoon nears.

INDIA
RUPEE DRIFTS, ALANG HOLDS 
India's Week 17 opens with USD/INR oscillating between 93.30 and 93.80, extending the drift that 
began at the close of Week 15. The Reserve Bank of India partially unwound its recent foreign-exchange 
restrictions during the week, a move that has supported trading flexibility but also reintroduced two-
way volatility into onshore-offshore arbitrage flows. The March 27 record low of 94.86 remains within 
a stress-event reach. The central bank's intervention capacity is not exhausted, but its appetite to deploy 
further reserves into a market whose underlying driver remains the unresolved Hormuz situation has 
visibly contracted. The currency continues to tell a cleaner story than the oil market. 
The US naval blockade of Iranian ports continues in full force, and Iran's seizure and attack on three 
commercial vessels in the Strait on April 22 has effectively reset the transit risk posture for India's 
energy import flows. Approximately 90% of India's LPG continues to route through Hormuz-exposed 
corridors, and approximately 70% of crude is similarly dependent. The Pentagon's formal six-month 
estimate for clearing Iranian-laid mines, even in the best-case ceasefire scenario, has materially shifted 
the base case for a durable strait reopening from weeks to quarters. Whatever directional benefit the 
Week 15 oil softening was supposed to deliver to India's import bill has been unwound by the Week 
17 escalation, and then some. 
Local steel plate prices at Alang have oscillated between INR 40,700 and INR 41,500 through the week, 
with the USD equivalent at approximately USD 435 to USD 444 per ton, keeping India as the lowest-
priced sub-continent destination. The gap to Pakistan holds near USD 200 per LDT. India's CPI for 
March 2026 at 3.40%, released April 13 and marking the highest reading in over a year, remains 
comfortably within the RBI's 2%-6% tolerance band that has now been formally reaffirmed for the 2026 
to 2031 period. Alang's structural differentiators - more than 110 HKC-compliant yards, a durable 
regulatory framework, deep yard capacity for regulated European and Japanese tonnage - remain fully 
intact. The constraint is the same constraint that the whole sub-continent is facing. The tonnage is not 
arriving. The monsoon is. 
Hormuz reseals.

PAKISTAN
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
    
PROXIMITY PREMIUM RESTORED 
Pakistan's Week 17 extends the stability theme that defined Weeks 15 and 16 and is now actively 
compounding it. USD/PKR has edged marginally to approximately 279.70 from 278.90 at the Week 
16 close, a move that is material only on a multi-week view and which the broader cash buyer 
community has registered as noise rather than signal. Local steel plate prices at PKR 188,000 per 
ton are unchanged, translating to a USD equivalent near USD 672 per ton and keeping Gadani at 
the firmest absolute USD pricing position of the year. The gap to India has widened further to 
approximately USD 228 to USD 237 per LDT. The March 2026 CPI print at 7.3%, up from 7% in
February and the highest reading since August 2024, sits above the State Bank's 5% to 7% target
band, but the composition - transport and housing driving the move, food decelerating - keeps
yard-level economics manageable given the stable PKR base. 
The Week 16 speculation that a broader de-escalation might moderate Pakistan's Gulf proximity 
advantage has been fully invalidated by the Week 17 escalation. The US naval blockade of Iranian 
ports, the Iranian vessel attacks in Hormuz on April 22, the failure of the proposed Pakistan-hosted
US-Iran talks to convene, and the Pentagon's six-month mine-clearance estimate are, in aggregate, 
a complete reversal of that speculative case. Gadani's proximity to the Gulf is not a marginal 
advantage in this environment. It is a compounding structural premium that is directing tonnage 
flow toward Pakistan and away from both Bangladesh and India on any candidate where Gulf 
transit economics matter. Three HKC-certified yards are now operational with further certifications
in the pipeline. The pool of recycling candidates in the medium-sized dry bulk, LPG, and offshore 
segments continues to align well with Gadani's capacity, and all three structural reinforcements -
compliance, pricing, logistics - are now operating simultaneously and with increased intensity. 
The one unresolved question for Pakistan remains whether this reinforced structural position 
translates into executed transactions before the monsoon window closes. The indefinite nature of 
the current ceasefire extension, absent any concrete proposal from Iran and with the US explicitly 
maintaining the blockade, makes the near-term durable resolution scenario less plausible than it 
was a week ago. Pakistan enters the second half of Q2's pre-monsoon window on the firmest 
footing of the year, and the window to consolidate that position into executed tonnage is now five 
weeks and shrinking. Pakistan has the most to gain if the current geopolitical configuration holds 
through the monsoon. It has the most to lose if a durable resolution arrives in the next three weeks. 
On current evidence, the former is significantly more likely than the latter.

HOLDING, NOT HEALING 
Turkey enters Week 17 with the lira at a fresh record low near 44.92 per USD, extending the 
controlled and slow devaluation that the Central Bank of Turkey has been managing through active 
intervention in foreign exchange markets. The TCMB held its key policy rate unchanged for the 
second consecutive decision in April, confirming the pivot away from the earlier rate-cutting cycle 
that the war in the Middle East forced the bank to abandon. The TCMB sold more than USD 8 billion 
in foreign exchange in the first week of March to defend the currency, and the halt on reverse repo 
auctions has driven the lira's overnight reference rate near 40% as the bank tightens liquidity 
conditions to support the currency. 
March 2026 CPI at 30.87%, down from 31.53% in February and below the market expectation of 
31.4 %, marks the third consecutive month of slower annual inflation, but the monthly print of 
1.94 %, while the lowest in three months, reflects transport inflation accelerating to 34.35% annually 
as fuel costs from the Gulf situation continue to pass through. Independent estimates remain 
materially above the official read, with ENAG at 54.62% and Istanbul Chamber data at 37.68%. The 
central bank's 16% year-end target remains formally in place but is increasingly difficult to reconcile 
with either trajectory. The disinflation narrative is narrow. The reconciliation is not arriving. 
At USD 265 to USD 288 per LDT across vessel types, Aliaga remains structurally uncompetitive for 
mainstream tonnage. The gap to Pakistan at approximately USD 445 to USD 480 and to Bangladesh 
at USD 460 to USD 495 has widened further with the lira's fresh weakness, and the bridge to those 
levels is unavailable, absent EU-regulated tonnage where Basel Convention compliance overrides 
price. Turkey's relevance this Q2 is confined to that niche. The fresh escalation in the Gulf does not 
change this. Nothing in Week 17 does. 
Lira breaks.
