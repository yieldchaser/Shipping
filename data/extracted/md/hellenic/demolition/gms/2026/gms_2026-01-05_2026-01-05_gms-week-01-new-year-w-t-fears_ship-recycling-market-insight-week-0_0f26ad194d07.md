NEW YEAR, W.T. FEARS! 
After what only seemed like a calm period while Donald Trump was flailing in the polls amid 
ongoing scandals and investigations, he ramped it up and brought 2026 in with a literal BANG via 
his latest incursion into — and seizure of — President Nicolas Maduro and his wife from their
Presidential Home in Venezuela, in a pre-dawn raid by special-forces. Trump even posted a 
Polaroid of the handcuffed Maduro, blindfolded, as they landed at a secure / undisclosed facility. 
While Trump’s true intentions remain unclear (just yet), it’s only a matter of time before we see how 
global / oil / shipping markets react in the coming week(s) to this globally dictatorial move that has 
most certainly violated a long list of international laws pertaining to sitting presidents and prisoners 
of war. Moreover, major news outlets are now reporting confusion over who maintains control. 
Although major news sources report that the U.S. is claiming to hold power over the country, Trump 
claims the current Venezuelan government (albeit with the President missing and all) will hold 
interim power while the U.S. administers the nation until such time that a new administration 
capable of resuming duties is in place to lead the country. Hard to reread that and not think that a
prominent three-letter agency was missing some South American sun this frigid time of the year; 
and after all the geopolitical craziness that gripped — and impacted — us through 2025, I don’t 
know how many were ready to see Trump Theatre & Co. deliver a cake factory to celebrate his 
madness this soon in 2026. 
In the meantime, global economies delivered mixed signals as U.S. stocks maintained a steady 
decline over the tail-end of December, China’s industrial growth slowed, and EU stocks reportedly 
surged. The U.S. Dollar started the year off-balance against ship recycling currencies, shipowners 
started to rejoice as the Baltic Exchange Dry Index showed signs of a resurgence, oil continued to 
slip on its way to USD 60/barrel even though it climbed to USD 57.17/barrel this week, local steel 
plate prices made no jarring moves beyond the recent trend, and beaching tides delivered twins in 
both India and Pakistan. At least it was encouraging to see the Indian market display some signs of 
improvement over the first week of 2026, as busier and less challenging times hopefully lie ahead. 
In the ship recycling markets overall, increasingly decisive times lie ahead for Bangladesh as a 
nation given that impending elections are nearing by the week and the country is being increasingly 
submerged by political protests, rioting, and instances of violence. Bangladesh levels and demand 
have plummeted as a result and now lie at the foot of the sub-continent price rankings board, given 
that Pakistan and India both made improvements as December 2025 ended, and they moved up the 
ladder. With more upheaval now expected across the world, continued economic and geopolitical 
shocks may temporarily prop up shipping markets and deprive recycling locations of vintage 
tonnage for a while longer — it seems as though 2026 is taking on the mantle from 2025!

PRICE PLUNGE! 
Ever since the tonnage engorgement back in November 2025, the Bangladeshi ship recycling 
market has been on a consistent nosedive, leaving recyclers in Chattogram largely uncompetitive 
when it comes to offering on the limited number of units currently paraded at the bidding tables.
Levels have shockingly come off by about USD 50/LDT since the highs of Q4 2025 — an 
uncharacteristically rapid, drastic, and unexpected deterioration that has left local demand and 
overall industry sentiment toward Chattogram on voicemail mode. 
Moreover, with significant uncertainty building ahead of the February elections, violent protests 
followed the death of youth leader Sharif Osman Hadi, who was shot by masked gunmen while 
leaving a mosque in Dhaka back in December. The death of Bangladesh’s first female PM, Khaleda 
Zia, last week at the age of 80 also sent much of the country into mourning, as these uncertain and 
dark times become headline news nationwide. 
Dripping down into the ship recycling sector, the situation clearly remains ever worse, albeit with 
a lemon-twist surprise at the waterfront, as Chattogram was the only sub-continent destination to 
report any arrivals — including the nation’s first prompt delivery — this week. But on the 
fundamentals side, things remain scattered: local steel plate prices recorded their usual weekly 
volatility, while the BDT lost six basis points against the U.S. Dollar and closed the week out at 
BDT 122.30. 
Overall, the trajectory of this market over the next couple of months does not seem appealing to 
the industry, as with price, tonnage follows suit. And it would not be an impossibility to see Far 
Eastern tonnage heading to shores west of Bangladesh.

INDIA
STEEL SURGE! 
The Indian market surged by nearly USD 30/LDT over the last week, sending Alang soaring to the top 
of the price rankings board for the first time in over a quarter, as 2026 began on a far cheerier note than 
the end of 2025 would otherwise seem to suggest. 
Surprisingly, despite the U.S. Dollar gaining 20 basis points against the Indian Rupee and closing the 
week at Rs. 90.28 – breaching into the dread Rs. 90s once again, demand still managed to improve and, 
after a fairly woeful 2025, Alang recyclers can be a touch more optimistic heading into what many are 
hoping will be a far busier year ahead — the perennial prayer of the recycling ports over the last five 
years that has yet to be answered. 
Yet, the biggest surprise of the week was local anchorage, which, for the first time in a very long time, 
reported a deserted theatre — a first for this market. Although in fairness, there are units idling at outer 
awaiting entry onto the port report so it’s only a matter of time before their scoreboard for 2026 starts
all over again.  
Overall, India is once again the best-placed market to take in tonnage. It seems odd not to mention 
HKC or “otherwise” anymore, since every vessel has naturally been subject to the Convention since 
June 26, 2025, when it formally kicked into gear. With Bangladesh overstuffed and facing a clear 
shortage of available HKC yards, and with shipowners/cash buyers staying away from the DASR 
dance, Alang anchorage might be the one to keep an eye on — at least until Bangladesh’s elections 
formalize, a modicum of stability starts to reign, and positive economic measures are implemented.

PAKISTAN
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
    
HOT WHEELS! 
Pakistan remains hot on the heels of its Indian neighbors/competitors, and as Bangladesh currently 
takes a breather, now could be the pivotal time for Gadani recyclers to step into the breach with 
some opportunistic vessel purchases. But will they? 
Local offerings are still positioned below USD 400/LDT on a majority of the tonnage being
presented locally, and it has been a fairly inert December 2025/first week of January 2026, after a 
promising few months in 2025 that briefly saw Gadani top the price board and have a flush
anchorage to match.  
Proof of this is the state of local anchorage, which has resumed its time in solitary — mirroring
India. It certainly is an eclipse moment for the ship recycling world when regional recycling 
anchorages west of Chattogram sit completely empty. 
In the meantime, yards remain busy upgrading and improving infrastructure to fall in line with 
HKC standards (with one yard already approved), with the HKC now fully in force as of June last 
year. But this remains a major limitation for the market, especially with an expected influx of 
tonnage and Bangladesh largely out of the running. 
Fundamentals, meanwhile, continue on their merry way, with local steel plate prices maintaining 
the status quo and the PKR losing 20 basis points, though it still managed to stay within PKR 280 
territory as it wound the week down at PKR 280.62. Domestically, there was good news for the
nation’s economy as well with inflation reporting a sharp drop down to 5.6%, its lowest level since
August 2025.  
Overall, it seems this market will suffer the indignity of an HKC-short beachfront, which will more
than likely see India consume most of the tonnage in the near term. New year and empty 
anchorages aren’t how Gadani recyclers anticipated the start of 2026.
