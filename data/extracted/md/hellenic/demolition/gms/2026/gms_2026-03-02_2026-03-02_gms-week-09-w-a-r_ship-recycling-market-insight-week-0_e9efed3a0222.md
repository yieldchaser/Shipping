W.A.R. 
Whackos Attacking Reality — WAR — is literally the definition of the weekend as the Middle East 
descended into all-out chaos between the U.S. & Israel on one side and Iran on the other, leaving 
regional and international markets bracing for the inevitable whirlpool of global insanity that is 
about to suck the entire world into short-term (at the very least) tumult. 
Oil was already on its way up during the week, but as the weekend came around, it spiked over 
10%, jumping from around USD 63/barrel last week to nearly USD 71.90/barrel at the time of writing. 
The Baltic Exchange was also on its way up (rising 1.1%) but remained comparatively subdued in 
comparison to oil futures…yet. The Cape, Panamax, and Supra indices all climbed 0.2%, 1.4%, and 
3% respectively. Currencies, too, took a hammering, as all of the major ship recycling destinations 
registered declines across the board. Local steel plate prices also declined across the board, albeit 
marginally, likely waiting for Monday to turn around before deciding whether to join the ranks of 
rising rates. 
The news immediately worsens for the maritime industry as insurance premiums, bunkers, voyage 
insurance, blockages, delays, and charters running afoul are just the tip of this ballooning iceberg 
while the dust settles amid a still inexplicable war campaign that is reportedly set to continue for at 
least another four weeks — which clearly means buckle up, folks. 
Especially as calls for calm and de-escalation intensify but are reportedly falling on deaf ears on the 
Iranian side following the assassination of Iran’s Supreme Leader in his compound and over 60 
others, including children at a local school, according to U.S. sources. 
The resulting backlash and retaliation from the pariah state have seen several major Middle Eastern 
states (Qatar, UAE (Dubai, Abu Dhabi), Oman and many more) come under direct Iranian fire across 
much of the last 36 hours. Iran has also reportedly halted shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, 
while the cancellation of thousands of regional and international flights to and from the region is 
another immediate knock-on effect to trade and commerce — not only in the region, but globally —
as the rest of us wait with bated breath to see how the coming week unfolds. 
As such, ship recycling activity, being so close to the action, has taken a back seat for now, as markets 
stall in anticipation of further drama and a lack of sales once again filters through to recycling 
markets that have already been deprived of tonnage due to recurring geopolitical setbacks. Not 
since the days of COVID have we seen such a significant disruption affect a single region so directly, 
with ripple effects spreading across the globe as the world wakes up to Monday and another conflict, 
with the next steps likely to reshape global politics and affect markets once again to a significant 
and unpredictable degree.

Bangladesh sits atop the Indian sub-continent ship recycling rankings for yet another week, amid 
fresh sales that have been hard to come by as trading markets endure another week of poking
volatility that is continuing to deprive recycling markets of any meaningful tonnage. 
The situation unfolding across the Middle East will be the chief focus of the world in the weeks 
ahead, and it is nearly assured that rising freight and oil rates will, in turn, see recycling markets 
forcibly face another spell of tonnage absence at the local waterfront, spelling trouble for the 
recyclers who are yet to receive their share of recompensate for all the yard expenses ensured 
during the HKC upgrade and accreditations phases.  
Speaking of tonnage absence at the bidding tables, all anchorages still have units arriving across 
the various locations, including Chattogram, which saw the arrival of a near 20K LDT bulker and 
a handful of smaller dry units, all while last week’s large LDT LNG was delivered to her new 
owners for recycling. But with a major conflict now underway and Far Eastern markets still in a 
holiday mood, the coming weeks are likely to be a pinching affair for this market. 
Fundamentals, meanwhile, have taken hits to varying degrees, as not only did local steel plate 
prices slide a couple of dollars to end the week at USD 507.81/ton, but the Taka also lost 0.20% and 
wound the week down at BDT 122.71 against the U.S. Dollar. 
Despite the globally brewing chaos, things domestically seem to be in a better state for the nation, 
as optimism lingers in the air on the back of the newly elected governing body in Bangladesh. They 
certainly have a job on their hands, deciphering where they sit on the international spectrum and 
with whom to form strategic alliances in this fractured and disparate world we now live in. 
As tonnage at the bidding tables dries up once again and Far Eastern owners are still not fully back 
in business after the Chinese New Year holidays, Chattogram Choppers at least have a month of 
fasting and celebrations to keep them busy.

INDIA
STALLED SUCCESSFULLY! 
As the lowest-placed sub-continent market, India has been — and continues to — face hardships in the 
current environment, having to make do with strictly Alang-only HKC vessels in recent times, along 
with the usual distribution of specialist units such as LNGs, reefers, and offshore vessels. Local port 
positions indeed show that all sub-continent recycling destinations have had their fair share of arrivals 
in recent times, including this week, with India taking the largest slice of the sub-continent anchorage 
pie. 
And the irony? Despite being the lowest-placed ship recycling destination in the sub-continent, Alang 
recyclers were busy taking in nearly three times the number of vessels as their Chattogram counterparts 
/ 1.5 times the LDT, but they took in nearly four times the number / about 20 times the LDT of the 
Gadani Gang. A juicy near-30K LDT LNG, a +25K LDT container was mixed in with a bevy of small 
LDT reefers, bulkers, and general cargo units that rounded off the arrivals list. 
And through it all, even with the lowest prices on show, geography was busy baking its cookies on the 
shores of Western India, where Alang had been stalled on pricing but still managed to successfully beat 
out the competition on acquisitions and arrivals this week. Moreover, India’s stance on the global stage 
will once again face scrutiny amid the latest Middle East madness, as was the case when it previously 
remained a neutral party between Russia and the West — a move that saw an enraged Trump levy the 
most tariffs against India in the sub-continent. 
The real kicker remains the state of local fundamentals, which have left India stranded in third and 
facing many of the same hurdles competing markets faced this week — namely, marginally declining 
local steel plate prices that fell about USD 1/ton and closed the week at USD 400/ton, while the Indian 
Rupee aimlessly followed suit, slipping 0.31% and wrapping up the week at a high Rs. 91.02 once again. 
Overall, and as previously stated, come hell or high water, Alang’s torches always seem to have power.

PAKISTAN
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
    
INCOMING ALERT! 
While Pakistan has been alert to any available larger LDT dry vessels coming its way over the last 
few months, elevated freight markets continue to perform, keeping global ship recycling starved 
of any significant tonnage — a trend that has persisted for several years now. Despite holding the 
top spot for a short while and delivering Aliaga-like bursts of performance across various quarters, 
including this most recent one, it seems Gadani may be starting to slip off the port reports once 
again. 
Proof? History. 
Last week saw four arrivals. This week, two of those were delivered, leaving just two on the port 
report — and no fresh arrivals. Other than the one vessel that has been idling at the waterfront for 
the last two weeks. 
Moreover, just like its regional competition, Gadani’s fundamentals — which have been among
the strongest in the sub-continent ship recycling community — also saw their share of jitters. Local
steel plate prices lost nearly USD 2/ton, closing the week at around USD 507/ton. The Pakistani 
Rupee also slipped 0.10%, ending the week at PKR 279.26. 
Making matters worse for Gadani is not only its proximity to Iran amid the ongoing war in the 
region, but also the escalating tensions between the Afghan Taliban and the Pakistani government, 
which are now reportedly engaging in open conflict of their own, adding further instability across 
the region. While this may further curtail the flow of Iranian steel into Pakistan — potentially
supporting local steel plate prices — the future inflow of regional tonnage into Gadani waters 
remains a toss-up at this time. 
Overall, even though Bangladesh overtook Pakistan for the top spot on the sub-continent price
board just a couple of weeks ago, the unfolding events across the Middle East, coupled with the 
political situation inside Pakistan — which remains unstable, with several deaths reported during 
pro-Khomeini rallies over the weekend — suggest that political stresses may be shifting westward. 
We may well see empty anchorages all over again. 
Probably why the beaches in Alang will continue to remain busy.
