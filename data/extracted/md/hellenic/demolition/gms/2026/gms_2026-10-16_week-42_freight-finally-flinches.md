# Market Commentary

## FREIGHT FINALLY FLINCHES

The dry freight retreat gathered pace this week, bringing awaited relief for recycling buyers without yet producing the ships they need. The Baltic Dry Index recorded a steep weekly fall, led by Capesizes, while recycling indications across all four destinations remained unchanged. Owners now face a less comfortable outlook for larger bulkers, but one week's correction cannot overturn months of profitable trading. Freight has blinked. The sellers have not.

The Strait of Hormuz supplied a troubling counterweight. Singapore-registered GFS Pride was struck on October 13 and subsequently sank after catching fire; GFS Giselle sustained hull damage in a separate incident the next day. All 43 crew members were reported safe. The attacks sharpened concerns over passage, security and insurance, keeping disruption in voyage calculations. For ageing tankers, altered routes and restricted effective availability can still make further employment more attractive than recycling.

Oil markets also absorbed an intervention. On October 15, the International Energy Agency announced plans to accelerate release of approximately 100 million barrels remaining under its March emergency stock programme, following around 330 million barrels already released. Brent nevertheless remained around USD 105 to 106 per barrel on Friday. Additional inventories may ease supply pressure, but cannot remove Gulf shipping risks. The cost of disruption survives the arrival of extra barrels.

Dry bulk was the more immediate source of encouragement for recyclers. The Baltic Dry Index fell from 2,973 on October 8 to 2,545 on Thursday, October 15, a decline of more than 14%. Capesizes dropped from 4,512 to 3,322, while Panamaxes eased to 2,356. Supramaxes moved against the trend, improving to 1,856. The uneven retreat matters: larger bulkers may be feeling pressure, but smaller and mid-sized ships continue to find worthwhile employment. Falling indices alone do not deliver vessels to the beaches.

Steel supplied no common regional signal. Bangladesh plate rose from BDT 64,000 to BDT 66,000 per ton despite subdued finished-steel demand. Indian plate finished at INR 42,300, below last Friday, although the stronger Rupee lifted its Dollar equivalent to USD 445.17. Pakistan eased to PKR 195,000, while Turkey faced weak domestic steel consumption and currency pressure. India's September inflation rose to 5.54%, complicating financing and demand. None of this altered the recycling price board.

Physical activity still exceeded fresh business. Alang delivered the 31,300 LDT LNG carrier Kano on October 15, reinforcing India's specialist position. Chattogram added several bulker and tanker arrivals, and Gadani delivered two previously secured bulkers. These movements represent ships already in the pipeline, not newly agreed sales. Bangladesh retains the conventional pricing lead, Pakistan remains competitive, India concentrates on specialist value, and Turkey holds its established regional position. No fresh market sales were reported this week.

The next test is whether dry freight declines for long enough to change owners' decisions. The correction is real, but tanker employment remains supported by Gulf disruption, while smaller dry sectors have surrendered little ground. Recycling buyers have maintained their numbers without securing a fresh supply of candidates. The market has started moving in their direction, although it has not arrived there yet. Owners will need more than one uncomfortable week before the beaches see the benefit.

# Bangladesh

## CHATTOGRAM NEEDS MORE THAN STEEL

*Margin notes: Buyers Ready. | Plate Advance. | Seasonal Watch.*

Chattogram remains at the top of the conventional recycling rankings, where buying appetite continues to exceed the supply of workable candidates. Recyclers are still prepared to compete for suitable bulkers, tankers and container units, but their leading position has not translated into a meaningful increase in new commitments. Freight earnings and second-hand alternatives still give owners reasons to keep trading. Conventional recycling indications remain unchanged across all vessel categories, maintaining Bangladesh's competitive advantage over neighbouring destinations. The demand exists; the opportunity to turn it into business remains scarce.

Local steel gave Chattogram a welcome lift, with plate rising from BDT 64,000 to BDT 66,000 per ton on October 11 and holding through Friday. Its Dollar equivalent increased from USD 522.24 to USD 538.56 per ton. The move improves the steel underpinning of vessel pricing, although demand for finished products remains subdued. At the waterfront, the 7,078 LDT bulker Uniorder arrived October 15 and the 8,275 LDT tanker Green October 14, following three smaller tankers earlier in the week. Petrova was delivered October 9, while the 911 LDT Pacific Leader is recorded as an arrival on October 6. These are movements of previously secured ships, not new recycling sales.

Attention is turning towards the approaching dry season, when construction activity and improved operating conditions may lift local steel demand. That improvement remains an expectation rather than an established trend. The upcoming Durga Puja banking closure from October 20 to 24, including weekly holidays, also makes advance coordination of documentation and settlement arrangements important ahead of the October 25 to 28 delivery tides. Limited banking services at port-related branches may continue during the holiday period. With domestic consumption still slow, stronger plate quotations alone may not support a higher bid. Chattogram has the leading conventional numbers and buyers to use them, but its next breakthrough depends on owners releasing suitable ships.

# India

## ALANG LANDS ANOTHER GIANT

*Margin notes: Specialists Deliver. | Currency Lifts. | Value Persists.*

The delivery of the 31,300 LDT LNG carrier Kano on October 15 underlines a part of Alang's market that conventional rankings do not capture. Gas carriers, reefers, passenger ships and other non-ferrous-rich units can command markedly different economics from ordinary steel tonnage, allowing recyclers to pursue opportunities beyond the regional price contest. The 4,815 LDT Kadija also delivered on October 15, while Maymei, a 9,877 LDT tanker, was delivered October 11. These are movements of vessels committed earlier, but they sustain yard activity while the fresh sales list remains quiet.

Plate declined from INR 42,600 per ton last Friday to INR 42,300 on October 16, after briefly dipping to INR 41,800 midweek. In Dollar terms, however, the stronger Rupee lifted its equivalent from USD 435.23 at the start of the reporting period to USD 445.17 by Friday. The currency improvement provides support to recyclers, although it does not narrow the pricing differential with Bangladesh and Pakistan by itself. September inflation rose to 5.54%, while the recent increase in the policy repo rate to 5.50% adds financing and domestic demand considerations.

The 8,767 LDT tanker Modeste arrived October 14, while the latest port position records the 9,886 LDT Enisey as delivered October 9. India's proposed inclusion of two facilities on the European List remains pending, but its compliance and specialist strengths continue to matter when owners assess recycling options. Conventional recycling indications remain unchanged, leaving India in third place behind Bangladesh and Pakistan. That ranking remains an honest description of Alang's position for standard tonnage. The specialist market continues to tell a different story.

# Pakistan

## GADANI'S PIPELINE COMES ASHORE

*Margin notes: Pipeline Delivers. | Steel Settles. | Margins Matter.*

Gadani's waterfront has continued to receive the benefit of vessels secured during its earlier buying run. Maestro, a 5,143 LDT bulker, was delivered on October 14, following the 7,381 LDT Portland 11 on October 9. These movements advance the existing pipeline rather than signal fresh transactions, and have helped satisfy some immediate yard requirements. Bangladesh remains ahead on conventional indications, but Pakistan retains competitive numbers and buyers willing to consider workable candidates. The more measured approach now reflects reduced urgency, not an absence of interest.

Steel began the week at PKR 192,000 per ton, or USD 691.39, before recovering to PKR 195,000, approximately USD 702.20, on October 12 and holding there through Friday. The increase reversed the early dip but left the market marginally below last week's closing PKR 196,000. With domestic finished-steel demand still cautious and imported scrap a competing source of supply, recyclers have limited incentive to chase vessel prices materially higher. The Rupee remains relatively stable and September inflation eased to 10.26%, providing some financial predictability without transforming the underlying demand picture.

Conventional recycling indications remain unchanged, with Pakistan retaining second place on the sub-continent rankings. The shortage of fresh recycling candidates continues to frustrate transactional activity, but buyers also have reason to be selective after receiving tonnage secured during the stronger August market. When owners bring appropriate units to market, Gadani should remain a credible competitor. For now, attention remains on margin discipline and downstream steel sales rather than indiscriminate competition. Ships are reaching the plots, even if the next wave of purchases is still waiting to be agreed.

# Turkey

## ALIAGA PICKS ITS MOMENT

*Margin notes: Prices Hold. | Currency Pressure. | Niche Endures.*

Aliaga's recycling indications remain unchanged after the correction recorded last week, with domestic finished-steel demand and cautious mill purchasing still limiting appetite for higher-priced candidates. Local scrap economics have not provided an incentive for further upward movement, particularly in a market where imported scrap pricing and processing costs compete directly with vessel acquisition. Conventional indications consequently remain at last week's reduced levels. The board has settled for now, but the underlying steel market offers little urgency.

Turkey's September inflation slowed to 29.73%, a welcome improvement that has not removed pressure on purchasing power. The Turkish Lira remains weak against the Dollar, while the central bank's 37% policy rate continues to make working capital expensive. Such conditions encourage disciplined buying rather than speculation on a near-term recovery in steel consumption. For yards paying internationally for vessels and selling into a largely domestic scrap market, the currency and financing calculations remain demanding.

Aliaga nonetheless retains a distinct commercial role through proximity to European owners, specialised vessel handling and relevant environmental requirements. These advantages can outweigh the substantial headline pricing gap with the sub-continent when vessel location and recycling compliance matter more than nominal price. The market remains selective, with no fresh sales reported this week, but its established niche continues to provide opportunities when appropriate tonnage becomes available.
