REVERBERATIONS! 
Post–New Year developments and shocking events have continued to send reverberations across 
the markets, directly and indirectly, following the surreal extrication of President Maduro and his 
wife from Venezuela under Trump’s orders — with clear intentions around the country’s oil riches 
— followed by his marble-chasing claims over Greenland that are based on similarly wet-spaghetti 
logic, which has since sparked a serious fallout with several European and even Canadian leaders
to match. What a time to be a witness. 
In the meantime, S&P markets saw at least one buyer in particular, who has been making some 
major plays on VLCC assets as secondhand values show few signs of deterioration — and the Baltic 
Exchange Dry Index echoed just that this week as it halted a nine-session slide to mark a 2.3% U-
turn, climbing to 1,567 points. This was driven by gains across segments: Capes (up 2.3%), Panamax 
(up 4.3%), and the smaller segments adding four points by week’s end. Notably, the overall 
benchmark index still finished the week down 7.2%. Oil too continued to trip on itself and stayed 
below the coveted USD 60/barrel mark despite a 0.4% increase, closing the week out at USD 
59.44/barrel. 
The U.S. Dollar meanwhile dominated all ship recycling currencies this week, while local steel plate 
prices across destinations dissolved into a mixed bag of opposing moves — both competitively and 
historically (i.e., versus last week). 
As such, ship recycling markets may be continue to deprived of tonnage as hopes for a brighter start 
to 2026 — indicated by the tapering indices back in November – December 2025 — begin to fade
once again. Prices across the Indian sub-continent have meanwhile continued their own song and 
dance on a downward path over the last month, with dry bulk indications at the bidding tables 
regularly below USD 400/LDT and several sales reportedly even being concluded in the USD 370s–
USD 380s/LDT range. 
Across destinations, India briefly surged by about USD 30/LDT before losing the full move the very 
next week as fundamentals softened. Pakistan’s demand has begun to show, with end buyers now 
increasingly hungry to fill plots as empty anchorages become more of the norm than the exception. 
Bangladeshi recyclers have largely been on the lookout for particular units based on their recent 
appetites as local offerings have helped them bounce back to the top of the market rankings again.
And Turkey? A bevy of European RoRos finally arrived Aliaga this week, delivering much-needed 
sustenance to an ailing local industry. 
Overall, the interim / near-future still looks like a quiet one – maybe not for Turkey? That’d a switch.

GOING BIG! 
For as satiated as the Chattogram market has been of late - given its healthy set of deliveries in late 
November and even the last couple of weeks, including this one, where at least one decent LDT 
tanker arrived locally – local ship recyclers are clearly becoming available for the right kind of 
tonnage - and that too at the best levels in the industry. However, this recent interest is deviating 
towards mostly larger LDT units like LNGs, tankers, and dry vessels such as capes (if any do come 
available).  
With prices to match and as determined as tier-1 local buyers tend to be, indications surfacing this 
week have helped push Bangladesh into the top spot of the market rankings yet again. So, now
you know, if you’ve got a large LDT hulker regardless of type, now’s as good a time as any to get 
in on it.  
Notwithstanding the limited spectrum of resurging optimism, this improvement has puzzlingly 
arrived at a peculiar time when inflation has risen from 8.29% in November to 8.49% in December 
and prices across the board (food, housing, utilities, furnishings, etc.) have risen of late. This was
followed on by a local steel plate price that tanked USD 12/Ton this week and end at about USD 
487/ton, which was accompanied by a declining Bangladeshi Taka that lost also 12 basis points to 
the U.S. Dollar and conclude the week at BDT 122.40 – one of the higher levels we have seen of late.
Moreover, as violence continues to mar the streets of the capital city Dhaka, amid escalating attacks 
that saw two newspaper offices become the latest victims, election fever has clearly been ramping 
up along with local agitation and its effects on day-to-day life. Blended into the mix is the domestic
economy, making it a commendable feat to witness the resilience of the Chattogram Choppers at 
the bidding tables, week after week. 
Overall, while this market stands open and willing to engage in serious discussions on tonnage, 
there remains an overall reluctance to engage in small(er) LDT units, which have been increasingly 
diverted Westward. Smaller, poor(er) condition bulkers are also seeing lower rates here, as well as 
the entire sub-continent, as a few recent fixtures well below USD 400/LDT come to light.  
With the critical election looming on the horizon on February 12th, Bangladeshi buyers need to 
remain alert enough to snap up their share of any of the slim pickings of the right vessels that may 
make an appearance before the elections, especially if the wrong choice of leader is elected (any
recent elections affecting the world grab attention?), and as Pakistan and India still remain on their 
heels.

INDIA
CURIOUS WEEKS! 
It has been a curious 2026 in India thus far, with steel plate prices and local offerings surging by nearly 
USD 30/LDT at the start of the year, only to find themselves tumbling down the very next week by 
nearly the same number and spectacularly lose any gains that may have otherwise been realized, 
leaving owners and cash buyers with little chance to gauge the way of the pricing winds from Alang 
in the coming week(s).  
And domestic fundamentals have certainly been to blame as they have been on an absolute bender of 
late, given that local steel plate prices reclaimed USD 11/ton this week (having lost USD 24/ton just the 
week prior) and clock out at USD 410/Ton. The Indian Rupee meanwhile lost over 0.55% and welcomed 
the weekend at Rs. 90.72 – approaching the psychological boundary of Rs. 91 once again. Should that 
come to fruition, ever-nervy local recyclers will find themselves promptly reassessing their offerings,  
The only rainbow on the otherwise grey horizon was the arrival of 3 new vessels at Alang’s waterfront 
this week, including an over 16K LDT tanker. Hopefully, all were concluded at levels safe enough to 
keep their deals in the green (or out of the red at the very least).  
Domestic inflation has meanwhile managed to climb out of stagflation territory from 0.71% back in 
November and ring the New Year in at 1.33%, clearly inching into healthier zones once again. And if 
this trend continues over the next couple of weeks, we may see fundamentals stabilize and bring prices 
up enough to grab some attention again, especially as the pivotal month of March (and businesses 
closing their books for their year) comes around.  
Overall, Alang recyclers certainly seem ready to engage in discussions at current levels and on any 
tonnage – as and when it may be tabled. For now, it’s clearly a “take it for what it is” situation.

PAKISTAN
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
    
SLAP HAPPY! 
Right alongside India, Pakistan has registered a strange start to the year with hungry end buyers 
finding themselves shifting from 3rd place on the market rankings to the 2nd without really having 
changed their offer levels at all, thanks to comparatively stable offerings than what neighboring 
India’s pricing trajectory has been of late. And while firmer levels are always welcome on the 
supply side of things, this has unfortunately not translated into any fresh arrivals at Gadani’s
waterfront so far this year.  
Clearly, several items have colluded to deliver this effect viz. a lack of supply, a lack of HKC yards
to engage in local competition and ensure a fair price target is achieved, and an unwillingness on 
part of the selling community to engage in DASR procedures, which generally result in delays at 
anchorage and potential loss of delivery time on what should otherwise be prompt deliveries, all 
while slow-rolling formalities are completed. Gadani recyclers, it would seem, are destined to enter
an unintendedly slap happy phase this January 2026.  
And with fundamentals playing the funk again, that local steel plate prices have resumed flatlining 
at USD 600/Ton, indicating a lack of local trading on account of virtually no local deliveries or 
generated recycled ship steel. The PKR also added to current stresses, as it too lost nearly 0.5% 
against the U.S. Dollar (like their Indian brethren) entering the weekend at PKR 280. 
Yet some positivity remains: Pakistan’s first HKC approved yard has been welcomed into the 
international fraternity, and several more are reportedly set to follow in the coming month(s).
Hopefully, Gadani starts to catch up to the international stage with HKC infrastructure upgrades 
and accreditations soon.  
For now, despite its firmer offerings, we do not foresee too many (if any) of the rarely proposed
units heading to local shores.
