# Market Commentary

## SHUTDOWN!

The decline of the sub-continent markets came into stark view once again this week, with even vintage tonnage being returned to trading lanes, such is the ineptitude and reluctance of collective sub-continent recyclers to commit on the paucity of any available units, at anywhere near sensible numbers.

Reliability and workable L/Cs remain a key concern for Cash Buyers and Ship Owners alike, with Bangladesh all but limiting new arrivals as the Central State bank refuses to sanction any fresh financing for ship recycling endeavors.

Reportedly, only essentials (such as food, fuel, and fertilizers) are able to procure Central State bank approval in Bangladesh, especially for the valuable reserves of U.S. Dollars that are budgeted for domestic expenses. This means that only a small number of Bangladeshi Buyers who have access to private funds are able to secure vessels for recycling at the moment.

Meanwhile, steel plate prices have been falling (more than firming) across the sub-continent markets and currencies have also depreciated astonishingly across all locations to record / historical lows and this is not giving Recyclers any confidence to offer up firm or even workable levels with any sort of certainty.

India remains perhaps the most reliable and performing of all markets, even though prices have come off significantly in Alang, and any available offers are tentative at best, coming in at the low USD 500s/LDT, levels that are now becoming an unfortunate reality of ship recycling today.

Pakistan is similarly low, with End Buyers (for the most part) keeping their eyes on competing markets before offering generally unworkable and depressed numbers.

Finally, after last week’s dip in plate prices, Turkey remains suspended in ‘airplane mode’ with an additional dip in plate prices this week, and no activity reportedly taking place in Aliaga this week.

# Bangladesh

## NON-ENTITY!

*Margin notes: LC woes. | Quiet end.*

Chattogram remains a non-entity of a recycling destination, with most End Buyers unable to offer on vessels due to currently imposed L/C restrictions. Moreover, those that do have private financing available for vessel purchases, are mostly preferring to wait-and-watch in case of further market falls, rather than commit on an eventually un-affordable unit.

Even the smallest L/Cs of USD one million (and lower) are reportedly finding it challenging to get approval these days. As such, there is very little point in even testing Bangladeshi Buyers with tonnage, so dire are sentiments and permissions at present.

There was no steel plate trading this week, but the Bangladeshi Taka weakened past BDT 103 against the U.S. Dollar, reminding Bangladeshi Recyclers just how volatile the currency is anymore.

Accordingly, it is sure to be a quiet end to the year for a subdued Bangladeshi market, with minimal vessels also incoming in port reports – as confirmed by this week’s performance, where no new vessels arrived the waterfront.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# India

## HEADACHES!

*Margin notes: Over 80. | Steering clear.*

Over recent times, the Indian Rupee has been the leading cause of headaches as the currency depreciated worryingly over Rs. 82 against the U.S. Dollar, only to have firmed up to Rs. 80.4X this week.

There have been very few sales to report (so worrying are prices) and obtaining any sort of reasonably firm offer has become a challenge unto itself, especially as fundamentals remain so shaky.

There has even been talk of numbers below USD 500/LDT (the first time we have seen talk of prices in the USD 400s/LDT for some time now) on less favoured, poor condition units - something that has steered Cash Buyers & Owners away from recycling markets at present, especially whilst freight rates remain decent across the board.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# Pakistan

## DISINTERESTED & DEPRESSED!

*Margin notes: Lacking confidence. | Few vessels.*

Pakistan is a market lacking confidence at present, and that has shown with an almost total absence of firm offers this week.

Gadani’s Performance has been in question for much of this year and this is also leading most Cash Buyers to avoid the Pakistani market given the opportunity, even for lower levels from competing markets.

Fundamentals remain depressed and shaky as the Pakistani Rupee treads around PKR 260 against the U.S. Dollar, while local plate prices took in a tiny correction this week.

Adding to the local depression is a remarkable lack of candidates to work on, so it isn’t surprising to see that all sub-continent markets remain so disinterested and depressed at present.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# Turkey

## ON IT GOES!

*Margin notes: Plate drops again. | 80% plummet.*

After last week’s drop in import and local steel plate prices to the tune of USD 10/MT, the trend continued as both (import and local steel) reportedly registered an additional USD 10/MT drop this week as well.

Even the Lira continues to wobble and weaken by the week (down to TRY 18.6X against the U.S. Dollar), in what has been a catastrophic plummet by nearly 80% (from TRY 10) over the span of the last 12 months.

Mixed in with the ongoing utter lack of available tonnage, it’s only a matter of time until yards start shutting down altogether and Aliaga becomes the only destination for large LDT EUSRR tonnage.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**
