“He is a wise man who does not grieve for the things which he has not, 
but rejoices for those which he has.”  
– Epictetus 
As sales dry up into sub-continent markets due to firming freight rates and plummeting vessel
prices, the industry is (once again and expectedly) entering a lull, especially as the traditionally 
quieter summer / monsoon months descend. 
At this time, we do not anticipate Ship Owners or Cash Buyers to offload any of their 
inventories (if vessels are even available) at these reduced rates and End Buyers still remain 
reluctant to commit units at any firm numbers being demanded, such is the volatility in the 
market at present. 
There have not been any sales to benchmark this recent fall in levels, but depreciating currencies 
and plummeting steel plates prices seem to have knocked over USD 100/LDT off across the sub-
continent & Turkish markets and sentiments do appear to be shredded as nerves remain frayed.
Accordingly, there have been some opportunistic numbers being dished around, far below 
where most in the industry believe prices should be at present, and as expected, no Ship Owner 
or Cash Buyer is presently willing to entertain such offers in the low USD 600s/LDT (perhaps
even lower), as recycling markets stabilize for the time-being.  
A gradual rebound is eventually expected, especially as supply remains extremely restricted 
and yards are more than empty, but just how far and how soon levels will return remains 
anyone’s guess. 
Overall, despite yard capacity improving across the sub-continent, freight sectors too have been 
performing admirably (Tankers, Containers and Dry), thereby resulting in a suffocation of 
resales to global Recyclers. Evidence of this even presents itself at the waterfront, as local port 
positions are starting to dwindle with marginal arrivals this week.  
As such, given the historical tenacity of these pricing trends, we can expect levels and demand 
to pick up as we head towards the third quarter of the year, with most in the industry hoping 
for a sustained recovery..

MARKET COMMENTARY

BANGLADESH 
SMELLING BLOOD! 
After a largely inert few months and as Chattogram yards process and sell off their 
inventory, demand is starting to creep back up in Bangladesh once again. 
The primary and growing local concern continues to be a currency that has rapidly 
deteriorated against the U.S. Dollar (about a 5% fluctuation through the week) and a 
pause on a number of high-profile construction projects due to spiralling costs. 
Even local port position has started to dwindle with just a few fresh arrivals reported this 
week. 
Overall, as the Bangladeshi budget is expected on June 9th, it is hoped that the 
government may provide some stimulus for the ship recycling industry, and perhaps 
even renegotiate some of the big domestic construction projects to get ‘business-as-usual’ 
underway in Bangladesh once again.

INDIA 
Seeking cheaper. 
 
AVERAGING DOWN! 
As some of the higher-priced inventory continues to be delivered & beached at Alang, 
Alang Recyclers are increasingly hopeful of acquiring some cheaper units, in order to 
average out some of these hefty recent losses. 
Unfortunately, incoming tonnage is now at a premium and likely to remain that way over 
the summer / monsoon months, particularly as these new lower realities start to gradually 
sink in. 
Steel plate prices continue to endure wild daily swings – often up and down over 
consecutive days by as much as USD 15 – USD 20/LDT, whilst the currency continues its 
worrying deterioration into the high Rs. 77s against the U.S. Dollar.

GMS Weekly 
SHUT SHOP! 
Pakistan, as a viable ship recycling destination, may as well shut-shop and close its doors 
for the time-being, as no Ship Owner or Cash Buyer is looking forward to heading towards 
a market where trouble has now become a key watchword. 
Inability to open L/Cs on time, blatant renegotiations at the waterfront and an utter refusal 
to perform deals have now become a shocking regularity in Gadani. Accordingly, vessels 
are better off being diverted elsewhere for a lower price, however, with a greater assurance 
of a far smoother performance. 
While it would seem unfair to isolate Pakistan whilst all markets have suffered recent 
reversals, however, nowhere has this been more acutely received by End Buyers than in 
Pakistan, where here too, the currency has depreciated alarmingly, yet, Gadani Recyclers 
seem incapable of putting up with it.

TIPPING AWAY! 
The performance of the Turkish market continues to cause concern as fundamentals 
continue to dither without mercy. Both import and local steel plate prices have reportedly 
tumbled by about USD 15/Ton this week, whilst the Turkish Lira gradually weakens 
towards TRY 17. 
The supply of tonnage remains just as weak and an overall lack of transactions has left this 
market gradually tipping away from any pricing reality at present. 
As such, much like its subcontinent competitors, the Turkish market remains suspended, 
devoid of any meaningful activity.
