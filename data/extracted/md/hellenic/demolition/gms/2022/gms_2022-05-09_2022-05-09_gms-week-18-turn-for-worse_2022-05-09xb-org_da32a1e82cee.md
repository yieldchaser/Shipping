TURN FOR WORSE! 
May 6th, 2022 
Volume 238, Issue 1030 
Week 18 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
“One man with courage makes a majority.”  
– Andrew Jackson 
Sub-continent markets have taken a turn for the worse this week, as collapsing steel prices in
India and Eid holidays in Pakistan, Bangladesh, and Turkey have led to depressed sentiments 
and virtually no new offers emanating on any available tonnage. 
Most End Users now want to wait-and watch-market developments before offering anew on 
vessels at far lower levels that seem more in line with the realistic USD 650s/LDT than the
struggling USD 700s/LDT most in the industry were gunning for earlier on select units. 
There has certainly been some increasing and noteworthy reticence from sub-continent
Recyclers about having to dip into levels at or above USD 700/LDT. So, there is perhaps some
justification for watching-and-waiting if units can be secured at these currently lower levels. 
Notwithstanding, despite this most recent volatility, fundamentals (including steel plate prices,
which are at record highs of their own) overall remain firm across the sub-continent markets
and the expectation is that appetite and sentiments will likely firm up once Eid holidays
conclude from next week onwards, especially before the onset of June / monsoon season. 
There has been some worrying currency depreciations in all recycling destinations (India,
Pakistan, Bangladesh and even Turkey) over the past few weeks and this week, the currencies
depreciated across the board, despite capacity remaining firm to take the paucity of units that 
are available. 
Since supply is so limited at present (with all Dry Bulk and Container markets firm & Tanker
charter rates starting to pick up again), recycling prices could possibly return to previous highs
going into the traditionally quieter summer / monsoon season. 
Finally, at the far end, the Turkish market continues to gradually degrade, as fundamentals and 
prices both recorded further declines this week, as levels finally fall below USD 400/MT for dry 
units.

MARKET COMMENTARY

BANGLADESH 
OUT OF PICTURE! 
The Bangladeshi market remained out of the picture for the most part this week, not only 
as the country celebrated Eid holidays, but the severely weakened sentiments also lead 
to the utterly unworkable levels at present, as local offerings are no longer competitive 
even with the traditionally weaker Indian and Pakistani markets. 
The currency has suffered some worrying depreciations over the past week, as steel 
prices have cooled off as well over the month of Ramadan. However, the overall
prognosis for Chattogram remains positive / firm in the months ahead. 
There also remains a general lack of supply of locally flavoured large LDT units and this 
may lead to some overly speculative purchases on any available tonnage once the 
Chattogram market resumes post Eid-holidays. 
This week however, as quiet as this market was while Local Recyclers celebrated Eid, so 
was Chattogram’s anchorage as not a single fresh unit arrived the waterfront this week.

INDIA 
Reluctant. 
 
STEEL SLIDE! 
Indian steel plate prices have continued their dramatic volatility this week as well, as levels 
declined by over USD 25/Ton, only to recover marginally before the week ended.  
As such, most Alang Recyclers remain reluctant to dive into fresh negotiations / new 
purchases and some are even hesitant to offer at all, especially at these incredibly high
overall levels being sought by the few Owners who are introducing the occasional unit 
into the recycling markets. 
Rising inflation rates and a currency that has depreciated significantly against the U.S.
Dollar during Q1 are some of the fundamentals fuelling a great portion of the negativity 
in the Indian recycling market, along with shaky fundamentals over the past few weeks. 
With the Ukrainian conflict still ongoing, India’s inflation will remain of concern going 
forward as Russia is one of India’s primary sources of oil and the current sanctions are 
making it harder for the Indian Government to acquire the same for domestic 
consumption, giving rise to the recent volatility / domestic inflation.  
There is always some extreme volatility from week-to-week in Alang, but the status of 
India as the top paying recycling destination after the conclusion of Eid holidays may not 
be so secure after all.

GMS Weekly 
WOEFUL WEEK! 
With a political coup and a dramatically depreciating currency still fresh in memory, it has 
been a rather woeful week in Pakistan – both in terms of fetching fresh firm offers and also 
getting Gadani Recyclers to honour those deals that have already been committed locally. 
At times like these, Recyclers need to take responsibility, rather than use every market 
movement as an excuse to fret and walk away from existing commitments. A reliable Cash 
Buyer counterpart is the only other element in the transaction that can help ensure a
smooth performing deal. 
As Eid holidays conclude and the traditionally firmer Bangladesh gets back to buying, it is 
hard to foresee any of the dwindling supply of recycling candidates ending up in Pakistani
hands (especially at this time).

TUMBLING STEADILY! 
The Turkish market continues its steady decline as levels have dropped nearly USD 80/MT 
in a matter of a few weeks.  
Declining steel plate prices (both import and steel) and a weakening Lira (that has now 
fallen increasingly closer to the dreadful TRY 15.0 mark against the U.S. Dollar) that have 
been the primary driving forces behind local offerings. 
The market has remained relatively quiet and most Recyclers are looking for tonnage basis 
later deliveries in the summer. As such, a permeating demand certainly seems to be
present. 
Just how this market will react moving past Eid remains to be seen. Yet, levels remain at 
record highs even today.
