“The best way out is always through.”

While the recycling industry seemed to witness some activity surfacing from the sub-continent 
markets last week, this week turned into a currency frenzy in both, Bangladesh and Pakistan, 
as the summer of minimal activity and sales seems as though it will sluggishly drag on. 
Both locations reportedly experienced a sharp decline in their domestic currencies within the 
week, leading to more confusion & caution in the sub-continent and this could take a week (or
more) of a ‘watch-and-wait’ attitude to see where post-currency-crash vessel pricing will 
eventually land. 
It was the Indian market that remained the only (sub-continent) recycling destination where
demand and fundamentals remained ironically stable through the course of the week, despite 
the ongoing (domestic) plate price volatility that has beset itself as an enduring reality. 
West end of things, the Turkish market suffered another decline in local plate prices this week, 
ensuring local sentiment remains disinterested & depressed and would seemingly remain so, 
for some time ahead. 
On the supply side, even as dry bulk charter levels cool off, the industry has still not witnessed 
a deluge of dry units being introduced for recycling, despite a collection of 90s built units that 
are still operating on various trades and this is where much of the supply is expected to come 
from as we head into the fourth quarter of the year.  
There is also the expectation that containers will return for recycling at some stage, as charter 
rates have also come off from some of the historical highs seen over this latest bull run.  
As such, the question as to whether it may be as busy an end to the year is up in the air, at least 
until pricing chart below eventually re-adjusts to the new realities on the ground.

MARKET COMMENTARY

BANGLADESH 
TAKA TUMULT! 
Historically, the Bangladeshi government tightly controlled the availability of U.S. 
Dollars in the country, in order to regulate the exchange rate between the Bangladeshi 
Taka and the U.S. Dollar. This resulted in the BDT being far more a stable currency over 
time, than its competing sub-continent neighbours. 
This week, however, the Bangladeshi Central Bank finally released the U.S. Dollar rates 
to be set by the free markets as opposed to governmental controls, resulting in the official 
U.S. Dollar bank rates against the Taka to sharply plummet from region BDT 95 to BDT 
106.6X within a matter of days – a drop well in excess of 10%.  
This is a pivotal moment in Bangladesh’s recycling history i.e. releasing local Recyclers 
of the security of a key fundamental in pricing ships, that will now be just another critical 
item for local Buyers to strongly consider on future negotiations. 
Meanwhile, as local recycling yards are at the emptiest they have been for some time 
now, there are signs of a lingering demand amongst local Recyclers who are looking to 
fill those empty spaces and get back to business. 
However, local frustrations of not being able to open L/Cs and beach vessels due to the 
recent government mandate on limiting Central Bank approvals on any L/Cs over USD 
2 – USD 3 million, runs deep amongst the domestic ship recycling fraternity and is 
severely limiting the top paying market from making a decent comeback on some of the 
recent units on offer. 
We can only hope for less drastic of a re-adjustment on the effects of the government’s 
decision, where prices will re-adapt sooner (rather than later) and that should hopefully 
affect the stability of this market in the near future.

INDIA 
Top of the ranks? 
 
NUMBER 1? 
As Pakistani and Bangladeshi currencies plummeted this week, we can certainly expect
their vessel offerings to negatively re-adjust moving forward and this could inadvertently 
slide India into the top spot, should the competing market declines be sufficient (drastic?)
enough. 
This certainly sounds optimistic for future sub-continent deliveries given that India has 
been the one notable performer whilst its neighbouring competitors continue to struggle
with L/C restrictions, currency depreciations, sliding steel prices and subsequent 
performance issues at the waterfront. 
Meanwhile, the excruciatingly dismal supply of tonnage leaves much to be desired for, 
despite where various markets levels stand today. 
On the local fundamentals front, the Indian Rupee suffered some declines, weakening
from Rs. 79.2X back to Rs. 79.6X against the U.S. Dollar this week while local steel plate 
prices jumped around, eventually ending the week just USD 1/Ton over last. 
As such, the coming week(s) may be a surprise for the Indian Recyclers.

ONE STEP FORWARD, TWO BEHIND? 
Like the Bangladeshi Taka (that depreciated over 10% this week), the Pakistani Rupee 
depreciated tragically by over PKR 10, ending the week at PKR 236.6X. In fact, the PKR has 
been gradually disintegrating over the last month, to the point it has depreciated by well 
over 10% within this time. 
Local steel plate prices aren’t helping the domestic recycling sector as they danced in sync 
with the currency, declining by nearly USD 17/Ton this week. 
Logically, and as has historically been the case with Gadani Recyclers, local sentiments
tend to be rather fragile and are naturally going to deteriorate moving forward, just how 
far down remains the question.  
Domestic flooding also continues to be a problem & the recent effects on domestic 
fundamentals have only exacerbated an already tenuous situation in Gadani. 
The recent decline in the PKR also calls into question the recent fixture of a Suezmax tanker 
to Gadani Buyers (at impressive levels over USD 600/LDT last week) as to whether the deal 
will eventually be ‘safely’ concluded, given Gadani Buyers questionable performances
during the recent collapse. 
As such, it seems as thought the Pakistani market may have taken one step forward and 
two steps back this week.

MORE OF THE SAME! 
Turkey continues with more of the same.  
With local steel plate prices that have declined marginally by about USD 5/Ton, an import 
steel price that continues to languish USD 20/Ton lower since a couple of weeks, and a Lira 
that is jumping around a record  TRY 18.2X against the U.S. Dollar, the ongoing situation 
remains a grim reminder to just how quiet Turkey has been since its USD 250/MT decline 
in vessel prices (and will likely continue to be). 
Sentiments remain weak, Aliaga Recyclers remain disinterested in the non-existent
number of units being proposed, and activity as a result, is as expected. 
It seems increasingly possible that the Turkish market could be out of commission, not 
only for the 4th quarter this year, but also well into 2023.
