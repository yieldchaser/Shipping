Following a somewhat slower start to the year, vessel prices seem to be bouncing back in both
Pakistan and Bangladesh this week. 
Steel plate prices have improved overall (especially in India) and the lack of tonnage continues
to frustrate Recyclers. As such, any available tonnage is seeing intense competition at and above
market pricing from hungry end Buyers. 
Meanwhile, following a sluggish few months of performance, the Bangladeshi market seems to
have come roaring back to the forefront this week, with a noticeable increase in levels anywhere
between USD 20 – USD 30/LDT, as the Chattogram market seems to finally wake up. 
Pakistan too is not far behind the Bangladeshi market and has also improved this week – so
much so that any geographically positioned tonnage is likely to end up in Gadani hands,
despite the far superior levels on offer from Bangladesh. 
India is similarly in a good place at present but is simply unable to compete with the rampant 
Bangladesh and Pakistani markets. As such, Alang Buyers are only able to secure HKC or
specialist / non-ferrous units, so uncompetitive is the Indian ship recycling market on any
available market vessels at present. 
On the Western end, the Turkish market continues its steady but sluggish performance as the 
Lira stabilizes at record lows, yet the relatively stable import and local steel prices have kept 
vessel offerings at impressive highs. 
Overall, as the Chinese New Year holidays approach, it is expected to be be a quieter period of 
time in terms of supply and activity. Notwithstanding, it is hoped that more Owners will take
advantage of these stunning rates, as the year of the Tiger commences!

MARKET COMMENTARY

BANGLADESH 
SURGING BACK! 
Bangladeshi Buyers have come surging back this week, as steel plate prices improve
nearly USD 18/Ton, and demand starts to pick up following a largely inert Christmas / 
New Year period. 
As a result, Chattogram Buyers have reportedly picked up a couple of vessels already in 
Cash Buyer hands (and sold on an ‘as is’ basis some time ago), including some locally 
preferred large LDT units. 
Given the way levels are persisting well above the USD 600/LDT mark, it is certainly 
surprising that more Owners have decided not to cash in on their older assets at these 
impressive levels, particularly in the beleaguered tanker sector where charter rates are 
still in the doldrums and showing little hope of any immediate rebound.

FIRM BUT FAR! 
Several specialist, non-ferrous units (stainless steel tankers and Russian fish factories) have 
made their way to Alang this year, especially as prices improve closer to the USD 600/LDT 
mark over the course of January. 
However, Indian Buyers are still unable to compete on competitive market tonnage, which 
is largely ending up in competing Pakistani or Bangladeshi hands. Even previously 
favoured passenger units are reportedly finding their way onto Pakistani shores. 
On the local fundamentals front, the Indian Rupee is trading at a steady level in the high
Rs. 74s against the U.S. Dollar, whilst local steel plate prices have improved by nearly USD 
16/Ton over the course of the week, leaving the Indian market positively poised as we 
enter into February.

GMS Weekly 
COMING UP! 
Having missed out on a number of market and even geographically positioned units, 
Gadani Buyers knew they had to raise the level of their game if they were to secure their 
share of any of the dwindling number of market / private vessels. 
The holiday season in the East is now upon us and this is likely to lead to an even greater 
dearth in the availability of units for hungry Recyclers. 
It will therefore be interesting to see how market dynamics resume post holidays, given 
that many end Buyers have plots cleared and are ready to buy and compete aggressively 
on any available units.

LETHARGY? 
The Turkish market seems to be going through a lethargic phase with virtually no news 
being reported on the SNP front. While reports of a trickle of tonnage arriving the 
waterfront do make it from time to time, there is little market tonnage to negotiate at 
present. 
Fundamentals too remain relatively stable as both import & local steel plate prices remain 
steady and the Turkish Lira buoys around the mid TRY 13s against the U.S. Dollar. 
As reported last year, most local Buyers are eager to get tonnage basis late Q1 delivery and 
as such, we can only hope this market stays steady (or even firms further) when the time 
comes.
