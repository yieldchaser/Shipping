# Market Commentary

## SPRING IN STEP!

Chinese (Lunar) New Year holidays concluded this week and there appears to be a real spring in the step of industry players, as international ship recycling markets continue to push on once again.

Both, Pakistan and Bangladesh, have been at the forefront of this recent resurgence, with an improved India (that is still unable to compete on any regular / non-HKC / non-specialist tonnage) and a relatively stable Turkey that has defied explanation for the most part with its remarkably firm / stable levels (especially in light of the collapsing Lira) and even a noteworthy improvement in import steel registering this week.

Sub-continent prices have managed to surge past USD 650/LT LDT on certain select units once again - such is the hunger of Cash Buyers and sub-continent Recyclers alike - just to secure a piece of the dwindling shortage of tonnage at present.

Steel plate prices across the board have been a key driver to the spectacular showings of late, as construction / infrastructure projects ramp up once again, with the worst of Convid-19 and its associated lockdowns and restrictions, seemingly easing of late.

Regrettably, the number of the comparatively less dangerous Omicron cases are on the rise globally even though the combination of vaccines and boosters appear to be (temporarily?) keeping the worst of it at bay – at least until news of another mutation invariably surfaces.

On the sales front, there was news of one 2002 built VLCC and another large LDT Aframax tanker sale to report this week, as opportunistic Owners returning from holidays seek to cash in at these admittedly fantastic levels, amidst continuing poor tanker charter rates.

# Bangladesh

## TIGERISH LEVELS!

*Margin notes: Roaring back. | Afra sold.*

As we chime in the Year of the Tiger, Bangladesh has come roaring back to the fore, displaying the most aggressive levels on show and in the process, even securing a deal or two.

Chattogram Buyers are getting increasingly hungry, with plots due to be filled and L/C limits to satisfy. As such, so some of the buying that is going on, occasionally surges above what is the reality on the ground.

Local steel plate prices (firming nearly USD 10/Ton this week) have certainly added to the flair of an increased local desire to bid on tonnage, boosting Cash Buyer eagerness on the prices they were eager to table this week.

One of the tanker sales for the week saw the 2003 Korean built and controlled by BULL of Indonesia Aframax unit PETROGARUDA (19,105 LDT) committed for a fantastic USD 650/LT LDT.

# India

## BUDGET AID!

*Margin notes: Side-lined. | Steel increase.*

India unfortunately remains stranded on the side-lines for yet another week, such has been the energy displayed by the rampant Bangladesh and Pakistan markets over the past couple of weeks or so.

The Indian budget was also announced this week and there is reportedly no material impact on the domestic ship recycling sector, rather, a positive impact is anticipated as government spending has reportedly been increased on infrastructure and defence.

The Rupee too has firmed marginally, trading in the mid Rs. 74 against the U.S. Dollar, whilst local steel plate prices have increased by nearly USD 11/Ton over the course of the week, leaving the Alang market positively poised as the industry heads into February.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# Pakistan

## VA VA VOOM!

*Margin notes: Stunning sale. | Sign of things to come?*

A stunning week in Pakistan led to some increasingly speculative offerings on various vessels this week, and even saw one VLCC reportedly concluded at well above market levels.

The Polemis controlled 2002 built NEW INSPIRATION (40,737 LDT) was finalised for an extraordinary price in the region of USD 660/LT LDT, gas free for hot works clean for delivery Gadani – as one over exuberant Cash Buyer seems ready to speculate on further upward movements in price, as this level is certainly not reflective of today’s prices.

With demand remaining rife across much of Gadani, the hope is that this particular sale may spur owners into introduce / conclude more vessels into the red hot sub-continent recycling markets in the weeks / months to come.

# Turkey

## ON THE RISE?

*Margin notes: Sluggish. | USD 400/Ton?*

Although the Turkish market has been sailing through a period of sluggishness (despite having some of the strongest levels on offer for tonnage), market demand remains steady and levels remain firm.

Despite the Turkish Lira coasting through some record lows (currently in the mid TRY 13.5X against the U.S. Dollar at the time of writing), import and local steel prices have remained relatively steady, delivering the necessary confidence to local Recyclers to maintain their current levels.

In fact, import steel has reportedly improved by a whopping USD 20/Ton this week and such an improvement on the back of stable prices is certain to bring further increases in local offerings.

Is USD 400/Ton the new frontier for Turkey?

Certainly, seems a possibility given that the Winter months are currently restricting movements of most undertow units through EU waters and with no major market tonnage being introduced for sale or any noteworthy sales to report, Aliaga Buyers are dwindling through previous deliveries at local plots and an eagerness to compete certainly appears evident, especially for post Q1 deliveries.
