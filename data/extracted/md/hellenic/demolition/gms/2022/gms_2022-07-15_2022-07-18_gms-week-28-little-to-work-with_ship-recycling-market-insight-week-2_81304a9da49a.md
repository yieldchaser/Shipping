### Editorial: Market Commentary

Currencies continue to depreciate across the globe and hit respective historical lows, as some dismal performances (primarily from Gadani Recyclers in the recent past), have left Owners and Cash Buyers nervous to touch this market whilst the situation remains so precarious. Fortunately, firm freight markets continue to deprive the recycling industry of any meaningful tonnage and this is likely to continue for (at least) the next few months ahead.

The summer / monsoon months are traditionally quieter due to the constant rains & tropical storms that tend to afflict the area resulting in slower cutting times of vessels during downpours. As such, labourers return to their hometowns as yards are ‘naturally’ condemned to a period bereft of activity. Notwithstanding, we have yet to see things get this quiet for many a year now.

The political (and now turning into a financial) collapse witnessed recently in Sri Lanka is also rattling sub-continent nerves, amidst fears from both the weaker Pakistan and Bangladeshi economies who feel they could well be next, as their respective currencies and the painful lack of available steel plates (that eventually affect domestic improvement and construction projects), all converge to an economy being suffered by the unfolding of times.

The only market that is displaying a degree of stability (at least where plate prices are concerned) is Turkey, with both import and local steel reporting no major changes through the course of the week.

Indian and Pakistani port reports too remain empty as recycling yards gradually turn barren across all of the major recycling destinations, whilst industry players hope for some stability and improved sentiments in the weeks / month(s) ahead.

## 3. Country Market Intelligence

### Bangladesh

- **Headline**: MOST LIKELY!
- **Key Notes**: *Signs of life.* | *Hoping to secure.*

Despite a minor dip in steel plate prices during the week, the Bangladeshi market appears to be the one most likely to provide some signs of life, as a few squeezed-out enquiries emanated from Chattogram Recyclers at generally (firmer, yet lower) levels this week.

There are also some low & below market / opportunistic number(s) being tossed around. Unfortunately, at these seemingly (and shockingly – because USD 600/Ton seem to be today’s) low levels, there seems to be little hope for End Buyers to secure any tonnage at the current rates.

The Bangladeshi Taka isn’t making life easy for any of the industry players, as the currency teeters on the edge of record lows against the U.S. Dollar, as it braces to breach BDT 94.

As such, Chattogram Buyers may perhaps hope to secure just one or two units post Eid holidays / in the weeks ahead, especially if they can build up their appetite and aggression to buy again.

### India

- **Headline**: CLAWING BACK!

Despite some marginal volatility this week (down by about USD 10/Ton), local steel prices have clawed back some ground of late – up by about USD 50/Ton since the lows seen only a few weeks ago.

Unfortunately, just as in Bangladesh, the currency continues to present a massive migraine for industry players considering an India re-delivery. The Rupee has finally (albeit briefly) spiked past Rs. 80 against the U.S. Dollar, only to settle back down towards Rs. 79.80 before ending the week.

Notwithstanding, India is a far more resilient economy when compared to its neighbours, one that is unlikely to go the way of Sri Lanka, but these are worrying times across the sub- continent and some stability on currencies will need to be seen for Recyclers (in general) to get back to the buying with any sort of confidence.

For now, with the monsoon in full swing and virtually no beachings this month, it is expected to be a quiet July for the Indian market.

### Pakistan

- **Headline**: LOWEST OF ALL!
- **Key Notes**: *Little hope.* | *Worth avoiding.*

Pakistan continues to languish as the lowest of all sub-continent markets and there really is little hope for Gadani Recyclers to get their hands on any meaningful tonnage whilst current sentiments, performance levels, and the deathly absence of tonnage, all persist in unison.

There have been some extraordinarily opportunistic offers in the low USD 500s/LDT, which of course were brusquely brushed aside by the concerned Cash Buyers and Owners alike.

Local steel plate prices seemed to flatline this week whilst the currency continues to take a beating. Like most global currencies, the Pakistani Rupee has been getting hammered as it briefly spiked past Rs. 210, only to settle back into the low Rs. 209 against the U.S. Dollar.

As such, Pakistan remains a market that will need some time to recoup local confidence and its pricing strategies moving forward. For now, however, competing Indian and Bangladeshi markets will more than likely be seeing much of the dwindling supply of vessels that do come for sale.

*Market Sales Reported*: **NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

---

### Turkey

- **Headline**: QUIET JULY!

The Turkish market has been virtually invisible all week, with a post-Eid holiday mood still prevailing, and at a great time too.

After weeks of merciless falls, steel plate prices finally evened out this week, with reportedly no further declines in both import and local steel. The currency however is the monster in the room that is gradually slithering towards TRY 18, as it seems destined to breach TRY 17.50 against the U.S. Dollar this week.

With no market units to speak off and a differential of more than double with the Indian sub-continent, it seems destined to be a quiet July (and likely beyond) for Aliaga.
