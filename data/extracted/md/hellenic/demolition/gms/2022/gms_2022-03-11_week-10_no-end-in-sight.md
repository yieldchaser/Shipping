# Market Commentary

## NO END IN SIGHT!

Markets have endured another boost off the back of surging commodity prices and there is every expectation that the USD 700/LDT mark will be imminently breached in the sub-continent markets once again.

These are comfortably (and certainly) the highest numbers we have seen since the historical highs of 2008, when over USD 800/LDT was surpassed and the recent universal skyrocketing steel plate prices across the recycling board (even in China) are carrying the weight of these recent prices.

The chief movement is coming from a rampant Bangladeshi market where steel prices continue to surge upwards (some of the most noteworthy increases of late), in line with soaring commodities in general, as the Russian invasion of Ukraine intensifies into an uncertain future.

Even in India, after two weeks of spectacular gains and despite registering further gains this week, steel prices had a minor correction during the course of the week, raising the guard of local recyclers who have been observing the rate of the recent climb. It may however be that prices continue their upward trajectory in Alang next week again, only to mirror global commodity prices moving upwards / forward.

Pakistan has also started to also improve once again, having missed several geographically positioned vessels that have since been diverted to competing shores.

On the Far end, the Turkish market, like the Bangladeshi market, recorded some impressive gains in local steel plate prices this week, pushing vessel prices into unchartered territory, and certainly worthy of exploiting on quick deliveries.

It remains to be seen just where the first transactions on market tonnage will be concluded at or above USD 700/LDT, as End Buyers do seem somewhat nervous to transact at these new decade long highs.

# Bangladesh

## LEADING LIGHTS!

*Margin notes: Surging on. | LC limits?*

Bangladesh leads the way once again, as steel prices surged upwards by nearly USD 65/Ton this week and vessel prices (as a result) pushed on towards the USD 700/LDT mark, in what have been nothing short of impressive gains from the market leaders.

Notwithstanding (and despite multiple enquiries), there has yet to be a sale to take place at these new levels as there does seem to be a growing reticence from Chattogram Buyers to secure ships at the peak of the market, perhaps anticipating likely falls ahead.

There is also a question mark on whether L/Cs can (will?) be opened by various banks at these new peaks, with certain Recyclers being required to place increased securities with the banks, in order to get those limits.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# India

## STEEL TURN!

*Margin notes: Reversals. | Pakistan back.*

Despite some worrying reversals towards the end of the week, local steel plate prices surged even further this week, jumping nearly USD 35/Ton – clearly a by-product of the global crisis that has affected all of the major recycling destination.

Consequently, this has led to increasing nerves from End Buyers and a reticence to offer on any units that are currently on the bidding tables, whilst they are increasingly adopt a wait-and-watch attitude towards further market developments.

Coupled with an Indian Rupee that has increasingly depreciated (briefly past Rs. 77) this week, ending at nearly 76.5X against the U.S. Dollar, a lot of the recent Indian lustre has been taken out of the market.

As a result, many of the market units (which were being discussed for an India delivery), now seem to be candidates for competing locations, as even Pakistan comes back into the picture this week.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# Pakistan

## WARMING UP!

*Margin notes: Back to buy? | Few candidates.*

Despite some recent nerves by Gadani Buyers, the Pakistani recycling market seems to be warming up once again, thanks to surging steel / commodity prices because of the ongoing crisis in Ukraine.

While not as impressive as Bangladesh, local steel plate prices reportedly jumped nearly USD 46/Ton this week – certainly impressive unto itself.

It would seem as though Recyclers in both Pakistan and India are just about on par at the moment (in terms of levels being offered), although there is a fundamental lack of candidates available for negotiation in order to test this pricing theory.

There are currently no signs of Gadani coming close to (or perhaps breaching) the USD 700/LDT threshold being (nearly) breached in Bangladesh, but at least there are some positive signs for this market after a period on the side-lines.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# Turkey

## FIRING ON!

*Margin notes: USD 500/Ton? | 2008 repeat?*

The ongoing crisis in the Black Sea is further pushing fundamentals into seemingly unchartered territory, as local offerings on tonnage make a noteworthy jump to get ever closer to the USD 500/Ton mark – certainly a first for this market.

Not only did local steel plate prices jump a remarkable USD 60/Ton this week, even import steel improved an additional USD 15/ton. Moreover, in keeping with the global competition and further displaying effects of the Ukrainian crisis, the currency continues to weaken, virtually sniffing the TRY 15.X mark against the U.S. Dollar.

The length of uncertainty surrounding the Baltic crisis remains uncertain, the conflict is invariably pushing scrap vessel prices back to the highs of 2008 (and the markets certainly remember how that roller coaster ended).

How this pricing strategy ends for Ship Owners depends on how promptly they are able to get their vessels into Aliaga and make best of the current incredible (for Turkey) pricing on offer.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**
