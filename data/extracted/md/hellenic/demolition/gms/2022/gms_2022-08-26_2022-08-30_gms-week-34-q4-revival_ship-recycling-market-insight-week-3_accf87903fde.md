## 1. Market Overview

- Activity increase.
- Inert summer.

- India performs.
- Charter dip.

### Editorial: Market Commentary

As a result of such crashing fundamentals, the Bangladeshi government responded by setting limits on incoming vessels, and any Letter of Credit (L/C) over USD 2 - 3 million now faces Central State Bank approval, where it is likely to be turned down (as all L/Cs have been so far) due to more pressing needs within the country for other uses of their foreign currency reserves.

Pakistan too has set limits on imports in order to watch their own foreign reserves, all while the country is facing terrible flooding, and hundreds of lives have been lost in the past week as international aid is sought, in order to help the millions that are being displaced & are losing their homes.

India has been the one performing constant sub-continent market presence of late, as several deals on specialist units have taken place in the last few weeks, to at least provide starved local Recyclers, a chance at filling their empty plots.

Turkey on the far-end, though stable this week, has a shattered local mind-set that is simply dis-interested to make any offers on units being privately proposed, as news of any publicly offered units into Aliaga has been virtually non-existent over the last few quarters.

Overall, as dry bulk (particularly Capesize) charter rates have cooled off recently, there is the expectation that we could see some greater activity going into the fourth quarter of the year, especially for the subdued ship recycling markets.

## 3. Country Market Intelligence

### Bangladesh

- **Headline**: STRUGGLING TO SECURE!
- **Key Notes**: *LC woes.* | *VLCC sale.*

Bangladeshi Recyclers continue to face difficulties opening fresh L/Cs on incoming vessels, with all L/Cs over USD 2 – USD 3 million now requiring special Central Bank approval to open.

Of course, End Buyers could always open Usance L/Cs, but so great are the currency depreciation fears at present that very few Chattogram Buyers are willing to take such a risk at this time.

Speaking of, the Bangladeshi Taka isn’t doing too well as it languishes at a record BDT 95 against the U.S. Dollar. Adding to the negativity / pressure onto local sentiments has been local steel plate prices that have fallen by about USD 11/Ton this week.

It was therefore curious to see a VLCC sold for an unexpectedly firm USD 610/LT LDT, potentially for a Bangladesh re-delivery within October as the VLCC URANUS (39,825 LDT) was reportedly committed to an over-eager Cash Buyer this week. Perhaps the concerned Cash Buyer is speculating on a loosening of L/C limits by the time October comes around or has another delivery location in mind.

---

### India

- **Headline**: CONSISTENT!

India remains the most consistent of the sub-continent markets in terms of levels and firm offers. Prices seem to have settled into the mid USD 500s/LDT (lower than competing markets), but at least Ship Owners / Cash Buyers can be assured of payments (L/C transactions) and End Buyer performances, where both Pakistan and Bangladesh have been faltering of late.

As such, for Ship Owners & Cash Buyers alike, Alang remains the safest delivery location in the sub-continent, even if they (Sellers & Cash Buyers) must accept a slightly discounted price.

Notwithstanding, prices in the mid-to-high USD 500/LDT are still some impressive numbers to beat, considering not too long ago, the markets had tumbled down to the mid USD 200s/LT.

The currency continues to (thankfully) buoy just south of Rs. 80 against the U.S. Dollar, after some significant depreciations earlier this year. However, the ever-present volatility of local steel plate prices continues by the week as levels fell by about USD 14/Ton.

If Owners / Cash Buyers are therefore looking for a safe yet marginally unprofitable / hassle free deliveries, India remains the safest location to bank on.

### Pakistan

- **Headline**: FLOOD AFFECTED
- **Key Notes**: *Under water.* | *Worst timing.*

Much of the focus has turned away from ship recycling this week, with some terrible monsoon flooding taking hundreds if not thousands of lives, subsequently leaving a lot of the country under water.

International aid has been sought to help in the hardest hit areas, with millions affected and displaced from their homes.

Making matters worse for the industry, the recent political coup that ousted PM Imran Khan, economic woes with a constantly Pakistani Rupee that is sitting at a devastating PKR 220 against the U.S. Dollar and local steel plate prices that too have fallen USD 20/Ton this week, these floods have come at the worst possible time for the beleaguered country.

*Market Sales Reported*: **NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

---

### Turkey

- **Headline**: SENTIMENTS SLAUGHTERED!

Despite registering no negative movements in import and local steel plate prices this week, and the Lira dangling precipitously at nearly TRY 18.2 against the U.S. Dollar, Aliaga Buyers are simply not interested in engaging in any negotiations at this time.

The destructive dearth of tonnage has done no favours to the local sentiment, which are still reeling from the record drop in vessel prices that have crashed by nearly 50% and that too not long ago.

Understandably, despite dwindling tonnage at local yards, the level of caution remains high, especially as the Russian-Ukrainian conflict in the Black Sea could affect the domestic economy even further.
