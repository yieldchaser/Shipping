Following recent upward moves on domestic steel plate prices at nearly every major recycling
destination, it was another week of firm pricing and a sub-continent market that is still on an
aggressive footing for the most part. 
Demand remains rampant and capacity at yards remains open for any available tonnage
(market or otherwise), leading to bidding wars on any of the marginal number of choice units
hitting the recycling markets. 
L/Cs are of continuing concern across the sub-continent destinations, and it is becoming 
increasingly important / urgent for industry players to have additional bank checks and
margins must be placed with the opening banks to ensure L/Cs are opened and released in a 
timely manner. Therefore, the right selection of Cash / End Buyers’ is an essential part of 
ensuring a smooth and successful delivery, especially at these record levels of today. 
On the Western end of things, the Turkish market seems as though levels here too are looking
to take off as import and local steel prices further firmed this week and the Lira sails through
its newfound stability in the mid TRY 13s against the U.S. Dollar. 
The supply of vessels has seen mostly tankers (often as young as 2003 - and younger at times)
being sold for recycling this year. However, we are also likely to see Capesize Bulkers (one was
sold this week) with charter rates having cooled off significantly over the previous few weeks, 
despite bouncing back towards the end of this week. 
Vessels (Dry Bulk or Tankers) with surveys due and BWTS due to be installed, are likely to
make up a majority of the supply for the remainder of the year, as owners look to take 
advantage of these fantastic prices, well over USD 600/LDT.

MARKET COMMENTARY

BANGLADESH 
HAVING TO COMPETE! 
Bangladesh has started to see some fierce competition from Pakistan this week, with 
rumours that levels on the VLCC NEW INSPIRATION (that was committed last week) 
were seeing similar levels in both markets, resulting in the deal becoming a potential 
candidate for either destination. 
With this in mind, it was no surprise to see Berge sell their latest Capesize Bulker, the
BERGE AORAKI (21,285 LDT) at a rather firm USD 640/LDT, basis a Bangladesh delivery 
and for strictly HKC green recycling to a local SoC certified yard. 
Steel prices, though weaker this week by about USD 11/Ton, still remain impressively 
perched at some very high overall levels, keeping local demand and prices elevated, as
Chattogram Recyclers hustle to try and secure some of the few vessels that are available.
Local port position certainly highlights the glaring shortage of tonnage, as only 1 fresh 
vessel reportedly arrived the Chattogram waterfront this week.

INDIA 
HKC lost. 
 
FRUSTRATIONS ABOUND! 
India has even lost a favoured HKC vessel to the one SoC certified yard in Bangladesh, 
such has been the exuberantly competitive form of neighbouring sub-continent markets
this week. 
There had been several specialist non-ferrous units concluded into India at the start of the
year, but even this variety in the supply of tonnage has iced up over the last couple of 
weeks, whilst Alang Buyers become frustrated at the painful lack of tonnage once again. 
Ironically however, despite both fundamentals registering declines this week, both 
demand and prices continue to buoy around the same region as last week.  
The India Rupee depreciated further past the mid Rs. 75 against the U.S. Dollar, whilst 
local steel prices depreciated by about USD 22/Ton through the course of the week, leaving 
End Buyers nervous about the market once again.

DECADE LONG HIGHS! 
It will be interesting to see where the 2002 built VLCC NEW INSPIRATION eventually 
ends up, with both Bangladesh and Pakistan options seemingly open for the Cash Buyer 
on the resale front, as both markets are now competing aggressively at some record levels 
not seen for over a decade. 
Of late, the industry has witnessed the Gadani market come roaring back to rival a rampant 
Bangladesh, with some of the highest levels on offer since 2008. 
Numbers on select tonnage are even starting to approach the mid USD 600s/LDT – yet the
ongoing shortage of fresh tonnage for sale is leading to some well-above market 
speculation on those units that are working, so desperate are certain Recyclers to fill their 
plots.

UPWARD?! 
The stability in prices and overall performance of the Turkish market has been nothing 
short of impressive of late.  
Prices have managed to remain relatively steady over the course of the last few months 
and despite the Lira breaching TRY 17 against the U.S. Dollar some time ago, demand and 
local offerings remain firm on the back of strong import and local steel plate prices. 
In fact, this week once again, import steel prices improved by USD 5/Ton and local steel 
plate prices jumped by about USD 10/Ton. While the Lira dances around its newfound 
stability around the mid TRY 13s against the U.S. Dollar, a firming in levels seems 
imminent. 
As such, as spring is on the horizon, the Turkish market certainly seems like it’s gearing 
up to take in its share of tonnage coming out of EU waters.
