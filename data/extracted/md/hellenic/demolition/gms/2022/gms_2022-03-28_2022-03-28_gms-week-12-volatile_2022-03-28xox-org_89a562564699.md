It has been another challenging week in the sub-continent markets, with a resurgent India and
a Bangladeshi market that is still reeling from some of the recent falls in local steel, which have
seen nearly USD 50/LDT knocked off the prices this week alone. 
India has managed to regain over half of the falls seen over the previous few weeks, but the
market overall does remain extremely volatile. 
Commodity prices and volatile currencies show few signs of cooling off any time soon, as the
Russian invasion of Ukraine enters its second month and thus far shows no signs of abating.
The Covid situation in China too may be a growing cause for concern as various cities 
(including the economic hub of Shanghai) enter a fresh series of lockdowns, while the rest of
the world is just starting to open again. 
The fact that there is very little natural immunity or a fully workable vaccine coverage (that 
hasn’t already begun to wane) mean that China could be faced with another challenging period,
both socially & economically and we can only hope that future virus mutations and inevitable
closures do not become a part of everyday life once again. 
While mills in Bangladesh had recently stopped buying steel at these higher overall levels
approaching USD 700/LDT, they have however, slowly started to acquire small portions of 
inventory just to stay open and this will surely lead to greater confidence in purchasing larger
amounts in due course, in iorder to bring Chattogram prices back up again. 
Finally, the Turkish market sails through an uneventful week, with no changes or fresh arrivals 
reported.

MARKET COMMENTARY

BANGLADESH 
BOUNCEBACK? 
The Bangladeshi slide has inadvertently brought India back into the picture of late, and 
the two markets are now competing on tonnage, with India being a preference for those 
owners with geographically positioned units, such is the high price of fuel at the moment.
Steel prices have tumbled by nearly USD 50/LDT, leaving Bangladeshi demand softening 
and a genuine lack of local interest emerging at these new highs that were approaching 
USD 700/LDT (and excess). 
Notwithstanding, despite steel mills not actively buying at these higher overall numbers, 
they are still helping keep the industry active by acquiring steel that was secured at lower 
levels. Though all of this may start to change if prices maintain / improve again and local 
Recyclers refuse to sell ship’s steel for a loss.

INDIA 
Competitive. 
 
BACK IN BIZ! 
India has come surging back into the picture this week and is now competitive with a 
Bangladeshi market that is currently enduring a brief lull due to softening steel plate prices 
there. 
On the flip side, steel prices gained further ground in Alang (despite ending the week a 
marginal USD 4/Ton lower) whilst much of the volatile currency depreciation seems to be 
settling too, with the Indian Rupee firming from Rs. 76.5X, closer to Rs. 76.10 against the 
U.S. Dollar. 
It will be interesting to see if India manages to secure any larger LDT market vessels, 
having primarily secured specialist, non-ferrous and HKC only unit this year, whilst much 
of the larger LDT Tankers and Bulkers have found their way to Pakistani or Bangladeshi 
shores.

GMS Weekly 
HALF ASLEEP! 
Pakistani Buyers appear invisible at the moment, as they are not even competitive with a 
resurgent India or worse, a weakening Bangladesh at the moment. 
Even geographically positioned units have been ignoring a softer and far more reluctant 
Pakistani market and have sailed off on even longer voyages across to Bangladesh 
(especially at the start of this year, so diverse and volatile have pricing differentials between 
sub-continent locations been of late). 
Yet, with the ongoing Russian incursion into Ukraine and the subsequent volatility in steel 
fundamentals, Gadani Recyclers remain nervous to offer at these higher prices and they 
are worried about just how far local fundamentals and subsequent sub-continent pricing 
on units eventually climb to. 
This worry has lead to the Gadani market reporting no fresh arrivals at the waterfront this 
week, a first in quite a while for this market.

SETTLED. 
After weeks of firming fundamentals, improving plate prices and a semi-weakening 
dollar, the Turkish market finally spent a week in relative modesty, with no major changes 
reported in domestic steel fundamentals (import or local plate prices) or even the currency,
that has expectedly weakened with the ongoing crisis in the Baltic, declining past the TRY 
14.80 mark against the U.S. Dollar this week. 
There is not much to report from Aliaga with minimal local activity being reported, logical, 
given the lack of available market tonnage. There were also no fresh arrivals even reported 
this week. 
Overall, a silent, yet firm period in Turkey prevails and with the Ukraine invasion in full 
swing, it is even expected to stay this way in the coming weeks.
