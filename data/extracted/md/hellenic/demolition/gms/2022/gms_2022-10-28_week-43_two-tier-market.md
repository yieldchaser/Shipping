# Market Commentary

## TWO TIER MARKET!

Activity and action remain sparse across all recycling destinations, with very few (market) sales to speak of, including minimal firm interest or serious numbers from End Buyers that may tempt Owners / Cash Buyers to engage in any meaningful negotiations.

Fundamentals, which remain the primary driving factors of the ongoing volatility, remain precariously poised across all locations, with steel prices volatile / down and currencies still struggling to find stable footing.

As a result, a sort of two tier market has emerged, one where Cash Buyers, Brokers and Owners expect the market to be at a certain level, and another where Local Recyclers seem to be intentionally positioning prices well below asking levels, and both are about USD 50/LDT apart at this time.

Only on select and favored tonnage, like Russian built tankers or smaller LDT specialized units in decent condition are welcomed with acceptable numbers from the markets, particularly from a picky Bangladesh.

Standard Handy, Panamax Bulk Carriers, and Capes are seeing prices in the mid USD 550s/LDT (if at all), as the number of End Buyers with the ability to establish workable L/Cs across the sub-continent continues to dwindle.

Diwali holidays in India have afforded the market there a break, but the persistent inertia evident in Pakistan and Bangladesh has become a prolonged malaise and is not giving Cash Buyers any confidence to offer on any of the small number of candidates available for recycling, instead preferring the far more bullish trading sector as a viable alternative.

# Bangladesh

## PICKY!

*Margin notes: Turning noses up. | Diverting.*

Bangladeshi End Buyers have started to turn their noses up at tonnage or tend to offer completely unworkable / below market levels on the absolutely minimal array of vessels currently available for recycling.

Bank restrictions have of course become exponentially tighter over recent times and this is leading to a dampened demand and a reduced number of capable & performing End Buyers.

Notwithstanding, the overall lack of appetite on standard / dry vessel vessels (mostly Bulkers) has become quite alarming. Many vessels have been put back into trading lanes or diverted to other markets, so out of sorts is Bangladesh at present (and has been for much of this year).

Fundamentals also remain quirky as local steel plate prices fell about USD 15/Ton while the Bangladeshi Taka firmed back from BDT 104.X to BDT 100 against the U.S. Dollar – a nearly 4% readjustment after having fallen nearly the same amount just a couple of weeks ago.

Certainly not the ideal scenario for a destination that has remained the market leader for some time now.

# India

## DIWALI DELIGHT!

*Margin notes: Rare bright spot. | Mute week.*

Diwali holidays, the traditional festival of lights, have proved to be a rare spot of celebration for a beleaguered and reluctant Indian market this week.

Very few sales have taken place to Alang Buyers over the last two quarters (as evidenced by the record low arrivals at Alang’s waterfront), as port reports lay virtually (and painfully) empty.

While local steel plate prices posted a marginal USD 3/Ton improvement this week, it is the Indian Rupee that registered some noteworthy gains, briefly firming into Rs. 81.9 before settling back towards a still firmer Rs. 82.30 against the U.S. Dollar - as another mute week passes in India.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# Pakistan

## FALTERING!

*Margin notes: Glum. | Long road to recovery.*

As Pakistani fundamentals continue to falter, the outlook in Pakistan remains similarly bleak to other sub-continent locations, while Gadani Buyers continue to talk down levels.

There have been one or two candidates purportedly discussed for a potential sale. However, not having seen anywhere near satisfactory offers on the units, both vessels have been reportedly withdrawn and returned to the trading lanes.

On the fundamentals front, local steel plate prices declined nearly USD 25/Ton, while the Pakistani Rupee gradually weakened past PKR 222 against the U.S. Dollar this week.

As such, there certainly seem increasing signs of a silent end to 2022 and probably early ’03.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# Turkey

## MORE OF THE SAME!

*Margin notes: Fundamentals unchanged. | Quite the quiet.*

The Turkish market remains suspended in animation, with an ironic degree of stability via unchanged fundamentals, prices, and local sentiments.

Local steel plate prices have remained depressed, yet unchanged for several weeks now. The Lira continues to bob around the TRY 18.5X mark whilst market supply remains abysmal, and local Buyers remain utterly disinterested in trying to present offers that they know will not be entertained as Aliaga remain less than half of what sub-continent markets currently have on offer.

As such, this remains quite the quiet spell that Turkey is currently going through.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**
