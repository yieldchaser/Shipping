Any vessels that were made available over the past few weeks have swiftly been withdrawn as 
recycling markets show an ineptitude to offer any sort of serious numbers, having seemingly 
forgotten how to buy ships after a prolonged period on the sidelines. 
Consequently, there have been no market sales for another week, especially as freight markets 
continue to impress across all sectors and recycling markets remain unchanged, depressed, and 
starved of tonnage all at once. 
Constant currency depreciations have been the chief culprit and there seems to be a lack of 
confidence to import vessels, with banks being much stricter on sanctioning precious U.S. 
Dollar Letters for Letter of Credit (L/Cs) for vessels for recycling. 
Most yards in India, Pakistan Bangladesh, and even Turkey, are therefore lying idle and will 
be expected to remain that way (likely) for the rest of the year, with sentiments and levels so
depressed across the board. 
Demand for new vessels is firming, but at completely unworkable numbers for Owners and 
Cash Buyers alike, especially given the decent earnings that the majority of these ships continue 
to make. 
As such, for another week, the outlook across all sub-continent markets stays glum and gloomy, 
with little meaningful activity to talk of and deteriorating sentiments & pricing afflicting the 
industry.

MARKET COMMENTARY

BANGLADESH 
NOT SERIOUS! 
Despite being presented with a few potential candidates, Bangladesh Recyclers feel as if 
they have the prerogative to pick-and-choose all from an absolutely shoddy number of 
vessels on offer! 
With a lack of any serious offers and what appears to be a nervy / tentative demand, Cash 
Buyers are therefore diverting their vessels away from Chattogram or put their respective 
units back into trading lanes. 
Fundamentals remain shaky as the BDT gradually declines by the week, losing another 
5% as it approaches BDT 105 against the U.S. Dollar.  
Although steel plate prices did jump USD 20/Ton, the level they have fallen to over recent 
weeks has kept the pressure on local frustrations and the willingness of Chattogram
Recyclers to engage in discussions.  
As it therefore stands, we expect a very quiet end to the year for Bangladesh, with 
minimal sales and / or negotiations, thanks to the currently enforced L/C limits and 
opportunistic / non-serious offers being tossed around.

INDIA 
Drying out. 
 
SPOOKED! 
India remains stranded as the lowest sub-continent destination for another week, but there 
are few firm candidates available to work on in order to test that theory, as yards across 
the sub-continent (especially Indian and Pakistan) continue to dry out. 
Even offshore, specialist, and HKC vessels have been lacking of late and with such a dearth 
in supply, perhaps End Buyers will have to consider temporarily shuttering their plots,
rather than leaving them operative and paying local labour, in the absence of any tonnage 
to work on. 
The Indian Rupee continues to fluctuate, as it jumps between Rs. 82.60 to Rs. 82.26 against 
the U.S. Dollar, whilst local steel plate prices have also endured further falls this week,
leaving End Buyers spooked and on edge.

GMS Weekly 
ENCOURAGEMENT! 
With a currency that has started to give some encouragement as it appreciates by the week 
(not surprising, given how far it has fallen), Pakistan is perhaps the market that has seen 
the most improvement and re-assurance of late. 
It will hardly be a feat to challenge an altogether dormant Bangladesh, but any larger LDT 
tonnage now available seems like it could be heading for Gadani shores.  
Notwithstanding, the shortage of tonnage only seems to exacerbate an already dwindled 
situation. Evidence of which presents itself with the fact that there no new arrivals in 
Gadani this week. 
Overall, after a catastrophic year that has seen currency collapse, flooding and even a 
political coup, there seems to be some a positive news for Pakistani Recyclers as we enter 
the final chapter of the year for the ship recycling industry and Pakistan as a whole.

QUIET! 
The Turkish market went through a silent and unchanged state since last week.  
The only movement critical to the domestic ship recycling sector that is recording any 
movements (and not in the right direction) is the Turkish Lira, which finds itself hovering 
just north of TRY 18.50 against the U.S. Dollar. 
No market tonnage, weak fundamentals, pricing that has fallen USD 250/MT and
sentiments in the doldrums, has left Turkey in a prolonged quietened state.
