# Market Commentary

## NO IMPETUS!

Recycling markets are ambling towards the end of the year without any real impetus, ability, aggression, or even an apparent willingness to engage in discussions to buy, so volatile has the situation been over the last 2 quarters.

Prices have of course come off by about USD 200/LDT in the sub-continent markets since the stunning peaks witnessed over USD 700/LDT just earlier this year – for reference, on a standard Capesize Bulker, that is over USD 4 million lost on the residual value alone!

Bangladesh still remains largely absent on the buying front with a majority of Buyers unable to get central bank financing to open fresh Letters of Credit. There is also talk of the shortage of U.S. Dollars in the country easing once the they can agree to an IMF loan, but we are unlikely to see any clarity on this until early next year (at best). As such, Chattogram seems like it would be unavailable for the remainder of this year.

After the collapse of the currency and the government, Pakistan has yet to fully find their feet again and most End Buyers are extremely tentative and reticent in their offerings, leaving the market mostly a nonevent.

This really leaves only India as a viable sub-continent destination, which itself has endured turbulent times as the currency has once again depreciated into the high Rs. 81 against the U.S. Dollar, while local steel prices gained ground after last week’s volatile plummet, especially after the recent reduction in export duties on steel products.

Turkey on the far end remains lost, with levels of its own having plummeted about USD 250/MT and fundamentals still hampering any sort of recovery, amidst a pinching shortage of any tonnage.

It is therefore likely to be a bleak and muted end to the year, with some desperately needed stability on currencies, finance limits and steel plate prices, in order to bring nervous end buyers back to the table.

# Bangladesh

## GRAVE CONCERNS!

*Margin notes: Lacking dollars. | Waving goodbye.*

The lack of financing in Bangladesh remains of grave concern, with most End Buyers unable to receive the relevant L/C approvals from the government, in order to buy fresh units.

An injection of U.S. Dollars into the country (as a result of an impending IMF loan to the country) may ease the ongoing liquidity crisis, but nobody in the industry is expecting a quick fix if this happens.

On the local fundamentals front, the Bangladeshi Taka continues to jostle around the BDT 100.4 mark, while local steel plate prices recorded no movements this week.

Chattogram Buyers have therefore spent another week watching the slim pickings of vessels head to competing markets, as clearly evident by local port position this week, with only one small LDT tanker having reportedly arrived the waterfront this week.

# India

## LOOKING UP!

*Margin notes: Recovering. | Completely empty.*

After last week’s plummet in steel plate prices, India has had a more positive week this week, as steel prices have come rebounding back to recover in excess of USD 26/Ton.

At first, it was curious to see steel plate prices improve after the announcement two weeks ago that export duties on iron and steel products would be reduced, only to see most of those gains lost over the subsequent week.

Even the Rupee continues to jostle in the Rs. 81.X against the U.S. Dollar, leaving the local market with a seeming sense of (newfound) stability – despite the record lows seen on the currency.

As such, local port position has been the most drastically empty it has been in a while, with no new vessels arriving this week and the last of the tonnage from earlier weeks, finally being delivered to Alang Buyers.

Should no fresh tonnage arrive next week, it would likely one of the few times that India’s port position would be completely empty.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# Pakistan

## YET TO FIND FEET!

*Margin notes: Sentiments shattered. | Focus away.*

Pakistani Buyers have yet to really find their feet, with sentiments shattered after the excessive losses suffered on ship prices so far this year.

Of course, the political and financial situation in the country remains similarly bleak, so it is perhaps of little surprise to see Gadani Buyers extremely reluctant to make anywhere near sensible or realistic offers for the few vessels being proposed for recycling.

Local steel plate prices have declined about USD 15/Ton this week, as the currency continues to languish in the mid PKR 223 against the U.S. Dollar.

As such, the focus has therefore fallen mostly on India, with Bangladesh also out of the picture due to the dire lack of financing available in the country, and all will be hoping for some greater stability and demand across the board in the sub-continent to fill empty plots again in the new year.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# Turkey

## MEDLEY OF NOTHING!

*Margin notes: Small LDT. | Moving further.*

The Turkish market continues its unremitting trudge through its ongoing recycling saga, as Aliaga yards remain deprived of meaningful (market) tonnage. While in passing, news and rumours of local beachings of off market small LDT units would surface, even those have virtually vanished over the recent months.

On the local fundamentals front, import steel improved marginally again by USD 10/MT this week, while the Lira seems to have entered a state of stasis as it jiggles around the mid TRY 18.6X against the U.S. Dollar.

With trading markets still performing well, it remains to be seen how long this lack of tonnage can keep this market moving further.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**
