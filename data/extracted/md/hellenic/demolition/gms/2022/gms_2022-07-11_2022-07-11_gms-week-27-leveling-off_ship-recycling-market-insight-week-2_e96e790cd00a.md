As we enter the traditionally quieter monsoon season, it is of little surprise to see recycling 
markets remaining inert and quiet, with rains / flooding hampering production at yards in 
Chattogram and Alang labourers returning to their hometowns as recycling activities come to 
a seasonal crawl. This may have inadvertently triggered the recent leveling of sub-continent 
steel plate prices as steel output diminishes and plate prices stabilize / firm in reaction. 
Although vessel prices have cooled off by USD 100/LDT in the sub-continent markets and about 
USD 250/MT in Turkey, global recycling sentiments remain in the doldrums given the rate of 
the recent declines. As such, there is no surprise to see minimal activity emanating from all 
markets at present, including the respective waterfronts that are displaying the shoddy state of 
current affairs. 
Notwithstanding, global currency depreciations remain the primary source of heartburn for the 
ship-recycling communities, as the worrying declines on steel prices seem to have 
comparatively stabilized and we hope it should start to show some signs of positivity in the 
coming week(s). 
Whilst there still remains a degree of caution and a prevailing nervousness to buy in local 
markets, there are unlikely to be firm / serious offers for Owners and Cash Buyers alike, and 
this is part of the reason why even the marginal few candidates have started to dry up of late. 
Of course, all freight sectors continue to be positively poised as most Owners are now passing 
drydock on their aging beauties, rather than scrapping their older tonnage even when recycling 
rates are at historically firm numbers.

MARKET COMMENTARY

BANGLADESH 
TORRENTIAL! 
Bangladesh has endured torrential rains these past few weeks, leaving parts of the 
country flooded and (especially) the Chattogram area, well submerged for any recycling 
activity to proceed at local yards. 
Monsoon season is truly upon us and the minimal cutting has resulted in the slow 
movement of product. As supply throttles back, demand for plate starts to build and 
plate prices seemed to have stabilized this week. 
While at most times, the monsoons can be a painful time for negotiations due to lack of 
demand, during the current time where vessel prices have plummeted USD 100/Ton, the 
weather may have inadvertently become a saving grace. 
In fact, local steel plate prices evened out and remained stable for the week in Bangladesh.
The currency, however, remains an entirely different beast as it trades at record lows 
against the U.S. Dollar, keeping local sentiments wary of the lingering volatility. 
While meaningful tonnage remains at large, the shortage of tonnage wasn’t particularly 
missed this week as the country geared up for Eid celebrations. As such, it maybe some 
time before we see some serious activity from this market.

INDIA 
More muted. 
 
BARELY STABLE! 
After spiralling steel plate prices and a depreciating currency have seen Indian prices sink 
back down to some of the lower levels seen since last year, local steel plate prices seemed 
to find some stable footing last week and even made a strong (nearly USD 25/Ton) 
improvement this week. 
Similar to Bangladesh however, it seems that the onset of monsoon mixed with firming 
freight rates is keeping tonnage away from Alang shores and has eventually kept Indian 
Recyclers from moving product from local yards, resulting in some stability slipping back 
into domestic steel markets. 
The Indian Rupee on the other hand, has been taking a beating against the U.S. Dollar as 
it gradually depreciates towards the psychological Rs. 80 barrier, as it currently trades at 
region Rs. 79.30. Reportedly, the government has taken some steps to discourage the 
descend. Time will tell just how fruitful their efforts would be. 
For now, however, Alang remains starved for tonnage and monsoon quiet on the 
waterfront as yard Owners conserve on operational costs whilst workers return to their 
home towns for the season.

GMS Weekly 
UNLIKELY! 
Pakistan, unsurprisingly, remains out of the market for another week, as Owners and Cash 
Buyers are still reticent to deliver to a market that has been far from reliable on deliveries 
this year. 
Moreover, this week, unlike its sub-continent competitors, both local steel plate prices and 
the Pakistani Rupee took tumbles of their own. As the Rupee continues to tread at record 
lows at PKR 207.50 against the U.S. Dollar, plate dropped by about USD 10/Ton through 
the week. 
Thankfully, the lack of tonnage and Eid celebrations should keep local Buyers distracted 
into early next week (even beyond). 
Prices are also the lowest across the sub-continent, and coupled with its poor performance,
it is highly unlikely we will see any meaningful candidates head to Pakistan for the much
of this year.

LEVELING OFF… 
A second consecutive week of firming plate prices (both local and import steel to the tune 
of USD 20/Ton and USD 30/Ton respectively) has certainly dead-ended the seemingly 
unending decline in vessel prices from a couple of weeks ago – a massive and nearly USD
250/MT abyssal plummet. 
The currency, however, is an entirely different nightmare as the Lira now approaches TRY 
18 against the U.S. Dollar, blowing past TRY 17.30 this week. Does it affect matters moving 
forward?  
Certainly!  
In the short term where Owners greeted with these new pricing realities in the mid USD 
200s/Ton will certainly take some time to digest this colossal plummet.  
In the long run of course, it becomes a matter of sustainability on two fronts i.e. on the one 
hand, how long can Owners face to lose more than double on their aging units (when 
compared to sub-continent levels) on any given day. On the other hand, how long can local 
Recyclers sustain keeping their plots open as tonnage scarcity drives business into 
“airplane mode”. 
One thing is for certain, just like the rest of the industry, a quiet time on the side-lines for 
this market is certainly on the ropes.
