“He that can have patience can have what he will.”
 
 
 
– Ben Franklin 
The international ship recycling malaise that has gripped the markets over these recent / 
summer months continues for another week, with minimal-to-no activity and barely any firm 
interest from Recyclers, whilst fundamentals remain ever so fragile. 
 
Steel plate prices have once again started to display their share of volatility in India, in 
addition to the currency that showed signs of firming towards Rs. 79.20 against the U.S. 
Dollar, whilst weakening back towards Rs. 80 mark before the week ended. 
 
The Pakistani and Bangladeshi economies continue to teeter precariously on the edge with 
currencies and plate prices continuing to display some grasping signs of stability (against the 
U.S. Dollar) at their weakest levels ever. Although there seems no danger of a repeat of the Sri 
Lanka collapse that most pessimistic Recyclers in the sub-continent have been fearing. 
 
Finally, the Turkish market continues to plummet and languish in the doldrums, with falling 
plate prices and vessel prices that are on their respective precipices of another drop away 
from oblivion. 
 
Meanwhile, dry bulk charter rates in the larger segments have started to cool off of late, but 
many are expecting (and hoping) for a rebound come fourth quarter of the year. As such, 
there is not too much of an expectation on an increase in the number of recycling candidates 
just yet. Even in the most beleaguered sector of the shipping industry – VLCCs – has seen 
charter earnings and rates / interest rise once again, and this is further depriving ship-
recycling markets of a supply of tonnage. 
 
Overall, in the industry, L/Cs over USD 3 million (rumored to be further reduced to USD 2 
Million) are struggling to get approvals from the Central State Bank in Bangladesh, so this is 
ruling out the import of most larger LDT tonnage into the country. It will take some time for 
the economic crisis and foreign currency woes to settle down there, before activity can return 
to some form of normality.

MARKET COMMENTARY

BANGLADESH 
BLEAK! 
Rumour has it that the Bangladeshi government has been seriously considering 
reducing L/C opening limits from USD 3 million down to USD 2 million, in another
catastrophic blow to Chattogram Buyers, who are becoming increasingly desperate to 
fill their emptying plots. 
It has been an extremely inactive couple of quarters in Bangladesh, with minimal sales /
sliding rates and until we see a greater supply of tonnage, it remains difficult to peg
exactly where levels are at the moment. 
There is also a question as to whether any large LDT tonnage will be able to beach at all,
given the recently tightened L/C restrictions (with further limits likely forthcoming), as
the government has deemed that the ship recycling sector is not an essential industry
for the dwindling U.S. Dollar reserves in the country to spend on, leaving a bleak
economic forecast for the Bangladeshi market.

INDIA 
Activity increase. 
 
CLAWING BACK! 
India has enjoyed several weeks of comparatively firmer activity & offers and local 
Buyers have even managed to secure one or two rare sales - a stainless steel tanker last 
week for a very firm price while a few more, small LDT candidates have also been 
discussed this week. 
Unfortunately, local port position has little to support the ongoing sentiment with no 
new arrivals this week. 
Moreover, after very real fears that the currency would cross Rs. 80 against the U.S.
Dollar, the Indian Rupee had mercifully settled back towards the Rs. 79.2X mark, whilst 
regretfully plummeting back down towards the Rs. 80 mark by the time the week ended. 
Steel plate prices too have continued their volatile dance (just like the Indian Rupee), 
where steel levels firmed up during the early part of the week but fell back down as the 
week ended, only to have firmed USD 1/Ton this week. 
This appears to have helped local sentiments overall, wherein the weakened state of 
fundamentals has become more of a “daily affair” and local Buyers are getting more 
eager to come forth and offer, even at these lower levels.

GMS Weekly 
NEED CONVINCING! 
With an absolute dearth of vessels on plots and arriving for beaching in the Port Report, 
Pakistani buyers have been absolutely starved of tonnage over these last two quarters of 
the year. 
End buyers can only have themselves to blame for this with some woeful performances
on those vessels that were sold earlier in the year – with blatant renegotiations and even 
refusals to perform, whilst prices started to collapse due to a constantly depreciating 
currency earlier this year. 
It is therefore expected take some convincing for Ship Owners and Cash Buyers alike, to
make their way to Gadani shores, even as demand and rates start to steady up again
locally.

NO END IN SIGHT! 
There seems no end in sight to the state of the plummeting Turkish market. While local 
steel plate prices have reportedly declined by about USD 10/MT, import steel has 
plummeted a whopping USD 25/Ton, keeping local sentiments in the doldrums. 
Even the Turkish blew past the TRY 18 mark, ending the week just north of TRY 18.10. 
As a result, even indications have been few and far between on units that remain 
excruciatingly invisible from the markets. 
Overall, it seems even Q4 will be a quiet one for Aliaga Buyers.
