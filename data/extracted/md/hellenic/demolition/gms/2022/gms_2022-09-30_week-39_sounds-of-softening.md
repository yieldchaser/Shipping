# Market Commentary

## SOUNDS OF SOFTENING!

Sentiments have softened across sub-continent markets once again this week, as Buyers appear increasingly reluctant to commit on fresh tonnage whilst fundamentals remain so shaky – especially as the markets have witnessed across the last couple of weeks, where currencies and steel plate prices took turns in tumbling.

The Pakistani Rupee has breached historical lows and despite the odd flash of appreciation, has given Gadani Buyers extreme discomfort over the course of a torturous year of monumental declines.

Bangladesh, likewise, has suffered a currency calamity of its own, coupled with declining steel plate prices and increased limits on establishing fresh L/Cs due to a dire shortage of U.S. Dollars in the country, most Chattogram Buyers are holding back or simply unable / unwilling to offer at all on any of the freshly proposed units.

India has lost about USD 80/LDT on steel prices over the last several weeks and despite gaining a marginal USD 5/Ton this week, remains under pressure as the lowest placed of all sub-continent markets once again. The Indian Rupee has also crossed the psychologically worrying Rs. 80 mark, leaving Alang Buyers as worriedly cautious as their competitors.

Finally, the Turkish market remains depressed and unchanged for yet another week, as there are no units available to this market and at these current levels, most Owners / Cash Buyers wouldn’t commit their units to Aliaga Buyers anyways.

The supply of tonnage in the sub-continent had started to marginally improve but remains relatively strained as all freight sectors continue to perform, leaving demo markets starved of vessels once again. Prices therefore remain stranded below USD 600/LDT for another week with little chance of crossing that psychological barrier any time soon. The tendency indeed seems to suggest that levels will cool off again (perhaps down to the low USD 550s/LDT) in worrying signs for the recycling markets once again.

# Bangladesh

## BACK DOWN!

*Margin notes: Retreat. | Tough LCs.*

Despite what seemed to be a brief revival for the country’s domestic ship-recycling sector, Bangladeshi Recyclers have retreated their offers once again, forcing Cash Buyers with any tonnage to sell to Chattogram, to now look to trade, hold / lay-up, or even sell to competing locations.

Many of the offers emanating on the paucity of available vessels are now surfacing below USD 600/LDT as Chattogram Buyers sense a bargain or two. Unfortunately, the number of workable units at these reduced rates remain few and far between.

The ongoing restrictions on large U.S.$ value L/Cs remain tight across the country. As such, End Buyers need to have their credit facilities and banks in line, in order to adhere to the necessary government approvals on Site L/Cs.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# India

## TORRID TIME!

*Margin notes: Steel falls. | Ports emptying.*

Despite firming a marginal USD 5/Ton this week, the Indian ship recycling sector overall has endured a torrid time of late, with noteworthy declines in steel plate prices of about USD 80/Ton over recent weeks, and this has left Alang Buyers spooked & reluctant to offer anew on any units being proposed.

The currency too has suffered as the Indian Rupee nearly breached Rs. 81.90 (rapidly coming up on Rs. 82) against the U.S. Dollar, retreating back to the mid Rs. 81s, in further worrying performances of a key fundamental for Alang Buyers.

With a diminished supply of tonnage and price levels trailing its competing neighbours, India remains mostly out of the market. Moreover, with all port reports emptying up, it could be a while before local Recyclers are able to snag a few units, fill up their plots again, and business is ‘back to normal’.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# Pakistan

## BELEAGUERED GADANI!

*Margin notes: Markets suffer. | No sales to report.*

After continued and devastating currency depreciations, in addition to a political coup that rattled domestic fundamentals and resulted in ship prices collapsing by over USD 100/Ton, the recent catastrophic & destructive flooding was just another source of tragedy for a nation that has suffered terribly this year.

The Pakistani Rupee has firmed marginally from PKR 236 to about PKR 228, while local steel plate prices remained flat through the course of the week.

As a result, very few sales have taken place recently and domestic recycling plots in Gadani are eerily quiet after several quarters of almost complete inertia. In fact, after a couple of months of no local deliveries, Gadani’s waterfront was finally greeted by a small LDT tug this week.

Despite India falling to levels lower than Pakistan and Bangladesh also starting to cool off this week, there appears to be no sign of Pakistan securing any vessels any time soon, even though demand is starting to ramp up in the sub-continent once again.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# Turkey

## AIRPLANE MODE!

*Margin notes: Unwilling to bet. | Q4 closure?*

The Turkish market seems to have entered into Airplane mode, with no change in the plummeted import and local steel plate prices, a Lira that has further collapsed towards the mid TRY 18s against the U.S. Dollar, an utter lack of tonnage, and a local sentiment that is simply unwilling to place their bets on any tonnage being proposed, especially until fundamentals display even a glimmer of stability.

As such, lack of sales and lack of any local activity is sure to see yards temporarily close, as we head deeper into Q4.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**
