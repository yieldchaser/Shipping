## 1. Market Overview

### Highlights
- Into the ether.
- Deteriorating.
- Hard to gauge.
- Frustrating.
- Lower LDT.

### Editorial: Sub-continent markets appear to have disappeared back into the ether for another week, as  trading markets rebound and candidates for recycling start to dissipate.

In recent weeks, volatile steel plate prices, a constantly deteriorating currency and starved U.S. Dollar credit lines across India, Pakistan and Bangladesh have all led to a near total halt on buying at anywhere near respectable levels.

As such, it is hard to gauge where prices really stand today, with so few Buyers either having the capabilities to open an L/C & perform on any sizeable vessel, and furthermore, the lack of any sort of confidence to offer and maintain any firm levels.

Even in Turkey, the situation remains unrelentingly gloomy, with little to no movement in any positive direction, all while local sentiments remain depressed on the back of a Lira that has been scraping to record-lows by the week and plate prices that remain in the dumps.

Overall, it has been a frustrating period of time for Cash Buyers with any tonnage to sell and it is becoming increasingly fraught to get vessels delivered into a beleaguered recycling market.

The rebound on VLCCs has also just come at the right time, and to see Suezmax and Aframax tankers flying and even a rebound on Cape rates of late has seen most larger LDT vessels bypass the beaches once again as they have yet another chance at squeezing out a few more voyages.

There are also very few small(er) LDT vessels on the buffet and as such, it increasingly looks as though it will be as quiet an end to the year as it has been for the last two quarters. Certainly, it is time again for the recycling markets to get their affairs in order, ahead of an anticipated higher influx of vessels next year.

## 3. Country Market Intelligence

### Bangladesh

- **Headline**: EMPTY HANDED!
- **Key Notes**: *Eerie silence.* | *Losing out.*

An eerie silence has descended across the Bangladeshi market this week, with no reasonable or even firm offers to speak of, on any of the available units on offer as end Buyers prefer to wait-and-watch the market movements to see if there are further falls ahead.

Of late, targeting prices anywhere near USD 600/Ton seems to be a challenge unto itself and it may be a case of the local market seeing how truly starved of tonnage they are, before any sort of demand / hunger resumes – and hopefully at some more aggressive levels.

Unfortunately, fundamentals remain shaky and provide even less impetus for local Recyclers to be aggressive, especially as the Bangladeshi Taka weakened towards BDT 103 against the U.S. Dollar.

Moreover, Chattogram port report was a surprise this week, with a couple of large LDT vessels showing up, including a VLCC. Similar to a couple of stranded large LDT units at the waterfront, it remains to be seen just how these deliveries will be concluded.

Overall, the Chattogram market is in danger of losing out to a re-emerging Gadani altogether, and it is certainly isn’t too long before the most trustworthy and performing Indian market makes a return to form, leaving Bangladesh empty-handed once again.

### India

- **Headline**: BARREN & IDLE!

The Indian market appears to be degrading even further as both a plummeting currency and steel plate prices declined during the week.

The INR has become the greatest source of concern for Alang Buyers as the currency breached Rs. 82.50 against the U.S. Dollar, in what will clearly be the most stinging reason for Alang Buyers to be ultra-conservative on their bids in the near future.

Even local steel plate prices have been dithering and declined nearly USD 10/Ton during this time.

As such, India remains at the bottom of the market rankings and is presently lacking any sort of confidence to offer anew on the small supply of vessels currently available, which is clearly evident by the virtually non-existent number of fresh arrivals at Alang’s waterfront.

As we move towards the end of the year, it certainly seems that there is little hope for Alang to improve, unless the markets find their footing and the volume of tonnage increases.

### Pakistan

- **Headline**: GLIMMER OF HOPE!
- **Key Notes**: *Currency appreciates.* | *Advantage.*

There has been a glimmer of hope in Pakistan this past week, as the currency appreciated by over 2% (towards the PKR 221 mark against the U.S. Dollar), finally giving End Buyers a little bit of encouragement moving forward – especially after what has been a dramatically silent Q3 for this market.

The local sale of a Suezmax tanker at levels close to USD 600/LT has further encouraged End Buyers to get back into the swing at reasonable numbers, and it does seem that there is a growing demand emerging from this market.

Pakistan also holds an advantage over Bangladesh in less stringent L/C requirements from the government & even L/C opening banks (despite the dire depreciations on the currency) and as such, it could be that we see a greater number of large LDT vessels heading to Gadani shores before the year is out.

*Market Sales Reported*: **NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

---

### Turkey

- **Headline**: SUSPENDED!

The situation in Turkey seems to be mirroring India, with both key fundamentals down in the dumps. The Lira remains of grave concern to local Buyers as it straddles a record- breaking TRY 18.60 against the U.S. Dollar.

Local and import steel prices, though marginally firmer this week, still remain so low that Aliaga Buyers feel suffocated in offering anywhere near firm levels.

The severity of the lack of tonnage isn’t helping this market any and as such, we can only hope for a firmer 2023.

*Market Sales Reported*: **NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**
