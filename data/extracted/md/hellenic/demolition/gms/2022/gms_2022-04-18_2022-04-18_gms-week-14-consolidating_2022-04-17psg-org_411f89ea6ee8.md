With holidays descending across the Indian sub-continent for Ramadan / Eid celebrations and
the rest of the world prepares for Easter weekend, it has been a comparably quieter week in
terms of sales and activity. 
Levels remain firm in Pakistan and India whilst Bangladesh remains somewhat muted, with
domestic steel mills still refusing to engage local yards in negotiations at these higher overall 
levels, as Bangladeshi steel plate prices start to reflect some of the steel volatility from India. 
Notwithstanding, it is expected that the Bangladeshi market will likely come back into the fray
once again, especially after a month or so on the sidelines as steel prices (though volatile)
remain strongly placed and this has perhaps been a period of consolidation over the 
traditionally softer month of Ramadan. 
Away from the sub-continent waters, the Turkish markets remain muted, with declines in local
and import steel, resulting in a quieter and (like the sub-continent markets) a silent week. 
Overall, the supply of tonnage also remains rather sparse, with fewer wet (large LDT) units to
speak of recently, given that charter rates have picked up significantly on select routes. Dry and
Container sectors have also been performing rather well and this has led to an overall decline
in firm and workable candidates for most of the year thus far – despite some repeat record
numbers at decade-long highs above USD 700/LDT. 
As such, the recycling markets remain on a firm footing overall and as the second quarter of 
the year gets well underway, there certainly seems as though there is still more positivity and
optimism moving forward, primarily on account of continually strong showings on commodity
prices and a decent demand / capacity to acquire across sub-continent locations. The Ukraine
crisis certainly has its place in adding to this recent volatility in fundamentals and it’s certainly
only a matter of time before this party retreats to more realistic levels.

MARKET COMMENTARY

BANGLADESH 
MARGINALISED! 
Ramadan holidays have seen Bangladeshi Buyers marginalised for another week, as both 
India and Pakistan push on to secure most of the working inventory (market or private 
units in Cash Buyer hands). 
Local steel plate prices, though volatile, remain firmly poised overall. Yet, domestic steel
mills are resisting to buy at these higher levels, which is seeing product fail to shift from 
local recycling yards and subsequently creating a backlog of inventory & slowdown in 
cutting. 
The general feeling is that steel mills may be eventually compelled to acquire at these 
present rates post-Ramadan, even though high as though they may be, a thorough lack 
of viable options leaves much to be desired.

INDIA 
Strong showing. 
 
ENCOURAGED! 
The Indian market put on another strong showing this week – perhaps even encouraged 
by a slower working period from competing sub-continent markets on account of
Ramadan holidays and the recent political upheaval in Pakistan. 
Sales have taken place into Alang from existing Cash Buyer inventories, as steel plate
prices, though softer mid-week, did end the week higher (about USD 5/Ton). Finally, the 
currency has regrettably continued to degrade, falling deeper into Rs. 76s against the U.S.
Dollar, as the Ukrainian conflict shows little signs of abating – given Russia’s recently 
renewed aggression on the East. 
Alang Recyclers are now competing on standard vessels once again, even large LDT 
Aframax and Suezmax tankers, not just specialist non-ferrous units, reefers, offshore 
tonnage, and HKC sales only.

GMS Weekly 
CAPTURING HEADLINES! 
Pakistan has been on fire of late and this week, a couple of sales captured the imagination 
of those Owners seeking levels on their units starting with a “7”, as the Capesize bulker 
SUNBEAM (21,018 LDT) was committed at an impressive USD 715/LT LDT, whilst the 
Aframax tanker ION (16,640 LDT) also fetched an equally firm USD 708/LT LDT. 
Both of these sales above USD 700/LDT by optimistic Cash Buyers have yet to find End 
Buyers at the required levels in Gadani. However, with local steel plate prices firmly 
planted at record highs and a firm local demand still persevering in Gadani, the Pakistani 
market has managed to find itself at pole position in the market rankings.  
The recent political upheaval in the country has certainly consumed most of the domestic 
headlines in Pakistan, with Prime Minister Imran Khan being ousted after a vote of no 
confidence, and his successor yet to be formally installed. 
The currency too has been a recently developing source of concern for the domestic 
recycling community as the Pakistani Rupee (which has been historically stable against the 
U.S. Dollar) suddenly spiked for a brief period. This caused increasing concern amongst 
local Recyclers (who are already skittish about the Ukrainian crisis) about where their
fundamentals might eventually lead them.

SHUFLLE TRUFFLE. 
While the industry has been muscle reacting to the ongoing volatility of the Turkish Lira 
against the U.S. Dollar, steel fundamentals (both import and local) have managed to 
display impressive gains and even maintain them for the most part. 
This week, however, import steel declined by about USD 10/Ton whilst local steel plate 
prices plummeted by an eye-popping USD 25/Ton – all while the Lira continues to float 
around the TRY 14.6X mark as the week ended. 
Demand remains firm, local offerings remain at record-highs, and as the weather continues 
to warm, demand remains firm as local Recyclers remain on the lookout for tonnage.
