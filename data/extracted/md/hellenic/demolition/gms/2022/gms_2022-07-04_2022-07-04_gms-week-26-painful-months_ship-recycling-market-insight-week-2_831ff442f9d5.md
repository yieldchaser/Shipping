The degraded performance across the ship recycling sector over the last few (and painful) 
months shows few signs of abating just yet, as it has been a veritable tale of misery, even though 
prices remain at some of the firmest levels we have seen over the last decade. Down from the 
peaks of $700/Ton, levels nearly USD 100/LDT lower have left a bad taste in most mouths in 
the industry. 
Certainly, the last time prices crashed from the psychological USD 700/LDT barrier was back 
in 2008, when even USD 800/LDT was briefly breached during the unprecedented boom years 
of the shipping industry, prior to the global financial collapse in September of that year. 
However, not for a while have we seen freight rates at such optimistic numbers, as all sectors 
(Dry Bulk, Tankers and Containers) continue to fly, giving a healthy collection of these aging 
ladies, an extended chance at life in their respective global trading fleets. 
As such, it remains a bit of a conundrum for the ship recycling sector, while candidates remain 
sparse and their incoming volume unlikely to pick up any time soon, even with prices having
declined by over USD 100/LDT and having left sentiments on the ground in pieces, levels of 
over USD 600/Ton seems insufficient to tempt vessels towards the shores of recycling. 
Steel prices have actually recovered a little in Pakistan, as they stabilize in India to an extent, 
ending the week at levels similar to those at the beginning of the week. However, it is the 
currency that continues to afflict Recyclers globally and this is leading to an overall lack of 
confidence in offering afresh on tonnage. Perhaps a period on the sidelines could reinvigorate 
sub-continent markets and bring demand back, of course, once steel prices and currencies settle 
at an acceptable level. 
On the far end, Turkey manages to finally wrest the unending slide of nearly 6+ weeks that has 
seen levels plummet about USD 250/MT, as both import and local steel plate prices record 
improvements of their own.

MARKET COMMENTARY

BANGLADESH 
POISED FOR PICK UP? 
News of several VLCC sales in the low USD 600s/LDT has been all the chatter over the 
last few weeks in Bangladesh. However, there is still some mystery over whether these
deals will eventually go through and if their final resale prices will be those as rumoured
to be fixed or will potential renegotiations take place at the waterfront. 
Overall, the last 2 – 3 months have been a far quieter affair across Bangladesh, as prices
cooled off and demand subsided after an extremely busy first quarter, when Local Buyers 
were busy acquiring a large chunk of the large LDT tonnage available in the market, and 
that too at the firmest prices in the sub-continent. 
Even now, Chattogram Buyers have managed to retake pole position and general vessel 
enquiries seem to be making the rounds albeit the lack of market sales is painfully
obvious.  
Overall however, what the foreseeable future looks like depends on when and where the 
currency stabilises at, as we are already seeing steel prices stabilize in comparison, to 
bring some greater confidence back to a beleaguered sector.

INDIA 
Turbulent weeks. 
 
DROPPING LIKE STONE! 
Having been the top placed sub-continent market for several months early in the year, 
India has endured a turbulent few weeks, sending vessel prices plummeting back down 
again. 
Ironically, the lowly place Pakistan market continues to prop the pricing table up, with
India not far off, on the back of a worrying currency depreciation as the Rupee is now 
trading at Rs. 78.50 against the U.S. Dollar – seemingly on its way further down towards 
the increasingly dreaded Rs. 80 mark, a fearful psychological barrier. 
As such, we do not anticipate much activity from this market, not only given the 
traditionally quieter rainy monsoon / summer months and an apt time for yards to reduce 
activity since labourers return to their hometowns during the slower monsoon period 
anyways, but also due to miserable availability of tonnage.

GMS Weekly 
UNWORKABLE! 
Having lost most of the recent sales (including the locally favoured larger LDT tonnage, 
including VLCCs) to the stronger and more resilient Bangladeshi and Indian markets, it 
seems set to be an extremely bleak summer ahead for Gadani recyclers. 
Not only are they quoting completely unworkable and opportunistic levels in the mid USD 
500s/LDT on any available unit, local Recyclers are also claiming that the State Bank of 
Pakistan is not approving any L/C above USD 100,000, such is the perilous state of financial 
affairs in the country at present, amidst a dire lack of foreign reserves / U.S. Dollars in the 
country. 
However, this seems like the usual excuses and an overreaction from buyers unwilling and 
afraid of the market tumbling further and this is one location worthy of steering clear of 
for now – at least until an overall sense of stability creeps back into the market.

NEW BOTTOM?! 
After what seemed like an unending ‘terminal velocity’ plummet for nearly a month-and-
a-half seems to have finally ceased this week, as a glimmer of stability surfaced this week 
via improving steel fundamentals, as local steel plate prices improved USD 10/Ton while 
import steel improved a healthier USD 30/Ton. 
This has arrested the declining vessel prices for now and, given the rapid rate of decline
from this market over an extended duration, we do not anticipate further drops and the 
current levels should be the new ‘new bottom’ on vessel prices. 
Notwithstanding, even with the ongoing shortage of tonnage, Turkey maintains a far 
greater avenue for tonnage coming in from EU waters, as Aliaga Recyclers are the only 
game in large LDT recycling in the EU. 
For now, however, like the rest of the ship recycling world, Turkey seems like to be 
traveling through a lull of their own and it may be some time before some market fixtures 
surface again.
