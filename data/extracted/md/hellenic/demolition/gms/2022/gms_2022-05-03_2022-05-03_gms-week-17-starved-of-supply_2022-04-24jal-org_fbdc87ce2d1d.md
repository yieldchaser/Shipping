As holidays descended across much of the sub-continent over the last week, activity and sales 
logically slowed as the recycling industry enjoyed a bit of a breather. 
Certainly, the recently ebullient Bangladeshi market has fallen out of contention over this past 
month, as domestic sentiments and pricing endured a sustained dip, despite having led the
industry for much of this year. 
Instead, it is the rampant Pakistani and Indian markets that continue to lead the way and 
continue to pay the most impressive numbers close to (and even above) USD 700/LDT (in some
cases). 
Having missed out on much of the market tonnage to Bangladesh over the last few months,
capacity at local yards remains healthy in both Alang and Gadani. As such, eager Recyclers are
snapping up the paucity of available units at increasingly firm numbers. 
Indeed, freight markets remain so impressively poised that a bit of a lull has descended over 
the industry of late (in terms of fresh market sales). Tanker rates across certain routes have also
started to fly again and coupled with an admirably performing container and dry bulk sector,
recycling markets are being deprived of most of the firm and workable age-appropriate
tonnage. 
Regrettably, on the ‘right’ of the globe, contradicting the aggression of the sub-continent
markets, is Turkey that continues to struggle through a surprising week of declines, with steel
plate prices and vessel offerings plummeting towards the low USD 400s/MT.

MARKET COMMENTARY

BANGLADESH 
DOWN AND DIRE! 
It was another subdued week in a reluctant Bangladesh, with few firm offers or even 
enquiries (for tonnage) emanating from this market, where sky-high sentiments during
the early part of Q1 2022 have ended up rapidly plummeting as the year progresses on. 
Logically, the ongoing month of Ramadan has kept the focus of domestic Recyclers on 
religious celebrations, even though domestic steel plate prices remain volatile and have
even declined by an additional USD 7/Ton this week, stranding Bangladesh as the lowest 
placed of all sub-continent markets. 
As a result, it was hardly a surprise to see no market fixtures into Bangladesh for another 
week, with most of the available tonnage being diverted to the better placed Pakistani /
Indian markets.

INDIA

TOP GEAR! 
As the top paying market of the moment, Alang buyers are seeing much of the tonnage 
that was previously destined for competing sub-continent destinations, finally being 
diverted towards Indian shores. 
It has certainly been some time since Alang sat atop the market rankings and this recent 
resurgence seems well deserved, given the (stunning) surge in domestic steel plate price
over the last few weeks.  
Surprisingly though, some worrying currency deprecations (have left the Indian Rupee 
straggling in the mid Rs. 76s against the U.S. Dollar) and alarming steel falls during early 
Q1 had put the Indian market well out of the competitive space. 
Notwithstanding, there remain a number of vessels of all types and sizes from existing 
Cash Buyer inventories that were working firm into India (even previously unfavoured 
higher LDT market units), as activity ramps into top gear in this market.

HOT ON THE HEELS! 
Following hot on the heels of India, is the similarly firm Pakistani market that has been on 
fire of late – an impressive feat given the recent political turmoil & ousting of Prime 
Minister Imran Khan, in addition to the succumbing of the Pakistani Rupee against the U.S. 
Dollar – hitting an 18 year-low of PKR 187.50 against the U.S. Dollar as the week ended. 
Despite these troubling declines in the Pakistani Rupee, there is the feeling locally that the 
Gadani market may push on further after Ramadan / Eid holidays, and once the new PM / 
cabinet has settled in, we hope this market continues to perform admirably.  
Especially since local steel plate prices have been performing admirably of late and even 
registered a firming of nearly USD 46/Ton this week alone – resulting in demand and local 
sentiments remaining firmly poised. 
As it stands, even well positioned vessels for Gadani are being diverted to the slightly 
better paying India (in some cases), and it may take a greater push on levels in order for 
Pakistani Recyclers to continue to get their fair share of tonnage moving forward.

WIGGLING OUT? 
The Turkish market seems to have gone through a bit of a noteworthy dither this week 
with import steel declining by about USD 30/Ton and prices on units declining about USD
50/MT over the span of a little over a week. 
The declining levels almost immediately brought about a sense of instant reservation
amongst the Turkish recycling community, given that the this market is witnessing record
prices amidst record (Turkish) delcines. 
As a result, the Turkish market went nearly silent this week, other than accounting for a 
couple of high-priced deliveries that seem to have reportedly made their way to Aliaga
anchorage this week.
