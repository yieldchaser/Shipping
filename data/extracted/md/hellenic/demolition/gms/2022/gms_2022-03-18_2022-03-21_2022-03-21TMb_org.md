### Editorial: Market Commentary

The unfolding crisis in Ukraine certainly has affected global oil and gas prices and those countries that regularly purchase their reserves from Russia (such as India) are now having to hammer out new plans to sustain their local economies, especially given the bevy of sanctions currently in place.

Yet, these are decade long highs that we are witnessing at present, and not since the heady days of 2008 have vessel prices been so firm. As a result, most owners are witnessing values on their end-of-life ships at least double over the past year or so.

Notwithstanding, chartering markets, particularly in the dry and container sectors, continue to perform admirably and certain routes on tankers have once again started to pick up, which is increasingly depriving hungry Recyclers of tonnage and is keeping prices (artificially?) firm. Furthermore, despite this latest dip of about USD 20 – USD 30 / LDT, commodity prices remain firm and there certainly is scope for improvement once again, given that the end of the Ukraine crisis seems far from over at this juncture.

The Indian Rupee, despite firming marginally this week, has suffered some noteworthy currency reversals of late, compounding some of the drastic steel falls seen over the last 2 - 3 weeks. Finally, in the West End, steel fundamentals (both import and local steel plate) recorded depreciations of their own, with the Lira further aggravating the situation this week.

Overall, it may perhaps take a week or two of stability before we understand where the new reality on prices actually is, as the coveted USD 700/LDT level (and above) still appears somewhat illusory for the most part and on most standard units.

## 3. Country Market Intelligence

### Bangladesh

- **Headline**: RESISTANCE!
- **Key Notes**: *Unwilling.* | *Bounceback?*

There is certainly some resistance from Bangladeshi Recyclers to dip back into the market at these extraordinarily firm numbers, even after this week’s decline in local steel plate prices (to the tune of about USD 17/Ton).

Much of the talk recently has surrounded levels exceeding USD 700/LDT, but it would appear very few Recyclers are willing to seriously engage in discussions at these incredible numbers, given this week’s cooling on commodity prices.

India has bounced back from its precipitous fall in steel prices early in the week, so it may be that Chattogram Buyers respond equally firmly, especially given the ongoing sparsity of candidates.

For now however, the market remains rather muted, fearful of potential falls further ahead.

### India

- **Headline**: YO YO!

After the precipitous drop in steel plate prices early in the week (where levels fell nearly USD 65/LDT) coupled with a recent currency depreciation (to the tune of about 4% percent), the Indian market appeared to be in the doldrums this time last week.

However, steel prices clawed back much of that lost ground over this past week, up by over USD 45/Ton by the time the week ended, eliminating at least half of the recent losses and regain some of the local confidence.

End Buyers are still somewhat cagey about negotiating at levels in the mid (and higher) USD 600s/LDT, however they may be left with no alternative in order to fill plots and satisfy their L/C limits.

The concern remains that should the Ukraine crisis be a prolonged one, India (who purchases a healthy chunk of its oil from Russia), may find it hard to sustain regular imports, especially in the wake of the recent crippling sanctions. It is feared that this in effect, may have long term economic implications for the Indian economy, whereby inflation may subsequently hit hard with the rising gas prices and especially if the Indian government is unable to sustain the sale of gas at current levels.

On the sales front, one specialist unit was concluded to Alang buyers this week as the Poland built, (locally favored - due to the prevalence of non-ferrous on board) fish factory SVEABORG (5,015 LDT) was committed for an excellent USD 795/LT LDT.

---

### Pakistan

- **Headline**: RELUCTANT!
- **Key Notes**: *Quieter.* | *Not stepping up.*

Pakistani Buyers remain somewhat quiet, despite recently coming back into the picture to compete with India (and even Bangladesh on a couple of units) once again.

There does seem to be some reluctance to acquire tonnage at these higher rates after another stunning surge in prices across the sub-continent market at the start of this year, but if Gadani Recyclers wish to compete and continue to secure tonnage, they are left with no other options.

The lack of local market (and even seemingly private) fixtures has made its presence known, with the lack of any fresh arrivals at Gadani’s waterfront this week and the only unit at local anchorage, finding its final resting place at a local yard.

As such, it has been another quiet week in Gadani, with end Buyers struggling to step up and get their heads around these higher numbers on show.

*Market Sales Reported*: **NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

---

### Turkey

- **Headline**: RE-ADJUSTING?

Just like in the subcontinent markets, local steel plate prices in Turkey went through a comparatively milder re-adjustment period this week. Not only did import steel decline by about USD 15 / Ton, but local steel plate prices also saw a minor correction – to the tune of about USD 10/Ton this week.

Notwithstanding, the far firmer levels on offer from Recyclers on regular tonnage remain steady and Owners in European waters looking for sub-continent levels from 2 years ago will not be disappointed in concluding a quick sale (or two).

With the ongoing conflict in the Black Sea, local Recyclers remain conscious about the jump in levels and that prices could come rolling down with the end of the fighting. While that remains to be seen how long it will take, levels remain beyond impressive for now and certainly worth a quick fixture.
