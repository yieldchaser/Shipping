Some unbelievably low and unrealistic offers have started to emerge from sub-continent
markets this week, and as such, both Owners and Cash Buyers would be well advised to leave 
ship recycling destinations alone for the time-being, especially until some sort of floor is
reached and stability regains a foothold. 
It has become increasingly difficult to obtain any firm or serious offers from any recycling 
market, as currencies continue to suffer across all recycling destinations and steel endures 
further volatile moves this week. 
Workable L/Cs are of chief concern in Bangladesh where End Buyers are struggling to obtain 
any sort of financing from local banks, amidst strict and ongoing governmental regulations on 
precious foreign currency reserves in the country. 
On the rare occasion when an End Buyer is able to open a workable L/C in Bangladesh (mostly 
on smaller LDT tonnage), the numbers offered are so pitifully low, it’s nearly instantaneous to 
see tonnage withdrawn and re-directed towards competing markets. 
We have even seen Local Recyclers attempting to fix deals with Cash Buyers and Owners,
subject to them obtaining approval from their financing banks within 3-4 days, which of course 
is never going to work and will see Bangladesh deprived of any meaningful tonnage until the 
end of the year - or at least until some sort of stability is seen. 
Finally, at the far end, after holding on and displaying some sort of stability (even though it’s
in the dumps), things couldn’t get worse for Aliaga Buyers as steel prices took a small tumble 
this week and the Lira gradually slips further. 
Overall, the economic situation across the recycling destinations remains precarious, with 
currencies weakening by the day and a shortage of U.S. Dollars in those countries, governments 
and banks are failing to sanction suitable financing for a ship recycling that they do not see as 
imperative under the current climate.

MARKET COMMENTARY

BANGLADESH 
SINKING LIKE A STONE! 
Bangladesh has sunk to the bottom of the sub-continent rankings this week, with a 
woeful display on any of the few (and mostly smaller LDT) tonnage. 
In some cases, we have seen Chattogram Buyers present opportunistic offers that are 
about USD 100/LDT lower than last week’s offerings – something that has seen Owners 
and Cash Buyers immediately withdraw their vessels, unwilling to even entertain 
discussions at such absurdly low and unrealistically numbers. 
Fundamentals remain one of the key driving factors as the Bangladeshi Taka remains 
around the BDT 100 mark against the U.S. Dollar, whilst local steel plate prices seem to 
have declined marginally this week. 
With financing from local banks increasingly hard to come by, it is therefore set to be an 
extremely quiet and frustrating end to the year in Bangladesh.

INDIA 
India resilient. 
 
NO NEED FOR PANIC! 
As the most resilient economy in the sub-continent, there is hope that India can lead ship 
recycling markets out of the current slump, or at least show them the way through some 
sort of stability during these trying times. 
On the fundamentals front, steel plate prices have recently come off by nearly USD 21/LDT 
and the currency continues to trade at a worrying level over Rs. 82 against the U.S. Dollar.
Notwithstanding, tonnage supply remains so low and yards so empty, that there seems to 
be no need for panic just yet as the excruciating shortage of supply seems to keep Indian 
prices propped up. 
Unfortunately, two ships with cheap scrap cargo have arrived Kandla and these billets are 
being offered at heavily discounted prices, which is part of the reason behind the present 
negative sentiments being experienced in Alang.

GMS Weekly 
CONSTANTLY PERPLEXED! 
Pakistan has followed the recent decline from competing sub-continent markets and is 
failing to offer anywhere near serious or competitive or even firm numbers on the absolute 
paucity of units available for recycling. 
Freight markets are still performing well and therefore Gadani buyers do not need to be 
confused about what to offer, as they are unlikely to receive any serious candidates for the 
remainder of the year (particularly with their recent performance and L/C issues) and so 
they may as well wait for some stability to return before returning to market. 
Gadani remains a market constantly perplexed, and that situation is unlikely to change at 
all any time soon, whilst fundamentals and the overall economic situation in the country 
continues to be so troubling. 
Local steel plate prices declined by about USD 10/Ton and even the Pakistani Rupee is 
slipping back down as it passes PKR 221 against the U.S. Dollar this week. It increasingly 
seems as though this will be a quiet end to the year even for the Pakistani market.

SLIPPING! 
Although the Turkish market had found a degree of stability, despite being in the 
doldrums, both import and local steel plate prices provided another stinger as they
declined by about USD 10/Ton this week. 
The Lira also weakened marginally this week as it ended the week just north of 18.60 
against the U.S. Dollar. 
This does not bode well for the Turkish market and is only facing an increased suffocation 
and disinterest from Aliaga Recyclers to offer on tonnage. 
Seems just like the sub-continent markets, we expect little from the Turkish market until 
2023.
