## 1. Market Overview

### Highlights
- India and Pak back.
- Improving steel.
- Losses made up.
- Bangla out.
- Some reluctance.

### Editorial: Both Pakistani and (especially) Indian markets have come surging back this week, to lead the way over a lackluster Bangladesh that has virtually dropped out of contention of late.

Firming steel plate prices in India have been the key driving factor behind this, and it still seems inevitable that the USD 700/LDT mark could be breached into these two markets, on upcoming sales in the near future (as long as these fundamentals hold).

Pakistan has been on the sidelines of late, but appear to have woken up this week, in the face of some serious and sustained competition from a resurgent India and a Bangladeshi market that’s begun to slip in the rankings.

Most of the losses on Indian steel plate prices have been made up over the last 3 - 4 weeks and the Alang market finishes this week in a rare position i.e. leading the market rankings ahead of all their sub-continent competitors, especially the usually pole-positioned Bangladesh.

Bangladeshi steel plate prices have been falling in recent weeks, and despite firming nearly USD 24/Ton by mid-week, the industry has seen them plummet from the summit to the bottom of the (sub-continent) rankings as their plate prices declined almost the same amount by the time the week ended.

Furthermore, such a pricing reversal certainly sees them positioned uncompetitively on any market candidates for the time being – even on geographically positioned units in the Far East, which are now heading towards Indian and Pakistani shores.

Finally, in the West, the Turkish market remains unchanged, firmly priced and positioned, with minimal tonnage to compete on.

Overall, there has been some hesitancy to dip in at some of these historical highs approaching USD 700/LDT, across all sub-continent markets. However, due to continually impressive and improving steel prices that seem to have been performing well on the back of the ongoing Ukraine crisis, it’s a wonder how long these numbers will stay around.

## 3. Country Market Intelligence

### Bangladesh

- **Headline**: PLUMMET FROM SUMMIT!
- **Key Notes**: *Rough weeks.* | *Sales lacking.*

Bangladesh has endured a rough few weeks, with steel plate prices declining from their previous highs and, local steel mills (as a result) have cooled their interest in buying re- rollable product.

Meanwhile, as most ships are now being diverted towards India and Pakistan (given the increasing gulf in prices), Bangladeshi mills will have to improve their levels in the coming week(s), in order to secure any inventory as there is likely to be a shortage of supply in the weeks ahead, so low are offers on units from Chattogram Buyers at present.

On the flip side, local anchorage remained busy with 7 fresh arrivals this week alone, which should hold over local Recyclers for the coming weeks as these units are digested and local offerings start to react to the competition and domestic fundamentals.

Consequently, there have been no market sales to report into Bangladesh over the past week and local Buyers will have to up their game in order to secure any available vessels moving forward.

### India

- **Headline**: TURNAROUND!

The scrap steel market in India has clawed back most of the losses sustained over the last three weeks, placing Alang as the number one ranked ship recycling destination in the Indian sub-continent – the first in a while, especially given Bangladesh’s US$ / LT supremacy of late!

Even conventional units now look destined for India – including large LDT Suezmax / Aframax Tankers and Capesize Bulkers that are normally heading towards Pakistan or Bangladesh instead – so impressive has been the turnaround from Alang in recent weeks.

The Indian Rupee also remains settled in the high Rs. 75s against the U.S. Dollar after some worrying depreciations earlier in the year, and this should give Alang Buyers further encouragement moving forward – especially in the near term.

However, the Ukraine crisis does stand to affect the Indian market in the long term, especially since both Iran and Russia are suppliers of oil to the Indian economy and with American sanctions targeting both nations, it is only a matter of time until the Indian Government can figure out how to continue to procure cost-effective oil for the masses and keep the wheels of the Indian economy in motion.

*Market Sales Reported*: **NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

---

### Pakistan

- **Headline**: STARTING TO COMPETE!
- **Key Notes**: *Political turmoil.* | *Missing out.*

Despite the political turmoil in Pakistan and question marks over the future of Prime Minister Imran Khan, vessel prices from Gadani have come back to become competitive with a soaring India and even firm past a recently roaring Bangladeshi market.

The domestic political uncertainty and the Ukraine crisis has led to Gadani Recyclers finding excuses not to compete of late, even though local steel prices remained firmly positioned. This has, of course, been a classic performance issue with the Pakistani market whereby local Recyclers have historically followed a rampant India vs. taking pricing cues from their own fundamentals.

Consequently this week, even though Gadani Buyers have lost out to Alang Buyers on much of the working market tonnage or those units in Cash Buyer hands, there is hope moving into the second quarter of the year that local Buyers will snag their share of favored tonnage.

*Market Sales Reported*: **NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

---

### Turkey

- **Headline**: QUIET.

It has been another relatively inactive week in Turkey, with not much to report on market negotiations or local fixtures. Local steel plate price remains stable whilst import steel dips by about USD 5/Ton.

As the conflict in the Baltic rages on, Turkish Buyers remain ever so attentive on local fundamentals, especially the Lira, that is once again continuing its descend towards TRY 15.0 against the U.S. Dollar.

Meanwhile, as the Indian and Pakistani markets head towards the hotly anticipated USD 700/LDT mark, Turkish Recyclers certainly have their work cut out for them, in trying to tempt Sellers of non-EU units into delivering their units to Aliaga’s shores.

For now, the Turkish market can remain content knowing that virtually all recycling units sailing out of EU waters have a designated spot on a Turkish yard.
