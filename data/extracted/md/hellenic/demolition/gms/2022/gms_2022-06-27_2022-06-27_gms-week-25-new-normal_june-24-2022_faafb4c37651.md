All of the major global recycling markets remain in turmoil for yet another week, with very few 
offers forthcoming (matching the near invisible number of units currently available for 
recycling), in addition to an utter lack of confidence from Recyclers across all locations that any 
sort of recovery is quite clearly not in the ropes anytime soon. 
There is the ongoing excruciating reminder that there are virtually no candidates to work on, 
as most Ship Owners are abstaining from recycling vessels from their aging fleets that have
surprisingly found profitable chartering businesses across their respective sectors. 
For those Owners with absolutely no other option on their near recycling-age units, other than 
a costly drydock & BWTS installations to consider, it would be a rude and jarring awakening 
to the fact that the sub-continent markets have tumbled well below USD 600/LDT, and as such,
offers beginning with USD 5XXs/LDT should now be considered the new norm.  
This is an astonishing fall from the exceptional numbers seen recently that were well in-excess 
of USD 700/LDT and that too only a few short months ago – certainly a well-timed achievement 
for the (lucky) few Ship Owners who managed to conclude their units at these remarkable 
levels.   
On the West end, Turkey seems to be the hardest hit with steel plate prices (import and local 
steel) continuing their nosedive into the abyss, with vessel prices being the hardest hit. 
Notwithstanding, as vessels of 20 (and even 25) years of age continue to trade and make money, 
there seems even less of a need for experienced Cash Buyers to panic, as there is certainly the 
opportunity to do a quick run (or two) on these aging units, just to ensure they break even on 
the deal.  
As such, it certainly seems destined to be a noticeably muted summer / monsoon season ahead,
with all markets deprived of tonnage, a monsoon season that is already hammering
Chattogram, and collapsing fundamentals contributing to the near comical state of affairs, 
especially as this new reality on lower prices starts to bite.

MARKET COMMENTARY

BANGLADESH 
FOLLOWING DOWN! 
Bangladesh has decided to follow the way of its Indian and Pakistani counterparts, as
Chattogram Buyers are refusing to entertain any of the few available vessels with firm or
even remotely reasonable offers (in some cases). 
Steel plate prices continue their volatility with reportedly, another USD 16/Ton decline 
this week, in addition to a depreciating currency that is now sitting at an all-time record-
low of nearly BDT 93 against the U.S. Dollar – a fact that has butchered local sentiments
given that the Bangladeshi Taka has historically been the stronger / stable element of 
domestic fundamentals. 
As such, minimal sales continue to take place on the back of shaky fundamentals, and 
despite the government trying to urge the economy and trade forward again (via the
recent budget announcement and stimulus package), it still may be some time before we 
start to see plots filling up again, especially with the locally favoured large LDT tonnage 
and business resumes at pace.

INDIA 
Prices descend. 
 
PANICKED WEEK! 
India has endured another panic-stricken week, as the currency continues to weaken 
against the U.S. Dollar (currently puttering around Rs. 78.30), in addition to local steel 
plate prices that spiralled down nearly USD 20/Ton by mid-week, only to recover USD 
10/Ton before the week ended – the true roller-coaster that has come to define the Indian 
market. 
As such, the domestic ship recycling industry remains in a perilous state for yet another
week, with the only saving grace having been the repetitive performance of this market 
where sentiments – though shaky even now – are starting to get immune to these often 
wildly fluctuating plate prices by almost USD 20/LDT per day. 
Like the other markets, there have been some opportunistic numbers thrown around and
mixed in with the ongoing scarcity of tonnage, the reality is it is not going to be a busy 
summer in Alang.

GMS Weekly 
DOORS CLOSED! 
As has become a bit of a tradition of late, the Pakistani market has plummeted to new 
depths with some insultingly opportunistic numbers towards the mid USD 500s/LDT being 
bandied about when tonnage inquiries are being presented to Gadani Buyers. 
Of course, no Recycler is going to secure any tonnage at or around such levels, and even 
then, there would still be a giant question mark surrounding the safe conclusion of the
transaction, so terrible has been the state of recent deliveries in Gadani. 
Therefore, the Pakistani market may as well close its doors for the time-being, especially 
until some seriousness and reliability comes back to the buying.

NO END IN SIGHT!! 
The seemingly limitless freefall that is now the Turkish market has entered its 6th week and 
there seems no end to the madness.  
Local steel plate prices have lost another USD 30/Ton whilst import steel does it worse 
with another USD 40/Ton shredded on its end. 
The currency isn’t doing much better, jumping around the TRY 17 mark against the U.S. 
Dollar (one of the lowest on record) keeping local sentiments on the fringe, through a 
scarcity of tonnage that hasn’t spared the Turkish market either.  
Making matters worse, with some vessels now being quoted as low as USD 240/MT, the 
decline in vessel prices from this market has been far more precipitous in comparison to 
the sub-continent markets.  
However, unlike the sub-continent markets, it will also be a quiet summer for this market.
