“If the highest aim of a captain were to preserve his ship,  
he would keep it in port forever.”
 
 
– Marcus Tullius Cicero 
The world finally gathered this week at the Tradewinds ship recycling conference in Dubai, for 
the first time in three years due to the onset of the pandemic. 
The big topics of the day were discussed, including various green and ESG measures being 
introduced to secure the sustainable future of recycling of older seafaring assets. 
Of course, there has been minimal activity to speak of in terms of sales, as we usually see one 
or two (market / private) deals being concluded and rates rising during the TW conference, but 
the event this year seemed to be an occasion for people to catch up, rather than conclude 
business, especially after the recent years fractured by Covid-19. 
Sub-continent markets remain subdued and deprived of tonnage as freight markets continue 
to fly. Depreciating currencies remain the chief concern (as do fragile steel plate prices), with 
rates well below USD 600/LDT and likely to move further down. 
Turkey too, remains in a depressed state with levels that remain suspended USD 250/MT below 
its recent spring peak, as its own currency and steel plate prices remain buried, along with the 
sentiments that too remain diminished. 
While the recent buildup seemed to suggest a greater level of activity come Q4 2022, this 
remains far from reality at this time as there is surprisingly little demand, due to strained 
banking / L/C limits and disconnected / high asking prices from Owners, leaving most plots in 
the sub-continent stranded in idle. 
The prognosis is therefore not good heading into the final months of the year, and all will be 
hoping for some increased positivity and ability to buy once the expected increase of tonnage 
starts to come in from next year.

MARKET COMMENTARY

BANGLADESH 
DULL & DEPRIVED! 
The sale of one previously concluded FSU from a Cash Buyer’s inventory registered at a 
decent USD 600/LDT basis a Chattogram delivery. However, the unit has been concluded 
to one of the few local Buyers who can open an L/C with their bank (even on larger units). 
Demand and open Buyers remain otherwise dull and even for smaller LDT units, there 
is not the appetite one would come to expect from a market that has been deprived of 
meaningful tonnage for most of the last few quarters. 
The currency, though marginally firmer this week, remains in a perilous state. Coupled 
with the ongoing L/C bank restrictions from government banks and local steel plate 
prices that dipped another USD 5/Ton this week, is resulting in very few sales locally - 
albeit from a minimal stock of available vessels, which now seem to be diverted

INDIA 
Lowest placed. 
 
DOLDRUMS! 
As the lowest placed sub-continent destination, Alang has seen very few firm candidates 
to work on in recent times – including locally favoured specialist units, offshore assets and 
HKC green vessels. 
Pakistan and Bangladesh have been able to offer significantly higher on the trickle of 
tonnage available, and it is therefore likely to be a slow end to the year for Alang Buyers, 
after a record low influx of tonnage heads to the beaches there – evident from the weekly 
port reports coming out of Alang. 
The Indian Rupee continues to create local tumult as it briefly breached Rs. 83 against the 
U.S. Dollar mid-week, only to settle at Rs. 82.60 – yet a record breaking (and worrying) 
level.  
Local steel plate prices too haven’t been too cooperative either, as  volatility ensues, despite 
ending the week USD 5/LDT over the previous week, leaving the local market unwilling 
to compete.

GMS Weekly 
STILL SHAKEN! 
After an altogether quieter year that was full of tragedy for a beleaguered Pakistan, it was 
encouraging to see many Recyclers taking a break from the silence of their domestic market 
and make their way across to the Tradewinds Ship Recycling forum in Dubai this week. 
As such, there were no market sales to a still Pakistani market (after catastrophic flooding 
and a currency collapse that is wavering once again), yet, there are signs that Gadani 
Buyers may be willing to step up and secure tonnage once again in the months ahead. 
Unfortunately, open and eligible candidates do remain starved from the market and it may 
take a further fall in freight rates going into next year before activity starts to pick up 
significantly again.
