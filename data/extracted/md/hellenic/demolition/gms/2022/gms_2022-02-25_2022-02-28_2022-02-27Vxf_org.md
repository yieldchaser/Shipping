## 1. Market Overview

### Highlights
- Markets firm.
- Pakistan quiet.
- Steel firms.
- Russian shame.
- Viable options.

### Editorial: Sub-continent markets remain firmly poised for another week, particularly in Bangladesh and a now resurgent India.

Pakistan, as seems to be typical for the market there, has gone quiet over much of the international uncertainty surrounding the possible outcome of the recent Russian invasion of Ukraine.

Bangladeshi Buyers have in turn, ramped up their buying and price offerings, mindful of the fact that some increased oil / gas prices due to the unfolding crisis in Ukraine, in addition to the inevitable sanctions that may starve Ship Recyclers of a supply line of ships for a short time.

Steel scrap prices have improved significantly in Bangladesh and India this week, leaving both markets positively poised going into March, whilst Pakistan slows down and waits to watch the international situation and subsequent market developments.

The Turkish market remains especially poised to suffer from the outcome of the Ukrainian invasion, with the supply of import of steel likely to be affected. Should this happen, we can possibly see import steel (and likely) ship offerings from Turkey firm up for a short period.

Overall, Putin’s disgraceful actions of the Russian invasion of Ukraine has certainly shocked the world over and the raft of justifiable sanctions is likely to have a profound impact on the shipping world – especially when negotiating state owned sanctioned Russian tonnage, which of course will restrict them from heading for recycling shores.

As it stands, despite the ongoing international turmoil, recycling markets keep giving owners a viable end of life option at fantastic decade long high levels, while freight rates too continue to perform admirably at present.

## 3. Country Market Intelligence

### Bangladesh

- **Headline**: SECURING SALES!
- **Key Notes**: *Big numbers.* | *Tanker sale.*

Chattogram leads the way for yet another week, with a stunning showing on available tonnage, as rates push on impressively once again.

Moreover, despite a limited showing of fresh sales, those vessels in Cash Buyer hands are already seeing increasing numbers from Bangladeshi Buyers – in what remains a nervous yet impressive showing from the market leaders.

The sale of one MR tanker was confirmed this week, as the HARMONY (8,356 LDT) was finalised at an impressive USD 650/LT LDT basis an ‘as is’ Colombo delivery.

---

### India

- **Headline**: BACK IN BIZ!

Indian steel plate prices surged back this week as the market improved by about USD 15/LDT, subsequently pushing them ahead of a dormant Pakistan.

Indeed, many geographically positioned vessels that were initially thought of as Pakistani candidates, are now being offered to India at much higher asking prices, given this week’s jump in local offerings!

However, Bangladesh is still so far ahead that (particularly) larger LDT vessels are being suitably diverted to Bangladesh, even if delivery remains W.C. India or in UAE / PG region, given the delta in levels over a Chattogram delivery.

*Market Sales Reported*: **NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

---

### Pakistan

- **Headline**: RETREAT!
- **Key Notes**: *Quiet.* | *Confused & nervous.*

Gadani Recyclers unfortunately decided to remain silent on any available tonnage this week, just as they had the opportunity to secure a few well-positioned units at resurgent and firm levels.

As a result, most of these recently working vessels have now been diverted to a resurgent India or (better yet) Bangladesh, and Pakistan has been left empty-handed once again, in a market which isn’t already seeing many potential candidates for recycling.

The reason for the currently restricted offerings is that confused and nervy buyers are concerned with the international situation unfolding in Ukraine and how it would affect the price of import scrap - without taking actual fundamentals and market conditions into consideration.

*Market Sales Reported*: **NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

---

### Turkey

- **Headline**: QUEIT, YET AGAIN!

Despite the conflict in the Black Sea, the Turkish market remained precariously steady this week. Most domestic Recyclers are suspecting a shortage in import steel coming up in the coming week(s), thereby, artificially pushing prices up.

Even the Lira briefly spiked past TRY 14.50 against the U.S Dollar this week, as global markets rattled to the news of Putin’s invasion.

While the Turkish market was slow moving for the last couple of weeks, with the uncertainty and possible volatility that may be forthcoming, we don’t expect much movement from Aliaga in the coming week(s).
