### Editorial: Market Commentary

On the far end, Turkey continues its dead drift through the recycling stream as rumors surface about discussions with Aliaga Buyers regarding 2023 deliveries, in addition a degree of stability in its fundamentals noticed over the last couple of weeks.

Overall, tonnage flow has started to increase as well – particularly in the container sector, which has come off significantly since the impressive peaks seen over the last two years. Tankers too, continue to trade profitably whilst dry bulk markets (and Capes in particular) endure volatile weeks of fluctuations.

Meanwhile, China’s relaxation of Covid restrictions is expected to bring some positivity back into the markets post New Year, as the world slowly gets back to normal after almost three years of varying degrees of Covid restrictions.

On the global front, stock markets have plunged this week off the back of announcements of further rate hikes, in order to try and ease some of the soaring global inflation, as recession fears in the EU, U.K., and the U.S. loom ever larger.

As such, for recycling markets, it is a slightly bleak and glum end to the year, with prices, economies, fundamentals, and sentiments showing no signs of stabilizing just yet, and thus, Sellers and Cash Buyers remain in a state of limbo when considering the sale of older assets.

## 3. Country Market Intelligence

### Bangladesh

- **Headline**: BANKING WOES!
- **Key Notes**: *Side-lined.* | *No loan.*

Bangladesh remains side-lined for yet another week, with minimal activity to report while the Central bank refuses to issue fresh L/Cs for purchases of recycling units.

Marred by plate prices that remain unchanged at their weaker levels and a Taka that continues to wiggle around the BDT 104 mark against the U.S. Dollar, local Recyclers are simply unable to compete or even complete deliveries.

As such, the industry focus has shifted towards a competing Pakistani and Indian market, who are now absorbing much of an increasing flow of tonnage for scrap.

Finally, despite talks of an IMF loan injecting some liquidity into the market, it is likely to be an extremely quiet end to 2022 in Chattogram, especially whilst these economic and banking woes endure without any sort of resolution.

### India

- **Headline**: OPPORTUNE TIME!

Much of the recent focus of ship sales has fallen on Indian Buyers over these past few weeks, especially with Bangladesh out of the game thanks to their ongoing L/C restrictions and a Pakistani market that is still undecided and confused on whether or even how much to offer on the odd locally (less preferred) units.

Indian prices have fallen considerably since the peaks seen earlier this year above USD 700/LDT, so it could be deemed as a good time to dip back into the buying at these lower overall levels, especially as an increasing number of Owners and Cash Buyer get accustomed to the current prices.

On the sales front and following the fixture of a similar container SSL CHENNAI last week, another small LDT container was committed this week as the VENUS C (4,394 LDT) fetched a decent USD 525/LT LDT basis an ‘as is’ Singapore delivery with 150 Tons of bunkers included in the sale at no extra cost to Buyers.

Finally, despite several beachings this week, fundamentals remain unsteady as local steel prices have fallen by nearly USD 25/LDT and the Rupee continues to waver in the mid Rs. 82 against the U.S. Dollar, leaving India behind their Pakistani competitors for the first time in several months.

### Pakistan

- **Headline**: FINALLY POSITIVE!
- **Key Notes**: *Buyers enquire.* | *Matter of time.*

The Pakistani market has finally enjoyed its first positive week for some time, with several Buyers enquiring on available units.

The next step is to see a few deals performed smoothly, especially after some of the shocking renegotiations and non-performances witnessed from Gadani earlier in the year. End users need to try and bring some confidence back to Cash Buyers and Owners, in order for them to reroute towards Gadani shores once again.

On the local fundamentals front, local steel plate prices jumped about USD 16/Ton as the currency continues to gradually weaken, nearly grazing past PKR 225 against the U.S. Dollar this week.

As such, it is surely only a matter of time before some deals are confirmed into Gadani, despite a plethora of container candidates that are looking likely to be sold into HKC only yards in India.

*Market Sales Reported*: **NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

### Turkey

- **Headline**: END IN SIGHT?

After what has been nearly 2 quarters of no positive movement and fundamentals that have crashed early in the summer, there finally seems to be an end in sight as the markets start to adapt to the lower prices on show from Aliaga, on the back of news about prospective deliveries for a 2023 arrival.

That is certainly good news given that the Lira though abysmally weaker, is still hanging around the recent lows of TRY 18.6X against the U.S. Dollar, while import and local steel plate prices have been relatively stable for the last couple weeks.

As such, we can only hope for an increased participation from Aliaga come January (and beyond).
