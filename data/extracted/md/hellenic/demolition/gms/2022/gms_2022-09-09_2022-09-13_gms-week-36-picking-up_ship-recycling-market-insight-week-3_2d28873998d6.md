### Editorial: Market Commentary

All of the major Ship Recycling destinations are still poised rather precariously, and Pakistan has seen some further depreciations on the currency towards the end of this week, whilst the country continues to battle floods, shortage of essentials, and the increasing spread of water- borne diseases, as thousands have perished in the floodwaters. Bangladesh too remains somewhat tentative, with limits on higher dollar value L/Cs still in place, local steel plate prices that took a noteworthy dive this week, and a Taka that seems to have found its stable place this week.

India has experienced (marginally less) volatile moves on steel across the week and remains the safest destination to deliver at present. Following an incredibly quiet 3 - 4 months of inactivity, there are signs of demand gearing up across sub-continent locations as most plots lie nearly dormant. Lastly, the Turkish market faces its own dithers this week, with further declines on fundamentals that are burying sentiments even further into the rut.

Overall, chartering markets have deprived recycling destinations of most tonnage, but Dry Bulk has recently started to cool off (particularly the Capesize Bulker sector), whilst Containers are also showing signs of softening, all while Tanker rates continue to shoot onwards and upwards.

It should hopefully be a busier finish to the year, as Owners look to capitalize on these still firm levels, despite the USD +100/LDT fall we have seen since the peaks of earlier this year. Rates in and around USD 550 – USD 600/LDT are still incredibly firm, given the lows we had witnessed of USD 250/LDT or so, during the early stages of the Covid pandemic.

## 3. Country Market Intelligence

### Bangladesh

- **Headline**: KEEN TO SECURE!
- **Key Notes**: *Emptying.* | *Restrictions ease?*

Having seen minimal vessels head to Chattogram shores over the last few months, Bangladeshi Buyers have been increasingly eager to secure tonnage.

Very few Local Recyclers are able to get permissions to open L/Cs on larger U.S. Dollar value / large LDT vessels as Central Bank restrictions on non-essential purchases to save up on the country’s foreign currency reserves have become very strict of late, affecting the domestic ship-recycling sector drastically.

However, there are signs that these restrictions could be eased before the end of the year (especially) if the economic situation in the country stabilises and there are certain Buyers who are now willing to open Usance L/Cs, since it seems that the worst of the currency depreciation appears to be over as the Taka steadied at a BDT 95 against the U.S. Dollar for the entire week.

Local steel plate prices, however, are an entirely different story as domestic steel fell by nearly USD 21/Ton this week, keeping local expectations and sentiments in check.

### India

- **Headline**: UP THEIR GAME!

The volatility on Indian steel continues on a daily basis – often up and down by as much as USD 10 – USD 20/LDT and this week was no different, as domestic steel ended the week down by another USD 8/Ton.

End Buyers seem to have become used to this however, as with the currency, which has not yet firmly breached the dangerous level of Rs. 80 against the U.S. Dollar, but has firmed down towards the Rs. 79.7X mark.

With competing markets like Bangladesh starting to wake up from a sustained summer slumber, Indian Recyclers will certainly need to up their game if they want to secure any of the growing supply of candidates heading into Q4.

*Market Sales Reported*: **NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

---

### Pakistan

- **Headline**: FLOODS DOMINATE
- **Key Notes**: *Still critical.* | *Suezmax sold.*

The terrible floods that have beset Pakistan over the last few weeks continue to dominate the headlines, with thousands having died and millions more displaced from their homes. As being reported, nearly a third of the country has been submerged underwater with mounting troubles of delivering essential supplies, medicines, and the growing spread of water-borne diseases.

It certainly is a difficult time for Pakistan in general.

Yet, there are those in Gadani who remain determined to get back to the business of buying ships once again as one Suezmax tanker CHEVAL BLEU (21,675 LDT) was reportedly committed for an impressively strong USD 615/LT LDT basis a Pakistan re-delivery.

Having experienced some shocking and blatant renegotiations over the previous few months, Cash Buyers and Owners will certainly need to see a show of strength and some good performances on any upcoming deals before tonnage starts trickling back to Gadani with any real confidence.

---

### Turkey

- **Headline**: FESTERING!

The situation keeps getting worse for the festering Turkish market that has continued to decline since a few months now. Local steel plate prices have reportedly dropped by about USD 20/Ton and import steel has dropped by about USD 30/Ton, essentially burying local sentiments even further.

Even the Lira has blown past the TRY 18.20 mark against the U.S. Dollar, leaving local sentiments so far down, that there remains virtually no interest from Aliaga Buyers to bid on the non-existent tonnage that is coming their way.

Having declined nearly USD 250/MT from the heady days in late spring / early summer, it seems Q4 (and likely even heading into 2023) will be a quiet time for the Turkish market.
