## 1. Market Overview

### Highlights
- Adjusting down.
- LC limits.
- Historical lows.
- Few purchases.
- India worries.

### Editorial: Market Commentary

This week, the Bangladeshi government introduced new limits to cap the outgoing volume of U.S. Dollars for ‘essential’ purchases only – raising question marks about higher priced vessels for recycling purchases in the foreseeable future. As such, the government announced that any L/C over USD 5 million has to be approved by the Central State Bank for opening, with the Bangladeshi Taka struggling to such a worrying extent of late.

Unfortunately, the currency situation is no better elsewhere as we are witnessing fresh historical lows by the day in all of the major recycling destinations on their respective currencies against the U.S. Dollar, and there have been elevated fears across these locations that international trade may eventually start driving domestic inflation up as the U.S. Dollar continues to strengthen rapidly.

Keeping things in check is the fact there are very few new units to work on, now that the tanker chartering market is firming further and this may give the recycling markets, some time to settle and stabilize before fresh units are proposed to Recyclers once again come Q4.

While Pakistan has registered a massive drop in local steel plate prices this week, India remains the most stable of the sub-continent markets despite the extreme volatility on Indian plate prices seen on a near daily basis and similar worrying currency depreciation is leaving Alang Recyclers confused and nervous on any firm offers moving forward.

On the West End, Turkish plates take another tumble this week as even the currency – like all the major recycling locations – continues its steady slow descent to oblivion.

## 3. Country Market Intelligence

### Bangladesh

- **Headline**: L/C FEARS!
- **Key Notes**: *State Bank approval.* | *Eerily stagnant.*

News that any L/C valued over USD 5 million would need to be approved by the Central State Bank, has rocked the industry this week and very serious questions have to be asked on any large LDT deals being done into Bangladesh for the foreseeable future.

Only ‘essential’ L/Cs over the aforementioned amount will need be approved to be opened, so critical is the shortage of U.S. Dollars in the country, as we have seen millions wiped of the Bangladeshi Taka due to the recent worrying depreciations here.

While local steel plate prices recorded an eye-watering jump of nearly USD 50/Ton within one week alone, the Taka continues to haemorrhage against the U.S. Dollar as it briefly breached BDT 94 this week.

Overall, many yards remain empty and local port report is seriously thinning, as demand for ships ramps up again, yet prices and ability to perform remains eerily stagnant.

### India

- **Headline**: DAZED AND CONFUSED!

Once again, we have seen some extreme volatility on local steel prices, in addition to a currency that continues to depreciate uncontrollably against the U.S. Dollar (even briefly breaching Rs. 80 and settling down in the high Rs. 79s), leaving the local market continually on edge and nervous about the ongoing volatility and further declines ahead.

Steel plate prices can often rise and fall by USD 15 – USD 20/Ton in a single day, making it impossible to book forward orders and hedge just where to place their offers on any incoming units.

Furthermore, Alang Buyers can see what is happening in the competing markets (Pakistan & Bangladesh) and this is hardly bringing a sense of stability to proceedings over this volatile uncertain summer.

The only consoling factor is that there are very few candidates to work on, so the hope is that markets settle soon before an expected busier Q4 push onwards.

*Market Sales Reported*: **NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

### Pakistan

- **Headline**: TESTING & TUMULTUOUS!
- **Key Notes**: *Lowest placed.* | *Fears.*

Pakistan remains the lowest placed of all sub-continent markets and the most unlikely of all three locations to perform on any new acquisitions, given the recent turmoil we have witnessed from this location.

The political coup that ousted Prime Minister Imran Khan has led to a crisis in the country and the ongoing global U.S. Dollar mayhem is resulting in a Pakistani Rupee that is breaching historical lows against the Dollar (virtually) on the daily, leaving most Gadani Buyers fearful of further falls ahead.

Moreover, despite a growing number of empty recycling yards and a pervasive demand for units, Pakistani Recyclers remain reluctant to offer on the paucity of units currently available.

Meanwhile, unlike the Bangladeshi market this week, where steel plate prices firmed by nearly USD 50/Ton, Pakistani steel plate prices plummeted by over USD 80/Ton, further damaging an already shredded local sentiment.

Overall, there have been lingering local fears that the country could go the way of the recently collapsed Sri Lankan economy, and as such, Gadani Recyclers are watchful and bracing for any potential falls and are taking their time testing the market ahead.

*Market Sales Reported*: **NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

---

### Turkey

- **Headline**: STRUGGLING, STILL!

After a couple of weeks of stability in local and import steel plate prices, the Turkish market tumbled further this week with an additional USD 20/Ton decline on local and USD 30/Ton on import steel.

Additionally, the Turkish Lira continues to hammer away at local sentiments as it heads unstoppably towards TRY 18 while flying past TRY 17.65 this week.

We get the odd news of small LDT local arrivals at the waterfront, but these have been few and far between and, overall, the number of ‘market’ fixtures are virtually unheard of over the last year or so.

While freight markets remain firm, global availability of market candidates remains flimsy at best and the immediate future for Aliaga, remains grim for now.
