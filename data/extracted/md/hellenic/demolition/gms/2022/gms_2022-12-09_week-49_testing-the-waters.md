# Market Commentary

## TESTING THE WATERS!

After the last few weeks of insufferable performances from the major recycling markets, a shade of positivity was observed this week, as Owners seem more confident in testing the waters and welcoming firm offers, especially those that are above USD 500/LDT.

On the back of this seemingly positive note, one or two market sales of note even materialized this week and it does seem as though prices have bottomed out and sentiments have flatlined after losing a rather rattling USD 200/LDT, seen from the peaks witnessed earlier this year.

Bangladesh continues to struggle with L/C financing limits, but the IMF loan is reportedly in the works of being approved and with most yards currently lying empty, we expect demand and consequently prices to come back, especially once some liquidity is injected back into the domestic market.

Pakistan remains utterly tentative on new purchases, so significant have the falls been so far this year on both the currency and steel prices.

On the West End, Turkey remains buoyant at its terrible lows, with marginal (at best) improvements in fundamentals being the only news emanating from this market anymore.

As such, the market of the moment continues to be India and despite a few of HKC units also working, nearly all Cash Buyer ‘as is’ sales are being presently finalized with an Alang resale in mind.

It still remains a mystery whether it may be a slightly busier end to the year than many had been anticipating, especially with further sales likely to take place to a more settled and confident Indian sub-continent recycling market.

Once the currency situation starts to stabilize in Pakistan and funds start to become more readily available in Bangladesh, then we really could see prices push on.

# Bangladesh

## WISHING ON A LOAN!

*Margin notes: Frustrated. | Seeking funding.*

Frustrations abound in Bangladesh at present due to a lack of financing and a growing demand whilst many plots lie essentially dormant, following a completely inert previous six months of the year.

The usually fevered buying we have become accustomed to in Chattogram has completely subsided on the back of Central Bank limits prohibiting even USD 1 million purchases these days.

The currency and steel plate also continue to toy with the local psyche as the Taka bounced around a record 104 against the U.S. Dollar and steel plates fell about USD 8/Ton this week.

As such, if the increasingly anticipated IMF loan comes, it will be a major input of funding that will transform the presently dull and lifeless Bangladeshi ship recycling market.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# India

## BACK TO BUYING!

*Margin notes: Record low arrivals. | Small container sold.*

India remains the most stable market for another week, as a handful of sales start to take place after a mostly bleak summer / monsoon (and perhaps year overall) that has seen record low ships head to Alang.

Of course, prices have fallen significantly since the market witnessed levels in excess of USD 700 / LDT seen earlier this year, but some signs of stability and a floor being reached are encouraging end Buyers to finally dip back into the buying again.

One smaller, decent sized container unit was committed this week as the SSL CHENNAI (4,039 LDT) managed to fetch a firm USD 560/LT LDT ‘as is’ Colombo, indicating a price in the region of USD 585/LT LDT in India needs to be considered once delivery costs are accounted for.

Overall, fundamentals too (though volatile) seemed to be less shaky this week as the Rupee continues to buoy around the mid Rs. 82 against the U.S. Dollar and steel plate prices, though declining towards mid-week actually firmed by about USD 5/Ton by the time the week ended.

# Pakistan

## YET TO SETTLE!

*Margin notes: Frustrated attempts. | Stasis.*

Gadani continues to be frustrated in their attempts to purchase fresh tonnage, especially against the more confident and settled Indian market that is trumping them for a majority of the (marginal) number of vessels being introduced for recycling.

Mostly lowball / unworkable levels and even terms are emanating from Pakistan – showing the current mindset of a market that is still in a state of turmoil and confusion and with good reason.

The Pakistani Rupee continues to gradually weaken by the week as it treads past PKR 224 and steel plate prices essentially flatlined through the week.

As such, it is hardly surprising that Pakistan is still in such a state of stasis, as the ongoing political instability and dithering fundamentals in the country display very few signs of a sustained stability on the horizon.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# Turkey

## DRIFTING!

*Margin notes: USD 500/MT | Collusion.*

Turkey continues to drift through the year, with no noteworthy activity to report since Aliaga Buyers broke all price records (in memory) when offers close to USD 500/MT were seen earlier in the year.

Regrettably, the short-lived spike in prices has vanished just as quickly, losing over half the value of recycling units that will head to Aliaga shores at present. As it stands, market units are virtually non-existent and even the occasional rumour of a private sale or small LDT unit arriving Aliaga anchorage have all but disappeared.

EUSRR requirements, domestic fundamentals, and a resulting vessel price have all colluded to make any market sale into Turkey an irrational competition against the Indian sub-continent.

On the fundamentals front, even though steel plate prices have marginally improved by about USD 10/MT this week, the Lira continues to degrade into the high TRY 18.6 against the U.S. Dollar – certainly a record low.

As such, Aliaga recyclers seem to be drifting through time, waiting for fundamentals and prices to improve so that the market can at least get to a competitive enough level where the voyage to the sub-continent makes less sense than delivering into Turkey.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**
