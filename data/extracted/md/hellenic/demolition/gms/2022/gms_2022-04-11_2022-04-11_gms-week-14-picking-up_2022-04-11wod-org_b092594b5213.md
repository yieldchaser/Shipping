April 8th, 2022

Week 14 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
“Either you run the day or the day runs you.”  
 
–  Jim Rohn 
It has been another impressive week in both the Indian and Pakistani markets, with reportedly
several sales taking place above the USD 700/LDT mark. These levels seem increasingly
indicative of a market that is firm and these repeat record levels should hold for the time-being
(especially after the recent volatility). Capacity also remains ripe across the sub-continent
markets and demand is certainly firm in both India and Pakistan, as levels push on off the back
of steel plate prices that remain positioned impressively high (even after this week).  
The Bangladeshi market is the only sub-continent destination that remains mute, despite steel
prices there having flatlined (at impressive high’s) through much of this week. Yet, Chattogram
Recyclers are refusing to buy at these high levels, even though evidence suggest these numbers
are here to stay for at least the next month (or so)!  
Much of the high-priced inventory concluded during the first quarter has ended up going to
Bangladeshi shores, including a healthy collection of the large LDT VLCCs, VLOCs, Capes and
Aframax tankers. As such, many Bangladeshi Buyers have already filled their plots with
tonnage and are likely enjoying something of a Ramadan breather (especially with domestic
activity having tempered of late). 
Meanwhile, Pakistan has been beset by some major political upheaval of late, with Prime 
Minister Imran Khan being ousted from office (after a vote of no confidence) and his
replacement is yet to be fully decided. Moreover, even the Pakistani Rupee has faced some 
volatility this week, raising eyebrows and caution. It is therefore unsurprising that the focus of
Gadani recyclers has been elsewhere, despite two high-priced headline sales this week (that are
seemingly set for a Pakistan redelivery). 
In the absence of a hot Bangladeshi market, India has been securing much of the market tonnage 
over the last few weeks - not only HKC and specialist tonnage, but also some larger LDT tanker
and offshore vessels finding their way to Alang. 
Finally, the Turkish market, whilst still stable at historical highs, remains drudged with not
much to report in terms of sale and purchase activity.

MARKET COMMENTARY

BANGLADESH 
BREATHER! 
As the traditionally softer month of Ramadan descends across Bangladesh, there have 
been very few market moves to speak of, into a decidedly softer Chattogram. 
Local steel mills are refusing to buy ship’s steel at these incredibly higher levels, even 
though steel plate prices do seem to be holding up at some impressive prices. As such, it
increasingly seems as though this latest slump in the Bangladeshi ship recycling sector is
being largely sentiment driven. 
Many local Recyclers have already filled their plots with much of the large LDT tonnage 
that was concluded earlier in the year. It therefore may be a few more weeks before price
and demand pick up again after Ramadan holidays and once more of the tonnage is 
consumed locally.

INDIA 
Missing out. 
 
STAYING STRONG! 
Steel plate prices have somewhat cooled in India this week, with levels having dropped 
over USD 15/Ton. Hence, the two market sales of the week appear to be heading towards
Pakistani shores - and at extremely strong levels to boot. 
Prices from India remain firm and perhaps the best placed in the sub-continent, despite 
strong competition from an emerging Gadani this week. Overall, however, it has been a 
quieter week in terms of sales to Alang Recyclers. 
On the local fundamentals front, the currency continues to trade in the high Rs. 75s against 
the U.S. Dollar, and demand for any units (even non-HKC / large LDT vessels) remains 
good, as India continues to lead the market rankings for another week.

GMS Weekly 
EVENTFUL WEEK! 
It has been an eventful week in Pakistan wherein, after a vote of ‘no-confidence’, Imran 
Khan has been ousted as the Prime Minister, subsequently leaving the country in a state of 
turmoil and without any immediate successor to step-in and stabilise the seemingly 
volatile situation. 
Despite all this uncertainty surrounding the leadership of the country, bullish Cash Buyers 
have acquired two vessels with an expected Pakistan re-delivery, at levels above well 
above USD 700/LDT. 
Firstly, the Capesize bulker SUNBEAM (21,018 LDT) was committed at an impressively
firm USD 715/LT LDT, whilst the Aframax tanker ION (16,640 LDT) was sold for an equally 
impressive USD 708/LT LDT.  
It would certainly seem as though these Cash Buyers are banking on a Pakistan rally in the 
wake of Imran Khan’s deposition, as these prices certainly do not represent the reality on 
the ground just yet.

TURKISH HAZE. 
Despite record offers on units, weeks of inactivity from Turkey have left many wondering 
just what is going on with this market. Logically, the gongs of USD 700/LT being breached 
this week will certainly have a collection of Owners being distracted away from the 
offerings from Aliaga. 
All whilst the Lira continues to trade in the high TRY 14.7X against the U.S. Dollar and
steel plate prices report no noteworthy changes in both import and local levels, all while
still trading at record levels. 
Even though there is news of a single cruise liner arriving the local waterfront this week,
the ongoing dearth of tonnage can only be equated to most units departing EU waters these 
days belonging to Owners who are continuing to capitalize on the currently high charter 
rates. 
As such, the level of tonnage remaining available for Aliaga Recyclers remains slim.
