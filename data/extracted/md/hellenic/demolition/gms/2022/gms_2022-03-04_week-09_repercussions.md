# Market Commentary

## REPERCUSSIONS!

It was another incredibly firm showing from the sub-continent markets this week, particularly Bangladesh and even a recently resurgent India (who now seem to be competing even more on tonnage), whilst Pakistan misses out on units once again, largely behind on the numbers due to local concerns surrounding the impact of the ongoing war in Ukraine.

Except China, local steel plate prices have made some impressive gains across the board in the top recycling international destinations this week, especially in India and Bangladesh. As a result, Alang Buyers have managed to secure the show stopping sale of the week, with a Capesize Bulker being committed for strictly HKC recycling and that too at levels competitive with those from Bangladesh.

Even in Turkey, both import and local steel plate prices have made improvements this week, with levels for all types of tonnage now firmly poised past the USD 400/Ton barrier – a first in memorable history.

Meanwhile, Chattogram continues to lead the way with some of the best rates witnessed in the sub-continent since the shipping boom of 2008, during which, the world saw recycling prices exceed USD 800/LDT. However, there seems little danger of that feat being repeated once again (at least for now).

Commodity prices have also surged during this bleak time of conflict and the cost to operate / deliver a vessel for recycling has also risen exponentially, primarily due to soaring oil prices that are now positioned well above USD 100 / barrel (at the time of writing).

The supply of vessels may cool off going into the second quarter of the year due to rising Tanker rates and continuing firm Dry Bulk income - unless those vessels that have SS / DD due imminently and BWTS installations required, that may make them uneconomical to continue trading.

# Bangladesh

## LEADING THE WAY!

*Margin notes: Missing cape. | No 700s.*

Bangladesh leads the way for another week but has started to miss out on vessels to competing markets, and this week (surprisingly), it was India on a favoured large LDT Cape for HKC recycling only.

Steel plate prices have been on an upward trajectory once again and this week alone, levels jumped nearly USD 42/Ton, leading to some of the best recycling offers we have seen since the highs of 2008.

We have not reached USD 700/LDT on standard vessels just quite yet, with mid USD 600s/LDT being seen for the time being, on the slimmer array of vessels that are working firm for sale - something which is contributing to a rampant demand in all sub-continent markets.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# India

## PUSHING ON!

*Margin notes: Steel rise. | Competing.*

The Indian market pushed on even further this week, as steel plate prices made some remarkable gains - firming by about USD 46/Ton within this week alone - whist the Indian Rupee (that is now reflecting the global currency volatility) is trading in the high Rs. 76s against the U.S. Dollar.

Indeed, Alang Buyers managed to secure the showstopping sale of the week as Berge Bulk of Singapore sold their second Capesize Bulker this year, for full range HKC recycling. The heavier ice classed and large LDT BERGE ARCTIC (27,461 LDT) was finalised for a whopping USD 660/LDT, on a delivered basis.

As it therefore stands, India seems there to compete on any and all market vessels available - not just specialist non-ferrous units and HKC only vessels (as was previously the case).

# Pakistan

## RECOVERING APPETITE?

*Margin notes: Too little too late. | Missing out.*

Pakistan may be starting to regain some appetite and confidence this week, but it seems to be too little too late, especially as the other markets have pushed well beyond Gadani’s offerings and vessels have already been diverted to competing shores.

Gadani Buyers did have decent possibilities to secure some of the geographically positioned candidates, but the ongoing confusion and nerves have (re)surfaced once the Russian invasion of Ukraine transpired.

As such, it seems as though Pakistani Buyers will (once again) be missing out on a majority of the tonnage on offer in the foreseeable future, given the stunning form and confidence / demand from neighbouring competitors at present.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# Turkey

## BALTIC BLUES!

*Margin notes: Prices breach USD 400/Ton. | Market to watch.*

This week again, the focus of the Turkish market remains the unfolding crisis in Ukraine. While certainly beneficial to the Turkish recycling market, the volatility of this recent improvement certainly remains of concern given that levels for all types of vessels have now firmly jumped across USD 400/Ton.

Steel fundamentals remain firm as both import and local steel plate prices recorded further improvements this week. On the other hand, like India, the Turkish Lira weakened past TRY 14.1X against the U.S. Dollar in light of the unfolding crisis.

Certainly, this market seem poised for a further improvement since the crisis in the Baltic seems far from over at this juncture. As such, Turkey is certainly a market to watch out for in the near future.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**
