After the announcement by the Indian government that they would reduce export duties on 
iron ore and steel products, the Alang market surged by Rs. 4,000 (about USD 49) in a single
day, only to come crashing down in subsequent days to the tune of USD 50/Ton by the time the 
weekend had arrived. 
In the midst of this surge, there was one Capesize Bulker sold for HKC / strictly green recycling
into India, and it is hoped that some stability on prices will be found in the week(s) ahead. 
Notwithstanding, sub-continent steel plate prices have plummeted greatly this week
(especially in India and Bangladesh), further adding to the ongoing stresses that have become 
routine in the sub-continent markets. 
Bangladesh remains on the sidelines with no new L/Cs being issued by the Central Bank and 
only a handful of private banks / local Buyers able to open L/Cs on (primarily) small(er) LDT 
units. 
It remains a very cautious and tentative market across the board, with most Ship recyclers
deeply embedded in a wait-and-watch mode, expecting prices to fall below USD 500/LDT and 
currently reluctant to dip into the market on fresh purchases until a some sort of a sustained 
period of stability is witnessed. 
Mercifully, the supply of tonnage remains low, despite dry bulk and container rates having 
cooled off considerably in the fourth quarter of this year. Owners have made decent money on 
their trading vessels this year and it seems they would rather hold and continue trading at these 
lower rates, rather than face the uncertain recycling realities of today. 
As such, it is not expected to be a frantic end to the year across sub-continent markets, but rather 
a trickle of candidates may arrive to the few keen and capable recyclers who are open to acquire 
ahead of an anticipated busier 2023.

MARKET COMMENTARY

BANGLADESH 
HANDCUFFED! 
Bangladesh remains largely inert with domestic Recyclers unable to acquire any fresh 
units, due to ongoing L/C restrictions imposed by the Central Bank, and End Buyers are 
compelled into a silence, handcuffed by their inability to acquire and spend U.S. Dollars.
Port reports are virtually empty as Chattogram Recyclers realise they are going to lose 
out on a majority of the market tonnage, to competing recycling destinations and as such,
they are maintaining a quieter profile. 
Volatility of domestic fundamentals aren’t being too supportive of the local mindset 
either, as the Bangladeshi Taka firmed nearly 6% this week, improving from BDT 106 to 
just over BDT 101, while local steel plate prices fell by USD 30/Ton. 
Notwithstanding, until the economic / LC situation in Bangladesh stabilises, there is very 
little sense in talking new purchases with end buyers since most are unable to perform 
and the very few who can open an L/C are just throwing silly, well below market 
opportunistic numbers.

INDIA 
Duties reduced. 
 
TURBULENT! 
India has endured a largely turbulent and rollercoaster week, with the announcement of a 
reduction in steel and iron duties by the government (initially introduced in April of this 
year) seeing steel prices reportedly shoot up by Rs. 4,000 rupees in a single day, only to see 
the remainder of the week beset by daily dramatic falls to wipe out those gains. 
In fact, by the time the week ended, local steel plate prices actually declined by USD 51/Ton 
overall, while the INR continues to trade in the high Rs. 81 against the U.S. Dollar – leaving 
this market devoid of much tonnage. 
As such, there has been very little material change in prices and sentiments despite this 
encouraging news on duties.  
On the sales front, one overly bullish Cash Buyer has speculated on the news on steel 
duties by purchasing Berge Bulk’s Capesize bulker for strictly HKC recycling, as the 
BERGE APO (20,668 LDT) managed to fetch a decent USD 523/LT LDT.

GMS Weekly 
SIDELINED! 
Pakistan has largely remained on the sidelines for another week, watching on as local steel 
prices first rose then collapsed in India, and even observing a muted Bangladesh that is
unable to acquire any fresh vessels. 
There isn’t a large number of prospective units to work on for an increasingly hungry set 
of End Buyers, but since they hope to position levels well below market realities, they are 
unlikely to secure vessels any time soon. 
Ensuring this, are shaky fundamentals and weakening prices from competing neighbors 
that will only encourage Gadani Recyclers to offer low. Steel plates fell by about USD
10/Ton and the currency remains in the PKR 223 region against the U.S. Dollar – certainly 
one of the record lows. 
Furthermore, with vessels now being sold for HKC recycling only (including another 
Capesize bulker this week to India), unless end buyers significantly upgrade their yards 
with class approved HKC certification, then they may miss out again on the majority of 
vessels sold next year and beyond.
