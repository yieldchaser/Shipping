## 1. Market Overview

### Highlights
- Reflecting.
- Decade peaks.
- Low supply.
- Charter highs.
- Hitting limits.

### Editorial: As sub-continent markets veer through the last couple of weeks towards the end of the year,  we can look back and reflect on what has been an extremely turbulent 2022.

Prices reached decade long peaks above USD 700/LDT in the first quarter of the year before crashing back down by about USD 200/LDT, with certain trades seeing below the USD 500/LDT barrier and even into the high USD 400s/LDT on certain occasions.

On the West End, the situation was no different in Turkey, where levels too hit a record USD 500/Ton for a brief period, only to plummet about half in value (about USD 250/MT) in a rather short period, and has remained on the sidelines ever since.

Steel plate prices and especially the relevant currencies have all seen record lows against the U.S. Dollar, shattering records by the month during the summer period.

We have also seen decade lows in the supply of tonnage, with almost all freight sectors performing strongly this year, as we finally seen a much-anticipated rebound on tankers following a number of years in the doldrums.

Dry Bulk and Containers have also had outstanding years, with minimal scrapping seen in both sectors during the first half of the year. As such, moving into 2023, we do expect to see more vessels enter the market for recycling from each of these sectors, especially as freight rates have cooled off considerably towards the end of this year.

There are also a number of vessels trading right up to their limits (in terms of surveys) and due to older age profiles and reduced earnings, Owners are unlikely to pass further surveys and may well scrap with recycling rates still looking relatively firm (USD 350/LDT being the historical average). A reduction in the fleet size amidst relatively low newbuilding deliveries for 2023 should help charter rates recover and for various earning cycles to start again.

## 3. Country Market Intelligence

### Bangladesh

- **Headline**: DE-FANGED!
- **Key Notes**: *Out of buying.* | *Tough limits.*

Bangladesh remains mostly out of the buying for another week, as L/C limits and lack of financing from the Central bank has essentially stripped away End Buyer capabilities when securing new vessels.

It was of course a stunning first-half of the year in Bangladesh with some of the highest sales for years taking place above USD 700/LDT and for all types and sizes as well – Capes, Aframax tankers, and even VLCCs.

However, in addition to the financial handcuffs that local Recyclers are currently in, fundamentals are still struggling and that is further hampering local sentiments. The Bangladeshi Taka closed at BDT 105.5X against the U.S. Dollar, while local steel plate prices reportedly flatlined through the week at its current lows.

While the currency collapses under the weight of the free markets, the government introduced tougher limits on its precious U.S. Dollar reserves spending – allowing only fuel, food, and fertilisers, and thereby de-fanging the domestic ship recycling sector for the time being.

Hopefully 2023 will find Chattogram Buyers back in the fray once the Central Bank eases its restrictions.

### India

- **Headline**: YEAR END LULL!

A number of sales have taken place into India of late, as this is the one performing market across the sub-continent that Owners and Cash Buyers can together rely on.

Prices have recently cooled off, however as steel prices have continued to dither through the week and display their ongoing volatility, while the Indian Rupee continues to struggle close to historical highs of Rs. 83 against the U.S. Dollar.

As such, many Alang Buyers remain reticent to dip back into the buying, perhaps fearing further falls ahead, as there is something of a lull going into the final weeks of the year.

*Market Sales Reported*: **NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

### Pakistan

- **Headline**: BOTTOMED OUT?
- **Key Notes**: *Sales register.* | *Economic fears.*

Having concluded a number of sales after a period on the sidelines, it appears that some confidence has returned to the Pakistani market, as End Buyers are satisfied to get back to the bidding tables, in the belief that the market has perhaps bottomed out and 2023 will help recycling markets overall.

Pakistan is not beset with the L/C difficulties that Bangladesh is, but there is significant turmoil in the country – both politically and economically - as the Pakistani Rupee now passes the PKR 225.5X against the U.S. Dollar.

Indeed, there have even been fears that Pakistan or Bangladesh may go the way of the recently collapsed Sri Lankan economy, and much uncertainty and fear has seeped into both markets at the end of this year as a result.

*Market Sales Reported*: **NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

---

### Turkey

- **Headline**: 2023 HOPES!

As with the expectation for the trading markets to slow down next year, there are increasing rumours from the Aliaga market of an increasing number of units being potentially worked basis a 2023 delivery.

Key fundamentals remain steady (as they have been for most of December) as both import and local steel plate prices have reportedly been steady with no noteworthy change as while the Turkish Lira seems to have found its new playfield around the TRY 18.6X mark against the U.S. Dollar.

With the year about to turn over and an overall holiday mood already set in, Aliaga Buyers are optimistic about a better 2023.
