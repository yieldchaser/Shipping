“You can never leave footprints that last  
if you are always walking on tiptoe.” 
 
 
– Leymah Gbowee 
All recycling destinations across the Indian sub-continent and even Turkey have been suffering 
a prolonged and agonizing malaise over the past six months (at least), one that’s showing no 
sign of abating any time soon. 
In fact, prices have deteriorated even further this week at all sub-continent locations and 
receiving indications / offers – even in the USD 400s/LDT on small LDT units - is gradually 
becoming a reality. 
As such, any Ship Owner expecting some of the better levels (even from last week) will have to 
readjust their expectations much lower, in order to bring any sort of firm offer to the table. 
L/Cs are continually proving to be extremely difficult to get approved these days, especially in 
Bangladesh, where the government is not sanctioning any fresh L/Cs for vessels, leading to a 
virtual standstill of the industry there. 
Indeed, we have seen multiple vessels enter the market and turn straight back around for 
trading (even at the reduced dry bulk and container levels of today), having either not found a 
Buyer with a ready L/C or having suffered embarrassingly low offers. 
The price ideas mentioned below are simply illusory, as the key thing today is finding a firm 
End Buyer at a reasonable level and with a workable L/C, which is turning out to be the most 
challenging of issues facing Cash Buyers at present. 
Overall, it is expected to be a dark and bleak winter for a withering ship recycling industry, 
especially after the decade long high in prices above USD 700/LDT whilst business boomed 
during the early part of this year.

MARKET COMMENTARY

BANGLADESH 
STARVED! 
Bangladesh is a country that is currently starved of U.S. Dollars and as a consequence, 
starved of tonnage as well. The Central State Bank is reportedly not approving any L/Cs 
at present, even for purchases of USD one million.  
As a result, no vessels are heading towards Chattogram for recycling at present. 
Port reports are emptying out and other than a tiny unit that arrived the waterfront this 
week, there is essentially no fresh recycling tonnage arriving Bangladeshi shores. While 
demand should be firming given the current circumstances, there is very little End 
Buyers can do without having their finances in place, in order to secure tonnage. 
Steel plate prices have also been volatile with nearly no movement over the last couple 
of weeks (at the new lows). Even the currency has been fluctuating wildly, jumping 
between BDT 102 to BDT 106 throughout the week, which is providing no incentive
domestic Buyers to come forward with any sort of firm levels in the market.  
No trading has even taken place this week on steel products, such is the dire market 
situation and currency crisis in Bangladesh at present.

INDIA 
Lacking confidence. 
 
UNCERTAIN! 
As the main performing market (without the L/C issues that Bangladesh and Pakistan have 
been facing of late), India is still suffering much uncertainty over where prices should 
realistically stand today. 
Volatility in fundamentals remain in place as steel plate prices have come crashing down
by nearly USD 27/Ton this week and the currency, though stable, is still standing at a 
worrying Rs 81.6X against the U.S. Dollar.  
End Buyers and steel traders in the country have thus taken to purchasing steel imports 
(one vessel laden with steel cargo arrived Kandla a few weeks ago) at much lower levels 
than current ship recycling prices. 
As it stands, Alang Recyclers have entered into a wait-and-watch mode, anticipating and 
fearing further falls ahead, even expecting to see new a normal into the USD 400s/LDT by 
the end of the year.

GMS Weekly 
TWO MINDS! 
Pakistan Buyers remain in two minds – whether to go for the paucity of tonnage available
in the market, or whether to hold off anticipating further market declines ahead – especially 
as U.S. 400/Ton prices start to emanate from India and the Pakistani market has been 
historically mirroring India’s offerings. 
Rates have come down into the low USD 500s/LDT – in tandem with competing markets.
Yet, obtaining any firm and serious numbers from tentative End Buyers is proving to be a 
real challenge once again. 
While local steel plate prices have declined a marginal USD 5/Ton this week, it was the 
Pakistani Rupee that has Gadani Recyclers on caution again as the PKR declined past PKR 
223 against the U.S. Dollar. 
Performance issues are also at the forefront of Cash Buyer minds after some terrible stories 
in the form of blatant renegotiations seen at the waterfront earlier this year on arriving 
vessels. 
Note: In last week’s edition, there was a typo in the reporting of the Pakistani Rupee 
against the U.S. Dollar. The correct value was PKR 220.60 against the U.S. Dollar. We 
apologize for any inconvenience this may have caused.

DEAD! 
After consecutive weeks of declines in an already languishing import and local steel plate 
price, the market has seemingly continued its dormant performance this week, with no 
market sales or even news of market tonnage being proposed to local Buyers. 
Aliaga yards remain empty, plate prices dead, the Lira is still buoying around the 18.6X 
mark, and with no units on offer, there is a deathly quiet emanating from this Aliaga. 
As the sub-continent markets continue to falter, Turkey seems to be stealing a book from 
them – especially in terms of overall performance. Pricewise, it does not seem as though 
Turkish levels will plummet further on fresh tonnage, given how disastrously their levels
have already fallen within the year. 
Certainly, seems like a quiet winter ahead.
