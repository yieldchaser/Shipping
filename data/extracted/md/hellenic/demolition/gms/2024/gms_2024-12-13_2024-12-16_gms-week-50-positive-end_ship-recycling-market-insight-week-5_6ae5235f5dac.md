### Editorial: POSITIVE END?

As we stand on the edge of the final weeks of 2024, compared to the rest of the year, it certainly seems as though there has been more of an uptick in the markets of late, in addition to a far more positive conclusion for ship the recycling sector in the Indian sub-continent, as news of fixtures on the back of firming levels has kept recyclers busy taking deliveries at the various waterfronts over recent weeks. Though the markets are not quite there to breach the coveted USD 500/LDT mark just yet, they have come out of a Q2 – Q3 and even early Q4, when recycling levels were at their lowest this year. Moreover, and pragmatically, rather than attempting to break the next high-priced fixture in the market, ship owners (especially) and even cash buyers need to historically reflect on the fact that these are still very strong prices today.

Meanwhile, unlike the unfolding chess moves from the tanker sector, supply from other sectors is expected to remain steady / likely improve into 2025 and despite trading markets performing impressively as they drive the global fleet to its highest average age it has ever been, increasing newbuild constructions in 2025 should phase out older units faster and ensure a steadier recycling supply. Additionally, despite China’s stimulus announcements promising stronger measures for its economy, Capesize bulkers managed to lose ground even further and all eyes will now be peeled on the Trump’s incoming tariffs that will manifest once he takes office from January 20th, including the tariffs he intends to impose on China, in particular.

## 3. Country Market Intelligence

### Bangladesh

- **Headline**: DARK FLEETS.
- **Key Notes**: *Questionable.* | *Lacking finance.* | *Coming back.*

As a growing number of large LDT vessels start making the rounds for a recycling sale – many of questionable background and legitimacy are now coming forth as a few purported dark fleet units are also trying to escape an end-of life-frenzy whilst trying to find a final resting place on the sands, as news of incoming sanctions from the West against Russian and Iranian (oil) exports surfaced this week. Although some might successfully find a place at a recycling yard worldwide, these issues will increasingly persist under the pressures of unfolding global crises.

In Bangladesh, the number of open and financially capable buyers who have the appetite, available yard space, and limits with their respective banks, remains fewer than a handful and most are therefore incapable of opening large U.S. Dollar value L/Cs on some of the larger LDT units on offer, even whilst indications for these dark fleet units will understandably remain far below market levels.

Keeping matters under constant pressure is also the ever-pervading state of the domestic economy, which, although on the mend of late, is far from performing at even acceptable levels. And understandably so as even this week, local steel plate prices (like Pakistan’s) have fallen back to sleeping on the floor and that too at an overall dishevelled level of USD 521/Ton, further cementing the current ceiling for local offers in the coming weeks. Even the Bangladeshi Taka continues to suffer as it loses another 5 basis points this week, closing out at BDT 119.98 against the U.S. Dollar. Like the Pakistani Rupee / economy, the lack of available U.S. Dollars has severely constricted its overall trading within the Bangladeshi economy, resulting in a Taka that though severely diminished this year, finally seems to have found stable footing between BSD 119 – BDT 120.

Economically as well, domestic construction projects are yet to take off the ground and the overall momentum of steel trade has still to get it’s gears in mesh, which will likely not happen until 2025 at the very least, simply based on the direction of steel plate prices, still unravelling demand for steel (and consequently ship’s steel), despite overall demand & pricing for ships starting to rear its head once again and Chittagong chop shops optimistically, yet cautiously, approach 2025.

Local port position too has been supporting the evidence of this unfolding optimism as another 43K LDT of total recycling tonnage, including a large LDT Gas Carrier, announces their arrival at the waterfront this week, marking an ongoing and unrelenting fight against declaring a bankrupt port position, unlike the sad traffic patterns at Gadani’s waterfront over the last couple of months.

### India

- **Headline**: RESURGENT TIME.
- **Key Notes**: *Picking up tonnage.* | *HKC ready.* | *Keen to buy.*

As with Chattogram, Alang’s ship recycling sector has welcomed a version of its own resurgence in the final moments of the year and likely into early 2025 as well, as news of even more deals being successfully concluded to Alang ship recyclers as a result of the firming offers – including specialist stainless steel units, containers, oil rigs, tankers, fishing vessels, and HKC only India vessels – have started to increasingly surface over successive recent weeks.

The fact that nearly all recycling yards in Alang essentially have their HKC certification in order, will immensely help India maintain pole-position on a global ship recycling level, to safely absorb much of the world’s environmentally conscious recycling tonnage when it comes for sale in the years ahead, as the Hong Kong Convention finally enters into force from the middle of next year and Bangladeshi and (especially) Pakistani recyclers are not only in the process of still gathering recipes to upgrade their facilities, but they also remain far from receiving any SoC approvals from IACS class-societies thereafter.

Even domestically, India remains the most financially secure destination for global ship recycling on account of a comparatively larger (than other ship recycling destinations worldwide) availability of U.S. Dollar reserves, further bolstering India’s financial stability in a sector that is heavily dependent on it. Moreover, as trade with the U.S. is about to breach a unique milestone and break a record USD 77.5 Billion in 2024, it marks an overall 10.3% rise over the last 30 years of India’s trade with the country.

Notwithstanding the economic stability, key fundamentals driving local mindsets remain shaky as ever amidst a Rupee that has now seen the better part of Rs. 84 throughout the week, even as it spikes to hit a record Rs. 84.99 against the U.S. Dollar and finally end the week an overall 14 basis points weaker at Rs. 84.80. Local steel plate prices weren’t seemingly supportive of the prevailing mindset either and although shaky, managed to end the week at just USD 1/MT below last week, easily chalking it down to market movements.

As the Indian ship recycling sector continues to surf the wakes of a relatively optimistic economy and much in contrast to this week’s local port position – where a healthy collection of units from rigs to reefers are awaiting deliveries – the future supply of tonnage may see hiccups amidst unfolding global tensions, rising average ages of the world’s overall fleets, and the persisting slowdown in the output of newbuildings, all of which would be the final nails in the coffin for ship recyclers worldwide. And the fact that it was only this past January where levels were at USD 600/LDT for containers and today stand almost lower USD 150/LDT, the industry is certainly eager to see global economies firm again to where levels at or above USD 500/Ton become the norm.

### Pakistan

- **Headline**: SAD REFLECTION!
- **Key Notes**: *Not easy going.* | *Liquidity issues.* | *HKC failures.*

It certainly has not been easygoing for Pakistan’s ship recycling sector this year and second only to 2023, where only 16 vessels were delivered to local recyclers throughout the year, 2024 looks on track to be the second slowest year for the ship recycling in Gadani over the last decade. Logically, supply has also been at its lowest over the same period, but the present can simply not be the reason behind Gadani’s ongoing performance, especially as neighboring India and (especially) Bangladesh continue to dominate the bidding tables and market rankings of late.

The ever-tailing Indian offers mindset aside, Pakistan has been beset with financial / liquidity issues and an unfolding lack of overall confidence in the state of the domestic economy, which has eventually filtered down to perpetually nervy end buyers who have seen their plots lie mostly dormant for a majority of 2024. As inflation continues to create hurdles amidst stalled discussions on IMF’s incoming loan, which itself continues to be delayed on account of pending investigations, the Paksitani government has continually curtailed expenses towards non-essential industries for the time being, including ship recycling, as the country hopes for the incoming bailout package to ease ongoing economic constraints.

As a result of the lack of availability of U.S. Dollars, the Pakistani Rupee, though ending the week only 2 basis points weaker at PKR 278 against the Dollar, certainly rollercoaster-ed its way to this point through the course of the week. Psychologically hurting the local mindset even further are collapsed steel plate prices, which, despite being the highest of all of the major ship recycling nations, is the least traded of all as it continues to flatline at a comparatively stronger USD 654/Ton – nearly USD 200/Ton higher than those from neighboring India and amidst cheaper imports, further highlighting just how weak domestic demand for steel currently is.

With the HKC coming into force a mere 6 months away and Gadani ship recycling yard owners still buying the recipe books for the ‘green’ efforts needed towards upgrading their respective recycling yards, 2025 may end up being just as bad, if not worse for Gadani, especially once the convention comes into force and yards owners will be compelled to make investments in order to continue operation, or shut shop by the time 2026 comes around.

### Turkey

As the Lira’s decline against the U.S. Dollar continues uncontested, declining 37 basis points and ending the week at another historic TRY 34.91, import steel plate prices corrected / improved about USD 12/Ton, matching local steel plate prices, which themselves remain constricting low. As local offers stable after declining nearly USD 30/MT over recent weeks, the new lows are going to raise the bar for Aliaga buyers to snag their share of tonnage even more, especially in the face of sub- continent levels that are firming / on the rise again.
