Despite both Bangladeshi and Pakistani markets making noticeable improvements over recent 
weeks, unfortunately, the ongoing shortage in the global availability of ‘market’ tonnage has 
ensured sales remain noticeably extricated, which has in turn restricted the industry’s ability at 
assessing where levels really stand today. As evident from the number of arrivals & beachings this 
week (see Indian sub-continent port reports on Page 8), an increasing number of Ship Recyclers have 
clearly managed to and are reportedly still in the process of obtaining further approvals on L/Cs 
from their respective banks. As a result, there is now a noticeable disparity in offers emanating 
from recyclers from the same sub-continent destination, so fractured is the sub-continent ship 
recycling purview on the immediate future of fundamentals & vessel pricing across the board. 
Moreover, despite both, steel plate prices stabilizing & even making noteworthy improvements 
(for e.g. the recent USD 50/Ton jump in Bangladeshi plate prices) and sub-continent currencies
continuing to find stable footing & even registering improvements this week, there remains a
limited number of aggressive end Buyers who have been actively concluding the number of small 
LDT, private tonnage that is seemingly on offer week-after-week – a volume that is not only 
insufficient in sustaining the needs of Ship Recyclers the world over, but it is also a volume that is 
continually and increasingly affected by the ongoing war in the Middle East.  
Speaking of, of increasing geopolitical concerns of late have been the ongoing skirmishes between 
Western Forces and Middle Eastern terror groups who have now pushed the U.S. into retaliatory 
strikes against key targets in Syria & Iraq. Moreover, as the overall conflict seems to increasingly 
gain a stronghold, attacks on passing merchant vessels in the Red Sea continue week-after-week, 
resulting in freight rates remaining unseasonably high and this continues to stifle the number of 
vessels that are potential recycling candidates, indicating that it will likely remain quieter and for 
far longer than previously anticipated. 
This trend is also likely persist as the industry approaches the traditionally quieter Chinese New 
Year Holidays next week, leaving a positively underwhelming Q1 2024 for the global ship 
recycling markets & potential vessel volumes to come, all a time interesting developments on the 
regulatory fronts are seeing HKC certified Indian recycling yards being cast into the spotlight & 
under (re)consideration for EU approval once again, while the Norwegian government has 
similarly pledged US$ 1.364 million last week, to help aid the upgrade of facilities in Bangladesh.

MARKET COMMENTARY

BANGLADESH 
FRUSTRATIONS BUILD! 
Despite the ongoing positivity being displayed from the Bangladeshi market through the
opening few weeks of 2024 thus far, a glaringly marginal number of market sales have 
been concluded into this market, all while Chattogram Choppers have been making do 
with smaller LDT private tonnage (as evident from Bangladesh’s local port report this
week), a move indicative of increasing local desperation that is clearly leaving some of 
the tier-1 domestic Recyclers frustrated at the ongoing & unforgiving shortage in the
supply of tonnage. 
Making matters worse for industry players who are looking to conclude vessels into 
Bangladesh has been the ongoing disparity in offerings from available local Buyers, a
move that has been making an increasing number of Ship Owners & Cash Buyers
nervous at where levels truly stand and if they were to conclude a high priced deal,
whether the agreed-to level would even be valid by the time the respective vessel arrives 
the country’s recycling waterfront – so far apart have local offerings been.  
This ongoing disparity in offers could also be due to the overall lack of confidence in
domestic fundamentals, especially after an extremely shaky last 6 months of market 
declines, restrictive financing & L/C practices by domestic banks, and a Taka that has 
seemingly found a new stable spot, albeit at the weakened BDT 109.50 levels from last
week. 
Notwithstanding, on the flip side of things, this ‘stable’ level of BDT 109.50 under the
new free market trade of U.S. Dollars seems like it could be the new BDT 77.X that the 
industry saw for years when U.S. Dollar circulation was heavily regulated by the
Bangladeshi Government. Moreover, even local steel plate prices made marginal 
improvements this week, reporting a nearly USD 60/Ton firming over the last few weeks.
Finally, a Panamax Bulker sale did manage to register from Turkish owners this week, as
the WADI S (10,247 LDT) fetched a healthy USD 530/LT LDT in light of being a good-
conditioned grain trader, having a spare propeller onboard, and including about 140
Tons of bunkers in the sale.

INDIA 
No MSC. 
LOOKING UP! 
After a string of sales over the last 5 weeks that saw virtually one container being 
committed every week, and that too to an exclusive number of pre-selected HKC yards 
intended for a strictly green recycling resale into Alang, surprisingly this week (and 
upon the successful beaching of their 5th unit), there were no fresh candidates or sales to 
report from MSC.  
Moreover, given that India remains the lowest priced / placed of all of the sub-continent 
ship recycling destinations, there have understandably been no market sales to report 
into Alang this week. At a time when the availability of tonnage remains unforgivingly 
absent, investments made over the years by Alang Recyclers at upgrading their facilities
to operate increasingly in line with the requirements of the HKC, are certainly paying 
modern-day dividends to those local Buyers who are now relying primarily on green /
specialist units that have been proposed for sale so far in 2024.  
Conversely, despite being better priced destinations, both Bangladesh & Pakistan still 
have a lot of, not only catching up to do with regards to upgrading their facilities to HKC 
standards, but also successfully recycling a collection of units on their own, before global 
confidence and comfort in the minds of Ship Owners reaches the point where their 
prospective units will eventually make their way to the shores of Chattogram (and even 
Gadani in the long run). 
Meanwhile, GMS’s SSORP offers the SAFEST option for Ship Owners who are willing 
to be pioneers in not only giving approved Alang and Chattogram yards a chance, but
also future approved yards in both Bangladesh & Pakistan their much-deserved
opportunities, further giving the efforts being made by the Indian sub-continent ship 
recycling industry the recognition that it rightfully deserves, as SSORP stands as a 
guardian of the recycling project on behalf of those Owners who are looking to maintain 
responsible recycling practices for their units. 
On the local fundamentals front & still puzzling to industry players is just how India is 
still the lowest placed destination, all while it’s fundamentals have displayed an 
impressive degree of stability & strength (as in the case of the INR this week) and India’s 
domestic economy has also been Asia’s star performer of late. Yet, local Recyclers find a
reason or another in keeping local offerings seemingly suppressed. All this, despite the 
Indian Rupee managing to gain impressive gains (trading in the high Rs. 82.8X against 
the U.S. Dollar) whilst local steel plate prices jumped an impressive USD 12/Ton this 
week, hopefully injecting a sense of optimism about the near future in the minds of 
Alang Buyers, as the industry trudges into February.

GMS Weekly 
DISPARAGING DISPARITIES! 
After nearly a year on the sidelines watching recycling tonnage sail by to competing 
neighbors and as the sub-continent ship recycling market placed second to Bangladesh, 
Pakistan has slowly & steadily come back into the picture this year, with some increasingly 
impressive, yet disparaging disparities in, local offerings.  
As a result of, few market sales have materialized into Gadani in 2024 thus far, leaving 
those end Buyers who are now successful at obtaining L/C / financing approvals from their 
respective banks, increasingly frustrated / disappointed & finally empty-handed, given 
how the global shortage of meaningful tonnage has been gradually & seemingly trying to 
put ship recycling yards worldwide, into Airplane mode. 
Nevertheless, given all what Pakistan has been through in recent years via severe economic 
turmoil, political strife, recent skirmishes with Iran, and a Pakistani Rupee that depreciated 
so alarmingly & astonishingly against the U.S. Dollar that it resulted in local Recyclers 
temporarily ceasing vessel negotiations just to see how far the penny would fall, it is 
certainly encouraging and refreshing to witness Gadani Buyers back at the bidding tables. 
Moreover, having privately concluded and beached about 6 ships over the last 5 weeks, 
Pakistan is seemingly on pace to pass last year’s ‘annual’ volume of 17 units. 
Overall, it certainly is a “pat on the back” moment for Pakistani Recyclers given that in 
addition to grabbing another juicy sized 11.6K LDT container away from Indian hands (that 
finally arrived Gadani’s waterfront this week), the recent decision by the country to ratify 
the Hong Kong Convention will certainly be another reason for Alang Buyers to worry 
about the Pakistani market in the long run.

FINDING SHIPS…AND WORDS! 
Just as Aliaga Recyclers have been on an eternal hunt for recycling tonnage coming up on
two years now, we are gradually running out descriptive words & ways to describe just 
how harrowingly quiet Turkey’s domestic ship-recycling sector has become over this time, 
and could continue deteriorate through much of, if not all of 2024. 
As President Erdogan pledges his allegiance with Iran and the Houthi’s in this increasingly 
unraveling conflict in the Middle East, the ability of Aliaga Buyers is not only being 
hampered by the effects of the war, but their potential inability to affect U.S. Dollar 
transactions on future deals via potential sanctions from the West could be the final nail 
this market should do its best to avoid. 
Meanwhile, as import and local steel plate prices remain volatile overall, the Turkish Lira 
is individually ensuring that any freshly proposed unit(s) in the coming future will likely 
face hiccups from the time the deal has been concluded until the time of physical delivery, 
so miserably remarkable has the Lira’s inability been at applying any type of brakes on its 
unending slide against the U.S. Dollar, having declined once again this week, ending it just 
under the shade of TRY 30.50.
