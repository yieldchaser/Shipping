As the world stands teetering on the edge of completing another circle around our sun, economies 
worldwide continue to struggle amidst a greater prevailing unease. On the back of a strengthening 
U.S. economy that saw the U.S. Feds cut another 0.25% in interest rates this week, the U.S. Dollar 
continued to make the present, a testy time for most ship recycling nations as respective currencies 
continue to either not trade (on account of a lack of available domestic reserves) or continue to lose
ground against a firming Dollar. Stresses around U.S. oil production that spiked the barrel price on 
account of fears that Hurricane Rafael in the U.S. Gulf would disrupt production. Notwithstanding, 
the far less fearful outcome has helped maintain current levels of output, easing at USD 70.38/barrel 
by the end of the week. Domestic disappointment over China’s recent stimulus measures to help 
easing local government debt strains failed to garner the desired outcome, seeing growing concerns 
from Chinese businesses and easing of Chinese import of oil. Even the Baltic’s main sea freight index 
eased its losing streak, rising about 1.4% by the time the week ended. Yet, the seeming supply of 
tonnage has maintained itself through the course of the week as sub-continent ship recyclers have 
certainly had their share of (still slim) pickings to negotiate on at the bidding tables. 
And although a slightly disappointing local port position earmarks the end of Week 51, it still 
remains a more encouraging end to the year that has left recycling markets in an overall more bullish 
mood heading into Q1 2025 – seeking to negotiate on and absorb the incoming flow of tonnage of 
some seriously aged beauties. Moreover, despite prices cooling from the peaks over USD 600/LDT 
down to USD 450s/LDT over the last 11 months alone, after weeks of abstaining from the lower 
levels, both ship owners and cash buyers are finally comfortable / willing to offer vessels back into 
the market at these now acceptable lower numbers being tabled. While 2025 is gearing up to be a 
big year from a safe / green recycling regulation stand point, as the Hong Kong Convention is finally 
set to enter into force and ship recycling yards at all of the major ship recycling destinations will 
need to be compliant and operate in line with the guidelines of the convention, in order to continue 
taking in the vast array of vessels that will eventually come available for a recycling sale. India has 
a majority (if not all) of its ship recycling yards already HKC compliant, and Bangladeshi recyclers 
are still recovering through their economic meltdown and trying to work towards having most of 
their yards compliant by June 2025. Pakistan, however, lags far behind with not even a single yard
HKC compliant, amidst desperate calls for the upgrade process to now start, in order not to be left 
too far behind the plate. Finally, Turkey at the far end continues suffering through its fundamentals 
collapse for over 3 years now. 
Overall, as elections will likely come to an end by Q1 2025 in Bangladesh as well, with some stark 
shifts in regime and power playing out this year, in the USA as well with President Trump winning 
another 4 years and PM Modi regaining a majority in India in November, the future is certainly a 
cautionary tale. As supply is expected to gain momentum once the ongoing sanctions are in place 
and businesses adapt to the post-sanction world once the fallout is known, 2025 may end up being 
a comparatively resurgent year to 2024, if not besting it.

RETURN TO FORM? 
The Bangladeshi economy is gradually crutching its way through a recovery phase and local 
confidence certainly was elevated this week, on the back of the news that a U.S.D. 500 million
budget support loan in order to greatly boost domestic reserves at a time the country is in desperate 
need of financial support and recovery through a year of unrelentingly negative outcomes. The 
government is expected to sign the agreement within next week, further delivering a message that 
a characteristic January firming may still be on the charts, at least for Chattogram Choppers. 
As reports of construction projects are yet to come forth, news of stalled trading of steel continues 
to haunt domestic recyclers as demand from steel mills remains stranded on the back of flailing
domestic demand. As demand from local ship recycling yards consequently stalls coupled with the 
lack of available U.S. Dollars for domestic recyclers, has resulted in most of the plots lying empty 
for a majority of the year.  
The lack of ship’s steel trade has also seen local steel plate prices remain stranded at a weak-ish 
USD 521/Ton, leaving the ceiling of local offerings constricted to how far recyclers are willing to 
bite into their margins, just to keep their yards operational through a time so many others are still 
functioning at or below desired levels of output. The U.S. Dollar too continues to dance vigorously
around late BDT 119s, managing to stay just above BDT 120 against it, ending the week at a slightly 
optimistic (compared to last week) BDT 119.47. This is finally starting to see local offering finally
take a hit as indications maybe taking a hit. Time will tell. 
This reluctance from domestic recyclers handicapped with prices capped at current steel plate 
levels is once again seeing no news of market or even private sales being committed to Chattogram, 
resulting in a waterfront that could likely deliver an empty port position next week, as the only 
activity at the waterfront were deliveries of last week’s arrivals to their respective Choppers and 
no new arrivals indicated at anchorage for this week.  
Notwithstanding, with the economy gradually on the mend and domestic reserves looking to get 
a healthy boost for the interim (at least carry the economy until the country’s national elections are 
concluded), domestic sentiments remain eager and recyclers are on the lookout for tonnage, amidst
a relative return to form has been seen from this market as we approach the end of this year, despite
it being a year of upheaval and concern for the country and in turn for Chattogram’s ship recycling 
sector. The volumes available for sale have also been at the lowest level in the last decade and yards 
have been wisely spending their time upgrading their infrastructure facilities during this period of 
slowdown, so much that class NK have thus far approved 4 facilities in Chattogram in 2024, with 
more set to follow before the HKC kicks into gear around mid-year.

LACKING TONNAGE! 
Despite India standing in a position to easily take first place on the market rankings on the back of 
a firming demand, many yards in Alang have partially shut down due to crippling levels of supply 
and reduction in the overall levels of recycling activity witnessed during the year, for the right 
vessel(s), the demand from Alang buyers has seen levels being driven into the high USD 400s/LDT 
and even above, on select, specialist / NF rich units, all in the face of local steel plate prices that 
remain stranded so far below where local offerings stand today. 
Moreover, with an unfavourable election outcome for the pro-business PM Modi, perhaps the 
biggest shock for the nation this year came from the performance of the Indian Rupee, which saw it
lose another 17 basis points, inching its way ever closer to Rs. 85, ending at Rs. 84.96. Of note, the 
Indian Rupee, like the Bangladeshi Taka, remained volatile throughout the week, even repeatedly 
spiking briefly to a record Rs. 85.31.  
Yet, inflation remains levelled at moderately acceptable levels, leaving the Indian government with 
an overall optimistic outlook for the near future, especially with the Modi Government garnering 
additional seats in Congress via a victory in the key state of Maharashtra. This momentum in 
positivity saw another fixture being concluded to Alang recyclers as the fire damaged / undertow
bulker TRUE CONFIDENCE (8,766 LDT) managed to fetch a decent USD 353/Ton with about 650 
Tons of IFO and about 180 tons of MGO onboard at the time of arrival. 
With the year end approaching rapidly and the festive moods setting across the world, deals might 
remain fewer than the marginal collective witnessed of late, even though Alang’s local port position 
remained the busiest in the industry, with only a couple of new arrivals and deliveries reported for 
the week, and Indian recyclers, with their HKC accreditations in hand, approach Q1 2025 on a better 
prepared note. 
Demand good.

PAKISTAN 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
STRUGGLES! 
Pakistan’s struggles continue unabated amidst the nation’s ongoing economic downturn as the 
shortage of U.S. Dollars has left them not only struggling to negotiate effectively for tonnage at the 
bidding tables, but they have failed to obtain the necessary funding to arrange for infrastructure
upgrade projects at their respective yards, so as to upgrade them and meet the deadline to operate 
in accordance with the guidelines of the HKC within 2025.  
The lack of market sales has corresponded with an empty port position that has remained devoid 
of any arrivals / deliveries for over 2.5 months now. This is seeing an increasing number of yards 
and recyclers go quiet over the recent months, as dormancy takes a hold and ongoing operability 
of the yards once again comes into question. 
Whilst still awaiting to clear the queries surrounding the misappropriation of USD 400 Million of 
previous funding, IMF’s upcoming loan hangs in limbo, as do hopes of this market returning to 
the bidding tables and that too in form, seems like a fading possibility.  
Fundamentals too aren’t dishing out their share of support to the local mindset as not only has the 
otherwise dormant local steel plate price record a decline to the tune of USD 8/Ton this week, but 
it has also further put downward pressure on the already disheveled state of local offerings. The 
Pakistani Rupee too reported minimal movement during the course of the week, as has come to be 
expected of the nation’s currency. 
How will local recyclers manage dip back in and fill plots remains an mystery unto itself, especially 
as Indian levels continue to firm and domestic pressures push local offerings down, remains to be 
seen in Q1 2025. For now, let’s hope this market finally starts overhauling facilities once the next 
tranche of financial aid package gets delivered to the nation, so that Gadani’s’ ship recycling
fraternity can fall in line to operate withing the guidelines of the HKC, else the industry risks 
becoming obsolete.
