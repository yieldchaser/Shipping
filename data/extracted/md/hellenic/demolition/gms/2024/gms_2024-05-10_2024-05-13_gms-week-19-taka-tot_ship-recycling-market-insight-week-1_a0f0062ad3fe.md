### Editorial: Market Commentary

Notwithstanding the expectantly shaky end to Bangladesh’s working week, a couple of pre-and- post Taka-drop sales did register into this market and given their fixing levels, the impact from this fall so far seems to be minimal. On the other side, as Indian elections continue into their third week and in the face of growing anticipation of PM Modi’s seemingly inevitable victory, not only have Indian steel prices been on a mission of late, but Alang Recyclers also seem to be growing increasingly confident that Mr. Modi’s victory will bring with it, the highly-awaited portfolio of infrastructure projects that stand to skyrocket India’s Economy, which is the 5th largest in the world today. Pakistani Recyclers meanwhile continue to kick-back & relax as their increasingly evident absence at the bidding tables has finally resulted in Gadani’s anchorage going ‘dark’ this week. Moreover, as India continues to improve on the back of their fundamentals, how long will it be before Gadani goes ‘dark’ for the rest of the summer? Having said that, a tiny faction of Gadani Recyclers has come forward with minor price improvements that remain woefully insufficient to snag any (market) units. Finally, the hungry collection of Aliaga Recyclers out West responsible for driving Turkish recycling prices to the moon last week, continue to negotiate for their share of the firmer tonnage that has come recently available, reportedly without any recourse.

As the overall flavor in the supply of vessels remains containers, this week saw several of the larger liner companies gearing up to sell their containers, amidst freight rates in the feeder sector that remain comparatively more depressed (than other sectors) and noticeably firmer recycling offers on the table. Whilst Dry Bulk / Cape freight rates (particularly on the larger LDT spectrum) push on, this has seen a near perfect dearth of juicy large LDT dry units for a recycling sale so far this year, while Sinokor commits a 4th container for a recycling sale at eye-watering levels this week.

## 3. Country Market Intelligence

### Bangladesh

- **Headline**: TALKING TAKA!
- **Key Notes**: *IMF conditions.* | *Buried horizontal* | *Unexpectedly expected.*

The true extent of the impact from this week’s crash in the Bangladeshi Taka (on the domestic Ship Recycling community) remains to be seen, especially as it received a BDT 7 jolt against the U.S. Dollar (a drop of about 7%) in the span of a couple of days towards the end of the week. While most of us in the industry were aware of this intentional depreciation in the value of the Taka as a part of the agreed to ‘terms & conditions’ of the IMF loan package (likely as a payback mechanism on the loan) that the country received back in January this year, and one that subsequently resulted in all but wiping-out L/C restrictions imposed on Chattogram Chop Shops since early 2023, it was the out-of-sight- out-of-mind quick arrival of this drop that left most in the industry in a state of shock this week, especially as this market continues to dominate the rankings since the start of the year and tonnage availability has finally started to ease once again.

This BDT 7 decline saw the Taka end the week at BDT 116.98 against the U.S. Dollar, all while local steel plate prices remain buried horizontal. Consequently, this weakening further stands to choke out the already suffocated margins that local Recyclers have been religiously scraping at, in order to continue to offer the firmest levels in the industry amidst the ongoing tonnage drought. As such, unless local steel plate prices fail to climb in the coming week(s), it remains to be seen just how much longer this market holds on to the top spot, especially as firming Indian steel plate prices that have been India’s Achilles Heel to her (ingratiated) performance since the start of the year, have finally started to climb over recent weeks.

Notwithstanding this week’s unexpectedly expected jolt, one chip carrier was concluded to Bangladeshi Buyers early in the week (prior to the Taka Tot) and at expectedly firm levels as the PINE EXPRESS (9,543 LDT) from Singapore based owners Nova saw an impressively firm USD 560/LT LDT on the unit, as Sinokor seemingly chased the shaky market (post Taka Tot), grabbing another eye-watering USD 581/LT LDT for their tiny container HEUNG A TOKYO (2,446 LDT), their 4th recycling sale in the space of a month. Given the sub-3K weight of the unit and faster recycling times, the respective Recycler likely snagged the unit hoping to stay in the green.

As the nation’s waterfront slows down with just 1 fresh arrival this week, Bangladeshi performance in the coming weeks remains to be seen, especially as Indian Elections and Indian Fundamentals both seem headed for positive times.

### India

- **Headline**: CONJOINTLY CREEPING.

With the fall of the Bangladeshi Taka this week, a 3rd week into the India’s General Election cycle, and Indian steel plate prices that jumped by about USD 12/Ton, it seems as though it is only a matter of time before we see Alang Buyers back at the negotiating helm once again, with an aggression to acquire that is likely to leapfrog even the Bangladeshi market in the coming weeks, especially as the industry witnesses demand & pricing conjointly creeping up in India once again. This firming has also helped vessel prices from India get another boost this week, with levels increasingly likely to climb in the weeks ahead.

Although domestic steel plate prices have been the Achilles Heel to Alang’s recycling performance since the start of the year, it has been the level at which plate prices have been marooned at, which has seen Alang Recyclers painfully and regularly losing out tonnage to neighbouring Chattogram & Gadani Buyers and even face weeks of empty port reports. It has therefore been nothing short of impressive to see plate levels rebound so consistently since April 24th, and that too to the tune of over USD 30/Ton. This firming has also been nothing short of a Godsend to the local purchasing mindset, one which has seen a spate of ongoing fixtures come to fruition at Alang’s anchorage over recent weeks. Yet, despite the Indian Rupee starting to weaken to an abysmal Rs. 83.52 against the U.S. Dollar once again, local sentiments seem to remain afire with PM Modi’s likely victory, in addition to the subsequent commencement of large-scale infrastructure projects / undertakings across the country.

Meanwhile, even though the only (market) sales of the week were into Bangladesh, India remains aggressive on several private / off market units as not only do Alang Buyers continue to take deliveries of units at a gradually increasing pace, with Pakistan steadily on the outs and the Bangladeshi market likely hitting a price ceiling sooner rather than later, India seems destined to become the ideal destination for not only geographically positioned tonnage / specialist (offshore / stainless steel) HKC / vessels, but a healthy majority of the units intended for a sub-continent delivery.

Given that week 3 of the election-fever is underway and the results are due to be announced on or shortly after June 2nd, the tea leaves seem to be spelling out a busier summer / monsoon season for WC India this year.

### Pakistan

- **Headline**: STAGGERING OFF PACE!
- **Key Notes**: *Empty Anchorage.* | *Marginally firmer.* | *Radius of chances.*

On the back of woefully weak & increasingly unworkable numbers from subdued Gadani Recyclers, Pakistan has endured a rough couple of few weeks with minimal sales & activity that finally saw an empty Gadani anchorage this week (See Port Position Page 6). As such, Gadani Recyclers are gradually staggering off pace and Pakistan is increasingly turning into a recycling destination that will likely not see 2nd or even 1st place in the market rankings any time soon, suspect at least until late Q3 / early Q4 this year.

Far from the higher placed Bangladeshi market, Pakistan has increasingly fallen off pace from even from India this week, compelling those Gadani Recyclers who remain serious to acquire tonnage, to have to sharpen their pencils once again and unfortunately return with only marginally firmer offerings, making Pakistan the only ship recycling destination to face unexpected struggles in the face of increasing tonnage supply - especially as the competition is also gunning for their share of vessels via increasingly firmer offerings of their own.

Domestic fundamentals have certainly done little to assist Gadani Recyclers appropriately position their offerings over recent weeks in that, the previously held mantel for being the recycling location of choice West of Sri Lanka, has now unequivocally passed to Alang for over a couple of weeks now. Moreover, as the Pakistani Rupee remains in a weakened state - ending at levels just north of PKR 278 against the U.S. Dollar this week - local steel plate prices still need be resuscitated back from the dead as they remain flatlined for what seems like an eternity now.

With India now encroaching on the radius of Pakistan’s probabilities at securing geographically viable units that are opening in the area, and even with their ‘improved’ offers falling far short of necessary, the way this act is playing out certainly delivers the impression that the final act in Gadani maybe wrapping up for the season.

### Turkey

- **Headline**: SILENT FIRE!
- **Key Notes**: *Twisted times for Turkey indeed.*

With no market fixtures to report, and a clique of Aliaga Recyclers clearly ablaze for a few weeks now, the Turkish market has remained silently on fire for tonnage, regrettably without any recourse in the face of a supply that has yet to grace the shores of Aliaga.

Import & local steel plate prices have reported no major changes for the 2nd running week, in addition to the Turkish Lira that continues making it increasingly challenging to gauge just how safe current Turkish offer really are i.e., will Ship Owners get paid as per the price of the MoA?

The genesis of the question certainly begs answering at this juncture given that Turkey’s inflation has been nothing short of a raging wildfire over recent years, in addition to the near total assistance of support from domestic fundamentals – especially the Turkish Lira, that essentially remains burnt ash at current levels and remains in excess of TRY 32.

As such, this inexplicable firming in vessel prices is certainly leaving Ship Owners and Cash Buyers more curious than comfortable to conclude into this market at current levels and for longer laycans.
