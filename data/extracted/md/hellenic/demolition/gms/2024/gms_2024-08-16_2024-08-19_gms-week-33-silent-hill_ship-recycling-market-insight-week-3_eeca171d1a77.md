## 3. Country Market Intelligence

### Bangladesh

- **Headline**: HARD TO CALL!
- **Key Notes**: *Shocking unrest.* | *Volatile moment.* | *Force Majeure ripe.* | *Petite LDTs.*

As Bangladesh recovers from the recent shock resulting from the riots and ensuing chaos across the nations that saw hundreds of (primarily) student protestors lose their lives on the back of a “quota” modification on future government jobs that was set in place by the current administration under PM Sheikh Hasina who fled the country a couple of weeks ago, the nation continues to limp through the formation of an interim government and the military retains control in an attempt to uphold law and order across a country beset by violent riots, and it will be some time before the makeup of the incoming cabinet will be realized and what new policy changes will be implemented at the onset, as the nation looks to stamp out purported corruption witnessed amidst the ranks during the short-term tenure of the now-defunct regime.

During the transitional period, government bodies continue to face mounting operational disruptions as various departments remain out of commission due to the recent chaos, abrupt change to the country’s leadership, and key officials associated with the ousted government having been reassigned to different posts or even going into hiding to avoid any possible reprisals against them. As the nation has and continues to stagger through crippling internet / telecom outages in an attempt to maintain law & order, there remains little to show for what clearly remains a fractured Chattogram ship recycling market today, with very few owners and cash buyers willing to consider Chattogram waters under the current climate, especially with Force Majeure conditions ripe for the picking and the certainty of facing problems upon arrival due to constantly weaker prices on show including ease of L/C issuances, which also remain difficult to ascertain at this time.

Locally, we have seen negotiations at the bidding tables predominantly spearheaded by a couple of Chattogram buyers who have ready L/C limits and are available to conclude units. A wider net of potential buyers does remain available but are handicapped / fearful of committing fresh units due to financial restrictions of their own, the unstable political climate, and the risks associated in opening an L/C on an incoming vessel, all at time when Bangladesh is struggling with even basic communication hiccups, which is resulting in a contraction of business activities and a general uncertainty about the local market. As such, Chattogram Choppers have turned Far Eastern tonnage into a serial dating affair as the regular inflow of petite (small-LDT) Far Eastern ladies continues to keep local port position sufficiently satiated for a short-term (recycling) affair, at least until the next one is ready for delivery. Interestingly, a large LDT tanker also took centre stage at the waterfront.

Finally, as local fundamentals remain shaky weak – the USD ending the week against the Bangladeshi Taka at the same level as close of business last week in addition to local steel plate prices that remain dead on the floor due to a total halt in local trading – they (fundamentals) were the last thing on the minds of local recyclers who are simply trying to stay safe through the violence.

### India

- **Headline**: INDIA…OH INDIA!

India’s ship-recycling sector has turned into the schoolyard kid who’s currently being bullied at the bidding tables via a lacklustre showing on account of an unsatisfactory FY budget, incessant volatility in prevailing fundamentals, and increasing imports of cheaper Chinese steel to compensate for the shortage of ships steel that hasn’t been as readily available to local steel mills over the last year or so, and this continues to cause consternations to buyers in the largest ship-recycling destination in the world.

As prices dither and slip, domestic fundamentals have been key in navigating the current course of Alang’s vessel pricing (as they logically should), one that stands feeble in the face of competition from neighbouring Pakistan, especially if a vessel of a ‘Gadani interest’ perks into the market. Most Alang Recyclers are therefore content to evaluate global and domestic developments rather than throwing themselves into negotiations on the few available units that will more than likely head Pakistan’s way, unless a Reefer or Stainless-Steel unit or HKC only vessel is offered for sale.

Locally, fundamentals have taken centre stage of late seeing how Indian steel plate prices finally halted a week’s long decline that saw about USD 60/Ton wiped off their levels, and mercifully even recorded a minor, yet optimistic bump via a marginal improvement as the week ended. Even the Indian Rupee strengthened nearly 10 basis points against U.S. Dollar ending the week slightly firmer, yet at a record low INR 83.87.

Alang’s anchorage also confirms the ongoing state of affairs as just like their Bangladeshi brethren who have found the inner circle to Far Eastern ladies (geography folks, it’s all it is), Indian ship recyclers have found their “in” with the fishing community who continue to setup the décor for Alang’s anchorage week after week, delivering either another reefer or trawler or even the occasional bulk carrier (at large) that couldn’t have been diverted to Gadani shores at present, and for whatever reason.

On the limp and trying to find its footing post-election & post-budget, the Indian market has one nostril above the waterline, staying afloat off the ration of vessels that the world crises can afford to deliver at this precarious time.

### Pakistan

- **Headline**: WAAAAAH!
- **Key Notes**: *Through the holster.* | *Chinese wrestling.* | *Little hope.*

Despite relevant fundamentals playing a far more impressive role than this markets current ranking placement would seem to indicate, Gadani recyclers continue to place the local mindset through a blender of hopelessness as this week, (comparatively more) satiated buyers managed to navigate local offers to the bottom of the market rankings on the back of a decades old strategy that involved positioning local offers ever so slightly below the competition, while simultaneously ignoring what they can actually afford to pay on the basis of their own fundamentals.

As such, despite having the capacity to be the pricing titans of the ship recycling world, Gadani buyers have elected to let a satiated mindset lead the present without realizing that if the floodgates for tonnage do open, they stand to suffer from the results of a self-inflicted gunshot through the holster, especially from a competing India and especially if Pakistani steel plate prices stay flatlined despite being the industry’s firmest, at USD 735/Ton, which is well over USD 200/Ton above those from India and just about USD 180/Ton above those from Bangladesh. Moreover, the Pakistani Rupee also remained steady at the same levels as close of business last week i.e. PKR 278.46 against the U.S. Dollar, offering a greater sense of stability to local recyclers, who themselves seem less worried about potential L/C restrictions in the country.

Moreover, and similar to Indian recyclers are their Pakistani counterparts who are also facing the recent import onslaught of cheaper Chinese steel that could result in disruptions similar to those being faced in India, but are unable to do so in Pakistan given that Gadani recyclers are confident to snag any vessel of local interest away from neighboring India – for the time being and should such a vessel come around. Moreover, despite the supply of vessels being inadequate, given the amount of tonnage this market has already taken in so far this year and continues to do so (including this week), it is hardly a surprise to see such a laidback attitude from Pakistan over the recent past. Speaking of deliveries, another juicy sized bulk carrier and a small sub-2K LDT bulker were declared at Pakistan’s recycling waterfront this week, as the comparatively smaller Pakistani ship recycling community is clearly in a comfortable place at the present time.

As such, with the global clock ticking ever so closer to war in the neighboring region and nearly 7 Quarters of diminished supply of recycling tonnage being omnipresent, there seems little hope that Gadani will get back to any serious form of buying any time soon, as a lack of interest in seriously competing, and the revival of the perennial complaints highlights the hallmarks of dealing with the Gadani market.
