“Rock bottom eventually becomes nothing more than a state of mind.”
 
 
 
 
D. Kaye 
Activity across the Indian sub-continent ship recycling markets has been climbing on the back of 
recently witnessed agitations in the trading lanes, which continue to ramp up with each passing 
week as a growing number of Ship Owners are biting the recycling bullet & selling their overaged
but still trading vessels. Containers have been the first segment to be squeezed out by this recent 
dip in freight rates as several units were (and continue to be) introduced for a recycling sale.
Moreover, given that much of the miniscule supply of tonnage over recent quarters has been 
focused on the dry and container sectors, which themselves have enjoyed eye-watering highs 
during / post Covid & even more since the escalation of geopolitical crises in 2023, many would 
consider these sectors being long overdue for a clear-out as we continue to witness impressive 
signs of a fervor to acquire that has come ‘dead alive’ at the bidding tables, as well as the various 
waterfronts of late – including Turkey, where vessel prices were seemingly torched by desperate
Aliaga Recyclers who are clearly willing to go above & beyond the boundaries set by domestic 
fundamentals this week; in that 4K – 9K LDT units are reportedly seeing a minimum jump in 
prices of about $20/Ton whereas +10K LDT units are seeing over USD 40/Ton of an increase in 
offerings – seemingly even higher on choice units.  
As such, as recycling prices inch up towards the mid USD 550s/LDT in key sub-continent markets
and even approach USD 400/Ton in Turkey once again, Ship Owners are clearly comfortable at 
these higher overall price points and are finally happy to sell their aged units. Owners of older 
Bulkers – particularly Handy & Panamax units – have also been discretely testing recycling rates 
of late, especially as the Dry Bulk sector has enjoyed its unseasonal surge in rates, one that now 
looks set to recede in the near future. As the wet market also remains bullish and there remains
little danger of seeing an increase in respective units’ head for recycling any time soon, several 
Tankers & FSOs have been delivered into India & Bangladesh of late, as Pakistan’s anchorage 
seems content in slowing down given that this market is just a ship shy of tallying its 2023 volume.
Overall, Bangladesh remains the primary beneficiary of units across recent times as Owners 
continue to hope to reach / breach the magical USD 600/LDT mark, and India remains close on the 
pricing heels of the market leaders, even taking Silver on the back of firming fundamentals and 
finally sneaking into 2nd place in the market rankings this week. Lastly, after a brief period of strong
performance, Pakistan has once again receded into the background, on account of largely 
impractical numbers on any of the geographically well-positioned units opening in the West.

- MARKET COMMENTARY -   
DEAD ALIVE!!!

BANGLADESH 
‘ PICKY WHILE STARVED ‘ 
Despite Bangladeshi Recyclers becoming extremely fussy on available tonnage at a level
historically unseen, turning down vessels not only due to the units age and country of 
build, but more importantly, on account of the overall condition of vessels as nothing
other than a fleet of laid up ole’ rusties has been greeting the nation’s waterfront, ever 
since Bangladeshi offerings climbed well into the USD 500s/LT LDT.  
As a result, the industry has seen many of these units not only face steep discounts (much 
to the chagrin of respective Owners), but a growing number of these units are also 
increasingly failing to generate the least bit of interest from Chattogram Choppers, who
remain content to wait for the rarer, better-quality unit to come around, rather than have 
to face the all-but-certain migraine machine that comes part-and-parcel with recycling 
contracts on these older laid-up units. 
Therefore, in line with prevailing market conditions i.e., Chattogram Choppers being 
‘picky while starved’, and given that even these poorer condition units still need a 
recycling future, albeit at an aforementioned discounted one, the markets saw the
Chinese controlled Bulker XIN LV BAO SHI (7,844 LDT) fetch a ‘surprisingly decent’
USD 520/LT, in the only market sale of the week. We mention ‘surprisingly decent’ on 
account of the fact that good quality units were seeing such levels until only a few weeks 
ago. Notwithstanding, as the nation is still atop the recycling market rankings week after 
week, the Bangladeshi waterfront remains the IMAX presentation of a return to recycling
form, as a healthy collection of private, small(ish) LDT, Containers & General Cargo 
vessels have arrived local anchorage just this week, including another large LDT tanker. 
Domestic fundamentals also remain in a state of volatile stillness as local steel plate prices 
and the Bangladeshi Taka conjointly start & even end the week at the same levels on an 
ongoing basis, in that, the Taka dune-bashes between BDT 108.90 to BDT 109.98 against
the U.S. Dollar where it has been closing out the working week on an ongoing basis, and 
domestic steel prices continue to flatline at levels that still support local offerings. This
ongoing plate paralysis may prove troublesome to Bangladesh’s (recently predicted) 
performance rather sooner than later, as neighbouring India starts to firm on the back of 
its own improving plate prices and is clearly grabbing at the heels of local levels.  
Nonetheless, we do feel confident about the near-future performance of this market, 
especially for decent units coming in from the Far East, and out of the pricing-reach of 
Indian / Pakistani Recyclers.

INDIA 
Lift-off. 
PUZZLING PONDERANCES! 
As we have puzzlingly pondered about the performance of the Indian Ship Recycling 
sector since the start of the year, wondering just why this market has been unable to 
leapfrog ahead of the competition on account of its comparatively (than Bangladesh & 
Pakistan’s) stable economy, decent domestic fundamentals / local steel plate prices that 
have far from flatlined, or even unheard of L/C restrictions that could have arrested the 
ability of Alang Recyclers at the bidding tables for about a year, the one thing this market 
has failed to deliver to date, is its competitive performance as the industry has been 
anticipating a lift-off to this market since the start of the year – a moment that seems to 
have mercifully arrived over the last couple of weeks. 
Elections are currently underway in India with the results being announced on June 2nd 
and election fever certainly seems to have ignited the nation’s economy as the Indian 
Rupee shakily gained ground against the U.S. Dollar, ending the week at levels 
approaching Rs. 83.30, in addition to an impressive boost in local steel plate prices that 
saw levels firm by about USD 13/Ton this week.  
Firming steel plate prices also seem to be offering a larger buffer for domestic Recyclers 
to consider when offering, in that, vessel prices immediately saw a firming this week,
one which resulted in India dethroning Pakistan for 2nd place and reporting a massive 
jump in LDT imported into Alang, with about 55K LDT via just 6 ships reported at 
Alang’s anchorage / on the beach vs. 66K LDT via 11 vessels at Chattogram, as the 
industry witnesses an impressive spread of LDTs and vessel types in India’s recycling 
waterways this week.   
This firming in Indian levels also did not go unnoticed, such that the German built 
PROFESSOR B (5,040 LDT) was introduced and swiftly managed to fetch a decent USD 
545/LT LDT, besting their Pakistani counterparts for a decent LDT market sale this week, 
and more of this performance expected to come in the near future.  
Overall, as Indian Buyers finally get back on the sales board, with more likely to follow 
(particularly HKC only containers), it remains a matter of time until India passes their 
Bangladeshi counterparts once again and assumes pole position atop the ship recycling 
market rankings once again.

June 2nd. 
Pole position.

SATIATED & SLOW! 
An expectedly slow week that the industry saw coming over the last couple of weeks finally 
witnessed Gadani Recyclers inch a vessel closer & a vessel shy of 2023s total ship recycling 
volume, as local offerings remain steady amidst local steel plate prices that continue to 
flatline, and a Pakistani Rupee that witnessed its own share of dune-bashing this week, as
levels jumped repeatedly between PKR 278 to PKR 278.50 and back down to PKR 278.13 
against the U.S. Dollar, where it ended finally the week.  
Moreover, with beaches & bellies full on account of recent deliveries & fundamentals that 
are offering no impetus for the Gadani Recyclers to firm up their levels any further, the 
Pakistani market finally sank to the bottom of the sub-continent market rankings this week,
as India enters the fore and is increasingly demanding the attention of Ship Owners and 
Cash Buyers of all units opening West of Sri Lanka.  
Nevertheless, the industry continues to witness the occasional fixture into this market as 
another decent LDT, General Cargo unit managed to find its permanent resting place at a 
domestic yard in Pakistan.  
As yet another week of muted activity (one of many to come) wraps up amidst firming 
competition and as the Pakistani market further slips into an (expectedly) early monsoon 
slumber, unless a one-off local Recyclers jumps back into the fray on a particular unit, we 
do not anticipate much activity from the shores of Gadani in the coming week(s), especially 
as India is increasingly expected to slip into overdrive post June 2nd and Bangladeshi levels 
are still firming as time goes on.

BA-ZZZING! 
From depressed to dashing, is what has been the performance of the Turkish market in the 
span of a week, which certainly is nothing short of spectacular and especially since we 
have not witnessed this impressive a jump in some time now. 
Despite no movements being reported in local & import steel prices, in addition to a Lira 
that, much like the sub-continent currencies, weakened past TRY 32.52 once again, only to 
firm and end the week at region TRY 32.30 against the U.S. Dollar, a collection of Aliaga 
Recyclers has suddenly woken from a deep slumber and ramped up local offerings to the 
max as not only have vessel offers jumped to the tune of USD 40/Ton on +10K LDT units, 
but these levels are also seemingly conservative as Owners could well witness levels 
approaching the magical USD 4XX/MT for the right unit – so eager have a few local 
Recyclers become to acquire vessels for their emptying yards. 
On account of these firming levels, news of a tiny 4K LDT container fetching levels north 
of USD 380/MT via a reportedly private sale has certainly caught the eye of Owners as the 
ongoing introduction of potential units into Aliaga continues on. 
As such, we certainly hope the Turkish market enjoys a refreshing sprinkling of tonnage 
this month and Aliaga anchorage enjoys a much-needed inflow of tonnage.
