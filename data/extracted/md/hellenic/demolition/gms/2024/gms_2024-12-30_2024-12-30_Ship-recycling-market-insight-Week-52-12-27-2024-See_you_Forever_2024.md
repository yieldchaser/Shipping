As a year of turmoil & global economic tumult winds down on a quiet note as has historically been 
the case for the week between Christmas and New Year, the world silently turns its way to 
anticipating a 2025 that is bearing all the signs of a comparatively busier period across all sectors. 
For week 52 however, it was a different story as not only did global trade ease this week, but 
economies also took a breather against the raging U.S. Dollar as well, as most ship recycling nation 
currencies seemed to irrationally display the same amount of instability this week, resulting in a 
quieter port position in both India and Bangladesh. Moreover, and shockingly of all, despite 
apparently performing off the charts for a majority of the year, the Baltic Dry Exchange Dry Bulk 
Index slid so rapidly over Q4 2024, it recorded an overall 52.5% annual decline, the worst since 2014. 
Sectors such as Capesize index fell 66.7%, Panamax index fell 48.7%, and even Supramaxes saw a 
32% decline across the year. While the wet sector continues to sprinkle its share of tonnage after 
quite a hiatus, the supply of recycling vessels heading into 2025 is certainly gearing up for a seeming 
uptick. The overall declining Baltic Exchange also saw oil prices relaxing around the USD 70.6 / 
barrel for the week as well, as non-OPEC+ countries are expected to drive up output despite the 
dithering Chinese demand, where China today is the largest importer of oil in the world.   
2024 has also marked itself as a year of noteworthy transitions and development across international 
recycling markets, as a recognition to upgrade facilities and officiate the HKC in the respective 
Indian sub-continent ship recycling destinations highlights the willingness of these governments to 
undertake the business of ship recycling, in a broader and internationally accepted standards on 
safety and environmental safety and 2025 should hopefully see Pakistan and Bangladesh ramp up 
yard upgrades to HKC standards ahead of its entry into force from the middle of next year. Local 
steel plate prices have been through the worst of times falling over USD 150/Ton from the highs 
seen earlier in the year, until the close of this week where both Indian and Pakistani steel plate prices 
reported further declines whilst Bangladesh and China flatlined this week. 
The Supply of tonnage has seen a year of record low recycling volumes, and yards are certainly 
yearning for busier days ahead, as freight markets increasingly start to divert more candidates for 
recycling, after a year of glorious charter rates across all sectors. Recycling prices have themselves 
seen significant declines over the course of the year, from a high of USD 600/LDT during Q1, right 
down to USD 450s/LDT come Q4 2025. This has kept most recycling yards suspended in a state of 
limbo as a permanent shut down would be far more expensive than a temporary operational 
closure, which several yards across the Indian sub-continent and even Turkey, have reported 
through the year. Political instability has been rife across the sub-continent, adding to the inability 
of these economies to stabilize enough and match their offers with the volatility of the U.S. Dollar. 
In India and Bangladesh, PM Modi failed to win the required majority during the India’s 2024 
election and PM Sheikh Hasina fled Bangladesh after an insurrection that lead to an interim military 
government taking charge, resulting in infrastructure projects being put on hold. President Trump’s 
second term will also be another test for global nations as looming tariffs only stand to spell disaster. 
Overall, an unprecedented year in recycling markets is therefore hoping to recover, with increased 
volumes, more regulations, and certainly a shade of volatility. Here we come 2025!

SLOOOW! 
A truly transformative year for Bangladesh has finally settled in the last two months of this year as
end buyers are gradually starting to look toward 2025 with a greater sense of optimism & hope for 
the country’s ship recycling sector, and the nation overall. After initially resisting the IMF’s 
assistance, a couple of years ago when the country eventually succumbed to apply for a bailout 
package, coupled with poor fiscal policies and an oligarchic control of the previous government 
that resulted in interest rates rising, exports declining, and the government dipping ever deeper 
into the nation’s reserves to balance out the needs of the populous.  
The resulting economic collapse coupled with the implementation of unpopular government plans 
saw violent outbreaks that saw thousands dead, a Prime Minister flee the country, and weather 
patterns batter the nation through an incessant monsoon that bought domestic construction 
projects to a complete halt. However, not all is down the tubes and into the ocean. Bangladesh’s 
upcoming budget for FY 2025 - 2026 promises greater economic stability and is expected to be about 
6.5% higher than the current one. While the budget is concentrating on health and education 
sectors, notices were surprisingly issued to curtail any major construction projects, at least for the 
time being.  
This is certainly going to hit Chattogram Choppers in the welding torches as the ongoing dithered 
volume in the trading of ship’s steel has seen demand from Bangladeshi steel mills come to a 
grinding halt, causing local steel plate prices to collapse in sharp declines throughout the year, to 
where they have now set the ceiling price for the Bangladeshi ship recycling fraternity. And this 
week was no different, where steel levels remained dead on the floor at a weakened USD 521/Ton. 
The collapsing Bangladeshi Taka too has done little to entertain the pricing comfort-zone from this 
market as the Taka continues to trade at a famished BDT 119.99 against the U.S. Dollar, after briefly 
spiking to BDT 120.05 mid-week. 
The lack of demand of recycled ship’s steel from domestic steel mills continues to hamper demand 
and the negotiating mindset of local recyclers at the bidding tables, who only welcomed a tiny sub 
2K LDT tanker at the nation’s waterfront this week, earmarking the true state of Bangladeshi affairs.
As impending sanctions stand to see an influx of wet tonnage from certain regions of the world, 
most of which will likely end up in India due to L/C values on the large LDT tonnage, but there 
will be more of those favoured larger LDT dry vessels for recycling sale next year, especially given 
the performance of the Baltic Dry Exchange of late, and a prolonged absence of juicy units this year
on account of consistently impressive freight rates. 
As the deadline for compliance with HKC standards gradually nearing and only 4 ship recycling
yards in Chattogram have received their NK HKC accreditations, the remaining yards will need to 
gear up and implement the necessary infrastructure upgrades over the course of this year, as the 
convention is finally enters into force in 2025.

CONSIDERABLE CONSTERNATION! 
Alang, like the rest of the sub-continent ship recycling industry, has endured a tumultuous year of 
declining prices, economic collapse, political shock, a declining Rupee, and all of it, swimming in a 
sea of the lowest ship recycling volumes seen in over a decade, resulting in an increasingly Buyers`
market that is compelling ship owners and cash buyers to finally accept current day realities on 
vessel prices. This has increasingly seen Alang’s waterfront get busier over recent weeks, as a 
healthy chunk of recent introductions into the market seemed to find themselves with navigation 
charts towards WC India.  
Cheap Chinese steel has caused considerable consternation, and the recently renewed alliance of 
ruling parties India has finally announced upcoming tariffs on incoming steel from China that are 
expected to range anywhere between 10% - 15%. The imposition of further tariffs would on the one 
hand, help it compete more effectively with Indian steel thereby driving more business towards 
Alang’s ship recycling sector. The flip side would not only see Chinese steel demand start to dither, 
but a possible redistribution of excess and resultingly cheaper inventories may garner further 
business on the trading front, possibly having a counter-effect on the supply of tonnage for recycling 
during this time. 
Notwithstanding, the steady volatility in steel plate prices have seen levels decline by over USD 
150/Ton, bringing down vessel prices by the same amount over the span of 2024. With the sudden 
new lows of USD 450/Ton being presented on vessels, it has taken some time for the ship owning 
and cash buying fraternities to finally adjust to the ongoing offerings from ship recyclers and resume 
negotiations in kind. With tonnage supply set to firm and hungry recyclers worldwide welcoming 
the potential increase, steel plate price and U.S. Dollar concerns continue to keep local sentiments 
in check as not only did plate levels drop by a couple of Dollars this week, but even the Rupee ended 
at a disastrous Rs. 85.40 against the U.S. Dollar.  
Local port positions too were left empty this week, with nothing more than deliveries of prior 
arrivals, and no fresh deliveries this week, resulting in India potentially suffering a possible empty
report next week? 
Finally, as HKC convention gears up to head into force this year and a majority of yards in Alang 
already HKC approved, India seems well geared to take on the influx next year. 
Rough year.

PAKISTAN 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
STRUGGLING WHILE DISINTERESTED! 
Pakistan, like its subcontinent counterparts, has struggled through a year of economic hardships, 
a PKR that also collapsed this year, shortage of U.S. Dollars that has practically halted its trading 
and restricted its availability via lack of available L/Cs for local recyclers who were already being 
crippled by the ongoing shortage of tonnage and a far more competitive India of late, the nation 
has struggled through all of 2024 and ending it with the 2nd lowest volume of tonnage recycled 
over the last decade. 
As pending IMF loans and those from Saudi Arabia still hang in the balance, there is little brewing
locally to ignite any meaningful discussions with Gadani recycles at the bidding tables, a trend 
that has been painfully prevalent for a majority of the year and especially Q4 this year, where 
nearly 3 months of an empty port position highlight the true state of not only Gadani’s ship 
recycling sector, but even the nation on the whole. And regrettably, this trend is likely expected to 
continue well into Q1 2025 as economic stability is yet to get back on stable footing. 
Fundamentals are doing little to help Gadani ship recyclers especially as the collapse of local steel 
plate prices continues again for the 2nd week this month and plate fell by another USD 7/Ton down 
to USD 639/Ton this week, but it was the depreciated Pakistani Rupee that fell 30 basis points to 
end the week at USD 639/Ton, further ensuring Gadani’s deliberate lack of participation at ongoing 
tonnage offerings.  
Moreover, as Bangladesh and India remain well ahead from Pakistan in terms of demand and 
pricing, both markets have already made considerable efforts and investments in upgrading their 
facilities up to HKC standards ahead of its entry into force by mid next year, while Pakistani 
recyclers are yet to initiate any yard upgrades whatsoever, severely diminishing the ability of local 
recyclers to prepare for the convention coming into force in the next 6 short months. 
As such, questions must be asked surrounding the viability of any serious business for the Gadani 
ship recycling market, whilst pricing, pending yard infrastructure upgrades, weak fundamentals, 
and a struggling economy still remain so woefully off the mark.
