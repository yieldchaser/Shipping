GLIMMER OF LIFE? 
As we stand on the eve of Week 31 this 2025, global market turmoil continues unimpeded as wars 
rage, freight markets firm, economies flutter, and oil, currencies, and steel prices all fluctuate in a
wavering tandem, delivering headaches along the way for all on this ride. While Trump announces 
further deals with Japan and the EU late on Sunday, his sketchy past is what keeps hammering this
administration down, which itself is scrambling to do damage control vs. attempting to fix the state 
of the U.S. economy that has seen the U.S. Dollar weakening against key global competitors, whilst
strengthening against virtually all recycling destinations. U.S. inflation meanwhile climbs from 2.4% 
to 2.7% in June as Turkey, India, Bangladesh, and even Pakistan all report declines in their respective 
inflations. Oil futures also declined 1.3% to settle at USD 65.2 / barrel on the back of said U.S. trade 
deals, in addition to concerns of an oversupply of oil as Trump announces the possibility of easing 
sanctions against Venezuela that could add a reported 200,00 barrels of oil to global supply-chain.
The Baltic Exchange’s main sea freight index meanwhile reported another improvement this week 
with the Cape index surging 1% of its total annual value via a 24% gain just this week, all while the 
Panamax and Supramax indices confoundingly fell 2.3% & 0.3% respectively. 
Notwithstanding, glimmers of life seem to be emerging from the Indian sub-continent ship recycling 
markets as offers on the large(r) LDT LNGs making the rounds (including various other types of 
tonnage) surpassed most expectations this week. But what remained the most impressive (and as
forecasted in weeks prior), Alang recyclers have been the ones to take in virtually all of the market 
(and private) fixtures as Alang’s anchorage was ‘fully loaded’ this week. This may be a sign that the
markets have likely bottomed out, but the situation may remain in a state of statis until the end of 
next week before any movements can be expected from any of the sub-continent markets, especially
after the recent and significant decline of about USD 60/LDT in residual values from the highs of
USD 400s/LDT seen at the start of the year, including the massive USD 600/LDT seen just 12 months 
prior. Moreover, we have also seen prices decline below USD 400/LDT during the historically slower 
monsoon / summer months as several deals are still being fixed at those levels in 2025 as well.  
Finally, while the adoption of the Hong Kong Convention (HKC) for recycling has been one of the 
biggest changes (and challenges) in the history of ship recycling, it has also delivered a healthy dose
of uncertainty surrounding the (changing) regulations and increased documentary requirements
filtering through to the various waterfronts, with much infrastructure catch-up work still to be
completed in both Bangladesh and (especially) Pakistan before the entire sub-continent falls in line 
with all of the HKCs requirements. Although a trickle of market candidates (aside from a plethora 
of large LDT LNG) candidates do seem to be making the rounds, supply concerns overall present 
challenges for recyclers as freight markets firm against the backdrop of pending tariff uncertainties
once again. Yet, it is expected to a busier Q4  this year and once HKC formalities and requirements 
are more ingrained after yard upgrades are completed, there is a chance we may see activity in the
sub-continent fire up once again.

REALITY BITES! 
The Bangladeshi market still seems to be struggling with the adoption of the HKC and the resulting 
yard upgrades, yard inspections, class ratifications, ministry approvals, and ensuing documentary 
requirements seem to be collectively presenting a much large hurdle for ship owners, cash buyers, 
and ship recyclers far more than previously anticipated. As such, most local recyclers who have 
imported tonnage pre-HKC adoption seem to have temporarily satisfied their demand. Meanwhile, 
recycled ship’s steel / steel product is still struggling to move from local recycling yards to domestic 
steel mills due to failure to initiate building / infrastructure projects by the frustratingly slow and 
lethargic interim government that has been in control for over a year now. And as the remainder 
of the marginal number of units at anchorage were delivered to their respective buyers this week, 
no fresh units have arrived locally, a forecasted reality for this market, especially in the short run. 
Interestingly, most of the recent recycling sales including large LDT LNGs and even dry bulk units 
have bypassed Bangladeshi shores over the last month or so, onto the far more HKC-stable Indian 
market where prices remain ahead of Chattogram’s offerings, which don’t look like they have any 
potential to mark any improvements anytime soon. The Bangladeshi Taka has meanwhile resumed 
its fall against the U.S. Dollar once again, as it declined 65-basis points this week down to BDT 
122.18. Even local steel plate prices were not spared from the brunt of recent steel fixtures by 
domestic steel mills at region USD 330/Ton, resulting in plate prices retreating nearly USD 2/Ton 
this week.  
Is this a sign of times for Bangladeshi vessel offerings through August as it has been through June 
thus far, with competing markets overshadowing the performance of Chattogram Choppers? It 
would certainly seem that way given that previous high-priced purchases are still languishing at 
local yards as local authorities have reportedly caused unexpected delays in granting cutting 
permissions amidst all of the new HKC requirements and ensuing red-tape between the various 
government departments. 
The resulting delays in cutting / recycling of vessels and ensuing inability to resell recycled steel 
will stand to stimy the ability of local ship recyclers to even hold a chair at the bidding tables if this 
trend continues. And the aforementioned decline in the Taka was enough for the central bank to 
step in and stall the decline through monetary means. Just how long these controls hold remains 
to be seen given that growth projections for the country remain weak despite domestic inflation 
falling to 8.48% in June (down from nearly 11% at the start of the year), but could likely start to rise 
on the back of looming tariffs that stand to disrupt the economy overall as the year progresses 
Overall, other than one off sale of a juicy unit to an HKC approved yard, we do not anticipate much 
from Bangladesh in the coming week(s).

INDIA 
 
LNG KINGS! 
After a series of Moss type LNG sales confirmed to local recyclers, it could be comfortably considered 
that India is at least getting back to a modicum of form this week, after what has been an extremely 
lacklustre few months of price declines and lethargic financial inertia that has seen this market at the 
2nd and 3rd place on the market rankings a lot more than as historically has been the case.  
And this week’s activity was certainly a welcome surprise given that two large LDT LNG carriers were
successfully committed to Alang buyers as ADNOC controlled GHASHA (33,406 LDT) and AL 
KHAZNAH (33,456 LDT) were both fixed on an en bloc basis, for an extremely firm USD 42.9 million 
(about USD 641.50/LDT) basis an ‘as is’ Malaysia delivery – the strong price being attributed to the 
large quantities of non-ferrous (including significant aluminium) on board these Moss type LNGS. 
India has certainly been the most sophisticated non-ferrous metals markets to trade with, even when
they are lower placed against both their Pakistani and Bangladeshi competitors, making them the go-
to market for stainless steel tankers, Moss type LNGs, and similar other non-ferrous rich units. 
And this return to form was also hard to be missed as Alang’s waterfront remained a sight to behold 
this week, with 11 vessels (including a large LDT tanker) and over 107,000 tons of recycling tonnage 
having arrived the waterfront and a majority being delivered to their final owners. And this trend is 
likely expected to continue as Pakistani yards are still grappling with yard upgrades across the board 
and there has not been much positive news from the remainder of the Bangladeshi non-HKC yards 
that still have their works ongoing. 
Local fundamentals too offer some hope as despite the Indian Rupee reacting like its sub-continent 
brethren against U.S. Dollar that saw it falling 40 basis points to Rs. 86.48, it was local steel plate prices 
that offered some Aspirin to local sentiments as levels reported firmed nearly USD 4/Ton this week 
and back to USD 432, offering an increased buffer on margins, especially on some of these recent and 
expensive deliveries. Moreover, with trade projections expected to grow in excess of 6.5% in 2025 on 
the back of increased domestic investments backed by trade deals concluded with the U.K. and a 2nd 
round of FTA talks with New Zealand that look increasingly promising, India could be in the limelight 
for the remainder of the year. Finally, Trump’s growing assertions of reaching an agreement with India 
could also further the overall trajectory of the nation’s economy. 
Overall, India will likely remain the go-to ship recycling destination through early August, perhaps all 
of it, making it a promising month for owners looking for prompt and hassle-free recycling-relief. 
Promising August.

PAKISTAN
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
    
SECURING SALES? 
The Pakistani ship recycling market remains the best placed for yet another week and was even 
able to secure of series of bulkers this week, reportedly with significant quantities of bunkers
remaining on board at the time of arrival. These sales are particularly interesting given that Gadani 
recyclers have traditionally been a selective group who tend not to veer too far left from the types 
of tonnage they have historically been comfortable recycling – and non-ferrous, Moss type LNGs,
LPGs, and the likes have not made the cut for local recyclers, more often than not.  Accordingly,
markets witnessed both the RISING FALCON (5,694 LDT) and the RISING EAGLE (5,440 LDT) 
fixed en bloc to cash buyers` basis a likely Pakistan redelivery, at a decent USD 446/LT LDT 
including about 250 - 300 Tons of bunkers onboard at the time of delivery, contributing to almost
USD 20/LDT to the purchase price of the vessel.  
With an increasing number of candidates expected to enter the markets in the fourth quarter of the 
year (sounds like 2024 wishes all over again) and as government bodies and end buyers finally
start taking their respective HKC infrastructure upgrades seriously (including authorities issuing 
provisional DASR certificates to those yards that have committed to, and made notable progress 
at upgrade works to their yards), it is certainly a pleasure to have a competing Pakistan back at the 
bidding tables – albeit, like their Bangladeshi counterparts, restricted to a limited a number of local 
recyclers. 
Domestic fundamentals aren’t doing this market any favors at this time either as on the one hand,
local steel plate prices stay marooned at region USD 615/Ton (still the highest in the industry), with 
no major market sales (of late) to take advantage of these impressive margins, while on the other 
hand, the Pakistani Rupee made a surprise bounce, firming about 55 basis points and end the week 
at PKR 283.20 against the U.S. Dollar. While certainly not a return to the saner days under PKR 
280, it is hopefully firm motivation enough for the rest of the recycling community to join in their 
HKC efforts. And while Pakistan’s economy and domestic stock exchange has faced increasing 
volatility over the recent past post-operation Sindoor, most businesses and even the government
are wary of the way ahead with neighboring India. 
Notwithstanding, Gadani is certainly a destination to keep an eye out on, especially for owners 
looking to retire a dry unit quick in August.

UNCHANGED? 
 
While on the one hand, Aliaga’s anchorages have been comparatively deserted for the most part of 
2025 leaving the country’s domestic ship recycling sector in “Airplane mode”, on the other hand, the 
ongoing decline in Turkey’s inflation rate has seen a drop from 42.12% back in January down to 
35.15% back in June, which has reportedly resulted in the Turkish central bank favorably re-adjusting 
national interest / lending rates – a promising sign for domestic businesses in the short run.  
But it has been the Lira that has been unable to escape its weekly badgering as it was reportedly 
subjected to another 50-basis point decline this week as well, ending it at TRY 40.56 against the U.S. 
Dollar. Import and local steel plate prices aren’t doing very well either as they remain relatively 
unchanged and subdued over recent weeks. 
Mercifully, the benchmark interest rate saw a 300-basis point reduction to 43% and might hopefully 
fuel the purchase of some of the cheaper, smaller, and regional end-of-life ships. 
Weekly badgering.
