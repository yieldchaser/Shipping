### Editorial: DISCONNECT?

Global markets seem to be going through quite a disconnect of late as on the one hand, the last couple of months has seen a multitude of directions that stock markets, trading markets, charter rates, financial and FX markets, and even the ship recycling markets have been greeted by versions of unfolding realities that seem to conflict with what’s been transpiring at the bidding tables of late. On the one hand, various reports have been consistently confirming positive movements in dry / trading markets, oil up and down (with more down expected), financial markets wreaking havoc on the global ship recycling industry, and ship recycling volumes seemingly improving over recent weeks (primarily in India). And this week was another roller coaster of confusion.

The Baltic Exchange Dry Bulk Index fell nearly 7 points on Friday as Capes, Panamax, and Supramax indices all slipped 14, 3, and 4 points respectively. Yet, the overall benchmark reported a net 2.5% positive gain for the entire sector, driven primarily by the strength of Capes that reportedly firmed another 5.5%. On the flip side, WTI crude slipped 1% down to USD 65 / barrel as Iraq’s Kurdistan region reportedly resuming crude oil exports after a 2.5-year hiatus, adding a near 190K barrel / day output that is expected to climb to 230K bpd. The restart pushed by U.S. pressure comes as OPEC+ eyes further outputs while risks to Russian supplies linger amidst their war of attrition.

The net trickledown effect of the above coupled with a quick stop at sanctions stations has seen ship recycling nation currencies unexpectedly take a royal hammering this week as Bangladesh, India and Turkey all reported declines in their currencies once again, while local steel plate prices flatlined across the board, expect in India. Ongoing macro market movements have also seen a degree of sporadic buying return to the ship recycling markets from certain needlessly optimistic cash buyers, which has punctuated what has been an overall gloomy 2025 for the ship recycling scene where supply, demand, and pricing have all suffered tragically.

In Bangladesh, recycled product is simply refusing to shift from frustrated end buyer yards (much as we have seen for a majority of the year), leaving the overall ship recycling scene in Chattogram lost at high seas. Pakistan, and surprisingly even India, appear to be the only bright spots over recent times with an increased demand and higher indicative pricing of late, even though there is still much in terms of HKC infrastructure upgrades left to accomplish in Gadani, given that not a single ship recycling facility is HKC ready here. And Gadani buyers have been left relying on provisional DASR certificates issued by local authorities in order to continue doing business.

As Fall gets fully under way with September 2025 entering its forever slumber next week, dimly lit hopes lie in wait for brighter times ahead, for a beleaguered ship recycling industry.

## 3. Country Market Intelligence

### Bangladesh

- **Headline**: BYPASSED!
- **Key Notes**: *Quietest.* | *Failing to secure.* | *18 yards HKC.* | *Lucky one.*

Bangladesh remains the quietest sub-continent ship recycling destination for yet another week and there are few signs that the pervading situation is likely to improve any time soon given that the overall state of affairs (as reported over recent weeks) has simply not changed an iota. Minimal recycled ship’s steel continues failing to shift from local yards due to the lack of development stimuli implemented by the interim regime (ahead of upcoming elections scheduled for February 2026).

Fundamentals too have stifled on the side of misalignment as not only did local steel plate prices fail to move - indicating that local recyclers are steadfast at current levels and refusing to buckle to the pressures of cheaper international steel imports, but the currency also started to groan like a retiree in hospice as the Taka ended the week on a lower note at BDT 122.04 against the U.S. Dollar.

And while tonnage availability has not been as big of an issue as it has been for much of the year, the state of the chartering markets has left nothing more than a healthy collection of small(er) LDT units being proposed at the bidding tables, which have remained beyond the grasps of local interests as most local buyers remain on the lookout for larger LDT units such as Capes / Panamax bulkers, which remain few and far between. Even worse? The rare few that are being proposed are being diverted towards competing India and likely even Pakistan (this week), so poor is the Bangladeshi sentiment at present.

HKC status too remains unchanged for the most part as from the last week’s count, there are still only 18 HKC compliant local facilities that are in operation, with a handful still struggling to complete their accreditation journeys. Reportedly, another 3 are set to be approved in October as Bangladesh continues to make meaningful progress with infrastructure upgrades, following the landmark entry of the Hong Kong Convention into force on June 26th.

At the local waterfront, at least one decent 7K LDT tanker made it into the hands of a local recycler as Chattogram’s waterfront continues to suffer week after week, virtually mirroring Pakistan’s recent performance. As advised in recent reports, unless you’re a lucky one with a large unit or simply don’t want to head further West, then only then is Chattogram your best bet.

### India

- **Headline**: TURBULENT TIMES!
- **Key Notes**: *Vessels diverted.* | *Container sold.* | *Fundamentals fail.*

India has remained on the turbulent side of economic uncertainties over the last few weeks as Rupee woes and steel declines marred Alang’s ship recycling landscape over recent weeks, delivering a collection of gut punches to an already fragile sentiment. Not only has the Indian Rupee started its descent towards glooming uncertainty, but it also backed it up by declining a massive 55 basis points against the U.S. Dollar this week, ending it at INR 88.62. Of note, this was an improved ending to the Rupee’s performance as it briefly broke all time historical records this week approaching dangerously close to INR 89.

On the other side of the fundamental’s coin, after weeks of flatlining like its neighbours, Indian steel plate prices slid a whopping USD 15/Ton and end the week it at USD 409.20/Ton, well below recent offerings and even fixtures. Given that at a time some cash buyers are feeding off a misguided interpretation by relating the recent inflow of tonnage to the possibility of an upcoming market improvement, it is simply not the case. Moreover, while some recent sales have also tickled the limits of prevailing levels, it may be that the owners of these units could well face problems upon arrival at Alang should the Rupee’s and steel’s performance continue migrating South for the winter months.

Resultingly, it was of little surprise to see active negotiations on several vessels slow down amidst the disastrous decline in fundamentals this week, with reports of some working vessels being potentially diverted to competing markets (i.e. Pakistan), so terribly has the local situation deteriorated in India. In fact, there were two dry bulk sales into Pakistan to report and only one small tanker and one container into India, and that too at a curiously high and speculative level from the concerned cash buyer as Sinokor sold their 1997 Netherlands built (with steel propeller) NIIGATA TRADER (4,810 LDT) at USD 480/LT LDT with 120 Tons of bunkers ROB – certainly not a viable or realistic price today based on local fundamentals.

As the INR continues down this disastrous and historical spiral since President Trump launched his traction-less sanction action on the world along with maddening 50% tariffs on India for their tolerance of and business dealings with Russia on energy trade, Q4 might just turn into a Pakistan only game. This week, however, Alang simultaneously surprised with fresh tonnage at the waterfront and another cape arriving locally.

### Pakistan

- **Headline**: BAGGING BULK!
- **Key Notes**: *Over the line.* | *Hmax sales.* | *Working hard.*

After nearly a month on the sidelines with empty port positions by the week, deals finally appear to have been concluded into a resurgent (and hungry) Gadani market this week, with several bulkers being reportedly sold to local recyclers including a unit likely inbound all the way from Indonesia, bypassing the traditionally hungrier Bangladesh and even Indian markets.

To start, the RISING HARRIER 8,600 LDT) was concluded for a decent USD 445/LT LDT from Middle East owners (with 150 Tons of bunkers included in the sale), whilst the PUTERI KIRANA (8,082 LDT) managed to fetch a fine USD 390/LT LDT basis an ‘as is’ Surabaya delivery and likely Gadani resale, wrapping up the two handy sales for the week.

Local port position also surprised the industry with at least one (but small LDT) handy finding its destination to a local recycler’s yard. Clearly, these DASR certifications are coming in at a hefty cost as this sub 3K LDT fixture highlights the growing desperation of local recyclers.

Fundamentals meanwhile continue to support the local mindset as not only have local steel plate prices continued to dominate the competing sub-continent steel charts for several months now, but they also remain flatlined at levels strong enough that seem determined to trudge through Trump’s uneducated business moves. The Pakistani Rupee also firmed a healthy 30 basis points against the U.S. Dollar this week to end it at PKR 282.50.

At the macro end, Pakistan is also idling with tariffs at a far lower 20% compared to Bangladesh’s 30% and 50% on India, making it increasingly seem as though the lack of HKC fervor was joined by local inaction towards a timely initiation of upgrades that have ended up partying hard into the night and created the tonnage hangover that the Gadani Gang continues to endure. And while the local buyers continue to work hard at getting their facilities up to HKC standards, the number of recyclers willing to negotiate on a majority of the market offerings, remains in the air.
