This week, the world unraveled further into chaos as India and Pakistan entered a 3-day armed 
conflict that saw an equal measure of casualties on both sides and Indian missiles & drones targeted 
key defense targets deep inside Pakistan, including Pakistani air force installations, surface-to-air 
missile systems, and targets uncomfortably close to Pakistan’s nuclear facilities & command centers,
in a show of force that reportedly resulted in urgent calls from senior Pakistani officials to their
Indian counterparts requesting an immediate cease-fire from both sides. Subsequently, reports 
surfaced that the United States threatened to withhold the upcoming USD 1.3 Billion IMF aid-
package to Pakistan that was due for release on May 9th, which Indian officials contested against on 
account of ongoing misappropriations by the government of Pakistan, which India claims is being 
used to fund cross-border terrorism, including how USD 440 Million from the last tranche remains 
unaccounted for and still under IMF investigations. Pursuant to the ceasefire, Pres. Zelenskyy seized
the opportunity and urged for a Russia-Ukraine ceasefire, even proposing a meet with Putin in 
Istanbul this coming Thursday, in an effort to stop the killing.  
In the meantime, Team Trump continues to steamroll the U.S. Economy and global trade volumes
as reportedly on Friday, not a single cargo ship had departed Chinese ports for the U.S. markets, in 
troubling signs of what is about to unfold as this is the first time the U.S. has encountered such a 
situation since the worst days of Covid-19 closures. As ongoing volumes of international trade start
to dissolve, the Baltic Exchange Dry Index reported a 1.3% drop with both Capes & Panamax indices 
falling 2.5% & 0.7% respectively, in clear signs that tariff infected products are rapidly running of 
out of supply chains. Surprisingly, despite trade volumes faltering and demand for bunkers starting 
to slip, oil futures made a healthy 1.8% jump this week and ended at USD 61/barrel on the back of
the news that Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent was due to meet with Chinese officials this week, in 
an effort to iron out US – Chinese trade agreements, in addition to the news of a separate US - UK
trade agreement that still leaves a 10% tariff on U.K. products exported to the U.S.  
As a result, sub-continent recycling markets too have been on a negative spiral of late, especially as 
we head into the quieter monsoon season and Pakistani & Bangladeshi recyclers need to speed up 
their readiness and ensure yard compliance with the Hong Kong Convention by June 26th. While
nearly all Indian yards are certified, the focus shifts to neighboring Pakistan and Bangladesh to get 
their facilities up to speed - and do it soon. NOCs on ships have meanwhile been granted as an 
exemption just this time, resulting in a busy anchorage not only in Bangladesh, but also in India, all 
whilst Pakistan remains stranded for a 3rd week with no fresh arrivals. Flatlining / declining steel 
plate prices including mixed performances by currencies (even Turkey) also didn’t help move the 
pricing needle at any recycling destination, especially given that the overall number of candidates 
has simply disappeared from the bidding tables of late, and ship owners collectively wait to see the 
eventual outcome of trade negotiations in the coming weeks before evaluating freight rates and 
deciding whether to continue trading their older assets at reasonable / above opex levels, or a trip
down recycling lanes is on the charts. Certainly, a hot cup of testy times is on the menu for May.

HKC ONLY! 
NOC struggles. 
HKC … OR BUST! 
After a backlog of ships piling up at OPL Chattogram over recent weeks on the back of missing No 
Objection Certificates (NOCs) from local authorities that would otherwise have permitted these
units committed to non-HKC facilities enter port limits, this week, an exemption was made for just 
those units already outside whose respective facilities had also managed to secure their HKC SoC 
accreditations and approvals from the Bangladeshi government, resulting in a healthy collection of 
local arrivals at Chattogram’s anchorage. It also came as quite the zinger to see the recently formed
ship recycling board announce that no further permissions / NOCs on any incoming vessel(s) or
any further extension(s) would be granted to non-certified yards, and ship owners therefore need 
to be aware that any upcoming / future sale(s) into Chattogram will have to be to an HKC only
yard, as units fixed to non-HKC facilities will simply NOT receive an NOC.  
At present, there are only 7 ship recycling yards with full HKC SoC accreditations in hand and 
another +20 yards are at various stages of their own HKC infrastructure upgrades, some of whom 
are reportedly closer to getting their approvals within the coming week(s). Continued audits will 
take place in Chattogram to ensure yards are matching the required rate of improvements needed 
to achieve their independent HKC certifications. One thing is now for sure, the Hong Kong 
Convention has cemented its permanence in Bangladesh! 
The situation with NOC restrictions has sadly also seen HKC accredited yards start to exploit the 
unfolding situation by talking down the market this week and arbitrarily presenting indications / 
offers on units that are clearly below last week’s levels. As being the only facilities to receive NOCs 
on incoming / future vessels now presents these yard owners with newfound leverage to import
vessels at a lower price - as indications have certainly started showing a downward trend by the 
time the week ended. The onset of Monsoon and increasing stress of yard upgrades further offers 
the likelihood of prices slipping from Bangladesh in the coming weeks, which would then see
Pakistan or even India retake the mantle from Bangladesh in the price rankings within May. 
As the pre-monsoon madness starts to push prices down, coupled with an upcoming budget that 
has garnered little positive news thus far far, and domestic fundamentals that have conjointly done
little to elevate local sentiments this week, saw the Taka make a mere 3 basis point increase against 
the U.S. Dollar and end the week at BDT 121.49, while local steel plate prices remained dead on the 
floor at USD 477/Ton – a level that remains dangerously close to breakeven on certain premium 
units. At the macro end, domestic investors remain weary of international tariffs as reportedly, a
formal trade agreement between the U.S. and Bangladesh has yet to enter into force and the added 
uncertainty on Chinese tariffs is also making domestic manufacturers nervous how their source-
pricing on raw materials might eventually affect their manufacturing costs. In the interim, the 
economic global cosplay saw Bangladesh’s GDP through the month of April. 
Seems like Bangladesh is headed for a quieter end to May.

INDIA 
 
WAR … AND RECYCLING! 
Overall, a crazy week of armed conflict with its perennial rival Pakistan saw the attention of the entire 
nation glued to their respective sources of media watching the unfolding of PM Modi’s decisive action 
against terrorist factions across Eastern Pakistan. As India decimated vital army and air force targets 
across Pakistan striking the deepest the country has ever struck Pakistan in the last 50 years, the 
sudden cease-fire saw the populous voicing their collective disappointment that the recent terrorist 
attack from Pakistan did not meet with a firm enough counteraction, which would more than likely 
see Pakistani terrorists attack at a future date. 
And while Pakistani officials certainly seem to have gotten the message given the close proximity 
Indian missiles hit Pakistani nuclear command and control centres, it remains to be seen just how 
long this newfound “peace” between the nations persists. The Indian Foreign Minister meanwhile 
voiced India’s strong opposition to IMF’s USD 1.3 billion loan package to Pakistan on account of 
Pakistani mismanagement of funds from the prior tranche that remains under IMF investigations,
and there are no securities from Pakistan’s side as to just where this money will be eventually diverted
to or if it will even be spent on future terrorist acts. 
While on the one hand, the conflict kept Alang recyclers distracted throughout the week, on the other 
hand, it may have well been a good thing given that another woeful week of underperformance from 
Alang recyclers saw India sink to the bottom of the sub-continent pricing board – and not for the first 
time this year either! Fundamentals are certainly playing their respective parts given that not only 
have local steel plate prices declined by about USD 4/Ton this week, but the Indian Rupee lost nearly 
1% as it plummeted back towards Rs. 86 midweek, only to end it at Rs. 85.30.   
Moreover, both Indian and Pakistani stock markets surged on the back of the ceasefire, yet Indian 
sentiments remain cautions given just how fragile the current ceasefire is. Tariff talks too have 
reportedly been positive between Indian and U.S. officials as news of a mutually beneficial bilateral 
trade agreement is nearing conclusion. Yet, domestic economists fear that the tariffs that do go into 
effect, will eventually affect the nation’s GDP by 0.5% for the rest of the year. 
For now, however, recyclers remain on the lookout for tonnage as surprisingly, a decent LDT tanker 
at Alang’s anchorage coupled with a collection of smaller sized units helped near India’s total LDT 
for the week over 26K LDT this week. Moreover, with tariff uncertainties abound and HKC taking
front and centre at neighbouring competitors, India could stand to become the market to go to for the 
time being - even in the face of lower pricing, once Pakistani and Bangladeshi recyclers initiate the 
process of upgrading their facilities and divert their collective attentions towards HKC accreditations 
over vessel negotiations. 
Interesting month for India.

PAKISTAN
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
    
SIDELINED! 
Pakistan has barely been on the ship recycling radar over recent years – struggling to compete 
(pricewise) on the back of an ailing economy and a drastically collapsed currency that resulted in 
a crushing lack of financing and available L/Cs for domestic ship recyclers. However, across 2025,
Gadani recyclers have thankfully managed to get by on scarce and small LDT, geographically
positioned tonnage that eventually raised hopes within the cash buying community of a resurgent
Pakistan, given that a growing number of enquiries and aggression to buy emerged across March 
and April. However, Pakistan once again slipped back down and has been relegated to the last few 
weeks of an empty port position, with its competitively weaker levels.  
Luckily for Gadani recyclers, a (once again) declining India has thrust the Pakistani market back
into 2nd place in the market rankings this week, even though local offerings haven’t changed much 
for some time now, which remains puzzling. Despite the Pakistani Rupee depreciating of late, it 
has all but depreciated less than 2% in recent months as it ended the week 12 basis points firmer 
against the U.S. Dollar this week at PKR 281.20, all while Pakistani steel plate prices remain over 
USD 100/Ton higher than its sub-continent competition offering more than a healthy buffer to local 
recyclers at current offer levels.  
Moreover, the approval of IMF’s USD 1.3 billion loan package will certainly ease any financial 
burdens the country or even local recyclers may have as they too approach the HKC deadline of 
June 26th. Unfortunately for Gadani recyclers, they remain lagging far behind the competition on 
the yard upgrades front and this may see Pakistani ship recyclers disappear from the bidding table 
all together once the HKC formally enters into effect. We can only hope that a sense of professional 
urgency prevails, and the incoming aid is put to good use for the future of this vital sector of the 
nation. 
The conflict with India too sucked most of the country’s collective focus away from daily activities 
and this saw no market sales being confirmed into Pakistan this week. However, the aftershocks 
of the conflict will likely be felt for some time as the nation recovers from India’s deepest attacks 
into Pakistan over the last 5 decades and an overall sense of insecurity in the economy could also 
see local recyclers take an increasingly conservative stance in their pricing strategies and deliver 
Pakistan a short stint at the #2 spot. 
Time will tell. Fow now, Pakistan may likely get lucky with the off-chance, smaller LDT 
geographically suited unit and keep a yard (or two) operating in the interim. What happens in the 
long run may end up surprising everyone.
