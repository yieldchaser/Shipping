DESPERATE DOWNERS?! 
With the last 6 weeks of 2025 now firmly on the horizon, the final stretch of the year continues to
demonstrate its sheer unwillingness to compromise as global economies are still reporting volatile
movements of commodity prices and oil futures that are still unfolding with each passing week.
But the ship recycling market economies are the ones generating even more confounding results
amid an increasingly precariously posture at just how this momentum has been unravelling over
the last 3 – 4 years and is now expected (feared) to likely continue deep into 2026.  
While the Baltic Exchange Dry Index reported gains once again this week on the back of virtually
all inclusive sub-sectors reporting improvements of their own through the week, oil futures on the
other hand seem to be taking a beating after levels clocked the week out at USD 59.50/barrel after
Ukraine successfully carried out a massive drone attack against Russia’s largest oil refinery located
at Novorossiysk. The U.S. Dollar meanwhile continues its carnage across the globe as it firmed at 
nearly all locations across the board while local steel plate prices in the two steady recycling 
destinations retreated in varying degrees. 
This double gut-punch targeted at the purchasing prowess of the ship recycling fraternity has 
driven demand and vessel offerings even further down this week as vintage tonnage starts to
unexpectedly arrive at anchorages i.e. Bangladesh, which healthily beat India’s beaching quota for 
this week. Moreover, with freight rates continually rising / yielding profitable results for relevant
stakeholders, recycling sales have been slow for the most part of the year even though sales
activity has comparatively picked up a touch since the barren summer slump. Worse yet, many of
the transactions concluded are at levels far below expectations and in the low USD 400s/LDT, with
several reportedly in the high USD 300s/LDT on less preferred / small LDT tonnage.  
Meanwhile, the poorer placed India and Pakistan continue struggling to register any worthwhile
levels for owners / cash buyers despite the supply of older dry bulk units – particularly vintage
handymax and panamax units including small -to medium- sized-tankers – registering an increase
in the number of candidates being proposed for recycling. Yet, just how many are actually being 
concluded amidst a sea of boring offers remains inconclusive even though several end-of-life MR
tankers, smaller product tankers, and a steady stream of occasional LNGs have kept ship recycling
markets from dozing off completely in the latter part of this year.  
Finally, as HKC developments have taken precedent over filling plots and with mandatory yard
upgrades marring the recycling progress, Bangladesh remains with only 18 HKC approved yards
while Pakistan is at the tail end of the race as it awaits its first HKC approval. And as the shipping
calendar remains busy with various events over the past month or so, including Bahri week last
week in addition to the recycling world convening in Hong Kong for the annual Tradewinds Ship 
Recycling forum, it remains a busy time for the industry – all while Turkey remains alone at the 
USD 2XX/Ton party awaiting a lone passerby.

BARREN, YET BOLD? 
Despite all their efforts and firmer offers emanating out of Chattogram, very few market sales
have been realized of late. Yet, it does seem as though several private units including those idling 
in cash buyer inventories that were likely awaiting a positive market movement without much
luck, have clearly helped local recyclers this week as Chattogram’s waterfront reported an influx
of tonnage that saw 7 vessels and nearly 66K LDT suddenly arrive the nation’s recycling 
anchorage just this week, including at least one large 21K LDT dry bulk unit. 
An anemic yet growing demand from an increasingly hungry / desperate set of local recyclers of
late has likely attributed to this outcome since Bangladesh has suffered a nearly barren 2025 ever
since the HKC came into effect - save for a promising start to the year that essentially banked 
primarily on large LDT dry bulk / LNG units, we have seen very few vessels heading to
Bangladeshi shores since June.  
30% tariffs, overly paralyzing monsoons, political instability and unrest over the upcoming 
February 2026 elections, economic downturns, volatile inflationary figures, a collapsing Taka, and
recently retreating local steel plate prices have all been delivering a death of a thousand cuts to 
the local ship recycling mindset over the last 2 quarters. In fact, just this week, we saw the Taka 
plunge nearly 40 basis points down to BDT 122.35 against the U.S. Dollar, whilst local steel plate 
prices took up the challenge and fell a meager USD 1/Ton after flatlining for the entire week. 
With political violence now being reported, even day-to-day life in Chattogram seems to be 
coming under stress as daily headlines news on security lapses make the rounds as economic
activity in the nation continues to take a hit. And with a healthy level of incoming units this 
week, it is very likely that we see the Bangladeshi ship recycling market enter a new twilight of 
absence at the bidding tables.  
Time will tell. 
For now, the forecast for Bangladesh’s 2025 end remains unchallenged and otherwise expectedly
barren for the most part, unless some of the better numbers for select tonnage seen over recent 
times continue to hold, especially as the industry moves towards the crucial local elections ahead. 
Unchallenged.

INDIA
VIEW FROM BELOW! 
Alang’s slide down the foothills of the price rankings mountain has seen an increasing silence
descend across the industry, given that India has been the only worthwhile destination to seriously
consider of late, with a far more capable set of local recyclers and a strongly established HKC base. 
And as clearly evident from this week’s port position, this still seems to be the case given that a
bunch of smaller LDT units, which more than likely did not find a retirement spot on the beaches of 
Gadani, have happily ended up in Alang instead.  
And with little demand and minimal offers emanating from the largest and most HKC approved 
destination in the world today, such a reticence and disillusionment over the ongoing performance of
industry is a worrying factor and for what has been an overall declining and lethargic market over 
the course of 2025 and even back in 2024, where levels not-since-seen the highs of USD 600/LDT has
turned the business of ship recycling into a struggle for survival for the 100 HKC approved yards that 
still stand in operation today.  
The crushing decline in supply has maintained posture and the sporadic flurry of incoming units 
certainly deceives the number of deliveries the entire sub-continent has otherwise been acclimatized 
to and left even ship owners and cash buyers worrying where their end-of-life assets will eventually 
price out on the books, especially as fundamentals continue taking a hammering. The Rupee lost 11 
basis points to end the week at Rs. 88.70, local steel plate prices were the only ones to make an 
unexpected turnaround by about USD 4/Ton, though it remains far from stable territory to where 
acceptable offerings on smaller LDT dry units could scrape past USD 400/Ton. 
Seems 2026 is gearing up to be a playback of 2025 with its own flavour of unexpected coming the 
industry’s way – especially given how things are playing out worldwide. 
USD 600/Ton? 
Unexpceted.

PAKISTAN
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
    
REDUCED OUTPUT! 
After an altogether (comparatively) healthier few months of deliveries and reported yard
upgrades that have yet to deliver the nation’s first HKC approved ship recycling yard, the 
Pakistani market’s performance continues to leave an unimpressive mark on the industry with
lackluster offerings at the bidding tables, assurances of compliance via provisional DASR 
certificates, poorly performing economy, an inflation that has more than doubled in the span of a 
couple of months, and a local ship recycling mindset that is being hammered by the inflow of far
cheaper Iranian product that continues undercutting local steel plate prices. 
In fact, we finally saw the first signs of this hitting the domestic ship recycling community where 
it hurts most, in local steel plate prices that not only opened the week lower than last, but 
remarkably, managed to even decline a whopping USD 13/Ton and below the USD 600/Ton mark 
for the first in a few quarters. Not to be outdone, the PKR too fell about 11 basis points and closed 
the week out at levels approaching PKR 283 against the U.S. Dollar, at PKR 282.80. 
This has left Gadani positioned just above a lackluster Alang and leaving not much else on the 
table in terms of safe redeliveries. Case-and-point, if a tiny 1400 Ton straggler can manage to 
remain stranded at anchorage for nearly 2 months, it certainly leaves no room for owners / cash 
buyers of larger LDT units to risk the Pakistani market, especially amidst this ongoing decline. 
HKC hurdles have certainly left this market stranded in the face of the competition as no new 
vessels have arrived Gadani this week and authorities will ultimately have to fuel progress at 
yards in order to remain relevant. Even with incoming cheaper steel product, higher tariffs on
imported steel need to be imposed so that the state of affairs doesn’t cannibalize local business 
viability. 
The big question remains, when and how soon will any of this come into effect as it is clear that 
Pakistan will likely spend the rest of the year (like India) in a disheveled state. Worse yet? Much 
like the last 2 years and especially 2023 have turned out, where a meager 16 vessels were 
delivered locally over the entire year, to snap that figure into perspective, this is the number of 
vessels across all anchorages just this week.  
A truly disappointing turnaround for such a big market.
