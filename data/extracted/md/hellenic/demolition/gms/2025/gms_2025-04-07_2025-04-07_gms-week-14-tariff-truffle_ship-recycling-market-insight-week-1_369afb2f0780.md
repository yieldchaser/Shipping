As Trump’s massive gamble on tariff wars kicked into high gear over ‘Liberation Day’ this week, 
global stock markets took unkindly to the tariffs as they were decimated and fell over 10%, with 
over USD 3 Trillion being wiped out from investor pockets, as consumers globally have yet to see a
dramatic impact filter through to their collective pockets. As nations retaliated across the board 
almost immediately and China even slapped reciprocal tariffs on the U.S., it maybe sooner rather 
than later that the impacts of this situation are already at the doorsteps of exporting nations (to the 
U.S.) as the situation is still freshly unfolding across all nations. And although President Trump is 
known for taking hard line positions as he golfed while the markets were in free fall, Republicans 
and Democrats are reportedly taking steps to pass law that would curb the powers of the Executive 
Branch through the unfolding lunacy, and the outpouring of protests may lead to a softened (or at 
the very least a negotiated position) from Trump’s side somewhere down the line – hopefully much 
sooner rather than later. Notwithstanding, the immediate effects were certainly dramatic as this
week, oil suffered the most egregious of collapses falling to USD 62 / barrel, a number not since seen 
since August 2021. And with OPEC+ countries vowing to increase supply as global demand eases
on the back of tariffs and an uncertain economic future, global trade also seems to be taking a hit 
with the Baltic’s dry sea freight index slumped another 3.3% down to a one month low.  
The global ship recycling sector also witnessed some interesting activity as not only have local steel 
plate prices continued to climb in India, but they also fell in China and flatlined in both Bangladesh 
and Pakistan. On the currencies front, while some reported improvements (India), others declined 
with Turkey surprising the industry, reporting no change this week. Recycling activity too has
somewhat inevitably been picking up in sub-continent markets over these last few weeks as the 
number of deliveries greatly improved of late, and this week has seen the various waterfronts take 
a beating with no fresh arrivals in Bangladesh, while India and Pakistan snared just one each. Even
Bangladeshi prices that climbed briefly following on a dearth of tonnage, seem to have settled as 
there is no tonnage to bid on, and Indian & Pakistani recyclers remain cautious & tentative in their 
offerings on fresh tonnage in light of the recent U.S. sanctions saga, while simultaneously observing
markets to see what the overall impact on prices and potential renewed dumping of cheap Chinese 
steel into the sub-continent could spell for their purchases, before presenting fresh offers on tonnage 
at numbers that could conceivably be far lower in a post tariff world. 
With minimum activity taking place in Bangladesh, Pakistan, and Turkey thanks to Eid celebrations
and holidays that have kept most markets closed through this week, the outcome of Bangladeshi 
restriction on imports remains unresolved on the back of the March 31st deadline for local recyclers 
within which, infrastructure updates at all domestic ship recycling yards were to commence, leaving
the fate of pending deliveries through the upcoming tide(s), an uncertainty. Overall, while we may
be witnessing an overreaction to a hardline, exaggerated, and thoughtless action from the highest 
office in the U.S., rather than collectively forecasting doom and gloom for global trade, a more 
pragmatic long-term view may be needed to navigate through these troubled waters together.

VOICEMAIL! 
It has been a voicemail week for the Chattogram Choppers as the ship recycling fraternity was 
collectively celebrating Eid and the end of the Holy month of Ramadan this week, resulting in steel 
plate prices that have started Q2 2025, flatlined at the bottom of the pool where they have been for 
about 7 months now. Moreover, even the 2024 experiment by the Bangladeshi government to 
permit the Taka to be controlled by market forces, saw it decline over 21 basis points / BDT 11 
during this time, ended the week at BDT 121.82, likely to pass BDT 122 in the next week(s) as the 
pending 37% tariff truffle against Bangladesh awaits delivery to its economy.  
The key ‘beneficiary’ of these tariffs, which are currently at 20%, will be the Readymade Garments 
(RMG) sector and is expected to be hit the hardest as they have been, ever since the collapse of the 
government in June 2024 that resulted in local violence, rioting, and ongoing unemployment.
Moreover, despite being the firmest placed sub-continent market atop GMS’s market rankings,
local recyclers have started to question what the overall impact on prices further down the line
might be, as the markets saw local offers being scaled back by about USD 5 - USD 10/LDT whilst 
other end buyers decided to take a wait and watch approach entirely.  
And despite the supply of tonnage being at concerning levels over the last few weeks and freight 
rates cooling off this week as well, the expectation that the BSBRA will again extend the HKC 
deadline to begin yard upgrades from March 31st to a later date remains up in the air as authorities
and governmental offices remain closed on the back of ongoing Eid holidays and end buyers are 
still pressuring authorities for an extension, as they remains keen to fill their plots before the HKC 
comes into force on June 26th, 2025 and certainly as the immediate future of incoming deliveries 
hangs in the balance.  
Weakening prices and the ongoing diminished supply of viable units also saw a drop in the number 
of market and private local fixtures, and subsequent arrivals at the local waterfront, where local 
recyclers reported no fresh arrivals this week, other than a handful of prior deliveries, including
vessel ‘THREE STAR’ that had been stranded at anchorage since January of this year. 
On the local economic front, the nation recorded the largest ever international personal remittances 
back into the country to the tune of aver USD 3 Billion, which saw domestic reserves being actively 
replenished through the volatile uncertainty that is currently affecting the economy and the
decisions of the interim administration. And due to the ongoing holidays, there remains little of 
importance to report from Bangladesh, as Week 14 winds to a halt with nothing of significance to 
report.

INDIA 
 
ALERT ‘N’ ALIVE! 
Despite being the most sensitive of sub-continent markets to unfolding global events and global
market turmoil, India the most part, has been for cautiously waiting on the sidelines to see how 
Trump’s tariff tantrums play out before committing afresh on any of the slim pickings of recycling 
vessels that are currently floating around the bidding tables.  
And despite Trump announcing 26% tariffs on India across the board, the reaction from India has 
been surprisingly filtered thus far, with no news of retaliatory tariffs or retreating vessel offerings (as 
yet), especially as domestic fundamentals continue to collectively strengthen through the week. Not 
only did local steel plate prices climb another USD 9/Ton to over USD 473.56/Ton (pushing local 
purchases on dry vessels further in the green), but even the Indian Rupee firmed down to Rs. 85.50 
(from the highs approaching Rs. 88), providing further confidence to Alang buyers, especially as their 
Bangladeshi counterparts are pulling back on their offers by up to USD 10/Ton this week and 
Pakistani recyclers continue to take a bite out of India’s tonnage buffet. 
And while most of the market tonnage in recent weeks has filtered through to the far better positioned 
Bangladeshi market, with India being the one destination that has been left to only taking HKC only 
limited vessels and specialist units, the recent global market turmoil may still see more vessels arrive 
for recycling in the near future and a greater supply of vessels are therefore expected to head to India 
and Pakistan later into Q2. Whether levels hold at prevailing rates remains to be seen, especially as 
some recyclers are already taking the opportune time to talking down the market, to take stock of 
unfolding market falls. But the reality is that steel prices and local currencies have not been impacted 
to such a dramatic overall effect (as yet), to justify a sudden decline in offerings. 
Meanwhile, Alang anchorage continues to simmer on the number of its local arrivals with only a
small LDT arrival of a supply vessel, as India seems increasingly destined for an empty port position,
especially in the face of all of the global craziness unfolding. India’s economy, however, seems to be 
on the right trajectory especially as the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) announced that it would likely 
cut interest rates by 25 basis points in the coming week, indicating a greater sense of urgency to 
induce local spending after the recent tax breaks.  
And while this could further assist India’s ship recycling sector on large U.S. Dollar transactions when 
the time comes, the lack of tonnage at the bidding tables is likely to see this market spend the next 
week(s) on a quieter note.  
Quiet.

PAKISTAN
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
    
MINI REVIVAL? 
A mini revival of sorts in Pakistan, bought on primarily due to India’s recently diminishing levels 
that inadvertently thrust the Gadani Gang into 2nd place on the market rankings, has brought the 
Pakistani market back into the picture of late, with a series of smaller LDT, mostly local Middle 
Eastern owned, geographically positioned sales to local buyers – and this week was no exception 
with another fresh arrival and with the most amount of LDT in the Indian sub-continent markets 
this week, given that 20K LDT is currently delivered or lying in wait at anchorage awaiting 
delivery.  
The overall impact of Pakistan’s recent moves in the sub-continent ship recycling markets has been 
rather limited so far however, with most of the larger LDT deals of the year (including a cape, 
several LNGs and larger tankers) ending up either in Bangladesh or neighboring India. However, 
Pakistani buyers need to really engage on incoming LDT after having spent a prolonged period on 
the sidelines, in order to sustain this momentum. Notwithstanding, fundamentals are deliberately
conspiring to keep local offerings in check, especially as local steel plate prices lie flatlined for the 
last week at USD 624/Ton (almost USD 40/Ton lower than previous highs), all while the Pakistani 
Rupee loses another 26 basis points, to end the week at PKR 280.34 against the U.S. Dollar. 
The recent tariffs placed across the globe have also not spared the Pakistani economy where Trump 
announced an additional 29% blanket tariffs on all imports from Pakistan, which could seriously 
affect Pakistan’s textiles and garments exports (like Bangladesh). And while this could spell 
disaster for the domestic economy, which is once against in the thralls of rising inflation, Pakistan’s
Finance Minister sees a silver lining via opening possible new avenues for negotiating a “balanced 
economic relationship”, not only with the U.S., but with other nations and economies worldwide.
And although this strategy maybe present viable long-term opportunities for the nation, in the 
short-term, the domestic economic situation will likely dip for the worse.  
Notwithstanding, all that is unfolding, it is good to see some recycling transactions finally being
concluded to local recyclers, all while yard upgrades are yet to be taken seriously, with several end 
users reportedly only now starting infrastructure works and investments in order to get up to HKC 
standards ahead of its entry into force in June of this year. For the rest, it maybe an even longer 
time on the sidelines until their certifications come through. 
Overall, with Raman holidays keeping the local ship recycling community distracted with 
celebrations, the week ended on a quiet note, just like the rest of its sub-continent neighbors.
