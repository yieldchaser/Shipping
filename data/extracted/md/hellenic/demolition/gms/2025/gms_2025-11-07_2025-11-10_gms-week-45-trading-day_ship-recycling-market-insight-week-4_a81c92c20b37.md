### Editorial: TRADING DAY?

Some other day, just not today, or this week, or this month, or this year, or the last few for that matter. As president economic turmoil tears down the White House back home and economies around the world, his actions are directly leading to a meltdown that has consistently been affecting international markets month after month, including the advent of November 2025 as inflation is still rising at key ship recycling destinations, global steel prices are collapsing, currencies devaluing like it’s an overdue assignment, and a disjointed ratio of trading units vs recycling volumes, all wrapped up in a 2025 burrito of unnecessary bombastic.

To start, inflation is on the rise again in Pakistan with Turkey and Bangladesh reporting barely noticeable corrections – all while India and USA figures are still lampooning with pending updates at their ends. The Baltic Exchange also rose to the tune of nearly 2% in just about a month with Capes, Panamax, and smaller segment indices all clocking the week out with 3.1%, 09%, and 0.5% respective gains and the overall index firming 7% just this week. As sanctions and bans increasingly strike at the world’s trading units and the balance of the legally trading group seems increasingly burdened with shouldering the load of missing tonnage, which itself cannot even be recycled anywhere, it is certainly starting to show at the waterfront once again. And for the world of ship recycling, is a disastrous turn of events as 2026 is already at attention on the horizon.

Oil is likely to see similar performance hiccups as it declined nearly 20 basis points to end the week at levels barely above USD 60/barrel thank to global supply being on cue to outpace demand as the U.S. ramps up domestic production and investors continue to monitor the effects of Washington’s pressure-tactics on Moscow via pending sanctions on their top oil majors, including Rosneft PJSC and LukOil PJSC (LukOil even rumored to be filing for bankruptcy this week) and purchasing nations such as China and India now facing further economic pressures of their own. And all this while Dollars and lack of diplomacy are steering currencies into neverland.

## 3. Country Market Intelligence

### Bangladesh

- **Headline**: DIM & DOUR!
- **Key Notes**: *Nothing viable.* | *Continue trading.* | *Quieter end.* | *Trump Cola.*

As the top placed market in terms of demand and pricing, it remains disappointing for Chattogram Choppers to have no viable market candidates to work on for yet another week as most owners of smaller-than-large-ldt units seem far more disappointed with the declined level of local offerings at the bidding tables and are happier to either engage competing markets or continue trading their units instead, eking out the final earnings for even overaged vessels lurking in the global fleets, rather than face the dour state of Bangladesh’s ship recycling reality today.

And that even goes for the better placed local open buyers who continue to pay well-above local market levels just to fill empty plots, utilize dormant L/C limits, and minimize operational and fixed losses in the interim.

But given Bangladesh’s economic reality? Rightly so, with the Taka having collapsed from a high of BDT 106 back in June 2024, down to BDT 121.93 against the U.S. Dollar as of this week. Moreover, after lackluster resales of recycled ships steel onto domestic steel mills and competing cheaper imports ongoing, local steel plate prices that consistently seemed to defy gravity and remain suspended at surprising levels, all but fell off a Bangladeshi cliff as the week ended with no trading reported. Next week will certainly present a clearer picture.

Inflation meanwhile has been see-sawing month after month as it fell to 8.48% in June, rose to 8.55% in July, back down to 8.29% in August, back up in September to 8.39% and now 8.17% as October ended. This is tumultuous for any nation’s economy as there is no resounding direction for the country and a clear failure of governance policy, which itself is due for a resurrection in February 2026 as riots and political instability are back in the headlines once again. And after a brief spell of marginal conclusions, the nation’s waterfront is back down to a stranded lone wolf with no fresh arrivals this week.

As such, it is set to be a much quieter end to 2025 than previously perceived – especially in a giant market like Bangladesh and especially after a promising start to the year, but an uncertain mid- year HKC + Trump-cola cocktail with an election cherry on top will ensure the end of the year for the Chattogram Choppers will be a messy one.

### India

- **Headline**: QUANTUM ENTANGLED!
- **Key Notes**: *Stranded.* | *Harrowed.* | *Mini-VLs.* | *Adept.*

One of the quirks of quantum mechanics is the ability a particle to be in two places at the same time in the micro-word, and Alang has managed to display a similar position in the real world where on the one hand, it has been and remains stranded at the bottom of the market rankings for what seems like an eternity now, and yet, it has been the top spot for incoming tonnage and deliveries week after, taking in a comparative plethora of tonnage while sailing through a drought of unavailability and being hit by financial storms and economic uncertainties.

And while a healthy majority of Alang recyclers have been forced to remain in a perennial wait and watch mode, Alang overall continues to surprisingly impress despite its poor market levels and a harrowing period of declines in steel and currency values.

Over USD 50/LDT has been wiped clean from domestic steel plate prices over the last few months including this week, where it fell a couple of Dollars to wind the week at USD 388.95/Ton – a clear indication of local recyclers destined to battling inevitable losses on upcoming deliveries. The currency meanwhile held on to the other end of the buckling stallions fraying economic reigns as it has declined to historical record-highs against U.S. Dollar of late – including this week winding it down at Rs. 88.67.

But Alang’s HKC prowess has certainly come through for this market as Alang has been the flourishing yet miserably priced destination that continues to report incoming units week after week, including this one where a couple of mini-VLCCs slid into local anchorage (one isn’t on the report as its still at outer anchorage and officially unreported).

As such, whether we see India get back to any reasonable level of buying before the end of the year or not, this market seems to be the puzzling and untold champion of 2025 and remains the “go to” destination, despite India and even the industry juggles economic meltdowns and fresh HKC hurdles – both of which nervy Alang recyclers seem clearly adept at handling.

### Pakistan

- **Headline**: DEAD CANARY!
- **Key Notes**: *Steel worries.* | *Yards must upgrade.* | *No go low LDT.*

Despite a fire brewing in Gadani’s ship recycling sector, the “canary in the coal mine” remains dead silent in the fiery face of severely degraded levels of inactivity over the last few months, as the Pakistani market sinks to new lows across recent weeks with levels across the offer board now well below USD 400/LDT - largely due to the ongoing dumping of cheaper Iranian steel imports that keep undercutting local steel plate prices, which themselves have puzzlingly managed to stay nailed at USD 614/Ton, a massive margin over competing markets - especially India.

And the PKR? Like its neighbors, down 5 basis points to close the week out as PKR 282.50 against the U.S. Dollar.

Moreover, while Pakistan was celebrating a lower-than-its-neighbors 20% tariff quota, reality has strung an entirely different chord for the nation’s economy as inflation has skyrocketed from 3% back in August to 6.2% last month, further adding to Gadani’s perennially confusing vessel pricing strategies.

HKC hurdles meanwhile remain the primary contributors to the afflictions of this market amidst a reality that has ship owners and cash buyers spoked about the possibility of international prosecution outcomes – and a Gadani market that is still twiddling on temporary DASR approvals just to keep the domestic sector alive as despite news of ongoing local infrastructure upgrades, not a single yard has received its accreditation, far from officially entering the HKC arena.

Local deliveries too have dried up with Pakistan and Bangladesh twinning their anchorages as no fresh arrivals were reported for this week in Gadani as well.

As such, quiet has mostly descended on the waterfront with little for end buyers to chirp about and like ongoing interactions at the bidding tables, ship owners and cash buyer options are invariably funneled down to a stable Alang. Go figure!
