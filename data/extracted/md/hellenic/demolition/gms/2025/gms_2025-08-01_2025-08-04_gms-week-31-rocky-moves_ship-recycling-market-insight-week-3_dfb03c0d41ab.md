### Editorial: ROCKY MOVES!

As the shipping and ship-recycling worlds seemingly collide going down a rabbit hole of this thoughtlessly induced global economic turmoil bought on by a blind surgeon, we can undoubtedly expect to see further issues arising in the coming weeks (unless immediate countermeasures aren’t enacted) as president “two weeks” finally approved a wave of tariffs (infamously declared on April 4th as Liberation Day) against U.S. imports from dozens of countries, including Canada, Taiwan, India, and surprisingly, even Russia as in a show of force against Putin’s unrelenting and truly unabated aggression in Ukraine, this week Trump reportedly instructed CentCom to strategically re-position two nuclear submarines at undisclosed locations off of Russian shores. This move, in addition to the sanctioning of not only Russian oil, but also those trading Russian oil, is reportedly expected to see over 1,000 vessels enter the dreaded OFAC list, leaving their futures in disarray.

And the inevitably of his actions is already starting to (sort of) bear fruit for the ship recycling sector as a gradual inflow of tankers into India and bulkers into Pakistan over recent weeks (including this one), has seen fresh arrivals at both locations this week. The Baltic Exchange dry index also reported a 0.75% overall jump brought on by the Cape and Supramax indices that firmed 1.8% and 0.1% respectively this week, all while the Panamax index fell nearly 1% of its total value, an over 10% drop in weekly trading. Unsuspiciously, both wet and small-to-medium size dry units are expected to continue sailing into the Indian sub-continent ship recycling markets through August, given that the sanctions have already seen oil futures in turmoil as they declined an unpopular (well any negative number is unpopular) 2.7% this week, closing it out at USD 67.30/barrel.

Fundamentals at ship recycling destinations also displayed results similar to a darts contest at a blind congregation, all over the board this week (just as they have been over the recent past) despite ‘some’ price stability and even improvements being registered, resulting in a growing collection of fixtures into the sub-continent recycling markets of late – most notably were the two larger LDT LNGs into India and even two bulkers into a resurgent Pakistan, with reports of slow but ongoing improvements to local yards coming forth.

## 3. Country Market Intelligence

### Bangladesh

- **Headline**: BACKLOGS BITE!
- **Key Notes**: *Lowest still.* | *Frustrations abound.* | *HKC underway.* | *Tariff tricks.*

The Bangladeshi ship recycling market remains the lowest placed of all sub-continent destinations for yet another week / month and as such, has been unable to partake in much (if at all) of the activity at the bidding tables of late. And this level of pervading disinterest to be competitive has seen the waterfront suffer over the last 3 weeks with minimal arrivals and deliveries ensuing, including this one, where just a lonesome albeit decent LDT tanker arrived the nations recycling waterfront this week, and remains idling (likely) awaiting its tide.

Meanwhile, the incessant rains in the face of reported global warming wrapped in the threat of impending tariffs saw fundamentals hammering the purchasing mindset of local buyers as despite the Bangladeshi Taka displaying a degree of stability and even firming 30 basis points against the U.S. Dollar to end the week at BDT 21.52 (thanks to the central bank’s reported intervention last week), it was steel plate prices that fell another USD 2/Ton as recycled steel fails to move from yards to domestic steel mills. And with the recently reported trade deal between steel mills and steel importers at levels far lower than current rates, there has been a growing frustration at the lack of constructive movement from the interim government to help move the domestic steel industry forward or even announce any government-backed or subsidized infrastructure projects to help the nation’s economy for over 12 months now, which is leading to a reduced overall demand for steel - and ships.

As such, much of the growing steel inventory at local yards that was previously concluded at much higher prices (than today), is now starting to backlog post recycling and this is creating even more frustrations for recyclers that are now starting to extend beyond merely financial. The ongoing shortage of a monsoon induced worker shortage at Chattogram recycling yards is also not doing anyone locally any favors, which may have its own silver-lining in that, a slowdown in recycling of recently delivered units stands to extend the overall process, likely beyond the monsoons and into late September during which time, there may hopefully be some economic reprieve.

Notwithstanding, HKC developments have reportedly continued at pace at the remainder of yards with works is ongoing, in order to get all local yards up to HKC standards and continue operating as the number of disinterested Bangladeshi recyclers at the bidding tables is clearly starting to affect Chattogram’s anchorage. Moreover, with inflation reducing in June and July hopefully following the trend (awaiting official July numbers), the Central Bank may hopefully re-adjust lending rates thereby helping local recyclers on future vessel purchases. Bangladesh’s stock exchange has also rallied since last year as it climbed a whopping 6.9% in July and over 46.45% during the same time 12 months ago.

But with tariffs against Bangladesh now reportedly in effect, the nation’s growing GDP is expected to take a notable hit across Q3 – and likely beyond. Just how this will affect the nation’s ship recycling waterfront remains to be seen. For now, we don’t expect any meaningful transactions to take place until August likely comes to pass.

### India

- **Headline**: ROSIER GLOW?
- **Key Notes**: *Competitive.* | *Non-ferrous desires.* | *HKC ready.* | *Tough call.*

Recent LNG sales into India have given this market, a rosier glow than they perhaps would otherwise have expected bearing in mind, the incessant volatility in local steel plate prices has seen them go through a bit of a roller coaster ride this week as they started and end the week at the same level. And this volatility in steel that has seen far more declines through Q3, was also joined by ongoing currency woes that saw the Rupee fall a drastic 93 basis points against the U.S. Dollar and end the week at Rs. 87.37, leaving Alang resting in 2nd and even 3rd place on the market rankings for a majority of 2025.

Logically, given that India remains a specialist in non-ferrous trade, has certainly helped this market keep the final destinations of most LNGs routed to Alang’s shores where demand for non-ferrous and non-ferrous vessel remains high, thanks to the above average quantities of aluminium and stainless steel onboard these units that has helped them achieve prices far in excess of prevailing averages, thanks to these vessels being particularly high in demand here.

Meanwhile, domestic yard capacity and HKC infrastructure upgrades were at the forefront of Alang ship recyclers since 2014, invariably helping key local recyclers snag high-profile vessels from the fleets of blue-chip owners. This fundamental shift in the global mentality from the ship owning fraternity saw a better quality and grade of maintained vessels arriving Alang, much to the envy of non-HKC neighbouring yard owners who then went on to pursue HKC certifications of their own, gradually planting the initial seeds for further progress across Alang’s landscape. 12 years later, this process has snowballed to where nearly every ship recycling yard is HKC SoC approved and the only thing needed for the local community and governmental agencies was to modify NOR documents at the time of arrival in a post June 26th / HKC world.

The result of which, continues to be a busy anchorage for Alang’s ship recycling community that has struggled with tonnage availability for well over a year now, and recyclers here have been the busiest in the sub-continent, taking in a breadth of types and volumes of tonnage over recent weeks including this one, where a couple of new tankers arrived locally, in addition to a few final deliveries. Clearly, the number of open and available local buyers over these stagnating monsoon months has survived behind the scenes, who have been monitoring tonnage movements and decidedly picking up units now that prices have bottomed out and global tumult gradually starts to drive tonnage towards retirement. Tariffs imposed this week are certainly on the minds of Indian businesses and these are expected to keep recycling vessel prices in check, at least in the short term / through August.

As such, for owners looking for their own shades of green recycling, India has and will continue to offer the best option while Pakistani buyers may offer suspect conditions, albeit at notably firmer prices. Certainly, a tough call for most owners who are pushed to recycling in recent times.

### Pakistan

- **Headline**: GLEEFUL!
- **Key Notes**: *Saving grace.* | *Pricing ahead.* | *Momentum makers.* | *Potential delays?*

The top placed Indian sub-continent ship recycling market, Gadani, continues to defy industry expectations over these miserable monsoon months despite having been altogether absent from the bidding tables about 6 months ago. Clearly, demand and pricing remains a hair ahead of competing Indian and Bangladeshi offerings thanks to domestic fundamentals that have displayed comparatively more positivity in recent times whereby not only have steel plate prices remained steadfast at USD 615/Ton for a while now – far above industry averages for 2025 and much better placed to offer even higher levels now that the currency has also reversed its course and firmed consistently over recent weeks including this one, where it jumped nearly 20 basis points and close the week out at PKR 282.42 (having nearly skirted the edges of PKR 285 a month or so ago).

Moreover, with local recyclers having failed to initiate meaningful steps towards improving their yards and local authorities now agreeing to grant provisional DASR certificates to those yards who have demonstrated recent progress and upgrades, has provided further impetus for recyclers to position themselves slightly above their neighboring competition. Consequently, geographically positioned tonnage and even some units from the Far East have been coming in, including a recent flurry of both wet and dry units have been winding up in gleeful Pakistani hands of late and even this week, where a couple of fresh, decent LDT dry cargo arrivals were reported, one of whom has already been delivered to meet her final end.

How long can or will this momentum be maintained is anyone’s guess, and certainly relevant in current times given that tariffs to the tune of 19% - reportedly negotiated down from 29% and being claimed by chief advisor to the finance minister of Pakistan, as the best rate to be negotiated in the Indian sub-continent and Eastern markets. While this is certainly better than the likely 30% tariffs that the Indian market, and already imposed 20% tariffs hitting the Bangladeshi market may certainly give Gadani recyclers an edge across August, and perhaps beyond, as long as they maintain their levels and don’t retreat to re-position themselves slightly below their neighboring competition, as they have historically done so and especially now that there is clearly an increase in vessel introductions.

Notwithstanding, bearing in mind that local yards in Gadani still have some ways to go before a decent number of local yards have completed their HKC upgrades and no longer have to operate basis provisional DASR certificates, the industry could continue to exploit Pakistan’s firmer offerings in the meantime. Although, they too will likely have to go through their own teething phase of inward clearance delays and procedural hurdles when the time comes.
