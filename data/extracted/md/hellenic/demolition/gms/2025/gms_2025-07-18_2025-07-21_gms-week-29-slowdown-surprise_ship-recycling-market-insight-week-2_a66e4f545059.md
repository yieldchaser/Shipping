### Editorial: SLOWDOWN SURPRISE!

The ongoing tariffs conundrum that has brought on the recent and ongoing global instability, has also seen ship recycling markets reacting in confusion for over 2 months now (perhaps even longer). As global trade and even trade route disruptions are being reported once again (on account of the Houthi’s recently sinking a couple of merchant ships in the Red Sea), inflation has started to rear its head again, rising in the U.S. to 2.7% in June (up from 2.4% in May). The U.S. Dollar has meanwhile found itself firming against all ship recycling destinations once again this week while oil futures find themselves on the opposite side, sliding 0.3% down to USD 67.3/barrel as both Trump and the EU threaten additional sanctions against ongoing Russian aggression in Ukraine, including those specifically targeting Russian energy exports. Topping it all off is perhaps the most critical of all essentials is the state of the Baltic Exchange’s Dry Index that found itself rising about 1.1% this week and reaching its highest level since September 2024, placing even more pressure on the supply of tonnage, which has made a surprisingly mild return over the last couple of weeks and as highlighted by the state of the various sub-continent waterfronts this week (including even in Pakistan).

As we filter down to the ship recycling end of things, the last few months of diminished activity across sub-continent recycling markets has shown few signs of abating while sentiments and pricing remain muted overall, whilst key markets grapple with recently imposed HKC realities that have resulted in the industry getting increasingly comfortable at tabling offers below the USD 400/LDT mark on certain tonnage, particularly as both Alang and Chattogram face pressures of their own. There have also been few (market) sales being done at these lower levels even though Pakistani offerings remain well above the USD 400/Ton mark and have been the top placed market for about a month now. During this time, the Gadani gang has even managed to secure a few dry bulk vessels in recent times as both Panamax and Handymax units have been heading to local recyclers who are able to provide provisional DASR certificates for inward permissions. Bangladeshi NOCs are still proving challenging as delays on vessels arriving here are now becoming customary as government departments and ministries take time on granting approvals, while local banks simultaneously prolong the approval and opening of L/Cs. Yet, there have been several larger LNG sales and ongoing negotiations in recent weeks, in what has been the most active sector of the ship recycling markets of late. India meanwhile remains close on its heels with a healthy batch of acquisitions of its own, as owners unwilling to test Pakistani DASRs are willing to take a hit on the lower, yet safer offerings from neighboring Alang.

## 3. Country Market Intelligence

### Bangladesh

- **Headline**: STAGNATED HICCUPS!
- **Key Notes**: *Perking up.* | *Grappling with docs.* | *No new sales.* | *In the spotlight.*

Bangladesh remains the lowest placed of all Indian sub-continent ship recycling markets since the advent of the customarily slower monsoon / summer months that have been greeted by a myriad of self-induced problems, which are driving prices mercilessly down and resulting in a staggered state of sales into Chattogram as port reports nearly dry up some weekends, while perking up on others such as this one, where 5 ships and nearly 66K LDT, including 2 large LNGS, arrived the nation’s waterfront this week, being delivered to HKC recyclers with yard space opening up.

And although the increase in tonnage is certainly a welcome surprise, both end buyers and local authorities continue grappling with the new realities of now having the HKC in force, as they continue working on getting the remainder of local yards approved after inspections are being conducted in conjunction with various class societies (such as Class NK, Rina, IR, BV etc.), requiring IHM parts I, II and III to now be a mandatory part of inward formalities for recycling vessels entering Chattogram’s port limits, as are SRPs (Ship Recycling Plans) SRFPs (Ship Recycling Facility Plans) and ‘Ready for Recycling’ certificates that are to be issued by the relevant flag on the select unit that is headed for recycling. And it is this inundation of local activities is also seeing delays in ward clearance of vessels as authorities scramble with multiple hats on at the same time.

While the inflow of tonnage this week was a welcome change from Bangladesh’s ongoing struggles, these will remain a hit or miss as local offerings come under pressure from domestic fundamentals once again as not only did local steel plate prices resume flatlining at region USD 552.58/Ton (a bad sign in light of local steel mills fixing steel imports at nearly half that), but even the recently stronger Bangladeshi Taka found itself in the crosshairs of depreciation as it fell nearly 70 basis points to break past BDT 121 again, ending the week at BDT 121.63 against the U.S. Dollar.

On the domestic front, there is still no news about the intended elections that were to be held at the end of June as the interim government continues to struggle getting key economic factors under control, including the incessant volatility of the Bangladeshi Taka. Trump’s recent announcement of levying 30% tariffs on Bangladesh has so far not hit the domestic ship recycling industry as it is primarily targeted towards the ‘Ready Made Garments’ (RMG) and textile and spinning Mills. Notwithstanding, an impact on the overall economy is expected and will likely only get worse, unless preventive economic measures are not formulated by the finance deparment as the country’s Government meanwhile tries to diffuse the situation working with the U.S. and find a reasonable solution to ongoing trade.

Overall, other than having the misfortune of opening in the Far East at a time Chattogram levels are the lowest in the sub-continent (and will likely remain there for the time being), owners of relevant units find themselves likely taking hits on their units, as the state of the Bangladeshi market conveniently delivers WC India / Pakistan into 2025’s monsoon recycling spotlight.

### India

- **Headline**: FLUMMOXED!
- **Key Notes**: *Marginal gains.* | *LNGs talk.* | *HKC ready.* | *Throuple*

As tariff dilemmas get kicked further down the road with various deadlines now looming large, ship recycling prices in Alang have shown marginal improvements over the course of last week, on the back of recently firming local steel plate prices and the Indian Rupee, which firmed to Rs. 84.9X against the U.S. Dollar in recent times. Yet, Alang recyclers were left flummoxed this week as both local steel plate prices and the Indian Rupee registered declines as steel levels fell about USD 2/Ton back into USD 428/Ton territory, while the Indian Rupee faced a 37-basis point penalty against the U.S. Dollar, backtracking its way to Rs. 86.15 as the week ended.

Notwithstanding, despite some of subdued supply of vessels starting to bite, Alang recyclers have managed to surprise of late and even this week as they took in over 42K LDT, including a couple of decent LDT units. Moreover, as some larger MOSS type LNGs (rich in non-ferrous) have been under negotiations over recent weeks, this has seen several eager Alang recyclers step up to the bidding tables in an effort to fill dormant plots once again, barely missing the mark against their Bangladeshi competition.

Meanwhile, given the current recycling state of the neighbouring competition, Alang’s anchorage might be the one to come out on top, at least until the rains are nearing an end / are over as HKC hurdles continue to fracture the ability of both Bangladeshi and Pakistani recyclers from freely negotiating on any available unit(s), restricting them to cherry pick on only the right unit instead and make their HKC efforts worth the unit at the given price. India on the other hand, faces no such restriction(s) having over 100 HKC certified yards in operation, some that have been certified for nearly a decade now. But with fundamentals acting funny again and mixed news on the tariffs front forthcoming week after week, Indian bids might see a displacement on the market rankings, sooner rather than later.

In the interim, however, Alang remains the safest bet at this time and levels aren’t expected to shake too far below recent offerings, always subject to the condition of some of the newly introduced aging old ‘rusties’ making it to local anchorages that have seen renegotiations on the rise once again. A safe price with a reliable cash buyer always makes for a safe delivery, especially in current times when rules regulations and fundamentals form an unlikely throuple that has seen hiccups arise across recent deliveries.

### Pakistan

- **Headline**: SHAPING UP!
- **Key Notes**: *Top placed.* | *Dry bulk mostly.* | *Taking seriously.*

Invariably finding themselves placed atop the global ship recycling rankings seems to have pushed Pakistani buyers into a renewed sense of aggression to negotiate on (and even acquire) some of the slim pickings of vessels that have been making the rounds of late (primarily, locally preferred dry bulk vessels). And this has manifested itself impressively at Gadani’s waterfront this week as currently 2 vessels are idling outside, including another decent LDT arrival this week, both of whom are awaiting their DASR formalities and a fresh set of permissions before making it on to their final deliveries at the respective recyclers yards.

As it has been evident by the type of deliveries at neighboring destinations, Gadani recyclers do not have a sophisticated local non-ferrous market that has resulted in this location consistently undervaluing high value non-ferrous assets. Moreover, for Gadani yards that have initiated HKC infrastructure upgrades and have received provisional DASR certs from the ministries in Pakistan, a growing demand has been emerging amongst these buyers over recent weeks.

Surprisingly so, given that not only have local steel plate prices resumed flatlining at USD 615/Ton, but even the Pakistani Rupee continues its Lira-styled nose-dive as it dropped another 14 basis points against the U.S. Dollar and ended the week at PKR 284.73, on its way to likely breaching PKR 285 and giving another reason for local recyclers to justify lower offers.

Yet, economic problems continue on the domestic front as reportedly, tax withholding on services has been raised form 9% to 15%, which could indirectly increase variable costs for the country’s ship recycling industry, as industry pundits call the government’s recent move, an act of economic- sabotage. How this will help Pakistan maintain top spot in the rankings remains to be seen.

For now, as the Gadani gang gets back to business of filling idle plots and with a few provisional DASRs still reportedly available for some local recyclers who are looking for units, it is good to finally see Pakistan take the imposition of the HKC seriously, as for a period of time it looked as though the market there may have been becoming redundant, they were so far competing India and Bangladesh where their own yard upgrades stood.

We maybe witnessing an exciting end of July / early August for Pakistan after a long time.

### Turkey

- **Headline**: ON THE DL!
- **Key Notes**: *Natural evolution.*

Aliaga’s ship recycling sector seems poised for a price correction as the Lira continues to collapse without mercy, falling another 50 basis points this week ,closing it out at nearly USD 40.40 against the U.S. Dollar, all while local steel plate prices remain subdued at their recently declined levels and offering very little motivation for local recyclers to maintain current offerings, despite many having expected a decline much sooner.

How ongoing tariffs and the declining Lira will affect Turkey’s ship recycling turned cash buying community, remains to be seen. For now, Aliaga buyers ended the week on an empty note and with no fresh vessels having arrived the nation’s recycling front.
