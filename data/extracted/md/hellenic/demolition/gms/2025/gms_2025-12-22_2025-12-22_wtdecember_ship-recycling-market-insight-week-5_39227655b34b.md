WT…DECEMBER! 
This year doesn’t cease to surprise, as December just showed what true volatility can be — in a mere 
two consecutive weeks at that. What a week this has certainly been, where freight rates continue to 
fall, the U.S. Dollar forgets which way is up, oil rises yet remains low, local steel plate prices slip 
oddly across the board at the various destinations, all while Turkey enjoys a forced retirement from 
the sidelines, watching its currency values fall ever deeper into the abyss. 
To start, inflation figures that were never released in the U.S. for October due to the government 
shutdown did come out for November. In Trump’s “hottest country in the world,” his regime 
reported inflation back down to 2.7% last month. India and Bangladesh were both up, but share the 
same reasons to celebrate, and Turkey was mercifully down as well. The Baltic Exchange Dry Index 
reported an overall drop of 2.3% this week, brought on by capes, panamax, and smaller vessels 
collectively dragging the Baltic down by 1.4%, nearly 5%, and 36 basis points, respectively. 
This could likely be brought on by the mixed signals out of the U.S. economic inflation figures, along 
with oil futures that barely rose 73 basis points and close the week out at a globally welcome low of 
USD 56.86/barrel. As freight rates continue to fall, a collection of market and private fixtures started 
to announce their arrivals across sub-continent beaches as famished recyclers await upcoming 
deliveries. And the U.S. Dollar? I’ll let the rest of the report speak for itself. 
Meanwhile, with Christmas just around the corner, we can reflect on what a disastrous yet pivotal 
year of upgrades and change across the Indian sub-continent ship recycling markets (and even the 
ship recycling industry writ large) it has been, with the Hong Kong Convention for safe and 
responsible recycling finally coming into force on June 26 this year. With this landmark set of 
initiatives and guidelines finally in force, it is encouraging to see most of the previously 100+ yards 
HKC-certified by various classification bodies in Alang, 22 yards now in Bangladesh, and only one 
in Gadani (the rest clearly have some catching up to do) fully operational to a set of globally
approved standards. 
And just like the previous year, we have seen even lower levels of supply this year, following a 2024 
that saw decade-low levels of supply. Although the performance of freight markets across all sectors 
has been largely responsible for the ongoing starvation in the supply of tonnage, could recent 
declines indicate a positive change for the ship recycling industry come 2026? Perhaps the timing in 
the shortage of supply was apt, as HKC upgrades and adapting to the new rules and regulations —
not only for recyclers, but even local authorities to come to grips with — certainly helped everyone.
For 2026, as the average age of a trading vessel climbs from 25 years to 30 years in some cases, and 
’90s-built bulkers, tankers (albeit in the shadow fleet), and containers aplenty are still trading — and 
due to be phased out to accommodate newbuilding vessels hitting the water next year — this should 
hopefully serve up a buffet for the industry. Fingers crossed, Santa!

WILD YEAR! 
While some might see that Bangladesh has endured an positive overall year where ship recycling 
is concerned — given the progress toward HKC-certified recycling, with 22 domestic ship recycling 
yards now HKC-accredited by various international bodies and work continuing in the 
background on a couple more yards — and given that it has enjoyed more time atop the market 
rankings than its regional neighbors for a majority of the year, offering regionally opening tonnage 
that extra USD/ton in the bank for all parties involved, that’s about where the parade of pride 
would likely end. 
On the flip side? Literally everything has declined in Bangladesh in 2025. To start, after breaching 
the USD 600/ton mark back in January 2024, local offerings have now declined by almost USD 
200/LDT down to the low USD 400s/LDT, and certainly lower now that a recent surge of tonnage 
has local bellies full for the time being. Tariffs, the taka, steel plate prices, and a dearth in the supply 
of tonnage has left this market frustrated and deprived of any regular flow of business sense amid 
exasperating yard upgrades and infrastructure work, thanks to unexpectedly firm freight trading 
rates across the board. 
And this week was probably 2025’s cherry for Bangladeshi recyclers, as not only did the U.S. Dollar 
gain 20 basis points — pulling the taka up to BDT 122.30 once again — but local steel plate prices 
truly stole the spotlight, plummeting a massive USD 12/ton in just five days and closing the week 
out at USD 485/ton. To remind you, they stood at just over USD 600/ton not 45 days ago, which 
makes this an instrumental decline behind the ongoing strength of local offerings. And amid the 
HKC celebrations, we lost track of the fact that Bangladesh has now gone from a market of about 
35 ship recycling yards to only 22 (even though a few more are reportedly still in the plans). 
For the nation at large, it’s been even worse: 30% tariffs from Trump; the interim government doing 
its best to redefine lethargy; a monsoon that betrayed local yard-upgrade operations; interim 
confusion around inward-delivery procedural formalities causing massive delays during August 
to October across the waterfront — all gift-wrapped in recently resurging political violence that 
even saw the untimely death of a local electoral candidate, with riots re-erupting at key locations 
across the nation and business closures once again seemingly imminent as the date of the upcoming
elections draws nearer. 
Overall, although we can recognize that a large number of LNGs — even for select HKC-only yards 
for green recycling — were concluded to select end buyers in Bangladesh this year and the nation 
has now started to feasibly compete with India on these specialist units, it is 2026 that remains 
pivotal for the nation overall as not only does a hopeful inflow of tonnage likely lie in wait, but the 
upcoming elections should hopefully jolt the nation’s economy back into gear, just in time for the 
pre-monsoon shopping bonanza to take on a new meaning.

INDIA
TAKING THE PRIZE! 
India has certainly taken the 2025 prize for having the most torrid year of declines experienced by a 
ship-recycling destination in recent memory, so drastic has the overall drop over the last quarter been.
And it’s not just prices — like Bangladesh, a smorgasbord of tail-spinning events colluded to drag 
Alang down across nearly all of 2025, and the fact that this market somehow managed to take in the 
most tonnage and/or HKC units of any ship recycling destination across 2025 while still retaining the 
title of epicenter of the recycling world across late Q3/early Q4 — and that too in the face of declining 
local offerings — is a stellar retelling of the convolutions of the ship recycling world. 
Levels have certainly dipped below USD 400/LDT for many competitive, and even green, units that 
have consequently found their way to competing markets in Bangladesh and even Pakistan at different 
points this year. Worse yet? As the previous leading vanguard market in terms of creating the path on 
HKC development work, it was certainly frustrating to not see Alang use that to its full potential at 
any point this year (despite the ongoing shortage of tonnage), or that it will likely ever use it to its full 
potential again — especially as Pakistani yards start stacking the competitive odds across the entirety 
of the Indian sub-continent as well. 
Fundamentals, fundamentals — where do we even start? The Rupee has turned into the bootleg paint-
huffing cousin that everyone somehow aware of, suffering several drops, recoveries, relapses, and 
recurrent recoveries since Q3 2025, and it took on a whole new energy spike this week as it jumped 
against the U.S. Dollar by 1.07%, regaining territory back into the INR 89s once again and closing the 
week out at INR 89.57. Local steel plate prices, especially, seemed to have forgotten what gravity is, 
reportedly firming a massive USD 10/ton across five days, nearly wiping out recent losses and bringing 
an added layer of optimism about the next few weeks. 
Moreover, some market/private deals seemed to mercifully slip into Alang’s waterfront this week and 
should keep local recyclers distracted in the face of ongoing sufferance(s), with nearly 26K LT worth of 
deliveries to look forward to — including a couple of decent LDT tankers. 
With a hopeful 202X (another in the series of declining years) coming up, and the outlook being backed 
by declining freight rates and ever-aging units facing imminent surveys, let’s hope the industry gives 
the HKC world a true 2026 workout. 
Vanguard market.

PAKISTAN
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
    
ONLY WAY IS UP! 
And for Pakistan, standing on the precipice of 2026, that remains a fact. Not only is it fantastically 
encouraging to see the first ship recycling yard gain HKC certification last month, but with more 
reportedly set to join the ranks within Q1 2026 and 2026 hopefully finally shaping up to be a 
healthier year in terms of supply, Gadani has certainly lined itself up to put a serious dent in the 
global ship recycling post-HKC era, where redistribution of recyclable tonnage is concerned. 
Unfortunately, given Pakistan’s ongoing and serious HKC handicap and despite being placed 2nd
on the market rankings (in terms of pricing), DASR approvals have done their utmost to ensure 
regular business remains a nightmare. With incoming tonnage being regularly subjected to delays, 
it is no surprise that the industry remains wary of a Pakistan redelivery. 
Case in point: despite being the only recycling destination in the Indian sub-continent that is not 
tide-reliant and has the least restrictive inward requirements, vessels in a post-HKC era seem to be 
regularly facing delivery delays, as evidenced by the currently idling mini-VL that has been sitting 
at anchorage for nearly 20 days now. 
Domestic fundamentals too have notably done more to help than hurt Gadani of late, as local steel 
plate prices jumped by over USD 4/ton and closed the week out near USD 580/ton — an industry-
leading margin at present. Moreover, the recently firming PKR — which found itself back down 
around PKR 280 (from PKR 283) — lost 13 basis points but still closed the week out at PKR 280.30.
Overall, as work continues locally and more yards are set to join the global HKC fray, and as long 
as fundamentals hold their ground, Gadani is certainly set to have a busy year ahead — especially
for the HKC-certified crew.

ENJOYING THE VIEW? 
Aliaga recyclers seem to have relegated themselves to a forced holiday, as prevailing government 
restrictions fine-tuned the madness of the ongoing shortage of tonnage and pushed this market 
further away from industry attention across all of 2025. And with little else to report beyond the lira’s 
ongoing ballet as it lost another 27 basis points against the U.S. Dollar this week and closed it out at 
TRY 42.81, edging ever closer to an imminent breach of TRY 43. 
Inflation last month, however, took a notable drop, falling nearly 2% down to TRY 31% (from 32.87% 
in October) and marking its lowest reading since November 2021. As domestic prices for virtually 
everything pivot south for the winter, this should also help Aliaga recyclers manage their operational 
margins a little better — especially in the face of an ever-declining Lira. 
With 2026 showing signs of promise, let’s hope Turkey’s current pricing trajectory holds long enough 
for local recyclers to step back into the game once again.
