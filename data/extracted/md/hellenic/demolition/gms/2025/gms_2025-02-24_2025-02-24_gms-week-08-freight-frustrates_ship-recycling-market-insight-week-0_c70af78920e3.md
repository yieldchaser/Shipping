A recent and ongoing uptick in freight rates across many sectors (especially dry, as wet rates have 
been performing far admirably in comparison) has resumed depriving global ship recycling markets 
of tonnage over the last couple of weeks and this has promptly manifested itself at the various sub-
continent waterfronts this week in both India and even Pakistan that has now returned to an empty
port position once again, resulting in a quieter time of late. Even the struggling Panamax sector saw 
a bounce in rates this week with owners of vintage 90s-built units seemingly managing to fix their 
vessels out yet again, eking out the ongoing remnants of earnings before their already life-extended 
assets eventually head for recycling. Whilst January saw a plethora of sales from the Panamax 
bulker and LNG sectors before the inevitable slowdown over Chinese New Year holidays and the 
release of Trump’s tariffs both of which delivered a fresh serving of market volatility, as further 
tariffs reportedly still remain in the works.  
Speaking of tariffs, while the ones already implemented have kept the oil markets subdued of late,
those tariffs still in the pipeline have pulled the barrel back closer to USD 70/barrel this week, as
recent sanctions coupled with growing U.S. oil reserves could continue to see the barrel buoying
around the early USD 70s in the near future. Whilst global economies adjust around these unfolding 
market dynamics, key fundamentals continue to see their shares of volatility given that local steel 
plate prices are either vacationing / laying horizontal in key markets while registering declines in
others, and the U.S. Dollar, which remains of key concern for ship recycling nation currencies, which 
continue to decline whilst others struggle to re-adjust / likely to lose further ground. 
As a result, ship recycling markets offerings have fallen by up to USD 30/LDT over the first month 
of the year and while further pressures on key drivers seem to have been working in unison to pull
recycling offers further down, the lack of tonnage has seemingly found a price floor around USD 
450/LDT despite indications far below on certain units are easily forthcoming. Now that workable
and firm candidates are drying up, the ever-present hunger to buy might return in the final month 
of Q1 2025 as we have even seen Pakistan come back into the picture of late, securing a smaller LDT
unit after a significant period on the sidelines. But it remains too little too late for a market that has 
seen just one arrival at the nation’s waterfront since Qct 2024. Moreover, despite Bangladeshi ship 
recyclers picking up the pace on respective facility and infrastructure upgrades, with the HKC 
finally entering into force by July of this year and urgent / pending yard upgrades are still needed 
in Chattogram and especially in Gadani, to ensure compliance with the convention now that a 
potentially bumper few years of tonnage supply and deal making lie ahead. As Alang buyers note 
the increasing presence of the Gadani Gang trying to take a bite out of their tonnage buffet at the 
bidding tables, they remain the epicenter of ship recycling West of Bangladesh with nearly all yards 
ready to operate post July 1 and business being as usual thereafter. Finally, Turkey continues to 
dissolve into its economic uncertainty as no meaningful market news has been forthcoming other 
than the steady descent of the Lira to where it is gradually approaching to half the value of the 
Indian Rupee. Times are changing.

REALIZING REDUCTIONS! 
Having endured an impressively active & busy start to the year by absorbing nearly all of the
tonnage that has emanated from the Far East, January has certainly helped Chattogram stay busy
through recent times, which have certainly been the most trying since 2008. And while external
factors remain responsible for the supply of tonnage, massive internal problems still ongoing have
left the domestic ship recycling sector in a state of stasis across much of Q3 - Q4 2024; a time that
looks like it may be returning once again now that freight rates are firming are on the rise now. 
For domestic, and a majority of now satiated ship recycling yards that have been busy taking in a
bulk of Far Eastern deliveries, has continued even this week where over 52K LDT and 7 vessels are
at anchorage / being delivered to their new owners. Ironically, the rate of recent deliveries seems
to outline a budding indicator of the positive movement of recycled ship’s steel from Chattogram
ship recycling yards that continue to trade with local steel mills at a level that has flatlined the one
of longest in the industry since October 2024, albeit a relatively healthier (than local offers on ships)
USD 529.41/Ton. As steel trade resumes amidst domestic infrastructure projects that remain
hanging in the balance, the spate of recent deliveries is expected to keep local ship recyclers busy
well into Q2 before seeing their return to the bidding tables just prior to Bangladesh’s budget,
which is expected around June of 2025. And although this should buy some time for domestic
businesses to prepare for the eventual FY 25 – F26 budget, it also afford them the time to initiate
yard upgrades in the interim.  
Purchasing abilities of local recyclers has left them atop the recycling market rankings as
Chattogram Choppers continue to table the best levels and some even higher than prevailing
figures, especially if a particular unit of interest comes to the fore. But it has been the Taka that has
delivered the most downward pressure on recycling offers as it nearly breached BDT 122 once 
again this week, only to firm up towards the end of the week and still end it 20 basis points weaker
at BDT 121.53. And although personal incoming U.S. Dollar remittances from  overseas to families
saw a healthy amount added to their national reserves, the economic condition of the nation state
remains far from healthy as stresses from the lack of economic reform are eroding profits from local
industries and the private sector, and the 6 month report sheet of the interim government has
resulted in a growing economic fragility of the future for the country for the rest of 2025. 
At the recycling end, this pressure remains tantamount as most yards have yet to initiate upgrades
to their facilities to HKC standards after the BSBRA extended the deadline to commence works
from January 31st to March 31st and failure to react in time could see even the nation’s ship recycling
sector face further headwinds and likely see Bangladesh lose to India for the top spot. For now
however, this market continues to bask in glory of dominating the tonnage inflow from the East
whilst its price differential with neighboring competitors stands less than the delivery cost further
West…primarily on smaller LDT units.

SHORT SHARP SHOCK! 
Like the rest of the world, India has not been immune to varying crises and tariff shocks that have 
sent ship recycling economies into a freefall, as Alang recyclers themselves continue to constantly 
adjusting to the varying degrees of supply amidst disastrous fundamentals despite which, has driven 
Alang buyers to maintain second place in the market rankings. And through the smorgasbord of the 
craziness of current time, the nation’s recycling waterfront stands on the edge of an empty port 
position with just one vessel reported to have arrived anchorage this week, albeit a couple of vessels 
are currently lying in wait awaiting inward permissions, including a reportedly sanctioned tanker.  
At the broader end of things, the Rupee remains the most disastrous of entities for Alang recyclers 
as it nearly breached INR 87 against the U.S. Dollar, only to settle the week unchanged from last at 
Rs 86.60. And while local recyclers went into a tailspin over PM Trump’s raft of tariffs introduced last 
week including a healthy 25% on Chinese steel and aluminum products, Chinese steel plate prices 
have continued to flatline over the last few weeks and that too at levels far below those from India, 
resulting in Indian steel plate prices dipping even further this week by a couple of Dollars, totaling a 
near USD 15/Ton decline over the last 3 weeks. As the only sub-continent destination to face declines 
in both fundamentals, the pressure on local pricing has been the most imminent at the bidding tables, 
as Alang buyers conservatively approach available tonnage as it starts to dissipate once again.  
While PM Modi was in the U.S. to welcome Trump into his second term in office and discussions 
pertaining to his administrations Indian outlook were reportedly tabled, pending tariffs still in the 
pipeline could see some of the highest tariffs in the world on products imported from India, amidst 
an already reported decline in exports for the first time!  
Overall, even though Alang remains competitive (even with Bangladesh for certain units) at the 
bidding tables and for the time being, an increasingly eager Pakistan and the ongoing downturn in 
Alang offerings since the start of this year does seem like the present may not merely be a short sharp 
shock to the system rather than a prolonged malaise that many are now fearing.

IS IT TIME?! 
The Pakistani market has been and will continue to remain one of the most confusing markets to
peg down given that local buyers are clearly not (and truly never have) followed the direction of
domestic fundamentals in determining just how to remediate local pricing, choosing instead to 
follow the direction of offerings from competing neighbors, and this has helped them set a price 
ceiling is already well below the capacity of what Gadani ship recyclers can table, but what remains
in question is their capability given that they are and have been the lowest placed at the bottom of
the market rankings while simultaneously struggling to secure any tonnage, as a result of. 
To start, when compared to global recycling destinations, the Pakistani Rupee has displayed the
greatest stability against the U.S. Dollar as it has stayed between PKR 277 – PKR 280 over the last
4 quarters including this week, where despite losing 22 basis points, it still ended the week at USD 
279.48. Simultaneously, local steel plate prices remain the highest in the industry at USD 643.78
although having flatlined the next longest. While this in itself, should more than justify a healthier
level of offerings at the bidding tables from the Gadani gang, they have in fact, barely managed to 
improve their levels in any positive direction over recent quarters.  
And although competing (and simultaneously flatlined) Chinese plate prices have stalled at a far 
more competitive level near USD 300/Ton, this has invariably helped domestic steel mills stay
afloat with far cheaper imports. So why then is this market still not re-adjusting to the influx, just
to keep the net return on its only delivery of 2025 in the green or even for those that still are / will
be in the pipeline? As uncertain as this question remains, Pakistan remains fairly close behind their
Indian sub-continent competitors in pricing amidst a return of tonnage scarcity that has seen
Gadani’s anchorage return to an empty one. 
The domestic economy remains of primary concern and although international remittances by
families has helped maintain a degree of equilibrium in the nation’s treasury reserves, the IMF has
downgraded Pakistan’s forecast for 2025 citing ongoing governmental irregularities and reported
mismanagement of funds, including the fear of the army taking on an larger role in the nation’s
governmental and economic affairs, leaving the question just as to when it will be before Pakistan’s
ship recycling sector is able to get back in business at the bidding tables vessels with a degree of
regularity.  
As it has been a tough few years on the sidelines for the Paksitani market, buying minimal vessels
and still far from initiating meaningful yard upgrades in order to become HKC compliant ahead 
of the official HKC entry into force in June this year (meaning non-compliant yards will not be able
to import any tonnage from July 1st), frustrations at the bidding tables are starting to build up at 
losing so many vessels to India and Bangladesh since the start of this year and this fervor could be
a good thing for Pakistan moving forward, as long as the Gadani Gang positively uses this
momentum.

DUKES OF HAZZARD! 
Aliaga recyclers have been hemorrhaging the worst of the ship recycling world since 2023 on the 
back of crashing economic fundamentals, a bevy of ship recycling regulations imposed by the EU,
weakening import and local steel plate prices, a Lira that has declined nearly 492% against the U.S. 
Dollar over the last five years (nearly a 1,355% decline if you go back a decade), a shortage of tonnage 
over the last 13 months, and save for the heady days of cruise vessels that once lined the shores of 
Aliaga, a near absence of market fixtures that has reportedly left some local buyers having to
temporarily shutter their yards while the bigger names have reportedly survived by negotiating off-
market smaller LDT tonnage emanating from regional waters. 
And like last week, the Dukes of Hazzard have nothing to report this week as well, as another silent 
week / month / Quarter comes to an end.
