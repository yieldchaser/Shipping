# Market Commentary

## STUMPED, YET PRIMED!

Global market chaos continues post-Thanksgiving like a bad stomach after too much turkey. Steel plate prices were the highlight of the entire sub-continent—and even China—where they unanimously retreated in unison this week, while the U.S. Dollar also decided to join in on the fun as it too retreated at all sub-continent locations (except—no surprise—Turkey!). Making matters worse are the rising freight markets that continue to surprise the industry, as the Baltic Exchange’s Dry Index climbed another 3.2% through the course of the week, hitting a new high since December 2023 as practically every segment assisted in with the rising rates. Oil, meanwhile, remains at 21st-century lows, idling with marginal fluctuations, yet it closed the week out at USD 59.16/Ton—a near 14% drop over the last 12 months.

As supply continues to be restricted by the widening net of sanctions and blacklists in addition to rising freight rates, it was still a kind gesture by the gods of tonnage that both Bangladesh and India mercifully reported fresh arrivals and deliveries at the waterfront, all while Pakistan’s dithering local anchorage returned zilch this week. Prices too remain fragile amidst dreadful fundamentals that haven’t gotten a chance to get off their respective trampolines, as most of the tonnage introduced to the markets is either an off-market questionably sourced unit or smaller LDT units that are regularly seeing sub-USD 400/Ton from key locations, creating a dual-priced reality for the markets.

Yet in the midst of uncertainty, a momentous development unraveled this week with the first Pakistani ship-recycling yard to be awarded HKC compliance, as Prime Green Recyclers became the first in the nation to join the array of international yards—bringing Pakistan into the new league. Prime Recyclers attained Bureau Veritas (BV) blessings after the requisite investment and infrastructure upgrades spanning this past year bore fruit, and the matter became critical after the Hong Kong Convention came into force on June 26th. This follows on the heels of 21 Bangladeshi yards receiving their HKC accreditations and a 22nd yard set to reportedly follow imminently, as compliance is firmly underway at both locations to revolutionize and improve domestic standards and catch up to Alang, where over 100 yards have been HKC compliant—some practically for over a decade now.

Overall, as market fundamentals remain confidently uncertain, prices have endured a turbulent ride across the board over the last few months. USD 400/LDT+ remains a struggle to achieve in some markets as steel and currencies continue to underperform, alongside the deadly dearth of candidates that has plagued markets for about four years now, with only a handful of sales taking place to increasingly hungry recyclers—particularly in a resurgent Bangladesh, which led the way for sub-continent pricing for another week. Another four weeks and another trip around the sun means the industry continues hoping for a market resurgence in 2026. Tough year!

# Bangladesh

## IN VOGUE!

*Margin notes: HKC focus. | Dollars and Cents | Work ongoing. | Uncomfortable.*

The focus in Bangladesh over the last five months has been clearly targeted at bringing domestic ship-recycling yards up to speed, with tremendous progress having been made on HKC infrastructure upgrade work. Twenty-one yards are now approved (under NK, IR, BV classes), and another yard has reportedly reached completion, taking the total to 22 set to follow shortly. While this is certainly encouraging news for the industry as a whole, on an individual basis the fractured state of supply continues to make ship recyclers at these yards wonder if such gratuitous expenses during a four-year spell of tonnage drought have been financially worth it, because at the end of the day, it’s all dollars and cents.

And deservedly so. The last four years have brought massive instability to the nation via skyrocketing and volatile inflation, a Bangladeshi Taka that has dragged local pricing down— collapsing from the highs of USD 600/Ton to USD 400/Ton since January 2024—political instability, national riots, uncontrollable monsoons, cheaper steel imports that have, for the first time, hammered local sentiments in unexpected ways, and local steel plate prices that are escaping down a chute like a bad dinner.

Speaking of local steel plate prices, they fell even further this week (as they have for several weeks now), dropping USD 11/Ton and closing the week at USD 506/Ton—a massive fall from above USD 600/Ton and now potentially approaching sub-USD 500/Ton through December. The Taka, on the contrary, managed to escape last week’s fate, firming nearly 30 basis points and ending the week at BDT 122.08 against the U.S. Dollar.

The nation’s prevailing sentiments also continue to remain fragile as the interim government stays in a paralytic state, and tensions and bursts of local political violence remain scattered across the nation as the upcoming elections of February 2026 linger on the collective minds of the populace. Steel imports also continue to affect the resale values of recycled ships’ steel that remains at local yards, even though incoming imports over the last couple of weeks indicate local resale movements and emptying plots that have respective yard owners eager to take in units. This week’s anchorage certainly reflected that sentiment as a collection of various sub-10K units earmarked the waterfront.

Pricing meanwhile has managed to remain the most impressive in the sub-continent (although that isn’t saying much given the quantum of declining levels across the industry), while levels over USD 400/LDT are regularly available for larger LDT, good-condition units, with smaller vessels generally taking the pricing brunt well below USD 400/LDT—if any interest were to arise at all. As such, the next quarter is certainly of interest to those with tonnage to sell

# India

## LOSING HOPE!

*Margin notes: Below 400. | Diversions. | Culprits.*

A lowly Alang market remains positioned below USD 400/LDT on most tonnage and is thus out of the picture for the collection of competitive vessels that have been making the rounds of late. Making matters even more challenging is their inability to negotiate effectively on HKC tonnage for select local yards, as Bangladesh is clearly catching up with a larger fraction of HKC yards—and even Pakistan finally entering the ring.

Indeed, even favored LNG vessels rich in non-ferrous (if MOSS type) have found their retirement spots in Bangladesh in recent times, so poor have levels from Alang been recently. Local anchorage, however, mercifully managed to take in their regular share of questionable tankers, which have been finding India a convenient location to offload.

What has been really affecting the local purchasing mindset are the key fundamentals: not only did the Indian Rupee plummet into the high INR 89s—despite firming 28 basis points against the U.S. Dollar this week and closing out at INR 89.35—but local steel plate prices fell USD 5/Ton this week, clocking out at USD 390/Ton.

Yet the Indian economy has been generating positive results, as the nation’s GDP climbed 8.2% on the back of improvements in the construction and manufacturing sectors, prompting industry pundits to improve future growth prospects for the economy.

Even so, recycling remains in shambles as competing nations are comparatively higher placed and keep chipping into Alang’s HKC share, while fundamentals affecting the nation’s ship-recycling sector have clearly been the pricing culprits for this struggling market.

# Pakistan

## FIRST HKC!

*Margin notes: Big news! | Others to follow. | Status quo.*

This has truly been one of the most momentous weeks not only for Pakistan’s ship-recycling sector, but also for the nation’s ship-recycling future, as Prime Green Recyclers of Gadani have become the first HKC-approved facility in Pakistan, with BV announcing the momentous news this week.

This is a major milestone for the sector as it finally brings Gadani into the big leagues, and all it will take now is a willing group of shipowners and/or cash buyers to propose vessels locally and evaluate the efficacy of the upgrades and ensuing recycling processes. As several more yards are also undergoing HKC upgrades, it is likely we may see the next round of approvals come through reportedly within the next 3–4 months.

Yet, like India, the nation’s economy remains in a fragile state, as not only did local steel plate prices fall USD 7/Ton and wrap the week up at USD 579/Ton, but the Pakistani Rupee firmed a relatively minor 9 basis points and ended the week at PKR 282. While these numbers are certainly far better than those from competing India (helping the Gadani Gang maintain their position in the market rankings), the lack of HKC yards has punished Gadani’s anchorage by ending another week with an utterly empty waterfront.

As such, it has remained a quiet week in terms of sales and activity, and with the bulk of the tonnage delivered into Bangladesh clearly opening up in the Far East and India taking in units, the future for Gadani across December will likely maintain the status quo.
