# Market Commentary

## TURKEY, AND HAM…STRUNG!

As we head into the traditionally quieter festive / Christmas season, ship recycling markets appear to be hamstrung on the back of a stubborn and persistent lack of meaningful tonnage, violently collapsing fundamentals that were promptly followed by declining offers, and all of this ongoing non-stop for nearly all of 2025 that has seen purchasing sentiment in a post-HKC world get crushed under the weight of it all.

To start, the onset of December brought with it a declining Baltic Exchange that saw the Dry Index fall by about 3%, this week, dragged down by mounting pressure across all segments that saw capes slipping 4.4%, panamax dropping 1.4%, and the smaller segments shedding 5 basis points, all while oil barely moved the needle as it closed another week at region USD 59.70/barrel (expected to breach USD 60/barrel in the coming week) and inflation reported minor improvements at a couple of key ship recycling destinations.

But it were financial currencies that really battered both India and Bangladesh, all while Turkey perennially decorated the top three of the declining currency marathon that it alone has been on. Not wanting to be left behind, local steel plate prices also fell by just under USD 5/ton across all sub-continent recycling destinations, leaving ship recyclers firmly holding off on further negotiations as the week ended.

As such, the lack of tonnage has delivered yet another characteristically low supply of vessels for a few weeks now, especially considering the lack of the occasional large LDT LNG vessels (with around 15 sold so far this year) drying up as freight rates continue to remain at elevated overall levels, thus depriving ship recyclers for yet another week. Further cementing this fact was the sheer lack of arrivals at every sub-continent ship recycling location – probably a true first in its own right.

Thankfully, compulsion has successfully driven remarkable developments across the Indian sub-continent ship recycling sector, with HKC infrastructure upgrades still ongoing and a confirmed 23 HKC-certified ship recycling yards now added to the existing sub-continent fleet of HKC certified yards – including Pakistan’s first HKC approval that was announced just last week. Ever since the Hong Kong Convention came into force on June 26th earlier in the year, it has been encouraging to see increased investments and upgrades taking place at these new locations, ensuring a better and safer set of standards for responsible recycling.

Overall, 2025 will probably be the cherry-year of the last four and 2026 hopefully brings more tonnage and stability across the globe.

# Bangladesh

## WAIT CONTINUES!

*Margin notes: Cooling off. | Demand ripe. | LNGs to arrive. | Silent Christmas.*

After a fruitful month of HKC progress that included an impressive couple of weeks of tonnage deliveries, the Bangladeshi ship recycling market seemed to rapidly cool this week, ensuring that any of the slim pickings of units making the rounds at the bidding tables continued to see lower overall levels now that most Chattogram recycling yards had taken in at least one unit across November.

Adding to the disappearing flavor of the local purchasing mindset has been the curated collective of fundamentals that mercifully timed their arrival this week, after all local deliveries had been completed as not only did the BDT lose 35 basis points against the U.S. Dollar ending the week at BDT 122.35, but local steel plate prices also fell by about USD 5/Ton to end the week at USD 502/Ton. Clearly afflicted by the recent performance of fundamentals and other than a sea of deliveries of tonnage that in fact arrived last week, not a single fresh unit greeted Chattogram’s waterfront this week.

Through it all, and as stated in the Market Commentary above, HKC upgrades continue at pace locally as 22 ship recycling yards have now received their HKC accreditations since March this year and, as space slowly clears up on the back of declining plate levels that clearly suggest recycled material is now moving off local yards, local appetite seems to be getting its rumbles in what has historically been one of the busiest times of the year for businesses wanting to close their books.

Economically overall, inflation is expected to continue lingering around the high 8.x% range as an economic stagnation seems to fill the domestic environment, given that there have still not been any economic policies set in place to accelerate domestic growth by the interim government, which itself is aware of its impending dissolution come February 2026 on the back of the upcoming elections and a fresh new government finally taking the reins.

In the interim, local recyclers will likely keep themselves busy with several of the larger LDT LNGs that have ended up in Bangladesh over the last few months (including HKC select yards only from both Seapeak and MISC). It seems like it’s destined to be a silent Christmas after all.

# India

## SET ADRIFT!

*Margin notes: Little hope. | Poleaxed. | No advantage.*

India has been set adrift at the bottom of the Indian subcontinent ship recycling price board as Alang recyclers seem to be losing hope of securing any standard, competitive ships any time soon – and rightly so, given that freight markets continue to soar at impressive highs.

Buckled tighter by the increasing number of vessels being added to the list of international sanctions and blacklists as governments globally battle for economic attrition against embattled nations, Alang’s waterfront highlighted the situation incredibly well this week, with not a single fresh arrival reported.

Fundamentals have also been dragging the sentiment canoe down level 1 rapids as not only has the Indian Rupee depreciated against the U.S. Dollar once again by a massive 70 basis points and clock the week out at a deathly Rs. 89.96, but local steel plate prices also retreated a milder USD 4/Ton and end the week at nearly USD 387/Ton – clearly highlighting the upcoming levels of local offerings in the coming weeks.

Unlike their competing neighbours, most Alang yards have been HKC-approved for nearly a decade now, but the state of fundamentals has driven prices so low that local recyclers have simply been unable to hammer home the HKC advantage while competing markets are still having their yards upgraded and infrastructure brought up to HKC standards. Notwithstanding, Alang yards have been comparatively busier than their regional competition would seem to suggest.

Overall, until the supply of tonnage increases, there remains nothing to really report from these markets – other than the unfolding state of fundamentals.

# Pakistan

## TAILGATING!

*Margin notes: Primetime! | Brief spurt. | Taking stock.*

Prime Green Recyclers has become the first ever HKC-approved ship recycling yard not only in Gadani, but also the first facility to bring Pakistan into the international playground within 2025. And while progress reportedly continues locally at pace along with infrastructure upgrades, the rest of the sector remains in shambles as prices have largely tailgated India’s (no surprise there), even though they remain higher on the market rankings.

But the double whammy brought on by both an overall scarcity of tonnage along with a prevailing scarcity of HKC-approved yards has left Gadani’s anchorage empty once again this week – and that too for nearly a month now.

After a brief spurt of activity in September backed by comparatively decent levels of DASR assisted local deliveries, local fixtures have all but flatlined over recent weeks as end buyers continue their HKC development work and take stock of where the market may be heading next.

And rightly so, given that local steel plate prices continue to slip by the week as they fell nearly USD 5/Ton again and drifted further down to USD 575/Ton (from the highs of USD 650/Ton), as cheap Iranian imports continue to influence prevailing resale prices of recycled stock. Furthermore, the Pakistani Rupee miraculously firmed despite its depressed overall state as it gained an impressive 45 basis points against the U.S. Dollar and end the week at PKR 280.42.

Overall, a lot of work still lies ahead for Pakistan’s ship-recycling sector and the next batch of yards to achieve HKC approvals will probably be in Q1 2026, whilst several more yards in Bangladesh have infrastructure works underway and are expected to reach HKC status in the months ahead as well.

Slow times for slow times.

# Turkey

## FIRMING?

*Margin notes: Russian Roulette.*

A surprising couple of weeks not only saw import steel prices but even local steel plate prices firm by about USD 10/Ton, which in turn promptly transitioned to firmer levels from Aliaga recyclers that jumped by about USD 10/Ton as well.

What jumped as well was the value of the U.S. Dollar against the Turkish Lira, which slipped another 20 basis points and end the week at a disastrous TRY 42.55. Just how soon and effective will the government’s intended economic measures to assist the Lira’s value be, remains to be seen. Until then, it seems like Russian roulette with pricing, just to tempt in more tonnage.
