As we firmly pass the halfway point of 2025, July has continued on with global economies suffering 
in unison, on the back of erratic trade policies emanating from a White House that seems to have 
got the car in drive while reversing off a cliff. As Trump fails (as usual) at his ’90 deals in 90 days’
tour, the U.S. sees an unsurprising rise in inflation for the first time in 4 months. This rocked the 
trading markets to such an extent that not only did the U.S. Dollar shatter international 
performances going all over the board this week, but it also weakened against the Turkish Lira in a 
rather unexpected turn of events. Oil futures also continued to hurt as Trump threatens more 
sanctions against Iran, OPEC+ countries continue to vie for greater market share and affirm an 
increase in oil putout across July (based on current sentiments), and an ensuing uncertainty that 
saw oil end the week at USD 66.5/barrel. In keeping with the flow of the money (and expense), the 
Baltic Exchange recorded another drop this week (in a series of recent declines) that saw it fall 2.1%
this week, primarily on the back of the Cape index, which itself fell by about 5.3%, even though the 
Panamax and Supramax indices rose 1.5% and 0.9% respectively.  
This has continued to deliver an absence of recycling tonnage for the markets at the bidding tables, 
which has represented itself rather well at this week’s anchorages. And speaking of anchorages, 
recycling destinations have continued to go through a volley of market sentiments amidst their own 
disjointed market disorders that saw their currencies and steel plate prices dance around. HKC 
troubles for 1/3rd of the sub-continent recycling destinations has also left ship owners and cash 
buyers with minimal options to consider, especially as Pakistani authorities have stated that only
those yards who have committed to HKC upgrades will be issued provisional DASR certificates to 
import vessels for recycling in addition to mandatory IHMs, as Bangladeshi HKC recyclers are not 
only taking the time to get accustomed to the ‘new’ way of recycling, but also digest recently 
delivered tonnage at a pace and in accordance with the HKC. Indian recyclers, meanwhile, were the 
only ones to have some positivity with at least one fresh arrival and some positive movement in 
local steel plate prices. An abundance of HKC ready Alang yards also means that owners looking 
to take make a quick sale, still do have the option to. But, as the HKC firmly enters into force, 
recycling markets overall remain on a glum and uncertain footing as demand and prices have
slumped alarmingly over preceding weeks and the trajectory doesn’t seem much too different at 
this time. While recycling tonnage is seeing far lower levels, some exploitative levels have once 
again been making the rounds as end buyers test the waters to see what might just bite.  
Logically, there have been unremarkable sales concluded over the past month and there seem to be
chances of fixtures taking place below the USD 400/LDT mark as a poorer condition Handymax
bulker was privately concluded in the USD 390s/LDT this week, basis an expected Bangladesh
redelivery. There also remains the question of increased regulations and additional documentation 
required now that the Convention is firmly in effect. Bangladesh will not accept any non-HKC 
approved recyclers to import vessels. In Pakistan, it is at least encouraging to see the nation’s
recycling sector and regulatory bodies / authorities finally moving in the right direction, after years 
of indecisiveness. In Turkey, as always, other than the Lira, there has been some news.

LOW SHE GOES! 
For the first time this year, as the lowest placed Indian sub-continent ship recycling market,
Bangladesh remains worthy of avoiding for any owners or cash buyers with geographically better 
suited / Far East positioned units that are considering a recycling sale at this time. Prices and 
demand from Chattogram have been so low of late that any potential recycling vessels are being 
introduced with the intention of being diverted towards competing Pakistani and Indian markets 
further East, despite the higher delivery costs on hand, and the fact that there are simply no offers 
from Bangladeshi shores at this time. Local authorities have been the primary drivers behind 
drastically reducing the number of available buyers as at the time of writing, only about 12 yards 
are HKC approved and these have already taken in a batch of recent deliveries. An additional 10 
yards are reportedly still interested to remain in the game and are working towards upgrades of 
their own at this time. 
On the back of rapidly receding offers from Chattogram recyclers and as confirmed in the market 
commentary above, local port position is once again starting to trim in numbers as no new arrivals 
were reported this week, save for a couple of final deliveries. One of the key reasons behind the
collapsing level of local offerings has been the inability of local yards to sell recycled ship’s steel / 
recycled product of late given that not only have local steel plate prices declined over recent weeks, 
but this week, they practically fell off a cliff on account of maddening monsoons and local holidays
/ strikes that halted any domestic trade. Adding to the fluency of the situation is the inability of the 
interim government to stimulate any building / infrastructure projects since June of last year, and 
this has led to the gradually exacerbating situation of growing stockpiles of material at local yards,
which in is now diminishing demand and dampening local offerings.  
Yet, a local buyer surprised the industry this week with the private sale of a poorer condition 
Handymax bulker in the USD 390s/LDT, the first sale that registered below USD 400/LDT in quite 
some time in the industry. Making matters worse is the fact that domestic steel mills have 
reportedly booked cheaper imported steel products at levels region USD 330/Ton, which has 
further earmarked Chattogram to remain on an extended period on the sidelines and away from 
the bidding tables, unless the Bangladeshi government is able to implement restrictive measures 
to economically protect the country, or become the latest victim of the seemingly never-ending 
Indian sub-continent ‘cheaper steel import’ tariff war. 
Overall, the only market sale of the week into Bangladesh wasn’t also the only silver-lining for this 
market as the Bangladeshi Taka firmed nearly 20 basis points against the U.S. Dollar this week, 
withdrawing from the dreaded BDT 123 territory and back towards BDT 121, as it ended the week 
at BDT 122.45. Moreover, with pending yard upgrades, new recycling routines, and backed up 
inventory, all taking a swim in the deep end of 2025s monsoon season, perhaps a quieter may just 
be what the universe ordered.

INDIA 
 
SURFING THE RAINS! 
Alang recyclers find themselves in a comfortable, yet soaking wet predicament as on the one hand,
they are battling not only their local fundamentals, but also the ongoing shortage of tonnage amidst
the rains; yet on the other hand, all non-HKC yards in Bangladesh and Pakistan are tapped-out while 
those remaining (in Bangladesh) have just returned from the tonnage buffet – essentially leaving no 
one at the bidding tables other than Alang recyclers, who now find themselves as the only game in 
town despite being 2nd on the price rankings. Whether they can benefit off this unique position as 
tonnage shortage continues on, or even how long they will benefit off of it if it ever gets to it, remains 
to be another 2025 surprise. For now, Alang buyers are certainly one-up on the regional competition 
with at least one fresh arrival this week. 
Currently, with well over 100 fully HKC certified, long operational yards in Alang, it does seem rather 
eye-opening as we consider the fact there are only 12 recently HKC certified yards outside of India in 
the entire Indian sub-continent. Moreover, even this recent rush to finalise upgrades in neighbouring
Chattogram and Gadani has regrettably come at the very last moment as the Hong Kong Convention 
finally entered into force last week, which will see some teething issues before the remainder of
recycling yards become fully HKC-certified, and owners and cash buyers fully come to grips with the 
new realities, regulations, and documentation that are, and will be additionally required at all 
locations as the process streamlines over time, in order to continue importing vessels into this HKC 
only world of ship recycling.  
In keeping Alang’s position tempered on the market rankings is their perennial love triangle with the 
U.S. Dollar and local steel plate prices, which continues to keep the Indian market alert on caffeine as 
this was probably the first week where despite crazy jitters, local steel plate actually ended USD 2/ton 
higher than that of last, ending the week at USD 427.35. Even the Indian Rupee that has yoyo-ed 
unrelentingly since the start of the year between Rs. 84 – Rs 88, thankfully saw a week where the 
Rupee practically ended where it started, at about Rs. 85.45 against the U.S. Dollar. At the Macro end 
of things, while the week started with more positive news between Indian and American authorities 
in coming closer to a trade agreement, the week ended with trade negotiations reportedly souring 
and Indian authorities once again turning to imposing counter-tariffs on American imports, stalling 
the process once again. 
Overall, prices are expected to hold at current levels for the time-being, but if what is happening in 
Bangladesh with their domestic steel industry where cheaper steel import fixtures recently undercut 
the price of processed recycled ship’s steel, should that bleed into India as well (just as it has in the 
past), we may as well bid goodbye to USD 400/Ton, just as the markets bid goodbye to USD 500/Ton 
not over a quarter ago, and fundamentals continue to deliver some ‘baby mama drama’ for Alang 
throughout the year including this week.  
Mama drama.

PAKISTAN
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
    
ROLL CALL! 
The ship recycling industry in Pakistan finally achieved a breakthrough of sorts this week, as a
select group of about 10-yard owners have committed to upgrading their respective facilities up to
HKC standards, in order to continue operating and keep the nation relevant in the international
ship recycling arena. Unfortunately for Gadani recyclers, their decision to appear for roll-call has
come at a time when several deals have already been committed to end buyers at competing
destinations across all of June, given the yet to be upgraded / non-HKC-certified state of all Gadani 
ship recycling yards despite having the best levels on show in the world for the last couple of 
weeks at least. 
And although it will be good to have Pakistan along for the ride when local yards finally get up to 
speed, their absence from the bidding tables and their offerings (competitive or otherwise) will 
remain irrelevant in the interim! Thus far, we understand that the various authorities and the PSBA
will allow for vessels to be imported and issue provisional DASR certs to the yards that have 
committed to HKC upgrades and begun their infrastructure work to an acceptable standard, along 
with the inclusion of mandatory IHMs parts I, II and III that are now required to be onboard for 
entry to all recycling locations, including Pakistan. 
Of little consequence are local steel plate prices or the Pakistani Rupee, which has flatlined at USD 
616/Ton – the highest in the industry – while the Pakistani Rupee fell 24 basis points against the 
U.S. Dollar edging ever closer to PKR 285 as it closed the week out at about PKR 284.20. If cheaper 
Chinese steel imports that are currently hovering under USD 300/Ton flood the international 
markets as the excess steel continues to float around and just as it has done so in Bangladesh this 
week, the overall specter of retreating recycling prices will likely continue through Q3 as well, 
earmarking 2025 for another tragic year in the Pakistani ship recycling industry – just as 2024 and 
even 2023 was for the Gadani recycling gang when only 16 ships were delivered into this market 
throughout 2024. 
Gadani certainly has its work cut out for itself ahead as at least a month (if not two) of yard 
upgrades will be required before Pakistani ship recyclers return to the scene during which time, 
neighboring India will be the only choice in the interim.

TRAGIC WEEK! 
 
The Turkish business of ship recycling that is more a marriage of rules and restrictions than objective 
ship recycling, continues subjecting ship owner, cash buyers, and even ship recyclers to lengthy and 
process intensive rules and documentary requirements stemming from exporting countries to flag 
state requirements to IHM evidences – a process that is more dissuasive for international players or
even affords asset owners a cost-effective recycling alternative for that matter.  
And to top if all off, accidents can and will continue to happen despite all the red-tape and incorrectly 
applied regulations designed to control an industry and detract the natural flow off commerce 
(where global ship recycling is concerned), as demonstrated in Aliaga this week were a large ore 
carrier caught fire at an Aliaga recycling yard. While thankfully no injuries or casualties have been 
reported, it is poignant to note that this vessel could’ve easily been redirected towards an India re-
delivery during her negotiations, but political plays had her destiny charted out to Aliaga instead.  
If only common sense was more common.
