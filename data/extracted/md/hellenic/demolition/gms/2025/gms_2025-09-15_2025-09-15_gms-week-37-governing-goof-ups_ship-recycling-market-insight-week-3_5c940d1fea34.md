GOVERNING GOOF-UPS! 
As global conflicts rage on with no signs of resolution or abatement on the horizon, the ongoing and 
deliberate spectacle of political goof-ups continues to dictate the destiny of global freight rates as 
well as the ship recycling markets — with determined impunity. Escalatory and retaliatory moves 
kept key trading routes and markets on edge as freight rates propped up this week, with the Baltic 
Exchange Dry Index reporting a whopping 7.4% overall increase. Capesize, Panamax, and 
Supramax indices all firmed in unison to the tune of 1.0%, 0.4%, and 0.5% respectively. As trading 
rates jumped, so did crude oil—albeit marginally—ending the week at USD 62.74/barrel (vs. USD 
62.14/barrel at the start of the week). 
Mixed in with the craziness of tariffs is the growing and murky collection of sanctioned units that 
continue to trickle into the markets along with legitimate ones. This week also delivered an early 
Christmas surprise as tonnage that had been missing from the bidding tables for weeks on end 
somehow managed to find its way onto Indian sub-continent recycling beaches—marginally in
Bangladesh and en masse into India, with over 155K Tons LDT and a few behemoth LNGs arriving 
at both waterfronts. Certainly an “ice-cube down the back” kind of flashback moment to the glorious 
days of loaded recycling beaches. 
As we segue into individual sub-continent ship recycling destinations, with yard infrastructure 
upgrades ticking forward by the week, HKC approvals remain the bright spot in Bangladesh as 18 
ship recycling yards are now fully accredited to global standards. Pakistan remains far behind, with 
only news of upgrades coming forth, but the coveted “first yard in Pakistan to get HKC certified” 
title remains missing a participants list htus far. Currencies also nailed down the week’s pressure 
points—especially in India—as virtually all recycling nation currencies depreciated in the teens 
against the U.S. Dollar. Surprisingly, it wasn’t Turkey that took the largest currency hit of the week, 
even though there is one to report from here too. Steel plate prices, by contrast, flatlined across the 
spectrum—even in China—as fresh tariff rumors made the rounds once again. 
Overall, as the stasis of inactivity evaporated in Alang this week, India remained whipsawed by the 
INR’s ongoing collapse, and protectionist-minded headwinds from local buyers will likely greet 
sellers with tumbling demand and pricing in response. Bangladesh remains inert overall, as its 
interim regime fails to revive activity and beachings are like drops of rain in the desert. Pakistan 
stays eager but forcibly idle, unable to secure vessels while freight trades rich and local buyers 
remain unwilling to discard their precious DASRs on small LDT units. Turkey remains in the cellar, 
red tape intact, and activity nil.

BLEAK AND WEAK! 
The bleak situation in Bangladesh that started festering back in June 2024, has further metastasized 
into madness over the last 2–3 months and it seems unlikely to improve any time soon, as the local 
mindset continues to devolve week after week amid inglorious inactivity. The inert interim regime 
has failed to fire up any meaningful infrastructure projects, leaving local steel mills sourcing 
cheaper steel from imported sources and further darkening Chattogram's future as a ship recycling 
destination—at least until the next elections that are currently set six months away in February 
2026. Recently imposed HKC requirements have further burdened the otherwise industry-leading 
performance of this market, as local recyclers stagger through their HKC approvals at differing 
speeds, which explains the trickle of tonnage we have seen drip into this market since June 26, 2025.
And as the last couple of weeks saw no fresh units arrive or even local deliveries, this week 
surprised the industry as at least one top-tier local buyer jumped at the opportunity of snagging a 
passing behemoth as the only arrival of the week saw a near 30K LDT LNG carrier greet 
Chattogram’s inner anchorage. However, the prior empty weeks of vessel reports highlight a 
deeper malaise: a complete lack of demand or even interest to negotiate on, let alone import new 
vessels locally. Inventories of recycled ship steel at recycling plots also continues to pile up as 
recyclers remain unable to shift any of it at prices anywhere near what their vessels were concluded 
at. 
As a result (and if history has taught the industry about Bangladesh), it is likely that local steel 
plate prices are flatlining as recyclers attempt to withhold raw material and artificially induce 
demand from local steel mills by holding prices firm. Although this can help in the short term, 
markets always correct themselves, and the resulting renegotiations that left Bangladesh souring 
at the bottom end of market rankings in the past, will almost certainly redeliver once again. And 
this does appear to be the case now as well since steel values remained stuck at BDT 519.59/Ton 
throughout the week and tariff pressures nudged the Bangladeshi Taka higher by about 18 basis 
points, ending the week at BDT 122.02 against the U.S. Dollar. 
On the domestic front, however, despite an inert administration, HKC squabbles, deteriorating 
fundamentals, and impending tariffs creating rumbling tummies, the nation’s economy managed 
to find some impressive resilience as inflation fell from a high of 8.55% back in July to 8.28% as 
August closed, the lowest inflation the nation has endured since July 2022. And clearly not 
intentional on the part of this or prior administrations. Yet local recyclers remain unable to get the 
gears of recycling turning enough to attract even those vessels positioned in the Far East or even 
smaller LDT units as most are being diverted to higher markets in Pakistan and India, while in 
Bangladesh, as in Pakistan with their provisional DASRs, Bangladesh's HKC approvals for yards 
and for intended incoming vessels remains a fruit too fresh for local buyers to overeagerly pluck at 
this juncture.

INDIA 
 
BIG…WHILE BUSTED! 
The big news from India came via the port of Alang this week, as local recyclers cautiously celebrated 
an anchorage holding over 155K tons of recycling tonnage either at anchor or delivered locally. Minus 
the prior long-term campers, Alang recyclers not only greeted a 10K and 14K LDT tanker, but, far more 
impressively, two massive 33K LDT LNG carriers that brought in the big waves—and some much-
needed excitement that was immediately been busted by the elephants of tariffs and a subsequently
weakening Indian Rupee.  
With the INR now trading at an unprecedented historical low in the high Rs. 88s against the U.S. Dollar 
over the last couple of weeks, a noticeable degree of caution and reticence has entered the local 
playbook, leaving end buyers surveying the financial terrain for potential currency moves before 
offering afresh once again.As reported in the market commentary above, India’s perennially volatile 
steel plate prices that have always prone to roller-coaster swings—witnessing two weeks of flatlining 
within the span of month certainly is a watershed moment for the nation’s economy. As plate prices 
levelled off at USD 448/Ton, the Rupee took over the reins sliding 11 basis points across the week to 
end deeper at INR 88.28 against the U.S. Dollar.  
As a result, the wild ride of the Rupee remains the chief talking point for Alang recyclers as the currency
has suffered an over 5% depreciation in the last 12 months alone, continually dragging down prices, 
demand and sentiments in the wake of aggressive Trump tariffs and sanctions against India. 
The macro picture does seem rosier in comparison as on the one hand, even though inflation 
accelerated from 1.6% in July to 2.1% in August, CPI remains very much in the healthy range and 
should be able to weather incoming storms. India’s fiscal deficit is also expected to reduce, while GDP 
is projected to climb to nearly 7% (as forecasted by Fitch). Yet these long-term potentials don’t feed 
current-day needs, and while LNGs have become a surprising driver of large LDT, non-ferrous-rich 
behemoths into Alang over recent months, with a growing number of sanctioned vessels expected to 
join recycling ranks before year-end, a nervy Indian market may see certain vessels diverted to 
competing Pakistan once again – especially if the Rupee continues to slip and Trumps bloated 50% 
sanctions do hit home. 
Hard times for recycling in Alang, HKC or otherwise. 
Hard times.

PAKISTAN
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
    
DATE WITH DESTINY! 
As the highest-placed Indian sub-continent ship recycling destination for probably over two 
months now, Pakistani ship recyclers will be disappointed not to get their hands on any of the 
decent LDT tonnage that was recently committed to competing India and even the far lower-placed
Bangladeshi market. DASR certificates are being issued (as evident from the handful of recent 
deliveries), and HKC yard upgrades are also reportedly ongoing. Yet it would be useful for Gadani 
buyers to test the new HKC regulations with a greater supply of vessels, rather than relying on the 
strained and conditional approvals currently being granted by the government to this vital 
industry via provisional DASRs. 
Making matters worse, with the Pakistan market not being as competitive for non-ferrous recovery
from recycled vessels—not to mention the fact that LNGs carrying perlite are banned from entering 
Pakistan altogether—the festering silence of the Gadani Gang at the bidding tables of late has
delivered the inevitable outcome of an empty port report for the second week in a row. 
Certainly not a good sign for a market where although seeing its own Rupee depreciate the most 
in the industry this week (by nearly 19 basis points and end it at nearly PKR 284 against the U.S. 
Dollar), has been blind to the unparalleled flatlined performance of local steel plate prices, which 
remain at or above USD 625/Ton. To put this in context: India’s plate prices are at USD 448/Ton, 
and Bangladesh’s are at USD 519/Ton. Even a generous grant of import tax relief, duties, and fees 
could not bridge a gap of over USD 100/Ton on the value of a ship—let alone one that’s
approaching nearly USD 200/Ton. 
While this may explain the ability of local recyclers to survive the mean and lean times, in truly 
bad times it bites even harder—and unnecessarily so. Of course, as the HKC / DASR / limited-
supply love triangle plays out on the beaches of Gadani, 2025 has still managed to deliver sporadic
phases of healthy deliveries in both Bangladesh and India. But not in Pakistan. So what does the 
future of recycling hold for this market? While the government has reportedly set up grants to 
ensure Gadani’s continuity with ship recycling, how local recyclers commandeer this ship through 
2025’s headwinds remains to be seen. 
For now, it’s a tough time for the locals, and if you’re an owner with a big unit and are okay with 
“pale green” recycling, this is your market. As comedians say, “Destiny awaits, let’s just hope she’s
pretty”.
