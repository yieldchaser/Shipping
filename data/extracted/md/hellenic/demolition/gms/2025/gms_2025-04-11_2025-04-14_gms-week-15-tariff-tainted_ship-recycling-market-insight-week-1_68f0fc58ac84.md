### Editorial: TARIFF TAINTED!

Although the 90-day suspension seems to have given recyclers some reprieve for the time being, the deadline realistically stands a mere quarter away. In the interim, oil has volleyed like a beach ball in the wind, declining all the way to USD 56/barrel mid-week (lowest levels seen since 2020), before climbing back up to USD 61.50/barrel where it ended the week, and future declines are still expected as OPEC+ countries re-affirmed their decision to increase output despite tit-for-tat trade wars raising global economic uncertainty and rising lack of energy demands abound. Even the U.S. Dollar spiked briefly against all ship recycling nation currencies before settling down with a surprising result, where even the Lira made decent gains against the Dollar. And global trade markets too continued to take a dunk in the tariff pool as freight rates cooled, although they did even out towards the end of the week and report minor gains. What comes of this dip in rates remains to be seen since no fresh tonnage has made its way onto the bidding tables of late. And this has demonstrated itself in plain sight at the respective anchorages this week as India (technically) reports its first ‘empty’ report, while Pakistan impressively takes in the most tonnage in the sub-continent. Through it all, Turkey continues to idle in stand-by, waiting for the opportune moment to set into gear – as long as there is some tonnage to feed into any of the markets at this time.

## 3. Country Market Intelligence

### Bangladesh

- **Headline**: EXTENSION EXPECTED.
- **Key Notes**: *Not yet in place.* | *Ministry at work.* | *Price woes.* | *HKC + Tariffs*

Despite local rumors that the Department of Environment had approved an extension to the March 31st deadline within which, Chattogram yard owners must have initiated the necessary upgrades to ensure their ship recycling facilities are conforming to the standards & requirements of the HKC within June 26th of this year, this does not seem to have been the case as this Department has no jurisdiction over NOC issuances and SRFP extensions, instead only being concerned with annual yard permissions and vessel visits to local ports. In fact, the Ministry of Industries has been occupied with the transfer of responsibilities to the newly formed Ship Recycling Board and it is this department that is expected to grant said extension upon their return from Eid Holidays next week, especially as the fate of several incoming vessels / deliveries for the upcoming tide(s) hang(s) in the balance.

Meanwhile, there has not been much of meaningful economic or political news emanating from the country over the last couple of weeks due to ongoing religious celebrations across the nation, which have reportedly been the longest in the nation’s history. And in keeping in line with religious traditions, record-breaking incoming personal remittances and monetary gifts helped boost the nation’s domestic reserves to well over USD 21 Billion, further facilitating Bangladesh’s foreign exchange and treasury reserves. Similar to India, this will only help ensure domestic recyclers have a steady stream of finance / funding needed to help with purchases (current and future) that are still ongoing, even in the face of a declining supply of recycling tonnage, which is clearly evident from this week’s arrivals where 2 really small units greeted Chattogram’s anchorage.

And with fundamentals and international tariff news keeping local prices in check for the most part, especially as the Trump administration announced 30% tariffs on Bangladesh and reversed course on them days later, resulted in the Bangladeshi Taka strengthening against the U.S. Dollar and ending the week 13 basis points stronger at BDT 121.76. On account of the local holidays, local steel plate prices too have logically remained flatlined (on account of the lack of domestic steel trade) and remained where they have been for a while now (USD 529/Ton). This has delivered a confusing time for Bangladeshi recyclers post Eid holidays – with local indications and demand nosediving on the back of global tariff announcements, before coming surging back up to current levels once the 90-day suspension was announced.

Overall, with deadline extensions expected for yards that have not begun improving their facilities in order to keep operating when the Hong Kong Convention comes into force on June 26th this year, along with the 90 day pause on tariffs that could rear its head up against the local economy from July, will all come wet-wrapped in the midst of a likely crazy monsoon that certainly doesn’t bode for a meaningful Q2 in Bangladesh.

### India

- **Headline**: RESPECTING RELATIONS!
- **Key Notes**: *Seemingly unaffected.* | *Steel key.* | *Short window.* | *Busy Q3?*

Despite all of the unfolding tariff drama across the world, Alang has been remarkably unaffected for the most part, especially as the Indian government had played it strategically with Trump even before he took office on January 20th, when PM Modi made several visits to the U.S. in order to address future trade between the nations. And although tariffs were initially imposed on India, the 90-day pause in addition to ongoing bilateral discussions between the administrations is likely to yield far greater results for the Indian economy post July, especially given how tenaciously volatile 2025 has already proven itself to be thus far. With January – February flooding the markets with units to a seriously muted supply of tonnage through March – April, which has left India devoid of any fresh arrivals at the waterfront this week, with the only vessel idling outside being an arrival from last week that is still awaiting delivery.

Meanwhile, the Indian Rupee, which has been a bane for local recyclers, continues its predictably volatile dance against the U.S. Dollar as it lost 80 basis points and ended the week at Rs. 86.05, having nearly approached Rs. 87 by mid-week. Even local steel plate prices fell by about USD 6/Ton overall, despite having made a recovery towards the end of the week. This has seen some local concern about what happens to these fundamentals from next week as the U.S. – China tit-for-tat tariff twitter-spat has yet to find a middle ground before imported steel plate prices can find stable footing once again, especially now that Chinese steel plate levels themselves plummeted over USD 15/Ton this week and ended the week at USD 293/Ton, well below their usual playing field of USD 300/Ton.

Logically, elevated local concerns about the potential dumping of cheaper products into India and competing markets due to a flood of cheap Chinese billets and far cheaper Chinese steel, which ship recyclers will have to contend with and this will more than likely drag vessel prices down with them, even though local punitive measures to prevent any undercutting is already in place. For now, despite assertions of a special relationship with the Trump regime, India will likely still be subject to its share of tariffs under new U.S. decrees even though the 90 day pause on activity may give recyclers a short window to import vessels, all while Pakistan continues to take larger slices of the available tonnage pie.

On the HKC front, and unlike their competing neighbours, Alang recyclers are full prepared for the advent of the HKC as local authorities have even gone to the extent of aligning necessary paperwork and formalities on all incoming vessels post June 26th, something that was highlighted in a previous edition of the GMS Weekly (ask us for a copy). On the domestic front, other than the ongoing deliverance of American financial hiccups, not much has changed with India’s economy over the last week as all key figures and trajectories are still pointing in the right direction. Q3 seems like it is gearing up to be one of the busiest times for the Alang market, especially considering how 2025 is playing out so far.

### Pakistan

- **Headline**: INSULATED?
- **Key Notes**: *Slim pickings.* | *Smaller LDT.* | *Eyes locked.* | *8,000 points.*

Despite Pakistan emerging as a viable ship recycling destination once again, the supply has trickled down to slim pickings of vessels on the bidding tables that have primarily been geographically positioned Far Eastern units, which remain better suited heading to a far more bullish Bangladeshi market, as trading markets / freight rates start to favor ship owners all over again - even on vintage / over-aged tonnage. As such, there have only been a handful of mostly regionally owned vessels of smaller LDT units heading to Gadani recyclers, even though demand is there to acquire any larger units that may come available. And with their firmer (than India) prices on show and even firmer indications coming forth this week, it was no surprise to see this market conclude the most tonnage in the sub-continent as local recyclers took in more LDT than neighboring Indian and Bangladeshi anchorages combined.

Local fundamentals, meanwhile, remain in a state of flux as not only have local steel plate prices finally settled (after falling over USD 40/Ton over March) at USD 624/Ton over the last couple of weeks, but the Pakistani Rupee Pakistan also resumes its slow devaluing trudge against the U.S. Dollar after it briefly gained a lot of ground mid-week, yet lost 10 basis points overall and declined to PKR 280.06 as the week ended.

And just like the rest of the world, Pakistan has not been entirely insulated from the now- suspended tariff wars that are largely playing out between the U.S. and China, in an increasingly retaliatory manner. Yet, all eyes will be firmly placed on the next moves from either side, as this unpredictable drama surely has its share of twists and turns yet to come, especially for the Indian sub-continent steel and ship recycling markets, given that Chinese steel plate has already plummeted about USD 15/Ton this week and the fear is that cheaper competitive steel products will imminently flood this market causing steel trade between domestic ship recycling yards and local steel mills to be severely affected and likely result in further declining Paksitani steel values.

In the interim, local recyclers have bigger fish to fry as with pending HKC upgrades from a bulk of domestic ship recycling yards are yet to commence at pace and this will have even greater implications for Gadani ship recyclers in a post Q3 world as not only will local recyclers be barred from importing vessels for recycling, but the tariff wars are also expected to resume, which could affect the nation’s economy moving forward. With monsoons being in full swing at that time and India leading Pakistan on the pricing chart more often than not, a healthy collection of Gadani ship recyclers might have to rapidly make amends in order to continue operating in a post June 26th

Overall, despite the Pakistani stock exchange plunging over 8,000 points on the back of economic hiccups experienced throughout the week, the nation’s financial position remains in decent standing and able to assist local recycling purchases through the interim.

### Turkey

- **Headline**: WAIT…WHAT?!?!
- **Key Notes**: *One eye open.*

The Turkish market really surprised industry players this week as a myriad of fundamentals defied explanation and reacted contrary to how much of the industry reacted to Trumps tariff tantrum this week as on the one hand, both local and import steel plate prices declined by USD 10/ton and USD 20/Ton respectively, but the Turkish Lira amazingly ended the week on a positive note, having firmed 10 basis points against the U.S. Dollar and closing the week out at a still whopping / record breaking TRY 37.94.

The supply of vessels to Aliaga, like the rest of the industry, remains lament-worthy, as does the number of available recyclers who are still operating with one eye open. Mercifully, the 90 day pause on tariffs should help keep local offerings where they are…for now.
