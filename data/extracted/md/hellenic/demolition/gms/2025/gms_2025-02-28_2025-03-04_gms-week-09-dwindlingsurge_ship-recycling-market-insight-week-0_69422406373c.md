### Editorial: DWINDLING…SURGE?!

As tonnage availability has been notably absent at the bidding tables of late, the industry was greeted by the start of the Holy Month of Ramadan this week, which descended across the Indian sub-continent (and Turkish) markets, providing India and Bangladesh some respite from the fiery January sales witnessed since the turn of the year, and all at a time where the recent decline in levels of about USD 30/LDT due to ongoing geopolitical volatility that has perpetuated itself via flatlining and even declining steel prices and ongoing currency depreciations that saw the U.S. Dollar surge against currencies of all ship recycling nations across the board this week.

At the respective ship recycling destinations, as currency and steel starts to hammer harder where ongoing Indian declines were joined by Pakistan for the first time since October 2024, further highlighting the unfolding effects of the tariff wars as news of 25% against Mexico and Canada going into effect on March 4th surfaced this week, including an additional 10% to be levied against China, which will maintain an unappreciable amount of pressure on ongoing vessel offerings. Political crises have once again resurrected themselves in Bangladesh as the law-and-order situation remains of concern there, whilst Pakistan’s treasury continues to hemorrhage cash as fundamentals here declined in unison this week. While India continues to take in questionable tonnage and recyclers here start to feel the pinch of a Rupee that’s gone AWOL, cash buyers are being greeted by Turkish offerings even below USD 280/Ton on units, all amidst a devasted Lira that remains clutching to life support.

Overall, with the Red Sea reopening and freight rates set to potentially cool once charters are set to refresh, ongoing peace talks for both Ukraine and Gaza continue to be hit with a bat as Norway cuts off fuel supplies to U.S. Naval vessels over Trump’s contentious meeting with President Zelenskyy and Israel cuts off all aid into Gaza amidst an unfolding ceasefire standoff that will unquestionably see freight dynamics start to shift once again. And this may lead to an increased supply of units / containers later in the year. Recent alarming news concerning a potential ban on Chinese built / owned tonnage calling U.S. ports also stands to devastate the U.S. economy in turn, eventually sending prices skyrocketing even further as U.S. inflation starts to rise once again and the Feds cancel their recently intended interest rate cut. Nevertheless, demand for units at flimsy levels remains firm across sub-continent markets and it will be interesting to see what levels upcoming sales are concluded at, especially as the supply of tonnage starts to slow once again.

## 3. Country Market Intelligence

### Bangladesh

- **Headline**: STARK CONTRAST!
- **Key Notes**: *Fewer sales.* | *Political shakes.* | *HKC deadline.* | *Historic shift!*

As the first week of Ramadan sets in, Bangladesh continues to trudge through a truly confusing time where not only are the highest payers in the industry facing the same music as the rest of the world’s economies that have started reacting to the unfolding tariffs, including ongoing political and economic hurdles of their own. While February ended up in the history books as a rather stark contrast to a far-busier January on the back of fewer sales being confirmed, and an instability in pricing emerging that was absent since the start of this year has bought in a bevy of fixtures through the last batch of acquisitions, which found themselves simultaneously arriving the waterfront this week, with over 105K LDT and 10 vessels (LNGs, Tankers, and Bulkers) that are either lying in wait or were already delivered to their respective new owners on the beach.

Domestic fundamentals continue to test the rigidity of local offerings as not only have plate prices held dead on at about USD 529/Ton, but it has been the Taka which, like the Lira, has lost a far less disastrous in comparison, yet disastrous enough on its own as it has plummeted over 11% in just over the last year alone including this week, where it fell another 20 basis points and ended the week at a calamitous BDT 121.97/Ton. With the budget expected in about 3 months from now, the economic fragility continues as despite seeing a massive collection of arrivals over recent weeks highlighting the ongoing trade of recycled ship’s steel at levels that have helped Bangladesh dominate pole position over the market rankings for several months now.

As the interim political party is also coming under growing pressure, particularly from the increasingly militant student body who ousted former PM Sheikh Hasina and have formed their own party, political (in)stability has trumped (pun intended) economic stability as despite seeing some easing in inflation rates reported across February, it (inflation) remains at a concerned 9.98% as the “to-do list” of the current intermittent administration starts to become rather tenuous across 3 quarters now. Moreover, despite key indicators highlighting a gradual recovery, the nation faces inflationary pressures, slow public spending, diminishing employment opportunities, and an overall sluggish investment climate amidst faltering large U.S. Dollar loans by domestic businesses.

All of this coming to a head and at a time the HKC stands to enter into force by July 1st 2025, earmarking a historic shift in the business of ship recycling and HKC yard upgrades, though delayed, have reportedly commenced at pace in Chattogram on the back of the BSBRA’s recent deadline extension for upgrades to start by March 31st and subsequently obtain relevant SOC certifications from accredited Classes confirming the ability of domestic ship recycling yards to operate post Q2 2025. For now, amidst a sharp decline in the availability of vessels, Ramadan prayers and celebrations, Taka tumbles, and growing pressure on local offerings, Chattogram Choppers may have bought some breathing time until Q2 before the need to engage in discussions from the same tier of buyers resumes again, all while the 2nd batch of hungry buyers are lingering at levels much lower.

### India

- **Headline**: FALLING…FAST & FURIOUS EDITION!
- **Key Notes**: *Price woes.* | *USD 17/Ton* | *PM Modi’s efforts.* | *Over a decade.*

India’s economy has been earmarking what is today, and what will more than likely be the near future for Alang recyclers as not only are their offerings coming under immense economic pressures, but Indian buyers are also coming under increasing competition from a Pakistani market of late, even though their purchasing prowess seems largely dithered over recent weeks on the back of a growing shortage of tonnage that is similarly affecting India’s ship recycling sector. Yet, like their Bangladeshi counterparts, Alang’s port position has recorded an impressive number of arrivals exceeding 40K LDT via 5 decent size units including tankers, dry units, and even a RoRo.

Additionally, Indian fundamentals are keeping Alang’s offerings at the bidding table in check as not only has the Indian Rupee suffered a devastating week as it lost nearly 1% in value to close the week out at Rs. 87.48 against the U.S. Dollar, but local steel plate prices have not stopped falling over the recent past as they fell another $2/Ton this week, pushing the total decline to over USD 17/Ton over the last month alone and this is making it harder for Alang recyclers to compete with the better placed Bangladesh market as well.

Notwithstanding, some of the key markers affecting India’s economic scorecard have been pointing in the right direction as Q4 2024 ended with an uptick noted on the back of increased government and consumer spending that even drove the country’s economic growth by over 6% during the same time. India’s recent budget providing massive tax cuts for low – median income families / individuals will more than likely maintain the nation’s economic trajectory and even gradually send India’s inflationary numbers South.

But for the nation’s ship recycling sector, none of it seems rosy under the current environment, especially since candidate introductions at the bidding tables have slowed over the past month, economic pressures on vessel prices are growing, and levels are without hesitation, seemingly ready for another adjustment. And despite the best efforts of PM Modi to build firmer ties with President Trump during a recent visit to the White House and maintain bilateral economic relations, sentiments also took a beating and weakened off the back of the tariff volatility that is engulfing much of the world at present.

Nonetheless, any Far Eastern fixtures will continue to wind up in Bangladesh and those vessels coming from the West are now under an increasing threat of heading to Pakistan as Alang offerings are the ones coming under the heaviest fire, unless of course needing HKC only recycling including those post June 30th 2025, for which Alang buyers already excel at and have been well prepared for this moment for nearly a decade now.

### Pakistan

- **Headline**: MORE COMPETITIVE?
- **Key Notes**: *Confirming deals.* | *Rockier start.* | *Time to restart.* | *Eke out.*

Even though the Gadani gang stand in 3rd place on the market rankings, their inability to perform at a sustained rate leaves the very question unanswered i.e. just how competitive has Gadani’s ship recycling sector truly been of late? Although Indian levels stand to approach Pakistan’s levels before the reverse takes place, but Pakistan (like the rest of the word) is facing economic wranglings of their own especially after the recent IMF downgrade and billions of dollars in aid and financial loans that remain hanging in the balance.

Notwithstanding the ship recycling act playing out in Q1, of late, Pakistan did become a competitive option rather than the primary destination for any geographically positioned units opening West of Sri Lanka that finally helped even confirme a smaller LDT deal to one hungry recycler whereafter, the market has gone silent again as it wraps up the 2nd consecutive week of an empty anchorage.

And with plots lying largely dormant after an extremely inactive and inert last few years that has seen many recyclers having to partially close shop and divest in alternative business interests, some of the top-tier buyers are still keen to conclude vessels and as such, interest on select units is lingering behind the scenes, albeit at a subdued level, especially after years of lagging behind the competition and registering some of the lowest sales volumes seen into the nation in nearly over a decade. And with the looks of it, 2025 is certainly off to a rocky start.

Meanwhile, in the face of the nation’s depleting reserves by the government are now being greeted by a healthy inflow of personal foreign remittances into the country leading to a stable February that even saw inflation sit at the perfect level of around 3% that is needed for every major economy to perform at, and this should hopefully ease the burden of finances in short time needed for Pakistani recyclers to initiate upgrade yards to HKC standards, given that a serious influx of recycling tonnage expected in the year(s) ahead!

For now, however, as India’s levels are all but certain to see the better part of south in the coming week(s) with their fundamentals written all over the recycling wall, helping Pakistan easily slip into 2nd place and eke out the next healthy batch of units before the next slowdown in the supply starts to hit the markets again, just as they currently are. At the moment however, local steel plate prices fell a massive USD 11/Ton while the Pakistani Rupee itself lost 4 basis points and ended the week at PKR 279.56 against the U.S. Dollar, likely putting their own pressures on Gadani offerings in the interim.

### Turkey

- **Headline**: DOWN…AND NEARLY OUT?

Turkey’s chances at securing tonnage and owners receiving a fair offer in the face of a near USD 200/Ton price differential with the sub-continent markets have left little for Aliaga buyers to nibble on. And on the units that are being proposed to test the waters are being greeted by indications that are permeating even below the current price floor of USD 280/Ton. Fundamentals continue afflict their pricing strategy as well as subdued import and local steel plate prices that continue face weekly jitters, all while the Lira remains in freefall as it collapsed another 20 basis points to end the week at TRY 36.53. Certainly not an even playing field for Aliaga recyclers under the current environment.
