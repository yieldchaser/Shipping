Despite a frantic start to 2025 that saw a marked return of recycling tonnage at the bidding tables,
SNP activity across the global ship recycling spectrum eased further this week as Chinese New Year 
holidays descended upon the world. It is the “Year of the Snake” and it is indeed expected to be a
far busier one than the preceding years, with record low volumes of supply and an ensuing inertia
that eventually led to the shuttering of yards across the major global ship recycling destinations in 
2024. Notwithstanding, should supply become unsustainable, this may apply even further pressure 
on recycling markets and eventually affect ship owners down the line, who will then have no choice 
but to bite the bullet of chasing falling prices in markets. So far, India & Bangladesh have shouldered 
the increasing number of vessels since October of 2024, when Pakistani recyclers all but disappeared 
from the bidding tables on the back of unfolding domestic financial crises. However, the Gadani 
Gang is now looking to get back into the game once again, at a time when locally favored dry bulk
(Panamax and handy bulkers) vessels and LNG tankers have been much of the ongoing flavor of 
recycling sales so far this year (see port reports below), with containers expected further down the 
road given the recent easing of traffic in the Red Sea shipping lanes. Yet, on a global scale, all is not 
well as the saying “The Rise of the Fall” seems to be unfolding across as President Trump’s Tariffs
to the tune of 25% on Mexico and Canada + an additional 10% on Canadian energy exports and a
blanket 10% tariff on China that is due to set into effect on February 4th, has already seen global 
economies reacting and leaders of Canada & Mexico promising counter-tariffs on American exports.
The Baltic Dry Bulk Index also gained ground as rates across the dry bulk categories displayed signs 
of positivity / improvement this week, which might still be late for a bulk of the already-aged still-
trading units that are being compelled to reconsider their immediate futures. Making matters for 
inflation and global economies were oil futures that are expected to surge and even reported firming
this week as levels approached USD 74/barrel once again, and Week 6 is likely setting itself up to be 
the pivotal point of global Tariff Wars of 2025. This also seems to be affecting the performance of 
the U.S. Dollar, which although seems to have made minor improvements against most of the major 
ship recycling nation currencies by the end of the week, actually started to decline across the board
early in the week with the uncertainty of the upcoming Tariffs, which even resulted in flattening 
steel plate prices at all ship recycling nations, except India where expectedly, they declined. The 
emergence of recycling traffic has also seen a remarkable amount of LDT at both India & Bangladesh 
this week, and this is not only happening amidst declining vessel offers that have cooled by up to 
USD 30/LDT over the course of January, but also from the few recyclers (in Bangladesh & Pakistan) 
who are financially secure enough to even be at the tables and drop an offer.  
Tariffs on Chinese steel stand to be of key concern to the sub-continent ship recycling markets and 
when China starts dumping their surplus post-tariff steel, it will be a critical factor in demand and 
vessel pricing for much of this year. Finally, Turkey at the far end was no stranger to the “doom and 
gloom” theme event as even though their plate prices remain relatively unchanged, amidst an
improvement in supply this week, offers of about USD 30/Ton lower shocked the industry as 
markets across the board seem to be falling in unison. Tastes like 2008 all over again.

BREATHER! 
In the wake of Chinese New Year holidays, Chattogram Choppers seem to have gotten a much-
needed breather this week as the recent supply of recycling tonnage has mostly been Panamax
bulkers from Chinese / Far Eastern owners since the start of the year, coinciding with ongoing offer
declines of up to USD 30/Ton on dry units from the few open and capable buyers. As such, it has 
been difficult to negotiate tonnage into Chattogram of late, especially as most of the tonnage has
even been opening in the Far East and has seen Bangladeshi buyers swallow nearly all of the
recycling arrivals (in terms of the number of vessels) this week, certainly the most LDT we have 
seen arrive any waterfront in a while with nearly 105K LDT either idling or already delivered to 
their new owners. Yet, the nation’s ship recycling waterfront disguises the true state of local affairs
as the ongoing economic instability highlighted by a resurgence of political instability, is pushing
once again domestic recyclers into a fresh corner of confusion after a year of tonnage scarcity. 
As highlighted last week, the timely effectiveness of the interim government seems to remain in
question as not only have some domestic infrastructure projects been entirely rescinded, but those
in question are being re-evaluated for over-bloated budgeting practices and is back to the drawing
board for this sector for the time being. Moreover, steel trade remains abysmal as fears of incoming
tariffs coupled with Chinese New Year Holidays have likely been the reason behind the flatlining
performance of steel plate prices, with a side of a large hangover of reigning political affairs. On
the political end, ousted PM Sheikh Hasina’s crumbled party is reportedly attempting to regroup 
and calling for renewed activism, whilst college students, who were the drivers behind the ousting
of her regime, are reportedly in talks to create a new political party in order to run in the upcoming
elections. As this only spells further delays for the nation’s economic recovery for the rest of the 
year, the present is unfortunately turning into a troublesome time for ship owners and cash buyers
who are misreading the tea leaves amidst a time these markets are performing contrary to historical
Q1 norms on the back of rare events. 
As local steel plate prices remain dead on the floor at USD 529.41/Ton, the Taka made a marked
and rather surprising improvement against the U.S. Dollar, to the tune of 21 basis points as the
week ended, closing it at BDT 120.59. This consistent volatility and weakening of the Taka across
2024 (and even of late), has seen prices gradually decline from the highs of USD 600/Ton, down to
the mid USD 400s/Ton, with unfolding indications of up to USD 30/LDT lower on tabled tonnage.
As such, any dry units currently for sale are only seeing USD 450/LDT depending on condition of 
the vessel and especially, the availability / interest of an open-end buyer given that many recyclers
are now booked with tonnage for the near future. Moreover, with the January 31st deadline to
obtain NOCs for incoming recycling tonnage now past, the BSBRA has appealed to the Ministry of
Affairs for an extension once again, in order to provide recyclers who have initiated infrastructure
upgrades on their yards, to receive time beyond the January 31 cut off and get up to HKC standards
well prior to entry into force in June of this year, as most yards are still on their way to obtaining a
an SoC backed HKC accreditation. Coupled with the extremely shaky state of fundamentals, 
February maybe a challenging time for Sellers/Cash Buyers with this market.

UNCHANGED! 
The Indian budget was announced this week without much fanfare for Alang’s ship recycling sector. 
Moreover, even though the government has dived nose first into improving the lives of the lower and 
middle classes via massive tax cuts / breaks in this budget, the only indirect effect to Alang’s ship 
recycling sector was the announcement of a 3-year plan to distribute intended infrastructure projects, 
a decision which stands to delay any steel recovery that maybe in store for the nation’s immediate 
ship recycling needs. Meanwhile, despite the threat of incoming tariffs flatlining plates from the major 
recycling destinations this week, India was the only one to register a decline to the tune of nearly 
USD 3/Ton, ending the week at USD 455.75/Ton and just barely above prevailing offers on any dry 
vessels currently making the rounds. Even the Indian Rupee fell drastically through the week, only 
to make a minor improvement towards the end of the week and still record an overall decline of 20 
basis points to close at Rs. 86.33 against the U.S. Dollar. 
The weakening state of local offers coupled with the fact the much of the tonnage across recent weeks 
has emanated from Far Eastern waters thereby completely circumventing any chance for Pakistani / 
Indian recyclers from even bidding on these units, has eventually resulted  in the first week of 2025 
where Alang reported only one large-LDT arrival for the week, along with a couple of deliveries of 
prior arrivals that saw local anchorage LDT balloon to well over 70K LDT this week – a number not 
seen at the Indian waterfront in some time.  
Notwithstanding, the budget did provide a breadth of far-reaching tax cuts and even complete tax 
breaks for individuals and families ranging from the lower and middle classes, delivering more cash 
for consumers through FY 2025 – 2026 along with the expectation that private sector infrastructure 
investments will likely go up, kickstarting demand for steel as well, which would then help 2025 hold 
a candle to 2024s performance. Yet, the return and unfolding of this performance remains up for grabs 
as the fine print of the budget is still being digested.  
Finally, as little to no infrastructure upgrades are needed for Alang’s ship recycling yards, there isn’t 
a need for financial loans by local buyers as most yards have already fulfilled their obligations at 
obtaining their HKC SoC accreditations and are prepared to take on Q3 with the same fervor as Q2.  
Overall, as Trump’s tariffs on Chinese steel will inevitably affect sub-continent markets in some way
and despite India also placing their own tariffs on Chinese steel imports, an impending excess of 
globally available Chinese steel could disrupt sub-continent steel and furthermore, vessel prices from 
India. As India, Pakistan and Bangladesh are all on a virtually level playing field in terms of price, a
vessel’s final discharge location a.k.a. geography seems to be the only determinant factor as to where 
vessels eventually end up.

PAKISTAN
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
    
CLOSE…BUT NO CIGAR! 
Despite offers from the Pakistani ship recycling sector lagging not too far behind their Indian 
counterparts (perhaps Indian offers declining to be more in line with those from Pakistan?), Gadani
ship recyclers need to get a lot more serious, not just to conclude deals but even continue to do 
business post July 1st, 2025.  
To start, not only do they need to take stock of the current economic situation within the country 
and gauge the immediate direction of the winds in the face of tonnage being offered, but to also 
bear in mind what is also happening with the competition in the near and far terms, so as to 
strategically start offering (even above market if needed) on the right unit(s) and get the wheels of 
Pakistan’s ship recycling sector turning once again. Not to mention, as Bangladeshi  ship recyclers 
pursue extensions with their respective Ministries in order to keep Bangladeshi ship recycling 
yards open for business post January 31, reportedly, neither the Pakistani government nor 
Pakistani ship recyclers have undertaken any serious measures to initiate infrastructure upgrades
on their yards or even have the possibility of completing them in time for when the HKC comes
into effect, further laying barriers in the ability of this nation to represent themselves at the ship 
recycling tables starting Q3, and as it seems the present / cemetery-like appearance of the shores
of Gadani that it is now past 4.5 months of missing tonnage arrivals at the water front, is likely to 
face another terrible year for ship recycling. 
Even the Pakistani government needs to do more, not only to assist Gadani buyers by way of more 
liquidity and financial instruments being made available to Gadani buyers, but also to the nation 
overall as Pakistan is hemorrhaging through its financial reserves as a handful of incoming 
financial aid hangs in the balance. The nation’s fundamentals are doing little to assist local ship 
recyclers as not only did Pakistani Rupee drop 12 basis points against the U.S. Dollar and close the 
week at PKR 278.44, but local steel plate prices, the highest in the Indian sub-continent, have also 
remained flat on the floor for months on end and closed the week at USD 643.78/Ton. 
Overall, having been largely absent from the buying for the last two years, a greater degree of 
confidence and certainty is needed locally, in order for the Gadani Gang to get back into the picture 
with any sense of normality for 2025. And while the demand for finished products remains slow
and low, and the possibility of an influx of even cheaper Chinese steel stands to greet February 
starting from the 4th (once Trump’s Tariff’s kick in), local plate prices will likely decline and lower
the intensity of local offerings to one that would only assure more of the same performance and 
the likely closure of even more recycling yards.

SPLASH! 
Turkey’s unrelenting collapse in fundamentals and eventual collapse in vessel pricing that has been 
teasing the industry for so many weeks, finally made a grand splash this week as local offers declined 
in sync with sub-continent ship recycling markets and to the tune of USD 30/Ton across the board. 
And while import and local steel plate prices have marginally remained in the same state since last 
week, the Turkish Lira’s unfinished dive into the abyss continued unabated, closing the week out at 
TRY 35.71 against the U.S. Dollar. 
Notwithstanding, and like the rest of the industry, even though a surprising uptick in the amount of 
tonnage being proposed to local recyclers has reportedly gone up this week, it is up to already 
disappointed ship owners to consider another USD 30/Ton officially wiped off Turkish prices as well, 
further exasperating the price differential with sub-continent markets and making matters that much 
harder for Turkish Recyclers. 
How many of these ship owners will consider repositioning their vessels with further cargo runs that 
relocate their units suitable for a sub-continent redelivery, remains to be seen. For now, what will be 
interesting to note is how many ship owners will actually bite the bitter bullet of falling prices once 
again.
