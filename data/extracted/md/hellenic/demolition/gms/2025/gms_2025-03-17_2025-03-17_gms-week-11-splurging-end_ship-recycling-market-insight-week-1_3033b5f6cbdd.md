After what has certainly been a hectic January of negotiations and a late February / early March of 
unending deliveries with nearly 200K of LDT delivered in recent weeks, a period of calm reflection 
descended across Indian sub-continent ship recycling markets with an alarmingly minimal number 
of deals being fixed over recent weeks including this one. And ever since prices have been at and 
even well under the now chased after USD 450s/LDT across the board (as confirmed via last week’s
logger sale into Bangladesh), barely any sales have even been rumored to take place. Speaking of, 
Bangladesh remains ahead of its regional competitors with (weakening) levels still emanating from
India and an inadvertently more competitive / stable Pakistan not far behind, snapping on their 
heels for the slim picking of units available. And this has once again presented itself via a severely 
dithered Alang anchorage and Pakistan’s 2nd delivery in 3 months this year.  
It has been a number of weeks since we saw more than one market unit concluded into the sub-
continent ship recycling markets as the Baltic’s Dry Bulk Sea Freight Index reported further climbs 
and registered its highest levels since November 2024, further deviating dry bulk & container freight 
sectors away from the bidding tables as firming rates push on and are maintaining the backlog of 
vintage / over aged vessels on the high-seas over the last few years. This will certainly drive global 
inflation up as logistics gets more expensive and trade wars aggravate an already volatile situation
that much worse. Even oil prices continue to see jitters after they declined another 1% in early-week
trading and rose back 0.9% by Friday, as oil closed the week out at $67.20 / Ton, all in the face of an 
ongoing over-supply of oil from OPEC+ countries despite a global easing of energy demands. 
On the domestic fundamentals front, there has been a mixed bag of confusing signals emanating 
from the recycling markets. On the one hand, the U.S. Dollar has suffered a stuttering time as it
weakened against some currencies while strengthening against others, and local steel plate prices 
hammer sentiments down further in both India and Pakistan this week, all while Chinese plate 
prices finally firm after a long time, which could have a positive affect on sub-continent markets in 
the long run and sub-continent levels continue to decline as an equilibrium finally settles things 
down…but that still remains an “if and when” type of situation.  
For now, the ongoing downtime via the lack of availability of tonnage is providing recyclers in both 
Bangladesh and Pakistan the perfect opportunity to upgrade their yards up to HKC standards ahead 
of its entry into force on June 26th, while a majority (if not all) Alang yards are already HKC 
accredited with SoC certifications. Turkey at the far end has receded into hibernation in the face of 
the ongoing lack of tonnage, other than a marginal private proposal this week. Finally, many of 
Trump’s tariff deadlines have now passed and it will be interesting to see how economies fare now
that China too is reacting in kinds. Finally, a number of larger LDT OFAC listed / sanctioned vessels 
also remain unsold in cash buyer hands amidst fresh sanctions being imposed on vessels currently 
idling outside Bangladesh, highlighting once again the risks some cash buyers are willing to take
and the international laws they are willing to violate, just to make a quick buck!

BANGING AWAY! 
As the price & tonnage leaders in the ship recycling sector across a majority of Q1 2025, Bangladesh 
ship recyclers remain keen to pick up tonnage despite being in an economic and political meltdown
that is currently taking the nation a lot longer (than expected) to recover  from, especially as there 
remains very little available at the bidding tables to bid on and this is leading to an accrual of local 
frustrations even though local anchorage continues to report healthy arrivals of nearly 72K LDT 
via 7 ships (including another LNG Tanker), that are either idling at the nations waterfront or have
already been delivered to their respective recyclers this week. As such, it would not be surprising 
to see a few above market sales concluded to local buyers in the near future, just to satisfy some of 
the demand that has been resurfacing of late, as now-aggressive tier 2 recyclers become keen to fill 
their plots with tonnage at these newly cemented and comfortable levels in the USD 450s/Ton. 
Rightly so, given that Bangladeshi steel plate prices continue to be the only pillar supporting local 
offerings at their current levels, helping local Chop Shops stay atop the market rankings week after 
week and even be the beneficiaries of virtually all of the tonnage that has been opening up in the 
Far East over the last 2 quarters, on the back of steel late prices that ended another week flatlined
at region USD 529/Ton. Notwithstanding, while the economy and the Bangladeshi Taka are doing 
their best to suppress local offerings to mirror those of regional competitors, prices may eventually 
head in the wrong direction as the Taka weakens another 5 basis points to end the week at BDT 
121.42 against the U.S. Dollar.  
On the domestic economy front, Bangladesh continues to suffer amidst ongoing protests, violence
and attacks on law enforcement as unemployment remains high, business slowdowns continue,
reduction in domestic exports to both the U.S. and the EU affect the nation’s treasury, slowdown 
in activity at port terminals due to labor shortages is causing delays and resulting in rising costs of 
imported goods that are further grinding against domestic inflation, which itself has climbed just 
0.5% in February 2025 whilst still at a high 9.32%, oil and energy costs are skyrocketing, and 
wavering bank deposits due to a growing lack of trust in the interim government continues despite 
rumors of the upcoming budget granting generous subsidies for the domestic agricultural and
manufacturing sectors, in an attempt to jump start the economy and keep the nation’s reserves in
check. With the budget still being over a Quarter away and economic pressures still at play, just 
how Chattogram Choppers will react in the interim remains to be seen. 
Finally, local sales continue as another bulker from Chinese owners (their third sale of this year so
far) was confirmed as the RUN FU 6 (6,297 LDT) fetched a weakened USD 435/LT LDT with a 
Pakistan option in hand, as the vessel may well end in Gadani due to her condition and the fact 
that Bangladeshi recyclers are being far pickier in acquiring rusty units these days.

INDIA 
 
TWINNING! 
Ship recycling markets certainly never fail to deliver their share of surprises given that on the back of 
declining local offerings (as previously forecasted in prior reports), not only have Alang recyclers 
managed to dislodge themselves to Pakistan on the market rankings, but Alang’s anchorage has also 
managed to become the inadvertent twin of Gadani’s waterfront as both destinations reported just 
one arrival this week, highlighting the relentless pressures on India’s ship recycling sector that 
continue to hammer away at local sentiments.  
At the micro end for the ship recycling markets, their nemesis have been local steel plate prices as
well as the U.S. Dollar, both of which have suffered catastrophically of late and even this week, where 
steel levels fell nearly another Dollar, down to USD 443/Ton and well below the current vessel pricing
strategy out of India, and the U.S. Dollar too made a (meaningless) 12 basis point jump this week, but
still ending the week at an astronomical level just above Rs. 87 (at the time of writing).  
Nevertheless, the Indian economy at large, continues to make gains (compared to the economies of
competing ship recycling neighbors), with retail inflation finally slipping below the 4% mark in 
February and news of upcoming interest rate cuts by the government make the rounds. Moreover, as 
Chinese steel plate prices start to rise on the back of recently imposed tariffs, this could definitely help 
Indian steel plate prices in the long run, but just when and how soon, remain the question. Moreover, 
with tonnage dithering at the bidding tables and firmer Pakistani levels taking a bite out of India’s
recycling buffet via a second fixture in as many weeks (with a Pakistan option in hand), is certainly 
laying out additional hurdles for Indian recyclers leading into Q2. 
This has pushed Alang Buyers into a corner where they remain patiently waiting on the sidelines to 
acquire specialist units (LNGs, stainless steel tankers, offshore vessels) and even HKC tonnage – some 
of which has made it to Chattogram of late while others (including HKC tonnage) have been entirely
amiss in the face of firming freight rates that continue to deprive global ship recycling markets of any 
serious tonnage over the last couple of months - at least.  
While January was certainly a busier month for India’s ship recycling sector, after the recent decline 
in sub-continent levels amidst tariffs and impeding fundamentals, the ability of Indian buyers to 
satiate their lingering appetite has been notably abysmal. Adding a salted cherry to the injured state 
of Alang’s ship recycling sector was the strong reaction to Trump’s announcement on tariffs that 
would affect Alang, but with no recent fixtures to test the market, it has been another quiet week in 
India with Holi holidays keeping the local populous distracted on religious holidays.

PAKISTAN
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
    
UNLUCKY, YET LUCKY! 
Gadani recyclers have been struggling at the bidding tables since Q3 2024 since when, they have 
only managed to secure 2 units thus far. Unluckily for Gadani buyers and in contrast to what is 
happening in India, even though domestic fundamentals have been far more stable here despite 
both the Pakistani Rupee that firmed 24 basis points to end the week at PKR 279.64 and local steel 
plate prices that are still plunging into the deep end as they recorded further declines this week, 
which ironically remain the highest in the industry and that too by a wide margin.  
Making matters worse for the economy is the state of the nation’s treasury reserves that are facing 
a beating despite international personal remittances into the country helping Pakistan’s inflation 
across February, thanks to the prior mismanagement of funds that has compelled the government 
to make drastic cutbacks for non-essential sectors - Ship Recycling being one of them. This has seen 
an exacerbating performance of Pakistani recyclers at the bidding tables as the nation struggles to 
stabilize domestic spending, despite national inflation bouncing around the 3% market. Even the
plethora of pending loans / financial aid instruments and extensions on loan repayments being 
granted by the U.A.E and China, the government seems to be incessantly struggling to keep all 
sectors of the nation running in sync. 
On the flip side and luckily for the Gadani Gang, their relatively stable vessel prices have helped 
them watch India slide right on by and invariably slip Pakistan into 2nd place on the ship recycling 
market rankings, in effect, helping them negotiate and even have 2 vessels with delivered locally 
with another 2 recent fixtures reportedly having navigational charts for Pakistani waters on 
standby. As such, arrival after a month of silence, Gadani’s waterfront finally reported a fresh as
another small LDT bulker greets the nation’s anchorage for recycling. 
Notwithstanding, despite Pakistani recyclers making an effort, some even at testy levels, they have
ended empty handed for another week as diminishing supply due to improving freight rates (dry 
bulk in particular) hammers the ship recycling industry overall. Pakistani recyclers have instead 
been busy trying to structure upgrades to their facilities to HKC standards during these trying 
times, and several have reportedly started the initial infrastructure work on their yards.  
Yet, it seems unlikely that these facilities will be ready to operate before the June 26th deadline and 
likely even after, as Gadani as a destination has yet to receive HKC and subsequent SoC approvals
or even recycle a single vessel under the HKC guidelines. As such, it remains to be seen just how
many ship owners will be willing to engage in serious discussions – at least in the interim and until 
a few smaller LDT units have become the test subjects for the industry overall.

ZZZZZZ? 
After what seems to have been an exciting fixture that certainly raised pulses across the industry last 
week, Aliaga has gone quiet again this week as tonnage is non-existent for this community once 
again and regulations coupled with domestic fundamentals keep Turkey’s pricing in check.  
Levels remain subdued and the last fixture was clearly below levels on the market rankings. Yet,
given that strict compliance rules that owners have to abide by, have a cost factor attached to them,
could justify the lower price on show for this particular fixture. Moreover, despite an odd private 
unit making the rounds, suppressed local steel plate levels and the Lira continue to place hurdles
along Aliaga’s pricing trajectory, having declined other 18 basis points and ending the week at nearly 
TRY 36.60 against the U.S. Dollar, all while Indian / Pakistani offerings remain nearly USD 150/Ton 
above.
