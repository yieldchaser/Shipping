As the fragile India – Pakistan ceasefire continues to barely hold at the seams with sporadic instances
of violence reportedly erupting along the Line of Control (LoC), PM Modi reassures the nation that 
Operation Sindoor is still very much in play as Indian Forces continue to target terrorists within 
Pakistan Occupied Kashmir, killing the mastermind behind reporter Daniel Pearl’s tragic execution 
about 24 years ago, Israel simultaneously ramps up its attacks against Hamas as plans to re-occupy 
Gaza make the rounds again, and finally Ukraine and Russia continue to hammer each other amidst 
a growing controversy of arms and military aircraft exports to Ukraine from the EU, further raising
the specter of a wider conflict between the nations, the world seems to have suddenly picked up 
speed for all of the wrong reasons over the last few weeks.  
All while President Trump visits the Middle East and accepts planes as gifts while complaining to 
senior staff about Congressional restrictions blocking such favors, world economies are meanwhile
having a hell of a rollercoaster ride with freight markets surging 6% by close of Friday and were
essentially propelled by higher rates across all trading segments. The 90-day tariff truce between 
China and the U.S. also saw crude futures climb 1.4% and end the week at USD 62.5/barrel, as global 
trade starts to stabilize to global post-tariff conundrums despite lingering uncertainties around the 
Iran Nuclear deal, which, if successful, could see an additional 200,000 barrels of oil enter the 
market, placing further uncertainty on oil markets and trade planning. 
Shifting from global economic chains to the ship recycling markets also saw the U.S. Dollar surge
against nearly all ship recycling nation currencies (except China) this week, whilst steel plate prices 
firm / decline / flatline in a series of confusing signals to ship recyclers and the industry at large. The 
recent trimming of tonnage at the bidding tables also seems to be easing as local port positions in 
both India and Bangladesh reported a healthy collection of arrivals and deliveries this week. In 
Bangladesh, the recent backlog of vessels idling outside, awaiting their respective NOCs that built
up across April also saw several deliveries ensue this week as exemptions came through from the 
ministry to recently approved HKC yards, all while a series of units committed to non-certified 
yards still remain stranded outside port limits.  
Pending HKC upgrades aside, markets overall feel like the they have been cornered as diminished 
pricing and confusing fundamentals have left sentiments uncertain and demand anemic. With HKC 
hurdles shifting the recent focus of vessels to the more active Indian (or non-HKC Pakistani)
markets, unfolding economic constraints stand to pressure prices down even as freight markets start 
to settle, charter rates start to climb, and the supply of candidates may start to increase through the 
second half of the year despite a traditionally slower and quieter monsoon season, thanks to trading 
opportunities for increasingly overaged units finally starting to disappear and these units should 
start truckling to recycling facilities at their end of their charters, after what has been a few years of 
buoyant freight markets across the board.

REGULATORY RED TAPE! 
With only 7 HKC certified yards open and currently buying vessels in Bangladesh, local offerings 
have conveniently cooled over the last couple of weeks as owners of the only operating yards with 
HKC approvals start to exploit the unfolding situation, resulting in recently proposed units for sale 
increasingly focusing on neighboring Indian and Pakistani markets. A few more local yards have 
reportedly completed their second audits with IRS class and are expected to receive their respective 
HKC certifications within the next few week(s). However, it seems destined to be a longer wait for 
a handful of yards that currently lag behind the necessary rate of  infrastructure upgrades required 
ahead of yard visits from local authorities, and for their subsequent HKC certifications to come 
through. As it therefore stands, regulatory red tape will increasingly holding up local deliveries 
and fixtures and until these new regulations are ironed out and in force until more yards are
approved, Bangladesh will be a difficult delivery destination for owners and cash buyers alike. 
And the situation for non-HKC ship recyclers who are unable to import tonnage may ironically be
well-timed given the performance of current fundamentals as not only are competing local HKC 
recyclers lowering their offers in the face of firming steel plate prices that jumped nearly USD 
90/Ton over recent weeks and remain flatlined in the face of missing trade between Bangladeshi 
steel mills and local recycling yards given that recycling tonnage itself has been missing for the last 
6 weeks, but the Bangladeshi Taka also fell nearly 30 basis points to end the week at BDT 121.82 
against the U.S. Dollar, all while the Central Bank of Bangladesh announced its decision to let the 
market determine the rate of the U.S. Dollar, as required by the terms of the current IMF loan 
agreement and this could result in further volatility from the Taka, as well as deterioration of local 
sentiment. 
DOmestic banks are also becoming increasingly stringent as a growing series of new demands from 
authorities whilst transacting L/Cs is causing a fresh batch of frustrations for local recyclers, local 
agents, cash buyers, and ship owners, as new-fangled documentary requirements are causing
delays in inward clearing vessels, releasing L/Cs, and onto their eventual deliveries that is resulting 
in further unexpected expenses. Adding to local frustrations has also been a diminished level of 
demand from domestic steel mills that is likely to get worse once the rains come pouring down. 
Finally, as the interim government announces a few ambitious projects this week, these have come 
far too little too late and much to the disappointment of the general public given that the optimism
and idealism initially displayed by the interim government is slowly disappearing and the same 
old formula of corruption through mega projects is reportedly rearing its head again. Until the new 
budget is announced and fresh elections earmark political stability in the region once again,
Bangladesh is likely to spend a quieter May / June than previously anticipated.

INDIA 
 
TENSIONS COOL! 
The fragile ceasefire between India and Pakistan this week has seen tensions cool between the two 
nuclear nations even though sporadic instances of small arms fire at the Line of Control has
continued. Moreover, as the nation as a whole remains disappointed at the restricted response from 
the Indian army after decades of terrorist attacks, PM Modi re-assured the nation that Operation 
Sindoor has merely been paused but is not completed. What this means for the future of the conflict 
remains to be seen, especially as Indian Special Forces reportedly took out senior terrorist leaders in 
various locations in Pakistan occupied Kashmir (PoK) this week, which could well see the conflict be 
inflamed in the near future once again.  
As such, focus has understandably been diverted away from recycling markets in recent times with 
the armed-conflict between the perennially warring neighbours taking centre stage for the nation at 
large. But armed conflict isn’t India’s only issue as tariffs and trade wars have seen the Rupee start to 
plummet once again as it fell nearly 35 basis points to end the week at Rs. 85.66, but even local steel 
plate prices dropped by about USD 2/Ton as the week ended, placing further downward pressures
on an already feeble sentiment that has seen local offerings retreat and move India into 3rd place on 
the market rankings. Ironically however, Alang’s waterfront reported far more arrivals and deliveries 
than Gadani this week as 6 ships and nearly 33K LDT had arrived / was delivered to local recyclers,
including a large(r) LDT Pax vessel. 
At the macro end of India’s economic overview, its performance was just as mixed as India’s ship
recycling sector as on the one hand, India’s forex reserves continue to increase as they surged past 
USD 690 billion this week on the back of firming domestic auto sales, the U.N. unfortunately 
downgraded India’s FY 2025 GDP growth outlook as unemployment remains stagnant and wholesale 
price index hit a 13-month low last month. Moreover, as the U.S. – India tariff situation remains 
precarious, the possibility of an incoming hike in tariff rates saw India threaten the WTO with 
retaliatory tariffs against the U.S., which only means further instability for India’s economy. 
Notwithstanding, there remains some respite for Alang recyclers as a constricted number of available 
buyers in Chattogram coupled with the tense army situation in Pakistan has left India a far safer 
alternative for incoming vessels and departing international crew. Indian recyclers also remain more 
active at the bidding tables than their Paksitani counterparts, further shrinking any price competitive 
advantage India may have lost over recent weeks.  
Unlike its counterparts, India may have a far busier monsoon in comparison.

PAKISTAN
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
    
MISSING A TRICK! 
Pakistan has had an opportunity to acquire India’s share of vessels over the last few weeks as
Bangladesh deliveries and fixtures have been stranded on account of the ongoing NOC restrictions
and India has likewise suffered with declining sentiments and prices due to tariff confusions and 
volatility in its fundamentals. The Hong Kong Convention also does not come into force until June 
26th so there remains a small window of opportunity for Gadani buyers to make the most out of 
the present and purchase a majority of the available tonnage before the landscape entirely changes.
However, it remains the same mix of excuses and confusion emanating from Pakistani recyclers as 
they unexpectedly find themselves higher up in the market rankings all while no positive changes 
to Pakistani fundamentals have been reported while the Indian Rupee continues to yoyo against 
the U.S. Dollar. Confusion over Alang’s recent diminished vessel offerings has also left Gadani 
buyer’s perplexed as to whether to maintain their currently higher pricing, or re-adjust down to 
match those from India. 
And Pakistani fundamentals have all the tell tale signs behind why lower offers from Gadani in 
the near future are more than likely. Not only are local plate prices now within arm’s length of 
Bangladeshi plate prices as they drop to USD 610/Ton (vs. the impressive jump to USD 577/ton in 
Bangladesh) and remain flatlined, but the Paksitani Rupee also depreciated 32 basis points and 
closed the week out at a whopping PKR 282.12. 
Economically as well, the nation continues to hemorrhage cash as despite incoming IMF funds to
support the economy, national exports during the month of April plummeted an extravagant 
17.65%, further afflicting the nation’s ability to recover on its own accord. The lingering conflict 
with India has also seen the army impose restrictions and movements along the Eastern front, in 
addition to the longstanding conflict in Balochistan where Baloch rebels hammered a Pakistani 
army convoy with IEDs killing several army personnel resulting in further blockades and closures.
The resulting uncertainty has left Pakistan suspended in the present times, accounting for the lack 
of any (market or private) deals to Gadani recyclers as a result. Highlighting the tonnage drought 
has been Gadani’s waterfront that has now ended a month with no arrivals. While all local yards 
desperately need to initiate infrastructure upgrades to their facilities so as to continue operating in 
a post HKC world, regrettably, not a single yard in Pakistan is anywhere near attaining these
standards and remain far behind the competition at this stage.
