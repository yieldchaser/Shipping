HALLOWEEN NOVEMBER? 
A spooky end to October delivered its share of unending surprises not only across the ship recycling
arena, but even global markets at large. At the onset, Trump wraps up his Southeast Asian glamour
tour touting deals made, especially one with China where an embargo on retaliatory trade sanctions
and set offs between the two nations was set for at least a year, bringing a sigh of relief to embattled
vessels enroute to discharge in Chinese waters and even global consumers at large. With September
inflationary numbers on the rise and U.S. figures being released only a couple of weeks back (due
to the ongoing government shutdown), November seems on track to deliver another set of surprises
for ship recycling markets. Oil meanwhile slipped nearly 1% during the week as it lingered around
USD 60.67, with OPEC+ announcing further cutbacks in the supply of oil in upcoming Q1 2026
(adieu 2025), leaving lingering expectations of increasing gas prices. 
Supply side was no different as the Baltic Exchange’s Dry Index also reported a noteworthy fall as
the overall index fell nearly 1.3% for the week (on the back of all sub-segments reporting losses),
down nearly 8% for the month of October and further marking its first monthly decline since April
2025. And this slowdown is increasingly presenting itself via tonnages of various sizes and types
across the Indian sub-continent ship recycling landscape, especially in India where despite the
lowest prices in the region, Alang continues to report an eye-popping number of occasional arrivals.
And yet, the glut of tonnage sailing the high seas remains disproportionately ratioed against the
number of units recycled over the last several years, leaving a potential surge of incoming tonnage
on the horizon. 
As a result, the ship recycling industry has continued to endure a stultifying and sluggish Q4 (in
line with the numerous quarters spanning the last few years) with declines evident across all sectors
and sales (barring the occasional stunners) occurring at ever deteriorating levels. An eye-catching
duo of LNGs sold last week (as a clear-out from this beleaguered sector continues) were just such a
highlight, including a seemingly steady trickle of older dissolute handymax sized bulk carriers sold
in recent weeks as well (as confirmed by the various port reports). Sales of sanctioned vessels have
also shaken the industry with fixtures being reported at well below market pricing into a dithering
Alang, establishing a two-tiered price system that is causing confusion and consternation in the
industry.  
Overall, despite a reported end to the U.S.-Chinese trade tit-for-tat, the constant volatility and ever-
changing headline decisions on tariffs and sanctions are now being treated with assured suspicion
and careful wariness by international businesses and MNCs. As such, the final months of the year
will wind up another haunting 202X for recycling locations, marked by declining levels, starved 
supply, regulatory red tape, HKC troubles, immovable recycled product, political unrest, tariffs, oil
sanctions, and a whole lot more. Someone call Hollywood and make a movie already.

FAILING TO IGNITE! 
Several sales to hungry Chattogram ship recyclers seemed have to sparked the market this week.
Yet, it really is a very few select end buyers who have been on the hunt for units and that too at
surprisingly decent levels (when compared to prevailing local offerings), in order to keep their
emptying yards active, and L/C & bank limits satisfied. But the overall trajectory and sentiments
have been unchanged for the most part as prices have otherwise been on a downward flight over
the last few months, primarily due to recycled ship’s steel failing to shift from local recycling yards
due to a festering demand as domestic steel mills reportedly continue to outsource raw material.  
And while this has clearly stalled local resales and left prices of recycled steel stranded - including
a deliberation in maintaining resale prices on part of ship recyclers so as not to lose their collective
shirts amidst the ongoing decline – finally saw steel levels slip USD 3/ton this week and end it at
USD 529.50/Ton. The U.S. Dollar was even less unforgiving as it slipped another 45 basis points
and ended the week at a healthily worrying BDT 122.37.  
Coupled with the crippling uncertainties of the political and economic directions of the country
under the current regime, domestic businesses remain on edge even though upcoming elections
scheduled for February 2026 could potentially repave renewed economic pathways for the nation.
Marginally increasing inflation in September and the possibility of another increase through
October also leaves little for the local ship recycling fraternity (and the industry at large) to remain
optimistic about either. 
On the plus side, HKC progress and issuance of accreditations continues at pace with a few more
yards set to get confirmed by the end of the year, taking the total number of HKC certified ship
recycling facilities in the country to 21 – encouraging given that there were only a handful of HKC 
approved facilities at the start of the year!  
For now, if you’re coming in from the Far East, be sure to check with your favorite Cash Buyer on
pricing and good / prompt deals.

INDIA 
 
LITTLE HOPE! 
The title reads just as the reader would interpret it i.e. based on your use case. But in India’s case under 
the present circumstances, it delivers a sign of hope despite being the lowest placed sub-continent ship 
recycling destination - and too by some distance.  
Accordingly, although on the one hand, there has been little in Alang to encourage or excite ship 
owners and cash buyers holding previously acquired expensive inventory, on the other hand, the 
present also offers hope to those ship owners and cash buyers who are more in agreement with 
prevailing market conditions and are ready to accept the lowest recycling levels of the moment, in 
exchange of a far more secure re-delivery that is devoid of theatrics, despite the rolling state of market
fundamentals and sentiments (subject of course all MoA terms being aligned).  
And rolling fundamentals are certainly the sign of the times with Indian steel plate prices putting on 
quite the show of movement, yet ending up where they began at the start of the week. The U.S. Dollar 
and Indian Rupee divorce has also hit peak momentum as the Rupee lost a massive 1.25% this week, 
wrapping it up at Rs. 88.70  and striking ever harder that at the foundations that have been keeping 
local levels precariously balanced during this financial earthquake. Worse yet, post-September pre-
November monsoon thunderstorms seem far from over just yet as Trump further threatens Putin with 
additional economic retaliations should the Ukrainian aggression not curtail – the beneficiary of which
has been the Indian market of late.  
Moreover, a great part of the problem has been a two-tiered market that has emerged, due to the 
dumping of cheap sanctioned tonnage (OFAC and EU / UK listed vessels) that have been concluded at 
prices well below market levels, thereby collapsing collective local expectations of where offer levels 
on conventional tonnage should stand at the bidding tables. And local port positions highlight just how
far tonnage inflow continues to confound as most are missing the fact these are likely underpriced units 
with no valid destination to arrive, with India being the only sub-continent market willing / able to 
accept these dark / shadow ships – but to what end?  
Overall, even though inflation has dropped far below the Reserve Bank of India’s 2% (stable) level and 
currently at 1.54%, there is clearly room for cheaper financial instruments and loans for ship recyclers. 
Question remains, how many will bite?

PAKISTAN
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
    
IMPORTS IRE! 
Whilst not quite as negative as a price-inert India at present, Pakistan has endured its own flavor 
of hurdles this year and even this week, with the ongoing dumping of cheap Iranian steel causing 
a reversal in vessel pricing even though local steel plate prices reman well above the entire industry 
and a whopping USD +230/Ton above those currently from India, local buyers have been unable 
to fully exploit the potential of such wide margins. Yet, recent odd maneuvers of readjusting local 
offerings in order to align themselves with India is more business as usual for the Gadani Gang,
who have miraculously managed to retain 1st place on the market rankings for a healthy portion 
of the year – with nothing to show for it.  
Similar to Bangladesh, both markets have seen a scattering of incoming tonnage over time and
although Bangladeshi buyers’ revel in the afterglow of geographically suited tonnage with no other 
competition on that side of the seaways, Pakistani recyclers have to compete with India, which is
proving to be a double-edged sword for this market. Moreover, despite having the highest offers 
at the bidding tables, local recyclers cannot escape the fact that nearly 4 months after the HKC 
coming into force, the focus to align all HKC yard upgrades towards fruition remains amiss as not 
a single yards is HKC certified yet (2026?).  
This is causing consternation for those owners and cash buyers who are considering options on a 
non-India only re-delivery. The cost of considering? Problems upon delivery as both local recyclers 
and authorities aren’t entirely grounded on the new format of arrival procedures and are thereby 
facing hiccups. Case and point, the single straggler at anchorage that’s been there since late 
September despite mediocre arrivals deliveries ongoing into his market – including this week.  
Notwithstanding, provisional DASR certificates have at least ensured some of the incoming
tonnage is going through its share of evaluation and safer recycling practices. But how long will 
this continue with plate prices flatlining and the PKR itself hitting 283.17 against the U.S. Dollar 
while Iranian steel pulls the rug from underneath local steel plate prices. It seems as though until 
HKC accreditations come firmly into play, this destination will likely fester at the top spot in 
isolation. And not because they are able to offer the highest, but because competing markets 
continue to slide.

SUPPLY PINCH! 
 
As the utter lack of supply continues to hammer Aliaga for several years now thanks to the red tape 
of bureaucracy and coupled with a near absence of Turkey-only market tonnage for nearly 4 years 
now, it wasn’t unexpected to see local recyclers raise their offers in order to tempt owners who may
only be in the deliberation stages of recycling their units at this time.  
But fundamentals remain far from their side as although local steel plate prices have made marginal 
improvements, the TRY (ending the week at 42.06 against the U.S.$) has done more damage to keep 
local business operating at usual speeds. 
And while inflation (at a whopping +33%) may have countered some of the resale potential of 
recycled ship’s steel, it’s a matter of time until Turkey’s government makes appropriate fiscal changes 
to balance the cost of living, while simultaneously squeezing the pace of the domestic ship recycling 
sector, especially as lending rates remain unattainable for tier-B (and below) recyclers.
