Compounding market uncertainties took center stage with Pres. Trump’s uncalculated tariff wars 
sending economies rocking this week, unintentionally targeting the U.S. Dollar as the beneficiary to 
a crisis that is still unfolding, sending it into a tizzy as it declined against nearly all of the major ship
recycling destinations, except? You guessed it, Turkey, where it plummeted nearly 0.5% this week,
creating an all-time record low. While some of the executed tariffs have taken affect and others are
reportedly still in the works, this limbo of a global inflationary explosion that’s seemly lying in wait
has even seen oil prices temper further this week, with the barrel falling further on the news that a
Trump / Zelenskiy and even Trump / Putin meeting to end the Ukraine war, remains in the works.
This saw oil end the week at USD 70.7/barrel as charter rates reflected the unfolding present with 
the Baltic Index Dry Index reporting another drop early in the week, only to end it a shade higher.
None of which, has tempered the supply of tonnage for the ship recycling markets as units remain 
in play and anchorages earmarked fresh arrival(s) this week as well.   
Through it all, the knock-on effects of the tit-for-tat trade wars have certainly been felt across the
ship recycling markets as well – perennially in India, where despite PM Modi being in America to 
visit Pres. Trump and ensure no tariffs were in the works to affect India and ensure the smooth and 
ongoing cooperation between the nations, the 25% tariffs announced on Chinese steel & aluminum
products is already seeing destabilizing effects on the recycling markets, and it remains unsure as
to where much of this surplus / excess and resultingly cheaper steel will end up, devastating ship 
recycling prices as a result of.  
Speaking of prices, downward pressure on tabled levels has remained unrelenting since the start of 
the year and the ongoing and intolerable performances of key fundamentals has continued to push 
levels down ever since USD 600/Ton was achieved back in January 2024. Flatlining steel plate prices 
across the board including declining levels from India that have fallen in excess of USD 11/Ton over 
the course of 2 weeks alone, has done little for prevailing offerings. In fact, over the course of last 
year, both India and Pakistan were rigorously subjected to the import of cheaper Chinese steel 
imports that kept undercutting local inventories, resulting in sub-continent steel markets collapsing.
Although new duties by both governments to curb the ill effects of this cheaper steel via anti-
dumping tariffs were introduced, there remains a great deal of nervousness as to how these latest 
rounds of tit-for-tat tariffs will end up hitting the ship recycling industry.  
As recycling markets have deteriorated by nearly USD 30/LDT over the course of this year already,
a drop that looks determined to increase, it has been an unexpectedly gloomier picture for the first 
7 weeks of 2025 despite the bullish start to the year that saw numerous LNGs and at least ten 
Panamax bulkers from the Chinese / Far Eastern markets concluded for a recycling sale. As such, 
questionably interesting times lie ahead, all of which could see a landmark year for ship recycling,
with yards still in the process of being upgraded to HKC standards in both Bangladesh and 
Pakistan, ahead of the HKC’s entry into force post June 30th of this year, and Pakistan ending the 
week with its only delivery of the year.

ROCKY RACCOON! 
Of late, an increasing focus on larger LDT vessels coming out of the Far East has seen Bangladeshi
recyclers book a healthy collection of decent-sized units since the start of the year – including an
FSU, a Capesize bulker, and even several LNGs tankers that followed a decent number of arrivals
at Chattogram’s anchorage across recent weeks including this week, which registered a couple of
fresh arrivals and a bevy of deliveries to their respective recyclers. Yet, the ‘busy’ anchorage hid
the deceptive illusion of Chattogram’s ship recycling sector that not all is well, even with the nation
at large.  
At the macro end of things, the economy has spent another dull week with not much movement
reported locally, amidst a political scenario that keeps on repeating in dialogue over reforms vs.
early elections, in order to speed up a potential recovery. Yet, no apparent resolution on either
stands in effect to this date and a specific direction for the nation at this time, remains uncertain.
The general law and order situation in the country also remains turbulent given that the interim
government remains unable to get a grip on unfolding events and riots (as reported last week) are
still ongoing. Even despite Asia Development Bank (ADB) pledging USD 1 Billion in concessional 
aid to Bangladesh every year for the next 5 years, local economists are calling on the nation to brace 
for incoming headwinds on the news that the country registered the slowest economic growth as
compared to the last 4. While this has previously seen the government curtail expenditures on
foreign reserves towards non-critical sectors through 2023 and even 2024, given the unfolding
economic crises, could be a foretelling of another financial crisis that is yet to potentially hit in
Bangladesh’s ship recycling sector the (near) future, especially in the face of a deadline extension
from the Bangladeshi Ministry with when to begin yard infrastructure upgrades from the original
January 31st cutoff date by the BSBRA, to March 31st, as work reportedly commences on yards who
aim to be fully compliant with the Hong Kong Convention ahead of its entry into force by the end
of June 2025 and recyclers face further cuts in financial aid.  
At the micro end, as inflation and ship recycling fundamentals remain extremely volatile, not only
are local steel plate prices still flatlining, but the Taka made a surprising and desperately needed
recovery of 31 basis point against the U.S. Dollar, ending the week at BDT 121.69. While domestic
offerings for tonnage remain constricted as a result of previous declines on the Taka into the BDT
120s, sentiments continue on a downward trajectory as most of the aggressive and capable local
recyclers are now booked with recent deliveries and the next batch of indications emanating are
far below than those of recent fixtures, further pushing ship owners and cash buyers to chase down 
markets that are clearly in fall. As such, Bangladesh seems to be treading on rocky terrain with a
momentary of binging of tonnage in the face of suppressed fundamentals and vessel pricing backed
by geography and timing that have somewhat come together and insulate this market from the
recent tariffs, even though the fear of the far-reaching effects of these potentially hitting
Bangladesh, remains high. And lower recycling indications seen since the start of the year are now
permanently snaking their way onto the bidding tables from the next tier of Chattogram Choppers.

DOOZIE! 
Panic gripped India this week as the U.S. slapped 25% tariffs on Chinese steel & aluminum products 
– the unfolding fear of which is that, this surplus inventory (some of it already ready for export) is 
eventually going to make its way to the sub-continent markets where it will more than likely face 
price adjustments made to undercut sub-continent steel prices just so that Chinese steel owners can 
offload their excess inventories at increasingly loss making prices despite previously imposed and 
potentially further incoming punitive duties from the Indian government set to limit this crisis, has 
clearly done little thus far.  
Whilst PM Modi was in the US to meet with President Trump this week in order to discuss this very 
issue amongst many others, in an attempt to layout potential damage control and aid India’s economy 
(which is in recovery) amidst growing international pressures that are all but certain to affect sub-
continent markets across 2025, as Alang itself struggles to maintain its levels through current times. 
The present-day worldwide economy is also already facing the domino effect of the tariffs as global 
steel including Chinese plate prices, continue to flatline at far lower levels than those of Indian steel 
prices this week. This has seen (and will likely continue to see) Indian steel prices decline in excess of 
USD 11/Ton over the last couple of weeks, seriously pressuring Alang ship recyclers to reconsider 
and lower their offerings for future tonnage coming out of the Red Sea, at noticeable lower levels.  
The currency has, however, strangely found stability this week as not only did it stall ongoing 
declines, but it also reversed recent losses by gaining about 1.2% against the U.S. Dollar and ending 
the week at Rs. 86.60. Although to the outsider, this may seem like a “win” of sorts, the truth is that 
the Rupee has lost nearly 5% over the last 4 Quarters and as a result, the ongoing decline in prices 
likely isn’t going to immediately translate into a price jump just yet.  
And like Bangladesh, India is the only nation Eastward that is helping maintain a balance in the 
ongoing re-distribution of global tonnage for recycling as prices remain in the neck of the woods for 
units intended for an India / Pakistan re-delivery. Yet, given the ongoing price differentials with 
Pakistani Buyers after nearly 5 months who are still tabling a healthy collection of weak offers on a 
majority of their ongoing offerings, has helped India consume all but a single unit that has come 
available for a recycling sale basis both destinations as potentially final ones.

PAKISTAN
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
    
NO TIME LIKE PRESENT…HOPEFULLY! 
After an altogether inert few years on the sidelines, some signs of life are finally emanating from 
the Gadani gang and on the back of their recent effort, a couple of sales have even materialized to 
local buyers albeit on comparatively smaller LDT tonnage, one of which was even delivered to her
new recyclers this week, the only delivery into Pakistan thus far in 2025. And unlike the other
markets, Gadani’s anchorage has reported no further arrivals since. Notwithstanding, local offers
remain not too far away behind their Indian counterparts, but sufficient enough to matter on the 
larger LDT units that have invariably headed further West to Alang, as Gadani recycling yards lie
virtually dormant following such a prolonged period of inactivity.  
But as welcome as this price aggression may seem, it remains a target too low for a majority of ship
owners and cash buyers who are looking to offload tonnage as on the broader end of things for
local recyclers, the Pakistani government is still struggling to maintain control of their unraveling
economic affairs whilst the Pakistani army is essentially aiming to regain control of the government
once again. Moreover, in the midst of a potential visit from IMF delegates to investigate corruption 
and governmental shortcomings in the nation, a turbulent time lies ahead for Pakistan’s domestic
ship recycling sector, which is entirely dependent on U.S. Dollar transactions for financial
assistance from the central bank, which in itself has been hemorrhaging cash up wards of USD 440
million amidst reported mismanagement. With the incoming IMF team ready to investigate and 
further lay out a framework to wash out wasteful / unnecessary / potentially illegal access to funds
to aid the nation’s economy, how this will affect the nation’s ship recycling industry and / or the 
ease of with which future access to funds / L/Cs becomes available, remains to be seen. 
Fow now, on the back of a reported few private fixtures, we don’t know how much more activity
to expect at Gadani’s anchorage as once these recent fixtures and eventual deliveries bottom out,
like in Bangladesh, will have likely done its share in satisfying tonnage to tier 1 Gadani buyers and
tier 2 of buyers is already and expectedly testing the market with lower offerings, hoping to snag
a deal at a time most markets seem destined to be heading South, and more importantly, ship
owners and cash buyers start to finally bite at these lower(ing) levels.  
Upgrade work does need to begin in earnest in Gadani so as to get domestic ship recycling facilities
up to HKC standards ahead of its entry into force in June of this year, and hopefully still remain 
relevant post July 1st once no non-HKC yard will be permitted to take in tonnage now that the 
convention has been officially ratified by the Pakistani Ministry. Thankfully, some yards seem to
have realized that the hard work must start now in order to remain relevant, and news of some
upgrades (in the works) being initiated has come forward. 
Ship recycling post June 2025 is going to be a wild ride in Gadani.
