and oil prices reversed course this week where charter rates jumped 1.5% by close of Friday while
oil receded by over 1% ending the week at USD 63/barrel. Flip flops seem to be the theme of the 
week as OPEC+ countries too are reportedly seeing this week’s version of the future from the White

President Trump’s playful grip this week.

90-day pause on tariffs was announced, ship recycling markets have remained cautiously stalled as
the ambiguity around U.S. - China tariffs has maintained volatility on recycling nation steel plate

with Easter Holidays across Europe now over, charter fixtures remain solid and will likely continue 
to deprive recycling markets of some much-needed tonnage for yet another week. It’s already May

Upgrades a must.

HKC STUCK! 
The Bangladeshi ship recycling sector came to a virtual standstill this week given that no fresh No 
Objection Certificates (NOCs) have been issued for over two weeks now, thereby suspending 
nearly all active negotiations with local recyclers on potential units currently at the bidding tables,
in addition to the likely redirection of incoming units towards India / Pakistan range as only now 
are Bangladeshi authorities starting to inspect local ship recycling yards to ensure that the level of 
infrastructure upgrades needed towards attaining an HKC Statement of Compliance (SoC) by its 
gradually approaching deadline, had been initiated by the respective recyclers ever since the
original deadline of January 31st to initiate such upgrades was extended to March 31st, and a 2nd
extension now hangs in the balance.  
Before any further extensions will be considered or granted, especially to those facilities that have 
units idling outside or even on the way, that indeed the necessary infrastructure works have started
and permissions on incoming units belonging to these recyclers could (hopefully) be granted
allowing these units inside. But with the nation officially having confirmed to abide by the rules of 
the convention with its entry into force by June 26th, a mere 8 weeks away, the pressure is on the
authorities to ensure the nation will deliver as assured and is the reason behind the seeming
unwillingness of local authorities to ‘play ball’ with local recyclers. As a result, Chattogram has not
reported any fresh arrivals of recycling units into Chattogram this week (on account of those pesky 
missing NOCs), all while a couple of last week’s arrivals to recently approved SoC yards of which, 
there are reportedly a total of 7 thus far ,managed to deliver to their respective owners this week.  
On the positive side, our Bangladeshi offices reported late Sunday that the Director General of 
Shipping did visit local ship recycling yards over the weekend and was pleased with the overall 
upgrades done so far, confirming that NOCs would be issued to certain yards with satisfactory
progress. Although how many ship recycling yards with disproportionate degrees of upgrades
stand to be given permissions and whether the vessels idling outside do indeed belong to this
freshly approved (or yet to be rejected) batch, remains to be seen. On the worst-case scenario, any 
yard not yet HKC approved will have to wait to import vessels or display substantial progress of 
updates before they can continue operations and open future L/Cs.  
This has put a virtual pause on the domestic ship recycling industry whilst the results from the 
inspections (potential re-inspections for some) are completed. And with domestic fundamentals 
performing horrendously – local steel plate prices opening post Eid at USD 478/Ton (a whopping
USD 61/Ton collapse from USD 529/Ton) and the Taka losing 10 basis points to close the week out 
at BDT 121.99 against the U.S. Dollar - the ongoing downtime may be worth the wait to see where
these turbulent fundamentals eventually settle. For now, we well see Bangladesh’s standing atop 
the market rankings come under threat from India, especially if Bangladeshi steel plate prices take 
another tumble in the coming week(s).

INDIA 
 
CONUNDRUM! 
With Bangladesh’s recent involvement at the bidding tables coming to a pause amidst their ongoing, 
slow-moving yard and NOC approvals and overall extension processes spanning the last two weeks 
over which, Indian ship recyclers jumped at the opportunity to step up and conclude a majority of 
the available market tonnage during this interim period. And with several small LDT, prompt 
delivery units being traded, this week’s anchorage was a display of purchasing prowess blended with
geographic convenience as 6 ships and nearly 40K LDT is currently idling at Alang.  
However, volatility continues to reign in India as not only did declining (towards the end of the week) 
local steel plate prices manage to still end USD 1/Ton higher than last, but the Indian Rupee suffered 
through extreme volatility through the course of the week and ended it 5 basis point lower than last
week, at nearly Rs. 85.40 against the U.S. Dollar. Moreover, the ongoing U.S. China tariff uncertainty
and overall economic global tensions continue to rear their collective heads in India (and Pakistan) 
once again, leaving most in the ship recycling industry calling for calm unless (and until) the situation 
actually deteriorates.  
Recent Pakistan-linked terrorist activity that also led to the deaths of several innocents in Northern 
India is seemingly escalating to a dangerous level between the two nuclear armed nations as India 
threatens a retaliatory suspension of water services into Pakistan from the Northwest of India, and 
extremist Pakistani politicians call for blood should India act on its threat. Amidst cancelation of 
student visas between the countries and other punitive retaliations, India is starting to build up a
significant presence of armed forces along the Line of Control (LoC) against the claimed Paksitani 
border, as the threat of another potential war has certainly seen a recently stabilizing sentiment take 
a hit again, resulting in an overall decline in aggression to conclude even on a unit of local interest
beyond safe margins, despite the overall economic state of the nation being in good standing.  
Moreover, as Pakistani purchases retreat into the shadows and Bangladesh is still out of the fray, it is 
certainly upon Indian recyclers to make the most of this current situation and conclude whatever the 
market has to offer. What happens in May is a mystery waiting to unravel itself, with looming tariff
whiplashes and reacting local fundamentals that could stand to up-end price levels and an overall 
lack of tonnage at the bidding tables. Ship owners could once again be chasing down the markets 
again, hoping the slide has ended.  
Finally, with monsoons around the corner and local yards being fully certified to HKC standards, 
Alang recyclers do have it comparatively easier than the regional competition is currently enduring. 
Mystery May.

PAKISTAN
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
    
TRYING BUT FAILING! 
There have been several opportunities for Pakistani ship recyclers to step into the ever-evolving
spiral that has been the ship recycling industry so far this year, in order to secure their share of
tonnage with aggression and firmer prices that were certainly higher than those from neighboring
India over a recent few weeks. But Gadani buyers have regrettably been unable to secure much of 
the market tonnage over the past 3 quarters unto their dormant plots, tonnage that eventually 
ended up in competing Alang including this week, where Gadani expectantly and regrettably
returned to an empty port position once again – a phenomenon that plagued local ship recyclers
for well over 2 quarters between late 2024 – early 2025.  
And like India, Pakistani fundamentals certainly have a fair share of their effect on local sentiments
as not only did local steel plate prices fall USD 10/Ton down to USD 624/Ton this week, but the 
Pakistani Rupee also continues to suffer as it fell dramatically to PKR 282.19 against the U.S. Dollar, 
only to eventually firm up and still end 16 basis points weaker than last week, at PKR 282.05.
Moreover, as Chinese steel plate prices themselves firmed to and above USD 300/Ton this week
and the future of tariffs hangs in the balance, should Pakistani steel levels continue to fall and 
Chinese levels continue to become more competitive, it may not bode well for Gadani ship 
recyclers as 2025 progresses, as local steel plate prices will come crashing down if there is no supply 
of steel / raw material from recycled ships and cheaper Chinese imports become more appealing 
to domestic steel mills.  
Moreover, even the domestic economy, though stable, remains a work in progress when compared 
to a recently firming India. And the possibility that we may see recycling tonnage heading towards
Gadani shores in the coming days or even weeks, is now an increasingly diminishing probability.
Notwithstanding, the more pressing matter of the moment remains around the pending HKC 
upgrades at Gadani yards, especially with all of the recently unfolding drama in Bangladesh via 
suspended entries for recycling units into Chattogram port limits, which should be a stark warning
to Pakistani recyclers of what could happen to them in the next 8 weeks, should they fail to follow 
up with their own yard infrastructure works (that have reportedly barely begun) before the HKC 
enters into force on June 26th, lest they too face the same NOC hurdles post  July 1st and as
Bangladeshi recyclers are now going through.  
Finally, as tonnage availability (even of regionally available units) increases (looking at you, Alang)
monsoon remains a mere 5 weeks away, HKC kicks in a short 3 weeks hereafter, and hesitant ship
owners unwilling to initially commit their units into Gadani will likely exacerbate the ability of 
Gadani buyers to procure tonnage through the last 2 quarters of the year, certainly leaving a bleak
2025 forecast for Pakistan, much like it could be for Turkey.

BOTTOMS…BLOWN!! 
Turkey has endured a crazy 3 weeks whereby import and local steel plate prices have dropped by 
USD 52/Ton and USD 40/Ton respectively, having each declined a further USD 20/Ton this week as 
well. Not to be outdone, the Lira took its parachute off and jumped out of the plane, plummeting
past a 1.6% decline this week and ending it at nearly TRY 38.50 against the U.S. Dollar.  
And despite the 90-day tariff pause kicking into effect, Turkey’s economy seems to be the one major 
ship recycling destination hit especially hard, with sentiments increasingly battered and Aliaga 
indications on prospective units dropping another USD 20/MT this week, the overall offer levels on
bulkers / dry cargo vessels from Turkey now hitting USD 250/MT – a remarkable drop from only a
couple of years ago when recycling vessels were being regularly concluded well into USD 400/MT
to Aliaga recyclers. 
And with sub-continent levels being steady overall, the now USD 200/Ton price differential will 
certainly be a hard pill for board members of publicly traded ship owning companies to swallow,
should they consider the recycling route on their again beauties, thereby further alienating Turkish 
recyclers in the process. Like Pakistan, the remainder of the year seems like it might be destined to a
two-market industry – shared between India and Bangladesh.
