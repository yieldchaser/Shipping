## 3. Country Market Intelligence

### Bangladesh

- **Headline**: TIK TOK TAK(A)!
- **Key Notes**: *Taka tumbles.* | *Backlog delivering.* | *Docs needed, delays* | *ensue.* | *Interesting June.*

Last week, the Bangladeshi government decided to let market forces dictate the value of the Taka, a move officiated by the terms and conditions of the current IMF Loan. This has seen the Taka fall 80 basis points and head nearly vertically towards BDT 122 (as the week ended) once again, ending it pennies short at BDT 121.80. And while the Taka suffocates Bangladesh’s ship recycling fraternity on the one hand, local steel plate prices on the other flatlined at levels region $475/ton post-Eid - pre-market / NOC closures in April and levels steel levels opened nearly USD 100/Ton higher at about USD 577/Ton, whereafter, in keeping with the depreciation of the Taka also saw steel plate prices lose a whopping USD 21/Ton this week. As a result, the movement of the overall economy has been increasingly spelling out disaster for the interim government that has finally committed to a June 30th date for General Elections to be held, as overall faith and confidence in the current administration loses further points among the domestic populous.

Notwithstanding the decline in plate prices, their current levels have certainly provided local recyclers with more than a buffer to present reasonable offers on available units under the present circumstances. However, with the HKC on the horizon, not as many local buyers have been able to bid on tonnage on account of missing HKC accreditations / inability to obtain No Objection Certificates (NoCs) on their incoming acquisitions until a certain rate of development has been confirmed by yard owners, as mandated by the recently formed Ship Recycling Board. And while several HKC yards have been approved over recent weeks - taking the total number of approved yards up to 9 – there are reportedly over 20 yards that are presently undergoing their inspections / approvals ahead of being able to get their respective NOCs once again.

This inability to obtain NOCs saw a healthy backlog of vessels build up outside port limits across April and even May whilst yard inspections took place, causing severe delays for ship owners and cash buyers alike. And given just how busy Bangladesh’s waterfront has been this week compared to its regional competition (8 ships and nearly 54K LDT), this market is certainly pushing the pedal to the floor with its acquisitions and even yard upgrades. But with all these regulatory changes come additional documentary requirements as now, a Ship Recycling Facility Plan (SRFPs), Ship Recycling Plan (SRPs), and even an Inventory of Hazardous Materials (IHMs) needs to be provided to, and approved by the ministry before NOCs can be granted, further highlighting that delays are all but guaranteed when getting vessels cleared inward, paid for, and eventually delivered.

With only 9 yards currently able to bid on tonnage being introduced for sale at the bidding tables and many in Chattogram having filled their yards over the last couple of weeks, a bare minimum number of open buyers are not pursuing fresh units aggressively in the face of pending approvals, which has seen prices from Bangladesh start to cool as the focus falls on neighboring markets to pick up the slack. The onset of rains isn’t helping local steel trade either as demand from steel mills itself remains curtailed, coupled with upcoming elections and a budget for the next FY leaves the potential for an interesting June lying in wait for this market.

### India

- **Headline**: ALWAYS AVAILABLE!
- **Key Notes**: *Falling steel.* | *LNGs sold.* | *HKC ready.*

Despite declining vessel prices in Bangladesh and the recently established fragile peace with Pakistan holding ground for the time-being, Alang buyers have persisted in the market, able to secure any unit at prevailing rates and even pick up some of the slack from a recently slowing Bangladeshi market. Such a case saw several Hyundai-controlled MOSS type LNGs sold into India this week as the larger LDT HYUNDAI AQUAPIA (34,066 LDT) and HYUNDAI TECHNOPIA (34,046 LDT) were both fixed for a decent enbloc price basis an ‘as is’ Korea / Batam delivery respectively – in what is earmarking some of the freshest sales of the market. And given their pedigree and green requirements, these units are more than likely destined to end up in India, with Bangladesh’s ongoing delays in having vessels cleared locally and a minimum number of currently open (Bangladeshi) recyclers.

India’s economy, meanwhile, remains much stronger than its neighbouring competition and its fundamentals continue to take the opportunity to inflate and simultaneously deflate local sentiments as on the one hand, the Indian Rupee firmed an impressive 60 basis points to end the week at Rs. 85.06 against the U.S. Dollar, while on the other hand, local steel plate prices remain the lowest in the Indian sub-continent, plummeting a whopping USD 11/Ton this week alone, ensuring that tonnage hungry Alang prices will likely remain stagnated (for now). Alang also did not report a single fresh arrival this week and to make matters worse, there is reportedly not a single vessel idling outside awaiting entry. Certainly, a surprising week for Alang’s anchorage given just how busy last week was in comparison.

Yet, India remains comfortably in the lead with over 100 HKC approved yards, leaving the country a far more open and available market for owners and cash byers alike, albeit at lower levels than their competitors. India’s economy also continues to flourish as a nation as it displaces Japan for the 4th place in global rankings of the world’s largest economies, and forecasts have also set the rate of GDP growth at 7% when FY25 winds up, driving up national economic sentiment and firming the INR. Notwithstanding, despite an overall depreciated currency, declining steel plate prices, and ongoing global tariff concerns affecting the nation, Alang remains the go to destination for straightforward and prompt deliveries, far from the self-induced and ongoing delays being currently witnessed in Bangladesh.

If India steel plate prices make a small jump in the coming weeks, Alang recyclers would be in an incredible position take in a majority of the tonnage that is likely to come available prior to June 26th and even thereafter, if Pakistan doesn’t step up its HKC updates. Plate issues.

### Pakistan

- **Headline**: TOO LITTLE, TOO LATE!
- **Key Notes**: *Greater demand.* | *Inadequate.* | *Need to improve.* | *2023 again?*

Some greater demand, pricing, and sense of urgency to acquire tonnage seems to be emerging from Pakistan given that there remains just about a month to go until the Hong Kong Convention comes into force on June 26th.

Unfortunately, these moves to acquire seem to be too little too late with undervalued (in Pakistan) LNGs make the rounds this week while yard infrastructure / HKC upgrades remain woefully uninitiated at a healthy majority of domestic ship recycling yards, which itself is likely to take many more months to even get to anywhere near HKC standards, far from even achieving their respective accreditations thereafter; meaning Pakistan is likely to be excluded in the short term (perhaps even the long term if HKC development doesn’t pick up pace asap) from being proposed any units for recycling.

Meanwhile, the ongoing ceasefire has kept domestic tensions high and domestic fundamentals deviated from their performances over recent weeks whereby local steel plate prices managed to miraculously firm up by about USD 4/Ton this week, all while the Pakistani Rupee appreciated notably towards the end of the week and still lost about 11 basis points overall, to clock out at PKR 281.84 against the U.S. Dollar with a high that breached PKR 283 mid-week.

At the Macro end of the nation’s economic state, the IMF’s recent tranche has certainly helped stabilize the Pakistani economy for the interim, whilst the Pakistani Prime Minister assured various chambers of commerce that steps are being taken to ensure easier access to business and financial loans and instruments. While this should certainly assist local recyclers with potential negotiations on active tonnage, it should provide further assistance to yard owners and help them fast track the necessary improvements needed at their respective yards.

With the nation’s upcoming budget for FY 2025 – 2026 due on June 2nd, 2026, hopefully some much- needed attention is heeded to one of the most capital-intensive and vital sectors of the nation and if any units are to destined head to Pakistan before the HKC comes into force, prices will need to improve much more so as to divert Far East tonnage away from Bangladesh, and even some of the dry bulk sales, or even take a bite out of the less preferred larger LDT LNGs from India. Sadly, for the Gadani market, their efforts at acquiring tonnage remain far from fruitful as Pakistan’s recycling anchorage registers a 5th week of no arrivals.

### Turkey

- **Headline**: SURPRISE!
- **Key Notes**: *39.02.*

As both India and Pakistan suffer dithering anchorages this week and tonnage fixtures remain non- existent for the Turkish market, this week, news of the Greek (ferry) giant Attica reportedly diverted another Ropax to Aliaga as the 1979 built Kriti I (about 12,850 MT) was towed to a Turkish recycler at a price of about USD 280/MT. Being EU-based and subject to the regulations of the EUSRR meant that the owners were compelled to commit the vessel at nearly USD 175/Ton below that of competing Indian. Certainly, a large value on a vessel this size.

Steel plate prices meanwhile remain relatively unchanged from those of last week, all whilst the grease on the soles of Turkish Lira continue to help its unabated slide as it fell nearly 50 basis points and closed the week out at a whopping TRY 39.02 against the U.S. Dollar. Although vessel prices have held for the time being, there is no telling where they will be when the Lira finally breaches past the dreaded psychological TRY 40 limit.
