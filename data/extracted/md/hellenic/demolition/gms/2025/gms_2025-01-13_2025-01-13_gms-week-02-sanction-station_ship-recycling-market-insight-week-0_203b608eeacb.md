“Forget about style; worry about results.”
 
 
– Bobby Orr 
This week earmarked President Biden placing sanctions against China’s national shipping arm 
COSCO, on the basis of the company being a part of the Chinese military, blacklisting its energy / 
tanker division. In fact, the octogenarian went on quite the sanction spree this week with the U.S. 
reportedly sanctioning over 180 vessels and dozens of entities in Russia as well, in what has turned 
out to be an unprecedented Russia crackdown in the final days of his presidency. Although experts 
in the field are quick to affirm that China’s COSCO sanction would likely not have the same impact 
as the 2019 sanctions did, but energy costs for nations on COSCOs supply-chain routes will likely 
face inflationary hiccups in the interim, the far-reaching effects of which are yet to be realized. Even 
oil futures reported hiccups towards the end of the week as the immediate effects on COSCO and 
Russia sent the barrel soaring to US$ 76.57 / barrel, a level not seen since last October. Additionally, 
the Baltic Index Dry Freight Index for dry bulk commodities jumped 8.2% by Friday, marking a 
second-consecutive week of firming rates. Still, the recycling traffic in both India and Bangladesh 
continued to surprise and impress (especially in India), where the water fronts reported a busy week 
of arrivals and deliveries. The pre-existing state of global economies also continues to persist in the 
wake of the unfolding present as the U.S. Dollar continues to hammer recycling nation currencies 
marking noteworthy increases by the week – including this one. Even steel prices affecting the ship 
recycling markets including domestic levels saw a weakening this week, with China leading the 
way in declining / cheaper steel that remains globally available. How far will India’s ongoing steel 
tariffs assist Alang’s ongoing performance, whilst Pakistani levels are just a hair away from an 
impending drop, remains to be seen. 
Meanwhile, the various ship recycling nations endured another week of mixed performances as 
there has already been a marked contrast in terms of actually workable candidates compared to a 
very muted 2024, which saw the lowest volumes of overall units sold for recycling in over a decade. 
Indeed, and as stated above, several high profile and large(r) LDT vessels are being offered for sale 
this week, including a bevy of LNGs and Panamax bulkers, after a slower period of time where 
specialist small(er) LDT units, handy bulkers, and reefers remained the primary diet for sub-
continent ship recyclers for a bulk of the year. It will therefore be interesting to see what finally 
becomes of these particularly old and sanctioned assets with nowhere to take them, or will they just 
be left to rot / become abandoned hazards at sea? With the HKC also gearing up to come into effect 
by the middle of this year, and yards yet to expeditiously gear up and align their operations with 
the requirements of the convention, it is likely that 2025 will be another hallmark year characterized 
by volatility, extreme and unpredictable moves / events that can turn markets in an instant, and 
financial worries for those yards that have redirected and even borrowed funds for yard upgrades 
and may end up suffering through ongoing tonnage shortages that deter their recovery from their 
financial liabilities. Prices are therefore likely to present their share of volatility over the course of 
the year, highlighted by the unfolding sanctions that would affect the supply of vessels, especially 
in the short-term. While the sub-continent ship recycling destinations continue on from last week 
in relatively the same state without much fanfare as this week winds up, not much remains changed, 
even in Turkey, where the only movements reported by the week, are on the Lira. Viva 2025.

VAT WOES 
As Bangladesh continues to battle economic uncertainty after one of the worst years in the nation’s
history, the situation doesn’t seem to be getting any better as government reserves continue to 
deplete amidst a dithering recovery under a temporary government that doesn’t seem to have 
gotten their administrative gears in mesh just yet, as the economy continues to lose traction through 
its unfolding inflationary disaster. In ongoing attempts to correct this trajectory, the government 
unexpectedly announced that the National Board of Revenue would be planning a rate hike in the 
VAT by the middle of the fiscal year, despite widespread concerns that this move will stoke 
inflation even more and further degrade economic activities – something that could see domestic 
steel transactions, ship recycling prices, and overall demand from this market start to retreat even 
more. On the one hand, although the rationale behind such an action is to assist with the interest 
repayments on the IMF’s upcoming remittances, on the other, yard upgrades, vessel negotiations, 
and everything near and far related to even affecting the price of steel, will be greatly diminished. 
And as the possibility of lower offers on future vessels seems well within the limit of a few weeks
from now and the negative sentiment continues to permeate, Bangladesh could soon slip past India 
and end up closer to Pakistani levels, which themselves are roiled in a similar situation. Yet, 
Bangladesh somehow seems to be worse off, as key economic factors affecting an imminent return 
to a recovery path also need to address (at the very least), prevailing political instability, banking 
sector instability, elevated (and likely to rise again) inflation, high interest rates (especially for local 
businesses), and the mother of all worries, the seemingly uncontrollable exchange rate volatility.  
And still certifying the latter is the ongoing performance of the Bangladeshi Taka that has, for the 
last 2+ quarters, suffered at the hands of the U.S. Dollar as it ends the week nearly 1% weaker and 
at a comparatively weaker BDT 120.96, despite having nearly breached BDT 122 once again. Local 
steel plate prices seem to be the only anchor keeping ship recycler sentiments in check, as despite 
being flatlined yet again, they remain suspended at a comparatively healthier USD 529.41/Ton.
And although this has helped maintain local sentiments steady enough to the where current vessel 
offers are still the highest in the industry, it has certainly helped bring in another 2 small tankers
with a total weight of just about 6.5K LDT at Chattogram’s anchorage. Just how will Biden’s
COSCO sanction affect the general inflow of future wet units into Bangladesh, remains to be seen. 
For now, Chattogram Choppers have managed to avoid a Gadani-gate of an empty port report. 
Overall, despite retaining pole position on the market rankings, the Bangladeshi market seems to 
cool a shade this week, even though a decent number of local sales (reportedly including a VLCC) 
and several unsold vessels are still trying to find their respective final resting grounds before the 
customary Chinese New Year slowdown drives things to a screeching halt. And those ship owners 
and excited cash buyers who were banking on a post-2024 Bangladesh at levels firmer than those 
currently unraveling, are having their expectations at the bidding tables continue to take a beating,
especially as over-aggressive cash buyers with unsold vessels bought at higher rates are now
starting to suffer and pay the price for their exuberance.

Like Bangladesh, the Indian market continues to strut through an unexpected January 2025 with the
poor performance of the Indian Rupee that continues to fluctuate against the strengthening U.S. 
Dollar and has remained as the primary deterrent keeping Alang recyclers from improving their 
prices over the last fortnight or so. Especially as it blew industry players expectations out of the 
water and ended the week at levels approaching Rs. 86.20 – adding another (yet solid) excuse that 
recyclers have on their itemized list of justifications to present behind their lower offers, despite 
varying degrees of economically positive news that continue to enter the market.  
At the ship recycling outset and unlike Bangladesh and Pakistan, India’s foreign reserves greatly 
exceed them, which cements India’s ability to not only offer a stable interest rate to domestic 
recyclers, but also a steady inflow of U.S. Dollars and L/Cs that has already helped nearly all Alang 
ship yards up to mark for the upcoming HKC rules by the middle of the year, and helped Alang 
become a destination of less economic concern for incoming deliveries via fewer renegotiations at 
the waterfront. 
And speaking of, just like Chattogram, Alang has not marked an empty port report through all of 
2024 and even so far in 2025, where this week, 6 vessels and nearly 58K LDT lies idling or delivered 
to their respective owners. This tonnage will certainly keep trade with domestic steel mills active, 
which have been the only ones registering any movements in the sub-continent over recent weeks, 
ending the week on a downward trajectory and a Rupee short, at USD 459.94/Ton. And although 
the expectation is that the tariffs intended to curtail the import of damagingly cheaper Chinese steel 
that continues to collapse week after week causing continued and tremendous pain to steel markets 
in both India and Pakistan last year (when it consistently undercut local steel inventories), the 
ongoing dither in Chinese plate levels doesn’t look like it’s done with the Indian sub-continent steel
markets just yet.  
Whether the ongoing declines in Chinese steel eventually trickle down to the sub-continent markets,
remains to be seen.  
For now, however, despite the otherwise (and comparatively) strong state of the India economy,
vessel prices aren’t displaying any positive signs or movements and perhaps, won’t do so any time 
soon as they currently lie stubbornly stuck in the mid USD 450s/LDT on most dry vessels, of which, 
Alang recyclers seem destined to be getting more of. Moreover, with the budget just a fortnight 
away and expectations of positive news running high, India remains the go to place for all types of 
tonnages, including those that require SoC certified HKC recycling yards as a final destination. 
Rupee deterrant.

PAKISTAN 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
ECONOMIC FRACTURES! 
Worse of than Bangladesh is the Pakistani market where in addition to having crippling effects on 
its economy with inflation still high, a near and total lack of available L/Cs has essentially bought 
the nation’s ship recycling sector to a complete halt for the last 4 months, as Gadani’s anchorage 
presents another empty position yet again this week. And although Gadani recyclers have been 
bobbing their heads above the waterline to check the prevailing recycling weather of late, this 
sector has, for the most part since the advent of Q4 2024, remained underwater. 
The fractured state of the nation economy is the key driver behind Pakistan’s ongoing financial 
distresses. With loans lying in the balance of pending investigations, domestic economists 
predicting return of inflation into double digits, and the World Bank’s promised aid that is still 
hanging in the balance as reported in last week’s edition of the GMS WEEKLY, the Gadani gang 
seem to all be preparing for the upcoming feast, with meals yet waiting to thaw.  
As a result of the indecisiveness and the government curtailing the issuance of large U.S. Dollar 
value L/Cs on non-essential domestic expenses, large Dollar value transactions out of the country 
have all but halted leaving the Pakistani Rupee as one of the most stable ship recycling nation 
currencies of all, across 2024. On the back of minimal fluctuations within a PKR 2 limit where it 
fluctuated and eventually ended every week in 2024, including this week one, where it wound up 
35 basis points weaker, at PKR 278.32. Even the lack of domestic trade in ship’s steel has left 
Pakistani plate prices stranded at the (comparatively) healthiest level of USD 639.37/Ton in the 
ship recycling industry. 
Even if offers / indications seem to be improving ever so slowly in Pakistan (at a snail’s pace and 
fraught with confusion / nerves), Gadani ship recyclers stand to face another herculean challenge
post-June 2025 once the HKC comes into effect, unless they initiate upgrading their yards in time.
Given the ongoing reluctancy on part of Gadani recyclers to expend finances and efforts on their 
yards, which themselves have hardly seen any ships being delivered over the last couple of years 
and as a result, (likely) see these upgrades as an unwarranted (at this time) and unworthy of 
investment / upgrades.  
Nevertheless, if buyers in Pakistan can display a touch more courage, confidence, and most 
importantly, stability at the time of taking deliveries of incoming units, perhaps only then can they
secure some of the market / private tonnage before the HKC cutoff mid-year and continue to do so 
thereafter, helping the nation stay relevant in the ship recycling game in the years ahead. Adding 
hope for the future was this week’s additional announcement that the United Arab Emirates (UAE) 
would rollover a parked USD 2 Billion, further assisting the Pakistani government maintain their 
level of U.S. Dollar reserves and providing a silver-lining to the otherwise ailing economy. Q1 
looks like it is gearing up to be a tricky January for the country, just like its Indian sub-continent 
counterparts.

JOLLY! 
As week 2 of 2025 wraps up in Turkey, they seem to be jolly, celebrating an over-extended Christmas
break with virtually nothing to report on local offerings, local deliveries, movements in local and
import steel prices, except for the Turkish Lira, which seems determined to head on its pilgrimage
of obscurity, as it records another 23 basis point reduction that saw it close the week scratching TRY 
34.45 against the U.S. Dollar.  
With freight rates set to climb again, the Dollar continually devaluing, and no change in vessel prices
to offset the ongoing declines clearly indicates the “on holiday” mode of Aliaga Buyers given that
there have been no local fixtures of late, in order to button down where levels truly stand today.
