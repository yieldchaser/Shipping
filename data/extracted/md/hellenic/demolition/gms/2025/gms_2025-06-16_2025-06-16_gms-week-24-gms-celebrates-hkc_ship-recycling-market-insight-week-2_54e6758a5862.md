GMS is pleased to announce a special three-part webinar series titled "HKC Compliance: What the 
Maritime Industry Needs to Know," being hosted on June 25th & June 26th, 2025. This event marks 
the entry of the Hong Kong International Convention for the Safe and Environmentally Sound 
Recycling of Ships (HKC), which officially enters into force on June 26, 2025. Implemented by
the International Maritime Organization (IMO) in 2009, the Hong Kong Convention (HKC) 
establishes binding global standards for the safe and environmentally sound recycling of ships. The 
Convention enters into force after 16 years, following ratification by nations representing over 40%
of the world's merchant fleets. It introduces legally binding requirements for shipowners, recycling 
facilities, and flag states worldwide. Participation as always, is free with advance registration. One 
registration gives you access to all three sessions. Register your spot via the following the link at
https://us06web.zoom.us/webinar/register/3217498297589/WN_kLd2bjyvRxuaO3ZnZ1o3jg 
 
In other worldwide news, the unending volatility that the world is barely getting used to through 
2025, skyrocketed this week not only did Israel strike Iranian nuclear sites, arms facilities, and oil 
depots across the country, but Trump also revived the tariff wars once again asking for all nations 
to present renewed offers and barter fresh trade negotiations that saw economic volatility return 
with a vengeance this week. To start with, as Israel struck several sites across Iran over 50 straight 
hours including major oil refineries, oil futures surged by over 7.2% in a matter of a couple of days
that saw oil futures settle at USD 73 / barrel, as further waves of attacks near the Gulf of Hormuz 
spark fears of oil export disruptions. Freight rates meanwhile tailed along with an overall 3.4% jump 
reported by the Baltic Exchange Dry Index, with Capes and Panamax sectors reporting 4.7% and 
1.9% jumps respectively, reaching their highest levels in 2 months. The resulting chaos saw the U.S. 
Dollar surging against all major ship recycling nations while local steel plate prices fell sharply in 
India, Bangladesh, and even China. 
The Indian sub-continent recycling markets meanwhile have been on a downward spiral for most 
of this year and this week was no different, with offers retreating across the market rankings and 
owners firmly in the area of chasing market levels amidst an economic and global uncertainty that 
was expected to deliver an influx of recycling tonnage and is yet to be fully seen. Indeed, constant 
geopolitical drama seems to be depriving recycling markets of all types of tonnage amidst rising 
freight rates as dry bulk, tanker, and even container sectors remain artificially propped up on the 
back of a depressing cycle of wars, tariffs, tensions, and regional traumas. A growing shadow of
dark fleets is also depriving ship recyclers from their customary supply of tonnage, as sanctions and 
constantly changing regulations bite, and then force lucrative trade routes out of those unwilling to 
risk it. Meanwhile, the imminent inception of the Hong Kong Convention into force in the next 10 
days has seen strides made (in terms of upgrades to facilities) as Bangladesh looks to fall in line with 
the advancements India has made over the last several years, whilst Pakistan lags a healthy ways
behind the competition. As we enter the traditionally quieter monsoon season in the sub-continent,
a trickle of tonnage is expected to make its way to sub-continent shores for recycling – giving yards 
in both Bangladesh and especially Pakistan, time to continue and complete their upgrades before 
supply increases again. Turkey on the far end, remains quiet and missing from the bidding tables.

CERTIFYING AT PACE! 
Upgrade works continue at pace in Bangladesh as several more ship recycling yards received their 
HKC accreditations ahead of the official entry of the Convention into force in less than 2 weeks.
Sadly, the crippling restrictions on NOC issuances have had a disastrous effect on the rate of vessel
imports into the country as no fresh units have arrived Chattogram over the last 2 weeks and a 
lonely small LDT RoRo cargo vessel has been idling outside during this time. Reports of previously 
concluded deals to non-HKC approved yards that have arrived outside port limits and are still
awaiting their respective permissions to enter once yards receive their HKC accreditations and the 
business of recycling can resume as usual.  
The Bangladeshi budget has meanwhile delivered with it, a few surprises last week that included
a marginal increase in taxes on the industry that are expected to affect vessel pricing by about USD 
5/LDT moving forward, adding further pressure on local sentiments that are already seeing vessel 
prices retreating in the face of falling fundamentals through the heavy rains. Not only have local 
steel plate prices fallen by about USD 7/Ton to end the week at about USD 548/Ton, but the 
Bangladeshi Taka also fell 19 basis points to end the week at BDT 122.10 against the U.S. Dollar, 
while barely grazing BDT 122.95 late in the week. 
The country also remains soaked through ongoing Eid holidays that are expected to conclude next 
week, and local trade has come to a virtual standstill while local businesses and industries remain 
closed. Moreover, on the back of a recently concluded ‘green’ deal to an HKC yard at a discounted 
price, lower overall offers have been emanating from local recyclers across the span of the week. 
Yet, inflation keeps falling as the domestic rate fell to 9.05% through May, down from 9.17% in
April and in conjunction with the recent budget, this should hopefully see thing improve towards
the end of the monsoon season in Q3. 
In the interim, tonnage continues to be clearly redirected towards WC India shores on account of 
no open yards in Bangladesh and Chattogram recyclers already lowering their prices, which will 
leave more time for local yards to continue upgrades as much work lies ahead – including 
installation of concrete cutting surfaces, installation of hazardous waste disposal facilities, and 
finally, conducting of work safety, and equipment and training programs for yard workers and 
supervisors alike.  
Overall, despite several larger LDT sales taking place into Chattogram over recent weeks and many 
of these newly certified HKC yards are now satisfied and occupied with tonnage, we are, like
clockwork, witnessing demand and pricing take a hit as we firmly enter the traditionally quieter 
summer / monsoon months.

INDIA 
 
ALARM BELLS? 
The alarming price declines witnessed from India over the last month have hardly shown signs of 
abating or even stabilising as yet, with levels in the low USD 400s/LDT for bulkers (some reportedly 
even seeing indications in the high USD 390s/LDT) that has now placed India as the lowest of all sub-
continent markets for another week. As the country recovers from Operation Sindoor back in May, 
local steel prices have unrelentingly softened off the back of ongoing tariff dramas emanating from 
the U.S. and the recent 50% tariffs on steel exports from various countries (including China) has 
already had a knock on effect in Alang across recent weeks, as Indian recyclers remain fearful of a 
repeat of the dumping of cheaper steel into the domestic markets that has caused so much trouble for 
vessel offers over previous years (despite India’s own punitive tariffs on same).  
And while local steel plate prices fell about USD 5/Ton across the week, the overall decline has been 
about USD 30/Ton over the last months or so. The Indian Rupee too has been taking a beating once 
again as it declined 42 basis points against the U.S. Dollar and closed the week out at levels Rs. 86.14.
And although India remains the financially strongest of all of the major international ship recycling 
markets, things might even out across Q2 / early Q3 as reports of ongoing discussions with the Trump 
administration seem to be making fruitful progress and possible news of potential trade deals being 
successfully reached are making the rounds. 
However, domestically the situation could change very soon given India has been a major importer 
of Iranian oil on account of healthy relations between the two nations and given that the IDF is 
targeting oil refineries and oil installations across Iran, could see fuel and gas hikes across the nation 
causing inflation to potentially rise again despite retail inflation hitting a 6 year low at 2.82%, as the 
RBI (Reserve Bank of India) decidedly cut interest rates in an effort to fuel local businesses. 
Alang’s ship recycling sector meanwhile continues on despite being lowest placed on the market 
rankings on account of Bangladeshi recyclers completely bowing out at the bidding tables and with 
the HKC now entering into effect a mere 10 days away, most ship owners and cash buyers are 
unwilling to take a chance on a non-HKC Pakistan and this has seen Alang’s waterfront balloon with 
tonnage arrivals this week, given that nearly 55K LDT and 7 vessels - including two large LDT tankers
arrived the nation’s recycling anchorage this week. 
Overall, and compared to the regional competition, Alang is certainly best positioned at the current 
time to take in tonnage without any hiccups (HKC or otherwise), just maybe not at a price that is
matching ship owners intended expectations of “last done” as the market is clearly on the drop.

PAKISTAN
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
    
WORK ONGOING! 
As Bangladesh forges on to complete HKC upgrades at all ship recycling yards in Chattogram and
remains keen to continue buying recycling tonnage ahead of a much busier next few years ahead, 
it is now Pakistan’s time to shine with rapid upgrades of their own! Authorities in the country have 
recently taken encouraging steps by mandating that all future recycling vessels that are arriving
Gadani, have valid IHMs on board in order to allow entry and beaching. However, far more
investment guarantees are needed at Gadani yards, in order to stay relevant and in the game in a 
post June 26th world.  
Nonetheless, given the devastating shortfall of infrastructure advancements and despite being 
placed above India on the pricing chart, only one 10K LDT vessel managed to arrive locally while 
competing India took in several units during the same time. Domestically as well, fundamentals
aren’t doing this market any favors as local steel prices remain flatlined across Eid holidays and 
remained steady at an industry leading USD 616/Ton, in addition to the Pakistani Rupee that also
declined 27 basis points against the U.S. Dollar and ended the week at PKR 282.90, inching ever 
closer to PKR 283.  
The Iran-Israel war has also affected local fuel and gas prices as local reports confirm fuel hikes 
through the week. Pakistan’s FY 2025 – 2026 too was announced this week with reports that a 20% 
increase in defense spending is being appropriated after the recent conflict with India, while a 
potential 2% tax cut on scrap imports is being reported as further details are expected next week. 
In addition to the recent IMF loan and the sudden realization of local authorities that the HKC will 
come into force in less than two weeks, it may sadly remain too little too late for Pakistani recyclers,
despite recent efforts and favorable vessel offerings, leaving them to playing catch up with the rest 
of the competition for the remainder of the year, and fall in line once HKC certifications start 
getting doled out locally. 
Overall, as forecasted last week, despite a fragile demand and stronger pricing, Gadani recyclers 
will be chasing prompt vessels and ship owners will likely continue to chase Indian levels down 
in order to recycle at an HKC yard. 
Slow Q3 seems imminent.
