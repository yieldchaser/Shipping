With week 7 of 2025 behind us, the raging fires of tariffs and counter-tariffs that continue to suppress 
recycling vessel prices via volatile fundamentals, are now creating a path for the (near) future of the 
ship recycling industry, one that seems to be headed for a Q1 and likely even Q2 of an industry that 
will likely be chasing falling prices. To start, some of the seriously older tonnage that has gotten an 
extension on their expiration dates via global events and wars that saw charter rates soar through 
the skies across 2023 and especially 2024, only subjected the ship recycling industry to one of the 
lowest volumes of tonnage offerings seen in about a decade. But with time trudging on and chances 
for further trades on these already rusty units getting slimmer at the seams with every passing week, 
the industry has as a result, seen a flurry of tonnage enter the markets since the onset of 2025, which 
was then followed by a surge in arrivals at respective waterfronts over the last couple of weeks 
including this one, with Pakistan finally breaking its mold of absence via a fresh arrival of its own. 
As the Baltic Exchange’s Dry Index reported dry bulk rates stabilizing across January 2025 and even 
start to climb across the last couple of weeks, the inflow of recycling tonnage at the bidding tables 
has continued unabated even in the face of declining offers. Even oil prices remain volatile as they 
close the week out at USD 71/barrel amidst further fears of rising fuel and energy prices and 
President Trump furthering President Bidens recent sanctions on both Russian and Chinese energy 
/ dark fleets, via a fresh set of sanctions on Iranian tankers and those trading oil with Iran. As global 
economies react to the unfolding present, the U.S. Dollar continues to dominate the currency race 
as it registers noteworthy improvements across the ship recycling board, sadly hitting India the 
hardest this week, given that India is the most viable option for units sailing East out of the Red Sea. 
This weakening in ship recycling nation currencies as well flatlining / declining steel plate prices 
have seen vessel offers continually cooling across the sub-continent markets, which remains no 
surprise given that the true nature of unfolding reality is yet to fully settle in and be felt, as levels of 
up to USD 30/LDT across the board were wiped out at a time that is a traditionally stronger period 
for global ship recycling markets ‘pre’ and now, ‘post’ Chinese New Year, as Chinese / Far Eastern 
owners are generally the most active in selling their overaged candidates including this year, where 
dry bulk / Panamax units have also been the flavor of late. While tanker rates continue to soar and 
owners globally re-positioning their units on the back of rising rates / sanctions, 2025 might be the 
year of the missing tankers despite a few wet sanctioned units making the recycling rounds. Even 
as a raft of LNG sales from the Korean market saw several respective units from this sector hit the 
beach, supply is expected to remain strong for the upcoming quarter(s) and perhaps, even the 
remainder of the year, all at a time tariffs on Chinese products - including steel – are due to take 
effect and where this excess and increasingly cheaper steel ends up, could spell disaster for the ship 
-recycling markets and vessel prices. As riots and violence re-erupts across much of Bangladesh, 
India’s budget ending up a miss, Pakistan still seeks funding, and Turkey dissolves in the West, 
post-Chinese New Year state of affairs is keeping the wheels of ship recycling turning, albeit at 
expectedly lower levels and a persistent demand from all markets.

The political situation in Bangladesh is starting to deteriorate once again as currently exiled PM
Sheikh Hasina delivered a fiery speech from oversees, calling on all her supporters to re-unite the
party her father (the founder of the nation) founded decades ago. This led to a massive unfolding 
of local riots that then saw several residences and government properties associated with her party,
torched in the process as protests unfolded across the nation. This instability is further adding to
an already deteriorating situation that the country’s economy is currently afflicted by i.e. minimal 
progress on construction projects, minimal steel movements being reported, very few breakers able
or even displaying interest at the bidding tables, and a spate of deliveries to keep those ready and
capable buyers, busy with tonnage and away from offering in the foreseeable future. 
Speaking of tonnage, Chattogram’s anchorage remains an impressive sigth with 7 ships and nearly
82K LDT either lying in wait at the waterfront or delivered to their respective owners through the
course of the week. And while this will certainly help maintain operations and regular business
activity at domestic ship recycling yards, including the resale of recycled ship’s steel to domestic
steel mills, the level of offerings remain constricted on the back of stranded local steel plate prices 
that have refused to move from USD 529.41/Ton for about a quarter now. Further suppressing the
ability of local offerings to move in a positive direction is the Bangladeshi Taka, which has been 
hemorrhaging in value since June 2024 including this week, where it ended nearly 0.5% lower than
last and at BDT 121.33 against the U.S. Dollar.  
Yet, in the growing face of vessels (from Chinese and Far Eastern owners) gradually steaming off
charter, SNP activity with local recyclers continues at pace despite ship owners and cash buyers 
progressively facing ever decreasing levels, and the hottest and most capable local recyclers book
their yards with tonnage leaving the next group of available buyers, positioned at notably lower
levels. Approximately TEN Panamax Bulk Carriers have so far been committed into the local
market since the start of the year and one would expect deals to continue taking place on these
vintage 90s built bulk carriers that have been and continue to ease off charter and as time only
progresses. Chattogram choppers have also been keen to take on some of the larger units in play
i.e. LNGs, VLCCs, FSUs, and surprisingly even a private Capesize bulker, despite rates in this
sector starting to firm up once again.  
Overall, as Bangladeshi pricing lies just ahead of Indian levels by a whisker and is, as a result, busy
taking in much of the Far Eastern tonnage, India and (even) Pakistan have started to take deliveries
of their own as an even keel on pricing squarely distributes geographical tonnage across the sub-
continent markets. However, with the HKC coming into effect by the middle of the year and the 
Ministry of Affairs granting an extension on deadline for domestic yards to initiate yard upgrades
to March 31st, an act that has merely extended the inevitable for Chattogram choppers, Q3 will be
the determinant factor as to where tonnage will eventually end up.

FIRMLY ROOTED! 
Despite being the only ship recycling destination to consistently face the wrath of unending volatility
in domestic fundamentals (including this week), Alang recyclers have managed to stay towards to 
the top of the marking rankings since 2024 and even managed to conclude their fair share of tonnage 
introduced into the markets since the start of the year, despite their firmly rooted offers in the mid 
USD 400s/LDT that continue to face immense downward pressures and are, as a result, displaying an 
ongoing reluctance to improve.  
Speaking of fundamentals, not only have local steel plate prices been the only ones to consistently 
register declines whilst some competing neighbors have remained paralyzed by flatlines for months
on end, but like the Bangladeshi Taka, the Indian Rupee has also seen its share of declines over the 
last couple of quarters as it registered the largest ship recycling destination currency decline of the 
week at nearly 1.6% against the U.S. Dollar, ending it at a historical record of Rs. 87.71. 
With the exchange rate now starting to bite down harder, salted in with a less than appreciable FY
2025 - 2026 budget (for Alang’s ship recycling sector), any form of domestic recovery in the domestic 
economy, the Rupee, or even local steel plate prices seems destined for delays, especially now that 
pending Trump Tariffs on Chinese products and the possibility of even cheaper Chinese steel coming 
into the markets in the near future, stands in the way of making any serious recovery in prices 
through Q1 and even Q2, an improbability.  
As reported in last week’s report, ship owners and overly optimistic cash buyers only stand to face 
the wrath of ongoing market movements as unfolding affairs are doing little to achieve breakeven 
figures on some of the prior expensive fixtures. Yet, fixtures have continued as Alang recyclers have 
managed to squirrel away another 6 ships and nearly 75K LDT, a healthy number at Alang given that 
India’s ship recycling waterfront nearly faced the indignity of an empty anchorage at least twice over 
the last two Quarters. Of note, included in this week’s arrivals was even a rare passenger vessel, the 
likes of which have primarily been a Turkish flavor since 2023. 
From the mixed number of units, several OFAC listed / sanctioned larger LDT tankers (VLCC and 
Suezmax size) have arrived and even delivered in Alang recently – calling into question activities of 
the cash buyers who blatantly skirt international regulations, the actions of which only stand to affect 
the ability of Indian authorities to permit allowing any questionable units from even arriving locally
in the future.

MEANING BUSINESS! 
On the eve of the 5th month potentially highlighting an empty port report, the Gadani Gang seems
to be leaping back into action over recent weeks via greater inquiries at the bidding tables and have 
as a result, even managed to secure several smaller LDT vessels and (finally) show that they mean
business, especially after an extended period on the sidelines watching competing India rake in all
of the recycling excess that has been coming into the market of late. Yet, unlike India, where levels
seem to have more of a downward hint than an even one, Gadani offerings have remained
entrenched in third place since Q4 2024, further compelling this market down the path of Turkey’s
abyssal trench. 
Like the rest of the industry, domestic fundamentals remain the prime movers behind the lethargic
performance of Pakistan’s ship recycling sector. Local steel plate prices, though strongest in the
sub-continent, have remained horizontal for the longest at USD 643.78 / Ton, being suppressed by
the influx of cheaper Chinese steel that has devastated ship recycling activity at all domestic yards,
and this only stands to potentially get worse as the effects from recently imposed tariffs on China
are yet to come through.  
Making matters worse for Pakistan’s economy are the numerous financial loans and international 
aid hanging in the balance and are still to come through, including that from the IMF who are still
investigating misappropriation of hundreds of millions of dollars and even send an investigate
IMF mission to Pakistan to assess the feasibility of governance, corporate affairs, and the ongoing
corruption in the country. Moreover, with the Pakistani army now taking an active interest in the 
nation’s economy, the shift in the balance of powers and government further stands to deteriorate 
(if history has been any example). Finally, pending / major infrastructure upgrades at all domestic
ship recycling yards needs to be initiated promptly in Gadani and bring ship recycling facilities up
to HKC standards by July 1st of this year, or gay of the way of the Dodo.  
As such, Pakistani buyers, though eager for tonnage and increasingly on the bidding tables of late,
are lagging severely behind the rest of the industry in both current infrastructure capabilities and
HKC standards at their respective yards, as some serious catching up needs to be done including
that on price levels so that Pakistan can remain a relevant destination heading into 2026.
