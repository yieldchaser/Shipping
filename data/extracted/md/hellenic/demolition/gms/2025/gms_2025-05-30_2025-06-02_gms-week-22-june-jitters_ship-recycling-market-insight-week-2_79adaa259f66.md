### Editorial: JUNE JITTERS!

On the receiving end of the mental economic milkshake is the sub-continent ship recycling sector, which, in addition to the above, has also seen subjected to unpredictable tonnage availability across much of 2025, along with a fresh batch of monsoon rains that ingloriously kicked-off across the sub- continent markets this week. And as has been case for sub-continent recycling markets, the rains will deliver the conventional decline in demand and pricing, regardless of availability of tonnage at the bidding tables over the next quarter or so. Currencies too continue to take a beating as nearly all of the major ship recycling destinations reported declines against the U.S. Dollar, except Pakistan, which also (surprisingly) reported more arrivals this week, than neighboring India has over the last 2. Indian recyclers have clearly endured another difficult week following the fragile cessation of an armed conflict with Pakistan, as Indian offers on tonnage have notably retreated by over 5% since the peaks seen of early May.

## 3. Country Market Intelligence

### Bangladesh

- **Headline**: GETTING TIGHT!
- **Key Notes**: *Upgrades continue.* | *Yards filled.* | *LNGs sold?* | *Flavors of June.*

Despite upgrade works continuing at pace in Bangladesh in an effort to get all local ship recycling yards approved to HKC standards prior to the June 26th deadline when the convention officially enters into force, realistically it seems as though only an additional 2 or 3 yards will be approved in the next few weeks, with the rest then playing catch up and unable to buy / import any vessels until their facilities are developed to the required standards and approved thereafter. Presently, of the nine domestic yards with HKC approvals, several have opted to remain inactive and are not negotiating vessels and a majority of the rest are filling up rapidly with leftovers from cash buyers’ unsold inventories that were delivered across recent weeks. It is therefore expected that the next batch of sales will likely be diverted to competing markets, due to the increasingly limited capacity of open HKC buyers in Chattogram. This has also seen prices start to cool as a satiated market diverts its attention towards recycling its recent deliveries in line with the HKCs requirements.

Local fundamentals meanwhile continue to demonstrate increasing volatility, especially after the interim government’s move to let market forces dictate the value of the Taka as required by the terms of the current IMF loan agreement, one that has seen the currency collapse from region BDT 106 against the U.S. Dollar from about a year ago, down to a massive BDT 122.16 on the back of a 33-basis point drop this week alone. Local steel plate prices also have been restrictively supportive of ongoing offers from local buyers as after Pakistan, Bangladeshi recyclers have the best margins in the industry with steel retreating a mere USD 1/Ton down to close at USD 555/Ton this week. This has certainly helped this market set the bar for the impressive volume of ongoing weekly deliveries into Chattogram (save for a few close calls) as HKC approved buyers welcomed at least 2 more units into port limits this week, more than both its competing neighbors combined.

Moreover, with four LNG vessels that were recently sold for recycling from the Korean market, which included two Moss and two Membrane types, the richer in non-ferrous Moss types usually end up in Alang given India’s propensity to beat recycling industry average offers on rich / non- ferrous loaded units, and the comparatively less rich might even see a diversion to a local HKC yard. The remaining yards will have a way to step away from the bidding tables until they get their infrastructure in place and audits concluded, meaning many will not be fully ready by June 26th

when the Convention finally enters into force.

Domestically, the political situation continues to present challenges in the face of upcoming budget and even elections, given that boycotts and strikes by government employees at the Secretariat Office in Dhaka is presenting its own challenges and ensuing delays in getting any pending matters resolved in timely manner will contribute to delays in grants and approvals on incoming vessels and yards upgrades. The rains have meanwhile soaked everything down for everyone to take a breather as domestic movements too have slowed, an expected return to life in the wet lane.

June will undoubtedly have its share of excitement for this market, just depends on if it’s your flavor.

### India

- **Headline**: PRECARIOUS LEVELS!
- **Key Notes**: *5% down.* | *Diversions?* | *Perhaps busier.* | *Funny how that is.*

Disastrously declining Indian steel plate prices and a currency that is backtracking its recent progress has seen the Indian market fall to precarious levels over the last month. While the U.S. Dollar made a 55-basis point gain against the Indian Rupee and ended the week at Rs. 85.57, it was Indian steel plate prices that the made the most stunning of manoeuvres as they sky dived USD 12/Ton, down to a lowly USD 441/Ton and left local recyclers increasingly likely to take a hit on incoming deliveries - especially on the large LDT Stainless Steel units that are heading to Alang and generally take longer to recycle, which will spell trouble in the face of declining fundamentals, incoming steel tariffs, and a perennially nervy sentiment that will, like Bangladesh, present a muddy milkshake of its own flavour through the rains.

Alang offerings have meanwhile registered a downward correction of about 5% through May, on the back of the incredible and ongoing volatility that remains ready 24/7 and a certain degree of panic begins to increasingly permeate Alang buyers in the face of a growing possibility that an increasing amount of tonnage would be forcibly diverted to Indian shores, given the overall lack of any HKC infrastructure upgrades in Pakistan so far, along with the fact that many Bangladeshi yards are still undergoing upgrades and those that were recently upgraded aren’t negotiating fresh units for the time being. Making matters worse is that June 26th stands a mere 4 weeks away and with tariff uncertainty still ahead of the Supreme Court of the United States, it could be a messy end of Q2 and perhaps largely Q3.

Although Alang recyclers like their Bangladeshi counterparts, have managed to take in tonnage that is not only geographically better suited for an Alang re-delivery, but even specialist (non-ferrous rich / SS units) and HKC units designated for an India only resale, a majority of these have gone missing of late and resulted in tonnage disappearing from Alang shores over recent weeks as not a single new vessel has arrived Alang’s OPL over the last 2 weeks, and the only vessels currently idling outside have done so over the same time frame. Notwithstanding, there remains a lingering expectation that at least two of the recently sold LNGs (MOSS types in particular), will likely head to Alang and alleviate the ongoing state of recycling affairs.

On the broader economic front, India and the U.S. continue to duke out the tariff debates with the WTO and it seems as though things might end on a positive note for the Indian administration after all, given that the country is now the 4th largest economy in the world - even surpassing Japan. This certainly presents a negotiating edge for the Indian economy across recent and troubling times and has helped keep the nation’s economic trajectory on the right path, with an inflation rate of about 3.15% and is nearly to that of pre-pandemic times. Coupled with the lack of any HKC upgrade needs and despite facing immense pressure on offers at the bidding tables, India might ironically become the go to market for June. Funny how that is.

### Pakistan

- **Headline**: RUDIMENTARY!
- **Key Notes**: *Upgrades needed.* | *Renewed aggression.* | *Too late?* | *Silent end.*

Rudimentary and basic upgrade work has reportedly commenced at a few ship recycling yards in Gadani who realize that their time may well be on the clock without the relevant investment and approvals, as the HKC inches ever closer to enforcement on June 26th. But at the current rate of progress, things aren’t looking good for this market despite a recently renewed interest and aggression to buy, which has seen Pakistani buyers reportedly secure a few geographically suitable, small(er) LDT units of late, the arrival of one of which broke Pakistan’s dry anchorage spell that lasted over 4 weeks, even beating India out for the number of arrivals this week.

Yet, the overall state of Gadani yards reports a bleaker overall picture for the nation’s critical sector and local buyers certainly have more than some ways to go before they get even starting to initiate infrastructure upgrades, let alone get anywhere near filling that many empty yards that have been empty for a majority of time over the last 2 quarters.

Pakistan’s economic fortunes, meanwhile, have certainly turned onto a better path with the recent release of IMF’s USD 1.3 billion bailout package that saw Pakistani fundamentals conjointly registering their individual shares of improvements across the week. While the Pakistan Rupee strengthened 26 basis points against the U.S. Dollar and ended the week (below PKR 282 again) at PKR 281.77, local steel plate prices themselves registered a modest USD 4/Ton jump as the week closed out, further keeping local sentiments balanced.

Pakistan’s upcoming budget is scheduled on or around June 10th and is expected to address items in line with the IMF that further require the Pakistani government to focus on domestic anti- terrorism activities. This tranche from the IMF along with a sensible budget should help local recyclers speed up development works so as to stay relevant in a post June 26th world, as all the financial stability in the world would amount to nothing if no owners or cash buyers are willing / able to diver their tonnage towards non-HKC yards along Gadani’s shoreline.

Overall, while the chances of securing any (if at all) tonnage until June 26th remains minimal, one thing is for certain. In the face of rising freight rates, prospective recycling tonnage disappearing from the bidding tables once again, and most units that will come for sale will likely be an HKC only unit, feels like we might bid adieu to Gadani for the time being.

A sad silent end to Gadani’s 2025 performance seems imminent.
