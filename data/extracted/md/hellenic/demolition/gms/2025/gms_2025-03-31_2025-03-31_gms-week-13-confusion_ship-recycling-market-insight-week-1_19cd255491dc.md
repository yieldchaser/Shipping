“If you know you are going to fail, then fail gloriously.”
 
 
– Cate Blanchett 
As highlighted in last week’s edition of the GMS Weekly, the two VLCCs that have fallen afoul of 
USDN rules and become victims of OFAC sanctions remain stranded outside Bangladeshi waters
with no prospects of a final resting place and this is now seeing cash buyers and ship recyclers being
vigilant in their appropriate ‘due diligence’ prior to negotiating on units, especially those deemed 
suspect as there reportedly are still a few more units in cash buyer hands that are waiting to be 
introduced for a recycling resale. However, given the fate that the behemoth duo has run into
outside Bangladesh, it remains to be seen how these pending and even future dealings with
candidates from sanctioned source(s) will eventually transpire.  
For now, like 2024, the 2025 tsunami of economic tumult continues to surprise week-after-week with 
macro fundamentals taking turns to shake up ever-changing sentiments across the board. Oil has 
started to jitter like the stock markets as despite registering declines through the week (and being 
low overall), it ended at a ‘higher than last week’ level of USD 69.40/barrel, as oil markets remain
cautious on the back of recently announced Iranian and Venezuelan sanctions. On the flip side (and 
rather surprisingly), charter rates declined this week across the dry bulk board as nearly all sectors 
(Handies, Panamaxes, Capes, and even Supras) registered falls, dragging the overall Baltic Dry Sea 
Freight Index down this week, which was followed by a minor uptick in recycling enquiries from 
sellers at the bidding tables. And while some of the discussions are still ongoing, local anchorages
delivered a Margarita Mix of surprises as Pakistan took in more vessels than India this week, despite 
a clear overall decline in the number of arrivals & deliveries across the sub-continent board.  
Zooming into domestic fundamentals of ship recycling markets, steel plate prices played opposite 
roles in Pakistan and India as while Indian levels recorded an impressive jump, Pakistani levels 
continue to bleed out as they have declined non-stop over the last month now, all while Bangladeshi 
and Chinese levels continue to flatline at the bottom of the pool. Even the U.S. Dollar remained
undecided against these currencies as it made firm improvements against some, while losing 
ground against others. Bangladesh seems surprisingly bullish despite ongoing Eid holidays and the 
end of the Holy Month of Ramadan, likely on the back of a few hungry recyclers, whilst competing 
India and Pakistan struggled to keep up even though there remains a distinct lack in the availability 
of units for a recycling sale as we end lucky number Week 13 and head into Q2 of the year. Prices 
in Bangladesh are slightly higher than USD 450/LT LDT on certain select / favored units (such as 
tankers and containers) coming in from the Far East and this has left India & Pakistan gravitating 
towards even smaller geographically positioned units to satisfy their own permeating demand.  
Finally, with the HKC June 26th deadline approaching closer and even the deadline within which to 
begin yard upgrades in Bangladesh by March 31st has not been extended as yet, a healthy number
of domestic recyclers will be unable to import vessels post-Eid. Pakistan, meanwhile, is at the very 
early stages of starting upgrades and needs to do much more work in the months ahead in order to 
stay relevant, whilst Turkey surfs away into the distance on the back of a receding Lira. Q2? Phew!

PRICE HYPE 
In local news, one of the OFAC listed VLCCs currently stuck outside Bangladesh has reportedly
made moves to imminently depart Bangladeshi waters for a potential India re-delivery, whilst the 
other remains stranded outside without a local resale / MoA and subsequently without an NOC 
(No Objection Certificate) that would otherwise have permitted its entry into local waters. And
with little hopes for a local resale at present, given the strong opposition from domestic authorities 
and banks on beaching prohibited / OFAC listed / sanctioned tonnage, her, and the unfortunate 
future of other sanctioned ladies like her, remains an unfolding mystery.  
Meanwhile, a renewed shortage of viable recycling units for sale in addition to the looming March 
31st deadline for local buyers to have initiated yard upgrades whereafter, they will not be permitted 
to import vessels for recycling upon resumption of normal business hours post-Eid holidays, any
units that are currently on offer and are locally favored are seeing premiums above market levels
from those recyclers looking to quickly book a unit prior to the deadline, thereby artificially raising 
the ship-recycling price floor out of Bangladesh this week. And as any potential extension on the
deadline to initiate yard upgrades is unlikely to come through at this time, local ship recycling 
facilities who have not already done so, will urgently need to initiate yard upgrades or fail to align
with HKC standards before its entry into force on June 26th of this year, risking falling out of the 
recycling scene entirely thereafter. This failure to extend is also delivering a lot of confusion in the 
local market as to the fate of vessels concluded to those recyclers who have not commenced yard 
upgrades and are still enroute, due to be delivered post-Eid opening. What happens to these 
deliveries remains to be seen. 
And as indications for prompt delivery units that are of local interest seem to have rendered the
illusion of a firming market, the reality of Bangladesh’s economy and even local fundamentals has 
failed to provide the much-needed industry-wide confidence that the interim administration has 
yet to deliver for nearly a year now. Moreover, even though Eid holidays have calmed the local 
rioting, they have also seen no movement of local steel from local recycling yards into domestic 
steel mills, resulting in an expected flatlining in plate prices that have remained in cold layup at 
about USD 529/Ton for nearly all year, and still managing to keep this market atop the market 
rankings for over two quarters. Even the Bangladeshi Taka reported marginal movements as it 
firmed a mere 10 basis points against the U.S. Dollar, ending the week at a still high BDT 121.17.  
Finally, the recent earthquake that devastated neighboring Myanmar has set off panic amongst the 
populous that far-reaching aftershocks may reverberate through the nation in the near future and
while this could create a major disruption in services, Chattogram Choppers seem like they more 
than have their hands full upon their return battling potential earthly jitters and administrative 
haltings on incoming vessels, all while Gadani’s anchorage remains the busiest of sub-continent 
destinations at the end of Q1 2025 and HKC upgrades are yet to commence. 
Earthly jitters.

INDIA 
 
DAMPENING! 
Of late, it has been a slower period for Alang recyclers on account of India’s weakening offerings and 
Pakistan’s inadvertent passing India into 2nd place on the marking rankings, in addition to minimal 
sales (apart from the rare few HKC only green vessels for India only redelivery and specialist 
nonferrous and offshore units), and finally, softening / dampening fundamentals that have decimated 
local sentiments over the last 6 – 8 weeks.  
Fortunately, this week, as both Bangladeshi and Pakistani markets were busy celebrating the end of 
the month of Ramadan and Pakistani fundamentals were being hammered, Indian plate prices 
jumped a massive USD 9/Ton this week and are on their way to USD 455/Ton, mercifully (yet barely) 
above where local recyclers are comfortable that local offerings are just about aligning with domestic 
fundamentals. Even the Indian Rupee that only recently saw the better part of Rs. 87 against the U.S. 
Dollar (nearly breaching Rs. 88), has finally been firming once again as it ended the week 55 basis 
points firmer at Rs. 85.53. 
This recent dampening of Indian sentiments that saw Pakistan slide up into 2nd place also saw India 
drop into 3rd place on the local port position via the number and total LDT at Alang anchorage this 
week, as a mere 2 ships and 6K LDT are being / have been delivered locally. And while Pakistan has 
managed to maintain the 2nd spot in the market rankings, it has an aggressive Bangladesh that has 
been capturing most of the headlines and most of the (geographically viable) market fixtures at some 
top numbers over the preceding few weeks, all while India and Pakistan start to lag and clearly have 
some catching up to do, especially in this most recent wake of tonnage delinquency.  
Moreover, being the 2nd largest Asian Economy, the effects of Trump’s tariffs are also expected to 
affect India the most given his recent blanket announcement of 25% tariffs including fresh ones on all
imported vehicles and thereby, vehicle parts coming into the United States, of which, India is a 
supplier of, including being a major pharmaceuticals exporter to the States, which will certainly see 
India’s economy take a hit through 2025. Oil sanctions too are making a situation for a heavily oil 
dependant nation such as India, that much more watchful of the U.S. government’s upcoming moves
given that the nation’s economy is traversing through recently buoyant times.  
Notwithstanding, the positive news on the horizon is that the Indian government continues to make 
efforts at ensuring that sector specific exports from specific countries (including India) are tariffed in 
a more structured way so as to ensure smooth ongoing trade with the U.S., thereby keeping currencies
stable. Moreover, even though major tax breaks for low – median income individuals and families is 
already seeing an uptick in India’s consumer spending index, the much-needed and far delayed 
infrastructure projects that would greatly help local steel plate prices and in effect, the world’s largest 
ship recycling destination i.e. Alang, become a booming place (from a purchasing standpoint) once 
again, remains out of the government’s collective minds at the moment. 
Infra projects.

PAKISTAN
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
    
TINY GAINS! 
Despite attempts to get back into the buying at competitive levels in recent times, Pakistan has yet 
to really transact on a significant portion of any of the market tonnage that has come available since
the start of the year. Other than a handful of small LDT / geographically better suited (for a Pakistan 
re-delivery) units that have been concluded to local buyers with largely dormant plots, a rampant 
Bangladeshi market remains significantly ahead of a seemingly improving Gadani.  
Moreover, Pakistani offerings remain marginally ahead of those from neighboring India where 
Indian fundamentals marked noteworthy improvements this week, all while local fundamentals 
continue sinking in a sticky mess that seems to be dragging them down by the week as not only 
did local steel plate prices fall about USD 4/Ton  and end the week at about USD 624 / Ton (a drop 
that has totaled nearly USD 40/Ton over the last 4 weeks), but even the Pakistani Rupee firmed a 
mere 5 basis points against the U.S. Dollar as it still ended the week at PKR 280.04, certainly in 
record-high territory for the currency.  
How this will affect Gadani’s purchasing mindset at the bidding tables post-Eid remains to be seen. 
For now, the Gadani Gang has managed to takeover the ice cream truck as over the last couple of 
weeks, the industry has seen a vessel arrive locally every week including this one, where a couple
of units welcomed the waterfront. Of these, one is a really tiny unit that was likely trading regional 
waters. Nonetheless, Gadani’s anchorage now has more units and LDT than neighboring Alang,
something the industry has not witnessed in a very long time, and this should keep respective 
Gadani recyclers engaged through a time the number of local units delivered into Pakistan can be 
counted on one hand – and that too through the span of an entire quarter.  
Gadani recyclers have yet to also make any moves towards upgrading the respective recycling
infrastructure and ship recycling practices at their yards to HKC standards, in addition to receiving 
their SOC (Statement of Compliance) Certifications’ from recognized / IACS societies, something
that nearly all Alang ship recyclers are fully prepared for and Chattogram Choppers are slowly 
getting their inertia moving on from static now that those who have yet to initiate upgrades of their 
own will fail to receive NoCs from April, which will certainly push their pedal further in. What 
seems certain is that a few (if not a majority) of Pakistani ship recycling yards will face recycling 
restrictions from the authorities post June 26th, unless by some miracle, speedy action by the Gadani 
Gang is initiated to get their respective yards ready just in time. 
For now, however, as we stand on the precipice of Q2 2025, Pakistani ship recyclers can at least be 
buoyed by the fact that they are ahead of India in the marking rankings, especially after a miserable 
showing from Alang over previous weeks (on account of their declining fundamentals), and the 
Gadani recyclers would certainly take these tiny gains after an altogether forgettable last few years 
spent largely on the sidelines of the ship recycling industry.

INCOGNITO! 
In the face of a rapidly declining Turkish Lira that has now recorded the largest decline amongst 
ship-recycling nations across Week 13 of Q1 2025, plummeting over 1.15% to TRY 37.99 against the 
U.S. Dollar, Aliaga recyclers were left in shock and retreated back to Eid celebrations as the sentiment
post-Eid is certainly going to be one of sheer alarm, especially as the Lira is a breath away from TRY 
38 and probably on the verge of jumping past TRY 40 within the next month. 
And whilst on holiday, even though there has been no trading of local steel plate prices, rumors of a 
collapse are circulating and only Week 14 / 15 will reveal just how the local market is reacting to the 
collapsing Lira. Turkey’s inflation, though improving, also remains precarious at nearly 40% (down 
from the highs of 80% only 3 years ago) and has seen a near collapse in vessel purchases from Aliaga
over the same time. Notwithstanding, a smaller LDT sale is rumored to have taken place into Aliaga 
this week and will only be confirmed next week. 
Aliaga has certainly gone incognito…and limping in the dark again.
