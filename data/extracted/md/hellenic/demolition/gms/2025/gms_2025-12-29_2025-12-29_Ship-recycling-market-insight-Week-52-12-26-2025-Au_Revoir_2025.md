AU REVOIR, 2025! 
A turbulent 2025 — not only for the ship recycling industry, but for global economies churning 
under the weight of tariffs, tantrums, and tumultuous international situations — finally comes to a 
close, there were certainly some positive highlights to note as remarkable progress on HKC 
infrastructure upgrades has been carried out across the entire Indian sub-continent ship recycling 
industry right before the year-end — a leaving a positive mark for the ship recycling industry and 
certainly a welcome sign for 2026. 
Yet, the glaring fact remains that prices are far poorer than the USD 600/ton highs of January 2024, 
driven by consistently declining steel plate prices across the Indian sub-continent markets, coupled 
with incredible volatility in global currencies/foreign exchange trading, never-ending political 
events, and a touch of monsoon rainstorms that even brought the weather into the mix. As such, 
markets continue to shift and slide, while the ongoing scarcity in vessel supply remains ever 
consistent. It has also been a quiet week at the bidding tables (Christmas mood notwithstanding) 
and another largely uneventful — and eerily quiet — year on the recycling waterfront, punctuated 
occasionally by the odd large LDT LNG and older bulker. 
But that we hope will change going into 2026, as the Baltic Exchange continued reporting downward 
trajectory for the dry sectors, falling another 0.8% this week to its lowest levels since October. The 
decline was driven by an overall retreat in capes (down 0.5%) and the smaller segments that shed 
18 basis points. Oil on the other hand, managed to climb to USD 57.21/barrel — a 0.82% increase 
this week — yet it registered an overall 3.56% drop across December, and a 19.2% overall decline 
compared to the same time last year. Local steel plate prices, too, surprisingly donned new hats this 
week, shifting from rising, to falling, to flat-out lack of trading. 
As 2026 rolls around next week, and with many expecting freight markets to continue performing 
into the first half of 2026, the introduction of vessels for recycling could therefore be potentially 
delayed even further — at least until surveys and dry dockings start kicking in. Hopefully, this delay 
should create the opportunity for both Pakistan and Bangladesh to make meaningful progress on 
their HKC journeys, as more yards are expected to be approved by then and ready to take on fresh 
units in 2026, in line with the Hong Kong Convention. 
As 2026 looms and a quiet week ensues, we at GMS wish all our readers and friends, a Happy 
Christmas and a Happy, Healthy, and Prosperous New Year ahead!

SLIGHT STAIN! 
The decline in sentiment and pricing that accelerated toward the end of the year has left a slight 
stain on what has otherwise been a comparatively healthier (for what it’s worth) fourth quarter. 
Prices have indeed cooled off noticeably from the Bangladeshi corner of the bidding table, and as 
many of the open and capable yards took in a good bit of tonnage during November’s buffet storm,
a shortage of “locally available” reselling options for shipowners and cash buyers looking to fix 
tonnage has come under severe strain this month. 
Notwithstanding, throughout the year, we have seen a collection of larger LDT vessels sold into 
Bangladesh, including LNGs, mini capes, a collection of large(r) tankers, and several comparatively 
cheaper green units for recycling. As this has significantly affected December sentiments around 
how conventional vessels should be priced, it seems the net effect will now be to drag down local 
offerings even further once the market is eventually back in play. 
What is dragging down the local ship recycling fraternity are domestic fundamentals, which 
continue to display unexpected movements week after week. This week, the Bangladeshi taka 
managed to firm a mere five basis points against the U.S. Dollar and close at BDT 122.22. But it was 
local steel plate prices that won the fancy-dress competition as, reportedly, there was a dead halt 
in local trading, resulting in levels being truly flat on the floor. 
Another surprise was local anchorage, which reported a healthy number of arrivals / deliveries, 
marking the end of the final beaching tide of the year and the closing of books for some buyers. It 
also clearly highlighted improving performance and speed of inward-clearance formalities at 
Chattogram anchorage, as some units arrived and were even delivered within days. 
Yet, with the nearing of Election Day on February 14, 2026, political violence continues to mar the 
streets of the nation’s capital, as growing instability is being reported across key political 
jurisdictions not only in Chattogram, but reportedly across the nation. 
Overall, for 2026, Bangladesh seems more optimistically placed on the ship recycling front — and 
should the political cards fall in favor of the populace, then hopefully the wider economy as well. 
It seems that for Bangladesh, as a nation, the only way from here is up.

INDIA
UNTAPPED POTENTIAL! 
India remains planted firmly at the foot of the global ship recycling market rankings for yet another 
week/month, capping off an overall miserable year of declining rates, a frighteningly depreciating 
currency, and collapsing local steel prices that seem to have lost track of time and space. 
As competing sub-continent markets continue to catch up on the number of HKC-accredited yards in 
their respective corners, Alang recyclers seem to have missed their opportunity to use their HKC status 
to their advantage this year, despite the overall dearth of tonnage that has still managed to produce 
sporadic phases of inflow of tonnage into competing nations – including HKC units. 
But what a year it has truly been for Indian fundamentals — both local steel plate prices and the Indian 
Rupee, and especially the Rupee over the last two quarters. While local steel plate prices started the 
year around USD 462/ton and ended the final week of 2026 at a noteworthy USD 393/ton (a USD 70/ton 
drop), it was the Rupee’s rapid descent that stole the show, with levels across the year declining from 
a low of Rs. 85.30 to above Rs. 91, and clocking week 52 out at Rs. 89.80 against the U.S. Dollar. 
Yet, the nation overall remains in a better economic posture than before, as inflation rises to gradually 
scoop the country out of stagflation territory. And while economic talks with the White House are 
reportedly still ongoing, 2025 has been a strong economic year for India, with GDP expanding over 8% 
in key quarters where projections hovered around 6.5%–7%. 
Overall, there are still reasons to be hopeful going into 2026, as more vessels are expected to come for 
recycling (eventually), which may finally help Alang plots begin to fill their quotas once again. 
Missed opportunity.

PAKISTAN
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
    
STARVED PRIDE! 
As 2025 draws to a close, Pakistan can only look back with pride as the nation’s first HKC-approved 
ship recycling yard joined the international fray — not only for Pakistan, but as a first step onto 
the global stage. 
While earlier in the year, it seemed like an impossibility for Pakistan to catch up with HKC yard-
upgrade requirements within 2025 (given how far behind local progress staggered), serious 
investments and infrastructure updates have taken place in the interim to speed up the process. 
And with many more yards reportedly on the way scheduled for Q1 2026 approvals, Pakistan may 
become the one market capable of putting a serious dent into India’s HKC prowess west of 
Bangladesh. 
Adding to Gadani’s favor, and unlike its neighboring competition, are Pakistan’s fundamentals 
that have remained comparatively tame and stronger. The PKR gained four basis points to close a 
year that started at PKR 278.30 against the U.S. Dollar, at PKR 280.13. And despite a USD 80/ton 
drop, local steel plate prices remained flatlined through the week at around USD 580/ton (well 
ahead of the competition), having started the year near USD 640/ton. 
While Gadani buyers did get back to buying with a mid-year spree, thanks to firmer fundamentals 
and timing, this was not at sustained throughput as imports of cheap Iranian steel products started 
to hammer local sentiment — and local offer levels for tonnage as a result. 
Yet, like Chattogram, with inflation now declining last month, relatively stable fundamentals, and 
only additions to the number of HKC-accredited ship recycling yards, the only way for the Gadani 
Gang in 2026 is up.
