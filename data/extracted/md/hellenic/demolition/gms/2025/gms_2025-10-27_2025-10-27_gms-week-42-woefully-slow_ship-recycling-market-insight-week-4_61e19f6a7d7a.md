WOEFULLY SLOW! 
Global markets seemed to take a breather this week as things turned slow across the board once
again, not only on the shipping side but even the ship recycling side of things. Currencies fluctuated
in moderate tandem, local steel plate prices made surprisingly marginal moves, all while the Baltic
Exchange Dry Index slipped and oil steadied itself. The BDI reported a declining week that shaved
off about 3.2% by CoB Friday, dropping to its lowest levels since early October as the Cape index
took the lead shaving off over 6% of its value, while Supramaxes dipped a comparatively milder 9
points and the Panamax index remained steady for the week even though the overall index is nearly
4% down.  
As inflation rose marginally in the U.S. (climbing to 3% by the end of the September), oil managed
to gain some traction this week, firming to USD 62.14/barrel – a 1% climb on the back of anticipations
on a possible China – U.S. trade deal, all while Trump threatens Putin with further escalations as
sweeping sanctions take effect, not only on Russian oil trade (particularly with India), but even the
EU now hopping on the wagon, reportedly blacklisting hundreds of container vessels this week.  
And as the global dark / shadow fleets start to grow, the woeful performance of the Indian sub-
continent ship recycling markets continues on and there are few emerging signs of light at the end
of the tunnel (more like an oncoming freight train) for the remainder of this year. Prices have fallen
so low over recent weeks that constant discussions of prices below USD 400/LDT are comfortably
prevalent and it seems increasingly certain that any viable recovery has still not bought a ticket to 
the sub-continent recycling sector.  
India has endured the roughest times over the last several weeks with levels plummeting on the
back of rapidly deteriorating local steel prices and a drastically depreciating currency that continues
to suffer on the back of 50% tariffs amidst the U.S. – India – Russia love triangle. Pakistan has not
been too far behind the recent falls with plunging demand due to an influx of cheaper steel imports,
all while Bangladesh shows moderate signs of life with a couple of local arrivals. The occasional and
aggressive offerings on larger LDT units have been so sporadic, it has left ship owners and cash
buyers confused as to where levels on the ground really stand. And as India celebrates a quieter
time thanks to Diwali Holidays, Turkey remains seemingly absent regardless of work or play. 
Overall, mounting global trade wars and tariff implementations are leaving global corporations
confused and fearing the worst, should these reciprocal and seemingly ever escalatory measures
continue to unfold thereby stifling shipping sectors, global trade, FX markets, steel products, and 
overall cost of living. For the time being, freight rates remain relatively firm, and the suffocating
inflow of recycling tonnage has remained painfully thin, as we ease into the November 2025 and
have little to show for yet another year of record low supply.

DEPRESSINGLY SELECTIVE! 
As the Chattogram ship recycling sector continues trying to balance itself on stilts in the middle of 
an earthquake, surprisingly decent levels have been tabled from some of the hungry / premium
buyers on some of the select large(r) LDT vessels proposed into Bangladesh - capesize bulkers and
Membrane type LNG carriers including some of the recent sales (mentioned below). However, the
supply of those units has been transient traffic with smaller LDT vessels making up the bulk of the
inward recycling rotation and is now seeing almost no interest from local buyers.  
As the economic situation remains confounded with inflation rising marginally last month, 30%
tariffs on the nation’s most vital exports (Readymade Garments), the Taka losing nearly 30 basis 
points against the Dollar this week (closing it out at BDT 122.35), and a local steel plate price that
made a surprise opening at levels far higher than last week’s closing, but still declining about USD
3/Ton is certainly a sign of impending times for this market. 
All eyes are therefore glued to the upcoming elections that have been tentatively scheduled for
February 2026 and loom like a beacon of hope (or an axe swinging on a rope) as locally, the drive
to oust an ineffective interim regime that has failed to stimulate the economy via any new
infrastructure / development projects since June 2024, is being attributed as the key reason behind
the nation’s economic decline. Notwithstanding, HKC yard upgrades have at least made decent
progress with almost 20 yards now fully HKC ready, having made the relevant infrastructure 
upgrades to keep on the right side of the latest regulations.  
On the sales front, the recycling LNG clear-out continued this week as after an agonizing wait, it
has been confirmed that two 1995 built Membrane units PUTERI NILAM (26,915 LDT) and PUTERI 
DELIMA (26,915) have been committed on an enbloc basis to a buyer on private terms. One other
sale was confirmed this week as the handymax bulk carrier from Waruna MONICA P (7,779 LDT) 
was fixed for a decent USD 380/LT LDT basis an ‘as is’ Belawan delivery, earmarking in the only
sale of the week with a likely intended resale into Bangladesh. 
Overall, dealing with Bangladesh in the present times is much like playing the lottery, until the
elections seal a win.

INDIA 
 
TIERED TRIBULATIONS! 
Diwali holidays have come and gone without much respite or signs of stability for Alang and even the 
Indian market overall. It is therefore unsurprising to see any market sales being concluded to many of 
the 100+ ship recycling yards that remain empty, for yet another week. It must certainly be frustrating 
for ship recyclers in Alang to see global and even the domestic markets struggling so much, with tariffs, 
oil pressures, sanctions, blacklists, and even the formalization of the Hong Kong Convention that has 
been in force since June 26th, 2025.   
And despite all 100+ yards in India being HKC certified even before the HKC came into force, market 
driven scarcity of viable tonnage that has been stalking the ship recycling industry for years now, has
left the Alang buyers with no real tonnage to work on as prices fade from the highs of USD 600/Ton 
down to below USD 400/Ton once again, leaving them at a greater disadvantage (as compared to the 
neighbouring competition) in terms of putting their HKC accreditations to use whilst competing 
markets are still getting up to speed.  
Part of the problem has been the supply of dark / shadow vessels into India and the willingness of 
certain end buyers to acquire these units cheaply despite the international regulations and sanctions 
being in place), meaning there is now a two-tiered market that is killing prices for clean vessels being 
proposed.  
And sanctioned vessels aren’t the only price hunters while they remain under the umbrella of India’s
fundamentals that have been taking a brutal beating of late even though the Rupee gained a minor 20 
basis points against the U.S. Dollar and end the week at INR 87.54 while local steel plate prices waddled 
out of the bar at sunrise with a USD 1/Ton improvement, yet leaning against the pillars of USD 389/Ton
– a number far lower than the current value of ships. 
Notwithstanding the widow’s tales, India continues to hold on to the mantle of the safest “go to” ship 
recycling destination regardless of tonnage type or size, as long as you’re willing to take a bite of their
pricing apple.

PAKISTAN
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
    
PAYING THE PRICE! 
Just as the Gadani market had seemingly gotten back on its feet over the last couple of weeks with 
fresh tonnage incoming on the back of the highest ongoing offerings in the Indian sub-continent 
ship recycling sector with some decent LDT dry tonnage palliating the local appetite, a sudden and 
recent influx of cheap Iranian scrap steel has local offerings and prevailing sentiments nosediving 
into the abyss this week.  
Whilst not quite as low as the miserable state of the Indian market at present, Pakistani ship 
recyclers are becoming feverishly concerned by this recent undercutting of their local inventories 
and have refrained from tabling offers on any of the slim pickings of vessels that maybe making 
the rounds.  
As it stands, Gadani is still positioned just above USD 400/LDT and as is the case with their 
Bangladeshi counterparts, Pakistani ship recyclers are looking mostly for larger LDT units 
(specifically dry vessels) if they are able to get their hands on any. This change in in the local flavor 
is primarily due to the difficulty and time needed to get provisional DASR certifications from the 
relevant authorities whilst HKC yards are still ongoing and upgrades taking place, thereby
rendering small LDT units increasingly less favorable (for the effort needed to import them and 
the shorter times to recycle them). 
Fundamentals too have been chocking as not only has inflation nearly doubled across September
on the back of U.S. sanctions and a fresh new war with the Afghani Taliban, but the Pakistan Rupee 
doubled back 20 basis points this week just like their Indian counterparts and closed the week out 
at PKR 283.50. Local steel plate prices also resumed flatlining at USD 614/Ton, after falling over 
USD 15/Ton over recent weeks. 
This is only adding to local frustrations as their ability to compete is being hampered by geography 
and the opening of Bangladesh viable tonnage of late, leaving Pakistan as the market to keep an 
eye out, especially if you have a dry unit opening in the area and a Gadani buyer gets up to grab a 
bite.
