## 3. Country Market Intelligence

### Bangladesh

- **Headline**: CERTIFYING AT PACE!
- **Key Notes**: *2 weeks away.* | *Upgrades in progress.* | *Occupied.* | *Clockwork.*

Upgrade works continue at pace in Bangladesh as several more ship recycling yards received their HKC accreditations ahead of the official entry of the Convention into force in less than 2 weeks. Sadly, the crippling restrictions on NOC issuances have had a disastrous effect on the rate of vessel imports into the country as no fresh units have arrived Chattogram over the last 2 weeks and a lonely small LDT RoRo cargo vessel has been idling outside during this time. Reports of previously concluded deals to non-HKC approved yards that have arrived outside port limits and are still awaiting their respective permissions to enter once yards receive their HKC accreditations and the business of recycling can resume as usual.

The Bangladeshi budget has meanwhile delivered with it, a few surprises last week that included a marginal increase in taxes on the industry that are expected to affect vessel pricing by about USD 5/LDT moving forward, adding further pressure on local sentiments that are already seeing vessel prices retreating in the face of falling fundamentals through the heavy rains. Not only have local steel plate prices fallen by about USD 7/Ton to end the week at about USD 548/Ton, but the Bangladeshi Taka also fell 19 basis points to end the week at BDT 122.10 against the U.S. Dollar, while barely grazing BDT 122.95 late in the week.

The country also remains soaked through ongoing Eid holidays that are expected to conclude next week, and local trade has come to a virtual standstill while local businesses and industries remain closed. Moreover, on the back of a recently concluded ‘green’ deal to an HKC yard at a discounted price, lower overall offers have been emanating from local recyclers across the span of the week. Yet, inflation keeps falling as the domestic rate fell to 9.05% through May, down from 9.17% in April and in conjunction with the recent budget, this should hopefully see thing improve towards the end of the monsoon season in Q3.

In the interim, tonnage continues to be clearly redirected towards WC India shores on account of no open yards in Bangladesh and Chattogram recyclers already lowering their prices, which will leave more time for local yards to continue upgrades as much work lies ahead – including installation of concrete cutting surfaces, installation of hazardous waste disposal facilities, and finally, conducting of work safety, and equipment and training programs for yard workers and supervisors alike.

Overall, despite several larger LDT sales taking place into Chattogram over recent weeks and many of these newly certified HKC yards are now satisfied and occupied with tonnage, we are, like clockwork, witnessing demand and pricing take a hit as we firmly enter the traditionally quieter summer / monsoon months.

### India

- **Headline**: ALARM BELLS?
- **Key Notes**: *Not stabilising.* | *Cheap steel repeat?* | *Currency woes.* | *Best suited?*

The alarming price declines witnessed from India over the last month have hardly shown signs of abating or even stabilising as yet, with levels in the low USD 400s/LDT for bulkers (some reportedly even seeing indications in the high USD 390s/LDT) that has now placed India as the lowest of all sub- continent markets for another week. As the country recovers from Operation Sindoor back in May, local steel prices have unrelentingly softened off the back of ongoing tariff dramas emanating from the U.S. and the recent 50% tariffs on steel exports from various countries (including China) has already had a knock on effect in Alang across recent weeks, as Indian recyclers remain fearful of a repeat of the dumping of cheaper steel into the domestic markets that has caused so much trouble for vessel offers over previous years (despite India’s own punitive tariffs on same).

And while local steel plate prices fell about USD 5/Ton across the week, the overall decline has been about USD 30/Ton over the last months or so. The Indian Rupee too has been taking a beating once again as it declined 42 basis points against the U.S. Dollar and closed the week out at levels Rs. 86.14. And although India remains the financially strongest of all of the major international ship recycling markets, things might even out across Q2 / early Q3 as reports of ongoing discussions with the Trump administration seem to be making fruitful progress and possible news of potential trade deals being successfully reached are making the rounds.

However, domestically the situation could change very soon given India has been a major importer of Iranian oil on account of healthy relations between the two nations and given that the IDF is targeting oil refineries and oil installations across Iran, could see fuel and gas hikes across the nation causing inflation to potentially rise again despite retail inflation hitting a 6 year low at 2.82%, as the RBI (Reserve Bank of India) decidedly cut interest rates in an effort to fuel local businesses.

Alang’s ship recycling sector meanwhile continues on despite being lowest placed on the market rankings on account of Bangladeshi recyclers completely bowing out at the bidding tables and with the HKC now entering into effect a mere 10 days away, most ship owners and cash buyers are unwilling to take a chance on a non-HKC Pakistan and this has seen Alang’s waterfront balloon with tonnage arrivals this week, given that nearly 55K LDT and 7 vessels - including two large LDT tankers arrived the nation’s recycling anchorage this week.

Overall, and compared to the regional competition, Alang is certainly best positioned at the current time to take in tonnage without any hiccups (HKC or otherwise), just maybe not at a price that is matching ship owners intended expectations of “last done” as the market is clearly on the drop.

### Pakistan

- **Headline**: WORK ONGOING!
- **Key Notes**: *Forging on.* | *Authorities move.* | *Playing catch up.* | *Slow Q3*

As Bangladesh forges on to complete HKC upgrades at all ship recycling yards in Chattogram and remains keen to continue buying recycling tonnage ahead of a much busier next few years ahead, it is now Pakistan’s time to shine with rapid upgrades of their own! Authorities in the country have recently taken encouraging steps by mandating that all future recycling vessels that are arriving Gadani, have valid IHMs on board in order to allow entry and beaching. However, far more investment guarantees are needed at Gadani yards, in order to stay relevant and in the game in a post June 26th world.

Nonetheless, given the devastating shortfall of infrastructure advancements and despite being placed above India on the pricing chart, only one 10K LDT vessel managed to arrive locally while competing India took in several units during the same time. Domestically as well, fundamentals aren’t doing this market any favors as local steel prices remain flatlined across Eid holidays and remained steady at an industry leading USD 616/Ton, in addition to the Pakistani Rupee that also declined 27 basis points against the U.S. Dollar and ended the week at PKR 282.90, inching ever closer to PKR 283.

The Iran-Israel war has also affected local fuel and gas prices as local reports confirm fuel hikes through the week. Pakistan’s FY 2025 – 2026 too was announced this week with reports that a 20% increase in defense spending is being appropriated after the recent conflict with India, while a potential 2% tax cut on scrap imports is being reported as further details are expected next week. In addition to the recent IMF loan and the sudden realization of local authorities that the HKC will come into force in less than two weeks, it may sadly remain too little too late for Pakistani recyclers, despite recent efforts and favorable vessel offerings, leaving them to playing catch up with the rest of the competition for the remainder of the year, and fall in line once HKC certifications start getting doled out locally.

Overall, as forecasted last week, despite a fragile demand and stronger pricing, Gadani recyclers will be chasing prompt vessels and ship owners will likely continue to chase Indian levels down in order to recycle at an HKC yard.
