# Market Commentary

## RESTRICTED!

Despite an overall neutral-to-positive budget in Bangladesh last week, the Central Bank has curiously decided to impose restrictions on L/Cs once again, reportedly due to the ongoing shortage of foreign currency / U.S. Dollar reserves in the country, as inflation / currency depreciations continue to hammer away at a majority of the global recycling destinations. Discussions are ongoing locally in order to address / resolve this situation. For the time being, however, there has been a pause on fresh sales into Bangladesh as without vessel financing / L/Cs in place, it is simply not possible to procure any tonnage.

Indian fundamentals have also improved, having seen some positive moves on the Indian Rupee over the last few weeks, in addition to steel gains that are providing End Buyers with further encouragement and confidence to improve some of their previously lowish levels on offers. This might eventually help those garish Cash Buyers who have been offering above market levels on some of the recent units.

For now, Pakistan remains the only sub-continent destination that is totally out of the picture, and this is due to the ongoing political unrest and economic calamity that has seen its currency lose much of its value over the last few years.

At the West end, Turkey passes through a reflection of last week, with opposing fundamentals and a Lira that’s essentially fallen off the wagon.

In the overall market today, the only thing lacking is a decent supply of tonnage to keep End Buyers busy and their yards occupied, as we head into the summer / holiday season and many ship owners and yard laborers head on holidays during the warmer / monsoon months.

Finally, after a busy first quarter of recycling, a minimal number of tankers have been proposed so far this year as containers seem to have all but vanished from the market, with over 25 container units sold for varying degrees of HKC recycling. Even dry bulk supply has started to dwindle (apart from a busy Far East / China market that is still shedding older units) and so until more tonnage is introduced, we can expect all of the markets to remain sluggish.

# Bangladesh

## TURNING (TO) DRY!

*Margin notes: Decent influx. | LC concerns remain.*

A positive budget with virtually no overall impact or increased duties on the ship recycling sector has been subsequently tempered by the news out of Bangladesh this week, that fresh L/C approvals will be temporised by the Central State government bank, reportedly due to the ongoing shortage of foreign currency / U.S. Dollar reserves in the country.

Consequently, and even though there has been a moderate influx of vessels from the Far East (Chinese Owners in particular), as of now, there are no easy means for Chattogram Buyers to satisfy their demand with fresh sales – given these most recent L/C hurdles.

Of course, much of the older dry bulk tonnage has tended to end up in Chinese hands (as is the case with older container units these days) and as such, it is perhaps only logical that we are starting to see owners from this market shed vessels - especially as these units approach survey times.

Even though in the past, Chattogram Buyers have had more success in negotiating acceptable L/C / MOA levels with the Central Bank in order to import tonnage, these most recent L/C and financing concerns therefore present a new set of hurdles for open and available Recyclers as reportedly, a brewing demand for steel amidst ongoing construction projects across the country is providing enough incentive for local Recyclers to offer afresh on tonnage.

As such, all those concerned locally (and even the industry at large) will be eagerly monitoring discussions with the Central Bank in the week(s) ahead, to see if sales into Bangladesh can resume at pace once again.

# India

## APPRECIATION!

*Margin notes: Source of comfort. | Supply starved.*

An appreciation in the Indian Rupee over the past few weeks has given Alang Buyers a source of comfort, especially as all currencies from the major recycling destinations faced depreciations (some notable) against the U.S. Dollar this week.

Indian local steel plate prices also improved by the tune of about USD 13/Ton this week, recovering / regaining a portion of the losses over the previous few weeks.

Sales have been hard to come by into India of late as the container HKC sales that we had seen at the start of the year have all but dried up. Owners have continued to trade or have even sold their units on for further trading, especially as charter rates in this particular sector picks up again.

Plummeting Chinese steel plate prices over recent weeks that led to negatively positioned LME futures (as a result) have remained a lingering cause for caution in the local market.

However, as supply remains starved heading into the summer months, it may not be too much to worry about just yet.

# Pakistan

## IDLE SUMMER!

*Margin notes: Inert. | Uncertainty reigns.*

An inert summer ahead seems a certainty for the Pakistani market, that undoubtedly has other things on its mind than ship recycling. Political and economic concerns still reign supreme in the country, and until one (financial) or both of these items can be stabilized, we are unlikely to see any sales take place into a beleaguered Gadani market.

Fundamentally, the Pakistani market remains in a terrible shape, given that local steel plate prices continue to flatline for months on end and the Pakistani Rupee nosedives past PKR 286.75.

Even local anchorage is reflecting the state of current affairs as despite a couple of units having arrived Gadani’s waterfront over the last couple of months, not a single ship has actually beached in Pakistan since the end of January.

As such, even though the local focus still falls on the dwindling supply of ships, L/C concerns – the type that have beset Bangladesh for much of this year – are the key obstacle in importing and getting ships successfully beached at the end of the day.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# Turkey

## WEEKLY MIRROR!

*Margin notes: TRY 23.50!!!! | Quiet Summer?*

In alliance with the state of affairs from last week, Turkey sails through a mirror week this week, with local fundamentals travelling in opposite directions.

While import and local steel plate prices remain relatively unchanged from last week, the Lira has depreciated in truly spectacular fashion this week. Only last week was the industry were worried about the Lira breaching TRY 20 against the U.S. Dollar (and it eventually breached TRY 21) , this week, the Lira breezed past TRY 23.50, only to close out the week at a staggering TRY 23.35.

As such, and mixed in with the global shortage in the supply of meaningful tonnage, Turkey seems destined for a quieter summer – especially until their fundamentals are sorted and supply comes around.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**
