## 3. Country Market Intelligence

### Bangladesh

- **Headline**: PAUSE!
- **Key Notes**: *Yet to get back.* | *No local sales.*

Following the conclusion of Eid holidays, Bangladeshi Buyers are yet to get back to the buying as very few offers at reasonable (or even acceptable) levels have been forthcoming this week.

As such, Ship Owners and Cash Buyers are starting to turn to other markets / India, so inert is the Bangladeshi market at present - and it may be that we see this market on the sidelines over the quieter summer / monsoon months.

The previous problem surrounding Central Bank permissions is continuing to affect the financing of fresh L/Cs once again, whilst fundamentals, especially local steel plate prices have continued to flatline for some time now.

Even the Bangladeshi Taka continues to tread at record-breaking highs against the U.S. Dollar, all while local Recyclers continue to present opportunistic below-market offers on units being proposed.

As a result, infrastructure projects are mostly on hold due to the incessant rains & lack of steel from the mills, and the fact that steel mills have yet to fully reopen / resume business following Eid holidays.

Notwithstanding, on the sales front, as Chinese owners continue their clear out of older tonnage, this week, the General Cargo / Bulk Carrier vessel XIANG HE (9,016 LDT) managed to fetch a decent USD 520/LT LDT basis an ‘as is’ Singapore delivery.

---

### India

- **Headline**: NOT WORKABLE!

Indian levels continue to confound and disappoint the industry as Alang Buyers believe that offering levels below USD 500/LDT will entice Ship Owners and Cash Buyers to part with their vintage tonnage.

Unsurprisingly, few sales have materialized into Alang, and any available tonnage looks set to head to competing markets (with three yards in Bangladesh now class NK HKC certified), including a newly funded Pakistan after news of the IMF loan broke this week.

Fundamentals too remain volatile as local steel plate prices declined by about USD 13/Ton this week, while the Indian Rupee straddles around the Rs. 82s against the U.S. Dollar.

As such, if the current trends persist, we can certainly expect a quieter summer in Alang as End Buyers continue to complain about market conditions and give unworkable & unreasonably opportunistic offers, all when Owners may as well sell elsewhere or continue trading their vessels.

*Market Sales Reported*: **NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

---

### Pakistan

- **Headline**: LOAN APPROVED!
- **Key Notes**: *IMF help.* | *HKC needs.*

News about the recent USD 3 billion IMF bailout loan has been forthcoming into Pakistan, with USD 1 billion already being received by the country, and this may give the ship recycling industry some hope that Pakistan could get back to some form of buying in the weeks / months ahead – especially if a portion of the funds are allocated to the domestic ship recycling sector.

As such, as soon as commercial banks start sanctioning fresh L/Cs, we may see some deals taking place into a recycling location that has otherwise been starved of tonnage for most of the year, due to the ongoing and dire political & economic circumstances in the country.

Of course, Gadani Recyclers will need to seriously focus on upgrading their facilities and keep up with the ongoing industry changes, especially as the HKC enters into force in Bangladesh, else Pakistan seems destined to miss out on tonnage altogether.

Fundamentals aren’t too supportive either, especially as domestic steel plate prices continue to flatline for months on end and the Pakistani Rupee that recently firmed to PKR 277 against the U.S. Dollar, has stayed there during the course of the week.

As such, and like its competing neighbors, we can certainly expect a quieter summer / monsoon in Pakistan.

*Market Sales Reported*: **NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

---

### Turkey

- **Headline**: GOING DOWN?

The Turkish market seems poised to soften moving forward, as not only did local steel plate prices decline by about USD 20/MT, even import steel prices followed suit with a decline of about USD 10/MT of their own.

All while the Turkish Lira glides along and gradually weakens deeper into TRY 26.2X territory against the U.S. Dollar. And this is unravelling whilst the shortage of tonnage continues to deteriorate local sentiments well past Eid holidays.

As such, it’s certainly a reasonable bet that the Turkish recycling market may face a correction in prices in the weeks ahead.
