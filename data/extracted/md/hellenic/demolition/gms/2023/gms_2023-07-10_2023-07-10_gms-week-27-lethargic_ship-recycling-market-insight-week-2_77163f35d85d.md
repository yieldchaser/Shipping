With many sub-continent yards still closed post Eid holidays, mixed in with the ongoing 
monsoon season, and all of it brewing in a stew of a total lack of tonnage, it has certainly been 
another lethargic and inactive week (and a majority of the year so far) for recycling sales as we 
enter the 3rd quarter of 2023 and prices being quoted are so unworkably below market
expectations at present, a bounce back anytime soon seems unlikely. 
As such, before proposing any further candidates, most Cash Buyers and (especially) Ship 
Owners have decided to continue to wait and watch the markets for greater stability and a 
better handle on pricing.  
Meanwhile, after a couple of volatile quarters, fundamentals presently seem to have leveled 
out across the board in the Indian sub-continent and even Turkish recycling markets, where 
steel plate prices and currencies (except in Pakistan) seem to have found a brief plateau of peace.
Moreover, an ongoing lack of workable Letters of Credit (L/Cs) & financing in Pakistan and 
even in a post-budget Bangladesh is once again driving prices and demand down as the Central 
Government Bank in Bangladesh seeks to impose tougher limits on the expenditure of its 
precious U.S. Dollar reserves. 
Overall, amidst the ongoing crippling lack of tonnage, those Owners with units closer to 
recycling age have been seriously reconsidering placing their vessels back into service and are 
invariably adding to the growing lack of tonnage.  
With monsoon’s being the only silver-lining for the sub-continent markets – in that cutting 
activities come to a crawl as it is - it certainly seems to be a quieter July (and perhaps even 
August) headed the industry’s way.

MARKET COMMENTARY

BANGLADESH 
HARDER TO COME BY! 
It has been another week of virtually no buying as the shortage of tonnage (post-Eid)
rages on and most End Buyers remain away from their offices, either on holiday, or now,
once again chasing local banks in order to seek financing on any prospective purchases
they may be looking to secure for their emptying yards. 
Since the start of the year, it has been a difficult affair to transact units for recycling into
Bangladesh due to the first set of L/C limits. However, as limits eased, especially on 
smaller LDT (less than 10K LT) vessels, Bangladeshi Recyclers gradually started to find 
a way back to the buying.  
Unfortunately, right after the recent budget of June 1st was announced, the government 
seems to have cracked down on the domestic budget for U.S. Dollar reserves once again
and imposed a fresh batch of restrictions on the issuance of fresh L/Cs. 
It may therefore take several weeks before domestic recyclers work out how they can buy
tonnage and at what workable levels the central bank will approve any L/Cs moving 
forward. 
As such, with L/Cs harder to open and no meaningful tonnage on offer, opportunistic 
offers have been forthcoming from some domestic Recyclers, especially on the dwindling 
supply of tonnage being proposed for sale.  
While no Ship Owner or Cash Buyer would be willing to sell at such lowly rates, even 
the industry at large is taking notice and is unwilling to even introduce vessels for a
potential Chattogram re-delivery – at least until some sensible offers / L/C stability
returns.

INDIA 
Looking up. 
GAINING GROUND! 
While Indian fundamentals (local steel plate prices and the Indian Rupee) recorded some 
improvements over the last couple of weeks, this week, a different story played out as steel
plate prices registered some volatility to end the week slightly weaker, all while the Indian 
Rupee took a nosedive past Rs. 82.70, only to end the week at levels north of Rs. 82.50
against the U.S. Dollar. 
However, unlike the rest of the sub-continent recycling industry, India has not suffered 
any L/C or financing issues over the year and this has certainly helped them offer on and 
even secure vessels on an ongoing basis – including their precious HKC units.  
Moreover, even of late, there have been some HKC green sales concluded into Alang –
including an LNG and a few MSC containers – and this is starting to compel recycling 
neighbours to compete and up their offers, if they stand any chance at securing any vessels 
– especially amidst the ongoing L/C issues in Pakistan and now in Bangladesh. 
Finally, we anticipate that the ongoing slowdown in the supply of tonnage will likely 
continue through the next month (or two), as shipowners head off on summer holidays 
and it is expected to be quieter (in terms of sale and purchase activity) over these monsoon 
months.

GMS Weekly 
NO EFFORT! 
Far from competing against any of their sub-continent competitors at present, Pakistani 
Recyclers cannot even compete amongst themselves for tonnage – so dire and low have 
Pakistan’s U.S Dollar Reserves been for much of this year, causing the Pakistani Rupee to 
depreciate uncontrollably. 
In fact, over the last couple of weeks, Pakistan was on the verge of running out of all its 
U.S. Dollar funds until the IMF (in addition to several other nations) stepped in this week 
and provided a financial U.S. Dollar stimulus package to Pakistan. 
While this did not help local steel plate prices, which have remained horizontal for 
practically most of this year, the Pakistani Rupee did however, immediately receive a boost 
and firmed up to PKR 277 (from PKR 287) against the U.S. Dollar. 
As the shortage of tonnage continues and India takes in its share of regular and HKC
tonnage, Pakistani Recyclers have yet to make any efforts at improving their facilities 
during this downtime, especially so that they maybe in a position to compete even strongly 
against its sub-continent neighbors.  
Of course, the Pakistani economy has struggled significantly as leaders have been ousted 
while the local currency has also collapsed, and this has seen end buyers and banks 
retreating from the buying of ships for recycling altogether.

ON PAUSE? 
Post-Eid, the Turkish market seems to have gone through a bit of a pause, as local 
fundamentals came together in unison and reported no changes for the week. 
Surprising much of the industry was the Lira that reported no further declines as it ended 
the week at levels just north of TRY 26. Even imported and local steel plate prices have 
reported virtually no change for the week. 
As post Eid-holidays / celebrations continue amidst the ongoing shortage in the availability 
of tonnage and a Lira that is completely unsupportive of current offers, it remains to be 
seen just where (USD / MT) or when this market will open for business ‘as usual’. 
For now, like the rest of its industry counterparts, it ended a quiet week in Aliaga.
