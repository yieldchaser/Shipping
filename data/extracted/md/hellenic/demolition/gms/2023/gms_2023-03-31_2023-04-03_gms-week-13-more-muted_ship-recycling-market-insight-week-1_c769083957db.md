## 1. Market Overview

### Highlights
- Quiet week.
- Holiday season.
- Markets stable.
- L/C delays.
- Expect a wait.

### Editorial: Market Commentary

With Container & Dry Bulk rates firming once again, the number of firm sales candidates also appears to have dried up and this is leaving keen recyclers empty handed for yet another week.

While there have been a plethora of sales at the start of the year, particularly in the Capesize Bulker and Container sectors, the supply has surprisingly slowed so much of late, it nearly seems like it has timed itself in sync with declining sub-continent prices.

Steel plate prices had also found themselves going through a phase of incredible volatility but seem to have stabilized (comparatively) more since, and there is still interest from the Indian and Bangladeshi markets to acquire at the present overall levels, which are hovering close to or even well above USD 600/LDT on choice units.

L/Cs remain the chief concern in Bangladesh amidst a chronic lack of liquidity in the country, and several L/Cs are left waiting for Central Bank approval, which is inevitably leading to local delays on boardings, payments, and beachings.

Owners are therefore reminded that it is not exactly business as usual (especially in Pakistan and Bangladesh to an extent), despite several sales taking place there recently.

Finally, at the far end, Turkey remains unchanged with its weekly (and marginal) fluctuations in import and local steel plate prices, as does the Lira continue on its seemingly unrelenting decline against the U.S. Dollar.

## 3. Country Market Intelligence

### Bangladesh

- **Headline**: BACKLOG!
- **Key Notes**: *Seeking approvals.* | *Demand ripe.*

After a number of deals concluded over the last month or so, there has been a backlog of sales seeking Central Bank approval for the opening of fresh L/Cs in Bangladesh this week and this is leading to local delays in getting vessels cleared and paid for beaching.

As such, there has been a slowdown in sales activity whilst local Buyers struggle to sort their delivery and L/C / payment issues out, and rates remain slightly softer than the peaks they touched several weeks ago, especially due to weaker steel plate prices that have declined about USD 28/Ton.

Adding downward pressure to the local sentiment was the Bangladeshi Taka that further declined past a record BDT 107.10 against the U.S. Dollar.

Notwithstanding the above, demand remains ripe (to an extent) and local Buyers are still there to fill empty plots, as long as L/C issues are sorted. As a reminder to our readers, current prices are still at historical highs, at or above USD 600/LDT on choice units.

### India

- **Headline**: HOLIDAY ADVANTAGE!

After some worrying reversals in recent weeks and despite steel plate prices in India jumping nearly USD 10/Ton by mid-week and ending the week only USD 2/Ton above last week, Buyers in Alang seemed willing to challenge Bangladesh once again, even on the small number of recycling units that are coming to the market.

Not only is this because of the diminished number of units that are in the market for sale, but the natural side effect of it is also being felt at Alang (and Chattogram’s) waterfronts as port positions over the last couple of weeks have barely seen any fresh arrivals.

Moreover, even the Indian Rupee stayed firm in the low Rs. 82s against the U.S. Dollar.

On the sales front, there are still a couple of HKC green sales that are reportedly heading to India (despite the addition of two more NK approved yards in Chattogram last week), but even those container Owners selling for green recycling are now seeing trading interests on their vintage tonnage, pop up again due to improving charter rates.

Finally, as Ramadan holidays descend across the Indian sub-continent, India may be able to secure some much-needed tonnage (with many plots still deserted) in the next few weeks ahead.

*Market Sales Reported*: **NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

### Pakistan

- **Headline**: FAR FROM COMPETITIVE!
- **Key Notes**: *Inabilities.* | *Holiday reflection.*

Despite Pakistan remaining far from being competitive with their Indian sub-continent counterparts, their chief concern is still their crippling inability to get L/Cs approved by the Central Government Bank for any new vessels.

Adding to their frustrations have been (a) the Pakistani Rupee that is now trading at a record PKR 283.60 against the U.S. Dollar, (b) steel plate prices that have flatlined once again due to lack of trading, and (c) even with the minimal supply of tonnage, most units will not be heading towards Gadani shores as domestic recyclers are unable to offer competitively.

As such, Gadani remains an inert market for ship recycling for another week, and there are few signs that this market will get back to being competitive any time soon.

Therefore, as Ramadan holidays commence, it is perhaps an appropriate time to reflect on just how a turbulent a period it has been for Pakistan as a country, and hopefully post holidays, there can be some real and workable progress to get Gadani back in action once again.

*Market Sales Reported*: **NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

---

### Turkey

- **Headline**: EAGER, YET STUCK!

Even though import and local steel plate prices registered improvements of about USD 10/Ton this week and local offerings are still strong (on the back of an excruciatingly diminished supply resulting in Recyclers holding their prices), Aliaga yards continue to suffer.

The Lira has also been dwindling, breaking records with each passing week that has resulted in the Lira now floating around the mid TRY 19.1X mark against the U.S. Dollar.

With the recent rumours of local fixtures still being unconfirmed and no further information of market units being proposed to Turkish yards, it remains clearly evident that this market is officially stuck until Ramadan is concluded.

*Market Sales Reported*: **NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**
