# Market Commentary

## FOURTH QUARTER POSITIVITY!...?

A glimmer of optimism seems to have been aroused in the sub-continent ship recycling markets this week, as several units were reportedly concluded to optimistically fervent Cash Buyers (at increasingly firmer levels) who are perhaps eager to book tonnage pre-Monsoon end, in order to potentially satiate some of the forthcoming increase in demand from the sub-continent markets once the traditionally busy year-end / Q4 kicks in.

Several sales have reportedly been confirmed into Pakistan over the last month (a couple of units have even greeted Gadani’s waterfront this week), especially as L/C approvals are now (precariously) coming through for some of the tier-1 Recyclers in Gadani, where capacity and demand has been steadily fermenting for a better part of the year.

India too has seen some positive spikes in domestic steel plate prices this week, and with next week’s G20 nations conference that is scheduled to meet in India, positive announcements on infrastructure projects / developments are expected to be announced that should further boost the country’s economy and global footprint.

Indeed, global steel plate prices have firmed up by about 2% over this past week, reportedly off the back of Chinese measures to boost the housing market (lowering mortgage rates for the first time for domestic home buyers) and to support the Renminbi (the official currency of the People’s Republic of China).

Only in Bangladesh have plate prices remained stranded / flatlined in the doldrums for yet another week, as no serious offers on vessels have emanated from local Recyclers as a result. Moreover, foreign currency / U.S. Dollar reserves are still struggling to being balanced by the powers that be (invariably leading to tougher limits to open L/Cs) and End Buyers who still believe they can secure bargain deals closer to USD 450/LDT (in vain, if we may add).

The reality is that with India and Pakistani markets picking up again amidst some much-needed (and traditional) fourth quarter positivity flowing into the market, Bangladesh could be set for an extended spell on the sidelines whilst such poor offerings and sentiments persist locally. Finally, Turkey remains stable and unchanged since last week.

# Bangladesh

## FAR FROM ACTION!

*Margin notes: Uncompetitive. | Marginalised.*

Bangladesh remains far from the action and unrealistically uncompetitive at present, especially as India and Pakistan have come roaring back over the past few weeks, and that too with some particularly impressive numbers and acquisitions.

Gone are the days of Bangladesh securing distressed sales below USD 500/LDT and even though several Bangladeshi Recyclers have managed to book some favorably-priced deals over the past couple of months, any Far Eastern tonnage that was recently committed and expected to arrive Bangladesh’s waterfront, now looks set to head towards competing shores – so disastrous have offers and prices been from this market.

As local steel plate prices continue to flatline for weeks (even months) on end, the Bangladeshi Taka continues to weaken and is currently dangling at record highs against the U.S Dollar, grooving around at levels above BDT 109.10.

Going into September / post-monsoon and with the recent influx of cheaper Chinese tonnage resulting in numerous local yards filling up once again and despite L/C limits being satisfied once again - it is expected that this market may remain on the sidelines for the foreseeable future, at least until some greater demand, stronger sentiments / pricing, and some more re-assured performance on L/Cs is witnessed.

# India

## BACK TO BIZ!

*Margin notes: Securing sales. | Container sold.*

In conjunction with a re-emerging Pakistani market, India managed to get back to business this week and secure several much-needed sales, that too at some decent numbers at last.

Container prices for recycling seem to be taking off once again, just as an influx of tonnage (including for HKC resale) starts to re-enter the markets once again. Moreover, even fundamentals seem to be elevating local sentiments as steel plate prices firmed by about USD 12/Ton, while the Indian Rupee continues to dance around the mid Rs. 82x against the U.S. Dollar.

Bearing this in mind, Norse of Singapore controlled feeder container vessel CHERRY (6,499 LDT) was fixed for a decent USD 545/LT LDT basis an ‘as is’ Singapore delivery, with 350 Tons of bunkers included in the sale, and basis a full range HKC resale only.

If this is the sign of things to come, it certainly seems as though some of the redline deals concluded over the last month (of two) that are still unsold to a Recycler, may finally have the opportunity to minimize losses, if not break even entirely.

# Pakistan

## SUCCESS STORY!

*Margin notes: Best numbers. | Bulker books big.*

Pakistan has been the success story over the past month, with some of the best numbers on show across the sub-continent emanating from this market, and slowly but surely, L/C approvals coming back at pace, especially for certain cash-rich Recyclers who are able to deposit close to 100% cash margins with their banks.

Such was reportedly the case with the most notable sale of many this week that was confirmed to a Gadani Recycler, as the Italian built bulker SOTIRIA (11,588 LDT) was concluded for a firm USD 545/LT LDT. This follows last week’s sale of bulker GLORIA 1 (11,259 LDT) that was also committed to Pakistan, for a healthy USD 525/LT LDT.

Gadani’s anchorage too dusted off the cobwebs this week, as not one but two vessels greeted the waterfront, further adding to the efforts being made by local Recyclers, in order to get back into the game.

Pakistani fundamentals, however, remain the dichotomy for this market, especially as Pakistani steel plate prices remain dead for several quarters now, while the Pakistani Rupee collapses past PKR 300, ending the week past PKR 305 against the U.S. Dollar.

As business gears grind into action after nearly 7 months on the sidelines, it may certainly be slow going at first, especially as L/C approvals are sought for any large LDT and higher value vessels. However, it is good to see Pakistan finally get back into the mix, especially after various political, economic, and fiscal turmoil witnessed by the country over the last year or so, and we can only hope for more deals moving forward.

# Turkey

## STABLE SPACE!

*Margin notes: New Units. | Back to the tables.*

As the rest of the sub-continent markets seem to be gaining momentum with the fresh introduction of tonnage, in addition to Owners and Cash Buyers starting to bite the bullet with current prices, the theme seems to have extended to Turkey where news of units being proposed finally came forth this week.

As fundamentals entered a steady / stable space and the Lira remains suspended in the mid TRY 26s against the U.S. Dollar, the introduction of units at a time when prices have remained stable for several weeks now should hopefully garner some quick sales into Aliaga.

September maybe the month that all markets, including Turkey, shake off the rust and return back to the tables.
