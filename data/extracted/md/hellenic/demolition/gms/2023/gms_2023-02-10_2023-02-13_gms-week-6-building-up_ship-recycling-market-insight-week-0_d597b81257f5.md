## 1. Market Overview

### Highlights
- Increased flow.
- India focus.
- Liquidity woes.
- No tankers.
- 2023 positive.

### Editorial: Market Commentary

Of course, India continues to remain the primary focus due to their far more stable market conditions and ability to finance new L/Cs regardless of vessel size.

Bangladesh and Pakistan continue to struggle with liquidity issues amidst a dire lack of U.S. Dollars in each country, despite continued hope that ongoing negotiations with regards to their respective IMF loans may bring some sort of relief to their respective ship recycling sectors (and more).

Lastly, the Aliaga market was understandably quiet after massive earthquakes rocked the Turkey and Syria this week that resulted in the tragic passing of over 10,000 souls.

Overall, the main supply of tonnage continues to arrive from the feeder container sector (few Panamax containers have yet to arrive), whilst there is also an increase in the flow of dry bulk vessels, as charter rates sink to fresh lows - especially after the Chinese New Year Holidays.

There seems to be little chance of seeing tankers this year, especially as charter markets push on to new highs – something which is long overdue, following the bloodletting witnessed in this particular sector over the last few years.

2023 has commenced in an overall positive fashion for the international ship recycling markets, with an increased flow of deals and a positive market with a healthy demand across nearly all locations – and it would seem that as soon as L/C issues are sorted in both Pakistan and Bangladesh, the industry can be sure of a busy and vibrant year ahead.

## 3. Country Market Intelligence

### Bangladesh

- **Headline**: TALKS CONTINUE!
- **Key Notes**: *Much talk, little action.* | *IMF loan.*

Amidst a renewed demand and improving fundamentals, discussions on new deals continue at pace in Bangladesh, but recyclers with the ability to perform on workable L/Cs to pay for vessels, remain few and far between.

As such, the number of locally realised deals is remarkably low as most firm tonnage continues to head towards Indian shores since the start of the year, where Sellers are assured of performances and timely payments.

Negotiations continue on the much-anticipated IMF loan, which should bring some much-needed U.S. Dollars into the country – yet, in the grand scheme of events, this will only amount to about USD 2 - 3 billion when in reality, much more is needed to ease the current liquidity crisis.

On the local fundamentals front, steel plate prices firmed an impressive USD 25/Ton, possibly on the back of some steel being offloaded from recent deliveries that is boosting local resale levels. Even the Bangladeshi Taka firmed back towards the BDT 103.50 mark, in further positive developments for the local market.

### India

- **Headline**: TEMPORARY SHOCKS!

Despite having one of the slowest year (2022) for ship recycling in decades, falls of about USD 12/Ton on local steel plate prices in India (this week alone), and the most recent scandal surrounding the potential collapse of the Adani empire, demand in Alang remains rife for new vessels.

As the currency continues to trade in the mid Rs. 82 against the U.S. Dollar and while few expect any significant long-lasting effects from the Adani affair, this temporary shock to sentiments is expected to recover as the weeks roll on.

Several container (in particular) candidates for green recycling continue to be discussed, but there were few sales realised for the week, with an uptick in activity expected in the weeks ahead.

One sale was concluded after protracted discussions as the Navy Support vessel (converted from container with 2,200 Tons permanent ballast on board) BUNGA MAS ENAM (4,750 LDT) was concluded at a decent USD 320/LT LDT basis an ‘as is’ under tow Malaysia delivery and for guaranteed HKC recycling in Alang.

### Pakistan

- **Headline**: LACKING BITE!
- **Key Notes**: *Lowest placed.* | *Trying best on LCs.*

As the lowest placed and least reliable ship-recycling market across the sub-continent, there has been little to cheer in Gadani of late, especially while the current inertia is expected to suffer for some time to come.

Until the U.S. Dollar credit / liquidity crisis is resolved in the country, there seems little that Gadani Buyers can do and there is little hope of fresh deals being concluded into this market any time soon.

On the local fundamentals front, domestic steel plate prices jumped nearly USD 60/Ton this week, however, the weakening Pakistani Rupee may have something to do with the change in levels as the Rupee briefly dipped past PKR 276, only to end the week at levels just north of PKR 271.

Overall, Gadani Recyclers are certainly trying to do their best to open usance L/Cs or obtain private financing / alternate means of opening L/Cs through Middle East and (especially) Dubai based banks / intermediaries in particular. Notwithstanding, whilst prices remain so off, business, and new transactions will expectedly remain muted.

### Turkey

- **Headline**: EARTHQUAKE!

Turkey and Syria were jolted by what seems like a once in a century earthquake that has resulted in the untimely passing of over 10,000 souls (some putting the number past 20,000).

As a result, the market remained relatively quiet and unchanged since last week with local steel plate prices stranded on even keel and the Lira gradually weaker, yet buoying around the last TRY 18s against the U.S. Dollar.

This market is far firmer than it was a few weeks ago and we do not expect any long terms absence from Aliaga Buyers.
