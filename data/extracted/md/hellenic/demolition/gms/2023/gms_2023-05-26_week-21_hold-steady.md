# Market Commentary

## HOLD STEADY!

Amidst the ongoing slowdown in the supply of tonnage and the onset of the traditionally quieter summer / monsoon months that would certainly help ease off the demand, prices across the sub-continent markets remained steady for another week.

There is also the impending budget on June 1st in Bangladesh and many End Buyers are waiting to see if any new changes / taxes are imposed on the domestic ship recycling sector before offering afresh on tonnage.

However, the general consensus on the upcoming budget is that no changes are expected and that it may actually be positive on the overall business outlook in Bangladesh, amidst a growing demand for steel and raw materials due to various upcoming construction and infrastructure projects that are due in the summer months.

Additionally, the recent meeting in Bangladesh on whether to ratify the Hong Kong convention produced some interesting discussions and outcomes, with the country amending its ship recycling standard from ‘Red’ to ‘Orange’, on the environmental outlook for the country. The next step is for flags to ratify the convention before it can be entered into force, in what could be a landmark ruling in Bangladesh – the first sub-continent country to be actively doing so.

Meanwhile, after a recent wobble in prices, India too has steadied and there is now an emerging demand as Buyers in Alang have missed out on a majority of the non-HKC (and reportedly, even some HKC) tonnage to the firmer Chattogram market.

Pakistan remains on the sidelines, with political and economic chaos rendering this market virtually redundant and there is no realistic hope of the situation stabilizing any time soon.

Finally, Turkey remains suspended in no-man’s land, as the situation remains unchanged from last week and the election results awaiting to be announced.

# Bangladesh

## WAKING UP!

*Margin notes: HKC talks. | 1st June budget.*

Over previous weeks, several (market and private) sales have taken place into Bangladesh, as this market appears to finally turn a corner post Ramadan and Eid holidays.

Moreover, just this week, Japan Shipowners' Association (JSA) sent its delegates to Bangladesh, along with representatives from four major Japanese shipping companies as Bangladesh’s regulatory bodies have expressed their commitment to ratify HKC recycling processes and requested Japan's assistance in the further development of a Treatment Storage Disposal Facility (TSDF).

The Japanese delegation also visited multiple ship recycling facilities in Chattogram and had constructive dialogues with members of Bangladesh Ship Breakers and Recyclers Association (BSBRA), with emphasis on increasing the number of HKC compliant yards.

As a result of this visit, Chattogram’s ship recycling industry has been re-categorized from ‘Red’ to ‘Orange’ code on the environmental outlook, which will assure further investments in yard infrastructures.

On the sales front, no fresh market sales registered for the week, as the supply of tonnage remains abysmal, especially as we enter the usually quieter monsoon season – when much of the seasonal labour returns home.

As fundamentals continue to jiggle as the weeks go by where the Bangladeshi Taka still trades in excess of BDT 107.10 against the U.S. Dollar and local steel plate prices flatline through the week once again, all eyes will be on the upcoming budget of June 1st next week – whereby even though few changes are expected to impact the ship recycling sector – many Buyers are being cautious in the event of unexpected changes.

# India

## MISSING OUT (ON HKC)!

*Margin notes: Bangla secures. | Fundamentals wobbly.*

India has recently missed out on some HKC sales, with Bangladesh securing tonnage to some of the newly approved Class NK HKC facilities (three now in total).

Prices in India are a touch lower than Bangladesh and as many of the recent fixtures are coming in from the Far East, a lower delivery cost to Bangladesh (vs. India) helps ease some of the expenses as vessels head to Chattogram, especially as evidenced by the number of arrivals in Bangaldesh over recent weeks.

Fundamentals in India remain wobbly too, with steel prices enduring another shaky week – down by about USD 5/Ton - whilst the currency finally settles back down towards Rs. 82.5X against the U.S. Dollar, keeping Alang Recyclers encouraged, despite the dwindling supply of vessels.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# Pakistan

## UNFULFILLED DREAMS!

*Margin notes: Lacklustre. | Nothing meaningful.*

Another completely lackluster week sees the Pakistani ship recycling sector devoid of any sales / activity and remains on the sidelines of the sub-continent ship recycling markets. No progress has been likewise made on upgrading facilities (as Bangladeshi Recyclers have been of late) to adhere to HKC standards, something that could easily see the Pakistani market left behind in the long run.

Understandably, the political and economic chaos that continues to beset the country has taken front and center stage, leaving the market in Gadani only able to hopelessly watch on as competing neighbors continues to take in units’ week after week.

It has certainly been some time since any meaningful sales have taken place in Gadani and with port reports and plots virtually empty, there remains capacity and demand to acquire, even though there may not be any realistic hope of fulfilling those yards (and dreams) any time soon.

A lone supply vessel did greet Gadani’s anchorage this week and time will tell whether local banking and L/C processes have improved the chances of tonnage beaching in this market.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# Turkey

## STRAPPED!

*Margin notes: TRY 20! | Coping by!*

Turkey remains strapped in no-man’s land, with levels nearly half of those from the Indian sub-continent, local steel plate prices that remain weak, and a Lira that is on a mission (and finally succeeded) to pass TRY 20.0, even though it ended the week at TRY 19.95 against the U.S. Dollar. We believe next week would be firm in the 20s, as the Lira has lost more than half its value in the preceding 18 months or so.

No change in supply, no news of local fixtures (market of private), and a country that’s awaiting the result of a crucial election cycle that’s managed to end in a runoff, as President Erdogan faces his challenger.

Just how much longer can Aliaga’s recycling sector trudge on in this state is anyone’s guess. For now, however, the market seems to be struggling on by.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**
