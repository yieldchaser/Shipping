# Market Commentary

## FADING!

Markets continue to struggle going into the final weeks of the year, and there has been very little activity or new sales to report as a result, with most end users abstaining from the buying and wishing to wait and watch developments before acquiring afresh.

Indeed, contrary to many expectations, India has lost around 3,000 rupees (USD 35/LDT) from steel prices and there has been completely the opposite of any post Diwali bounce as all buyers turn to caution at the end of another highly volatile year in ship recycling.

Pakistan was buoyed last week by the news that their country will adhere and sign up to the Hong Kong Convention – which will enter into force from 2025, so yards have time to upgrade and improve their facilities in line with HKC standards before then.

The EU will also revisit plans to approve select yards in India and add them to the EU list of approved ship recycling facilities – having previously visited but rejected plans to include them on any list several years ago.

So there is much progress to report and much to look forward to going into 2024, but the thorny issue of available financing and LCs remains of chief concern in both Pakistan and Bangladesh. Constantly fluctuating and depreciating currencies need to settle before banks can start to approve fresh LCs in those locations.

However, there is the general expectation that very few deals or new financing will be seen in Bangladesh until after the elections there in mid January, with the hope that a new government comes in to revitalize a struggling and stagnant economy.

# Bangladesh

## STATUS QUO!

*Margin notes: Out of picture. | Election hopes.*

Bangladesh remains completely out of the picture for another week as workable financing / LCs is still hard to come by and demand is virtually non existent as a result (with no way to pay for new ships).

There is an expectation that this will be the status quo at least until after the general elections in mid January of next year, and there will be very limited purchases up until that time.

The hope is that a new government will come in to revitalize the economy (particularly the burgeoning garment export industry) and stabilize a drastically deteriorating currency, so that new ship purchases and the freeing up of much needed US dollars for anything other than essential purchases can resume.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# India

## DISAPPOINTING & DETERIORATING!

*Margin notes: Looking down. | Limited sales.*

After a couple of high priced purchases last week (including one Vietnamese bulker and one 11kldt container – sold ‘as is’ China), there was very little to report in terms of activity this week in India as prices keep on deteriorating and sentiments disappointing by the day.

Indeed, steel prices have lost some 3,000 rupees in the last month alone (USD 35/LDT) and the declining prices show few signs of stabilising or halting as yet. This is in stark contrast to a dry bulk chartering sector that has unexpectedly taken off over the last week.

This therefore suggests that it will be a far slower end to the year, with limited sales candidates and a depressed recycling sector that may even see levels fall below 500/LDT for the first time in months.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# Pakistan

## HOPES RECEDING!

*Margin notes: Unable to pick up slack. | Few deals.*

The hope in recent times is that the Pakistan market can make a sustained push to acquire vessels before the end of the year, to pick up some of the slack from the ailing India and Bangladesh markets.

However, a lack of available bank / LC financing remains the chief culprit in Pakistan and a constantly fluctuating and extremely volatile currency is not giving end buyers confidence to start buying again.

As such there is the possibility that only a few deals will be concluded to Gadani buyers (despite the completely empty port report there) before the year is out.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# Turkey

## FIRMER YET!

*Margin notes: USD 8/MT jump. | Closer to TRY 29?*

Turkey continues to surprise the industry, with weekly ongoing improvements amidst a key depreciating fundamental that should have taken the steam entirely out of its domestic ship-recycling market.

As import steel plate prices improved by another USD 8/Ton, which may have (directly or indirectly) affected local offerings by another USD 5/MT this week, the Lira continues to commensurate its ongoing demise against the U.S. Dollar, as it treads deeper towards an eventual TRY 29 leap.

Overall, this market, while firming, continues to suffer from the deathly starvation of meaningful tonnage.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**
