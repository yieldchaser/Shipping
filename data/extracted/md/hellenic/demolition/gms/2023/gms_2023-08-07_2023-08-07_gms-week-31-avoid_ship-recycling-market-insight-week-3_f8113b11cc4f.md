This week, recycling markets essentially became an unfeasible business-place as very few 
Recyclers were willing to table any firm offers, instead, tabling only low-ball indications as the 
remainder of the end Buyers remained rather quiet, waiting and watching the market before 
deciding, which direction the domestic industry is ready to proceed in. 
There remain a number of unsold units that are currently being diverted to other destinations
from Bangladesh, including India, in additional to a recently re-emerging Pakistani market. 
Owners with vessels to sell are being price-shocked into diverting their vessels to another
market or withdrawing them from the market entirely, due to a lack of sensible offers that 
present no encouragement / motivation / interest from Owners (or Cash Buyers) to commit for 
recycling. 
We could therefore be entering a bleaker period of sales and activity through August, especially 
whilst this standoff in prices persists, and congestion abounds. 
Deliveries have started to become troublesome as well, particularly for those units sold at 
recently higher levels and are now facing the inevitable song-and-dance at the waterfront with
unreliable Cash Buyers and painful renegotiations being reported at the waterfront. 
As such, it is an extremely frustrating and fraught market at present, with Owners and Cash 
Buyers alike, who are becoming disillusioned by the falling levels and lack of serious offers. 
There is only so much chasing down a market that can be endured before some sort of stability 
is seen, especially once prices level out and the industry finally accepts the levels on the ground.
L/Cs also remain an area of concern in Bangladesh as it has been taking much longer to get L/C
approvals over USD 5 million these days, and this is leading to delays in obtaining offers and 
getting vessels delivered.

MARKET COMMENTARY

BANGLADESH 
UNTENABLE! 
Several sales have reportedly taken place into Bangladesh this week that seemed to belie 
the overall negativity in this market as present.  
Firstly, a favored Polish built container unit SOL STRAITS (7,770 LDT) was fixed to one 
particularly keen hot Bangladeshi Buyer, for an impressive USD 592/LT LDT (with 250 
Tons of bunkers included in the sale). 
Additionally, another favored type, RoRo vessel TAI HWA (4,043 LDT) from Taiwan 
Navigation was committed basis an ‘as is’ Kaohsiung delivery, for a decent USD 460/LT 
LDT. 
However, the overall sentiments and demand in Bangladesh remain dire and these sales 
are once again outliers and not indicative of the true conditions of this market, especially 
one that is becoming untenable for most Owners and Cash Buyers to negotiate with. 
Fundamentals too remain dire as the Taka nearly breached BDT 109.40 (highest ever) 
against the U.S. Dollar, only to retreat and end the week at BDT 108.95. Even local steel 
plate prices depreciated last week and flatlined through this week, further deteriorating 
local sentiments and confusing local Buyers where local prices should truly stand.

INDIA 
Lowest placed. 
SLIPPING! 
India remains the lowest placed sub-continent market and has once again seen tonnage 
slip through their hands, despite being the one location that is capable of reliably
performing financial / L/C transactions. 
Indeed, offers below USD 500/LDT have not been received well by Ship Owners or Cash 
Buyers and they have either diverted their units to alternative destinations, or even 
entirely withdraw them from the market.  
The biggest question that remains is WHY?  
The markets need to stop evaluating every transaction as a lottery win each time – trying 
to beat the ‘last done’, especially considering the cyclical nature of the industry. Prices at 
every level are firm, price-shock or not. While remaining in the green is certainly essential
for the parties involved, watching the direction of the market when concluding units is 
just as critical, especially in order to avoid unpleasant surprises at the time of physical
delivery and just accept the declining nature of market conditions.  
At a time when prices hit the mid USD 200s/LT during the worst of the market, we are 
presently at levels that are over double those for recycling back in ’08 and certainly a long 
way from the early USD 100s/LT from only a couple of decades ago. 
As such, price amnesia certainly remains a detriment in current times. 
The traditionally quieter period over the monsoon and summer months are also upon the 
industry, so perhaps prices and aggression to acquire may improve from September 
moving forwards. 
For now, with a Rupee that came close to breaching Rs. 83 and local steel plate prices that 
have moved in the opposite direction (firming by about USD 12/Ton), India is certainly in 
the crosshairs of confusion this week.

GMS Weekly 
BACK & BIDDING! 
Pakistan has finally started to re-emerge as a viable force after almost a year on the 
sidelines, unable to conclude any units due to the ongoing financial, economic, and even 
political woes besetting the country. 
Consequently, it seems certain that several deals may need to be concluded into Gadani as 
vessel Owners and Cash Buyers seek alternate options to a lowballing India and a 
Bangladesh lacking demand. 
Local fundamentals too remain weakened like the rest of the industry, with local steel plate 
prices flatlining for months on end due to a total lack of domestic steel trading, in addition 
to the Pakistani Rupee that briefly breached PKR 289, only to retreat to PKR 287 against the 
U.S. Dollar, by the time the week ended. 
As such, it may take some time to work through what are likely to be some L/C delays, but 
it is certainly encouraging that enquiries are emerging, and the feeling is that it is only a 
matter of time before deals will start being concluded into Pakistan once again.

SLIPPING FURTHER! 
Like the Bangladeshi market, the Turkish market seems to be slipping even further as local
steel plate prices reportedly declined by about USD 10/MT and the Lira briefly breached 
TRY 27.20, only to retreat to TRY 26.9X as the week ended. 
Such fundamentals are certainly not conducive to maintaining market levels and although 
Turkish prices seemed far more resilient to declining this year than the years gone last, just 
how long can Aliaga Buyers maintain their pricing through such unrelentingly negative 
fundamentals, including a domestic economy that has been set alight due to inflation,
remains to be seen. 
We can only hope that the ongoing ‘regulations’ for environmentally conscious recycling 
are also taking into account, the livelihoods of people that are so dependent on this sector
and may likely be displaced due to the prevailing rules on recycling.
