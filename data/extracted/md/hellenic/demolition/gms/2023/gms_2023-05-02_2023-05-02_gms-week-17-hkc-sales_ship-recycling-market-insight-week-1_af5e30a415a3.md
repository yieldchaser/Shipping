"You have to be where you are to get where you need to go." 
 
 
 
– Amy Poehler 
Several sales have taken place into India for HKC restricted yards / green recycling only, as end 
Buyers seek to fill empty recycling plots, especially after a quieter period during Ramadan / Eid 
holidays across the sub-continent & Turkey, when minimal-to-no deals were concluded at the 
various destinations. 
As the holiday period concludes, ever optimistic industry players are still hopeful that they 
may see an increase in the number of recycling candidates, as demand in Bangladesh, India,
and even Turkey, ramps up again. 
Notwithstanding, rates have taken somewhat of a battering off the back of declining steel plate 
prices & volatile currencies, and as stated in last week’s edition of the GMS WEEKLY, it is 
becoming increasingly difficulty to get vessels delivered into Bangladesh and Pakistan, with all 
parties waiting on Central Bank approvals for Letters of Credit (L/Cs) to open. 
Indeed, Sellers and Cash Buyers should budget for at least two tides to get vessels delivered, 
particularly for larger LDT & large U.S. Dollar value vessels, where these approvals are taking 
longer than expected to come through. 
On the other end of the sub-continent, Pakistan remains largely absent from the buying due to 
unworkable bank limits / restrictions on the expenditure of dwindling U.S. Dollar reserves, in 
addition to uncompetitive pricing on units that has subsequently placed them a long way at the 
bottom of the sub-continent demo rankings. 
At the far end, Turkey remains worse on the fundamentals front of than last week, as their 
dithering numbers place an incredible downward pressure on vessel prices. 
Overall, due to an uptick in both dry bulk and container charter rates at the start of this year,
there has been a relative slowdown in the number of available recycling candidates. However, 
we have witnessed a slight increase in the inflow of (HKC) tonnage over these last couple of
weeks as markets level off, and India has been the chief beneficiary of this over these units.

MARKET COMMENTARY

BANGLADESH 
LARGELY ABSENT! 
Bangladesh has been largely absent from the buying over the last few weeks due to 
ongoing Ramadan and Eid holidays. 
Levels have declined by about USD 50/LDT since their peaks seen earlier this year,
leaving levels well below the ongoing expectation of Owners and Cash Buyers alike. 
Fundamentals do remain shaky as steel plate trading came to a complete halt while the
markets remained off during Ramadan, coupled with a Taka that weakened past BDT 
107.2X, ending the week at BDT 106.40 against the U.S. Dollar. 
Finally, despite the inclusion of several new yards in Bangladesh on the class NK HKC 
list, the supply of vessels into Chattogram has dwindled of late as the focus has instead 
redirected towards Alang where several HKC deals were concluded this week.

INDIA 
Tonnage flows. 
 
DEALS DONE! 
As tonnage flow increases after various holidays have concluded across the globe, several 
deals were concluded into India this week. 
MSC has concluded yet another unit for resale to a limited number of HKC yards as the 
MSC LUCIA (8,911 LDT) was committed for a decent USD 530/LT LDT. Additionally, the 
Chinese built MR tanker MARITIME LIRA (12,235 LDT) managed to fetch USD 515/LT 
LDT basis a recycling resale restricted to 10 local yards only, in addition to strict 
monitoring – further highlighting an increased commitment of Vessel Owners towards 
responsible recycling. 
On the domestic fundamentals front, local steel plate prices fell another (marginal) USD
5/Ton, while the Indian Rupee further firmed and has been trading in the high Rs. 81s
against the U.S. Dollar, as demand and rates slowly improve for the week, inching India 
closer to Bangladesh. 
Unfortunately, Alang’s waterfront has been subject to the effects of the lack of tonnage
through the previous weeks, as not a single new vessel arrived local anchorage during the 
course of the week.

GMS Weekly 
MILES OFF! 
Despite the conclusion of Eid holidays and with it, the end of the Holy month of Ramadan, 
there has been little change to the status quo in Pakistan whilst Gadani Recyclers remain 
miles from the action. 
As it stands, the Pakistani Central Bank remains even less likely than the Bangladeshi
Central Bank to approve any forthcoming L/C openings, due to the ongoing and dire lack 
of U.S. Dollar reserves in the country. 
This has further reflected dreadfully at the waterfront as no new vessels have arrived in
Pakistan since March 03rd and the only vessel that has, remains stranded outside. 
Even fundamentals remain precarious as the Pakistani Rupee is still traversing in excess of 
PKR 283 against the U.S. Dollar, all while local steel plate prices continue to flatline, week 
after week in Pakistan.  
As a result of the ongoing volatility, vessel prices remain some ways off even India, and it 
therefore remains unlikely that any deals can or will be concluded into Gadani in the 
foreseeable future.

DEGRADING? 
The Turkish market has been gradually slipping (from a fundamentals perspective), week
after week. 
Local steel plate prices fell by about USD 10/Ton whilst import steel declined by about 
USD 15/Ton, all while the Lira continues its unending drag down quicksand lane passing
TRY 19.40 against the U.S. Dollar – as an overall downward trajectory registers in Turkey 
for several weeks in a row now. 
Meanwhile, the omnipresent problem of the shortage of tonnage has not spared the 
Turkish market either, as the bitter absence of vessels has kept local Buyers hungry / 
desperate enough to have maintained their vessel prices despite key fundamentals 
degrading on a weekly basis. 
Overall, it certainly feels as though an inevitable degrading of this market is forthcoming,
especially if the absence of tonnage continues.
