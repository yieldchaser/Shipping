# Market Commentary

## HOLIDAY SEASON!

With holidays underway across much of the world, it has been a far quieter week in terms of sales & activity, whilst sentiments and pricing remain further muted as a result.

Several vessels have arrived in Bangladesh for delivery this week despite it taking much longer these days to get the L/Cs in order. As such, to have smooth boardings & beachings, it is therefore recommended to maintain at least five days prior arrival, in order to allow for local Buyers L/Cs to open, release, and be credited to Owners / Cash Buyers before vessels can beach.

Particularly in Bangladesh, the situation remains somewhat precarious in obtaining Central Bank Approval on the particularly higher value assets, so drastic remains the ongoing liquidity problem in the country.

In Pakistan, it has been nearly impossible to open any L/Cs and neither Owners nor Cash Buyers are considering Gadani as a viable destination, especially in the new future.

Finally, Turkey continues its dreary drudge through the year, week after week, with not much change to report other than local steel plate prices that have been fluctuating within a margin and a determined Lira that is steadily deteriorating against the U.S. Dollar.

Overall, pursuant to the conclusion of Chinese New Year holidays, there was an improvement in both Dry Bulk and Containers for trading / charter, and this has led to a dearth in vessels being proposed for recycling and arriving at most waterfronts, as port reports remained virtually empty across the sub-continent & Turkey through most of March.

Notwithstanding, recycling prices remain relatively steady / strong, as do local sentiments, which are expected to pick up further after the holidays this month, as demand increases and fundamentals stabilizes in the form of steel prices and most local currencies.

# Bangladesh

## FACING DELAYS!

*Margin notes: Added time. | Still positive.*

Several vessels have faced delays upon arrival in Chattogram, due to the added time it is now taking to get Central Bank Approval to get L/Cs open, particularly on the larger LDT units.

As a result, tides are now being missed, with Cash Buyers and Owners left frustrated at these ongoing delays. It is therefore recommended for Owners / Cash Buyers of arriving tonnage to be prepared to wait additional times when delivering vessels into Bangladesh.

Fundamentals remain weak, yet steady, as the Bangladeshi Taka firms towards BDT 106 against the U.S. Dollar, while local steel plate prices flatline on the back of virtually no new arrivals / deliveries through March.

This week however, a series of arrivals does indicate there may be some movement in the markets and more units may be introduced going forward, given how painfully absent any tonnage has been of late.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# India

## READY AND WAITING!

*Margin notes: Improvements. | Holiday lull.*

Amidst a ‘less-than-often’ phase of stability for local fundamentals overall, both local steel plate prices and the Indian Rupee sailed through the week on an even-to-positive scale.

Not only did local steel plate prices diminish by less than USD 1/Ton this week, but the Rupee also continued to gradually firm past Rs. 82, down to Rs. 81.8X against the U.S. Dollar as the week ended.

Meanwhile, India also remains the leading market for (especially) HKC green vessels, despite the addition of more class NK yards in Bangladesh, yet the container sector has seen an uptick in chartering levels leading to many vessels being withdrawn from recycling sales.

As fundamentals remain firm, we should get some greater clarity on market conditions, pricing, and demand overall, after the various bank holidays are over from next week onwards.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# Pakistan

## CHRONIC ISSUES!

*Margin notes: Uncompetitive. | Little point.*

Chronic financing abilities persist for yet another week in Pakistan, and there is little getting over the fact that levels remain far from competitive, and banks remain far from willing to open L/Cs on any available vessels.

Even the unit arrived a few weeks ago is languishing at the waterfront, with rumors that the vessel is still awaiting opening of an L/C.

As such, the Gadani market remains non-event, particularly with the month of Ramadan ongoing and demand down as a result.

Like Bangladesh, local steel plate prices flatlined through much of the week, while the Pakistani Rupee ended the week at a massive PKR 286 against the U.S. Dollar.

Until Pakistan can sort out its liquidity issues and find ways to pay Owners / Cash Buyers alike on arriving vessels, and until levels improve in line with both India and Bangladesh, there remains little point discussing vessels into Pakistan for the time being.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# Turkey

## NO CHANGE!

*Margin notes: Fundamentals weaken. | Spring rejuvenation*

Turkey, like India, remains steadily fluctuating in its fundamentals, although in the opposite direction.

The Turkish Lira has been gradually weakening against the U.S. Dollar as it sails past TRY 19.25 as the week ended. Even local steel plate prices dropped by about USD 10/Ton while import steel fell by about USD 5/Ton, which seem more along the lines of weekly fluctuations than anything more serious.

With the world of ship recycling starved of tonnage, Aliaga was no different as Turkish Recyclers continue to starve for tonnage, the much-awaited Spring rejuvenation has yet to arrive, much to local Buyers dismay.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**
