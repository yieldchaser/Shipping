## 1. Market Overview

### Highlights
- Price cool.
- Market peak?
- Above 600/LDT.
- Supply slows.
- L/C hopes.

### Editorial: Sub-continent markets appear to be more tentatively poised this week, with a cooling demand  and lower levels that are increasingly reflective of domestic steel prices and have likely peaked.

Several sales were concluded at impressive numbers over the space of one or (maximum) two weeks before prices started to decline again by about USD 20/LDT, returning to the relative normalities of recent times.

That being said, prices are still trading above USD 600/LDT in Bangladesh and are not too far from that in India, with only Pakistan (and even Turkey) lagging significantly behind and out of the reckoning for another week.

As far as Turkey goes, other than the weekly fluctuation in steel plate prices and the Lira depreciating, not a whole lot has changed with this market that has been deprived of tonnage for a while now.

Overall, the supply of tonnage also seems to have slowed, which is encouraging for Cash Buyers with existing inventories in order to get their desired levels and not distracted by the supposed deluge of tonnage that many ship recyclers have been fearing will greet their shores.

Indeed, container charter rates have picked up from their lows seen earlier this year and so too have dry bulk levels enjoyed a rise in rates, which will see vessels from these two sectors arrive at a slower overall pace over the immediate future.

This will (hopefully) allow both Bangladeshi and Pakistani markets to sort out their chronic L/C financing issues, with it still taking much longer in order to open L/Cs and obtain bank approvals (often if going through state central government banks no approvals are forthcoming at all for the time being).

## 3. Country Market Intelligence

### Bangladesh

- **Headline**: DOWN A TOUCH!
- **Key Notes**: *Concluding sales.* | *Need to up prices.*

As the leading market of the ship recycling industry, much of the sales activity has been focused into Bangladesh of late, and some tempting sales have taken place on some choice tonnage over the last few weeks.

However, much of that demand appears to have died down a hair this week, off the back of declining steel plate prices (that fell about USD 25/Ton), and the second category of Buyers are at lower levels. Even the Bangladeshi Taka weakened slightly, ending the week at BDT 106.50 against the U.S. Dollar.

Overall, the trouble for the market is that the supply of vessels has also eased up, so those hoping to still get their hands-on tonnage will have to up their prices just to secure.

### India

- **Headline**: CANNOT REST ON LAURELS!

India remains slightly behind Bangladesh in their bidding on the dwindling supply of ships for recycling and need to be aware of resting on their laurels to avoid missing out on their usually guaranteed supply of HKC green tonnage.

This is because three yards in Bangladesh have obtained NK HKC certification with more seeking to upgrade their facilities similarly. As such, it may be that the advantage India currently holds could start to be increasingly challenged.

On the domestic fundamentals front, local steel plate prices firmed by about USD 10/Ton while the Indian Rupee still floats around the low Rs. 82 against the U.S. Dollar, providing some much-needed positivity to the local psyche.

Notwithstanding Bangladesh’s recent yard certification, certain owners still prefer an HKC sale into India only, but as more yards in the sub-continent are approved, those top level facilities will need to keep pushing themselves and achieving higher standards to keep the top, dark green candidates with them, especially as several container owners have fixed their units for select top yards in India so far this year – including one more private Panamax sized container only this week.

*Market Sales Reported*: **NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

### Pakistan

- **Headline**: SOME WAYS BACK!
- **Key Notes**: *Inert and unable.* | *Plots empty out.*

Pakistan remains some ways back from their Indian and Bangladeshi counterparts, and has largely been unable to perform on any vessels this year due to their lowest prices in the sub-continent, in addition to L/C restrictions, amidst an ongoing currency crisis and liquidity crunch across the country.

Of course, political uncertainty continues to reign, and it is unlikely we will see any serious sales taking place into Pakistan, with so much uncertainty and turmoil gripping much of the nation.

With little to no recycled material moving to domestic steel mills, local steel plate prices flatlined through the week as no material was being traded. Even the Pakistani Rupee collapsed to its lowest yet this week as the PKR hit 283.2X during the week.

Even as plots empty out and demand once again rises, prices being offered up are so far off their sub-continent competitors that there is little sense in even discussing units into Aliaga.

However, the fact that most in the industry are still distracted by the higher prices from neighboring competitors is that levels in the USD 500s/LDT are still historically impressive.

*Market Sales Reported*: **NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

---

### Turkey

- **Headline**: UNCHANGED!

As a recycling destination, even though levels are still nearly half of what they are in Bangladesh, the Turkish market has displayed an impressive degree of stability. Although import steel prices fell by about USD 10/Ton, local steel plate prices have remained relatively steady through the week.

Even the Turkish Lira, that has been depreciating steadily against the U.S. Dollar, has ended the week at TRY 19.0X.

There is still no confirmation on local fixtures and domestic yards have remained empty for much of the last few quarters.

Overall, little has changed in the Turkish market and the industry is still awaiting local fixtures into Aliaga.
