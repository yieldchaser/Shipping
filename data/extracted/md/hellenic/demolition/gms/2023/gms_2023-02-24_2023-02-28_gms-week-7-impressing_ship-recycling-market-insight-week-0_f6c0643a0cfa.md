## 1. Market Overview

### Highlights
- Bangla flies.

- L/C magic.
- Pakistan inert.

### Editorial: Market Commentary

Indeed, we have once again seen levels over USD 600/LDT in Bangladesh for the first time since the same time last year, and competing markets are increasingly struggling to keep up.

India has been trying its best too and has managed to secure a number of HKC green sales this year, but there is no competing with a rampant Chattogram market when they start to fire up, and it is now a case of ensuring L/Cs are in order before committing vessels at decent levels once again.

Of course, not every buyer is able to secure the requisite financing to open L/Cs, especially those that have to go through government/ state banks and are not able to transact government- controlled U.S. Dollar reserves on vessels for recycling.

Therefore, through private financing channels or with usance L/Cs, end buyers in Bangladesh are finding ways to secure much needed tonnage, with yards having virtually emptied over the last 6 - 8 months as the current L/C crisis rumbles on and markets declined from the peaks of over USD 700/LDT seen last year.

Pakistan regrettably remains sidelined for yet another week, neither competitive on price nor are local buyers able to come up with appropriate L/C financing to import vessels for the time being, resulting in a port report that has not welcomed any fresh tonnage for the last two weeks.

Finally, the Turkish market continues to firm up as both import and steel plate prices registered improvements this week, vessel prices climb another USD 20/Ton, and private sales have reportedly registered into this market.

## 3. Country Market Intelligence

### Bangladesh

- **Headline**: SOARING BACK!
- **Key Notes**: *Story of past weeks.* | *Container sold.*

The story over the last few weeks has revolved around the stunning Bangladeshi resurgence, securing several impressive sales over USD 600/LDT, and finding ways to open L/Cs and import vessels once again.

The ongoing IMF loan discussions are expected to bring in about USD 2 - 3 billion of foreign capital into the country, but these discussions have yet to be concluded and as such, end buyers have been busy finding alternate means of financing and have apparently been successful on select vessels through non-governmental banks.

On the local fundamentals front, local steel plate prices flatlined through the week once again, whilst the Taka breached past BDT 106.50 against the U.S. Dollar this week – all while sales and deliveries continues at pace into Bangladesh.

Finally, another sale managed to rack up this week, with a likely resale into Bangladesh, as the smaller 1984 built Tanto of Indonesia controlled container TANTO SENTOSA (2,726 LDT) managed to fetch a rather healthy USD 490/LT LDT basis an ‘as is’ Malaysia delivery – especially considering the smaller size of the vessel.

---

### India

- **Headline**: DUTY EXEMPTIONS!

Despite the firm performance of the Alang market for a majority of this year, local buyers have been struggling to keep up with the recently strong showing emanating from Bangladesh over these past few weeks and have lost several choice / quality units as a result.

There was however a boon for Indian buyers this week as news that customs duty and GST leviable on vessels for recycling, amounting to 2.5% on the value of imported vessel will be exempted.

While this should see prices push on in the coming weeks, just how competitive Indian buyers will be as compared to a rampant Bangladesh remains to be seen, especially while fundamentals remained relatively steady through the course of this week.

### Pakistan

- **Headline**: EXCLUDED!
- **Key Notes**: *Not workable.* | *Little point.*

Gadani recyclers have largely been excluded from the buying and offerings for most of this year, whilst they remain suspended without being to establish workable L/Cs, all while prices remain far from being competitive with their sub-continent neighbors.

Indeed, with Bangladesh pushing on so far on, Pakistan could find themselves almost USD 100/LDT off the pace, if and when they eventually get back to any sort of sensible / performance on the buying!

As it stands however, state banks are unwilling and unable to sanction fresh U.S. Dollar L/Cs, so there remains a moot point of discussing new vessels into Gadani, regardless of the continued uncompetitive pricing.

There maybe some hope on the frontline however as the PKR continues to firm, dropping just below the PKR 260 market against the U.S. Dollar, all while domestic steel plate prices (like the Bangladeshi market) flatlined for all of the week.

*Market Sales Reported*: **NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

---

### Turkey

- **Headline**: SPRING JUMP?

The Turkish market has reportedly jumped once again this week, as both import and steel plate prices registered improvements. The TRY also continues to trade around the 18.8X mark and there have even been rumours of local fixtures this week into Turkey.

Of course, the momentum of positivity continues as the local market remains eager to take on fresh tonnage (especially after almost a year on the absolute side-lines), as local levels also jumped by about USD 20/MT.

As most deals being concluded are direct to end buyers, hence there is not much market talk on prospective units, however, we should have further news once the units arrive locally.

*Market Sales Reported*: **NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**
