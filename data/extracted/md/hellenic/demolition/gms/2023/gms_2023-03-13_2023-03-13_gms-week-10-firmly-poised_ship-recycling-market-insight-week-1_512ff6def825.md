"When it comes to luck, you make your own."

Recycling markets remain firmly poised for another week, following a stunning resurgence by 
the Bangladeshi market (in particular) that leaves them atop the price rankings board. 
Indeed, levels close to and even over USD 600/LDT are now being regularly presented on 
various vessels and this is inducing more Ship Owners to sell their vessels, especially as freight 
rates in the dry bulk and container sectors are failing to improve sufficiently for Owners to keep 
holding onto their aging tonnage. 
Cape rates have come back this past week, leaving question marks over whether the larger LDT 
dry units will hit recycling markets any time soon. Overall, container and dry bulk chartering
levels are still sufficiently down from the previous few restorative years. 
Tankers are pretty much assured not to come for recycling anytime soon - especially as wet 
tonnage continues to earn impressively high numbers after the last few years of bloodletting 
and pain. 
On the local markets front, after an extremely barren 8 months or so in Bangladesh, it is good 
to see the market there back firing on all cylinders and some excellent numbers have been seen 
in recent weeks, particularly on various LNGS, Capes, and Containers. 
India lags a rampant Bangladesh by some ways but is still competitive on the raft of green HKC 
vessels that continue to be made available, and sentiments are indeed firming again in Alang 
off the back of a repeal in import duty on floating assets for recycling. 
Pakistan is stranded far from any meaningful activity – both struggling with L/Cs and 
competitive pricing, so there is little sense talking vessels into a marginalized Gadani at present.
Finally, the Turkish market has reported a softening of import steel rates as the Lira plummets 
to a new record low approaching TRY 19.X against the U.S. Dollar.

MARKET COMMENTARY

BANGLADESH 
TAKING TONNAGE! 
After a raft of recent sales – including a Sinokor LNG and several high-priced Capes &
Containers, it was a slightly slower paced week in terms of activity this time around, 
although sentiments and pricing remain extremely positive in Bangladesh. 
Indeed, offers on select vessels are in excess of USD 600/LDT for the first time in almost 
a year, and this has encouraged several owners to cash in on their vintage tonnage. 
The question of workable L/Cs (Letters of Credit) still remains a sensitive one, but the
IMF discussions on loans, which is set to inject billions into the country and ease liquidity
issues, is still ongoing.  
Notwithstanding, end Buyers have managed to secure alternate means of financing 
(including personal / private source of funds and Usance L/Cs) to ensure Cash Buyers get 
paid on their incoming tonnage. 
On the fundamentals front, the Taka remains steady just shy of BDT 106 against the U.S. 
Dollar, whilst local steel plate prices seem to have flatlined for much of the week. 
Finally, in further local developments, reportedly two more Chattogram recycling yards
have obtained class NK HKC certification – a testament to the hard work and desire to 
improve and upgrade standards locally that has led us to this impressive feat.

INDIA 
Second placed. 
 
KEEN TO COMPETE! 
As the second placed recycling destination, it was unsurprising to see a quieter week in 
Alang as Bangladeshi recyclers continue to steal the limelight with the best priced deals. 
The Indian Rupee is trading in the low Rs. 82s against the U.S. Dollar and local steel plate 
prices (though marginally stronger this week) seem to have settled after a volatile period, 
with extreme fluctuations seen by the day, often fraying end user nerves / shredding 
confidence. 
India remains in the hunt for any available vessels – particularly favoured containers or 
HKC green sales, of which there have been several already this year after one of the lowest 
years for recycling volumes during 2022.

GMS Weekly 
UNABLE & UNWILLING! 
Another dour week of inaction has passed in Gadani and there seems little hope of any 
serious activity taking place on offers or sales, all whilst fundamentals remain in the 
shambles they currently are. 
For starters, Pakistani Buyers are unable to open L/Cs to import vessels and until there is 
greater liquidity / willingness to sanction financing on ships for recycling, it would be 
premature to expect any sales taking place into Gadani. 
Furthermore, prices remain well off from what Bangladesh and even India are paying, so 
Pakistan is both unable and unwilling to acquire tonnage for recycling at present. This is 
also evident via local port reports, where it has been about a month since any fresh units 
have arrived the local waterfront. 
Adding fuel to the local fire are domestic fundamentals, with local steel declining about 
USD 15/Ton and the Pakistani Rupee performing even worse than before, as it stands at 
the doorstep of PKR 280 against the U.S. Dollar. 
As such, local confidence remains in shambles as fundamentals are simply not helping the 
Gadani ship recycling community.

Minimal activity to report from the Turkish market this week as there is still no news of 
fixtures (market of private) that are emanating from Aliaga. 
Local steel plate prices reportedly declined by about USD 15/Ton this week, while the
