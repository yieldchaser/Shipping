## 1. Market Overview

### Highlights
- Steel pressure.
- Charter turn.
- Supply slows.
- L/Cs ease.
- IMF hopes.

### Editorial: Market Commentary

An improvement in Container and Dry Bulk charter rates has reduced the flow of candidates for recycling over the last couple of weeks and so much of the firming local demand may still go unrealized for some time.

Indeed, there has only been a trickle of vessels for sale in this last week, and so only a few Owners appear to have cashed in at these higher rates, perhaps at the peak of the market.

As such, given the diminishing supply and firming demand, prices continue to impress, particularly in a Bangladeshi market that has essentially been starved of tonnage over the last 6 - 8 months due to persistent L/C issues. Alternate methods of financing have been established (including usance L/Cs and private financing solutions) to secure some of the recent tonnage for Chattogram Buyers.

Yet, the current preference amongst local Recyclers (given the ongoing L/C restrictions) remains in trying to acquire smaller LDT tonnage, rather than the costlier large LDT vessels that may not get L/C approval – so dire is the shortage of U.S. Dollars in the country at present.

Talks over ongoing IMF loans continue in both Pakistan and Bangladesh, and once that is finalized, we should see some greater liquidity on L/Cs and the ability for all Recyclers in these markets to secure larger LDT vessels at firmer levels.

Finally, Turkey at the far end remains healthy and steady, with no changes reported in steel and still no reports on the mysterious negotiations that were reportedly taking place over recent weeks.

## 3. Country Market Intelligence

### Bangladesh

- **Headline**: FOCUS FALLS!
- **Key Notes**: *Impressive sales.* | *L/C solutions.*

Over the past few weeks, much of the recent focus has fallen on a rampant Bangladeshi market and some impressive sales have taken place before steel prices started to show signs of reversals / flatlining in recent days.

However, this should not put too much of a dampener on local prices, with demand still ripe and End Buyers eagerly looking to fill plots that have remained largely empty for the last half a year (at least).

Many of the ongoing L/C issues appear to be easing up gradually, particularly for the larger appetite Buyers who are able to arrange private financing on various vessels, and this is where the majority of recent sales have ended up.

Finally, whilst local steel plate prices did flatline for the much of the week, the Bangladeshi Taka approached BDT 106.75 before settling in the low BDT 106 against the U.S. Dollar for the week.

### India

- **Headline**: OFF PACE!

Declining steel plate prices (by nearly USD 12/Ton) and a depreciating currency (that saw the Indian Rupee approach Rs. 83 before settling in the mid Rs. 82 against the U.S. Dollar) have seen the jitters return to Indian Buyers and they have fallen some ways off the pace from the leading Chattogram market.

When Alang was the highest placed market in January, several HKC green vessels were introduced for sale and Alang Recyclers successfully secured several units, given their sub- continent monopoly on the number of HKC certified yards.

However, this appears to have slowed in the last month as the supply of green vessels has slowed down.

Moreover, three Bangladeshi yards are now HKC accredited to offer up some healthy competition on green tonnage, which further places increased competition and opportunities for Owners and Cash Buyers alike.

Overall, it has been a rather quiet week in Alang, stretching over onto the waterfront as well, where only one fresh arrival has been reported this week.

*Market Sales Reported*: **NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

---

### Pakistan

- **Headline**: GENERAL INABILITY!
- **Key Notes**: *Willing but unable.* | *Little point.*

There remains an ongoing willingness from Gadani Buyers to acquire tonnage, however, a general inability to do so permeates through the domestic ship recycling industry, given the ongoing L/C restrictions that remain in place and the overall lack of liquidity in the country.

There are also severe political distractions still across the country (including the ongoing arrest saga of former PM Imran Khan), whilst prices remain nowhere near their Indian and Bangladeshi counterparts.

Like Bangladesh, as local steel plate prices continue to flatline and lament, the Pakistani Rupee keeps shifting farther into oblivion as it remains suspended above PKR 280 against the U.S. Dollar.

On the waterfront, even though a vessel arrived Gadani last week week, it remains an isolated case as Pakistan has been entirely devoid of meaningful tonnage for over a month now.

As such, there is little point discussing the paucity of market units currently into Gadani, and it may be some time before we see them back in the picture with a stable currency and permission from the central state bank to spend precious U.S. Dollars on available vessels.

*Market Sales Reported*: **NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

---

### Turkey

- **Headline**: SILENT!!

Another week of silence and minimal activity has been reported from the Turkish market for yet another week.

While local steel plate prices (both import and local) remained reportedly stable throughout the week, the Turkish Lira has been on an entirely different trajectory as it blew past TRY 19 against the U.S. Dollar (an all-time record low).

On the sales front, there is still no news of any local fixtures and Aliaga’s waterfront reportedly remains silent as well.

As such, as levels from the sub-continent continue to firm and so does demand, it only becomes harder for Owners to consider Turkey, and for Turkish Buyers to compete with sub-continent levels.

*Market Sales Reported*: **NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**
