“Belief creates the actual fact.” 
 
 
 
– William James 
As we close out another year, sub-continent markets remain suspended at their weakest point 
for some time now. Pricing remains firm (given the historical price average has been around
USD 350/LDT or so), but sentiments and conditions on the ground across all locations remain 
somewhat precarious. 
While Bangladesh has been the main proponent of such concern – given the ongoing financial 
crisis that has resulted in the Bangladeshi government restricting the sanctioning of new Letters 
of Credit (L/Cs) for even the smallest purchases of USD 1 million and below – this week, the 
Pakistani government threw its hat in the ring as well, restricting a bevy of L/Cs being 
sanctioned on recent local purchases. 
Only very few Bangladeshi buyers with private financing and Usance L/Cs are able to acquire 
new units, but even so, many end users seem content to take a break and watch market 
developments before committing afresh, with prices so uncertain. 
The picture in India remains slightly rosier, even though levels have cooled off some USD 
200/LDT since the peaks of over USD 700/LDT seen earlier this year. On the far end, Turkey 
spent another slow week devoid of tonnage, but reported marginal improvements in import 
and local steel – without much fanfare. 
Overall, it seems many end buyers are likely still cautious, given the sustained ferocity of the 
falls, and having filled their plots with more expensive units at the start of the year, are now 
troubled as to when to buy anew to even out those losses. 
Port reports have been a testament to the fact that there have been very few arrivals over the 
last 6 months and only now that tonnage flow is starting to increase are we seeing some 
movement in those markets that are able to import new vessels at some increasingly bullish 
numbers.

MARKET COMMENTARY

BANGLADESH 
DORMANT! 
No market sales of any significance have reportedly taken place into Bangladesh over the 
last few months of 2022, and the outlook indeed remains somewhat bleak unless a swift 
and steady injection of cash can be made to free up funds for acquiring new units. 
The heavily anticipated IMF loan may bring some stability early next year, but it is still
uncertain to what extent this will help prop up the market again, given that fundamentals 
here are still shaky. 
The Bangladeshi Taka briefly skipped past 105 against the U.S. Dollar, only to end the 
week at BDT 102.30. Even local steel plate prices, which remained flat for most of the
week, did find the opportunity to fall back about USD 8/Ton before the end of the week.
With an increase of vessels set to potentially hit the market (particularly in the dry bulk 
and container sectors) next year, this would be a good time to kickstart a presently 
dormant industry back to life.

PAYBACK! 
Many HKC sales (particularly from blue chip container owners) have been focused on 
Alang yards of late, who are finally seeing the fruits of their HKC dedication and 
investments into improving yards and safety standards that are finally bearing fruit. 
None of the recent array of vessels sold can go to Pakistan or even the few HKC approved 
yards in Bangladesh, and this is of great advantage to an Alang market that is struggling 
on prices at the moment and the fundamentals aren’t doing much to help either. 
Volatility of local steel prices continues to play with the local mindset as levels dropped 
by about USD 10/Ton, only to have climbed USD 15/Ton by the weekend. Even the 
currency continues to trade in the high Rs. 82s against the U.S. Dollar, leaving End Buyers 
reluctant to even pitch in on an increasing number of new sales at firm and workable 
market numbers.

GMS Weekly 
HOPE...SHATTERED?! 
Gadani remains the top priced market as we close out the year and End Buyers have finally 
been able to commit to fill idle plots with a variety of mostly dry bulk sales, or at least 
attempted to as this week, news finally broke that Pakistan’s central bank is not opening 
any fresh L/Cs for local recyclers – which is catastrophic to the industry, especially as 
vessels now start to arrive the local waterfront. 
Several capes and Panamax bulkers have been sold to local Buyers who finally seemed 
keen to make the most of a market that has slid into the New Year and with levels in the 
low 500s/LDT (down about USD 200/LDT from the highs earlier in the year). 
Like with global markets, fundamentals here remain weak as well as plate prices flatlined 
this week, whilst the Pakistani Rupee continues to deteriorate by the week against the U.S. 
Dollar as it passes PKR 225.50 
As such, while we had hoped Pakistan would look on with renewed hope towards 2023, 
perhaps the worst woes of 2022 are not entirely behind them.

2023 HOPES? 
Just as the Pakistani market felt like it would have made it across the New Year with fresh 
sales and optimism to back, but have sadly taken 3 steps back, the Turkish market seems 
to be in a similar position. 
Although local steel plate prices have finally made some improvements this week, a case 
of “too little too late” seems to have permeated in Aliaga as well, as no real movement in 
prices has been noted for some months now. Even the Lira – like the PKR – is dangling at 
its lowest against the U.S. Dollar (around TRY 18.6). 
As such, we can only hope this market makes a return to form so that at least EU based 
tonnage can find a home at a yard in Turkey.
