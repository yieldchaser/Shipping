# Market Commentary

## BACK IN BIZ?

This week, several more sales have registered as sub-continent ship recycling markets come roaring back – and a previously subdued Bangladesh starts to seemingly find some form once again, with some workable L/Cs from privately financed (rather than state controlled) banks.

Despite the ongoing discussions about an IMF loan in the amount of about USD 2 - 3 billion, end buyers have been working to find alternate ways to pay for vessels for some time now and without the troublesome central bank approval, which is denying the disbursements of U.S. Dollars for everything but essential items.

Demand has seems hotter in both Pakistan and Bangladesh this week, where they have been unable to import vessels for most of this year and it is good to see several deals being concluded off late, and at firming numbers to match.

India remains on the hunt for tonnage but has failed to keep up with a resurgent Bangladesh this week and will have to raise their respective game up once again, just to keep in contention to secure any available vessels.

Finally, Turkey records a small drop in import steel prices whilst levels for ships hold firm for the past month now and still no news of any market fixtures coming forth.

As the industry heads into March, the supply of tonnage has started to increase as dry (particularly Capesize bulker) rates suffer, and containers likewise show few signs of picking up just yet.

As such, after a year of reduced supply and tumbling prices, we can finally see the recycling markets starting to resume fire again, and we should expect further impressive sales and pricing in the weeks ahead.

# Bangladesh

## RESURGENT!

*Margin notes: Highest for a year. | Stunning sales.*

Having secured some hugely priced sales – not seen since this time last year, Bangladesh has enjoyed a stunning resurgence to finish atop of the price rankings this week!

The most notable sale of the week concerned that of the Sinokor controlled container XIUMEI SHANGHAI (7,276 LDT), which was fixed for an astonishing USD 640/LT LDT. Despite having a decent sized propeller, bow thruster, and about 300 Tons of bunkers included in the sale – this fixture still appears to be one well above the market and it will be interesting to see how the resale progresses!

The Panamax bulker VADI (10,085 LDT) was also finalised from Turkish owners for a decent USD 575/LDT – with about 300 Tons bunkers and a spare propeller contributing to the firmer price on show.

On the fundamentals front, despite a firming demand and local fixtures, fundamentals have in fact traded in the opposite direction (i.e., deflated) this week as the Bangladeshi Taka drops towards BDT 106 against the U.S. Dollar. Even local steel plate prices dropped a healthy USD 41/Ton, as an increasing number of vessels arrives the waterfront over the last couple of weeks.

# India

## PICKING UP!

*Margin notes: Second placed. | Deals done.*

As the second placed sub-continent market, India continues to pick up tonnage by virtue of workable L/Cs and a more reliable performance from local Recyclers, along with an increased flow of vessels that are headed for HKC green recycling.

Such was the case again this week, as the LNG carrier SEAPEK ARCTIC (23,667 LDT), was concluded for a massive USD 15 million basis an ‘as is’ Malaysia sale and for select yards & for strictly HKC recycling only.

Another container was also committed this week as the OEL SHRAVAN (7,281 LDT) fetched a decent USD 587/LT LDT basis an ‘as is’ Colombo, in the second sale from these owners this year.

Meanwhile, domestic fundamentals continue to support the local mindset as the Rupee continues to bobble around the mid Rs. 82s against the U.S. Dollar, while Indian steel plate prices firmed up another USD 10/Ton this week, keeping Bangladeshi Buyers on their toes for prospective competitive units.

# Pakistan

## LOOKING ON!

*Margin notes: Envious. | Lacking LC approval.*

Pakistan has only been able to look on enviously as competing markets have surged past and been able to acquire some decent tonnage at firming rates this week.

The main problem in Pakistan has been unworkable L/Cs and a lack of state / government bank approvals for end buyers to open fresh L/Cs and import vessels for recycling.

Adding to the state of affairs has been a steel plate price that has been unchanged this week (likely due to the utter absence of tonnage at the waterfront), in addition to a Pakistani Rupee, though marginally firmer since last week, is sitting disheveled at nearly PKR 264 – not a particularly pleasant state for domestic fundamentals.

Finally, even deals that had been previously concluded into Gadani have since failed to deliver as L/C opening banks have rejected fresh L/Cs due to the severe shortage of U.S. Dollars in the country, and as such it has been another bleak and miserable week for Pakistani ship recycling.

# Turkey

## SILENT WEEK!

*Margin notes: Fundamentals drop. | Blue-chip tonnage.*

After the devastating earthquake in Turkey and Syria that has reportedly resulted in the untimely passing of over 20,000 souls, it was a relatively quiet week in Aliaga with not much activity being reported.

Steel plate prices did drop by about USD 10/Ton this week (likely on the back of diminished business dealings) as the Turkish Lira continues to buoy around a slightly weaker TRY 18.8X against the U.S. Dollar.

With not much dry tonnage being a candidate for Aliaga based recycling and sub-continent levels that are still twice as much as those from Turkey, it seems private blue-chip tonnage are the only units that seem likely to be Turkish candidates (if at all), for some time.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**
