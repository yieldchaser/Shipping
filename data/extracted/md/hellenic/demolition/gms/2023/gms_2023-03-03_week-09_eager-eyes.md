# Market Commentary

## EAGER EYES!

A strong showing from nearly all of the major recycling markets over the last few weeks has left a far rosier picture (especially in India, Bangladesh, and even Turkey) and has encouraged a few more Sellers to market their vessels for sale for recycling.

Indeed, several sales market (and reportedly even private units) have also been concluded for the week, including another large LDT LNG (rich in non-ferrous) and another Capesize Bulker.

However, it has been a good week for dry charter rates (Capes in particular) with some healthy gains posted to alleviate some of the doom and gloom witnessed over the last few weeks. Recycling markets can therefore rest easy that the hitherto expected deluge of tonnage is unlikely to materialize any time soon.

Overaged containers and dry bulk units are still the most likely to head for recycling, amidst a healthy outlook in the tanker / wet sector at last, but the recent bullishness displayed in freight sectors has overall put a bit of a dampener on the expected output of supply.

On the local markets front, India has done away with the GST tax on the import of ships for recycling, in order to improve margins for End Buyers by about 2.5% and this has already started to reflect in the local markets that are showing greater positivity this week.

Bangladesh has been the main proponent of the surging markets for the week, with prices pushing on and sentiments soaring as demand has built up after a fairly inactive 6 - 8 months. This is in contrast to a glum Pakistani market, where L/C approvals are struggling even more than Bangladesh, whereas the Bangladeshi market is starting to ease up somewhat on L/Cs for select Buyers with private banking means for financing on fresh vessels.

At the far end, the Turkish market spent a week of inactivity, with steady plate prices (import and local), strong offerings, and still no confirmation on local fixtures.

# Bangladesh

## GRABBING HEADLINES!

*Margin notes: Bagging deals. | LNG sold.*

Bangladesh continues to steal the headlines for another week, with a couple of showstopping larger LDT purchases, casting off the negativity that had engulfed this market for much of last year.

Demand has steadily grown as Chattogram Buyers have struggled with L/C financing on fresh purchases, but recent efforts by local Recyclers to obtain alternate / private means of financing and switching to Usance L/Cs is finally starting to pay dividends with more units being concluded of late.

First up, the Sinokor controlled LNG ADRIATIC ENERGY (32,204 LDT) was concluded for USD 22 million (with 3,760 Tons of aluminium reportedly on board) in the latest LNG sale for recycling this year, whilst the Capesize bulker AGIA TRIAS (22,132 LDT) was fixed from Greek Owners at a decent USD 578/LT LDT basis an ‘as is’ Singapore delivery with sufficient bunkers for the voyage.

On the local fundamentals front, local steel plate prices also had an impressive showing this week as levels jumped nearly USD 56/Ton while the Taka depreciated past BDT 106 several times this week, finally ending the week at said level.

# India

## MISSING OUT!

*Margin notes: Customs exemptions. | Stranded.*

India has missed out once again on market tonnage to a surging Bangladeshi market for yet another week – even on a favoured LNG - rich in non-ferrous and aluminium - which tends to find a home in Alang more often than not!

This is all despite news that customs duty and GST leviable on vessels for recycling, amounting to 2.5% on the value of imported vessels, will be exempted.

Despite this recent financial benefit, end Buyers could not turn that to their advantage, and despite some lower priced green HKC sales this year, it looks set to be a struggle for Alang Buyers in the immediate future, all while prices remain stranded so far from Bangladesh.

Fundamentally, India has finally seen a stronger showing on the Rupee, which has appreciated to Rs. 81.66 (down from nearly Rs. 83) against the U.S. Dollar, while steel plate prices firmed a marginal USD 4/Ton this week.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# Pakistan

## AVOIDING!

*Margin notes: Miles off. | Stability needed.*

Pakistan is off once again, miles away from the lower priced Indian market and as such, there is simply no chance of Gadani Buyers being able to compete with a soaring Bangladeshi market as well!

Overall, Gadani Buyers are still struggling to achieve the requisite L/C approvals to import vessels, and once again have had performance issues on various deliveries where things have not gone smoothly, with some vessels even rumored to being diverted prior arrival Gadani.

Fundamentally, the Pakistani Rupee is worse off after briefly firming to about PKR 261, it depreciated to a record PKR 285.2X, finally ending the week PKR 275.8X against the U.S. Dollar. Making matters worse, a likely lack of local trading of ship’s steel has resulted in steel plate prices flatlining through the week, given the lack of any fresh arrivals over several weeks now.

It is therefore of little surprise that Owners and Cash Buyers are avoiding the Pakistani market for the time being, at least until some sort of stability and confidence in the buying can be seen.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# Turkey

## UNCHANGED!

*Margin notes: Quiet week. | ‘Spring fever’*

It has been a quiet week in Turkey this week, with not much activity to report. There has still not been any confirmation of local sales to Aliaga Buyers this week, and no further news of further discussions on fresh units either.

There has also been no reported change in import and local steel plate prices, neither has the Turkish Lira changed must beyond the TRY 18.8X levels it has been sailing through, over the last few weeks.

As such, the much anticipated ‘Spring fever’ has barely materialized, although with the market still steady, we can expect improvements moving forward.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**
