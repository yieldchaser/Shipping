Amidst the ongoing slowdown in the supply of tonnage and the onset of the traditionally 
quieter summer / monsoon months that would certainly help ease off the demand, prices across 
the sub-continent markets remained steady for another week. 
There is also the impending budget on June 1st in Bangladesh and many End Buyers are waiting 
to see if any new changes / taxes are imposed on the domestic ship recycling sector before 
offering afresh on tonnage. 
However, the general consensus on the upcoming budget is that no changes are expected and 
that it may actually be positive on the overall business outlook in Bangladesh, amidst a growing 
demand for steel and raw materials due to various upcoming construction and infrastructure 
projects that are due in the summer months. 
Additionally, the recent meeting in Bangladesh on whether to ratify the Hong Kong convention 
produced some interesting discussions and outcomes, with the country amending its ship 
recycling standard from ‘Red’ to ‘Orange’, on the environmental outlook for the country. The 
next step is for flags to ratify the convention before it can be entered into force, in what could 
be a landmark ruling in Bangladesh – the first sub-continent country to be actively doing so. 
Meanwhile, after a recent wobble in prices, India too has steadied and there is now an emerging 
demand as Buyers in Alang have missed out on a majority of the non-HKC (and reportedly, 
even some HKC) tonnage to the firmer Chattogram market. 
Pakistan remains on the sidelines, with political and economic chaos rendering this market 
virtually redundant and there is no realistic hope of the situation stabilizing any time soon. 
Finally, Turkey remains suspended in no-man’s land, as the situation remains unchanged 
from last week and the election results awaiting to be announced.

MARKET COMMENTARY

BANGLADESH 
WAKING UP! 
Over previous weeks, several (market and private) sales have taken place into 
Bangladesh, as this market appears to finally turn a corner post Ramadan and Eid 
holidays.  
Moreover, just this week, Japan Shipowners' Association (JSA) sent its delegates to 
Bangladesh, along with representatives from four major Japanese shipping companies as 
Bangladesh’s regulatory bodies have expressed their commitment to ratify HKC 
recycling processes and requested Japan's assistance in the further development of a
Treatment Storage Disposal Facility (TSDF). 
The Japanese delegation also visited multiple ship recycling facilities in Chattogram and 
had constructive dialogues with members of Bangladesh Ship Breakers and Recyclers 
Association (BSBRA), with emphasis on increasing the number of HKC compliant yards. 
As a result of this visit, Chattogram’s ship recycling industry has been re-categorized 
from ‘Red’ to ‘Orange’ code on the environmental outlook, which will assure further
investments in yard infrastructures.  
On the sales front, no fresh market sales registered for the week, as the supply of tonnage 
remains abysmal, especially as we enter the usually quieter monsoon season – when
much of the seasonal labour returns home. 
As fundamentals continue to jiggle as the weeks go by where the Bangladeshi Taka still 
trades in excess of BDT 107.10 against the U.S. Dollar and local steel plate prices flatline 
through the week once again, all eyes will be on the upcoming budget of June 1st next
week – whereby even though few changes are expected to impact the ship recycling 
sector – many Buyers are being cautious in the event of unexpected changes.

INDIA 
Bangla secures. 
MISSING OUT (ON HKC)! 
India has recently missed out on some HKC sales, with Bangladesh securing tonnage to 
some of the newly approved Class NK HKC facilities (three now in total). 
Prices in India are a touch lower than Bangladesh and as many of the recent fixtures are 
coming in from the Far East, a lower delivery cost to Bangladesh (vs. India) helps ease 
some of the expenses as vessels head to Chattogram, especially as evidenced by the
number of arrivals in Bangaldesh over recent weeks. 
Fundamentals in India remain wobbly too, with steel prices enduring another shaky week
– down by about USD 5/Ton - whilst the currency finally settles back down towards Rs.
82.5X against the U.S. Dollar, keeping Alang Recyclers encouraged, despite the dwindling 
supply of vessels.

GMS Weekly 
UNFULFILLED DREAMS! 
Another completely lackluster week sees the Pakistani ship recycling sector devoid of any 
sales / activity and remains on the sidelines of the sub-continent ship recycling markets.
No progress has been likewise made on upgrading facilities (as Bangladeshi Recyclers have 
been of late) to adhere to HKC standards, something that could easily see the Pakistani 
market left behind in the long run. 
Understandably, the political and economic chaos that continues to beset the country has 
taken front and center stage, leaving the market in Gadani only able to hopelessly watch 
on as competing neighbors continues to take in units’ week after week. 
It has certainly been some time since any meaningful sales have taken place in Gadani and 
with port reports and plots virtually empty, there remains capacity and demand to acquire,
even though there may not be any realistic hope of fulfilling those yards (and dreams) any 
time soon. 
A lone supply vessel did greet Gadani’s anchorage this week and time will tell whether 
local banking and L/C processes have improved the chances of tonnage beaching in this 
market.
