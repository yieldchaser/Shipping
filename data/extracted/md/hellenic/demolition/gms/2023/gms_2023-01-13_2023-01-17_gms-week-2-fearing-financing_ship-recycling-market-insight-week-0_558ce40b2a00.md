### Editorial: FEARING FINANCING!

Financing remains of chief concern in both Bangladesh and Pakistan, with very few end users capable of opening fresh L/Cs on the import of vessels, although some are getting on by with private financing, which usually means and usance (rather than sight) L/Cs or higher interest rates.

The focus therefore falls on India for another week, on the few vessels that are available from larger Container Owners. Most of these are for HKC recycling only, and as such, would be heading to Alang anyway, regardless of the L/C concerns elsewhere.

With the Chinese New Year only about a week or so away, there is perhaps a shortage of candidates as owners wait to see if freight rates improve after China returns, particularly with its Zero-Covid policy having been recently abandoned and world economies continue to try getting back to normal.

If the mooted IMF loan to Bangladesh can finally be negotiated to ease the financial crisis there, we may see further competition from a traditionally bullish Chattogram market. Buyers in Pakistan are similarly keen to buy, but without L/C financing, it is currently very hard for Cash Buyers to get paid at present, so this is another wait-and-watch scenario for this market for now.

On the far end, Turkey continues to impress as plate prices improve and despite a continually weakening Lira, local prices continue to firm up, perhaps thanks to some of the Turkey only candidates that are rumored to be working basis an early Spring delivery.

## 3. Country Market Intelligence

### Bangladesh

- **Headline**: BANKING ON IMF!
- **Key Notes**: *Lacking LCs.* | *Bereft.*

Following another week of inability to open L/Cs, Bangladesh remains a market bereft of confidence, ideas, and tonnage.

Sentiments remain dull as even though end Buyers want to offer afresh on tonnage, they simply cannot under the present circumstances (restrictions).

Local steel plate prices continue to flatline, indicating bare minimum trading activity on the back of non-existent tonnage at local yards. The Bangladeshi Taka too continues to fluctuate violently between BDT 102 and BDT 106, currently having ended the week at BDT 104 against the U.S. Dollar.

As such, much will depend on the progress of the ongoing expectation that the IMF loan would likely inject some much-needed U.S. Dollars into the country, so that business and industry can resume as normal.

### India

- **Headline**: MARKET OF MOMENT!

As the go to market of the moment, Alang Buyers have been increasingly enquiring on the number of container vessels in the market that are largely for HKC green recycling.

Even for those units not intended for green recycling, given India’s financial reserves and ability to open fresh L/Cs, it may be the only destination in the sub-continent that will invariably be the final destination for recycling candidates in the foreseeable future.

On the fundamentals front, steel prices have improved over the course of the week by about USD 8/Ton, whilst the Rupee has gained some much-needed ground to finish trading in the mid Rs. 81s against the U.S. Dollar, having previously been nearly sniffing Rs. 83.

As such, most of the market activity and sales will be focussed on India for the time being, at least until Pakistan and Bangladesh sort their respective financial situations out.

### Pakistan

- **Headline**: STUNNED!
- **Key Notes**: *LC inability.* | *Alternatives.*

The Pakistani market has been stunned by the sudden inability of domestic Recyclers to open fresh L/Cs, after restrictions were recently imposed by the state bank on spending their precious foreign currency reserves of U.S. Dollars on ships for recycling.

Several deals have therefore entered troubled waters, having previously been concluded at decent levels - the highest the sub-continent was offering just before the Christmas break.

Gadani Buyers are therefore trying to open Usance L/Cs or use private financing, rather than going through government state banks to get LCs opened and get their vessels delivered.

On the local fundamentals front, the Pakistani Rupee has been gradually decaying as it passed PKR 228 against the U.S. Dollar, only further adding to the ongoing restrictions to preserve domestic U.S. Dollar reserves.

The only impressive event that bolstered the local sentiment are local steel plate prices that skyrocketed by nearly USD 58/Ton within the week, adding to the local sentiment to figure out financing in order to ride this wave out.

### Turkey

- **Headline**: JUMP!!!

The Turkish market appears to be traversing through its own private resurgence as local steel plate prices firmed another USD 7/Ton whilst offers on units jumped another USD 20/Ton this week.

All this despite import steel falling about USD 5/Ton and the Lira (like the Pakistani Rupee) slowly deteriorates towards TRY 19 against the U.S. Dollar.

Rumours of several units being talked basis an early Spring delivery have been making their rounds and given the ongoing famine of recycling tonnage, perhaps local Buyers are trying to get a jump on the market in the hopes that construction projects will resume at pace during the warmer months and plate prices will only continue to firm.

For now, Turkey is increasingly the market to consider for units coming in from the West, especially as the price gap with the Indian sub-continent continues to minimize.
