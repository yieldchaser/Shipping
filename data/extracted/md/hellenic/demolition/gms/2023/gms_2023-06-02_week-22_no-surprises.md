# Market Commentary

## NO SURPRISES!

As the Bangladeshi budget of June 1st starts to be digested in full, it has been an altogether quieter week in Chattogram as local banks and Recyclers evaluate the outcome to ensure no unexpected surprises emerge, before committing afresh on tonnage – and thus far, none seem to be reported.

As such, it should be a busier time for sales and activity from next week once business resumes, although we are now entering the traditionally quieter monsoon and summer months when much of the labor starts to return to their hometowns due to the persistent rains, rendering yards far too difficult for cutting/recycling activities.

Meanwhile, India has also seen some positivity return as the Indian Rupee gained some good ground to fall below the Rs. 83 level against the U.S. Dollar, where it had been recently trading.

The supply of tonnage also remains surprisingly scarce, so any vessels that do come to market are perhaps seeing levels above the reality on the ground, as Cash Buyers look to secure first and work end Buyers’ thereafter, hoping to create some competition and obtain firmer levels.

Pakistan remains completely out of the picture for yet another week, with political and economic uncertainty continuing to beset the country and little hope of ship recycling activity resuming any time soon.

Furthermore, despite a busy few tides gone by in Bangladesh and end Buyers starting to get around their L/C hurdles with the Central State Government bank, plot capacity remains healthy in both Bangladesh and India, so there is certainly potential to absorb more incoming tonnage.

Finally, Turkey sails through another week of opposing fundamentals, as President Erdogan cinches a narrow win and secure his position, with his primary aims reportedly being that of battling the rampant domestic inflation.

# Bangladesh

## AS YOU WERE!

*Margin notes: Budget news. | Bulker sale.*

The announcement of the annual Bangladeshi budget has brought few surprises with it that would affect the domestic ship recycling sector, and buying activity is expected (hoped) to resume in the coming weeks, at the previously energetic levels witnessed through early May.

After several busy weeks and tides of activity, many local Buyers and banks had paused, to ensure that the budget wouldn’t bring any surprises with it, especially in the form of new taxes / duties being levied on the domestic ship recycling sector.

Of course, the fine print remains to be studied and we may have some further clarity next week, however, for the moment it appears to be ‘as you were.’

A couple of sales did register for the week as the ZE RUI 1 (7,735 NET LDT) was committed on an ‘as is’ China basis with 150 Tons of fuel included in the sale, at a healthy USD 531/LT NET LDT. Additionally, a Vietnamese controlled bulker GOLDEN SUN (6,179 LDT) also managed to fetch a decent USD 581/LT LDT for its Owners.

On the local fundamentals front, local steel plate prices flatlined once again for the week, while the Bangladeshi Taka volleys between the TRY 107.3 – 107.5 mark against the U.S. Dollar.

Finally, the pre-budget slowdown can also be witnessed at the waterfront, where only one new vessel reportedly arrived local anchorage this week.

# India

## GAINING GROUND!

*Margin notes: Currency gains. | Tonnage slow.*

This week, some positive moves on the Indian Rupee finally brought some confidence back into a previously lacklustre and stagnant Alang, and one or two fresh sales have reportedly been discussed with End Buyers (primarily for HKC only recycling at Alang).

As stated, having previously breached Rs. 83, the Indian Rupee gained some good ground to fall back into the low Rs. 82.3X range against the U.S. Dollar. Local steel plate prices also registered marginal improvements (about USD 4/Ton) this week, only to keep local sentiments healthy.

Alang anchorage has had a minimal number of units that have arrived and are awaiting beaching this week, as India continues to lose out on tonnage to a firmer Bangladeshi market, especially on ‘as is’ deals that are opening up in the Far East.

The ongoing slowdown in supply is also keeping Alang Buyers cautioned, especially as we head into the quieter monsoon months in addition to many owners still keeping their vessels trading at these reduced charter levels of today (most containers are now finding trading buyers again).

Even Bangladesh is starting to snap up HKC tonnage, to the three NK class yards now in service and as a result, no new market sales were reported into Alang for the week.

# Pakistan

## ENVIOUS GLANCES!

*Margin notes: Out of picture. | Barren summer.*

Pakistan is out of the picture once again and for yet another week, as local Buyers can only look on enviously towards the positivity and (even the few odd) sales being reported from their competing Bangladeshi and Indian counterparts.

Perhaps competitor is the wrong use of the term in this scenario, as Pakistan is unable to compete at all on ships for recycling with its sub-continent neighbors and there have been no meaningful sales into this market for quite some time as the local port report lies empty.

In fact, even last week’s small arrival (undertow) ended up sinking at anchorage due to reported flooding in the engine room and as such, local yards have been completely deprived of tonnage for at least two months now.

On the local fundamentals front, the Pakistani Rupee is still hemorrhaging at PKR 285.2X against the U.S. Dollar, while local steel plate prices appear to have entered a self-induced coma, having reportedly flatlined for several weeks.

Finally, the economic and political chaos that has engulfed the country this year shows few signs of abating, so for ship recycling, it will almost certainly be a barren summer (and likely beyond) in Gadani.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# Turkey

## ERDOGAN WINS!

*Margin notes: TRY 21? | Quiet Waters.*

As President Erdogan finally cinched his victory this week, domestic inflation continues to run amok, and the Lira continues to degrade against the U.S. Dollar without pause or mercy.

While last week, we questioned whether the Lira would finally breach Try 20.0 against the U.S. Dollar, regrettably this week, the Lira has fallen far quicker and is even likely to breach TRY 21 within next week.

As of close of Friday, the Lira ended at TRY 20.92.

Meanwhile, steel fundamentals (import and local) have remained unchanged, as President Erdogan’s priority remains tackling the rampant inflation, thereby firming Turkey’s domestic economy.

No news of sales or even any market units being proposed. As such, Turkey continues to sail through quiet waters.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**
