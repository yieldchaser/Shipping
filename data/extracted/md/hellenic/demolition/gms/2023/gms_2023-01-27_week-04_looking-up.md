# Market Commentary

## LOOKING UP!

Following a virtually inert 2022 and a slightly busier start to 2023 at the various recycling destinations, much of the Far East was off celebrating Chinese Lunar New Year holidays this week and it has expectedly been a quieter period - in terms of sales and activity.

A larger number of container vessels have so far been sold for recycling at the start of the year, and we are beginning to see signs that older dry bulk vessels with surveys due may follow suit in the near future as well.

Additionally, following a glut of tanker vessels concluded for recycling at the beginning of 2022, this is the one segment that is still flying and it wouldn’t be entirely unsurprising to see any meaningful recycling take place from this particular sector any time soon.

Prices and demand have also started to bounce back across Indian sub-continent markets (except Pakistan) and Turkey, although whether Pakistan and / or Bangladesh can open LC/s to import units remains an ongoing question of serious concern.

Meanwhile, after a year of sustained instability and turmoil in 2022, currencies (except in Pakistan) have finally started to settle at their current lows and there is growing optimism (certainly in Bangladesh) that talks regarding an IMF loan may bring some much needed liquidity into the country so that end buyers can open L/Cs and import tonnage once again.

Finally, steel plate prices in India, Bangladesh and Turkey were on a slightly upward trajectory this week other than Pakistan, where plate prices and the Pakistani Rupee recorded one of the worst plummets in recent memory.

# Bangladesh

## FEW REALISED!

*Margin notes: Improving. | Sales elsewhere.*

Sentiments and prices have certainly improved in Bangladesh over these past few weeks, yet there have been very few sales recorded into Chattogram due to the ongoing finance and / LC issues.

Buyers are still trying their hardest to open Usance L/Cs or provide private financing, rather than going through government banks who are currently refusing to open any non-essential L/Cs.

As a result, a majority of the tonnage being proposed for sale has been focussed on India for the time being - not only HKC green sales, but also favoured containers, which are currently receiving the best prices of any market.

Finally, on the domestic fundamentals front, steel plate prices improved by about USD 7/Ton, whilst the Bangladeshi Taka seems to have settled at the BDT 105 region, further boosting the local mindset and willingness to bid on tonnage, even though most options are limited due to ongoing financial hurdles.

As such, it certainly seems to be a conflicting time ahead for the Bangladeshi market.

# India

## READY TO FIRE!

*Margin notes: Quieter. | Demand rising.*

After several sales over recent weeks at increasingly firmer levels, it has been an altogether quieter week in Alang due to various holidays, including the 74th Republic Day of India and the Chinese New Year.

On the local fundamentals front, the Indian Rupee has been trading at far steadier levels in the mid Rs. 81 against the U.S. Dollar – having previously seen hovering close to Rs. 83, whilst steel plate prices, though ever volatile, have managed to maintain their levels over the course of the week.

Indian Buyers therefore seem primed for a busier year, given the growing number of empty plots after a very slow 2022; indicating a demand that is gearing up again, leading to improved sentiments and pricing.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# Pakistan

## OUT OF IT!

*Margin notes: Frustrated. | Currency woe.*

Frustratingly for Gadani Recyclers, having offered the lowest (sub-continent) prices in addition to their recent inability to open fresh L/Cs, has unsurprisingly stranded the Pakistani market at the bottom of the sub-continent rankings, leaving local Buyers unable to import any new vessels.

Further hampering their ability to offer firm on units has been the Pakistani Rupee that has depreciated an alarming (nearly) 9% in a single day, wrapping up the week at over PKR 262 against the U.S. Dollar – a historic record for the PKR.

Pakistani steel plate prices also recorded a noteworthy decline, plunging nearly USD 64/Ton during the course of the week, leaving nearly all Gadani Recyclers on the sidelines as they sit by and watch other markets push on to acquire tonnage.

As such, until the currency, plate prices and L/C issues settle, it appears as though Pakistan will remain out of the buying for the foreseeable future.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# Turkey

## JUMPY…AGAIN?!

*Margin notes: Steel back? | USD 50/MT.*

After last week’s surprising drop in import steel by about USD 15/Ton and local steel prices by about USD 5/Ton, this week, both import and local steel plate prices rebounded by USD 20/Ton and USD 5/Ton respectively.

Even the Lira, seems to have stabilized a touch over recent weeks, winding down the week at TRY 18.80 against the U.S. Dollar.

Meanwhile, as per news from local sources, demand does remain strong and negotiations on units basis Spring deliveries are still ongoing, however there has been no confirmation of any fixtures into Aliaga of late.

Notwithstanding, given the recent firming of levels by over USD 50/MT, we can certainly expect news of fixtures and / or arrivals at Aliaga anchorage in coming week(s).

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**
