## 1. Market Overview

### Highlights
- Further decline.
- Working firm.
- Freight ok.
- Correction evident.
- Pakistan apart.

### Editorial: Market Commentary

To further compound the presently bearish ship recycling sentiments, local steel plate prices have further weakened at key destinations and LME steel futures show few signs of any sort of recovery in the immediate future.

That being said, several vessels have been working firm over the past few weeks and sales have been taking place. However, these are primarily HKC intended units being committed into India, even at these gradually deteriorating rates.

Moreover, due to a recent uptick in freight rates, fewer than expected Containers and Dry Bulk units have been introduced into the markets for recycling and this is giving the ship recycling industry, an opportunity to draw breath and find true bottom, before reoffering at these new (lower) realities.

The overall correction appears to be between USD 50 – USD 60/LDT since the peaks seen earlier this year. Due to such turbulence, it may be that End Buyers wish to wait and watch market developments before getting back into the buying once again.

Bangladesh & India remain the two most active / competing markets for the time being, as Pakistan remains a non-entity for over two months now, completely out of the market action due to unworkable L/Cs, uncompetitive pricing, and a precarious financial and political outlook (especially in the near future).

Finally, the Turkish market continues to deteriorate (as predicted last week), with weakening steel plate prices and a currency that seems likely to breach TRY 19.50 against the U.S. Dollar next week. As a result, Aliaga’s recycling prices have (negatively) re-adjusted by USD 10/MT.

## 3. Country Market Intelligence

### Bangladesh

- **Headline**: BREATHER!
- **Key Notes**: *Quieter week.* | *Missing sales.*

It has been a quieter overall week in Bangladesh in terms of sales and activity, with End Buyers offering even lower levels on tonnage, but with little success in securing any units thus far.

It is clear that prices have come off significantly since the peaks witnessed earlier this year, however, Cash Buyers and Vessel Owners have yet to share the currently brewing sentiment / appetite in negotiating at such discounted rates on offer from Chattogram Buyers.

As such, for yet another week, India has continued to secure a bulk of the recent fixtures, with several more interesting HKC sales being concluded to the market there, much to the chagrin of the few Bangladeshi Buyers who have recently obtained Class NK HKC certification for their respective recycling yards.

Finally, domestic fundamentals remain shaky as the Taka continues to buoy around the mid BDT 106.5X against the U.S. Dollar, while local steel plate prices find themselves absent from trading altogether (for the second consecutive week).

### India

- **Headline**: PRECARIOUS!

Despite the precarious nature of recent falls in Alang, news of several deals from last week has surfaced as sales of an FPSO and a General Cargo vessel reportedly registered at decent levels.

Firstly, the General Cargo vessel KUMUL ARROW (10,998 LDT) from Gearbulk managed to fetch an above market USD 560/LT LDT for class NK certified HKC recycling only. The gantry crane and bowthruster likely adding value and justifying the price on show.

Subsequently, the FPSO FERAN VAZ (41,716 LDT) was committed for a decent USD 260/LT LDT basis an ‘as is’ under tow delivery from Gabon, for HKC green recycling in India only. The sale included a whopping 4,000 CBM of cargo residues on board for tank cleaning, in order to make the vessel gas free for hot works clean for recycling.

Meanwhile, after some hefty price declines of over USD 20/LDT in one week alone, most End Buyers are maintaining radio silence, rather than offering firm numbers, perhaps fearful of further falls ahead – and justifiably so.

Even though the Indian Rupee continues to gain ground against the U.S. Dollar as it wraps up the week at levels in the region Rs. 81.6X, local steel plate prices are the ones that have been declining steadily (for several weeks now) as levels reportedly fell another USD 18/Ton during the course of this week.

### Pakistan

- **Headline**: INACTIVE AND INERT!
- **Key Notes**: *Missing in action.* | *Off the pace.*

Pakistan is miles off being competitive with their sub-continent counterparts and, as a result, has spent another week inactive & inert, missing out on all of the recent market negotiations and fixtures.

The ongoing domestic political turmoil and financial crises that have crippled the country show few signs of abating just yet, as local steel plate prices flatlined over the last few weeks. Additionally, the Pakistani Rupee, which has yet to stabilize to any meaningful degree, continues to trade in the mid PKR 283 against the U.S. Dollar.

Crippling L/C restrictions have also kept negotiations at bay and the only vessel idling at Gadani’s anchorage that was reportedly awaiting L/C approval, has finally sailed away this week leaving not only Pakistan’s waterfront after two months, but also a country devoid of any tonnage for the same duration.

As such, it is perhaps unsurprising to see Gadani Buyers so off the pace and unable to acquire fresh units, and until some confidence starts to flow back into this market, there is little sense in discussing any fresh deals into Pakistan.

*Market Sales Reported*: **NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

### Turkey

- **Headline**: DROP!

The much-anticipated decline from last week finally materialized this week as, on the back of import steel plate prices that dropped in excess of USD 20/Ton and local steel plate prices fell about USD 15/Ton, Aliaga Buyers rolled back their vessel prices by about USD 10/MT this week.

While on the one hand, the lack of tonnage would indicate that the drop in levels is not a big loss for the local market considering there are no vessels to bid on, on the other hand, declining levels may not tempt Vessel Owners to consider Turkish levels or even Turkey as a viable destination – especially when you consider that sub-continent market levels are nearly twice those from Turkey.

Will the traditional summer / monsoon months in the sub-continent come to the aid of Turkish Recyclers, remains to be seen. For now, however, Turkey seems to have no reprieve from the shortage of supply.
