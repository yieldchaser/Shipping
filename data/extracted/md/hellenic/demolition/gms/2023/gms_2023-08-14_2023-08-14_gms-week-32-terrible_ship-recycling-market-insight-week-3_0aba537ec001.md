## Editorial Commentary

It has been another intensely inert week in the Indian sub-continent ship recycling markets, 
with Bangladesh sinking to the bottom of the price-ranking board and neighboring India & 
Pakistan taking the lead (in terms of pricing and ability to secure unsold tonnage currently on 
offer).  
 
Not only does the Bangladeshi market have a vanishing appetite for vessels at any sensible 
levels, but the lack of L/C and financing approvals also appears to be making local resales just 
as difficult, even months after the budget was announced and even amidst this most 
challenging of times.  
 
At least Pakistan has shown some promise and appetite to come back into the picture, with 
further discussions on unsold units reported as this market leapfrogs past India’s prices, 
which still have the advantage of, and are backed by, L/C and financing abilities on units of 
any size, especially when compared to the Pakistani & Bangladeshi markets. 
 
Ironically, despite being the most reliable of the sub-continent destinations on L/C / financing 
issues, Indian prices continue to underwhelm as most Alang Buyers seem content on waiting-
and-watching market movements before offering firm once again. 
 
Consequently, we have witnessed many Owners withdrawing their vessels from the market 
entirely, so disastrous has been the ongoing decline in levels, and it may not even be worth 
considering the recycling option for some Owners - especially not at some of the ridiculously 
opportunistic numbers. 
 
In terms of supply of vessels, amidst a dire weakening of sentiments from this sector, it 
continues to primarily be a flow of dry bulk units. Moreover, a majority of containers (even 
those with surveys due) continue down the trading lanes and have been mostly missing from 
the recycling markets of late. 
 
Finally, on the far end, the Turkish market remains unchanged and inert for another week, 
with fundamentals facing opposite directions and no news of market units being proposed.

MARKET COMMENTARY

BANGLADESH 
NO GO! 
Despite Owners & Cash Buyers attempts to commit their vessels into Bangladesh, it
turned into an extremely frustrating (& failed) exercise that resulted in an inert week in
Chattogram, since local Buyers continue to present lowball and opportunistic offers,
only to lose out on those units to competing markets. 
The weakened state of domestic fundamentals coupled with prevailing economic
concerns, all of it mixed with the monsoon rains certainly seem like the key drivers
behind the current state of pricing affairs.  
For starts as demand for steel intended for national infrastructure projects has slowed
significantly amidst the intense monsoon rains this year, local steel plate prices have
resumed flatlining on account of a standstill in the trading of ship’s steel with domestic
steel mills.  
Moreover, the Bangladeshi Taka hit a record BDT 109.38 against the U.S. Dollar this 
week, further adding to the ongoing hassle that the Bangladeshi ship recycling sector
has become today. 
Notwithstanding, even though a couple of geographically convenient ‘as is’ dry bulk
sales have taken place this week that could see Chattogram Buyers get their hands on 
these units, the chances that these could handily be redirected to a competing
destination remains rather high, given just how disastrously Chattogram Buyers are
toying with their local offerings are at present.  
At the onset, bulk carrier B H MALLY (9,379 LT) was concluded for a decent USD
465/LDT, basis an ‘as is’ South China delivery and with 200 Tons of bunkers included in
the sale. Subsequently, the Handymax bulker AN YUE (7,985 LDT) was committed for
an equally firm USD 475/LT LDT basis an ‘as is’ Singapore delivery, also with 200 Tons
of fuel onboard.

INDIA 
Lowering. 
FLACCID! 
At a time when Indian Recyclers have a real chance to strike out much of the competition
and secure most of the meagre amounts of tonnage that is currently on offer, local Buyers
have failed to distinguish themselves from the crowd by lowering their prices this week. 
Unfortunately, at a time when resilience is the key, the chance to secure vessels seems to
be squandered away by Alang Recyclers. 
Like Bangladesh, fundaments certainly have their share to add to the current state of
affairs as the Indian Rupee once again approaches Rs. 83 against the U.S. Dollar, while 
local steel plate prices declined by about USD 12 / Ton by the time the week ended. 
Should this continue, we may well see previous and even fresh market deals concluded
by various Cash Buyers (with an India redelivery in mind), eventually ending up in a
competing market with firmer offers, so flaccid are Alang prices at present.  
 
The old sale of Panamax Bulker AGIOS NIKOLAOS (9,743 LDT) surfaced this week as
the vessel was previously committed to a Cash Buyer at a decent USD 540/LT LDT basis
an ‘as is’ Singapore delivery and a reported India redelivery in mind. The unit remains
unsold due to the declining prices and is still idling in Singapore, almost certain to lose
money on this transaction.  
Additionally, container vessel HENG HUI 2 (14,775 LDT) was likewise concluded for a
surprisingly firm USD 550/LDT, basis an ‘as is’ Colombo delivery and with 300 Tons of
bunkers included in the sale.

GMS Weekly 
QUESTIONABLE! 
Pakistan has inadvertently found itself in a situation whereby, Gadani Buyers could 
secure any of the recently committed ‘as is’ units that are currently (or may soon be) on 
offer, and this most recent declining of Bangladeshi and Indian prices has found the 
Pakistani market thrust atop the price / market rankings this week, with some 
unexpectedly firm indications coming forth as well. 
Pakistani L/C and financing issues logically remain a big concern for the industry, 
especially after a dire year of economic and political hurdles in the country, coupled with 
declining fundamentals that have only further added to the crippling of this market. 
On the domestic fundamentals front, not only has the Pakistani Rupee collapsed back to a 
record PKR 287.2X level against the U.S. Dollar, but local steel plate prices also need to be 
resuscitated from their months long flatlined demise.  
Moreover, Pakistan has had virtually no L/C and finance approvals on vessel purchases
for quite some time now and as such, the country remains a precarious destination to 
consider business dealings with, at least until a couple of sales have been concluded into 
Gadani and the industry gets acclimatized to doing business here once again.  
Notwithstanding, given the incredible lowball offers emanating from Bangladesh and 
even India at present, Owners and Cash Buyers might as well be giving the Pakistani 
market a serious second look and as such, it will therefore be interesting to see just which 
vessels will end up at Gadani’s waterfront.

DEAD QUIET! 
For the most part, the Turkish market remained reportedly devoid of activity for yet 
another week as the recently deteriorated vessel prices remained unchanged for the week
and the Turkish Lira, though stable, remains depreciated at a historic TRY 27.X against 
the U.S. Dollar. 
The only positive movement for the week (without any positive outcome in return) were
steel prices whereby import steel firmed by about USD 10/Ton and local steel prices 
improved by about USD 6/Ton. 
Notwithstanding, despite all the ongoing volatility in fundamentals, there is little this 
market can do in the face of intense tonnage starvation, which has left this market unable 
to maintain a pulse.  
As such, this market remains dead quiet for now.
