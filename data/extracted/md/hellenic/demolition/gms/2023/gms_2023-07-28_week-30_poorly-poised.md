# Market Commentary

## POORLY POISED!

Recycling sentiments remain negative overall, and it seems to now be a declining market of one that’s frustratingly left with very few open Recyclers who are simply unwilling to offer realistically on the few vessels that are currently available in the market.

Ravaging monsoon rains are the leading cause of the ongoing slowdown in the sub-continent as minimal cutting / recycling activities at yards is taking place and as such, the market is barely moving forward with the minor number of vessels being committed.

There is no question that Cash Buyers and Vessel Owners are unaccepting of some of the opportunistic offers being put out there and as such, we are witnessing something of a standoff on any number of unsold vessels at present.

The one confirmed sale for the week saw a Sinokor controlled container that was committed for an extraordinary price into Bangladesh, but we would like to caution our readers that this is not a true reflection of current market conditions at all, and it increasingly seems to be that just one particularly hot Buyer who was able to arrange L/C limits / bank financing on a favored small LDT unit.

Encouragingly, enquiries have been emerging from Pakistan as well, and it may take one or two confirmed sales and beachings to go through, what would inevitably (and eventually) establish a new direction for Cash Buyers and Gadani Recyclers, for clearing L/Cs & various other payment issues, especially having been out of the game for so long.

India once again slips to the lowest placed of all sub-continent markets and as the supply of green ships and specialist units dries up, Alang Buyers are seeing minimal tonnage and new arrivals, as evident from this week’s local port position, which reported no fresh arrivals.

Finally, the Turkish market remains suspended in no-man’s land (just like the sub-continent markets), with the Lira briefly breaching TRY 27 and no movement reported in steel prices.

# Bangladesh

## MARKET OF ONE!

*Margin notes: Sale over 600. | Withdrawing.*

The big news this week surrounded a well-above market sale of a Sinokor container as the SINOKOR TIANJIN (4,672 LDT) managed to fetch an astonishing USD 622/LT LDT on a delivered basis.

The sale included about 250 Tons of bunkers (about USD 10/LT worth) and the favored small LDT unit has likely assisted the Vessel Owners in achieving a big price, as more Buyers could obtain USD / Ton financing on smaller LDT vessels.

Nevertheless, it is certain that it is an above-market sale of one into Chattogram, especially as very few End Buyers are actively pursuing tonnage at sensible levels.

The ongoing incessant rains are hampering domestic construction projects in the country as well as ship-recycling activities, in addition to domestic fundamentals that are jointly degrading local sentiments. Not only have local steel plate prices declined by nearly USD 20/Ton this week, but the Bangladeshi Taka also continues to languish at record-lows against the U.S. Dollar - at nearly BDT 108.50.

As such, Cash Buyers and Vessel Owners are withdrawing sales candidates from this market, or are simply diverting them to other markets, or continuing to trade them while sentiments, demand, ability to perform (amidst ongoing strained financing and Central Bank LC approvals), and pricing remain so low at present.

# India

## LOWLY!

*Margin notes: Opportunistic. | Steel fall.*

India slips once again to become the lowest placed sub-continent destination as Alang Buyers started placing opportunistic numbers – even below USD 500/LDT on occasion on any proposed tonnage, which is subsequently drying up the local market of tonnage.

No new vessels arrived Alang anchorage this week, with only one beaching of a prior arrival from last week. Even an emerging Pakistani market (albeit a market with ongoing LC / financing difficulties) is now ahead of India on pricing.

Fundamentals continue to crisscross in opposite directions as volatile steel plate prices firmed marginally this week, all while the Indian Rupee deteriorated past Rs. 82 against the U.S. Dollar once again.

Accordingly, the only constructive way forward for Alang Buyers will be to raise their game and offer realistic levels, should they wish to secure vessels moving forward.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# Pakistan

## EMERGING!

*Margin notes: Demand increase. | HKC needs.*

Pakistan has started to re-enter the game in recent weeks, as enquiries for various working vessels have started to be fielded by Pakistani Recyclers after a year (and then some) of being out of the game.

As domestic financials (and the Pakistani economy overall) have gained a degree of stability amidst the incoming financial bailout from the IMF, we can only hope that funding for the domestic ship recycling sector may eventually get a slice of the incoming pie.

It will certainly take a couple of sales to see how L/C approvals will unravel moving forward and in order to import & beach ships in Gadani once again, all while domestic fundamentals continue to suffer.

The Pakistani Rupee is still in the high PKR 280s against the U.S. Dollar as local steel plate prices remain flat for months on end.

Of course, local Buyers will also need to upgrade their facilities (like India and Bangladesh have been busy doing) up to HKC standards, as the convention is due to be finally entered into force and officially in 2025.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# Turkey

## DULL WEEK!

*Margin notes: Below USD 300/MT. | Remains on ice.*

Aliaga is now going through a lull after last week’s decline in prices, as these sudden low offers (some reportedly even below USD 300/MT) are probably not going to entice many Ship Owners or even Cash Buyers to successfully complete a sale into Turkey, other than market proposed EUSRR specific units, of which, we have not seen any for a while.

Fundamentals continue to affect local pricing as the Lira remains weakened at record levels, briefly surpassing TRY 27 against the U.S. Dollar, while local steel plate prices reportedly recorded no changes this week, after both import and local steel reported noteworthy declines last week.

Other than the summer heat burning up the nation, everything recycling remains on ice.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**
