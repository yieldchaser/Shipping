## 1. Market Overview

### Highlights
- Relative gloom.
- Ramadan ongoing.
- Falling steel.
- Busy start.
- Supply slows.

### Editorial: Market Commentary

The ongoing month of Ramadan has also led to much softer sentiments across the board, as ongoing religious festivities are observed, and business takes a backseat for the time being. Volatile steel plate prices have not helped the current situation brewing at the various recycling destinations either, and this has seen most end users of fresh tonnage abstain from buying, choosing instead to wait and watch developments and how the market opens post Ramadan, before offering afresh on any new vessels being proposed.

The primary problem for recyclers at all the major locations is the ongoing and noticeably painful shortage in the supply of vessels for recycling, as freight markets in Dry Bulk and Containers have shown some renewed positivity of late.

As such, after a decade long low supply and activity, last year even though it seemed as though it would been an overall busy and bullish start to 2023 for the major recycling markets. However, things seem to be easing off even before May arrives and the customary cooling of performance in the sub-continent markets due to the oncoming Monsoon season will only further aggravate the present situation – despite the state of supply and the current (and plenty of) availability of space at dormant plots.

Making matters worse, with both Bangladesh and Pakistan markets still requiring lengthy central state bank approval processes to open new L/Cs, it is certainly going to be a painful process to get vessels delivered (in time) moving forward, and patience / extra waiting times will certainly be required from those Owners / Cash Buyers seeking to deliver units moving forward. Lastly, the Turkish market echoes virtually identical sentiments and fundamentals as the sub-continent markets, including the Lira, which continues to relapsefurther every week.

## 3. Country Market Intelligence

### Bangladesh

- **Headline**: STRESSES AND STRAINS!
- **Key Notes**: *Difficult deliveries.* | *Various sales.*

A difficult week on deliveries and L/C approvals has descended across the Bangladeshi ship recycling sector, despite several new well priced sales being concluded and even large LDT units greeting the waterfront through the course of the week – all this bearing in mind the ongoing financing hurdles faced on the large LDT transactions.

Adding caution to the ongoing situation are local fundamentals, whereby local steel plate prices flatlined again through the course of the week, while the Bangladeshi Taka continues to volley in the early BDT 106 against the U.S. Dollar.

As such, the sales of the week certainly raised an eyebrow, given that the General Cargo vessel ATLANTIC RAY (2,461 LDT), built in Turkey, with a steel propeller, eventually managed to obtain a fantastic USD 620/LT LDT – showing that smaller LDT units are very much the flavour of the moment. This was also the case with the LPG APOLLO PACIFIC (2,188 LDT), sold from Vietnamese owners for a very firm USD 655/LT LDT.

Finally, the handy Logger Bulker OCEAN GREAT (6,311 LDT) from Chinese Sellers was committed for a decent USD 575/LT LDT.

Time will tell whether central bank permission on these various transactions (as well as Cash Buyer inventories) will get approval, especially while the strained financial situation in Bangladesh continues to show few signs of abating just yet.

---

### India

- **Headline**: DELUGE TO TRICKLE!

As the charter market picks up in this previously beleaguered sector, India has seen the marginal number of its favoured HKC container candidates snatched away from their grasp over the last few weeks.

The above, coupled with the overall supply of vessels that has started to slow, as recycling prices continue cool off and what was once a deluge at the turn of the year has eventually turned into a trickle of tonnage, and it may be a quieter second quarter to the year if the current trends persist in Alang, especially as the monsoon season gradually rears its head over the horizon.

Pricewise, India does remain second placed to Bangladesh, although there is the greater comfort factor of comparatively more reliable and prompter performance on L/Cs, giving Owners and Cash Buyers more incentive to head towards Alang on any available ships, despite the cheaper rates.

Fundamentals-wise, local steel plate prices declined a marginal USD 5/Ton, while the Indian Rupee continues to slide around the Rs. 81.8X mark against the U.S. Dollar.

### Pakistan

- **Headline**: OUT OF CONVERSATION!
- **Key Notes**: *Uncertain.* | *Bleak outlook.*

Pakistan is out of the conversation regarding negotiations on any vessels for recycling, so low and so unreliable / uncertain are Local Buyers on price and workable L/Cs at present – and have been for a couple of months now.

The Pakistani Rupee has certainly endured a torrid and unprecedented collapse over the last year at least, and this has left most Buyers incapable of performing with yards closing and the government stepping in to work with the central bank on approvals on the expenditure on any precious US dollar reserves in the country.

Notwithstanding, despite local steel plate prices flatlining (like the Bangladeshi market) through the week – the Pakistani Rupee firmed to the early PKR 284 against the U.S Dollar.

As such, local port reports remain bleak, as does the outlook in Pakistan for the remainder of the next quarter (if not the rest of the year), unless financing issues can be resolved, and some stability can be brought back to the currency and local fundamentals in the country.

*Market Sales Reported*: **NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

---

### Turkey

- **Headline**: GLOBAL SYMPTOMS!

Like all of the major ship recycling destinations, Turkey continues to suffer through the ongoing shortage of supply. Tonnage is painful to come by and the easing fundamentals is making harder for Aliaga Recyclers to maintain their historically impressive levels for much longer.

Both import and local steel plate prices have fallen by about USD 5/Ton this week as the Lira continues to gradually sink against the U.S. Dollar approaching TRY 19.40.

It does seem as though the rumours of local fixtures were just that, and as though the Turkish market is similar to the Pakistani market, with a declining currency, absence of tonnage, and a market that may well start weakening in the near future, it is going to be a tough summer for the Turkish market.
