SOLID SHOWINGS! 
February 3rd, 2023 
Volume 247, Issue 1068 
Week 5 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
“Try to be a rainbow in someone's cloud.” 
 
 
 
– Maya Angelou 
Another solid showing from sub-continent markets has given encouragement to Ship Owners 
and Cash Buyers, to start testing potential prices with firm candidates. Indeed, several deals 
have been concluded off the back of these improved numbers, including one more Capesize
bulker at firm levels basis an ‘as is’ Singapore delivery. 
Now that the Chinese New Year holidays have concluded, the expected bounce in freight 
markets has yet to materialize, so an increased number of dry bulk and container units (in 
particular) have come under serious negotiations for recycling. 
Even global currencies are starting to settle against the U.S. Dollar and steel plate prices have 
found a relatively steady place, as demand and a firm buying interest is increasing across all 
markets, even for Pakistan and Bangladesh, where it is often not feasible to do deals there due 
to the ongoing L/C restrictions. 
Talks on the much-needed IMF loans are still ongoing in each country, to bring back some 
essential liquidity and U.S. Dollars needed to establish fresh Letters of Credit so that domestic 
recyclers can start to import vessels / steel once again. 
In Bangladesh, there are certain / select end buyers who can open L/Cs with private financing 
(away from the government bank restrictions) or with Usance L/Cs, but this still provides only 
limited options to Cash Buyers / Owners with vessels to sell. 
Finally, Turkey too continues its stable streak with firming steel prices and a currency that’s
found a new plateau to rest on, resulting in vessel prices firming an additional USD 10/MT this 
week. 
In other news, the Brazilian government owned warship Sao Paolo has tragically and 
unnecessarily been scuttled off the coast of Brazil with a plethora of hazardous materials on 
board, all while NGOs and outside observers (with no real knowledge of ship recycling) keep 
putting pressure on various countries (including Turkey) to deny entry of vessels for recycling.

MARKET COMMENTARY

BANGLADESH 
HAMPERED! 
As much as domestic recyclers desire to acquire fresh units, the lack of L/C financing is
severely hampering local efforts. 
Indeed, a Capesize bulker was committed basis an ‘as is’ Singapore delivery and has all 
the hallmarks of a Bangladeshi resale. However, the ongoing restrictions on financing 
mean that any substantially sized vessels are unlikely to end up here for the time being. 
Similarly, there are also a number of mostly container vessels working for only HKC 
recycling into India, which rules Bangladesh out on that tonnage as well. 
Fundamentals are steady for the time being as local steel plate prices have been
unchanged for the duration of the week (likely due to minimal steel trading on account 
of the dearth of tonnage recently delivered into Chattogram), in addition to the 
Bangladeshi Taka that weakened marginally past BDT 106.50. 
It would certainly be interesting to see how the Bangladeshi market will perform in the 
coming weeks, given that there are increasing rumours of the Bangladeshi government
being a few steps closer to concluding the IMF loan and hopefully return this market into 
the fray.

to secure any of the admittedly small number of recycling units available so far for the year
(primarily capes and containers), whilst competing Bangladesh and Pakistan continue to 
suffer on account of their L/C limitations.

steel prices have fluctuated a bare minimal amount for the week but remain in overall 
positive territory.

GMS Weekly 
LITTLE POINT! 
Pakistan remains far from competitive at the moment, with the lowest numbers across the 
sub-continent and the most uncertain / unstable destination for L/C financing. 
As such, many of the recent sales here have failed – only to be resold elsewhere at higher / 
more improved numbers, as Cash Buyers or Owners are not even checking with the Gadani 
market, so precarious is the situation here at present. 
Steel plate prices have declined as well (about USD 12/Ton this week) and the currency 
plummets further past PKR 276 against the U.S. Dollar, as Gadani Recyclers continue to 
witness increasingly deteriorating fundamentals across the country of late and until there 
is stability and banks allow local Recyclers to establish fresh L/Cs once again, there is little 
to discuss where Pakistan is concerned.

FURTHER ON! 
On the back of firming import steel prices and a currency that has found fair footing 
around TRY 18.80 against the U.S. Dollar, the Turkish market has pushed on further with 
vessel prices finding themselves improving an additional USD 10/MT this week. 
While there is still no information on market tonnage being actively worked on, local 
associates continue to advise that a few yards are in talks for some private units basis a 
Spring delivery. 
As such, as the dry bulk markets face possible decline, Turkey may finally be in a position 
to snag some units.
