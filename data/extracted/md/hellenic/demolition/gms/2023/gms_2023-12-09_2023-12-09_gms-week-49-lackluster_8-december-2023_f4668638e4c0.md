“Strive not to be a success, but rather to be of value.” 
 
 
– Albert Einstein 
The lackluster end to the year in sub continent recycling markets continues for another week as
disappointed owners and cash buyers face selling their inventory into markets where prices 
have slumped. 
Indeed, several dry bulk sales were done below USD 500/LT LDT into India in the lowest
confirmed deals for several months. 
Steel prices have declined in India by almost 10 percent, in a shock to many, particularly as 
international steel prices have pushed on to an impressive extent over the last month. 
As such the offering and buying has been extremely tentative of late, with end buyers preferring
to wait and watch market developments before offering firm on tonnage again. 
Bangladesh and Pakistan meanwhile continue to face LC / financing difficulties and minimal 
deals have been confirmed to those markets as a result. 
Chittagong may stay inert right up until the elections in mid January next year, with many 
hoping a change in regime brings some stimulus to the local economy and international exports 
in particular. 
It is therefore encouraging to see dry bulk charter markets showing signs of life, and this is 
subsequently depriving recycling locations of tonnage at a time of considerable malaise there. 
In other news, the UAE issued a new law yesterday which will come into force in March, 2024 
and limit any UAE flagged or any foreign flagged vessels coming from UAE for HKC only 
recycling in the sub continent – along with some contradictory wording prohibiting ‘beaching’. 
Further clarifications are needed however to understand the full extend of the restrictions.

MARKET COMMENTARY

BANGLADESH 
RESTRICTED! 
As the lowest placed sub continent market with virtually no offers emanating from local 
buyers, there was very little to report for another week in a subdued Bangladesh. 
Many are targeting purchases post election in mid January of next year – assuming a 
change in regime brings with it a boost to the economy. 
Financing from banks remains the chief struggle for most buyers, and as long as LCs 
remain restricted, expect very few deals to be confirmed to Bangladesh for the remainder
of the year.

INDIA 
Slumping. 
RATTLED! 
The Indian market has slumped yet further this week, and several dry bulk deals have 
been done below USD 500/LDT by desperate cash buyers, fearful that perhaps further falls
may be forthcoming ahead. 
Steel prices actually improved by USD 10/LDT across the week in India, but so rattled have
end buyers been by the recent deterioration in prices, that there was little realised impact
on new offers. 
The hope is that sentiments may improve going into the new year, but as it stands India is
a market worth avoiding at present, and a slowdown in supply has perhaps come at an 
opportune time to give end buyers there a breather.

GMS Weekly 
FEW DEALS DONE! 
As the top placed sub continent market, local buyers in Pakistan were able to secure several 
deals – mostly panamax sized bulkers to the few capable end buyers that have LC approval. 
However, it really is slow progress in Gadani as LC approvals are only coming through 
very slowly to limited buyers who have bank approval. 
The hard work must start in earnest in Pakistan to upgrade facilities now that they have 
agreed to adhere to the Hong Kong Convention ahead of its accession into force in 2025. 
As the closest market to the UAE, this has become even more relevant and important given 
the news from there this week on new laws concerning the recycling of ships – affecting 
both UAE and international flagged tonnage.

FIRMER YET! 
Turkey continues to surprise the industry, with weekly ongoing improvements amidst a 
key depreciating fundamental that should have taken the steam entirely out of its domestic
ship-recycling market. 
As import steel plate prices improved by another USD 8/Ton, which may have (directly or 
indirectly) affected local offerings by another USD 5/MT this week, the Lira continues to 
commensurate its ongoing demise against the U.S. Dollar, as it treads deeper towards an 
eventual TRY 29 leap. 
Overall, this market, while firming, continues to suffer from the deathly starvation of
meaningful tonnage.
