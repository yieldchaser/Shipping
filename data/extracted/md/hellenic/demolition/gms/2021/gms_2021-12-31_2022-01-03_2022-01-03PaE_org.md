### Editorial: Market Commentary

The sub-continent markets more than doubled since the lows seen at the height of the Coronavirus pandemic last year, as levels managed to hit decade long highs of about USD 650/LDT, up from the lows of USD 260/LDT or so, seen during the height of the pandemic halfway through 2020 – eventually transpiring into gains of almost USD 400/LDT!

Even though levels have crushingly cooled off by almost USD 50/LDT since the peaks seen in September / October of this year, we are still seeing levels above USD 600/LDT, in what remains a decade-long high for the ship recycling industry in the Indian sub-continent.

Turkish levels too displayed impressive improvements during the second half of 2021, with levels suddenly jumping USD 65/Ton from the average USD 300/Ton figure. While no market sales have reportedly taken place at these highs, we have certainly seen the Aliaga Buyers not starving for tonnage through the year either, given the number of cruise / offshore vessels delivered here for strictly green (or otherwise) recycling.

Overall, the hope and (general) consensus is that these prices should endure going well into 2022 Q1, such is the (overall) bullish state of steel prices at present – despite a couple of currencies still trembling with inconsistencies and the fact that China is importing, not exporting steel. Construction projects and growth throughout the sub-continent also means demand remains firm and we will likely see prices maintained (for the short term at the very least).

The big question for the New Year is, which sector will be the primary supply of ships for 2022? Many are expecting the beleaguered Tanker sector to turn around, whilst dry Bulk and Containers continue to impress. So, the question on everyone’s mind is whether we are going to continue see a shortage in supply despite recyclers finally being eager for tonnage?

## 3. Country Market Intelligence

### Bangladesh

- **Headline**: FEW SALES!
- **Key Notes**: *Demand good.* | *Supply weak.*

There have only been a few sales in the lead up to the Christmas / New Year holidays in Bangladesh and even those that did take place, were at the higher numbers before the supposed fall.

Therefore, it is clear that demand in Chattogram is still ripe for tonnage and it would not be surprising at all to see prices push on again going past the New Year, such has been the paucity of new units for sale of late.

Local steel plate prices have enjoyed a surprising degree of stability over recent weeks and the overall decline in the recent past has not been significant enough to justify some of the lower numbers being offered by opportunistic end Buyers, and it certainly is only a matter of time before Bangladeshi Buyers are back to doing their best in 2022.

### India

- **Headline**: MIND THE GAP!

The Indian market had recently declined by over USD 50/LDT over the last month due to falling steel plate prices that declined in excess of USD 60/Ton, leaving the Alang market a good way off their Bangladeshi and Pakistani brethren.

A collection of deals of more specialist units (non-ferrous laden stainless-steel tankers in particular) and HKC only green vessels have reportedly been concluded in the run up to Christmas, else, Alang Buyers are simply unable to compete on standard units in the sub- continent at present.

However, plate prices have been relatively stable over the last couple of weeks and even reported a USD 13/Ton increase as we enter 2022. The currency too has firmed as it approaches Rs. 74.3X against the U.S. Dollar.

As such, even though it has been a quieter overall year in Alang (compared to 2020), with far fewer HKC vessels being concluded and a diet consisting primarily of specialist units (Offshore, Passenger, Reefers, stainless-steel tankers, etc.), we do expect levels to start firming in Alang once again (especially as the anticipated Q1 / Feb – Mar firming comes around).

*Market Sales Reported*: **NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

### Pakistan

- **Headline**: IMPROVING!
- **Key Notes**: *In contention.* | *Encouraging.*

Despite trying to talk down the market in order to secure a low-priced deal away from Indian Buyers, Pakistani Recyclers have finally had to make some improvements at the bidding tables, in order to compete with what has been a rampant Bangladeshi market for a majority of this year.

Indeed, there have been occasions this year when Gadani has led the way as the top-placed sub-continent destination and has secured several high-profile and higher LDT vessels as a result of their offerings – one reportedly even taken away from Bangladeshi hands despite being more suitable positioned for a Chattogram delivery.

It is therefore encouraging going into the New Year for the Pakistani market, having somewhat recovered (or at least become used to) some of the frantic and historical currency depreciations witnessed throughout the year and will be hoping for a positive start to 2022!

*Market Sales Reported*: **NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

### Turkey

- **Headline**: STABLE MIRAGE?

Amidst the ongoing record-breaking volatility of the Turkish Lira (breaching all-time lows in excess of TRY 18.30 against the U.S. Dollar) and creating an increased sense of concern in the minds of domestic Recyclers – Aliaga has surprisingly gone through a week of stability as the exchange rate ends at a far firmer TRY 13.5 and reportedly no fluctuations in local and import steel prices as well.

While Turkey has had a rather busy Q1 and Q2 of 2021 taking deliveries of numerous cruise vessels, offshore units, and other large LDT units intended for EUSRR recycling, the breadth of local deliveries has kept domestic demand relatively in check, despite prices jumping a whopping USD 65/Ton During Q3 – Q4, on the back of a similar jump in import steel prices.

Notwithstanding, despite the fact that the recent Lira fluctuations certainly shuddered local sentiments, local offerings on vessels have not declined as drastically, with levels just USD 10/Ton shy off recent peaks.

Similar to the sub-continent markets, the outlook for Turkey does remain neutral to positive, barring any other nosediving contests for the Lira or that may deliver a few unannounced splashes.
