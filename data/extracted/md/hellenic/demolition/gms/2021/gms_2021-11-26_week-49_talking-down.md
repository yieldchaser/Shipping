# Market Commentary

## TALKING DOWN!

Over the recent weeks, following a sharp decline in Indian local steel plate prices (in excess of USD 60/LDT) has expectantly resulted in a sub-continent recycling sector that has been rocked and left many Recyclers spooked and opting to wait and monitor developments, rather than negotiate / commit on fresh tonnage.

However, the acute shortage of vessels continues and there is really very little working firm for Cash Buyers and Recyclers alike, to even offer on at the moment.

As such, this pause in activity may somewhat be artificial and the impact on prices may be limited in real terms, especially once we have actual workable candidates.

Such was the case with the one market sale of the week, a Suezmax tanker that still managed to fetch a decent USD 625/LT LDT basis an ‘as is’ Batam delivery, from one bullish Cash Buyer.

Most in the industry are still attempting to talk the market down, with either depreciating currencies in Pakistan and especially in Turkey (with record lows being recorded by the week), fluctuating steel plate prices in Bangladesh and India, and uncertainty on forward markets, being one of the chief concerns of the moment.

As such it may be a quieter end to the year across the sub-continent, as end Buyers take stock of a remarkable 2021, where prices have more than doubled and the industry now adjusts to these new realities, at and above USD 600/LT LDT.

# Bangladesh

## LISTLESS!

*Margin notes: Mostly quiet. | Tanker sale.*

It has been another largely listless week in Bangladesh, with very few end Buyers coming forth to even offer on units, as they remain fearful of some of the dramatic steel falls that have been posted in India over recent weeks and wonder whether their market may be similarly affected before the year turns.

The Chattogram steel market has not been affected as badly as its neighbours, with major steel falls and future prices suggesting a relatively stable to firm market moving into the final month of the year.

As such, the only firm unit for the week saw the Suezmax tanker JACOB (22,572 LDT) committed for a rather firm USD 625/LT LDT basis an ‘as is’ Batam, in gas free for man entry condition.

# India

## STILL REELING!

*Margin notes: Steel decline. | Currency fine.*

The Indian market is still reeling from some of the significant steel falls witnessed over the last few weeks, including this week, where prices fell an additional $14/Ton, resulting in an overall whopping decline of over USD 60/Ton in plate prices.

While this has certainly affected local sentiments and left most end Buyers in panic mode, a healthy majority are preferring to wait and watch market developments, rather than offering on any of the limited number of market vessels available.

The Indian Rupee continues trading in the mid Rs. 74s range against the U.S. Dollar, displaying few currency concerns – unlike in Pakistan and even Turkey, where the Lira is in freefall.

Notwithstanding, the constantly fluctuating nature of the local steel market does however suggest that prices could well regain their ground in due course and Indian offerings could remain competitive as we enter 2022.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# Pakistan

## EXCUSES EXCUSES!!

*Margin notes: Steel still firm. | Little chance.*

As is ever the case in the Indian sub-continent markets, when one market declines, others use this as an excuse to follow, and Pakistani Recyclers have historically led the way on this front and this this week was no different, despite their own steel prices firming nearly USD 25/Ton.

There have not been as many firm candidates to work on of late, but any available tonnage is being met with mostly indifference, especially at the higher market prices from even last week.

Of course, there seems there’s little chance for Gadani Recyclers to secure any anticipated bargains (especially on choice units), with the ongoing paucity in the supply of tonnage and despite the overall performance of Pakistani steel markets – local prices and sentiments might not remain subdued for too long.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**

# Turkey

## TUMULTOUS TIMES!!!

*Margin notes: Lira breaches 13.25 | Plummeting levels.*

The performance of the Turkish market has been extremely confusing and especially tumultuous of late. Although a massive jump in import steel prices had certainly assisted in the firming of Turkish demand and vessel offerings at the time, however, the Lira has fallen off a cliff ever since and import steel has also retreated by about USD 7/Ton this week.

The Lira, of note, has been extremely troubling, as it briefly breached a record low TRY 13.25 against the U.S. Dollar before it settled in at TRY 12.40 as the week ended.

This has shaken up the caution-meter for local Recyclers and though demand is omnipresent, it certainly has shaken the expectation of possibly plummeting levels in the coming week(s), in the minds of local Buyers.

**NO MARKET SALES REPORTED**
