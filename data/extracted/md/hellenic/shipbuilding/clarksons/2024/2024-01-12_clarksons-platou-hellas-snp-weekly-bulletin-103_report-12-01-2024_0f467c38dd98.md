# Clarksons Weekly Sale & Purchase Report - 2024-01-12

**Publisher**: Clarkson Hellas Ltd. | **Issue Date**: 2024-01-12 (Week 2)

## Desk Commentary

### Newbuilding
In tankers this week, Hyundai Mipo announced that Pertamina International Shipping ordered fifteen firm 50k dwt MR product tankers, with the series being split into three distinct series; five firm clean product MRs, five firm dirty products and five firm IMO2 / 3 product MRs. with the vessels will deliver from 1Q 2026 through to 4Q 2026. Erik Thun ordered two firm 8k dwt Chemical tankers at Ferus Smit, with the vessels set to deliver in June and October 2027 respectively, bringing their series to eight at the yard. James Fisher declared their two optional LNG dual-fuel 6k dwt Chemical tankers at CMJL Yangzhou, which are both expected to deliver in 2H 2026. The vessels will bring the series to four at the yard. In dry bulk, clients of Safe Bulkers ordered a firm 82k dwt Kamsarmax at Oshima with the vessels set to deliver in 3Q 2026. A busy week in gas carriers, with Nakilat announcing a joint order for two firm 174k CBM LNG Carriers and four firm 88k CBM VLAC's at Hyundai Samho. The LNG carriers will both deliver in 2027, with the first VLAC set to deliver in 2H 2026 and the three remaining vessels in 1H 2027. Hyundai HI announced contracting two firm 88k CBM VLACs for an unnamed owner, reported to be PascoGas, with the vessels expected to deliver in 4Q 2026 and 1Q 2027 respectively. In containers, Huangpu Wenchong announced that Elbdeich Reederei declared two optional methanol dual fuel 1,250 TEU Containerships, with the vessels slated for delivery in September and December of 2026. Brings their series to four vessels. Finally, in RoPaxes, Stena announced ordering an LNG dual fuel 1,100 passenger, 2,500 Lane Meter RoPax at CMJL (Weihai). The vessel is expected to deliver in 1Q 2026.

### Demolition
**Reaction to NGO pressure!** This week, a sudden announcement emanated from the Ministry of Environment in Turkey affecting any vessel arriving to Aliaga for ship recycling and causing the ship recyclers to rethink their policies when offering for tonnage. A notification was circulated to all local ship recyclers concerning the import procedure of a scrap vessel and according to this, the new application applies to any vessel, no matter what flag or departure port. It states that prior to arrival and pre-landing. permission from the harbour master of Aliaga, several documents are required, the main one being the IHM report (part 1 only). Obviously, as is always the case with any sudden new directive put into place, there are onward discussions internally if these can be eased at all and time will tell if the recyclers hands are untied. This was basically put into effect by a recent unproven attempt by the local NGO to stop one vessel entering Aliaga for inward clearance because of unsubstantiated reports about the vessel having toxic paints and other hazardous materials, an attempt at which was quickly ended by the Owner providing sufficient proof that no such materials were included. However, their action has since brought into this new procedure. Elsewhere, recycling buyers in the Indian sub. Continent remain starved of tonnage with the year starting on the backburner in Owners minds due to positive freight/charter rates. There was no change in the Bangladeshi Government following last weekend's elections as the Prime Minister again won a landslide victory and sworn in for a fifth term. What impact this will have on the domestic financial situation remains to be seen.
