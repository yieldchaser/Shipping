# Clarksons Weekly Sale & Purchase Report - 2023-09-01

**Publisher**: Clarkson Hellas Ltd. | **Issue Date**: 2023-09-01 (Week 35)

## Desk Commentary

### Newbuilding
In the gas carrier market this week, CSSC Dalian announced orders for three firm 175k CBM LNG Carriers for China Energy Shipping. The first two vessels will deliver in 1H 2027 and the final vessel in 2Q 2028. The yard also contracted a firm 7.5k CBM LCO2 carrier for Northern Lights, which will deliver in 2025. This is the third vessel in the series. In tankers, CSSC Chengxi announced contracts for four firm 50k dwt MR's with Singfar. The vessels are expected to deliver within 2026.

### Demolition
**A September to Remember?** As the days grow shorter now that the first of September has arrived, the engaging thoughts are whether this is the month which will see more supply of recycling candidates come into the market. We continue to see some of the vintage, Far Eastern controlled, dry bulk units coming for sale or arriving to the recycling shores having previously been sold off market. Following on from our report last week, Pakistan has jumped back into the arena and this week, a bulk carrier was sold delivery Pakistan for region USD 545/lidt, a significant increase on the last similar sale which was at USD 525.00 per Idt. However, some critics suggest this was a one-off price for a particular recycler who has a Letter of Credit readily available. Looking at the arrivals list to the Chattogram, Bangladesh, there seems to be several units arriving or waiting to deliver. There are still significant issues surrounding the domestic payment availability and therefore it will be interesting to see if these finally get beached on the local yards or, cash buyers lose patience and divert across to their counterparts. The Recycling volume, whilst on par with 2022 levels, remains notably down on a 10-year average but this certainly does not look set to continue and the oncoming winter months may see a turn of events with more supply arriving to the recycling yards. The questions, and concern, is can the Indian sub-Continent destinations improve their financial restrictions to cope for the eventual increase in supply?
