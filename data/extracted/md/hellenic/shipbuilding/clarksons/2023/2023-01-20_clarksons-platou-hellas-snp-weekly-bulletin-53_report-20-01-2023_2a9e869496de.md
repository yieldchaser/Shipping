# Clarksons Weekly Sale & Purchase Report - 2023-01-20

**Publisher**: Clarkson Hellas Ltd. | **Issue Date**: 2023-01-20 (Week 3)

## Desk Commentary

### Newbuilding
In tankers this week, it has been reported that Clients of Cardiff ordered three firm 115k dwt LR2 Product Tankers at COSCO HI (Yangzhou), with deliveries expected in June, September and December 2025. The vessels will be scrubber fitted. Erik Thun AB announced ordering four firm 8k dwt Chemical tankers at Ferus Smit SY, with the first vessel to be delivered in 2024 and the remaining vessels in 2025. In dry bulk, COSCO HI (Zhoushan) announced contracting four firm 63.6k dwt Ultramaxes for an unknown European owner, with the vessels to deliver in February, April, June and August 2025. It has been reported that Fujian Guohang Group ordered four firm plus two optional 73.8k dwt Panamax bulkers at Haitong Offshore, with the first firm vessel to deliver in 4Q 2024 and the remaining firm vessels in 2025. Erik Thun also announced ordering four firm 5.1k dwt mini-bulker at Ferus Smit SY, with the first firm vessel to deliver in 4Q 2024 and the remaining firm vessels in 2025. In the gas carrier market, Hyundai HI announced contracting three firm 200k CBM LNG Carriers for an unknown owner, reported to be Clients of Dynagas, with the vessels slated for delivery in May, August and November 2026. Hyundai Samho announced contracting two firm 174k CBM LNG Carriers for an unknown owner, with the vessels expected to deliver in August and November 2026. Hyundai HI also announced contracting a pair of LPG DF 88k CBM VLGC's for undisclosed owner, with the vessels set to deliver in November 2025 and February 2026. Another busy week in Car Carriers market, with CSSC Shanghai Waigaoqiao contracting five firm plus two conventionally fuelled 9,000 CEU PCTC's for Grimaldi. The vessels will hold an Ammonia ready class notation and will be fitted with Air Lubrication, with the first three firm vessels to deliver in 2025 and the remaining two in 2026. SAIC Anji placed two separate orders for conventionally fuelled 8,900 CEU PCTC's, with four delivering from CMJL Nanjing and three delivering from CSSC Jiangnan. At CMJL Nanjing, the first two vessels will deliver in 2025 and the remaining two in 2026. At Jiangnan, the first two vessels will deliver in 2025 and the remaining vessel in 2026. All the vessels will hold a Methanol ready class notation. Finally, in MPP's, Longships announced ordering four firm 8.6k dwt, 500 TEU MPP's at Atlas Shipyard. The vessels will have a diesel-electric hybrid propulsion system and will deliver from April 2024 through to January 2025.

### Demolition
As we hop towards the end of January, market activity remains distinctively quiet with the lack of available tonnage still evident and only a smattering of units seemingly workable. Some concerning news emanated from Pakistan this week where the Pakistan Ship Breakers Association made a direct appeal to their Prime Minister and government to save their industry from complete closure. The recent financial turmoil that has engulfed the country has resulted in the reluctance of banks to open Letters of Credit (L/C's) to import vessels for recycling which in turn affect the production of raw materials for the steel industry. The delay, or non-opening, of L/C's for ship recycling will have a negative effect on the ship recycling industry and subsequently the steel and construction sectors. The direct appeal from the association this week is reportedly a final push to obtain some assistance and help in saving all industries connected with ship recycling including construction and the domestic steel markets. Elsewhere, Bangladesh continues to suffer from a similar situation to Pakistan with only the smaller units able to deliver to the recycling yards and therefore, India continues to be the most dominant with the vibrant domestic steel markets providing firm price levels for any vessel that is circulated. With the Lunar holidays commencing from today, we are expecting a very quiet time in shipping as a whole next week.
