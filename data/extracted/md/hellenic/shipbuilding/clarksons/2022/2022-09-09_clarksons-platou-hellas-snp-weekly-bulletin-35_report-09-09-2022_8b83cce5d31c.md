# Clarksons Weekly Sale & Purchase Report - 2022-09-09

**Publisher**: Clarkson Hellas Ltd. | **Issue Date**: 2022-09-09 (Week 36)

## Desk Commentary

### Newbuilding
This week in the gas carrier market, DSME announced contracting seven firm 174k CBM LNG carriers, signed with various undisclosed owners. The vessels are understood to be linked to the Qatar Energy project and are set to deliver within 2026. Samsung announced contracting two firm 174k CBM LNG Carriers, these also with an undisclosed owner and again linked to the Qatar newbuilding program, with the vessels expected to deliver in 2025. Samsung also announced orders for two firm 174k CBM LNG Carriers for an unknown African owner, with both vessels slated for delivery in 1Q 2025. Clients of Evalend Shipping announced ordering three firm plus two optional 88k CBM VLGC's at Hyundai Heavy industries. The vessels will be equipped with LPG dual-fuel propulsion with the first two firm vessels to deliver within 2H 2024 and the final firm vessel in 2025. In containers, OY Langh Ship ordered three firm Ice Class 1A 1,200TEU Baltic Feeder vessels at PaxOcean Zhoushan, with the vessels all expected to deliver in 2H 2024. Taizhou Kouan confirmed contracting a firm 2,700 TEU Containership for Fujian Hensheng Shipping, with the vessel slated for delivery in 2Q 2024.

### Demolition
Moving to the recycling market, as anticipated, the sudden drop in the dry freight market has paved the way for more dry bulk units to circulate into the marketplace. There are some more capesize bulk carriers that have been reported sold this week, the question is whether they do finally arrive to the recycling yard or if indeed, the cash buyers attempt to trade further. Time will tell if we see them arrive at a recycling destination in due course. Price levels are moving upwards and it would appear that some higher L/C's are being approved by the banks in Bangladesh. Although it does appear that this is purely on a case-by-case scenario. More 'as is' transactions are taking place which is most likely to avoid any hazard for the ship owner should the vessel arrive to Chattogram and a Letter of Credit is not approved/opened. Thus, again more risk is attached to the cash buyer. India continues to look like the most stable destination in the Indian sub-Continent and Pakistan continue to suffer tremendously from their current flooding for which, our thoughts and prayers go to all those that are being affected. There are even reports of some yards being washed away and therefore, little activity to this area is expected.
