# Clarksons Weekly Sale & Purchase Report - 2022-10-07

**Publisher**: Clarkson Hellas Ltd. | **Issue Date**: 2022-10-07 (Week 40)

## Desk Commentary

### Newbuilding
In tankers this week, DH Shipbuilding (formerly Daehan) announced two sets of tanker orders. The yard announced that Euronav contracted two firm 157k dwt Suezmaxes with the vessels expected to deliver in 3Q and 4Q 2024 respectively. The vessels will be scrubber fitted. Daehan also published that Clients of Atlas Maritime ordered two firm 115k dwt Aframaxes at the yard, with both vessels slated for 1Q 2025 delivery. In the Gas carrier market, there were two separate orders at DSME linked to the Qatar Gas project this week. The yard announced an unknown European owner ordered four firm 174k CBM LNG Carriers, with the vessels all expected to deliver within 2026. DSME also disclosed that an Asian Owner, understood to be MISC Berhad ordered two firm 174k CBM LNG Carriers, also expected to deliver in 2026. In Containers, Maersk announced ordering six firm 17,000 TEU Containerships at Hyundai HI (Ulsan). The vessels with be Methanol dual fuel and will deliver throughout 2025.

### Demolition
There has been widespread chatter this week about container vessels possibly circulating into the recycling market, but it is still too early to get excited. Freight and time charter rates in this sector have indeed seen major softening, with the Shanghai Comprehensive Index (an indicator of spot container freight rates), down 62% from early 2022 and falling below 2,000 index points for the first time since November 2020. We have seen two container feeder types sold to date this year, both recent sales, however many potential candidates are likely still on charter employment from earlier in the year and freight/time charter rates, while significantly softening, remain above 'pre-Covid' 2019 levels (for now). Recycling is likely to pick up into 2023 as markets soften further, while the orderbook for containerships shows an enormous volume of tonnage to be added to the global fleet – this should then ensure more vintage container vessels will open for recycling. In the meantime, the lack of available candidates for recycling continues. There have been some small private sales taking place but certainly not sufficient to quench the thirst of the recyclers as they seek to fill the gaps in their yards. The annual Tradewinds Ship Recycling conference is on the horizon in Dubai (Tuesday 18th and Wednesday 19th October) although the usual 'deals in the corner' discussions will most likely not occur, given the current quiet climate.
