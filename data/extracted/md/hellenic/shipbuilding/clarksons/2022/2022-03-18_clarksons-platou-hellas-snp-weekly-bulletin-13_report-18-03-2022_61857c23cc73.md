# Clarksons Weekly Sale & Purchase Report - 2022-03-18

**Publisher**: Clarkson Hellas Ltd. | **Issue Date**: 2022-03-18 (Week 11)

## Desk Commentary

### Newbuilding
In the gas carrier market, Daewoo (DSME) announced contracting two firm 174k CBM LNG Carriers from an unknown owner, with both vessels expected to be delivered within 2H 2025. In dry bulk this week, Jiangsu Dajin announced contracting four firm 40k dwt Handysize bulkers from Reederei H. Vogemann, with deliveries expected to run from Q4 2023 to Q2 2024. In Containers, it's been reported that Evergreen Marine ordered one additional 24,000 TEU Containership at CSSC Jiangnan, with delivery of the vessel expected in 2026. Clients of Danaos announced ordering two firm plus two optional 7,100TEU Containerships at CSSC Dalian Shipbuilding. The vessels will have a Methanol Ready Class Notation with the firm vessels set to be delivered in 2Q and 3Q 2024 respectively. Fujian Mawei Shipbuilding announced orders six firm plus four optional 2,900TEU Containerships from Eastern Pacific Shipping, with deliveries of the first five firm vessels expected in 2024 and the final firm vessel in 2025. It's been reported that Penglai Jinglu Shipyard took orders for two firm 1,800TEU container vessels from MTT Shipping, with deliveries of both vessels set for 2Q 2024, bringing their series to five at the yard overall.

### Demolition
**Mini Correction!** This week saw price levels tumble southwards on the back of falls in global steel/commodity markets against the speculation that a peace agreement is near fruition between Russia and Ukraine which would allow a resumption of increased steel exports. In particular, the domestic steel markets in Bangladesh and India witnessed a sharp correction at the beginning of the week with reports India fell by some USD 60-70/ldt and Bangladesh by some USD 30-40/ldt for ship recycling. Having seen a continued rise in rates over several weeks, the recycling market has certainly mirrored global steel markets with these significant drops, however the latter part of the week did find finally see a sense of stability return and talk of levels rebounding. However, even if a peace agreement was reached, and let us pray that it will be soon, it is likely to be months before any lifting of sanctions will be discussed and steel exports allowed to resume.
