# Market Insight

# By Vasilis Fousekis, Research Analyst

With El Niño now established and forecasters pointing to a strong event, the question for shipping is where it affects freight first. Timing matters as much as strength: the largest effects on crops, water levels and trade usually arrive in the first half of the following year, which places the main freight impact in 2027.

El Niño is a dynamic disruption and its effects vary by region. Drier conditions are expected across Central America, including the Panama Canal watershed, while the impact on Brazil is different by region, with drier conditions in the central and northern grain producing states. Argentina and the southern United States, by contrast, tend to receive more rain. India, Southeast Asia and Australia are set to face droughts.

For dry bulk, the canal is the most direct link. The Panama Canal Authority, after cutting daily transits to 32 in September – only the second such reduction in history, eased restrictions following late-September rain. The relief, though, may not last. Panama's meteorological institute expects the dry season to begin as early as the second half of November, cutting into what is normally the wettest month, when Gatun Lake is replenished. The Authority projects an average of 29.5 crossings a day over the coming fiscal year. It is also auctioning long term slots for the older Panamax locks for the first time, covering January to early April 2027. Re-routing is already visible, as deviation through the Strait of Magellan rose sharply in August and September, supporting Panamax and Kamsarmax utilisation, even when volumes are flat.

In coal, El Niño brings drought to Southeast Asia, reducing hydropower output, and increasing coal burn, notably in India and Vietnam, contributing to the shift to coal as a source of energy, in a period of high natural gas prices. However, seaborne trade is more selective. India and China are expected to import less thermal coal this year, as they draw on high domestic stocks, impacting total seaborne thermal volumes, with growth concentrated on short-haul Southeast Asian routes, such as Indonesia or Australia to Vietnam, limiting the tonne-mile gain.

In grains, El Niño is expected to affect Brazil's 2026/27 crop mainly through irregular rainfall in central and northern Brazil, while the south faces excess rain. The key risk lies between November and January, when dry spells and heat could reduce soy-

bean yields in Mato Grosso and the northern states, leaving the outlook for a record harvest skewed to the downside. Any delay to the soybean cycle would also push second-crop corn planting beyond its ideal window, threatening yields and Brazil's corn exports in the second half of 2027. Some offset in South American grain trade may come from Argentina, where favorable rainfall is set to support export volumes. The United States stands to benefit, as a record soybean crop and the second-largest corn crop on record leave it well placed to fill the gap, with a longer export window into Asia in early 2027 and stronger demand for its corn later in the year. The constraint is logistics rather than supply, as Panama Canal restrictions during the dry season would limit how much of this opportunity US exporters can capture.

Drought in the Central American Dry Corridor could also lift short-haul grain imports from the US Gulf, a modest gain for Handysize and Supramax owners. In Australia, a dry spring is already reducing wheat yields in the eastern states as the harvest begins, although large carry-in stocks could cushion exports, including shipments to China.

Effects of the phenomenon extend as well beyond dry bulk, with a notable example being Indonesian palm oil. Prolonged drought and severe wildfires are likely to lower exports, pushing importers towards more distant vegetable oil origins and lengthening hauls for product tanker owners.

Overall, El Niño effects on shipping spread through reshuffling of logistics and trade flows rather than through large swings in volumes, as also past events showed. Panama is the clearest channel for dry bulk and the most likely to tighten into early 2027, while other important impacts include potential for uplift in thermal coal demand due to declining hydropower and shifts in seaborne grain trade patterns.

# Tanker Chartering

Crude tanker freight rates continued to climb to record highs as sustained cargo demand met increasingly restricted vessel availability across most regions. Middle Eastern crude exports have recovered to around 80% of pre-war levels, yet operational adjustments amid disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz, including rerouting via Sidi Kerir/SUMED and STS transfers off Oman, continue to limit effective supply. Meanwhile, scarce VLCC tonnage has prompted charterers to split larger cargoes into Suezmax-sized parcels, extending upward pressure across crude tanker segments. Against this backdrop, the BDTI surged by 32% w-o-w to 7,625.

VLCCs maintained their strong upward trend across all major trading regions, as brisk fixing activity and a dwindling pool of available vessels further reinforced owners' bargaining power. With cargo flows showing little sign of slowing, average VLCC TCE earnings rose by 19% on the week to $936,646/day, underscoring the extraordinary scale of the market's advance.

Suezmax spot earnings also reached fresh highs, with average TCE returns rising by 13% on a weekly basis, to $699,887/day. The Atlantic Basin remained the principal driving force, with West African activity benefiting from increased VLCC cargo splitting, while sustained Brazilian demand continued to absorb tonnage. Lucrative western employment drew additional vessels towards the Atlantic, further depleting eastern availability and sustaining rates despite subdued Fujairah STS activity. The USG was a notable exception, with limited fresh enquiry keeping activity muted.

Aframax gains were most pronounced in Asia and the North Sea. In Asia, scarce prompt tonnage and attractive Pacific returns strengthened owners' leverage, while North Sea charterers raised bids to discourage vessels from seeking employment elsewhere. The Mediterranean also advanced, benefiting from stronger Atlantic earnings and port delays, although fresh enquiry eased towards the weekend. Aframax TCE earnings soared by 39% w-o-w to $384,665/day.

# Dry Bulk Chartering

The dry bulk market saw uneven trends last week. The BDI averaged 6% lower on the week, primarily reflecting the correction in Capesize rates.

Capesize earnings declined amid persistent Atlantic weakness, despite comparatively resilient conditions in the Pacific. Excess vessel supply and an increasing focus on later loading dates put the South Atlantic under sustained pressure, while limited enquiry kept the North Atlantic subdued. Pacific iron ore activity offered a brief counterweight, lifting rates, before momentum faded towards the weekend.

After a hesitant start, Kamsarmax/Panamax rates edged higher as cargo demand picked up across both basins. Increased transatlantic activity and South American fronthaul interest improved the Atlantic outlook, though abundant open tonnage capped further gains. Pacific business also revived, driven by post-holiday Chinese demand for Australian and North Pacific cargoes, while muted Indian interest in coal cargoes kept the recovery measured.

Ultramax/Supramax market remained tepid, with marginal rate movements despite contrasting regional developments. The US Gulf and East Coast South America faced sluggish demand, whereas Continent scrap cargoes continued to generate attractive returns. The Pacific fared somewhat better, benefiting from North Pacific grain shipments and additional enquiry from Indonesia and Vietnam. Despite muted overall activity, tightening prompt tonnage availability sustained cautious optimism for late October.

Handysize segment exhibited divergent regional trends last week, with the South Atlantic emerging as a pocket of support, as strong cargo flows and tightening vessel supply underpinned rates. Conversely, the US Gulf remained under pressure, with insufficient enquiry to absorb the extensive tonnage list. The Continent and Mediterranean remained broadly stable, with little movement in freight levels, while holiday-related disruptions curtailed Pacific activity without materially affecting rates.

# Macro-economic headlines

In the United States, the trade deficit widened to $105.6bn in August, from a revised $92.8bn in July, exceeding market expectations of a $100.8bn shortfall. The widening was driven by a surge in imports, particularly of industrial supplies and raw materials, which outpaced the increase in exports.

In Germany, industrial production rose by 2.33% y-o-y in August, surpassing market estimations of a 0.7% increase and reversing a 1.47% decline recorded in July.

In Japan, PPI eased to 6.8% y-o-y in September, from 7.6% in August, below the consensus forecast of 7.6%.

In India, the Manufacturing and Services PMI rose to 55.90 in September, up from 54.30 in August, although it fell short of market expectations of 56.50.
