## Weekly Market Report

## Market Insight
<u>By Nikos Tagoulis, Head of Research Department</u>

The European gas market is heading into the winter heating season against a challenging backdrop, shaped by relatively low inventories, constrained Middle Eastern LNG availability in view of the phase-out of Russian gas. EU gas storage currently stands at around 71%, compared with a five-year seasonal average of about 86%, leaving the market with a considerably thinner buffer heading into winter. The phase-out will advance from 1 January 2027, when long-term Russian LNG imports are due to be prohibited, followed by remaining pipeline imports at the latest 1 November 2027. At the same time, the US is taking a growing share of Europe's LNG supply, accounting for around 58% of EU imports in 2025 and about 62% so far in 2026, according to LSEG.

That exposure has been amplified by persistent strain in global LNG supply, as Middle East disruptions curtail Qatari exports and QatarEnergy extends force majeure on deliveries to several term buyers into the winter period. In parallel, the company has turned to US LNG to cover the resulting shortfall, buying replacement cargoes and discussing further purchases to honour its contractual commitments. Prolonged weakness in Qatari output is therefore tightening the market from both sides: fewer volumes are available, while QatarEnergy's own buying is adding fresh demand for the very flexible cargoes on which Europe relies.

US LNG has consequently become important not only to Europe's own gas balance, but also as a source of flexibility when disruptions emerge. The gradual removal of Russian gas from the European energy mix is increasing the call on alternative LNG suppliers. In Asia, however, current competition for uncommitted volumes has been less intense than the tight market might suggest. With spot LNG expensive and coal offering a cheaper option for power generation, the more price-sensitive Asian importers have adopted a wait-and-see approach, limiting purchases in the hope that prices will ease, although some markets, such as South Korea, still need to replenish inventories. This hesitancy is giving European buyers more room to attract flexible cargoes as they seek to rebuild storage.

As Europe becomes more reliant on US LNG, energy security is taking on greater relevance within the broader transatlantic framework. While it is unlikely to determine policy on its own, the growing importance of US supply adds another consideration to Europe's strategic calculus, particularly as potential friction with Washington could carry higher economic and energy-security costs. LNG is therefore becoming not only a commercial issue, but also a more significant component of Europe-US strategic ties.

At the same time, Europe is looking to broaden its LNG supply base over the longer term, with Canada emerging as a potential new source. The proposed Ksi Lisims LNG project in British Columbia, with initial export capacity of around 6 mtpa targeted for 2029, has already attracted interest from German buyers including SEFE and Uniper, while France has also signalled interest in future Canadian LNG supply. With deliveries expected to build from the early 2030s, these developments underline Europe's broader effort to diversify its LNG portfolio and reduce concentration over time.

For LNG shipping, the key factor will be the arbitrage between Europe and Asia. A sufficiently wide Asian premium to offset the higher costs of the longer voyage would encourage more US cargoes to head east, supporting tonne-mile demand, while stronger European pricing would favour shorter-haul Atlantic trades. At the same time, the phase-out of Russian LNG imports could provide additional support to ton-mile demand as Europe replaces nearby Russian volumes with cargoes sourced from more distant suppliers. With Europe needing to rebuild storage and price-sensitive Asian buyers holding back amid elevated prices, more US cargoes could be drawn to the Continent, particularly if alternative supply remains constrained.

In these market conditions, Europe increasingly depends on its ability to compete for flexible LNG cargoes amid constrained supply. Low inventories, reduced Qatari availability and the phase-out of Russian volumes are reinforcing the role of US LNG in the European balance, while Asian importers could return to the market more actively during the winter heating season, or should prices retreat. With LNG crucial to Europe's energy security, growing reliance on US supply increases concentration risk, while also adding a new strategic dimension to transatlantic diplomacy.

## Tanker Chartering

Last week, crude tanker spot markets continued to strengthen, driven primarily by a sharp rise in Aframax rates, while earnings for larger crude tankers held broadly stable at historically elevated levels. The BDTI averaged 5,366, a 13% increase w-o-w.

The VLCC segment remains exceptionally strong, although rates retreated marginally amid reduced activity in some regions. In the Arabian Gulf, firm cargo flows, STS activity and constrained tonnage continued to support the market, while several private deals reduced visibility. West Africa was quieter on weaker demand, with charterers increasingly splitting stems across smaller vessels, which offered more favorable economics. US Gulf activity was also limited, as some cargoes moved onto smaller units, adding to vessel supply. In Brazil, enquiry is still insufficient to absorb the open tonnage, and further cargoes will be needed to re-balance the market. VLCC TCEs eased by 1% w-o-w to \$714,143/day.

The Suezmax market remained resilient despite more muted activity across some regions, with the Middle East providing solid support, as healthy demand and firm VLCC earnings continued to generate split cargoes for the segment. In West Africa, activity faded as the week progressed, lengthening the tonnage list. The Med stayed balanced, with a steady flow of enquiries absorbing available ships and keeping levels stable, while the US Gulf came under pressure. Suezmax TCEs slipped by 1% on the week to \$299,424/day.

The Aframax segment was the week's outperformer, with earnings climbing across most trading areas. In the Mediterranean, the backlog of end-month cargoes met limited tonnage, and although enquiry eased thereafter, the robust US Gulf market prevented any meaningful correction. The North Sea followed, supported by a thinner local tonnage list as some vessels repositioned. In Asia, a constrained tonnage pool allowed owners to hold out for higher freight levels despite sluggish activity. Overall, the average TCE rose by 44% w-o-w to \$234,370/day.

## Dry Bulk Chartering

The dry bulk sector posted a positive performance overall last week, led by gains in the Kamsarmax/Panamax segment. Activity, however, slowed towards the end of the week amid China's Mid-Autumn Festival holiday. The BDI averaged 3,432, up 2% w-o-w.

The Capesize market was largely stable last week, although activity eased towards the end of the period, particularly in the Pacific, due to the Mid-Autumn Festival holiday in China. West Australia nonetheless recorded steady fixing activity, albeit at softer levels. The Atlantic provided some support, with strong enquiry and reduced tonnage availability in the North Atlantic underpinning rates.

On the Kamsarmax/Panamax side, earnings firmed overall, driven by increased activity in the Atlantic basin. Stronger enquiry in the North Atlantic and Mediterranean, coupled with relatively scarce tonnage, supported the market. South America added further momentum, as Asian grain buyers moved to secure vessels ahead of China's holiday break, bringing some shipments forward. In the Pacific, Australian coal shipments provided steady support, while Indonesian trades recovered later in the week.

The Ultramax/Supramax market edged higher, amid mixed regional trends. Support was concentrated on the Continent, where sustained scrap exports kept tonnage well employed, while the North Pacific held firm as tight vessel availability underpinned rates despite limited fresh enquiry. By contrast, the Mediterranean remained under pressure amid tepid demand, prompting owners to consider repositioning opportunities. Activity in the US Gulf was subdued, although underlying demand remained healthy.

Handysizes recorded a positive week, underpinned mainly by firmer Atlantic activity. The Continent and Mediterranean gained momentum on stronger scrap demand, while the US Gulf remained well supported despite rising tonnage availability. Elsewhere, the South Atlantic was steady, while Asia lagged as holidays restricted activity, though tightening vessel supply cushioned the quieter demand backdrop.
