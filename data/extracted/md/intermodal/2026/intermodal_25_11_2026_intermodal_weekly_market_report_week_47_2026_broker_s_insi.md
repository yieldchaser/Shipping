# Weekly Market Report

## Week 47 | Tuesday 24th November 2026

Market Insight

By Nikos Tagoulis, Head of Research Department

----

Brazil traditionally holds a prominent role in Atlantic Basin crude trading, with the expansion of its offshore production further underpinning its influence in global oil flows. Rising output is increasing export availability and providing greater flexibility in directing cargoes between European and Asian markets, while offering buyers a source of crude supply largely insulated from geopolitical disruptions. Moreover, on the policy side, favourable trade terms under the EU-Mercosur agreement provide an additional commercial incentive for crude shipments to Europe.

The driving force behind Brazil's production growth is its vast pre-salt fields, where substantial oil and gas reservoirs lie beneath thick salt formations deep below the Atlantic seabed. According to the country's National Agency of Petroleum, Natural Gas and Biofuels (ANP), crude output reached a record 4.71 million b/d in September 2026, up 20.3% y-o-y, with nearly 84% originating from pre-salt fields. At the heart of this expansion lies the Santos Basin, off Brazil's southeastern coast, which accounts for around 80% of national crude production and hosts some of the country's largest offshore oil fields, notably Búzios and Mero. The scale of the Búzios oil field is particularly striking, with production reaching 1.19 million b/d, equivalent to approximately one-quarter of Brazil's total crude output. With additional FPSOs scheduled to commence operations at the Búzios field in 2027, Brazil is well positioned to consolidate its role as a major crude exporter outside OPEC.

The rise in output is also reflected in seaborne volumes, with Europe gaining ground as a destination without displacing Brazil's established Asian markets. According to LSEG data, Brazilian crude exports rose 21.9% y-o-y to 719 million barrels during January-October 2026. EU-bound shipments increased 19.3% to 137.8 million barrels, while China received 15% more and India's intake almost tripled. The sharper change in European trade came after April. Following a decline of 12.9% y-o-y during January-April, EU-bound volumes rebounded by 41% over May-October, with October shipments alone more than doubling y-o-y.

This increase may partly be attributed to the EU-Mercosur agreement. Signed in January and provisionally applied from 1 May, the agreement aims to deepen bilateral trade relations. However, Brazilian government's decision to impose a 12% crude export tax in March gave Mercosur's preferential provisions greater commercial importance. Under the agreement, crude exports to the EU are subject to a reduced 6% rate, compared with the full 12% levy imposed on non-EU destinations, providing EU buyers an advantage in securing Brazilian barrels. The timing is particularly relevant for the EU, given its efforts to diversify away from Russian energy and the geopolitical uncertainty affecting oil supplies and tanker movements through the Arabian Gulf.

For the crude tanker market, the impact of Brazil's expanding crude exports largely depends on the geographical distribution of trade flows. Increased shipments to Europe support employment for VLCCs and Suezmaxes, which account for the bulk of these flows. However, while additional cargoes to Europe generate greater transatlantic activity, any diversion of shipments previously destined for Asian longer-haul markets, would shorten average voyage distances, potentially offsetting some of the gains in ton-miles. At the same time, India's near-tripling of Brazilian crude imports lends support to long-haul tanker demand, while rising Brazilian production creates scope for further growth in export volumes. Moreover, Brazil's rising offshore production also supports demand for shuttle tankers able to operate in open-sea conditions, equipped with dynamic positioning (DP) systems, which transport crude from FPSOs to coastal terminals or STS transfer locations.

Aside from tankers, the impact for the offshore segment is clearly positive. Brazil represents a principal employment region for such vessels, with sustained investment in deepwater field development underpinning demand for PSVs, AHTSs and specialised subsea units. Further expansion of offshore activity is expected to strengthen demand for offshore vessel capacity, creating additional employment opportunities.

Whether EU retains its preferential tax advantage beyond 2026 depends on Brazil's crude export levy. The tax has been extended through three successive government resolutions, most recently in November, until 31 December 2026. If extended, EU-bound shipments would continue to benefit from the lower rate. If the levy expires, exports to all destinations would become exempt, removing Europe's relative advantage and potentially intensifying competition for Brazilian barrels. More broadly, Brazil's growing offshore oil output is establishing its position as a major global supplier, with both the tanker and offshore segments poised to benefit.

## Tanker Chartering

Crude tanker spot freight markets recovered following the previous week's losses. Robust Atlantic crude flows drove much of the improvement, with USG charterers generally favouring Suezmaxes and Aframaxes over VLCCs. Nevertheless, the BDTI averaged 4,678, down 2% w-o-w.

In the VLCC segment, the Middle East led the improvement, as increased cargo demand and steady fixing activity coincided with shrinking tonnage pool. West African enquiry remained muted, and despite a tighter vessel list, competitive Suezmax rates continued to divert cargoes away from VLCCs. Stronger Brazilian exports supported Atlantic freight rates and owners' sentiment. The USG remained sluggish, with competition from smaller tanker classes limiting fresh VLCC business. Overall, VLCC TCE earnings reached \$543,172/day, up 4% w-o-w.

The Suezmax market was primarily driven by the USG, where activity accelerated considerably during the week. Charterers initially favoured Aframaxes, but surging rates in that segment shifted preference to Suezmaxes, attracting additional cargoes and absorbing prompt tonnage. ## Dry Bulk Chartering The Capesize segment displayed contrasting regional dynamics last week, with the West Australia-China route rebounding post mid-week, as the Pacific market benefited from the active presence of all three major miners. The Atlantic basin experienced more subdued conditions, with the South Atlantic providing the main counterweight, as a shrinking pool of available vessels coincided with a recovery in cargo demand, reinforcing owners’ bargaining power. Conversely, lacklustre cargo activity and ample tonnage availability continued to depress earnings in the North Atlantic.

In the Kamsarmax/Panamax market, the backdrop remained largely unchanged last week, with the Atlantic continuing to outperform the Pacific. In the North Atlantic, firmness was primarily demand-driven, with steady mineral and transatlantic grain enquiries against limited prompt vessel supply. In ECSA, positive sentiment largely reflected forward tonnage constraints. By contrast, the Pacific continued to grapple with excess tonnage and tepid cargo demand. A prolonged divergence between the two basins could incentivise more vessels to ballast towards the Atlantic, potentially easing regional vessel scarcity and limiting further freight gains.

Newbuilding Market

Newbuilding activity was robust, with 18 orders for 80 firm units, plus 1 option, of which 59 vessels were in the offshore segment placed by COSL.

On the tanker side, Advantage Tankers ordered 2 ULCCs of 320k dwt at Hanwha Ocean, due in 2029, for \$136m each. Thenamaris also booked 2 scrubber fitted units of 303k dwt at New Times Shipbuilding, for 2030 delivery, priced at \$130m each. Neda Maritime followed at the same yard with 1 firm, plus 1 optional, unit of identical size and delivery. Teekay signed for 2 tankers of 157k dwt at DH Shipbuilding, scheduled for 2030, for \$93m per vessel, while Aegean Shipping inked 2 ice-class 1C product carriers of 114k dwt at COSCO HI Yangzhou, also due in 2030. GEM exercised options for 2 units of 74k dwt at K Shipbuilding, due in 2029, at \$66m per ship. Stamford Shipping secured 4 MRs of 49.9k dwt at Nantong CIMC SOE for the same year.

In offshore, COSL placed an order for 59 vessels at Fujian Shipbuilding, with deliveries spread across 2028-2030. The shipbuilding programme comprises of 46 LNG-powered PSVs: 7 units of 4k dwt, 23 units of 3k dwt and 14 units of 2k dwt, plus 2 same vessels of 10k hp and 6.5k hp. The remaining 13 are conventional AHTS, namely 4 units of 15k hp, 6 vessels of 12k hp and 3 more of 8k hp.

Elsewhere, Sanjiang Shipping inked 2 dual-fuel VLECs of 102k cbm at Jiangnan, due in 2030, for \$160m each, while K Line added 2 LPG gas carriers of 90k cbm at HD Hyundai Samho with delivery in 2030, for \$116m each. Singapore Shipping Corp booked 2 LNG PCTCs of 7k ceu at GSI, due in 2031.
