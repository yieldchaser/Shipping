## Week 46 | Tuesday 17th November 2026

**Market Insight**

By Vasilis Fousekis, Research Analyst

This week’s spotlight is on the LPG carriers, as VLGC earnings are at exceptional levels. The trade is also now attracting capacity from the broader gas segment: the ACS SP Nereus, a 100k cbm VLEC, has loaded the first LPG cargo for its ship type at Houston. Rates on the US routes surpassed the record-high levels of September 2023 and the summer of 2026 this week, as fixture activity in the West held steady. Houston to Chiba is currently traded at about a TCE return of \$184,600/day and Houston to Flushing at approximately \$233,600/day TCE.

Behind these earnings lies the continued loss of Middle East supply. Hormuz has remained effectively under geopolitical uncertainty in November, with talks on a reopening yet to produce an agreement. Volumes remain below 50% of pre-war levels and Red Sea loadings remain exposed to further attacks. Long haul US cargoes, therefore, have become the main replacement for lost Middle East supply for Asian buyers, keeping the Baltic LPG index to 21k territory, above the Q3 average.

Vessel availability, rather than export capacity, had so far set the pace of US flows, which are crucial for the LPG trade. A constraint has since emerged on the supply side. Phillips 66, a US based company which operates the Freeport LPG export terminal in Texas, declared force majeure on butane loadings after a power outage and compressor failure incident in early November, lifting US butane prices and raising concern over prompt availability, although overall US LPG loadings have so far shown no clear slowdown. A prolonged outage would mainly tighten butane supply to Asian buyers, rather than reduce the number of cargoes available to fix.

In Asia, demand remains firm and at the same time price sensitive. Chinese imports rose by about 1m tonnes q-o-q in Q3, supported by positive margins at propane-to-propylene plants. India consumed 2.6m tonnes in October, approaching the monthly volumes seen before the Middle East conflict, while holiday-season inventory building may extend import strength into November and December. However, if spot prices of propane and butane remain elevated, further buying by Indian importers is likely to slow, especially without any state support.

On the supply side, the outlook is less comfortable, with 8 VLGCs delivered within Q3 and 3 more expected within Q4. About 170 VLGC and VLAC newbuildings, totalling 15.5m cbm, are due through 2030, equivalent to roughly 40% of the existing fleet. Contracting has continued at a strong pace, with 8.5m cbm of LPG carriers ordered in the first nine months and a further 14 large gas carriers signed in October. With the fleet averaging about 11.7 years, demolition is unlikely to offset much of this growth.

Looking ahead, long-haul US exports and Asian restocking should keep earnings well supported in the near term, despite periodic corrections. Seaborne volumes are expected to fall by around 12% this year, yet longer voyages should still lift tonne-miles by about 2.5%. High rates are also drawing marginal tonnage into the market, as the Nereus lifting shows, although such crossovers are likely to stay occasional, mainly when ethane employment is weak. The orderbook is a more significant source of new capacity. A potential settlement of the Hormuz crisis should support the rebound projected for 2027, but a possible recovery in Middle East exports would also shorten average voyages, just as deliveries accelerate in 2027 and 2028. The key question is whether US export growth can absorb that tonnage once trade routes normalize, with the timing of a settlement between Iran and the U.S. remaining the decisive factor.

## Tanker Chartering

Crude tanker markets underwent a broad-based correction last week, as recovering Middle Eastern crude exports reduced Asian reliance on longer-haul Atlantic Basin supplies, eroding tonne-mile demand amid increasing vessel availability. Despite ongoing disruptions, flows through Hormuz and overall Middle Eastern exports continued to recover, approaching pre-war levels, with a growing number of vessels transiting with their AIS switched off. Renewed vessel attacks and strikes on Saudi oil infrastructure highlighted persistent security risks, although Red Sea loadings remained largely unaffected. Against this backdrop, markets increasingly discounted a gradual normalisation of regional trade flows, potentially reinforced by the Democrats' victory in the US midterm elections and expectations of diplomatic de-escalation.

The retreat from earlier record-high freight levels therefore appears to reflect an unwinding of the geopolitical risk premium, rather than a definitive resolution. Meanwhile, declining crude prices exerted additional downward pressure on freight rates, as transportation costs accounted for a larger proportion of commodity values.

## Dry Bulk Chartering

A firmer week for the spot dry bulk market saw gains across all vessel segments, led by the larger sizes. The BDI averaged 2,935, up 9% w-o- w.

The Capesize segment extended the previous week’s gains, with the Pacific taking the lead as stronger miner activity drove increased iron ore shipments from Western Australian ports to China. The Atlantic, meanwhile, presented a more uneven landscape, with improving transatlantic and fronthaul demand underpinning rates in the north, while subdued activity on the Brazil-China and West Africa- China trades weighed on the southern basin.

The Kamsarmax/Panamax market displayed divergent trends, largely reflecting regional differences in tonnage supply conditions. The Atlantic emerged as the main source of support, with healthy transatlantic demand reinforced by a relatively tight vessel supply, particularly in ECSA. Northern Europe offered a note of caution, however, as a growing pool of open tonnage began to temper sentiment. The Pacific faced the opposite backdrop, with an expanding tonnage list overshadowing Australian mineral demand, while limited enquiries in the North Pacific and Indonesia weighed on rates.

Newbuilding Market

Newbuilding activity remained limited last week, with 8 orders reported concentrated mainly in the tanker and gas carrier segments, while bulkers recorded no new contracts.

On the tanker side, DHT ordered a 320k dwt VLCC at Hanwha Ocean, scrubber fitted and ammonia/methanol ready, for delivery in 2029. JHI Steamship booked 2 MR product tankers of 50k dwt at K Shipbuilding, due in the same year.

In gas carriers, Celsius Tankers secured a 180k cbm LNG carrier at Samsung HI for \$256.7m, due in 2029 and against TC contract, for \$256.7m. Thenamaris contracted 2 gas carriers of 174k cbm at HD Hyundai HI for \$247m each, also for 2029 delivery, while Naftomar ordered 4 LPG carriers of 9k cbm at HD Hyundai for \$51m each, with delivery in 2028-2029. SwissChemGas placed an order for a pair of VLAC units of 93k cbm at Hengli Heavy Industries, due in 2028-2029.

Elsewhere, MSC signed for a pair of PCTCs of 8.6k ceu at CMHI Weihai, due in 2030-2031, for \$100m each and Ve-Na Di Navigazione ordered a methanol ready general cargo vessel of 9.2k dwt at CSSC Guangxi, with delivery in 2028.
