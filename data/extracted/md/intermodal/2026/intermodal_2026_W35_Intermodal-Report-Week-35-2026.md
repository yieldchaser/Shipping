## Market Insight
**By Nikos Tagoulis, Senior Analyst**

The EUA market maintained its upward trend in August, despite the quieter trading conditions and lower liquidity that typically characterize the holiday period. The advance extended the recovery that began in late March and left the EU ETS on a constructive footing heading into autumn. The Front Dec-26 EUA contract averaged **€82.36 /ton** during August and at the time of writing read **€83.30 /ton**, around 6.5% above the 2026 y-t-d average. Prices remained within a relatively narrow range during the month, and the contract continued to trade above its 50-, 100- and 200-day moving averages.

The European gas market has contributed to the firming of EUAs. Prices at the Dutch TTF hub rose significantly as geopolitical tensions leading to Qatari volumes off market, low European storage levels and competition from Asian buyers for LNG cargoes heightened concerns over winter supply.

Although the correlation between front-month TTF gas prices and EUAs fluctuated in August, it has strengthened recently, suggesting that prevailing gas-market dynamics are supporting EUA demand. The principal transmission channel is fuel-switching economics: higher gas prices make coal more competitive with gas for power generation, encouraging more carbon-intensive output and increasing utilities' demand for allowances.

Furthermore, recent economic signals add to this supportive backdrop, pointing to firm industrial demand. The August euro-zone manufacturing PMI reached its strongest level in more than four years, accompanied by stronger new orders, a recovery in exports and the first increase in factory employment after more than three years. Together, these developments strengthen the outlook for EUA demand.

Despite the positive backdrop, traders remain cautious. Investment funds have reduced their net-long exposure in recent weeks, while commercial participants have continued to increase their forward exposure, reflecting hedging strategies built around expectations of further EUA price increases. The divergence indicates that commercial buying remains driven by risk management even as speculative interest weakens. Compliance buying adds another source of demand ahead of September 30, including from shipping companies as the maritime sector's obligations continue to phase in. In 2026, shipping companies must surrender allowances covering 70% of their verified 2025 emissions within the scope of the EU ETS, rising to 100% of 2026 emissions in the following year, with higher EUA prices increasing the compliance cost of voyages covered by the scheme.

Looking ahead, policy is re-emerging as a major driver. The Commission's July proposal for the post-2030 EU ETS would slow the contraction of the emissions cap, extend conditional free allocation and ease the operation of the Market Stability Reserve. The measures would leave more allowances in circulation than under the present framework, easing concerns about future supply constraints. The initial market response was positive, however, this is only the starting point for further negotiations, leaving scope for revision.

Beyond the EU ETS reform, the proposed linkage with the UK ETS remains an open issue. A summit expected to advance negotiations in July was postponed following the change in UK leadership, leaving the timetable uncertain. Progress towards linkage should narrow the spread between UKAs and EUAs as UK allowance prices move closer to EU levels. Once implemented, the linkage could become a bearish factor for EUA prices by expanding the pool of available allowances, although its impact would depend on supply conditions across both systems.

Concluding, the EUA outlook for the remainder of 2026 will depend largely on developments across the European energy complex. Geopolitical uncertainty, disruption to Middle Eastern LNG flows and Europe's efforts to replenish low gas storage levels will shape gas and power prices, with changes in fuel-switching economics feeding through to carbon. In the near term, compliance buying, improving industrial activity and commercial hedging should underpin EUA prices, while progress on EU ETS reform and EU-UK linkage will shape the longer-term direction.

## Tanker Chartering

Crude tanker spot markets eased overall this week, with Suezmaxes suffering the largest losses while VLCCs posted modest gains. The BDTI averaged 2,855, down 3% w-o-w.

Within the VLCC segment, regional performance was mixed. The Middle East market remained stable, supported by the growing use of AIS-dark transits through Hormuz, followed by STS transfers off Fujairah or Sohar. Conditions stayed healthy, with steady enquiry and balanced tonnage availability. West Africa spot rates retreated w-o-w despite limited tonnage, as demand faded progressively through the week. The US Gulf opened firmly, carrying forward the previous week's momentum, but a series of failed fixtures pulled rates lower and weighed on sentiment. Meanwhile, owners sought longer-period employment for their tonnage. VLCC TCE rates averaged \$334,066/day, up 3% w-o-w.

The expected retreat following the elevated rates of previous weeks materialized for Suezmaxes, shifting negotiating power towards charterers. Rates corrected sharply in the Americas, Black Sea and West Africa as demand and fixing activity weakened, leaving ample tonnage chasing limited cargoes. In the Black Sea, some owners avoided the region in search of safer and more economically attractive employment, while at USG, Suezmaxes lost cargoes to more competitive Aframaxes. MEG remained broadly stable. Average Suezmax TCE earnings fell 34% w-o-w to \$184,914/day.

The Aframax segment also moved lower, mainly due to subdued conditions at USG. In the Mediterranean, enquiry was brisk early in the week but gradually lost momentum, however TD19 (CrossMed) proved resilient. With the US market still muted, owners kept their vessels in the area, anticipating stronger activity from several Mediterranean ports next week. The North Sea followed a similar pattern, opening firmly before activity slowed towards the end of the week as Monday's UK bank holiday approached. Average Aframax TCE earnings stood at \$68,644/day, down 15% on a weekly basis.

## Demolition Market

Ship recycling markets remain broadly healthy, but a shortage of available tonnage continues to constrain activity. Recyclers retain a strong appetite, yet robust freight rates across several vessel classes are leading owners to defer scrapping decisions and take advantage of the favorable market conditions. The supply outlook should improve as vessels age and freight markets normalize, prompting a larger flow of recycling candidates.

At Alang, the market remained stable, with recyclers actively seeking available candidates and focusing on niche units. This focus continues to sustain activity amid a shortage of mainstream tonnage, while India remains less competitive than its subcontinent rivals but is trying to build on its compliance advantage, since most of the sector is HKC-certified. Firmer domestic steel prices and a partial recovery in the rupee are also improving market fundamentals and sentiment.
