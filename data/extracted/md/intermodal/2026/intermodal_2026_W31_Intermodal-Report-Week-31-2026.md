## Market Insight
**By George Vitsos, Offshore Broker**

# Weekly Market Report
**Week 31 | Tuesday 04th August 2026**

The Offshore sector has been greatly affected by the current geopolitical situation, as challenges that are substantially greater than those faced by merchant vessels are presented, due to the limited flexibility the floating units have under tow. Floating rigs, accommodation units, crane barges, vessels and FPSOs that are being repositioned, typically move at speeds of only 4-7 knots and are unable to take evasive action, as their slow speed, predictable movements and large radar signature make them particularly vulnerable targets in any environment where missiles, drones, unmanned surface vessels or small craft attacks are possible. The International Maritime Organization has repeatedly highlighted that commercial shipping and seafarers are increasingly exposed to geopolitical conflicts, while maritime security experts continue to warn that vessels operating in both Hormuz and the Red Sea face elevated risks regardless of nationality or flag.

The Strait of Hormuz currently represents the most difficult environment for offshore movements. In practice, a towing operation might not only require just the tug and the tow, but also support crafts, fuel logistics, crew changes, emergency response arrangements and reliable Ports of Refuge. Recent attacks on several tankers in the Middle East and Red Sea, are still exposed to military actions, whereas hundreds of tankers remain anchored or drifting throughout the Gulf awaiting improved security conditions, creating significant navigational congestion. Reports of very low daily transit numbers compared with normal trading patterns indicate a de facto restriction on navigation, even if the waterway remains open.

For offshore contractors, the immediate consequence is that finding suitable ocean-going tugs able to enter or exit the Gulf has become increasingly difficult. From a commercial standpoint, many operators are reluctant to expose crews to a region where vessels have been struck by projectiles, drones or explosive craft. From an insurance perspective, war-risk premiums have risen sharply, and underwriters are scrutinizing every planned voyage. The recent decision by Lloyd's market participants to terminate coverage for vessels making unauthorized transit payments or tolls in the Strait of Hormuz adds a further layer of uncertainty to voyage.

The Red Sea presents similar concerns. Although some commercial traffic continues to pass through Bab el-Mandeb, Houthi attacks have demonstrated a threat to vessels over a wide geographic area using drones and missiles, while maritime security advisories continue to classify the southern Red Sea, Gulf of Aden and Bab el-Mandeb Strait as high-threat risk areas, with the Houthis recently escalating their campaign through renewed threats against shipping and the announcement of restrictions targeting Saudi-linked maritime trade. Security organizations have reported evidence of vessel diversions and reduced traffic levels in the Bab el-Mandeb area, underlining the ongoing operational challenges for commercial shipping.

For a large tanker, the exposure window in Bab el-Mandeb may be measured in hours. For a tow moving at four knots, the same exposure can extend across several days, significantly increasing operational risk. As a result, offshore operators contemplating the mobilization of commercial vessels, rigs, barges or floating production units into or out of the Gulf should expect restricted tug availability, elevated insurance costs and potential difficulties obtaining charter-party and lender approvals. Industry feedback from sources active in the region already suggest that many tug operators are unwilling to undertake Hormuz transits under present conditions, effectively limiting available towing capacity.

Developments during the past week have further demonstrated how quickly the threat environment can evolve. Reports of reduced transit levels through both Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb, together with renewed attacks and threats directed against commercial shipping and energy infrastructure, continue to influence operator decision-making and insurance market sentiment. For offshore projects requiring prolonged exposure within these areas, the risk profile remains substantially higher than that faced by conventional merchant shipping.

Looking forward, the outlook remains highly dependent on geopolitical developments. Any renewed escalation would likely trigger further issues against commercial shipping, as under such scenario, offshore movements would be among the first activities postponed, as the risk-reward balance for tug operators, offshore contractors and insurers becomes increasingly unattractive.

## Tanker Chartering

Last week, the large crude carrier rates firmed, supported by renewed disruptions in Middle East resulting to longer-haul diversions via Suez, while the Aframax market corrected, reversing the strong performance recorded in previous weeks. The BDTI averaged 2,592, up 7% w- o-w.

The VLCC segment strengthened, underpinned by Middle East activity despite heightened geopolitical risks. The decline in Iranian attacks in the Arabian Gulf encouraged owners to load in the Gulf of Oman. Red Sea also added ton miles, with some crude flows moving through Suez and via the SUMED pipeline and around the Cape of Good Hope. West African rates rose as prompt tonnage tightened, though stronger demand is needed to sustain the gains. The US Gulf firmed amid robust enquiry, which absorbed tonnage and strengthened rates and sentiment.

Geopolitical risk and navigational safety remain a key driver of the Suezmax market, with the two major trading regions, Black Sea and Middle East, exposed to ongoing hostilities. Although activity eased in Black Sea, overall rates firmed as heightened security risks and recurring disruptions to CPC operations lifted premiums. At the same time, ballasting vessels to alternative markets increased tonnage availability in West Africa, placing rates and sentiment under pressure. Elsewhere, the Atlantic basin found some support from a pickup in Guyanese crude cargoes.

The Aframax segment reversed the previous week's firming momentum, with most regional spot markets posting losses. Following a period of strength, the North Sea market turned quiet amid weak levels of enquiries. Mediterranean rates also retreated as fading demand failed to absorb the growing tonnage pool. The US Gulf weakened as incoming ballasters lengthened the tonnage list. Finally in Asia, several privately concluded deals up ticked activity levels, however, charterers retained the negotiating advantage, preventing rates from gaining meaningful traction.

## Dry Bulk Chartering

The dry bulk sector followed contrasting trends last week, as smaller-vessel rates declined, Capesize earnings remained stable, and Panamax rates strengthened. Overall, the BDI averaged 2,680, representing a modest 1% week-on-week decrease.

The Capesize spot market remained broadly steady, strengthening after midweek as demand recovered in the South Atlantic and Pacific, supported in the latter by increased miner participation. The North Atlantic was the weakest region, where only sporadic transatlantic demand emerged, being insufficient to lift sentiment or generate meaningful upward momentum.

The Kamsarmax/Panamax segment firmed, recovering from earlier subdued conditions. Rates strengthened progressively as increased Atlantic demand coincided with a limited pool of available vessels, improving market sentiment. The Pacific also gained momentum, with demand concentrated on North Pacific grain car-goes and Australian coal shipments. Combined with constrained **supply, this stronger cargo flow supported higher spot rates.**

The Ultramax/Supramax market softened last week, primarily due to weak cargo demand across most regions. The US Gulf was a relative bright spot, showing signs of recovery as fixing activity increased. The South Atlantic experienced its seasonal slowdown, limiting activity and rate gains. Asian and Pacific markets remained under pressure from scarce enquiries and excess tonnage. In the Mediterranean, demand was uneven, with favourably positioned vessels securing better employment opportunities.

On the Handysize side, fundamentals pressured rates, as constrained demand left both basins with excess supply and shaped a market backdrop short of the cargo volume needed to absorb open tonnage. This was most evident in the US Gulf, South Atlantic and North Pacific. In the Mediterranean and Continent, with few fresh cargoes emerging, employment prospects and fixing levels came down to vessel position, making regional trade position driven rather than demand led.

## Demolition Market

Last week, ship recycling markets across the Subcontinent witnessed a more positive sentiment, supported by increased demand for recycling candidates.

India's ship recycling market improved modestly from the subdued conditions of the previous week, underpinned by stronger, albeit selective, buying interest. Steel fundamentals remained weak as monsoons continued to constrain output. Nevertheless, dollar-denominated plate prices rose as the Indian rupee strengthened against the US dollar. Although India remained less price-competitive than its Subcontinent peers, it continued to reinforce its competitive advantage through regulatory compliance. With the majority of yards being HKC-compliant and the recent proposal to add two Indian yards to the EU List, the country is further enhancing its position, potentially paving the way for EU-flagged candidates.

Chattogram's market outlook has become more positive, supported by stronger tonnage demand, the approaching end of the monsoon season, and the return of recycling candidates delayed by last month's severe flooding, creating a healthy tonnage pool. Purchasing appetite favors bulkers, general cargo vessels, and units with clean records, while sanctions-related histories continue to depress prices. Steel market activity has recovered and plate prices remain firm, but recyclers have not yet reflected this strength in their offers.

At Gadani, underlying demand continues to strengthen but has yet to translate into meaningful activity, with vessel arrivals still at a standstill. Recyclers remain willing buyers, though scarcity of candidates rather than any weakness in appetite is what is holding volumes down. Steel prices are advancing on the back of the cessation of Iranian imports and monsoon-related operational disruption. The consumption outlook is also constructive, with housing subsidies and reduced property withholding taxes expected to encourage construction activity.

At Aliaga it was a quiet week with little change in demand or sentiment, amid a sluggish flow of candidates. The hub is an attractive destination for EU compliant recycling segment.
