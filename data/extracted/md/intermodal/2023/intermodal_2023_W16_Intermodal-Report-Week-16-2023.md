Issue: Week 16 | Tuesday 25<sup>th</sup> April 2023

## Market Insight

**By **Chara Georgousi****

****Research Analyst****

## Global oil supply/demand fundamentals

With a robust US growth of 5.5%, bringing volumes to 18.8 million bpd this year, it is predicted that global oil output would increase by 1.3% to 101.7 million barrels per day in 2023. On the demand side, due to weakening economic trends, the world's demand for oil is expected to climb by about 2 million bpd in 2023 after rising by over 2.4 million bpd in 2022. Two major factors will propel demand through the year: rising jet fuel consumption and improving Chinese demand. OECD European and North American growth would be muted this year, with volumes hitting 13.5 million bpd and 24.8 million bpd, respectively. This decline could be partially offset by an estimated growth in Chinese demand of 970,000 bpd y-o-y.

## Crude Tanker Fleet supply-demand fundamentals

The fundamentals indicate that although there will be typical fluctuations, they will occur at greater levels. The need for oil is recovering, while the replacement of ships occurs at a slower pace. Tanker demand has increased on the back of the realignment of trade flows triggered by sanctions imposed on Russia after its invasion of Ukraine in 2022. At the same time, dirty tankers' fleet growth is estimated at 1% in 2023, compared to 4% in 2022. OPEC+ appears rather bullish about China, and if demand is robust, imports will be sourced from the Atlantic rather than the Persian Gulf, boosting ton-miles and decreasing the impact of supply cutbacks on freight. Time-charter rates for periods up to one year are currently robust reflecting the market's expectations for increased freight in the short term. On the other hand, OPEC+'s recently announced oil production cuts have partially softened the previously bullish outlook for the crude tanker sector, as they are anticipated to cause some softness in the oil market, particularly in the VLCC segment. However, the impact may be reduced if the cuts are lifted earlier than expected. Overall, global oil tanker market conditions are still anticipated to stay firm through 2023 on the back of the currently supportive supply-side factors, as well as positive demand drivers.

## Second-hand Market

Secondhand prices of crude tankers have surged to a 25-year high, after falling at their lowest level in 2020, as owners scramble to benefit from the increasing ton miles associated with Russia's conflict in Ukraine. Older VLCCs are still widely sold on the second-hand market and are sold at unprecedented prices. Currently, most VLCCs are largely being sold to Chinese buyers, while Middle Eastern and Indian buyers seem to mostly prefer Suezmax and Aframax vessels.

## Newbuilding Market

Currently, the total number of crude tankers on order is 80, a figure that represents 3% of the total fleet. Out of them, 53.75% will be delivered within 2023 and almost half of them are Aframax vessels. Despite a peak in ordering activity at the beginning of the year, the orderbook is currently standing at the lowest level in 25 years amid extremely tight capacity in shipyards. The current fundamentals in the tanker newbuilding market are extremely buoyant for the second-hand market.

## Chartering (**Wet: **Softer** / Dry: **Firmer****)

The dry bulk market closed off on a positive note last week surpassing the 1,500 points mark. The BDI on Thursday (21/04/2023) closed at 1,504 points, up by 69 points compared to previous Friday's closing (14/04/2023). The crude carriers market saw rates across the board moving down last week. The BDTI on Thursday (21/04/2023) closed at 1,146, a decrease of 96 points and the BCTI at 968, an increase of 10 points compared to previous Friday's (14/04/2023) levels.

## Sale &amp; Purchase (**Wet: **Softer** / Dry: **Firmer****)

The SnP tanker activity was subdued last week with only a handful of orders being concluded, while in the case of bulk carriers, interest was healthy with all sectors catching owners' interest. In the tanker sector, we had the sale of the "EAGLE BRENDA" (164,626dwt-blt '01, S. Korea) which was sold to undisclosed buyers, for a price in the region of \$26.0m. On the dry bulker side sector, we had the sale of the "STELLA CHERISE" (177,832dwt-blt '10, China), which was sold to undisclosed buyers, for a price in the region of \$24.5m.

## Newbuilding (**Wet: **Firmer** / Dry: **Firmer****)

After the Easter holidays when newbuilding activity was slow, contracting interest came back stronger with a total of 23 vessels being ordered. The majority of orders come from the tankers sector where freight rates continue to be high despite them softening during the past week. Of the 17 tanker orders, the Greek owners ordered 8 of them, with Capital Gas and Maran Tankers ordering 4 firm and 4 optional Suezmaxes each. The vessels will be LNG dual-fuelled and costed \$ 87.0m each, while their delivery is expected between 2026 and 2027. On the bulk carriers sector, the UAE based owner Emarat Maritime ordered one 63,000 dwt Bulk Carrier from New Dayang in China, expected to be delivered in 2025 for an undisclosed price. There is also strong movement on the Gas Carrier sector, with 4 ships being ordered, all of which from Eastern Pacific. The vessels are 93,000 cbm dual-fuelled, and shipbuilders Hyundai Samho and Samsung Heavy Industries will build two each, costing the owner \$ 100.0m apiece.

## Demolition (**Wet: Stable- / Dry: Stable-**)

Ramadan came to an end last week, meaning that activity in the market and top destinations has been quiet and buyers are expected to return to the market soon. Freight rates are mixed across all segments, but still not at levels that would encourage owners to scrap. There have been few transactions in India and these have been at lower prices. The steel market is in decline as inventories build up and demand fails to catch up. In Pakistan, there seems to be no end to the financial problems, with the World Bank predicting that inflation will reach 29.5% this financial year. The country is seeking a further \$3bn from the IMF by submitting a plan with further assurances to unblock the IMF bailout. In Bangladesh, the market has been slow and prices low. L/C opening remains a problem in Bangladesh and only smaller tonnages are possible. India and Bangladesh decided to facilitate some of their bilateral trade in their own currencies (INR and BDT) to reduce their dependence on the US dollar. In Turkey, the lira hit new lows against the USD, while inflation was 50.51% y/y in March. Turkish crushers are in for a tough few weeks due to low steel prices and weak fundamentals.

## Chartering

Sentiment in the VLCC market further weakened last week. Freight softened as rates for main routes to Asia, namely MEG-China (TD3C) and WAF-China (TD15) were quoted lower w-o-w. TD3C was seen at WS65.18 on Friday, lower WS7 w-o-w, while TD15 was assessed at WS65.65, lower WS4.4 w-o-w. In the meantime, the rate for a 270,000mt US Gulf to China (TD22) route fell to \$8,900,000, \$738,889 lower w-o-w (\$40,364 per day round trip TCE). A similar sentiment dominated in the Suezmax market amid muted enquiry, with rates in the MEG sitting at WS65.19, WS1.94 lower on the week, while TD20 was assessed at WS97.5 on Friday, WS9.25 lower w-o-w. TD6 was seen at WS139.11, WS18.5 lower w-o-w, amid intense competition with the Aframaxes. The Aframax market experienced another challenging week. TD19 was tested for yet another time, as cargo enquiry has been consistently surpassed by the tonnage list. The North Sea was also considerably weaker as the rate for the 80,000mt Hound Point/Wilhelmshaven route significantly declined to WS128 (a round-trip daily TCE of \$33,400), lower WS31.5 w-o-w.

VLCC T/C earnings averaged \$ 39,826/day, down -\$4,455/day w-o-w, and closed off the week at the \$36,600/day mark.

Suezmax T/C earnings averaged \$ 52,474/day, down - \$8,577/day w-o-w. On the Aframax front, T/C earnings averaged \$ 43,643/day, down - \$8,115/day w-o-w.

## Chartering

It has been a positive end to the week yet with the performance of all sectors still lacking the anticipated boost that the start of Q2 could progressively bring, while FFAs are pointing to a mild improvement in May as well given the uncertainty that prevails with regards to the Chinese demand recovery and the impact of the world economic slowdown to the trade globally. Last week, the Capesize earnings were supported by increased iron ore Australian coal stems from Chinese demand and a supply/demand mismatch in favor of owners. Panamax market activity was steady with tight supply in the Atlantic underpinning sentiment while in the Pacific, Indonesian and Australian mineral demand supported rates against a longer tonnage list in the regions. Geared sizes saw an uptick in rates as well amidst stronger demand out of USG and ECSA, while in the Pacific the forthcoming Golden Week coupled with the ongoing Eid Holidays pushed charterers to cover their Indonesian coal stems.

Cape 5TC averaged \$ 14,699/day, down -5.10% w-o-w. The transatlantic earnings increased by \$ 1,822/day with transpacific ones rising by \$ 1,609/ day, bringing transatlantic earnings premium over transpacific to \$ 1,081/ day.

Panamax 5TC averaged \$ 15,020/day, down -6.15 % w-o-w. The transatlantic earnings decreased by \$135/day while transpacific earnings declined by \$142/day. As a result, the transatlantic earnings premium to the transpacific widened to \$231/day.

Supramax 10TC averaged \$ 12,629/day, up +3.17% w-o-w, while the Handysize 7TC averaged \$ 11,564/day, up 1.86% w-o-w.

After the Easter holidays when newbuilding activity was slow, contracting interest came back stronger with a total of 23 vessels being ordered. The majority of orders come from the tankers sector where freight rates continue to be high despite them softening during the past week. Of the 17 tanker orders, the Greek owners ordered 8 of them, with Capital Gas and Maran Tankers ordering 4 firm and 4 optional Suezmaxes each. The vessels will be LNG dual-fuelled and costed \$ 87.0m each, while their delivery is expected between 2026 and 2027. On the bulk carriers sector, the UAE based owner Emarat Maritime ordered one 63,000 dwt Bulk Carrier from New Dayang in China, expected to be delivered in 2025 for an undisclosed price. There is also strong movement on the Gas Carrier sector, with 4 ships being ordered, all of which from Eastern Pacific. The vessels are 93,000 cbm dual-fuelled, and shipbuilders Hyundai Samho and Samsung Heavy Industries will build two each, costing the owner \$ 100.0m apiece.

Ramadan came to an end last week, meaning that activity in the market and top destinations has been quiet and buyers are expected to return to the market soon. Freight rates are mixed across all segments, but still not at levels that would encourage owners to scrap. There have been few transactions in India and these have been at lower prices. The steel market is in decline as inventories build up and demand fails to catch up. In Pakistan, there seems to be no end to the financial problems, with the World Bank predicting that inflation will reach 29.5% this financial year. The country is seeking a further \$3bn from the IMF by submitting a plan with further assurances to unblock the IMF bailout. In Bangladesh, the market has been slow and prices low. L/C opening remains a problem in Bangladesh and only smaller tonnages are possible. India and Bangladesh decided to facilitate some of their bilateral trade in their own currencies (INR and BDT) to reduce their dependence on the US dollar. In Turkey, the lira hit new lows against the USD, while inflation was 50.51% y/y in March. Turkish crushers are in for a tough few weeks due to low steel prices and weak fundamentals. Average prices in the different markets this week for tankers ranged between 340-600/ldt and those for dry bulk units between \$330-590/ldt.
