## Tanker Market Commentary

### Crude Tanker Comments

The VLCC market started slowly, with lists long and volumes still limited. Early activity in the West reflected this; rates continued to ease with some action in the Americas, including some failures. As the week progressed, some activity surfaced in Yanbu and the Atlantic, though it lacked the consistency needed to shift sentiment. A solid test in Brazil resulted in rates moving lower, confirming that the West remained under pressure. The back end of the week saw a more pronounced correction, particularly in the Atlantic, where levels dropped further as charterers took advantage of softer positioning and owners showed more willingness to secure employment.

The East holds more constructive news, with reports of a potential agreement which could allow more activity inside the AG. Still, for now, the outcome is uncertain, and owners are still hesitant to commit until confidence improves. Despite the softer tone, a number of ships have continued to disappear quietly, indicating underlying private fixing and helping prevent lists from building excessively. The market now enters next week with a softer backdrop, and much will depend on whether fresh volume emerges and whether developments in the East translate into real activity.

On this day in 1953, Sir Edmund Hillary and Tenzing Norgay became the first to scale Mount Everest. After this week, it feels like Suezmax owners will have a similar mountain to climb before rates return to their previous levels, as following a long weekend, tonnage had built up significantly. This and the glacial pace of action in the Atlantic, Med, and Black Sea regions led rates to drop drastically. WAFR rates began around WS 175 basis Nigeria but have fallen just below WS 150 and could drop even further given the over 20 FOC ships with ETA to Bonny up to the end of the current fixing window. CPC rates also fell, with WS 225 last done for TD6; however, with dates still working second decade and the general programme still very light, this market is expected to follow the trajectory of its peers.

Guyana and the USG have also been very quiet, with Guyana volumes being upscaled onto VLCCs and USG-TA volumes being undercut by the cheaper Aframax option. Both regions require testing on Suezmaxes, but considering the collapse across the Atlantic, rates will likely slide here too. Rates East of Suez have again been pushed lower, as the parking lot of ships idling there leaves charterers with a very strong hand. Yanbu-India has now fallen to WS 165, 55 points below last done. But the US and Iran seem to be edging closer to an end to the conflict and the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, which would provide the shot in the arm which the tanker markets need as summer approaches. Increasing AG volumes would be a theoretical “safety harness” for owners trying to navigate the peaks and valleys of the Suezmax market.

The week ended on a high for Med Aframax owners. There was a fair amount of under-the-radar activity on Tuesday, but the list was still too long for rates to rise. The same followed on Wednesday, and rates stayed flat, though by Thursday, enough tonnage was leaving the area to support rates. Going into the weekend, the list now looks more challenging for charterers to navigate. For now, owners are in the driving seat, and TD19 sits at WS 180 on Friday. It was a more subdued week in the North Sea, partly because the decline of TD25 has reduced the number of ballasters, supporting tanker supply and doing nothing to hold rates up. Things stayed quiet as the days passed, and the outlook is poor. TD7 sank on Thursday after one quote received almost 10 offers, and it ends the week at WS 145.


### Product Tanker Comments

There is much unknown about the current state of the Arabian Gulf LR market, but there are slightly greater volumes coming from the STS loading areas of Oman and Fujairah. It is incremental, and the small lift in demand has not done much to freight rates, but it is something to keep in mind. A few laden LR2s have managed to exit the AG now, a relief to crews after three months trapped inside. These two ships now join the few VLCCs which were pioneers of the resumption of passages, showing the particular importance of this sizeband to energy security.

The details of how these ships left is unclear and could remain so for the foreseeable future, but this week, at least, there is a growing sense of a “chink in the armour” of the imposed restrictions on the Strait of Hormuz from both sides. Freight levels have held at USD 4.25 Mn for Yanbu-UKC, but it would be unsurprising if this suffered a bit along with the weaker Med-East market. Duqm-West via Suez was failed at USD 4.75 Mn. On a brighter note, WS 180 was failed for STS Oman-East on 75,000 T naphtha, which was then replaced and repeated at WS 190. This shows a small pocket of tightness being optimised to the tune of 10 points in this otherwise listless marketplace. These improvements are minor, but one should not be too discouraged about the health of the tanker market; daily LR2 earnings remain between USD 40,000 and USD 50,000, which is not insignificant – if a stem is actually secured.

A very disjointed week has kept AG LR1 activity levels pinned down, with very little fixing done. Almost all deals were naphtha runs East ex-Red Sea or ex-AG/WCI, accompanied by some X-Red Sea business. Tonnage is well supplied throughout, so rates have broadly trended downwards. Yanbu-East levels have taken a significant haircut and hit WS 255, while Duqm-East and Sohar-East are down to WS 200 and WS 185, respectively. X-Red Sea rates are falling at every fixture, and there has been little alternative business done in the region. Next week comes with a weak projection.

For Med MRs, the short week kicked off much like it did in the rest of Europe – quietly. Nothing fresh was seen, with only  a replacement stem covering in line with last done at the time, or WS 180. As the week went on, activity improved. 10 cargoes were worked on the surface, which has helped add some red to the top of the tonnage list. Despite this, the fundamentals do not lie, and with ballasters plentiful and still heading towards Europe in the coming week, rates have come under pressure. The latest ex-Med TA run was agreed at WS 175, but a Sines load tested further down to WS 155. Sentiment is softer in Europe currently, but with the USG ticking up over this week, some vessels currently inbound for Europe could turn their noses away, which could lighten tonnage counts come Monday morning.

TC14 initially saw a sharp correction to WS 140 amid the heavy tonnage supply, and with a quieter start to the shortened trading week, rates continued to come under pressure as supply increasingly outweighed demand. TC2 also traded down and is now being freighted at WS 155, and ARA-WAFR has faced fresh downward testing, with the latest fixture at WS 215. X-UKC has also softened, now done at WS 210 and continuing to fall. Overall market sentiment remains soft, with the first half of June currently appearing well-supplied with vessels. Still, with TC14 rebounding sharply and latest levels reportedly freighted at WS 235, some ballasters might begin to divert from NWE, which could in turn provide some underlying support to UKC freight levels going forwards.
