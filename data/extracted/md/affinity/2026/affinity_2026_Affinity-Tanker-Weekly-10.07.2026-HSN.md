## Tanker Market Commentary

### Crude Tanker Comments

VLCC tonnage has been tighter in the West, and from Brazil in particular, we have seen owners showing plenty of resistance, and rates have firmed a little bit in the West. However, these gains may be short-lived, as with the latest uncertainty from AG and the SOH, we could well see a redistribution of tonnage from the East into the West and thus downwards pressure on rates owing to the increase in tonnage supply for the next fixing window, along with decreased demand from the AG owing to the latest escalations between the US and Iran.

If you have followed the Suezmax market this week, similar to watching Star Wars: The Force Awakens, it feels a bit like watching history repeat itself, with owners content to fix off their dates around last done while the AG looks set to close and cause a tidal wave of ballasters to head west for the upcoming fixing window for the Atlantic. It may not be as cinematic as a desert orphan finding a droid with a secret message with regards to a galactic superweapon, but it does feel eerily similar to what we have experienced before. WAFR rates have depreciated from WS 240 levels for TD20 down to WS 232.5 as older tonnage and a cheaper Spain run set the tone for the market, then charterers managed to hoover up ships around last done levels on private deals.

CPC rates have seen a slightly smaller decrease from WS 280 for a replacement to WS 275 as the Med tonnage list proved slightly more abundant compared to the Atlantic. On the other side of the pond, the USG has remained quiet yet stable at WS 190 for a TA run; yet while Aframaxes have firmed beneath us, they currently remain the preferred route for Charterers on a LS freight as well as USD per T basis. Guyana also has been quiet this week, with end-Jul  early August dates still to be accounted for. In the East of Suez, rates have remained stable at WS 200 basis Fujairah/East; however, with the latest break in the ceasefire between the US and Iran, the East of Suez market is expected to come under more pressure next week as volume should decrease owing to the lack of STS activity from Fujairah because of the cut in SOH crossings. Consequently, we should see more ships ballast from the east heading into the Atlantic, adding supply pressure to the western markets from next week.

The Med Aframax market strengthened consistently over the first four days of the week, driven by sustained cargo demand and a tightening tonnage list. Monday opened with positive momentum, as strong activity and rising rates shifted sentiment in owners' favour. The market gained further traction on Tuesday as a busy fixture pace reduced the vessel list and allowed rates to climb higher. By Wednesday, prompt tonnage had become increasingly scarce following several days of heavy activity, giving owners greater leverage in negotiations. This trend continued into the end of the week, with tight vessel availability and steady inquiry pushing rates higher once again, and market sentiment remains firm. TD19 sits at WS 222.5 on Friday. The North Sea remained largely subdued during the first four days of the week, with ample vessel availability continuing to outweigh demand. Monday opened on a quiet note following a soft finish to the previous week, with rates expected to remain flat. Trading activity stayed muted through Tuesday and Wednesday, and although several prompt vessels ballasted out to the US, sufficient tonnage remained in the region for charterers to comfortably repeat previous fixtures, keeping rates steady at best. By Thursday, the tonnage list appeared more balanced, while strengthening sentiment in the Med and US markets began to provide owners with some support. The list had contracted significantly by Friday, and TD7 ends the week at WS 150.


### Product Tanker Comments

For AG LR2s, it was another week of disappointment. Demand has been piecemeal, and the brakes were hit mid-week after the re-emergence of hostilities in the region. Many ships have been lining up to take advantage of the growing freedom of transit through the Strait of Hormuz over recent weeks. Before Tuesday night, this brought some success; USD 8 Mn was fixed for Jubail-UKC, with WS 375 concluded last week for TC1. Owners are now making themselves more available for loadings within the AG, demand in the Gulf is still very low. Refiners are reluctant to amp up production and throughput amid fears of continued disruptions to vessel traffic.

Many are still unwilling to cross the strait, preventing the “feeding frenzy” which so many owners had expected. 75,000 T naphtha has fallen to WS 190 for STS India- Japan – a 17.5-point w-o-w drop. Red Sea-EAFR was also recorded on subs at WS 230 for 90,000 T, a decline of 32.5 points. Westbound has been recorded at USD 4.9 Mn from WCI via Cape of Good Hope, down from over USD 5 Mn according to most estimates. The list is not overloaded, but with this dismal view on how demand might look off the back of renewed hostilities, the view is flat to poor for the near future.

As for AG LR1s, it was another quiet week, and cargoes remain scarce. Tonnage supply has stayed flat but lengthy over the last few weeks, and there has been more off- market fixing in recent days. TC5 had been agreed at WS 175 ex-Duqm, a 25-point drop from the previously agreed (but failed) naphtha rate and a startling 60-point drop from the last fully fixed TC5 ex-Duqm. Public Westbound demand has been low, but freight could be estimated at USD 3 Mn via Suez at best. Red Sea and short haul business has been extremely limited. The market remains very firmly at the peril of ongoing geopolitical tensions, with attacks from both sides and no end in sight.

Northwest Europe continues to firm, with more vessels now on subjects and prompt tonnage being absorbed. As a result, vessel availability has tightened significantly, supporting the firming sentiment. TC2 began the week at WS 130 and has continued to firm, now freighted at WS 147.5. ARA-WAFR and X-UKC have also firmed, with freight levels now at WS 192.5 and WS 170, respectively, also influenced by owners’ preference for TC2. TC14 is freighted at WS 250, and more ballasters are repositioning towards the USG. It was an active week for NWE Handysizes, with levels opening at WS 170. Activity accelerated through the middle of the week, with five vessels going on subjects, tightening prompt tonnage availability. As the week has progressed, WS 177.5 has now been repeated, and sentiment is steady.

Med MRs put up a bit of a fight this week, with Red Sea demand and a firmer USG market striking up interest from European positions once again. Rates have not leapt yet due to decent tonnage supply. Last done are WS 130 TA, WS 150 UKC, and WS 170 Med, but there is potential for rates to improve next week. NWE feeling stronger is helping owners’ sentiment here, which adds fuel to the fire. Med Handies have not managed to kick into gear this week, with enquiry lacking and tonnage – albeit mostly with Russian history – stacked. Most deals have been private, and rates have been widely repeated at WS 165, with a few WS 170s subbed in certain circumstances. Sentiment is flat for now, and sustained enquiry is needed.
