## Tanker Market Commentary

### Crude Tanker Comments

The VLCC market has firmed significantly this week, with rates pushing higher as prompt tonnage becomes increasingly difficult to find. Security concerns following Iranian threats to vessels involved in “unauthorised” Strait of Hormuz transits and STS activity are likely to make some owners more cautious about their employment options. The 14 per cent drop in Bab-el-Mandeb transits in August might suggest that owners are already taking account of this risk.

The most dramatic rise in rates came out of the Atlantic, where USG-East rose from USD 24 Mn midweek to a whopping USD 29 Mn and Brazil-East climbed from WS 217.25 to WS 234. There have been few ex-WAFR voyages, but this might reflect the comparative “cheapness” of the Suezmax WAFR market. With Suezmax rates now also starting to firm, it is possible that VLCCs become an option for charterers in the coming weeks. East of Suez has also enjoyed firming, with inside AG remaining elusive and outside AG-East rising from WS 250 to above WS 270. There has been much under- the-radar business, further tightening the lists. With plenty of cargoes still outstanding and the Atlantic list continuing to look tight, the firm sentiment may well continue.

The Suezmax market saw a sharp turnaround this week, with a firm VLCC market breathing life back into it through a number of split stems and a significant increase in activity. Tonnage lists at the start of the week were abundant, putting pressure on owners and pushing TD20 assessments to the low WS 180s and CPC down to WS 277.5. However, with a sharp increase in activity and many ships picked off the list quietly by the middle of the week, TD20 assessment rates started to climb, reaching WS 260 by Friday. CPC is paying up to WS 282.5 and is expected to firm further into next week.

The increased Suezmax activity across the Atlantic and split stems saw a significant proportion of cargoes move east, considerably tightening the tonnage list and improving owners’ sentiment. USG remained largely an Aframax market on a dollar- per-barrel basis, while WAFR, Guyana, and Brazil led the Suezmax recovery in the Atlantic. With rates having bounced from the floor and the list now tighter, sentiment is expected to carry into next week.

The Mediterranean Aframax market softened steadily over the week. Activity was limited at the start due to the UK public holiday, leaving charterers facing a growing position list as owners struggled to find fresh business. By Wednesday, charterers were able to test rates lower, with TD19 reportedly fixed at WS 230 ex-Algeria, reflecting weakening sentiment. The correction continued on Thursday as ample tonnage comfortably met enquiry, keeping downward pressure on rates. Overall, sentiment went from steady to soft, but it seems that the market had bottomed by Friday, and rates could rise with more enquiry. TD19 ends the week at WS 230.

The North Sea Aframax market remained subdued this week, with charterers generally retaining the upper hand. Following a quiet start, sentiment softened slightly as only a handful of largely off-market deals emerged over the long weekend. By midweek, activity remained limited and fixtures were reported around WS 207.5, aside from an extremely prompt replacement. This rate held for a few days; while ships ballasting TA shortened the list somewhat, there was little demand for it. Though ballast demand across the Atlantic offered some support, it was not sufficiently attractive to tighten tonnage, leaving rates broadly stable. TD7 sits at WS 205 at close on Friday.


### Product Tanker Comments

The AG LR2 market has been very quiet on the surface this week, with barely any freights being reported. Considering the massive inefficiency involved in STS loading off Oman or India, it is no great surprise to see pauses in the cargo liquidity as the STS ships run the gauntlet back into the AG to retrieve the cargo. This seems to have happened this week, and demand has virtually disappeared. One of the more competitive and compromised ships was agreed at 75KT x WS 240 for WCI-Japan, about 10-20 points below that for which others were aiming. This subsequently failed, and the energy for the balance of the week also died off a bit as a result. If demand remains low, it would not be a shock if this level started to apply across the board.

The AG LR1 market has also been very underwhelming. 60KT x WS 275 was agreed on subs for WCI-EAFR, as was 55KT x WS 295 for Sohar-Japan, a five-point slip on the week. The list should be far more populated on Monday, so – as for LR2s – some further softening should be on the cards should this dearth of demand remain. Next week should bring more demand here, but when keeping in mind the Greek LR2, 2 KPC relets, and 2 Saudi-owned VLCCs which were hit in the Red Sea and the Strait of Hormuz in the past nine days, the enthusiasm for continuing the mothership shuttling at pace could be under threat for a short while.

Southeast Asia opened on a measured footing, with sufficient tonnage and a stream of enquiry depriving the market of any meaningful catalyst. But as the week went on, many vessels were attracted to the lucrative East Asian market, whittling away the pool of workable tonnage. Rates rose in response; TC7 went on subjects at WS 330 on before a replacement stem traded at WS 350. As owners remain interested in ballasting elsewhere, charterers might have to continue paying a scarcity premium.

Med MRs had a decently active week, with 11 stems seen working on the surface. Tonnage remains stacked despite a continuing flow of ballasters to the more attractive US market. As a result, the market remains flat, and the latest tests here sit at WS 105 TA, WS 140 UKC, WS 150 WAFR, and WS 155 Med. Looking to next week, much the same is expected, with the list looking set to replenish for mid-month laycans with discharge positions from the USG and NWE due to arrive and open. Handysizes fared similarly; activity slightly picked up on Tuesday and Wednesday, but it felt as though the market was simply catching up after Monday’s UK public holiday. Tonnage now feels tighter, especially in the Western Med, and rates moved for the first time as WS 175 was subbed on a replacement. The market is currently load- dependent, with West Med likely still at WS 175 and Central and East Med at WS 165.

NWE MR activity been slow this week, with tonnage continuing to grow and a large vessel recirculation driven by X-UKC fixtures and no fresh ballasters. With the USG still firming, vessels are expected to depart over the weekend, which could help balance the list. X-UKC stems have been repeated at WS 145, while ARA-Med has come with higher premiums given the even larger tonnage oversupply there. With bunker prices still high, TC2 has stayed flat throughout the week at WS 100 and looks set to stay there, as the alternative for owners is to focus on short-haul options or ballast. It was a quiet week for Handies in NWE after Monday’s public holiday, with TC23 being repeated at WS 180. Tonnage has begun to replenish, putting rates under pressure. Last done is now at WS 177.5, while ARA-Med continues to pay a premium over X- UKC, given the oversupplied Med market. Unless fresh enquiry emerges, levels could come under more pressure as the tonnage list continues to grow.
