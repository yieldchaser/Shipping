## Tanker Market Commentary

### Crude Tanker Comments

The VLCC market is quiet for the most part in the West. Some movement out of the Americas has emerged, showing that things have likely bottomed. Elsewhere, WAFR remains dead quiet with very little coming out. As for the AG, inside is moving well, likely propping up sentiment globally - business is not booming, but charterers are doing well drip-feeding cargoes into the market. Many fixtures have been reported, though few confirmed. Outside has come off slightly, but it is likely to rise soon in line with the inside market. All major VLCC routes saw rates fall relatively notably on Tuesday and continue to fall until Friday, when they managed to correct, even if not by much. TD3C had dropped by 16 points by Thursday but rose a bit on Friday, ending at WS 297. TD15’s fall was marginally more dramatic, dropping 18 points in four days before only rising just one, landing at WS 149. TD22 sits at USD 17.7 Mn.

For Suezmaxes, the week started out on a steady to firmer note, with the Atlantic supported by a tightening list and firm owner sentiment. Early on, TD20 appeared to have reached a ceiling at around WS 230; however, continued off-market enquiry and a tightening position list ultimately pushed levels up to WS 245 by the end of the week. CPC and Med remained in a standoff for much of the week, with rates eventually printing higher as owners resisted repeat levels. With a tight prompt list and a heavy July programme ahead, sentiment remains tilted towards further firmness if enquiry persists.

In Brazil, activity was relatively steady, with levels repeating around WS 240 for UKCM options, broadly tracking the firmer Atlantic tone. Guyana followed a similar pattern to TD20, with a tight natural window and WS 245 achieved on several fixtures. The USG remained comparatively quiet throughout, with Aframaxes continuing to look more competitive on a dollar-per-barrel basis. With the Fourth of July holiday approaching, activity is expected to remain subdued in the short term. The AG remains dominated by VLCCs.

The Mediterranean Aframax market was largely steady until Friday, with rates holding around WS 150 to WS 152.5 despite subdued activity and several prompt vessels remaining uncovered. Limited enquiry and a balanced list kept sentiment unchanged in the first half of the week, while Libya dates appeared well-covered.

By Thursday, however, signs of softness had emerged as Sidi Kerir-Trieste fell to WS 143.75, though other X-Med voyages continued to trade closer to WS 152.5. With most obvious fixtures covered, more ballasters arriving from the Atlantic, and enquiry remaining lacklustre, sentiment was soft until the end of the week, when multiple vessels were put on subs. TD19 ends the week at WS 160 and is expected to rise further.

The North Sea Aframax market had a quiet and softer tone throughout the week. An accessible tonnage list and limited enquiry left charterers firmly in control, with the only notable fixture reported at WS 140 for Hound Point-Baltic on Monday. Activity remained scarce throughout the period, leading to growing pressure on rates, with market participants increasingly doubtful that last-done levels could be maintained. By Thursday, the market was effectively devoid of activity, reinforcing a softening outlook as owners faced a lack of fresh cargo enquiries. TD7 sits at WS 135 on Friday.


### Product Tanker Comments

There was very little fresh AG LR2 activity this week. 75,000 T naphtha was tested early on loading from Sikka, and with several offers in, this level relaxed down another 2.5 points, with WS 207.5 now agreed on subs. Unlike the LR1 and MR lists, the LR2 one will soon be lengthened by multiple ships which have unwound from the DPP market which took so many vessels at the start of this year. Still, with meagre recent demand, there were more than enough vessels for one to describe the market as weak; tonnage supply far outstripped demand, even with the supply count well inside the average six-month count. Even so, many owners feel that with lists in their favour to such an extent, it should not take much cargo flow to kick freight on.

However, in the past few weeks, demand from the AG has been patchy – and in some areas non-existent. While we are confident that this will improve, the timing of a re- emergence of something resembling previous norms is hard to predict. There was a dearth of Westbound market cargoes this week, but comparative earnings suggest around USD 5 Mn for an STS load off Oman or India, while the Red Sea has stabilised a bit; USD 3.75 Mn was agreed for a 90,000 T ULSD Yanbu-UKCONT voyage. This represents an improvement of USD 250,000 on the week. Sentiment for the coming week is flat, and nothing will change if demand stays this weak.

There were signs of life in the AG LR1 market, with fixing levels slightly more elevated than those of preceding weeks, but some activity also originated within the AG. Fundamentals for the “vanilla” outside-AG market have put pressure on rates as tonnage supply heavily outweighs demand, while the trickier inside-AG rates have seen quite a spread. TC5 ex-STS Sohar has tumbled to WS 200, while rates ex-AG have ranged from WS 300 to WS 712.5, the latter albeit a slightly older fixture which just hit the market. Westbound rates were not tested this week, but levels will have fallen if loaded outside the AG; estimates are USD 3.3 Mn via Suez and USD 3.7 Mn via Cape of Good Hope. Red Sea activity has progressed steadily at slightly weaker levels, with Yanbu-Ain Sokhna at USD 650,000, Yanbu-UKC at USD 2.95 Mn, and Yanbu-Singapore at WS 250. Updates from the US and Iran were limited this week, which is a good sign.

This was an active week for Med MRs, with 15 fixtures loading seen on the surface. Despite activity improving this week, a stacked tonnage list after a quiet period was needed to be worked through, and off the back of this, rates never had a chance of moving, for the most part holding stable. Looking into next week, tonnage counts could look thinner off the back of the rallying USG market, which is likely to continue to attract potential ballasters. If enquiry levels are in line with this week’s, there could be potential for owners to try to push levels.

As for the North Sea, the MR market has continued to absorb workable tonnage, with vessel availability now extremely tight inside 8 July. TC2 has edged higher, now closing the week at WS 130. Differentials for West Africa, Brazil, and X-UKC voyages have once again widened over TC2, supported by a strengthening US Gulf market, which continues to attract ballasters and create attractive repositioning opportunities for owners.
