## Tanker Market Commentary

### Crude Tanker Comments

This week the VLCC market moved from a quiet, largely steady start to a firmer tone towards week-end. Early optimism faded as anticipated activity took longer to materialise, while healthy tonnage lists kept charterers willing to test the downside. In the Atlantic, a more manageable list and busier WAFR market offered some support, but subdued Suezmax sentiment initially limited momentum. By mid-week, this had flipped, strengthening Suezmaxes and Aframaxes helped western VLCCs gain ground, with Brazil/East voyages moving from WS 263.5 onto subs at WS 270 and then WS 279.

East of Suez, well-supplied lists limited the impact of fresh cargoes for much of the week. A Fujairah/East voyage went on subs at WS 305 on Wednesday, below expectations but establishing a floor. Friday’s notes point to a firmer market, with Fujairah/East looking towards WS 330, although information remains limited and that level is not yet confirmed. Overall, this firming is expected to continue. The East of Suez market will likely lead the next move if eastern cargo volumes rise as anticipated, which will put additional pressure on Atlantic tonnage.

‘A return to form’, ‘Spellbinding’, ‘Worth the wait’ - some of the reviews for the eagerly anticipated GTA VI, which could also summarise the Suezmax market. The wider Atlantic market was under pressure tonnage-wise but the ever-chaotic protagonist (not GTA VI’s Trevor Phillips) that is the USG was the main cause of a significant rebound this week. In WAFR, rates initially looked under pressure with tonnage looking long and rates did sink to WS 300 on a Nigeria/Spain run earlier in the week; however, the USG pendulum swung in owners’ favour with an avalanche of cargoes, subsequent list of ships on subs and the rise in rates for TD33 that enabled owners to push for higher returns from WAFR. Rates have now reached WS 350 by close of play. CPC rates were also not immune to the excess tonnage and rates corrected down 5 points to WS 420 and, whilst we are yet to see a fresh test in the open market, rates are likely to be nearer to WS 450 on next done. Guyana rates also saw a minor decline earlier in the week with WS 300 being done on Tuesday, but the rebound wave did also make landfall here as well with WS 355 now on subs for a Med run. The USG started off this week very dreary with rates around WS 250 and very untested given the disparity between Suezmax and the commonly preferred Aframaxes. However, Aframaxes rocketed in value, reaching WS 590 for TA and the $ per barrel discrepancy between the two classes eventually saw charterers begin to favour Suezmaxes where they could and rates for TD33 currently sit at WS 300 basis last done. However, with 4- 5 cargoes outstanding, realistically it will more than likely be a WS 325 market at least for next done. East of Suez was again a more slow burning affair, not much unlike the prologue for GTA VI … with rates for Fujairah/East trading sideways at WS 310.

The Aframax market in the USG has surged this week, nearly doubling from its close last week of WS 315. On Friday evening, it has closed at WS 620. The spread between US WTI and European Brent futures has widened to its widest level in 11 years, fuelling demand for vessels in the USG. Since mid-February, Aframax availability in the USG has fallen around 70 per cent, according to Signal, and the sudden tightness has caused freight to surge. This, in turn, has boosted rates across the Atlantic. In the Med, owners have used the surge in TD25 to their advantage, pushing rates higher. The market came to life on Wednesday, with TD19 leaping 30 points to WS 300, before jumping another 50 points on Thursday, followed by 30 points on Friday to close the week at WS 380. The North Sea was a little slower to get going, but rates have climbed and the market closes at WS 315. With limited local players left in the region, rates are poised to climb higher.


### Product Tanker Comments

The LR2 market has held up as well as it could have this week. Demand from the two previous weeks has petered out a little, not least because of the rise in IRGC attacks that hit so many mother ships in the Hormuz area. The count of LR2s has been a little longer than the demand that has come in, but there are some owners holding quite a bit of the tonnage at the top of the list who remain of the view that last done levels are not for them and they don’t mind waiting for markets to rally a little.

This is decent firm sentiment being displayed in a week where cargo flow has been negatively affected by attacks on vessels but, in this fundamental owners’ market, we have seen plenty of occasions where a pause in liquidity does not necessarily lead to wholescale softness. We have seen the WS 325 that was fixed for 75,000 MT naphtha last week not repeated, but instead WS 365 was the more recent level agreed. By the same token, USD 8,300,000 is on subs for STS Sohar/West via BEM but there are two owners holding tonnage at the top of the list who won’t settle for anything under USD 9,000,000. We have UAE Holidays from Wednesday next week which might impact the level of trading, so let’s see if there is any cargo resurgence on Monday which then validates the strong sentiment ‘holding of nerve’ or is this more meagre cargo throughput maintained off the back of continued strangulation of Arabian Gulf oil being exported from West of Hormuz. If the latter is the case, then rates should slowly continue to come off as the fixing window stretches into 5 – 10 December.

There have been similar volumes to last week on the LR1s, but with further softening across the board – not a total surprise. Naphtha runs ex-WCI/Oman fell to WS 325 at its lowest, and there would probably be a bit more to go if charterers played it quite aggressively. That said, the list isn’t too damaging as we close the week; there is still a bit of a tonnage clear-out required, and it wouldn’t take mountains of demand to do so, but current levels are not fulfilling this requirement. Fresh westbound demand has been limited, and we do require a fresh test, but we estimate levels sitting around USD 5.25 Mn basis UKC via BEM. There appears to be a bit more activity related to inside AG business this week admittedly, with a few more head owners willing to make the transit in search for better returns; the full extent of the deal’s details will of course be challenging to ascertain but it is definitely something worth keeping an eye on to see if this market starts to open up further despite ongoing risks in SoH.

In the West, things have been largely steady. In the Med, MRs have traded sideways for most of the week but, as we moved into the back end, there was further enquiry basis 37KT as well as 30KT stems looking to capitalise on the cheaper MRs. Quickly, tonnage has tightened after 11 vessels were subbed over Thursday to Friday. Little was working on Friday with very little left available to be fixed and, with the owners bullish, anyone that can would wait until next week to work. Looking forward to next week, there is potential here if Handy activity picks up alongside the MRs. However, with NWE quiet this week and under pressure, we may begin to see a few north positions look south for better earnings. Overall stable feel here as we look forward.

On the Med Handies, rates have remained mostly stable, starting the week at WS 410 and closing at WS 400 for 30KT X-Med. Most of the activity has been going on the MRs this week with the bigger size pricing cheaper, leaving the Handies better supplied going into the weekend. However, bad weather around the Med is leaving a lot of the top half positions uncertain as we go into the weekend. Off the back of this, it has been a quiet Friday while the market waits until Monday before going again.
