## Tanker Market Commentary

### Crude Tanker Comments

The week got off to a fairly typical and subdued start for VLCCs, with little visible activity and much of the focus on working through a number of mysteries carried over from the back end of last week. A Brazil cargo bound for China set the early tone, fixing a strong unit at a discount to last done, which curtailed any hopes of owners pushing for firmer sentiment. Despite limited lifting, elevated tonne-miles have prevented lists from becoming overly long, leaving the market relatively balanced. As the week progressed, the direction tilted lower, particularly in the West.

The East was sidelined again, with little meaningful activity circulating. There has been some renewed talk of an agreement between the US and Iran, but this has yet to translate into any real shift in trading patterns. Still, several ships have disappeared quietly throughout the week; this has helped prevent a sharper correction, even as visible enquiry remains light and sentiment cautious. Looking ahead, much will depend on geopolitical developments and whether any clarity emerges from the Middle East. If sentiment improves and volume begins to materialise after this quieter spell, there is scope for a more active market next week. For now, however, the tone remains soft, with participants largely waiting for a clearer signal before committing.

Warren Buffet once said that “the stock market is a device for transferring money from the impatient to the patient”. Suezmax charterers are taking this to heart, as rates have fallen across the Western markets this week. In West Africa, rates have taken a steep correction after what had been a very slow week on the surface, with charterers broadly preferring to go to one ship only due to a tight list. Tonnage supply then increased and rates declined to WS 170 ex-Angola, with the cargo drawing in nine offers, mostly from Eastern ballasters. USG rates have also fallen slightly, lingering at WS 167.5, just 2.5 points below the value from the start of the week. CPC rates also fell – from WS 240 to WS 232.5 – though with rumours that maintenance may now be deferred at the terminal, owners have managed to prevent a steep decline.

Buffet famously amends his daily breakfast order at McDonald’s depending on stock market conditions. If he were following the Eastern Suezmax market instead, he would not be having his USD 3.17 bacon, egg, and cheese biscuit anytime soon; with the Strait of Hormuz still closed and enquiry scarce from Yanbu or Fujairah, even dominant AG players are ballasting their ships towards the Cape of Good Hope. Should the strait reopen, this market will spin on a dime, but given the lack of a peace deal, the market is stuck in this current miserable status quo.

Entering this week, the last X-Med Aframax fixture had been reached at WS 175. There were some natural ships on the list which could only have fixed X-Med, and sentiment was weak. The next few days were characterised by limited activity and stable rates, and charterers retained the ability to choose the right ships easily.

After a desolate week volume-wise for North Sea Aframaxes, it was difficult to see any positives at the beginning of this one. Rates continued to test down, and the list was packed with logical options. The only caveat was that the US was attractive enough to entice ballasters. As in the Mediterranean, activity was lacklustre and rates were steady through the week, and by Thursday, more volume was still needed to justify owners’ asking for higher rates. Despite more under-the-radar activity on Thursday, rates continued to change little. TD7 and TD19 sit at WS 175 on Friday.


### Product Tanker Comments

It was another listless week for AG LR2s, but there are now signs of the re-emergence of the STS loads of the Omani coastline, which had been put on hold for two weeks. This has not provided much of a lifeline, as today it was reported that WS 180 had been agreed on subs for 75,000 T naphtha for STS Sohar-Japan. Rates below WS 200 had not been seen for eight weeks, and this fixture was emblematic of the fact that the lack of barrels has led to very uninspiring levels.

Red Sea exports seem to have been quieter this week, but there was a prompt midweek test with USD 750,000 taken off the previous level for Yanbu-UKC, settling at USD 4.25 Mn. In other news, a few more VLCCs passed through the Strait of Hormuz this week, but this is more linked to inter-governmental collaboration with Iran to secure safe passage. Still, the idea of money changing hands seems to be very muted, a reminder of the sheer value which versatility and size allow VLCCs to offer to the countries which need them most. An LR1 cannot meet energy demand to the same extent, and the full importance and clout of the VLCC world is on display.

It was an extremely quiet week for AG LR1s, with just a handful of public fixtures, but there was some private business. The list is still more than long enough to deal with potential demand spikes – which are unlikely – so sentiment is soft. TC5 paid WS 205 for an STS Sohar loading, substantially lower than the previous rate. Westbound demand has featured little, but freight levels will likely be suppressed heavily by competing vessels. Red Sea business is also under pressure, with WS 325 being paid for Yanbu-East naphtha; this is strong but visibly lower than those which came before. Ultimately, there remains paralysis in the market as uncertainty continues off the back of the ongoing war. All one can do is wait for potential improvements or setbacks.

AG MRs have come under pressure this week as demand continues to be pinned back by the ongoing war. Until now, the list has been fairly lean, with vessels fixed away or ballasting to greener pastures, but this week welcomed back a lot of tonnage into the fixing window, and rates have softened. TC17 went down roughly 100 points on the week, with WS 325 on subs. There has been a slight bounce back to WS 335 given a pocket of tightness against a reasonably prompt cargo, but overall sentiment remains soft. Loadings this week have almost exclusively been out of Sikka and the Red Sea, which has been fairly active. All in all, it would not be a surprise to see rates fall next week as more tonnage arrives on natural laycan dates; demand could also drop as many participants will be celebrating Eid.

It was a tough week for LR owners in North Asia. Cargoes have been few and far between; LR1 owners have been trying to scrap for back haul stems with MRs, but with limited success. LR2 cargoes have been non-existent, and with rates plummeting for both sizes ex-WCI and ex-Middle East, the situation does not bode well for the direction of travel freight rates in North Asia next week.

There has been ample tonnage in Southeast Asia for charterers to choose from this week, and as a result, freight rates have taken a step down. TC7 has gradually been whittled down by five to 10 points per fixture as rates edge closer towards WS 300 going into the weekend. While owners will be disappointed with this, it is important to remember that historically, these TCEs are very decent. Unfortunately, though, it also means that there is no room for a further decline, and given the softening in WCI and the Middle East, more of the same is expected next week.
