## Tanker Market Commentary

### Crude Tanker Comments

It was a generally quiet week for the VLCC market, though a fair amount seems to have been working under the surface, with plenty of ships building on the mystery list. Early in the week, there were some solid tests, with charterers able to push rates down effectively, quickly setting a softer tone across the market.

With very little visible activity to counter this, the softer sentiment carried through most of the week, leaving little to drive any meaningful shift in direction. That said, towards the back end, there have been signs of increased activity in the East, which could begin to support a rebound if volumes stat to materialise more consistently across both basins.

VLCC counts remain on the lower side, which gives some underlying support, and next week will begin with cautious optimism. Charterers will likely look to cover outstanding stems, while earlier in the week some owners acted tactically to secure employment before further declines. More recently, however, owners appear more confident and are beginning to hold firmer in an attempt to stabilise levels and regain ground. Overall, sentiment remains subdued, but not without potential, with next week likely to be shaped by whether this anticipated volume finally comes through.

The week began on a quieter note for Suezmaxes, with limited enquiry on the surface and a softer tone across the Atlantic as Posidonia kept fixtures off the market. Early in the week, tonnage remained abundant and sentiment was laying on the softer side, with WAFR estimated around WS 145 levels. Guyana followed the same trend, and CPC repeated WS 220 a few times. Midweek saw signs of rates steadying; while tonnage remained well supplied, as Posidonia ended, a gradual increase in activity was anticipated.

Aframaxes out of the USG also started to influence sentiment, supporting Suezmax rates and helping steady the overall softer tone. Towards the end of the week, activity sharply picked up, with many additional private stems pumping out and vessels being picked off the list, tightening prompt tonnage. Owners’ sentiment became firmer as the week ended. WAFR held steady with WS 152.5 paying for an East run and trading sideways, and Guyana and CPC repeated at WS 220 a few times as barrels pumped in. The market has broadly moved from a soft start to the week to a more balanced and firmer close, but everything depends on continued enquiry flow and USG support.

There was a decent amount of activity for Med Aframaxes in the days before Posidonia began. Fundamentals began looking better for owners, and they had reason to feel positive going into this week. Tonnage decreased immediately, leading rates to spike and prove the firm sentiment to have been warranted. As the days passed, rates plateaued, but with increased activity, there is room for them to rise further. TD19 ends the week at WS 230.

By the end of last week, rates had bottomed, sinking to WS 150. After the weekend, the front end of the list was short, leaving charterers with limited options. Still, there was not enough activity to make a clear trend emerge. By midweek, success in surrounding markets allowed owners to demand higher rates, making it difficult to repeat last-done figures. An increase in tonnage allowed charterers to maintain rates on Thursday, but the firming US market has generated firm sentiment. TD7 sits at WS 155 on Friday.


### Product Tanker Comments

The AG LR2 segment was much busier this week than it had been in much of the previous three months. Vessel supply met demand easily, but there were a few moments to secure a bit more money here and there. Last week’s 75 x WS 190 for naphtha STS Sohar-Japan has turned into WS 200, and STS Sohar-West warmed from between USD 4.5 Mn and USD 4.75 Mn to USD 5 Mn. The Red Sea has seen a renewed level of interest with four fresh cargoes either fixed, on subs, or there to cover. Rates have been somewhat varied, with both WS 175 and WS 275 being agreed for two Red Sea-EAFR cargoes. Of course, there would always be some disparity from the cheaper number being from a more compromised owner, but a 100-point spread had not been seen for a while.

The long list of ballasters into the Red Sea from the East is now thinner, with STS Sohar being much more active than it had been of late; this has naturally sniped a few more ships which would otherwise have been completely at the mercy of the Red Sea exporting market. Even with these much thinner LR2 lists, there are still ships, and Yanbu-West can be assessed at below USD 4 Mn. USD 3.5 Mn could be there for the right vessel and timing.

LR1s had a steadier week, albeit with less activity than the LR2s experienced. STS Sohar or Duqm-Japan has been agreed at both WS 195 and WS 200, and more local barrels have seen USD 700,000 agreed for Sohar-Karachi. The Red Sea export market, which can be quite buzzy, has had a week off. Rates for X-Red Sea cargoes would be assessed at around USD 800,000, and last done of 55 x WS 225 for Red Sea-Japan could realistically be repeated when the route is tested again. Ships, while not as abundant as usual, are still around for whenever enquiry returns.

MR activity has been largely subdued this week, with Posidonia keeping most of the market occupied. As market participants slowly returned to their desks, the back end of this week saw three ships taken on the surface and rumours of some more stems working quietly in the background. Most of the activity in Europe has been driven by the busier North market, which – despite better enquiry – saw levels come under pressure, with options plentiful.

The latest tests ex-Med sit at WS 150 TA and WS 170 UKCM on a warrant NAP stem, and options remain plentiful to close out the week. Looking forward, the USG, which has strengthened throughout this week and looks set to continue to do so, will pull ballasters across from Europe. It will also turn heads from any potential ballasters from WAFR/SAM over the weekend, lightening tonnage counts. Overall sentiment this week is quiet, with potential for an uptick in activity next week.

It was another difficult week for Handysize owners, with surface activity quiet during Posidonia. Sentiment therefore remained bearish, and X-Med levels corrected further to below the WS 200 mark; at the time of writing, they sit at 30 x WS 180 for standard runs. While activity has seemingly picked up in the 24 hours ending on Friday afternoon – about 10 units are now on subjects privately – the front of the list remains rich in tonnage. While further softening would be expected in the near future, owners are beginning to show resistance, citing poor returns if TC6 falls any lower. Time will tell if they have to accept lower rates on Monday in order to get covered, as competition for cargoes will certainly be there.
