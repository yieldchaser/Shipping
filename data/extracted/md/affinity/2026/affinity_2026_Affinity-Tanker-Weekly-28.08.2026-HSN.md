## Tanker Market Commentary

### Crude Tanker Comments

This week, the VLCC market has softened, and it could do so further. An outside AG- East voyage recently went on subs around WS 230, down 10 points from earlier in the week but still far above last week’s WS 180. The firming was driven by strong demand and the firmer tone in the Atlantic, and with these both starting to normalise, some easing East of Suez is unsurprising. Still, a tighter list and continued demand should prevent dramatic falls. But Iran’s blacklisting of vessels and warnings over “unauthorised” maritime activity warrant attention; should owners and charterers become more cautious about operating in the AG, it could support rates.

Two USG-East voyages failed at USD 25.5 Mn, with last done now around USD 24.75 Mn, and Brazil-East fell 23 points on the week to WS 192. This softening has been driven by a weakening Suezmax WAFR market and several failed Atlantic voyages, and WAFR activity has been minimal, adding to the notion that momentum is slowing. Some additional easing is anticipated, with USG and Brazil likely to converge as the recent extremes unwind; however, the prompt Atlantic list remains tight and, with firm enquiry East of Suez, further correction will probably be measured.

After the ballooning of Suezmax rates last week, there was an expectation that there would be a decline. This was proven correct, with rates in the West tumbling this week amid an oversupply of tonnage and cargoes drawing multiple offers, many going on subs on a first counter basis. TD20 rates have softened by more than 100 points, down from WS 320 at the start of the week to around WS 210 at its end.

Guyana levels have dropped down similarly, with 145 x WS 185 on subs briefly before the ship was dropped for an own-programme vessel instead. USG-TA cargoes have been non-existent, with Aframaxes hoarding the volumes given their cheaper freight economics. CPC rates have also corrected sharply, going from WS 530 to WS 400 on subs as owners chased higher-paying markets. East of Suez rates remain strong, with WS 350 paid for Fujairah-East, but owners seem to prefer to stay in the East rather than ballast West given the recent increase in volumes from both Fujairah and Yanbu.

The Med Aframax market strengthened slowly this week, with owners retaining the initiative despite a balanced tonnage list and softer TD25 sentiment. Early September enquiries helped generate firm sentiment at the start of the week, letting owners push rates higher. The market remained highly date-sensitive, with isolated cargoes capable of tightening conditions and lifting returns further. Charterers attempted to slow the momentum by holding back requirements, but resistance had limited effect as rates continued to edge up. The end of the week has been quiet, and since Monday is a public holiday in the UK, lower rates are expected. TD19 landed at WS 265 on Friday.

Early this week, North Sea Aframax owners gained some ground as activity picked up and a prompt Hound Point cargo secured a notable premium. Despite this, momentum remained fragile as a sharp correction in TD25 limited confidence in a lasting rally. Midweek offered a fresh test of the natural window, and early September dates were still difficult. As the U.S. Gulf market weakened and ballast opportunities became less attractive, more vessels were expected to remain in the local market, increasing available tonnage. By week’s end, the tone had softened somewhat, with charterers showing little urgency to fix as recent levels began to look expensive and owners faced growing local competition. TD7 sits at WS 210 at the time of writing.


### Product Tanker Comments

AG LR2s had been fundamentally poised for a very firm week, with tonnage supply at its lowest levels so far this year across a 20-day window basis ETA Fujairah. There has been some firming recently, but demand has stuttered this week, so there has not really been anywhere for the market to go, given the lack of fresh fixing. Nevertheless, sentiment is robust and freight levels remain strong.  Westbound rates do need a refresh, as levels could well be higher than last done, but freight is estimated at at least USD 6.9 Mn paid basis UKC via Bab-el-Mandeb. Naphtha runs East have not been popular this week; last-done levels were agreed at WS 235, but there is every chance that fresh levels surpass WS 280.

AG LR1s have mirrored their larger counterparts for the most part this week, albeit perhaps not quite as severe on supply and demand characteristics. Yes, tonnage supply is very tight in comparison with the rest of the year, but the list does provide a bit more optionality. Demand has also tailed off as the week has progressed. There was some strong firming early in the week, with the latest TC5 (ex-WCI/Oman) fixture reaching WS 300, 95 points up on the week. Westbound rates were tested early on, with USD 4.4 Mn being paid Sikka-UKC via Cape of Good Hope, but newer levels could be agreed just above that at between USD 4.7 Mn and USD 4.9 Mn. Short-haul business has not featured, and Red Sea activity remains incredibly limited; we are only aware of a few X-Red Sea fixtures taking place privately.

It was an active week for AG MRs, with consistent enquiry levels and a tightening list supporting continued rate strength. Charterers remain active on forward dates, and some have been extending laycans incrementally, reflecting the tightness and confidence in the underlying market. Unconfirmed reports suggest levels as high as WS 370 ex-Duqm, while firm sentiment across Asia continues to draw tonnage away from Chittagong, ECI, and – in some cases – Colombo.

After a slow start to the week with very little seen working in the market over Monday and Tuesday, activity did find some legs for Med MRs, and seven or eight stems have been worked. Rates have held stable at WS 115 TA, WS 140 UKC, and WS 160 Med. A close eye has been kept on ballasters, with five MRs sailing from the Med to the US this week. With TC14 softening, though, volumes might decline next week. Sentiment is steady, and much of the same can be expected until autumn. Med Handies also had a stable week, with rates at WS 165 throughout. Activity did pick up mid-week, but with tonnage stacked, a lot more is needed for this market to be able to find any real footing. Activity is muted today, and little change is expected next week.

It was a much busier week for MRs in NWE, with over 20 vessels fully fixed and several more on subs. This, along with 10 ballasters heading to the USG, helped clear out the heavily oversupplied tonnage which had built up. TC2 has repeated at WS 100 despite unimpressive earnings, supported by better prospects upon arrival in the USG. Most of this week’s voyages have been short-haul, and a healthy recirculation of tonnage is expected, along with fresh vessels entering the market in early September. This has let levels remain steady in the face of increased activity. Handies in NWE have continued last week's active momentum, with tonnage remaining consistently tight in the UKC. Levels have firmed up this week, with TC23 at 30 x WS 180. The usual differential between ARA-Med and X-UKC has now reversed, with ARA-Med commanding a premium over X-UKC, as owners’ preference for NWE continues.
