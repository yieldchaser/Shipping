## Tanker Market Commentary

### Crude Tanker Comments

VLCC rates have surged across the board this week. The escalating Middle East war has restricted Strait of Hormuz transits, which fell to just three ships on Wednesday, far below the 10-day average of 17. This has strained the STS shuttling method inside the AG and Gulf of Oman, where labour shortages were already delaying transfers. At the same time, strong prompt crude demand and a tight list have forced charterers to seek cover earlier and in less conventional ways, including taking ships on subs before they discharge from previous fixtures. Owners are firmly in control.

East of Suez has seen the strongest activity. A Fujairah-Southeast Asia voyage is reported on subs at WS 830, while Fujairah-Far East is reported at WS 800, up from WS 700. With even fewer owners willing to risk Strait of Hormuz travel, rates for vessels loading inside the AG have surpassed USD 1 Mn per day. Bab-el-Mandeb transits are effectively non-existent; limited oil movement continues within and out of the northern Red Sea, but under heavy pressure. Italy is deploying warships to support safe passage, and China is privately pressing Iran to rein them in.

The Atlantic has been quieter, and tight tonnage and rising rates have led some VLCC stems to be split onto Suezmaxes. Recent fixtures reflect the wider sentiment: there is a reported USG-East fixture at USD 50 Mn and rumours of one at USD 55 Mn; the rate was USD 34.5 Mn at the beginning of the week. Further firming is likely across dirty tanker classes, led by VLCCs. The market is far from stable, and tight tonnage and ongoing disruptions should keep rates volatile, expensive, and well supported.

The Suezmax market remains at unprecedented highs as geopolitical uncertainty and very tight VLCC supply still boost rates. WAFR rates continued to reach new heights, with WAFR-East paying WS 530 on the latest deal, up from WS 480 at the end of last week. TD20 is currently pegged at WS 480, but owners’ ideas remain very firm indeed.

TD6 has also firmed to WS 500, but charterers have managed to get several repeats at these levels, so it seems that more gains in the Atlantic will need to be made before rates firm any further. USG levels have so far peaked at USD 25 Mn for East, and since USG-East for VLCCs might have hit USD 55 Mn, it seems that there is still headroom for rates to push up further. Guyana remains quiet on the surface though, with rates apparently tied to TD20, but it requires testing in the open market. East of Suez rates remain even more volatile, with WS 975 on subs for Fujairah-WCI.

Mediterranean Aframax rates rose at the beginning of the week, testing at WS 400 last Friday before extending to WS 440 on subs ex-Ceyhan on Tuesday, with local TCE much higher than USD 150,000 per day. Momentum then stalled as the US market plateaued, prompting owners to weigh locking in gains against pushing further. Uncertainty over Libyan loadings added to mixed sentiment, with easing Zawia unrest offset by continued Sidi cancellations. Fundamentals hinted at correction, yet owners kept offers high, leaving rates steady. TD19 ends the week at WS 510.

North Sea Aframax rates were difficult to price at the beginning of the week, with thin local tonnage pointing towards firming, though to what extent remained unclear. A high number paid on a straight Med run set a clear floor for TD7, with owners freighting against TD25 reinforcing expectations of further gains. A stalemate followed as charterers resisted owners' ideas, but TD7 slightly surpassed WS 300 midweek and is likely to rise further. TD7 sits at WS 305 on Friday.


### Product Tanker Comments

It was a successful week for AG LR2s even though they were not overwhelmed with fresh business. The availability of tonnage is a huge challenge within the normal date window, so it is no great surprise that the market is very firm. Westbound demand has been the flavour of the week, and rates have climbed to USD 7.5 Mn via Bab-el- Mandeb for outside AG business basis UKC. Naphtha runs East have firmed about 100 points to WS 330. Public cargoes have been limited this week, with most deals being done privately, as expected in market conditions like these.

LR2s have dominated the conversation over the last two weeks, but this week, AG LR1s finally moved into fifth gear. The list is very tight due to off-market activity, which has added pressure as the days have passed, so the market is very firm. Also influential are skyrocketing MR rates as logic dictates that the size above moves in the same direction. Although public demand has been extremely limited and off-market fixing has been steady, the sector has required little fixing to expose the sheer lack of ships around in the window. TC5 has firmed over 100 points, with last done at WS 390.

Westbound demand has been patchy, but the last recorded rate basis UKC via Cape of Good Hope was USD 4.1 Mn, and a boost is expected at the back end of this week if tested, perhaps to USD 5.5 Mn. Issues persist in the Red Sea between the Houthis and the Saudis, and this has had an unsurprisingly negative effect on trading volumes, but barrels continue to move East via Cape of Good Hope to avoid any unwanted risk; the last agreed deal for naphtha heading East via Cape of Good Hope was WS 320.

Med MRs began the week on a steady note, with good off-market activity balancing what remained a healthy tonnage list. However, a strong spike in naphtha enquiry combined with a firming North market saw levels improve, with owners able to threaten ballasting. WS 190 and WS 225 were subbed yesterday for UKC and Med, respectively – up 25 and 50 points from last done. Only time will tell, but those still outstanding may consider downsizing in order to secure a Handy at better rates. Regardless, it has been a positive week for MR owners, who will be hoping to build on this momentum come Monday.

It was a steady week for Handies, too, with X-Med paying between 30 x WS 180 and 30 x WS 190, depending on load: a tighter W-Med competing with a firming X-UKC market has seen stems loading in the region paying at the higher end, with C-Med and E-Med provided with more tonnage optionality.

NWE MR tonnage was very tight all week due to what has been the largest amount of activity seen in months. With the arb opening back up, more TC2 stems seen, and longer-haul WAFR, SAFR, and Brazil stems fixed, the continued re-circulation of short- haul tonnage has now stopped, supporting the firm market seen all week.

TC2 has traded up, with WS 150 last done, and WAFR was tested at WS 220 for Tema. Cargoes remain outstanding to be worked next week and more seems to be being worked under the radar. Sentiment remains firm and tight, and next week is likely to bring the same momentum, with the fixing window being pushed even further out, keeping tonnage tight and the market bullish.
