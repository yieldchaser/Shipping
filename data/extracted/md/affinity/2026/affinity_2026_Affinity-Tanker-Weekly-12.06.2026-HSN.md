## Tanker Market Commentary

### Crude Tanker Comments

For VLCCs, it was a quiet week overall, albeit one which has ended on a firmer note. Multiple tests out of Brazil and the Gulf of Oman throughout the week have produced mixed results, but on the whole, they have shown slight progression, showcasing a modest improvement in fixing levels. This has been supported by some firmer signals out of both the East and the USG to end the week. WS 150 was concluded on a Fujairah-South Korea run, and a USD 17.5 Mn TD22 voyage suggests that the turnaround which owners had been expecting is now materialising.

Despite this, much of the fixing has taken place quietly, with a noticeable number of ships disappearing from the lists as charterers looked to keep any strengthening of sentiment under control. Looking ahead, there is evidence to suggest that the market will continue to firm up, particularly if recent geopolitical rhetoric in the Middle East leads to material progress. If volume starts to build as a result, next week looks like it could be very busy.

The World Cup has kicked off in dramatic fashion this week, with two goals and three red cards in the opening game. Suezmaxes have not been as dramatic, but there have been some highs and lows. Tonnage supply was rather balanced overall at the start of the week, but there was then an uptick of enquiry, particularly from South America, which improve sentiment amongst most owners. In West Africa, rates have improved from WS 150 to WS 157.5 for UKCM, with consistent under-the-radar activity and some more surface-level action including replacements pushing rates up. Further gains could still be made on a cargo off end-month dates owing to the lack of tonnage.

CPC rates, however, maintained their level of WS 215 as vessel supply kept a lid on things, although the programme for July looks very active, so one can expect sentiment to be much stronger next week. The USG-ECM market has been very quiet due to the cheaper economics of the softening Aframaxes. Rates have slumped from circa WS 142.5 to WS 135. Guyana also lagged behind the rest of the markets, with more poor chartering from one owner taking WS 149 basis 130 KT – slightly below last done in a firming market. Frankly, there were a few owners who wished they could show a red card to their competitors and remove them from the field of play. Turning east, rates are holding at the WS 160 for Fujairah-East. Given the renewed sabre- rattling from a certain inaugural FIFA Peace Prize winner, the Strait of Hormuz looks set to remain closed – along with the AG market – for the foreseeable future.

Many charterers refrained from seeking Med Aframaxes for their cargoes last Friday so that positions would firm up, so fresh inquiry was needed at the beginning of the week. There was indeed a fresh test on Monday, and rates corrected sharply, which set the tone for the week. By Wednesday, WS 210 had been repeated on multiple runs, solidifying the rate, and the general feeling was that the market had bottomed, but this proved not to be the case after a subdued end to the week. After continued limited activity, TD19 sits at WS 200 on Friday.

TD7 ended last week on a similarly quiet note. Though surrounding markets were firming up, charterers were able to select the right vessels at last-done levels. Interest was low in the first few days of this week, and rates were flat, as the length of the tonnage list continued to allow charterers to avoid rate hikes. By Thursday, they had managed to lower TD7 slightly, and it ends the week at WS 150.


### Product Tanker Comments

AG LR2 activity over Posidonia and into the start of this week has introduced more flow than the recent norm, which has led to cautious optimism from owners. Better demand flies in the face of the geopolitical posturing which has been taking place, suggesting that the US and Iran are each “banning” the oil of the other. Two national oil companies have found ways to allow their own relets to transit the Strait of Hormuz and then make an STS transfer onto third-party tonnage. With this in mind, the market might be beginning to be shaped by greater demand than has been seen in recent weeks, in which some cargoes have had to “make a market” each time as the time between deals is so long, rendering the reference obsolete.

The position list remains leaner than normal, and STS business has allowed rates to rise incrementally. 75,000 T naphtha has gone from WS 200 to WS 207.5, and Westbound has moved from UDS 5 Mn to USD 5.3 Mn for a number via Suez. The end of last week also saw a flurry of activity in the Red Sea, particularly from Gizan and Yanbu. Many deals have remained private, but freight would likely stay at last-done levels, or between USD 4 Mn and USD 4.1 Mn for Yanbu-UKCONT and 90 x WS 275 for East Africa. There are fewer East ballasters this week because of an increase in STS Oman cargoes taking ships, but more and more ships are open in the West of Suez market, which is the more typical source of Red Sea loading stems, so levels could stay flat into next week with enough ships around.

AG LR1s had minimal activity this week. Fixing was largely contained to naphtha runs heading East and a few short hauls. TC5 ex-Sikka or –Sohar has crept up by 10 points at last done, landing at WS 210 despite general inactivity and abundant tonnage. Sohar-Karachi was agreed twice at USD 700,000, the only short hauls from outside the AG. Westbound activity has been lacking for a while, but this week USD 3.5 Mn was paid for a Sikka-Med jet cargo. Overall, it was a flat and uninspiring week.

NWE MRs had a week of two halves, with an active start seeing prompt vessels begging to get cleared out and vessels ballasting over towards the firm TC14 market. As the week progressed, things began to slow down. TC2 has remained steady at between WS 140 and WS 145, options-dependent, and differentials for WAFR over TC2 are starting to narrow; ARA-WAFR is now freighting at WS 200 due to TC14 sentiment. And as the latter route took a large correction midweek, three ballasters were observed turning around while en-route to the USG, now back working out of NWE or the Med following the large correction and soft sentiment in the USG.

It was a steady week for Med MRs; Med-TA traded at 37 x WS 145 throughout, with activity cooling off sharply in the past 48 hours bar the odd off-market deal. UKC and Med experienced initial gains, subbing at WS 180 and WS 200, up by 10 and 30 points on the week, respectively. All routes require above-market testing, but the needle is not expected to have moved. Next week, tonnage is expected to be healthy, with prompt positions struggling for coverage today adding to those opening for early third decade laycans. Pressure could thus start to mount on owners early in the week.
