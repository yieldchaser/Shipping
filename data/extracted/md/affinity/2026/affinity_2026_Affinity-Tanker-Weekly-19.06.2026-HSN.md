## Tanker Market Commentary

### Crude Tanker Comments

After a slow start to the week, momentum gradually built for VLCCs, through activity was fragmented. There was early testing in the East, and the Atlantic hinted at an upward correction, but only midweek did the market begin to show clearer direction. Though there were a number of Fujairah and Yanbu cargoes on the surface throughout the week, activity inside the AG itself remained fairly lacklustre following the initial testing from charterers, with owners still cautious about fully committing. At the same time, activity in the Atlantic picked up, with rates firming notably as the week progressed. Brazil-East runs moved from the mid- WS 110s last week to WS 150, WS 165, and then a failed deal at WS 185, highlighting the strengthening sentiment out of the West. Appetite for East of Suez coverage remains strong, while tightening lists in the Atlantic’s natural fixing window allowed owners to push levels higher.

Geopolitical developments continued to play a key role, with the proposed US-Iran deal proving far from straightforward. Uncertainty and reports of further disruptions have kept many owners contemplating the right time to move, limiting AG activity, but a handful of reported failures have not derailed the firmer tone. With more activity towards the end of the week, lists have thinned, and there is room for further firming. Overall, the market closes in a stronger position. With momentum building and sentiment firmer, the market looks well-placed heading into next week, particularly if volumes continue and there is more clarity regarding the Strait of Hormuz.

Due to balanced Atlantic Suezmax tonnage supply and news of an agreement in the Middle East, sentiment was there to back up fundamentals this week. WAFR rates climbed from WS 157.5 on Monday to just over WS 180 on the BITR, though a fixture would be needed to back up the numbers. CPC rates have repeated their conference level all week, even with WAFR rising from the theoretical floor, as the elevated earnings CPC provides mean that owners are keener to lock in rather than pushing for more. Rates are rumoured to have firmed to WS 222.5, but again, evidence remains elusive; it is, however, certainly plausible given the ex-WAFR earnings spike.

The USG saw some activity this week, and rates have firmed up by 10 points on the back of the improving WAFR market. The Guyana programme is light for the current window, with the only fixture of note being a heavily discounted rate done for a Black Sea discharge on a relet. The AG saw some enquiry, but rates have yet to take off as charterers exercise restraint in a market flaky because of the postponement of the peace agreement following further conflict between Israel and Lebanon.

Though there was some clear-out last week, the Med Aframax list was long enough by this Monday that rates were essentially flat. There was a solid amount of activity at the beginning of the week, but dates had moved forward, allowing charterers a larger number of positions from which to choose. As this happened, the market trended down, and ships continued to enter the area each day, but by Friday, the feeling is that rates might have bottomed. TD19 ends the week at WS 185. Owners in the North Sea managed to keep rates up last week despite a weak TD25 market. But the list was decent on Monday, and USG earnings discouraged ballasting, giving charterers the upper hand. There was little change on Tuesday, but the following day, TD7 finally fell to WS 140. Ballasting remained limited, but there were multiple off-market deals done midweek, and owners refused to let rates fall below WS 140. Going into next week, a slightly firmer TD25 could lead more ships to cross the Atlantic, and sentiment is steady. TD7 sits at WS 140 on Friday.


### Product Tanker Comments

The AG LR2 segment took a breather this week, and while there is some flow from STS Sohar, it is limited to the logistical constraints of vessels pulling the product out of the Arabian Gulf. It is therefore unsurprising that there have been pauses between each tranche of deals. Though refineries there are running at normal capacity, Red Sea exporting has also been quiet, partly because more relets are being used for shipments as opposed to third-party chartering. 75,000 T naphtha for STS Sohar-Japan was last fixed at WS 205, but with after news of the USA/Iran memorandum of understanding, owners have shown cautious optimism which is likely to be shown in the next fixture. Westbound has not been tested much publicly, but a newbuild was agreed at USD 5 Mn for WCI-UKC, which should place a trading LR2 in the USD 5.5 Mn region.

More cargoes are expected. Though the Strait of Hormuz will remain contentious for some time as the IRGC issues warnings not to cross, many ships are managing transit through several closely guarded methods. Lists are balanced on new vessels coming into the region, so owners will be looking to see if there really is the upside on freight or if a return to normal flows drags on further. Though the aforementioned MoU has affected sentiment, the market response is likely to be delayed as everyone tries to understand the safety limitations and how much oil there is to move

The AG LR1 market faced another muted week in terms of demand from WCI/Oman; almost all has stemmed from the Red Sea. TC5 needs a fresh test, but last done for STS Sohar is WS 210, and general bullishness from owners would suggest firmer rates next time. Again, there has not been a market for Westbound runs this week, but freight levels can be assessed at USD 4 Mn for STS Oman-UKC via Suez. As mentioned, Red Sea loadings have been the bulk of fixtures this week; short-haul rates in the sea are weaker, with Yanbu-Ain Suk fixed at USD 725,000 and Yanbu-Djibouti subsequently fixed at USD 675,000. Naphtha runs heading east have remained quite flat at WS 225. Though some owners are keen on AG imports or ballasting inside, many are more cautious and will wait to see what others do first. DPP is likely to gather momentum earlier than CPP does.

It was a quiet week for Med MRs, with uncertainty in the AG playing a big part. Volume was muted, with little seen on the surface, and of what has been subbed, short runs have taken a few of the spot MRs. WS 140 TA, WS 160 UKC, and WS 185 Med are the latest done, with a feeling of softness around Europe. It is expected that 25–30 window stems will meet the market next week after the slow one just witnessed. Sentiment is quiet, but there is a feeling that rates have bottomed.

Activity also slowed for NWE MRs, with freight levels beginning to soften as more tonnage became available. No new ballasters have been observed heading to the USG, where the large correction on TC14 has continued; the market is now assessed at WS 150, reflecting a softer tone. TC2 is now at WS 135, but differentials for ARA-WAFR and X-UKC over TC2 have narrowed further. The latest X-UKC fixture was seen on subs at WS 165, while WAFR could be expected to freight at WS 195, largely driven by a softer USG market and continued decline in bunker prices. ARA-Argentina has seen its first fresh market test in months, now being done at USD 1.85 Mn. Brazil rates have also softened, with the latest fixture reported at WS 185. Overall, market sentiment for week close is softer.
