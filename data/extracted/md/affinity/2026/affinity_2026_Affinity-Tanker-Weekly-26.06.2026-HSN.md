## Tanker Market Commentary

### Crude Tanker Comments

The VLCC market opened the week on a very firm footing, with the Atlantic leading the way. Strong levels were recorded early, with Brazil-East around WS 197 and WAFR-East at WS 207.5, supported by tight front-end lists and continued uncertainty around the Strait of Hormuz. In the East, sentiment remained harder to gauge, with reports of WS 300+ for Fujairah-East and significantly higher ideas inside the AG, though clarity was limited and much of the activity appeared more test-driven than repeatable. Owners remained cautious on committing inside the AG.

By midweek, the tone had shifted. Headline numbers began to unravel, most notably a reported WS 410 AG fixture failing and being replaced closer to WS 310, triggering a broader correction. The release of a WAF fixture at WS 207.5 added to the softer sentiment. As AG risk premiums eased and levels aligned more closely with Yanbu and Fujairah earnings, momentum faded and charterers regained some leverage in the short term. While this may encourage more enquiry East of Suez going forward, the immediate impact has been softer. The week ended quietly, with little fresh action beyond market rumours. Despite the pullback, underlying supply remains relatively tight, and next week’s direction will depend on whether clearer AG activity emerges and owners regain confidence.

Suezmaxes saw another week of firming for the market, with rates pushing upwards across the board. West Africa rates have continued their ascent, with rates now around WS 230 for TD20 – roughly a 60-point jump from last Friday’s rate, with a tight position list and consistent levels of enquiry enabling owners to push forcefully for further gains. The Americas market has also been active, more so from Brazil than from the usual regions of Guyana and the US Gulf. Brazil rates are now up to WS 235 for UKCM discharge. CPC rates have also seen a major climb this week, with last done now at WS 275, 50 points higher than at the start of the week.

East of Suez has been very slow on the surface this week as uncertainty regarding the passage of the Strait of Hormuz persists. Rates are rumoured to have been paid at WS 625 for East for an inside-AG load, but this has not been confirmed. Moreover, now that VLCCs are crashing above Suezmaxes, it seems like the window of opportunity for Suezmaxes is starting to close.

The Med Aframax tonnage list expanded over the weekend, and a lack of fresh cargoes led to the expectation that rates would continue to decline. Enquiry was low on Monday, and by Tuesday, X-Med rates had corrected down to WS 155. The list still had numerous options, including many which would not ballast TA, preventing a tightening of tonnage. While rates did not plummet at the back end of the week, they still fell, and it seems that the market has bottomed. TD19 sits at WS 155 at close on Friday.

Unlike the Mediterranean, the North Sea was tightening at the beginning of the week after a few end-month cargoes were worked at the end of last one. Many owners continued choosing to ballast TA, decreasing tonnage supply in the area. Despite surrounding markets’ not having found a stable floor, these potential ballasters restricted natural candidates, allowing rates to remain at WS 145 in the middle of the week after rising from WS 140 on Monday. TD7 ends the week at WS 145, with sentiment steady.


### Product Tanker Comments

The week started very slowly for AG LR2s, and few details have emerged since then. A few Red Sea agreements were made after one or two weeks of little open market visibility. USD 3.5 Mn was agreed for Yanbu-UKC, and USD 4 Mn for Gizan-UKC. Some speculators suggest that these numbers are not as soft as some might think, and when considering that the West loading market has softened to USD 4.325 Mn for Skikda- Japan, these rates are quite respectable.

There are a few shipowners in the Arabian Gulf in a position to transit the Strait of Hormuz, but the IRGC’s presence has discouraged many from travelling, so the marketplace is still stagnated. USD 10 Mn has been rumoured for AG-UKC this week, and there is an own programme ship due to perform a Kuwait-East naphtha run, though the rate has been kept internal. The market is as secretive as it has ever been and, considering crew safety, one can understand why. There are signs of life, but the market outside the AG has remained flat, with WS 210 being repeated for 76,000 T naphtha STS Sohar-East and seen for a newbuild 90,000 T Sikka-Singapore. The position list mid-week was the longest it had been in 10 weeks, so charterers have had ships at their disposal to maintain last-done levels as the hopes of stratospheric rates for loading outside the Strait of Hormuz have not materialised.

There were flutters of activity amongst AG LR1s this week, but the market is still struggling to gather momentum in the wake of the disruptions in the region. Supply greatly outstrips demand, but there has not been enough cargo to test rates. Even Red Sea activity has taken a downturn in recent weeks. TC5 had paid WS 210 ex-Sikka, five points lower than the previously agreed ex-Duqm rate. Westbound rates have not really leaked into the market, but Duqm-Argie was paid at USD 4 Mn, so the UKC equivalent could be assessed at USD 3.85 Mn via Cape of Good Hope. Questions are being asked within the AG, but little has come to fruition. Short-haul runs have been limited, but USD 700,000 was agreed for a Sohar-Pakistan run. Though sentiment is strong amongst all three segments, LRs overall lack the fundamentals to support such sentiment in the market beyond the short term. Owners will be cautious following the news that a cargo ship had been struck while transiting the Strait of Hormuz.

After a very quiet first half of the week with just one stem seen working over Monday and Tuesday, the Med MR market picked up, with five or six fixtures concluded over the back end. Sentiment is weak, with Europe as a whole oversupplied, and despite owners’ argument that earnings are tight, rates have dropped in small increments here. The latest tests sit at WS 130 TA, WS 150 UKC, and WS 170 Med-WAFR. There is nothing outstanding going into close of business for the week, and looking forward, it seems that there is little hope of a rebound for owners. The outlook is bleak.

The week opened with ideas in the Med Handysize market spread due to two WS 160s subbed late last Friday. It later came to light that these levels were agreed off the back of specific circumstances, including that one was a discharge reload and the other was a vessel without a sire being taken. Off the back of this, owners held the market at WS 170, with the number now widely repeated. High bunker costs have been the main argument for levels not softening any further, and until these prices change, owners’ mindsets will not either. Overall, it was a quieter week, with some back-end fixing helping add red to the tonnage list. The market is stable for now, but if the inactivity continues, pressure could begin to mount.
