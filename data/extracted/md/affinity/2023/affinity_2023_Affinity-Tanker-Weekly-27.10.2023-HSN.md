## Tanker Market Commentary

### Crude Tanker Comments

This was a strong week for VLCC’s and to be honest mainly driven from the Atlantic and the smaller sizes again with overspill cargoes in play, whilst MEG had an active first half which trimmed tonnage, on top of further owners ignoring the shorter runs and deciding to seek the pot of gold at the end of the Atlantic rainbow. Those left with MEG cargoes less than say 15 days forward face a tricky proposition and the likelihood of TD3C levels pushing in to the WS 60’s and beyond. The list will refresh, and 15-20 November fixing may prove a little more straightforward, but if nothing else, with TD15 heading towards mid WS 60’s and TD22 back to USD 10 Mn owners will look to maintain a balance on TCE’s across all regions. The prevalence of inter-Atlantic runs returned as Aframax and Suezmax earnings moved in excess of USD 80K/day and USD 60K/day respectively, so VLCC owners were able to increase rates even if merely to prevent capping Suezmax freight for WAF/Brazil/USG - Europe runs. USG has actually been relatively quiet with arb margins reportedly tighter this week, but it was interesting to note that even when tonnage committed to the Atlantic swells, there’s no haste displayed by these owners, and they remain confident that another flurry is just around the corner.

It was a busy start to the week for Atlantic Suezmaxes, with activity bubbling in both the Americas and WAF, and owners now in control, pushing freight higher. Cargo enquiry kept hitting the market throughout the week - TD20 reached WS 145 levels, guaranteeing earnings in the USD 62K/day for an Eco ship. Also, the American side of the Atlantic remained sanguine, boosted by a very active Aframax market, and a VLCC market gaining some traction, especially ex-Brazil. Last done ex-USG is rumoured to be near WS 130 levels and, given the current momentum, owners are expected to at least try beat it. The Med has also been showing signs of life, boosting owners’ sentiment there. Overall availabilities have been tight throughout the week in the West of Suez, and we head into the weekend with enquiry still outstanding as well as a tight tonnage list, which bodes well for next week. Meanwhile, there has been little action to speak of in the MEG, where rates have remained largely flat.

Afras in the Med had an active start to the week, with prompt enquiry once again fuelling morale and rates - we have seen numerous short-haul cargoes paying even WS 245, while plain vanilla X-Med has been assessed in the WS 220 levels throughout by the Baltic Exchange. In terms of earnings, this translates to around USD 70K/day for an Eco ship. As close of play nears, dates are finally shifting to a more natural fixing window. Ceyhan loadings have started to settle significantly below the escalated WS 240+ levels sees earlier. However, the list remains pretty slim, without any owner with more than two ships, and with a USG spiralling out of control, which could attract some ballasters over the weekend. In fact, earnings here reached USD 84K/day levels for an Eco ship, slightly overshadowed by the USD 89K/day of the North Sea, but with significantly more activity. Overall, morale is set to remain strong.

On Monday, the North Sea finally reached its true potential after months of disappointment. Prompt replacement cargoes were left with a dire list, and owners didn't hold back, pushing freight up by over 35 WS points to WS 208. However, things have slowed down significantly since then, with rates settling between WS 210 - 215. Still, sentiment remains bullish as the list is fairly slim, and weather forecasts remain dire until 3 November, meaning that there is some replacement enquiry looming. Strong neighbouring markets should also keep sentiment sustained.


### Product Tanker Comments

LR2s in the AG have been wobbling along this week. While initially lighter, especially on the prompt side, the list has lengthened and is now in line with annual averages. Owners have generally maintained positive sentiment,  but there is only so much one can do with such minimal demand, consequently we have seen incremental softening. TC1 failed at WS 175 for Japan but two cargoes since then have been agreed at WS 172.5. Even westbound, which generally stays competitive even in softer weeks, has been flat around the USD 4.4-4.45 Mn levels. The thinking here is to take a shorter run at less and reposition for final voyage in December, as optimism for Q4 is still strong. Despite a slightly softer week, earnings remain positive, and the medium-term picture is still optimistic, especially given the rumours that refining capacity is due to return towards full throughput around mid-November.

The LR1 market has seen enough demand to hold freight rates well, with WS 170 agreed for TC5, and westbound holding at USD 3.45 Mn for WCI/UKC. The list is balanced and overall sentiment appears stable. However, a softer AG MR market and a stuttering LR2 sector could start impacting the LR1s next week.

Uninspiring and dull seem to be the most appropriate adjectives to describe the North and South Asian MR markets this week. The absence of Chinese cargoes due to the tight export quota surely does not raise any positive hopes for owners. There might have been numerous vessels going on quiet subjects with no further details emerging, but the long tonnage list and oversupply of prompt tonnage continue to pose as a big worry for the market. Freight rates have been under heavy pressure, which has resulted in a Korea/Australia run being traded 20 WS points below last done at WS 205. It might be a matter of time for owners to see the light at the end of the tunnel, but without doubt that will not be in the coming week.

It has been a positive week overall for UKC MRs, but there needs to be more enquiry to help leverage freight rates up further. The tonnage list is limited, which pushed TC2 up by 15 WS points from Monday to WS 155 at the time of writing, guaranteeing TCEs of around USD 17K/day for an Eco ship. However, there is potential for it to firm further, if the available units left continue to push.

Meanwhile, the USG MR market has been doing an impressive job of being not in the slightest bit interesting this week, whilst at the same time teasing that the it may soon reach an inflection point. The fixture count is higher than last week, but the market has been held back by an oversupply of tonnage. Owners have by and large hit the repeat button on short-haul routes, which is viewed as the least bad decision right now.  The intrigue surrounds the question of what owners are willing to fix their ships at on long-haul routes given such a steep FFA curve for November and December and the answer is a wide spread. Of course, the physical market isn’t destined to follow the FFA curve and Europe needs to strengthen as well to prevent an influx of ballasters into the USG region, which would stifle rates.

It has been an active week for Med Handies. Tonnage has become increasingly tight, while rates have been climbing steadily to WS 200 being repeated multiple times for TC6 towards the end of the week. Prompt tonnage is looking very tight now, yet with many units waiting in the 5–10-day window to replenish those that have been absorbed, rates aren’t likely to shoot up significantly just yet. UKC Handies have been quiet this week with X-UKC paying WS 175.
