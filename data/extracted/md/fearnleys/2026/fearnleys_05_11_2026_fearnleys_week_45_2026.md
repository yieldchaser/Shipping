# Fearnleys Weekly Report - Week 45, 2026

**Week 45 - November 4, 2026**

## 01 Tankers

### VLCC

Asia's imports of crude oil recovered in October to match levels prior to the Iran conflict which goes far in explaining the stronger sentiment last week. Alas, the rear-view mirror trumps real time information these days with owners and charterers alike having become masters of deflection and obfuscation with plenty of artful dissembling, which have taken a bit of the sting out of the market, and this week the market took a bit of a hit. Suezmaxes led the charge down yesterday, and with now a considerable aperture between VLCC and Suezmax rates the former will be at a disadvantage and under pressure to narrow the gap, especially in the Atlantic. TD34 has seen a downward adjustment with WS 610 now reportedly concluded, albeit on a relet – and although there’s been a bit of a cleanup oil company- and trader relets are still dominating the position list. However, all said and done, we’re still talking historical high rates and corresponding returns, even if there should be a further correction, which appears likely.

### Suezmax

Supply keeps lengthening in the Atlantic with 14 FOC excluding the flood of 18 FOC ex East that we are excluding from West Africa counts. Demand expectations remain solid with Guyana & West Africa still holding good volumes back, 25MBBLS expected from the latter for the balance of November. The working window keeps coming prompter and prompter however and a strong FOC availability, the weakness of the forward curve and the high RELET presence (10 ETA up to 20th for West Africa) may encourage charterers to be patient and allow it to move to a 2 week window. Oil price changes in the wake of the midterms may alter this complexion & we are significantly more competitive than VLCCs basis last done levels. However USG crudes are priced better for transatlantic moves - domain of the AFRAs - and Johan Sverdrup into the East - domain of the VLCCs - and FFAs continue to erode, so don't hold your breath.

### Aframax

#### North Sea

What a difference a week makes. Western Aframax markets have all fallen rapidly this week with limited activity allowing charterers to take freight down. North Sea has seen market correct from freight in the WS 500’s down into the 300’s. Dates have pushed into the 2nd decade but we still have enough prompt free of cargo tonnage looking for employment. With US and Mediterranean markets also becoming weaker, alternative options for owners are not as obvious at the moment. We are heading into the time of year where unpredictable weather does have an influence, which may help owners find some stability, although some more softening expected before that occurs.

#### Mediterranean

The softening of the Mediterranean market continues as activity remains lacklustre for the 1st half of November with more than enough tonnage. Owners are looking for alternatives, but we have only seen a handful of vessels ballasting out. As in the North Sea, returns are strong on the right dates but we need to find a new floor.

## 02 Dry Bulk

### Capesize

After a slow start to the week, markets found new traction on Capesize, with big volumes being fixed on both Atlantic and Pacific trade routes over the last 24 hours. Pacific gained traction today with a 3.5% gain on paper, as Baltic prints 13.69 for C5 West Australia/Qingdao. So far today we have seen over 15 fixtures ex West Australia spanning between low USD 13s - high USD 13s with last done in the USD 13.8s. Similar momentum was also seen on C3 Brazil/China with owners offering in high USD 36-37 levels. A series of fixtures concluding today in USD 35.5 levels and unconfirmed rumors of a modern Cape fixing at USD 37 for an end November stem is yet to be confirmed. A handful of fresh stems in the North Atlantic sees some transatlantic rounds being done, with most recent Erdemir tender fixing in the low USD 20s. After some quiet weeks in the North, renewed interest has seen rocketing rates on paper as the Baltic prints a gain of 15.89% for transatlantic C8 round voyage. FFAs are in the green across the board, with more upside expected in the near term.

### Panamax

The Panamax market has begun to recover after a softer start to the week, with firmer Pacific fixtures leading the improvement. In the Atlantic, a shorter tonnage list and better NCSA grain demand are helping transatlantic rates find a floor, although US-origin fronthaul inquiry remains limited. ECSA has steadied, but available tonnage still outweighs fresh cargo, and December dates remain largely untested. The Pacific has picked up more clearly, with Aussie coal stems and Japanese tenders drawing charterers closer to owners’ offers and lifting fixture levels, while NoPac grain business remains quieter. We see scope for further near-term gains, but a sustained recovery still needs more Atlantic cargo to absorb the ships available.

### Supramax

The Supramax market remained under pressure across most regions, with Atlantic rates gradually softening and activity in the Indian Ocean weakening amid limited fresh enquiry and growing tonnage availability. The Pacific, which had shown resilience, also lost momentum as NoPac demand came off, while parts of the Pacific remained relatively steady. The Handysize market was generally quiet, with little activity across both basins and limited fresh enquiry. The Continent and Mediterranean remained largely stable, while the US Gulf and South Atlantic faced softer sentiment due to more tonnage.

## 03 Gas

### Chartering

Coming close to the mid-point of this mid-term election week, freight has shown resilience. After seeing freight touching USD 300 flat last week, rates now appear to have stabilized just below the USD 320 level. At the time of writing, we are counting four confirmed spot fixtures in the USG so far this week, with at least one more active cargo in play. In addition to these, we are seeing cargoes being worked in West Africa, and fixtures being concluded both out of Vadinar and Australia.

With nine December spot deals concluded so far, all in the first half, freight looks fairly balanced with what looks like less than 10 remaining ships before 15th of December, including a likely overhang of a couple of ships open on November dates. Freight is expected to remain largely arb-driven going forward. That said, with a fairly balanced to tight position list, rates are expected to remain robust.
