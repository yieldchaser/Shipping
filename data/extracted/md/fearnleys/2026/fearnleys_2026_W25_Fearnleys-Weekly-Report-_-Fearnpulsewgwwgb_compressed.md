## Tankers
## VLCC
"Much Ado About Nothing". Friday can’t come quick enough, in more ways than one.
In the meantime, players are sitting on their hands in anticipation of the Strait of
Hormuz opening. However, even if US and Iran put pen to paper we doubt we’ll see an immediate rush inbound any time soon. Seeing is believing and so on. Priority will likely be to get ships stuck in the Gulf out. The immediate urge for impulse oil buying may also be curbed as there are still tens of millions of barrels of crude stuck onboard those trapped ships in the Gulf, and refineries will need to process and digest this backlog first. There’s of course a rate out there for anything, money always talks, but the spread between what those owners willing to break the mould would want and what charterers would be willing to pay is going to take a broking job and a half to bridge. With the pot of gold at the end of the rainbow now becoming more tangible owners are naturally also pricing up all other less precarious alternatives as well. He who blinks first… 17/06/2026, 23:05

## Aframax
## Rates
Fairly active start to the week in the West, with the East beginning to see increased attention amid growing chatter around a potential Iran/US deal. While West Africa supply remains healthy for now, a thin list of local openers in USG continues to leave charterers reliant on UKCM ballasters, effectively drawing on the same tonnage that would otherwise service West Africa. Growing expectations of a deal appears to support the sentiment, with more vessels likely to remain positioned in the East in anticipation of a potential opening of the Strait of Hormuz.
### North Sea
North Sea market has taken a hit this week with WS 140 on subs for a North Sea –
Baltic cargo and it feels like more to come. On paper relets should come back into the fixing window, and we expect a fair amount of programming to occur, as with all
Western Aframax markets being softer, ballasting doesn’t necessarily give you an outlet, and reducing waiting times will be key.
### Mediterranean
Market has adjusted downwards after 1-2 market quotes, but we haven't seen it collapsing. There is still underground activity that is taking out tonnage quietly but at the same time there is a couple of vessels stuck prompt hoping for earlier dates. We are more or less done for Ceyhan June dates and Libya hasn't much cargoes left either, so owners need to think about their next move while there aren't many alternatives.
