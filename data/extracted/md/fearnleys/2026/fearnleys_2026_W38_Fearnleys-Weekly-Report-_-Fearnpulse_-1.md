# Fearnleys Weekly Report - Week 38, 2026

**Week 38 - September 16, 2026**

## 01 Tankers

### VLCC

VLCC rates for a Fujairah/East run are now 3/4 on the way to WS 1000! Arguably charterers and trades alike also have themselves to “blame” as they have relet ships at increased intervals as well. The Atlantic market is also firing on all cylinders although mainly left for Suezmaxes now as VLCC supply is running out. All in all access to physical crude trumps freights at the moment as refineries are scrambling for supply. The big concern is the disruption to Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline and Yanbu export infrastructure, following attacks on Saudi energy facilities. Saudi Arabia is reportedly now offering more loadings of crude oil to Asian refiners via ship-to-ship transfers off Oman's Sohar port. However, visible vessel transits through the Strait of Hormuz remain in the single digits.

### Suezmax

Split Stems ex Brazil & West Africa along with an influx of USG/East serves to populate the Atlantic basin heavily with enquiry. Rates, whilst astronomical, did not price in the huge demand upside this market had & still has amidst a dearth of VLCCs. VLCC availability for Brazil up to end October could be as low as 4-5 ships including projections and with USG/West Africa markets drawing down on the same pool that leaves colossal volumes for Suezmaxes. 36MBBLS ex West Africa alone are outstanding up to 20th October, vs 38 logical ships either FOC, ex local positions or ETS within 7 days. Again these must be portioned across both sides of the Atlantic.

CPC has failed to keep pace. In fact the recent fixtures at 500 level are more an indication that some owners, in spite of market momentum, are prioritising minimising waiting time to optimise TCEs. This is characteristic of the market in general and, along with the tonnage taken on Short TC cheaper than market TCEs, has stopped the market in general from roofing for now. Rather than fixing forward as usual, CPC, partially due to a delay in stem releases is working prompter than the Atlantic basin, but once it shifts to 8-10 onwards the expectation is that TD6 ticks up.

Meanwhile the MEG, bolstered by VLCC rates, has climbed to 130x690 levels Fujairah/East - there are availabilities east of Suez, but a good amount of these won't go near the region, instead they are set to head to COGH. Given the severely limited availability on VLCC solid demand is expected here also.

### Aframax

#### North Sea

A less populated list, firm surrounding markets and charterers reaching further out have all pushed the North Sea up pushing close to WS 100 points since the middle of last week. Natural working window is now pushing towards 28-29 dates. The first 5 days of October stems are relatively balanced month on month but we should start to see natural tonnage come back around heading into October. Market remains firm for the time being it seems.

#### Mediterranean

TCE remains massive in all markets across the West with the Mediterranean market still busy despite the withdrawal of all STS Port Said/Sidi cargoes. Ceyhan dates have pushed to end-month now while Libya is still around mid-3rd decade. The list is balanced and won't replenish too much before 22-23 Sep and West Mediterranean positions could give priority to the USG market.

## 02 Dry Bulk

### Capesize

This week we saw softening sentiment across the Capesize basins. Today activity across Atlantic and Pacific was fairly quiet following last week's market highs; sentiment turned negative as we saw C3 and C5 easing yesterday. Pacific weakness dominated as yesterday's fixtures concluded in the mid USD 16s, well below the earlier week USD 17+ offers, reflecting a lack of bidding in the pacific. This morning a recent fixture reported for a West Australian fixture at USD 15.95 for early October laycans, signaling potential further softening. Atlantic sentiment also flattened with TA and FH under pressure as forward offers struggle to find support. Although todays C3 index rose slightly to USD 42.46, owners continue to offer at mid USD 43 levels with little feedback, and yesterday we saw last done levels for mid-October dates around mid-low USD 42. Both basins overall have seen a loss in momentum compared to last week's hot market. FFAs has mixed sentiment with future freight showing slight upside going forward.

### Panamax

The Panamax market has drifted lower through the week, with the softer tone now evident across both basins. In the Atlantic, a steady build of prompt tonnage in the North Continent, together with a lengthening list of ballasters arriving into ECSA, has shifted the balance in charterers' favour; fronthaul has proved more resilient on the back of continued US-origin grain and coal demand, but TA business has borne the brunt of the correction. The Pacific, which had outperformed into early September, has likewise lost momentum, with charterers stepping back and bidding conservatively – NoPac grain rounds and a steady flow of Australian and Indo coal are providing a floor rather than a driver. Period interest remains genuine, with charterers engaged on one- and two-year cover, but owners' ideas and charterers' bids have yet to converge, and modest backwardation beyond the fourth quarter is tempering appetite at the top end. Our read into next week is cautious: absent a fresh wave of cargo replenishment in the Atlantic, we expect a sideways-to-softer tone, with any recovery more likely to originate in the Pacific.

### Supramax

The Supramax market saw mixed sentiment, with softer conditions in North America offset by improving levels in the East, particularly on backhauls. The Continent also showed signs of improvement, although reported activity remained limited. ECSA remained active, while the Indian Ocean continued to see healthy demand with little change in levels. Improved sentiment in Asia brought renewed period interest, with owners showing greater confidence. Overall, the market continued its upward trend, supported by stronger Eastern sentiment and steady activity across several regions.

The Handy market remained relatively quiet, with limited fresh information but a generally healthy underlying tone. The Continent and Mediterranean stayed broadly balanced, with rates holding steady. The South Atlantic and USG continued to show firmer sentiment, supported by healthy demand and tightening prompt tonnage. Asia remained subdued, although sufficient cargo volumes helped keep the market broadly stable. Overall, the market was steady to slightly firmer, with regional variations in momentum.

## 03 Gas

### Chartering

VLGC freight ex US back at all-time high levels with several deals subfixed at USD 300+ HCvP so far this week. Last done is currently at USD 325 HCvP on a Neo Panama vessel which brings the fixture count for October to 25 spot deals (5/12/8 by decade). There are currently only a handful of vessels available for spot enquiries during last decade October and it is worth noting that the market is 10 fixtures short of the YTD average on a monthly basis (35). While we still see several uncovered requirements ex US in October, some players have already surfaced for 1st decade November cargos as freight remains very tight.
