# Fearnleys Weekly Report - Week 23, 2026

**Week 23 - June 3, 2026**

## 01 Tankers

### VLCC

A noticeably quiet week for VLCCs, shaped by industry participants’ temporary migration to Greece for Posidonia. Activity has slowed, position lists fairly balanced, but ships disappearing quietly with no details. Prompt cargoes in the Middle East showing signs of paying extra, but a slightly softer feel in the Atlantic with USG/East holding for above 16 million for the time being.

### Suezmax

High off market activity pruned lists, primarily characterised by Mediterranean/West Africa fixtures whilst the US still fails to pull its weight. Charterers eagerness for simple deals pre/during-Posidonia and with the easy excuse last Friday of the "risk" of a resolution in the Middle East spiking sentiment lead to high activity countering weak supply fundamentals and stagnating rates. CPC enquiry is working but plenty of ships are keen to cover it, some pointing towards Canakkale on spec. West Africa is fixed into the 3rd decade with over 10 natural & pushed FOC vessels despite high erosion. Charterers can now sit and enjoy the remainder of their Athens escapades and let availabilities rebuild.

### Aframax

#### North Sea

The majority of the first decade stems are now covered with little in the way of actual market activity. Rates fell to WS 145 at the end of last week but with US and Mediterranean markets picking up the North Sea should have stabilized and with tonnage expected to ballast away may have bottomed. With some Norwegian field maintenance now over the 2nd half of June on paper looks to be busier than the first half which should give owners some optimism.

#### Mediterranean

The end of last week was very busy with charterers fixing both ahead in dates and ahead of Posidonia. This week has remained active with a mixture of new business and some replacement business which has pushed the market up over 40 points very quickly. The list is on the tighter side of balanced for good itinerary tonnage and some ports are experiencing some delays which is helping to keep it balanced. Sentiment is firm from owners. We may see some vessels ballast from the North Sea but probably not enough to change the momentum for this week at least.

## 02 Dry Bulk

### Capesize

This week on Capesize, C3 Brazil/China remains firm but thin fresh activity, with ideas hovering around USD 38 today for July stems. With slightly softening forward curve and lack of prompt tonnage we expect to see slight easing for Q3 stems. The Atlantic remains hot for another week, with transatlantic C8 pushing USD 58,000 per day and C9 at USD 76,972 reflecting strong front haul demand and limited prompt tonnage in continent. Ballasters are still favouring Brazil/China C3 but North Atlantic continue to command premium rates. C5 Pacific still remains firm but slightly off the recent highs. Some forward softness is expected coming into late June. Owners ideas are currently hovering around USD 16 and bids reported in the mid USD 15s for June 18-22 stems.

C3 Brazil/China last done USD 38 21-30 June. C5 West Australia/China last done USD 15.85 16-18 June, last done USD 15.7 22-24 June.

### Panamax

The Panamax market opened the week on a softer note, with Posidonia and regional holidays keeping activity limited and many players still reassessing direction. The Atlantic remains under pressure, with limited fresh mineral and grain enquiry meeting a growing prompt tonnage list in the Continent and Mediterranean, forcing earlier-position owners to trim expectations. ECSA is broadly flat but feels toppy for index dates, with spot tonnage still discounted due to limited cargo volume. In the Pacific, Indonesia and Australian mineral flows continue to provide some support, but sentiment has turned more cautious as tonnage builds and fresh requirements remain thin. Overall, near-term indicators still point bearish, while coal demand and potential Chinese weather disruptions remain the main upside risk.

### Supramax

Despite Posidonia & regional holidays around, market continued to exhibit a generally constructive tone. In the Atlantic, sentiment remained positive, particularly in USG where tightening vessel avails continued to support owners’ expectations. ECSA maintained firm underlying fundamentals, although fresh business remained limited. In Asia, it was a mixed picture, with stronger demand evident in the north while ample prompt tonnage weighed on sentiment further south. The Handy sector showed comparatively firmer momentum across Asia, supported by tightening vessel supply and steady cargo demand, helping offset the subdued activity seen across much of the Atlantic basin. With the upcoming monsoon season in India, demand is expected to slowdown in the coming weeks. Period activity remains limited with very few fixtures reported in the region.

## 03 Gas

### Chartering

USG market remains quiet with limited visible fixing. Two deals reported at USD 270, with broader Apr/May activity at 48 liftings ex-Houston. Forward July coverage is still thin at just four fixtures, split between USD 270 and USD 285, pointing to a firmer tone. Most activity this week has been done discreetly, masking underlying positioning. An Asian oil major tender is currently in play and could set the next benchmark. Baltic sentiment remains stable-to-firm, supporting the view of gradual upside.
