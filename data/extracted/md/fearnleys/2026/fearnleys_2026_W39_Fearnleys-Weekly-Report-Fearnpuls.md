# Fearnleys Weekly Report - Week 39, 2026

**Week 39 - September 23, 2026**

## 01 Tankers

### VLCC

After last weeks fixing frenzy with rates jumping into the WS 800’s for Fujairah-GOO eastbound cargoes things have calmed down this week. That is not to say that rates are set for a downward spiral, but the immediate upward pressure has eased and some “profit taking” is to be expected. But even if rates were to drop, say 100 points, a WS 700 Fujairah-GOO/East is nevertheless historically highs and if you add 3 zero’s, daily earnings are not far off the WS number. It’s also a logistical challenge to match and load ships at Fujairah to ships coming through the strait of Hormuz and the recent high fixing volume could explain in part why activity has eased as charterers need to clear the decks before adding new ones. However, position lists short term are still tight in both the east and west so players should be warned against a false sense of security in lieu of a perceived activity slowdown.

### Suezmax

A few mysteries have emerged east and west of Suez. The East remains tight on tonnage and rates red hot, whereas in the West supply continues to build. For West Africa supply, we have strong relet presence, a dozen FOC and a dozen to sail over the next 5 days (inclusive of 5 local openers) ETA Nigeria by 15th. In terms of West Africa demand, further tracking suggests there are around 15 cargoes left to move in the 2nd decade. Under normal market conditions, the supply/demand ratio would likely lead to some softening. That said, now more than ever, eastbound voyages are likely to command significant premiums. With the armada already fixed East, those remaining are discouraged from joining the wave, albeit there is always a price. Additionally, there are other forces at play that continue to keep the market interesting and capable of swinging. Aframaxes remain very hot, Sidi barrels are reportedly returning, VLCCs remain extremely tight, and a Trump gasoil export ban will leave Europe yearning for even more crude.

### Aframax

Rates for local North Sea voyages are reaching record levels and owners are trying to push for a bit more for each cargo being quoted. The tonnage list remains very limited for short voyages, but a bit more candidates for longer voyages. Some owners see ballasting to USG or Mediterranean as good alternatives hence list will continue to look tight for 1st decade October loading dates. Rates in the Mediterranean is also very firm, but feels a bit toppy. However, some voyages give returns over USD 200k pd and as every waiting day is very expensive so owners will look at surrounding markets to minimize waiting adding pressure on tonnage availability. So no expected downside in this market yet.

## 02 Dry Bulk

### Capesize

Capesize markets across the board have seen minor variations but generally very hot on all routes. C3 Brazil/China has seen high rates all week, however quieter activity with slightly flatter rates today as fewer fresh stems came to market ex Brazil and West Africa. C3 Tubarao/China is currently set by Baltic Exchange at USD 43.12. Recently mid October dates have seen offers close to USD 45pmt, with 2H October hovering in the USD 43s and 1H November seeing around USD 43 flat on the offer side. The Pacific markets have seen a flurry of recent fixtures ex West Australia, with early October stems fixing in USD 16.5-16.75 levels. North Atlantic activity has seen a fair amount of fresh interest this week with more fronthaul and transatlantic stems entering the market. Recently offers for transatlantic from Seven Islands to Djen Djen were heard around high USD 24 levels. Earlier in the week a tender was awarded USD 23.75pmt (or USD 52K equivalent) for a Narvik/Erdemir cargo for early October dates. Recent rumors of 60K being done for a transatlantic stem is yet to be confirmed.

### Panamax

The Panamax market has turned this week, with the index reversing the softer trend of the preceding fortnight and all main routes moving higher in unison. The Atlantic has led the recovery: fresh transatlantic enquiry, a steadier flow of NCSA and USG grain stems and a tightening prompt list in the North Continent have allowed owners to hold their ideas, while fronthaul remains the firmer leg and is lending support to shorter intra-basin business. The Pacific is constructive rather than tight – NoPac and Australian coal and grain demand is well distributed and Indonesian volumes have improved, but tonnage remains adequate and owners have found it harder to push materially above last done. The South African and Indian Ocean leg is the swing factor, with that route firming and beginning to compete for the same ships, which should limit downside in the Pacific into the coming weeks. Period interest is active on both Kamsarmax and Panamax with owners quoting to the firmer side of last done, and while bid and offer remain apart, the paper curve carries a modest premium into Q4 and the overall tone into next week is constructive.

### Supramax

The Supra/Ultra market remained broadly steady over the past few sessions, with activity uneven across regions and sentiment generally cautious. While some isolated fixtures achieved notably strong returns due to vessel positioning and specific requirements, overall trading volumes were relatively limited. The Atlantic showed mixed signals, with firm pockets in some areas but a lack of broad-based momentum. In Asia, activity was affected by holiday in Japan/Far East, although selective opportunities continued to support earnings. Period interest remained stable, reflecting underlying confidence despite a wait-and-see approach from many owners and charterers alike. Overall, the market appears balanced for now, awaiting fresh demand and clearer direction before its next significant move.

## 03 Gas

### Chartering

#### WEST

The positive momentum seen at the end of last week continued into the start of this week, with freight rates moving higher as vessel availability tightened and the arbitrage improved. Mid-October positions were quickly taken up, leaving charterers with fewer options and giving owners greater confidence. Although fixture activity has slowed in recent days, the market remains firm. Relets have gradually been absorbed back into FOB programmes, further reducing the number of available ships for the remaining October cargoes. With tonnage supply continuing to tighten, owners remain in a strong position and freight levels are expected to stay well supported.

#### EAST

In the East, activity remains focused on short-term employment, with continued interest in short-period time charters for STS operations via Sohar and Vadinar. This provides owners with an attractive alternative to the spot market and keeps more vessels in the region. As a result, fewer ships are expected to move West, helping to maintain a tighter tonnage balance.
