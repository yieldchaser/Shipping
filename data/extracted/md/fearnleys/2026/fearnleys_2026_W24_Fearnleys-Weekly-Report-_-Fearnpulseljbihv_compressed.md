## 01 Tankers

### VLCC

Ships continue to disappear under the radar on the VLCC front and the position lists both sides of Suez are becoming wanting in the front end, which should dictate a further uptick in rates. Capping that is lack of transparency. A tight supply/demand picture is one thing but not having a pricing reference is clouding proceedings. If owners really want to push this, they will have to start leaking a bit more information – real time – if not the assumption will be that it’s just more of the same. Rumours suggest there is still prompt interest from Fuj/Oman from the likes of Admic and KPC et al., and not much to choose from with WS 140 eastbound seemingly a “given” if nothing else comes to light. Petrobras keeps the flame burning from the Atlantic with almost daily cargo quotes Brazil/East and rates have inched up a couple of points north of the WS 115 mark. Sinkor is back in buying mood if we are to believe reports, the Maxim being the latest to be added, at a stonking price for an older lady ('11 built). Never have so many had one player to thank for so much to paraphrase Churchill, without Sinokor’s big market share and restraint in fixing we wouldn’t be even close to current levels.

### Suezmax

A busy first half of the week, with an enormous amount of off market activity alongside some reported fixtures. Tonnage in the West has tightened, however, supply should remain healthy enough to prevent aggressive upside. With this level of activity, the demand outlook has also been somewhat undermined. We are likely down to the last 5 mbbls to cover in West Africa for June, with a similar volume remaining out of Guyana for the month. Naturally, with July stems only just entering the 3 week window, charterers may opt to take a step back from fixing into July.

### Aframax

#### North Sea

The market has remained stable through the 2nd decade in the low WS 150’s. Natural window is now moving into the 3rd decade and with relets still in the mix taking stems activity has been limited. With surrounding markets also looking softer there isn’t much in the way of pressure on charterers so steady as we go for the near future.

#### Mediterranean

A Libyan cargo on Monday managed to attract 6-7 offers in and as a result cross Mediterranean runs corrected by 15 points which was then repeated multiple times. Activity has been consistently healthy, but the fundamental issue is the excess tonnage supply as 70% of the total LR2's on the water are trading in DPP with majority in the West of Suez markets. US markets took a blow yesterday however the flow of ballasters is expected to remain steady from Europe. Overall, steady/softer with owners to try and stop the downfall.

## 02 Dry Bulk

### Capesize

A softer week on Cape with Pacific basins down from recent highs over the last week with further downward pressure expected. June C5 West Australia/China fresh fixtures indicate last done levels in the low USD 12 range with late June last done seen USD 12 flat and end June seeing USD 12.35. For Atlantic, both C3 Brazil/China and North Atlantic are trading firm with mixed sentiment. C3 Brazil/China stems see offers for late June dates in the low USD 37 range and early July offers at mid USD 36 levels. One fixture reported today was 11-20 laycan fixing USD 35 on a Newcastlemax. With strong demand and limited fresh tonnage in the North Atlantic, transatlantic routes remain firm as owners hold out for premium fronthaul rates to return amid continued tightness in the basin. C3 Brazil/China 21-30 June offers USD 37.25, 1-10 July bids USD 34, offers USD 36.5, 11-20 June last done (Newcastlemax) USD 35. C5 West Australia/China 19-21 June last done USD 12, 25-27 June last done 12.35.

### Panamax

The Panamax market remains divided between a gradually improving Atlantic and a weakening Pacific. Atlantic sentiment has turned slightly more constructive, supported by better transatlantic activity, firmer fronthaul demand, and increasing NCSA enquiry, although ample tonnage and wide bid-offer spreads continue to cap gains. In the Pacific, limited cargo volumes, a growing tonnage list, and cautious charterers are keeping pressure on rates, particularly on Indonesian business. While vessel tightness indicators still point to near-term softness, strong shipment volumes, supportive demand fundamentals, and a coal forward curve now moving into slight backwardation suggest the market could find better support as July approaches.

### Supramax

The Supramax market maintained a positive tone, supported by stronger enquiry in the Atlantic and improving activity across Asia. Market reported firmer sentiment in the US Gulf and South Atlantic, while growing backhaul demand in North Asia helped support overall vessel demand. The Handysize also strengthened, with tighter tonnage availability and balanced supply-demand conditions contributing to a firmer market environment. Activity remained steady across most regions, with the US Gulf and Asian markets showing the most encouraging signs of improvement.

## 03 Gas

### Chartering

#### WEST

Spot fixing activity has been subdued in the West this week, except from one Asian major fixing a ship at around USD 250 HCvP for 3rd decade July via tender. Forward fixing out of the US Gulf remains limited, with just 8 spot fixtures recorded so far for July loading (3/4/1 by decade). This is a marked slowdown compared to previous months and highlights the current lack of visible cargo flow. Meanwhile, prompt ship availability remains tight, with only two VLGCs available for loading up to 10th July, and just a handful of ships for 10-15 July window. This constrained availability suggests that the market retains upside potential and could quickly firm once more cargoes emerge.

#### EAST

MEG exports remain constrained amid the ongoing Iran situation, limiting conventional cargo flow. However, alternative setups have recently created some business, with STS cargoes reported off Sohar at premium rates. These have been fixed around USD 215 RT/CH.
