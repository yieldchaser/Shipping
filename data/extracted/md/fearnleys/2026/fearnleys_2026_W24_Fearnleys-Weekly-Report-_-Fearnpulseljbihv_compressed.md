## VLCC
Ships continue to disappear under the radar on the VLCC front and the position lists both sides of Suez are becoming wanting in the front end, which should dictate a further uptick in rates. Capping that is lack of transparency. A tight supply/demand picture is one thing but not having a pricing reference is clouding proceedings. If owners really want to push this, they will have to start leaking a bit more information – real time – if not the assumption will be that it’s just more of the same. Rumours suggest there is still prompt interest from Fuj/Oman from the likes of Admic and KPC et al., and not much to choose from with WS 140 eastbound seemingly a “given” if nothing else comes to light. Petrobras keeps the flame burning from the Atlantic with almost daily cargo quotes Brazil/East and rates have inched up a couple of points north of the WS 115 mark. Sinkor is back in buying mood if we are to believe reports, the Maxim being the latest to be added, at a stonking price for an older lady ('11 built).

The Panamax market remains divided between a gradually improving Atlantic and a weakening Pacific. Atlantic sentiment has turned slightly more constructive, supported by better transatlantic activity, firmer fronthaul demand, and increasing
NCSA enquiry, although ample tonnage and wide bid-offer spreads continue to cap gains. In the Pacific, limited cargo volumes, a growing tonnage list, and cautious charterers are keeping pressure on rates, particularly on Indonesian business. While vessel tightness indicators still point to near-term softness, strong shipment volumes, supportive demand fundamentals, and a coal forward curve now moving into slight backwardation suggest the market could find better support as July approaches.
The Supramax market maintained a positive tone, supported by stronger enquiry in the Atlantic and improving activity across Asia. Market reported firmer sentiment in the US Gulf and South Atlantic, while growing backhaul demand in North Asia helped support overall vessel demand. The Handysize also strengthened, with tighter tonnage availability and balanced supply-demand conditions contributing to a firmer market environment. Activity remained steady across most regions, with the US Gulf and Asian markets showing the most encouraging signs of improvement.

### WEST
Spot fixing activity has been subdued in the West this week, except from one Asian major fixing a ship at around USD 250 HCvP for 3rd decade July via tender. Forward fixing out of the US Gulf remains limited, with just 8 spot fixtures recorded so far for
July loading (3/4/1 by decade). This is a marked slowdown compared to previous months and highlights the current lack of visible cargo flow. Meanwhile, prompt ship availability remains tight, with only two VLGCs available for loading up to 10th July, and just a handful of ships for 10-15 July window. This constrained availability suggests that the market retains upside potential and could quickly firm once more cargoes emerge.
