## Aframax
## Rates
The market has resumed its upward trajectory this week, building on the momentum seen at the end of last week. CPC/Mediterranean rates have broken through the WS 250 barrier, and TD20 looks set to move above WS 240. Tonnage availability, both in the West and among vessels committed via the Cape of Good Hope, remains thin, and charterers look set to face increasingly emboldened owners, particularly given that there are still cargoes to cover for the second decade in both the Atlantic and ex-
CPC.
North Sea rates have held relatively flat over the past week with last-done levels being repeated at WS 145. Activity has been healthy and a steady flow of ballasting has helped thin out the list, although positions are gradually pushing into end/early fixing windows. We expect the traditional TA players to continue heading West, with sentiment leaning towards a firmer US market, in turn lending some support to TD7 going forward.
The Mediterranean market has had a softer week, with charterers capitalising on a long tonnage list to push rates lower, while some deals have been concluded quietly under the radar. Despite overall healthy activity, availability has remained too high to be fully absorbed, keeping downward pressure on rates. A handful of prompt vessels remain, and the balance looks weak as we settling into July stems. Owners, now, may look to resist further erosion of current levels, although without a fresh wave of stems it is difficult to see a meaningful recovery.

The Panamax market has remained uneven this week, with the Atlantic showing more life as fresh inquiry improved across both fronthaul and transatlantic trades. However, direction remains divided and the tonnage list looks less tight than last week. Owners are still managing to hold firmer ideas in certain pockets where prompt tonnage is tighter, but the wider basin feels broadly balanced rather than outright firm. The
Pacific remains under pressure, with supply still outweighing demand despite better activity and some fresh cargo appearing, while owners are showing more resistance, particularly on longer-duration business and better-spec units. Overall sentiment remains soft, with the market continuing to ease, though the pace of decline appears to be slowing.
On Supramax, market lost some momentum after recent gains, with sentiment becoming more cautious across both basins. In the Atlantic, owners largely maintained a firm stance, although activity slowed and the market appeared increasingly positional. Elsewhere, the region seemed to be taking a natural breather following a stronger run, while arbitrage opportunities became less prominent. In
Asia, limited fresh enquiry and wider bid-offer gaps weighed on sentiment, resulting in a quieter and more subdued environment. Overall, the sector remains supported by underlying optimism in the Atlantic, but short-term direction appears less clear as owners and charterers alike adopt a wait-and-see approach.
