# Fearnleys Weekly Report - Week 26, 2026

**Week 26 - June 24, 2026**

## 01 Tankers

### VLCC

More of the same in the VLCC market, lots of under the radar fixing, and with all this “need to know only", very few do know, although the different camps are trying their best to influence direction of travel by divulging snippets or half truths if you like. Loading inside MEG now pays everything from WS 400 to almost WS 900 depending on destination and who you choose to listen to, but tracking still suggests volumes going through the Strait of Hormuz are limited and mostly existing inventories. A bit of Fuj-MAF and Yanbu continue to keep the flame burning and being mostly prompt liftings underpin the current rate level. All in all, the map does not fit the terrain at the moment, but that isn’t a first in our industry. As for the Atlantic, thank the Lord for Brazil continuing to pump and export, and to a degree compensating lack of appetite for WTI and West African grades in Asia.

### Suezmax

The market has resumed its upward trajectory this week, building on the momentum seen at the end of last week. CPC/Mediterranean rates have broken through the WS 250 barrier, and TD20 looks set to move above WS 240. Tonnage availability, both in the West and among vessels committed via the Cape of Good Hope, remains thin, and charterers look set to face increasingly emboldened owners, particularly given that there are still cargoes to cover for the second decade in both the Atlantic and ex- CPC.

### Aframax

North Sea rates have held relatively flat over the past week with last-done levels being repeated at WS 145. Activity has been healthy and a steady flow of ballasting has helped thin out the list, although positions are gradually pushing into end/early fixing windows. We expect the traditional TA players to continue heading West, with sentiment leaning towards a firmer US market, in turn lending some support to TD7 going forward.

The Mediterranean market has had a softer week, with charterers capitalising on a long tonnage list to push rates lower, while some deals have been concluded quietly under the radar. Despite overall healthy activity, availability has remained too high to be fully absorbed, keeping downward pressure on rates. A handful of prompt vessels remain, and the balance looks weak as we settling into July stems. Owners, now, may look to resist further erosion of current levels, although without a fresh wave of stems it is difficult to see a meaningful recovery.

## 02 Dry Bulk

### Capesize

This week on Capesize we have seen flat levels across the pacific earlier in the week with marginal decline today on C5. Physical fixtures for C5 West Australia/China have seen last done levels in the USD 11.25 range for early July dates and USD 11.75 for mid July. Earlier week fixtures were hovering between mid USD 11s-11.75 range for July dates. On C3 Brazil/China, a more active on fronthauls from Brazil, with active fixing yesterday. Latest fixtures today indicate levels hovering in the USD 31s with the latest stem fixed for late July dates. Earlier week fixtures were seen between mid USD 31s-33s, however Baltic today is down again slightly. In the North, tonnage demand has remained healthy with support through July dates, though transatlantic levels remained flat over the last week. West Africa cargoes have been an attractive substitute for limited fronthauls in the north. Recent fixtures ex West Africa are shown in mid USD 31 levels, with last being fixed at USD 31.5 from West Africa to China. Daily earning spreads for C3 vs C5 show stronger earning potential on C3 Brazil/China, with the latest spread around USD 8671 per day.

### Panamax

The Panamax market has remained uneven this week, with the Atlantic showing more life as fresh inquiry improved across both fronthaul and transatlantic trades. However, direction remains divided and the tonnage list looks less tight than last week. Owners are still managing to hold firmer ideas in certain pockets where prompt tonnage is tighter, but the wider basin feels broadly balanced rather than outright firm. The Pacific remains under pressure, with supply still outweighing demand despite better activity and some fresh cargo appearing, while owners are showing more resistance, particularly on longer-duration business and better-spec units. Overall sentiment remains soft, with the market continuing to ease, though the pace of decline appears to be slowing.

### Supramax

On Supramax, market lost some momentum after recent gains, with sentiment becoming more cautious across both basins. In the Atlantic, owners largely maintained a firm stance, although activity slowed and the market appeared increasingly positional. Elsewhere, the region seemed to be taking a natural breather following a stronger run, while arbitrage opportunities became less prominent. In Asia, limited fresh enquiry and wider bid-offer gaps weighed on sentiment, resulting in a quieter and more subdued environment. Overall, the sector remains supported by underlying optimism in the Atlantic, but short-term direction appears less clear as owners and charterers alike adopt a wait-and-see approach.

The Handy market continued to display a steady-to-firm tone overall. Atlantic regions, particularly the South Atlantic and US Gulf, remained supported by healthy demand and positive sentiment, helping maintain a constructive outlook. The Continent and Mediterranean saw more limited activity, with conditions broadly unchanged. In Asia, a balanced supply-demand picture and consistent cargo flow helped keep the market stable, with charterers generally maintaining a positive approach. Overall, the sector remains on a solid footing, supported by steady fundamentals and resilient sentiment across most regions.

## 03 Gas

### Chartering

Subdued activity in the VLGC market so far this week. The vessel balance in USG/USEC remains largely unchanged, however, a narrowing arbitrage has halted any new activity. In the East, we have started to see more enquires ex MEG appear following a handful of deals fixed STS Sohar week prior, but so far nothing reported fixed.
