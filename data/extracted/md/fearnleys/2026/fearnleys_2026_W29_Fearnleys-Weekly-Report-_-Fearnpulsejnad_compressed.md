## 01 Tankers

### VLCC

The market never sleeps of course, and ships are being picked off quietly, but the sense is nevertheless that things have slowed down as hostilities have worsen in the Middle East. President Trump reimposed a naval blockade on all Iranian ports and Tehran launched strikes on US infrastructure in the region. Early today, the US began a fresh round of strikes to continue degrading Iranian capabilities used to attack commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz and any further escalation involving the Strait of Hormuz or additional sanctions on Iranian exports could quickly tighten market sentiment and add further risk premiums. Iran's army also said that it had launched drone attacks against US positions at Jordan's Azraq base and targeted weapons and storage facilities in Bahrain and Kuwait. War risk insurance is back in the melting pot, to the extent it is available, and with that rates reported are not necessarily comparable like for like. The devil is in the detail as the saying goes. Fujairah and Oman are now back as priority number one in addition to Yanbu of course, where possible additional risk is added as the Houthis have started saber-rattling again. Although the Atlantic has seen a bit more action as far eastern refineries seek alternative supply the market in general is left searching for direction.

### Suezmax

The mystery list is piling up again, much like last week. This implies that the majority of rates have been in and around last-done levels. Despite the eased supply at the week's outset, healthy demand has counteracted this and kept the market in equilibrium. The list has now tightened slightly but unless charterers have very prompt dates in hand, owners on the front end are expected to remain pragmatic, as there are a reasonable number of vessels currently on berth in the West, as well as tonnage arriving from the East for the forward window. In isolation, Suezmaxes appear steady. However, there are reports that East-bound enquiries have reignited in the Atlantic in response to heightened tensions in the Middle East, while the Aframaxes are firming in the West - this is bringing Suezmaxes closer to being competitive, albeit not quite there yet.

### Aframax

#### North Sea

All of the western Aframax markets are active and firm putting upward pressure on rates. North Sea rates have jumped 30+ points so far this week and the pressure looks likely to remain on charterers for the balance of the month stems. The list has also been depleted with early tonnage heading out of the area both to US and Mediterranean with owners looking to lock in longer voyages at high daily returns. Next natural North Sea stems now in the 28-30 window.

#### Mediterranean

Who would have said that TD19 will be printed at over WS 300 in the middle of summer. Expensive Suezmaxes, fuel inquiries and a bit of forward fixing have pushed rates probably at all-time high for the month of July. Charterer are trying to find viable solutions while owners from surrounding areas are ready to ballast into the Mediterranean with the dates pushed slowly even to August.
