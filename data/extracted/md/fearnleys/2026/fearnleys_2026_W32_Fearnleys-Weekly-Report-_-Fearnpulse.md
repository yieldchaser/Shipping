# Fearnleys Weekly Report - Week 32, 2026

**Week 32 - August 5, 2026**

source: `corpus/01-brokers/fearnleys/2026/fearnleys_2026_W32_Fearnleys-Weekly-Report-_-Fearnpulse.pdf`  |  pages: 19

## 01 Tankers

### VLCC

Geopolitical tensions continue to fuel the volatility in rates. Houthi attacks are now starting to restrict movements and transits into the Red Sea. Owners willing to transit are able to attract healthy premiums over the standard Fuj/East run. USG barrels were a flash in the pan last week with not many ships actually getting fully fixed.

### Suezmax

After yesterday's downward correction it comes as no surprise that we have a lengthy mystery list this morning, along with more enquiries on the sheet, given the overall fixing hiatus from charterers the week prior. The uptick in activity will help to stabilise sentiment, however, there are still 15+ ‘natural’ vessels capable of making both Guyana and West Africa by 20th August. Owners should remain pragmatic in fixing their vessels away, particularly as the flow of tonnage remains consistent heading into the third decade.

### Aframax

#### North Sea

Market has quietly been covering the 1st half of the month with freight levels remaining relatively steady. Ballasting has slowed with softer surrounding markets but is still attractive rather than accepting waiting time. The Suezmax market has softened which may rekindle charterers' appetite for larger vessels with Aframax activity potentially coming under pressure. Market looks like it will remain steady/soft through mid month unless we see a jump in activity.

#### Mediterranean

Very busy Mediterranean market after the corrections of last week. Rates have found a floor and are hovering around last-done levels, but the position list is slowly becoming thinner. Volumes are remaining high as Syria and Sidi are pumping additional volumes at the moment keeping the market interesting.

## 02 Dry Bulk

### Capesize

The Capesize market began the week on a solid footing and has continued to strengthen throughout the week setting a positive trajectory. South Atlantic and West Africa continue to underpin improvements on C3 and healthy demand also pushed rates on C5 West Australia/China. In the Atlantic, the charge was led again this week on C3 Brazil/China with a flurry of fixtures in the low-mid USD 35s yesterday and today we see owners bidding in the mid USD 35s up to USD 36 for Brazil and West Africa stems for first half September laycans. In the Pacific, some ballasters are forced to delay as Typhoon Dolphin brings severe weather off the coast of China. C5 West Australia/Qingdao shows good support with four new fixtures from one miner today as the Baltic jumps by 6.9%. Refreshed offers for late August laycans are between USD 16.5-17 and one fixture was reported at low to mid USD 16s. First half September stems see offers ease to high USD 15 levels.

### Panamax

The Panamax market firmed steadily through the week, with momentum broadening across both basins and owners increasingly able to hold their ground. In the Atlantic, the fronthaul grain trade remained the principal driver – a healthy flow of South American and US Gulf cargoes against a tightening tonnage list kept sentiment constructive and lifted end-month levels, even against continued charterer resistance. The Pacific firmed in tandem, underpinned by tight prompt tonnage, a steadier stream of North Pacific grain alongside Australian and Indonesian coal, and some weather-related disruption that lent owners additional confidence. Period interest was active and the bid-offer gap narrowed over the week, though owners continued to price forward cover with a firm hand and a modest premium on shorter durations. With cargo replenishment healthy in both basins and the forward curve holding a shallow backwardation, we enter next week on a constructive footing.

### Supramax

The Supramax and Handymax markets remained relatively stable this week. The US Gulf continued to see the highest activity, while the Continent/Mediterranean remained balanced despite limited fresh enquiry. South America softened slightly as cargo volumes eased, and Asia remained quiet with rates edging lower. Overall, the market remains steady, although fresh cargo demand will be needed for further improvement.

## 03 Gas

### Chartering

One fixture concluded in the West this week at USD 280 pmt H/C, broadly in line with last week's levels. Owners' and charterers' ideas remain far apart, with the geopolitical picture still uncertain and capable of moving the market meaningfully in either direction once there is more clarity from Iran/US.

On the position list, one ship remains open end-August, with 6 first-decade, 5 second-decade, and 6 last-decade September. We enter the September fixing window with 5 fixtures done so far, all first-decade.
