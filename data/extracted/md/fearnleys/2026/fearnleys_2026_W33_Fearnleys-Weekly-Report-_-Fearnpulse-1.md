# Fearnleys Weekly Report - Week 33, 2026

**Week 33 - August 12, 2026**

## 01 Tankers

### VLCC

There’s no lack of VLCC activity judging from a long list of ships reported fixed or on subs. Details of which are few and far in between, these days motivated further not wanting to advertise to hostile elements where and when the ship in question will be appearing in the wider Middle East. This makes navigating the market even more difficult, although if there were deals done considerable different from “last done” levels I am sure it would have been alluded to. There is huge money to be had for the risk takers out there, of which there are not too many for the top prize inside MEG load. But Fujairah-Oman load still pays a decent premium and Yanbu/East even more as the Houthis continue to attack Saudi controlled ships and installations – although third party ships/owners have not yet been targeted. The MEG position list is balanced, albeit littered with oil company and trader relets so what is really available, or not, remains to be tested. With a solution of the US-Iran conflict looking increasingly further away, and soaring refinery margins, logic dictates Atlantic eastbound interest will increase going forward, albeit as of now several ships ballasting west on spec has kept a lid on rates.

### Suezmax

Activity continues to build across August and September dates in the West. While vessel availability remains reasonable on paper, the tonnage to cargo ratio continues to tighten, strengthening owners’ leverage and limiting their willingness to move first. In addition, September CPC stems are expected to be released imminently. With rates reaching astronomical levels, owners capable of trading in the Black Sea are likely to prioritise CPC cargoes over adjacent markets.

### Aframax

#### North Sea

The stems through the 2nd decade have been depleted for Aframaxes with some Suezmax activity and a few relets in the mix. With a limited amount of fixtures the market has steadily fallen over the last week by around 20 points. US markets are strengthening and pulling tonnage out of the North Sea, so we may have started to bottom out, and heading into the 3rd decade date wise we could start to see some pinch points which will see rates bounce.

#### Mediterranean

A steady week activity wise so far but rates have been eroded. Natural window pushing into 3rd decade with a list that has been trimmed but still looks relatively healthy for charterers. With the issues in the Black Sea, tonnage that wouldn’t normally look X-Mediterranean is recalibrating. Early West Mediterranean positions are looking at turning right and heading for the land of opportunity across the pond.

## 02 Dry Bulk

### Capesize

This week in the Capesize market we see the lowest tonnage congestion in China following Typhoon Dolphin. The typhoon hit the coast of China later last week and has since weakened, moving inland. Ballasters heading for Brazil on C3 (Brazil/China) were welcomed to a fresh injection of cargoes this week ex Brazil and West Africa. Last night fixes concluded in the USD 35-35.5 range, with owners offering USD 36.5 and traders bidding USD 35 for C3 mid-late September dates. Today rates are flat/slightly down with Baltic printing USD 35.93 and latest offers in low USD 36 range. Pacific C5 ex West Australia/China saw significant activity yesterday in the basin with three majors in the market. One fixture was done at USD 15.5 for late August dates and an East Australian stem fixed in the mid-high USD 31s for early September. Today the Pacific has also seen a slight dip on paper with Baltic down 2% today. Latest offers are in the low mid USD 14s for late August and early September. Last fixture concluded a late August Dampier stem at USD 14.25.

### Panamax

The Panamax market has held a constructive tone through the week, with the index grinding higher after a brief pause mid-week and both basins now pointing in the same direction. The Atlantic remains led by the fronthaul, where steady South American and Black Sea grain programmes together with a thinner list of prompt North Atlantic tonnage have allowed owners to hold their ideas and to resist on shorter transatlantic business as well. In the Pacific, sentiment has improved on the back of a fuller NoPac grain order book and firmer Australian coal demand into North Asia, with Indonesian volumes providing a steady if less dynamic floor; the gap between available tonnage and cargo requirement is narrowing and charterers are having to work harder to cover. Period enquiry is genuinely active across both Kamsarmax and Panamax, with a clear preference for index-linked structures, although owners' ideas continue to sit above where charterers are prepared to bid. We expect the market to remain sideways-to-firmer into next week, with the fronthaul retaining a healthy premium over backhaul and tonnage supply the main variable to watch.

### Supramax

The Supramax market began the week on a subdued note, with limited fresh activity and sentiment generally steady across most regions. The Atlantic showed signs of increased enquiry, particularly in the Mediterranean, although a widening gap between bids and offers and softer nearby positions suggested some caution. The Continent remained largely unchanged, while Asian markets were mostly flat, with backhaul activity correcting and regional demand remaining muted.

Handysize conditions were similarly quiet, with sentiment remaining cautious in both basins. Europe faced pressure from an increase in available tonnage and limited fresh cargoes, while the USG continued to struggle with weak demand and ample vessel supply. The South Atlantic showed early signs of improvement due to a more balanced cargo-to-tonnage ratio. In Asia, demand remained steady but insufficient to materially tighten the market, resulting in an overall soft but stable tone.

## 03 Gas

### Chartering

Activity picked up this week, with 4 fixtures reported yesterday following a wave of fresh cargoes into the market. Rates firmed on limited positions between 5-15 September, with the latest subfixtures on HCvP basis reported in the low-mid USD 280s. The list remains heavily relet-dependent and tight in the first decade: 2 ships first, 5 second, 7 third.
