# Fearnleys Weekly Report - Week 42, 2026

**Week 42 - October 14, 2026**

## 01 Tankers

### VLCC

The VLCC market has gone a tad quiet on the surface but there is more going on than meets the eye. Earlier on in the week Saudi giant Bahri took a trio from Yanbu paying top dollar. Arguably the immediate pressure has eased from Fujairah-GOO, partly as a result of Iran having ramped up attacks in the Strait of Hormuz but also due to a logistical backlog sorting out already concluded deals. Elsewhere Petrobras continues to be active having taken 3 ships so far this week for Brazil/East runs, chipping off a bit on last done for every successive fixture but still yielding euphoric returns close to USD 800k/day. Suezmax rates close to the WS 1000 mark West Africa/East continues to be a carrot for economy of scale and more ships will likely ballast west to seek their fortune in lieu of a slower pace from the Middle East. We may see a further downward correction, but it will likely be just that: a correction – and from historical highs.

### Suezmax

Off-market activity continues, with cargoes from CPC and the Atlantic steadily absorbing tonnage without generating any headlines. Despite vessel supply remaining historically tight, dates continue to push beyond three weeks, which has allowed charterers to stifle owners' momentum, taking vessels that are sailing outside 5 days. The Fujairah market remains quiet, which has led multiple owners in the East to turn their attention towards the Atlantic and a similar trend on VLCC provides supply side relief via COGH on both segments. We anticipate significantly lower demand for Suezmaxes ex West Africa month-on-month, having hit a historic high of over ~84MBBLS in October. Expect some downward pressure around the corner.

### Aframax

#### North Sea

A slow week so far with what looks like programming of tonnage taking out stems for the balance of the 2nd decade and pushing into the 3rd. There is a huge amount of tonnage coming from the US in October, but we would expect a lot of that to head straight back rather than compete for North Sea business. So far this month we have seen less month on month ballast transatlantic, but expect this to pick up in the 2nd half of the month, the list will be kept thinner in the North Sea with vessels leaving to the Mediterranean and the US.

#### Mediterranean

Like the North Sea, Mediterranean activity has been muted so far this week, but non compromised owners are still able to pick favourable cargoes and are holding back on just rushing in to offer, which is still making it tricky for charterers.

## 02 Dry Bulk

### Capesize

This week, continued weakness in Capesize has seen markets slip further in both Pacific and Atlantic regions. The Pacific remained under pressure with minimal miner participation and few tenders. Softening sentiment and quiet trading saw a further paper slip today on C5 West Australia/Qingdao of 7.16% with Baltic printing USD 12.05. With some Pacific bidding this morning, today's sharpest offers were in the mid-high USD 12s, and rumors of a fixture at USD 12.1 were reported earlier in the day for early November. Similar softer market levels have also been seen in the Atlantic, with C3 Brazil/China with Baltic printing USD 31.61 today and a fixture reported at USD 31.5 for a 1H November laycan, and mid-late November see bids in the mid-low USD 33s. Transatlantic in addition has seen very few fresh stems today and Baltic C8 fell by 12.75%. Although forward enquiry for mid and late November dates is emerging, the current imbalance between supply and demand continues to weigh on sentiment, as long tonnage lists keep near term outlook cautious.

### Panamax

The Panamax market has traded broadly sideways this week, with Atlantic weakness offsetting firmer Pacific fixtures. In the Atlantic, grain and mineral demand has not kept pace with the expanding tonnage list, while additional ballasters for November ECSA loading are weighing on fronthaul rates. The Pacific remains well supported by Aussie coal, NoPac grain requirements and improving Indo inquiry, with prompt, well-positioned tonnage commanding premiums. Period interest continues, although weaker FFAs suggest greater caution on longer-term commitments. Our shipment and vessel-tightness indicators favor a broadly sideways near-term outlook, with Pacific strength unlikely, on its own, to support a sustained recovery across both basins.

### Supramax

The Supramax market saw improving sentiment overall, with the USG strengthening on fronthaul demand, while the Continent remained firm and the Mediterranean showed signs of recovery. ECSA softened slightly, although the outlook remains relatively stable. In Asia, NOPAC and backhaul demand continued to support rates, with Indo activity improving for the usual indo x india/Bangladesh flows. Period interest remained evident, and the overall market tone was positive.

The Handy market remained mixed, with the Continent and Mediterranean broadly stable amid balanced fundamentals. The South Atlantic continued to strengthen, supported by positive sentiment and improving rates, while the USG remained under pressure from ample tonnage and limited fresh demand. Asian activity was relatively quiet, although shorter tonnage lists and healthy enquiry levels offered some support. Overall, positional factors continued to drive regional market differences.

## 03 Gas

### Chartering

After last week's flurry of fixtures concluded in the USD 340-350/t-plus range, market activity has started the week on a more measured footing. A Southeast Asian oil major entered the market with a second-half November cargo tender, while five trader relets were circulated and subsequently fixed at around the USD 313/t level.

Despite freight rates easing by approximately 5% from their recent highs, the VLGC market remains exceptionally strong. The BLPG3 Houston-Chiba index is currently assessed at USD 335/t, supporting round-voyage spot earnings well in excess of USD 200,000 per day for a modern conventionally fuelled VLGC. This highlights the underlying strength of the market, with vessel availability remaining tight and sentiment broadly constructive.

In the East, activity remains relatively subdued. A couple of STS FOB tenders ex Sohar and Vadinar for mid-October loading are currently being marketed, although their outcomes remain uncertain. Market participants continue to watch these cargoes closely, as they are expected to provide valuable price discovery and a clearer indication of near-term market direction.
