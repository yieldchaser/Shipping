## Tankers
## Comments
## VLCC
Despite the threat of OPEC+ cuts, the MEG volume remains healthy, at least for the 1-10 window of May. We are already up over 50 deals with the 10-20 window now starting to be actively worked. However, the 2nd decade is usually a little quieter and threats of these cuts weigh heavy on owners’ minds. Coupled with a market shrouded in mystery, as ships with no details fix away and owners’ relief as subs lift at higher numbers. WS 60 TD3C is all the rage at the time of writing, but numbers in the WS 50’s are imminent, if not done at the time of this going to press. Little support from the West, as VL’s step on Smax toes and USG/Ningbo falling down through the USD 8m levels.
A positive note, 1-year TC on a eco scrubber at USD 57/day fully fixed, shows that even charterers feel there is light at the end of the tunnel for owners.
## Suezmax
Last week saw the Atlantic experience a high volume of off-market fixing that looked, at one stage at least, like there could be an impending rally. That scenario has failed to play out and with the Atlantic market recycling ships on shorter voyages and subsequently replenishing the list quicker than the market had hoped for, the outlook is soft. TD20 is now focused on second decade May dates with rates struggling to stay in the WS 90's whilst the much anticipated cavalry in the US Gulf has not arrived with a flat outlook across the pond. In the East, rates took a further tumble when a MEG/Brunei run traded down to 130KT x WS 110 which will now attract VLCC's, with the fray. On the back of this, TD23 might find itself facing WS 50's.
## Aframax
In the NSea, rates bounced back in the end of last week, and so far they have remained steady. Relets are dominating a well-balanced list and that prevents owners from pushing higher while at the same time there is for a downward potential if charterers decide to use their own tonnage aggressively. Moving forward we expect rates to move sideways for now before we see laycans for May being worked actively.
Med/BSea rates moved sideways last week as activity was moderate with enough vessels in position. Owners were trying to push for higher on every fixture, however there was little room for push. Heading into next week we might see rates being under upward pressure as tonnage list becomes tighter.

## Capesize
Winter doldrums finally passed, fundamental industries back in swing, more positives than negatives. Last 5 trading days at times "rollercoasting", nevertheless average daily earnings up 28 pct w-o-w to come in at just short of USD 18k. Major iron ore routes Brazil/China and West Australia/China showing solid volumes, with consequential value of long China-Brazil-China rounds climbing almost 20 pct, short Pacific rounds more than 40 pct. Significant assistance from coal transactions in both basins, taking value of transatlantic trading up almost 50 pct to well above USD 18k/day. Healthy period demand as spot and FFA's support levels worth considering, last conclusions exemplified by vintage 177,000 dwt Far East early May said done for more than 12 months at around
USD 17k.

Tonnage was beginning to build in most areas, pitched against mostly inactive demand owners’ resilience was being tested to the full. The trend across all markets remain positive despite some areas such as USG and North Atlantic could see softer rates as the number of ships is greater than the number of new requirements. Indian Ocean pushed charterers to pay higher levels. A Supra open WC India was fixed at USD 18,000 pd for EC
India range, while Ultramax opened WC India was fixed around USD 20,000 pd. The market in the Pacific basin remained sideways with a potential upside due to coal imports to China on seasonal demand.

Chartering
### EAST
East activity is remaining on the low side compared to the more active West market. Freight seems to be finding some support in the activity found West of Suez helping freight in the East from falling further despite the relative lack of cargoes, so far. Some ships on the East list will likely go West, but for now the East positions remain well populated. Last done concluded at high 70s RT/C.
### WEST
At the time of writing, we have seen 7 spot fixtures in June (6 first decade and 1 second decade) ex USG/USEC while May looks to end at 26-27 spot as we currently see only one open vessel left in the very end of the month.
Looking at the position list, it is looking relatively tight as we only see a small handful of workable vessels left in 1st decade June and 4-5 vessels for the first five days of the 2nd decade. Last done fixture out of the USG concluded at mid 130s H/C and high 70s H/F.
