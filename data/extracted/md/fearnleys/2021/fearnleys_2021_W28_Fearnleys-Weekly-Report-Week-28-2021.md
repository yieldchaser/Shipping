Fearnleys Weekly Report
Week 28 - July 14, 2021
## Tankers
## Comments
## VLCC
Another week and more of the same. The MEG cargo count creeping up to the 110 level for the month of July, however, we are now heading into the twilight zone prior to the major August stem release (likely late this week, early next). Therefore, limited activity expected. At present, just an Indian cargo and a Vietnamese system cargo for early August dates and rates ﬂattering to deceive, moving nowhere at the speed of light in the MEG. The tried and trusted tactic of underground ﬁxing continues in charterers favor.
However, a little more movement in the Atlantic, with the slightest of upticks (a point in Wafr, albeit a Ghana load) and ws35 on an ex Brazil cargo is promising. With bunker prices creeping up (to USD 550 in Spore), there is little downside to the market, but that’s mainly because there is nowhere lower to go.
## Suezmax
The market continues its pitiably mundane run with no obvious signs of abating, at least not in the ultra short term. The East market is simply devoid of adequate cargo enquiry which means that a gushing ﬂow of ballasters continue to cap and/or dilute rates in both the Atlantic and Mediterranean basins. With some Asian countries reexporting excess crude inventories, there are serious questions posed about when pre-pandemic demand will return to a region that is in almost full lockdown. Td20 is ﬂirting with the ws50 mark but limited upside whilst Wafr/East is pinned down in the low/mid ws50's. As we advised last week, Td23 was looking "comparatively» overvalued and now trades mid ws20's with possible further downside.
## Aframax
It was a rather depressing week for Aframax owners trading in the NSea/Baltic as rates declined further resulting in negative returns for owners on speciﬁc voyages. Some of them decided to ballast out of the area seeking alternative employment. However, this didn’t help the market as the tonnage list still got enough vessels to cover for the rest of July dates. Patience is the key at the moment with the market keep struggling at bottom levels. In the Med/Blsea the activity level has unfortunately not been suﬃcient enough to support the brief optimism seen last week, with levels taking a slight dip this week. Surrounding markets not oﬀering all to many attractive alternatives either. We foresee another week in the summer duldrums with freight rates remaining stable/soft.

## Dry Bulk
## Comments
## Capesize
Rates for the big ships not quite living up to expectations, although at very healthy levels and fair volumes. Average daily earnings pretty much unchanged w-o-w, standing at usd 29k for standard 180000 dwt units. Iron ore remains main driver. Volume shift from
Australia to Brazil, as the china-brazil-china round voyage route is improving to almost close the gap and par with paciﬁc rounds at around usd 26k/day – providing hope and promise for the weeks to come. General outlook bright, both due waust/china volumes expected to pick up after a slow period, but even more so as major miners very busy picking ships for the tonne/mile-crucial
Brazil/Far East trade. Commodity prices indicate further strong demand. Period activity moderate, with recent ﬁxtures limited to modern standard units concluded on index-basis.
## Panamax
It would seem the “summer market” has hit both hemispheres the last week, both on physical and FFA. The slight ﬂow of fresh requirements in the North Atlantic is also seeing competition from larger vessels cutting size, hence the positive eﬀect of the fresh requirements seem to be minimal. In general it would appear that there is simply less demand in both the Atlantic and Paciﬁc basins at the moment. As a consequence of this, and also the dive in FFA values, the period market has also put the brakes on, with very few vsls reported ﬁxed for period.
## Supramax
Supra slightly sliding this week. Limited fresh orders not contributing to the rates, although Atlantic still looked more balanced than
Asia, where rates were more under pressure.
Supra from Cont via Ust Luga to USG was ﬁxed at 32000 USD . Clinker run on Supramax deliver at Gibraltar for trip via South Spain to West Africa covered at usd 39000 . Med region still balanced mostly due to a seasonal support with grains from Black Sea.
Fhauls from Black Sea to Far East was discussed tick over 50 000 USD. Less exciting number from the East where Supra open
Japan via Australia back to Singapore-Japan range was concluded at 28000 USD. Vsl open Chittagong to China heared to ﬁx 38- 39000 usd region. Ultra with nickel ore via Pihilippines to China performed at 31000 USD. From WCI to China it was ﬁxed at 40000
USD . Usual steel runs from China to USG payed usd 27000 USD.

## Gas
Chartering
### EAST
VLGC ﬁxing East of Suez has picked up its pace this week with more cargoes quoted out of MEG and Australia. Still with a hefty premium in the West a good amount of ships is sailing towards this direction or planning to do so - helping the supply of available tonnage to decrease. On the back of both more activity and vessels disappearing out of the region we expect the bottom has been reached for now in the East.
### WEST
Activity wise so far this week US has been lagging a bit behind the East – with freight now hovering around 80 pmt Houston/Chiba, continued diﬃcult arb economics and delays in both North and South bound in the Panama Canal some Charterers are sitting on the fence to see the direction of the market before making their next moves.
