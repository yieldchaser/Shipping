Fearnleys Weekly Report
Week 40 - October 06, 2021
## Tankers
## Comments
## VLCC
A busy week on the whole, but VLCC rates very much like an arm wrestle, edging to the owners’ side bit by bit as charterers ﬁght for every inch. Most rate increases driven by the busier Atlantic market, as we have drifted ﬁrmly north of USD 5m in the USG to
Ningbo and the latest West Africa deal able to squeeze a half point above last done to the WS 43 level. Similar in the MEG, as fractions of points are achieved on successive ﬁxtures. No major swing just yet however and charterers may try to keep things under the radar, or hold oﬀ to keep a lid on things, but with 85 or so cargoes for MEG, still plenty to come 3rd decade. Worth noting some Japanese and Indian power utilities have boosted operating rates at their oil-ﬁred units enabling them to limit the reliance on gas power generation and slow LNG-inventories.
## Suezmax
The Suezmax market looks to have found a ﬁrmer footing with most load zones experiencing solid chunks of enquiry for the ﬁrst time in well over a year. MEG enquiry, and most notably Basrah, looks to have gone into overdrive with Bot/West ﬁrming signiﬁcantly. Last done Td23 jumped ﬁve points to WS 35 with owners so bullish it would be unwise to bet against rates pushing on again. The key factor that has helped balance out supply and demand has been the Atlantic where signiﬁcant gains have been made in the Caribbean and West Africa with Td20 now comfortably in the low WS 60's and likely to add further weight. It now remains to be seen if NWE and the Mediterranean will have a further injection of Aframax/Suezmax interplay which could fuel further volatility. Outlook is ﬁrm in all regions.
## Aframax
The Nsea/Baltic market fulﬁlled the expectations of last week and continued ﬁrming up. Current window proved to be tighter than expected as charterers took out many ships quietly, and as a result there was a signiﬁcant improvement especially for the Baltic runs on the back of healthy activity on both crude and fuel oil. As all three Western markets are simultaneously improving for the ﬁrst time this year, we expect rates to remain steady with some more upward potential. The activity in the Med/Bsea has continued this week and rates have picked another 5-10 points. Suezmaxes taking out Afra-stems have not capped the market just yet. We are now in a health 3rd decade October ﬁxing window ex Bsea, and combined with the current cargo activity out of Libya, we expect the market to remain ﬁrm going into next week.

## Capesize
Another amazing week for the Capesize market. The average of all basket is up by 16% adding another USD 11,994 per day to the daily average, now at USD 86,870 per day. The massive push has come from an impressive increase in the Atlantic basin and backhaul segment. This is highly correlated with the massive backwardation in ﬀa curve encouraging owners to seek longer duration as the forward market is showing a lack of conﬁdent. That said there is also an increasing amount of charters, and cargo owners looking to cover forward freight exposure, hence period market being paid up.
## Panamax
With Golden Week in China and despite the rush in Capesize rates, this week has been very slow. In the Atlantic we see less demand and P1A has seen steady declines every day - currently priced at around USD 30k. In the eastern hemisphere rates are also slipping, although not to the same extent as in the Atlantic. Paciﬁc rounds are currently ﬁxing in the 36/37k level. The paper market is still strong and giving support to the period market. Kamsarmaxes are being ﬁxed at 32k with Far East delivery for 1-year trading.
## Supramax
Slow start to the week and limited fresh orders in light of Golden Week in China. Index losing ground from Asia routes, while in the
Atlantic USG making most of the gains. From the paper side, Supra FFAs joined the larger sizes in making decent gains, especially for Cal 22. From the Atlantic, the Mediterranean remained balanced where inter Med trips were covered at USD 40,000. Grains from the Black Sea to the Continent were ﬁxed at USD 39,000 and to the Far East around USD 52,000. Fertz from the Continent to the USG covered around USD 40,000. More push from the USG with several ﬁxtures this week. Coal run from Mississippi River to the West Med was concluded around USD 45,000. From similar position to ECSA, an Ultramax was paid USD 47,000 levels. Grain runs to China settled around USD 53,000. Little activity from the Paciﬁc Basin. Indo/China runs ﬁxed around USD 45,000. The
Paciﬁc RV close to USD 40,000. Ultra from WCI via MEG to Bangla with aggregates got ﬁxed around USD 51,000.

Chartering
### EAST
A couple of Indian cargos being discussed for last decade of October, together with two Australian cargos ﬁxed on subs for end
October, sums up an otherwise muted week in the East thus far. Public holidays in China and Korea have kept activity on the low, and most of the focus has been on the West where activity has picked up. Freight levels in the MEG seems to have stabilized around the mid USD 40s mark for now, and despite more ships being sent back West, we are still left with a variety of open ships from the main owners and traders in MEG from last decade of October into early November.
### WEST
As October positions are wiped out from the USG position lists, all eyes are now on ﬁrst half November freight. Low USD 80 was done on a handful of occasions during early November, however, the market is now established in the mid USD 80’s Houston/Chiba with even a couple of ﬁxtures just north of this level. Second decade November freight enquiries are increasing in frequency, and if owners’ get their way, we may see rates pointing north of mid USD 80’s.
