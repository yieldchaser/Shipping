## Tankers
## Comments
## VLCC
Step by step the market moves up, with MEG-East now reportedly at the WS 42.5 level at the time of writing. West Africa also holds last done at similar levels at WS 42 to test, attracting modern ballasters around the Cape. Likewise, prompter dates in the
USG/Caribs - rumours of USD 5m+ heading East. However, we are a little light on enquiry at present, and with Golden week in a few days, we will need a pre-holiday rush to keep pushing rates upwards, certainly in the MEG. And it is worth noting, as rates go up, charterers may have the tactic to let standards slip a bit. Suddenly an over 15-year vessel becomes workable, dry docks are not so oﬀensive, and remote or bunker sires are overlooked.
It is easy to get carried away. TCE's are still awful for owners, barely covering Opex, but the improvement is certainly welcome.
## Suezmax
Whilst there's a general consensus that the market will continue to improve, the pace of improvement remains pedestrian and dull.
The East Suezmax market is unfortunately more VLCC-sensitive than the West, and at the time of going to print, we are way oﬀ from seeing any meaningful interplay between the two segments . In the West, the main potential drivers are the highly nuanced regional Aframax markets that have recently been well into crossover territory, especially in the Mediterranean. This potentially holds Suezmax tonnage away from West Africa which could create more bottlenecks. Wafr/East is on subs at WS 60 showing positive market direction whilst MEG/East should challenge low WS 60's for a pure long East. Td23 is an enigma and will possibly hold at sub WS 30. A pre-Golden Week rush may yet occur!
## Aframax
While fundamentals remain the same in the Nsea and Baltic Aframax market, it is correct to say that it was more sentiment that pushed rates slightly higher this week. Going forward the tonnage list is looking shorter and as the October lifting program looks busier, owners will try to put some upward pressure on rates. The market is looking steady but ﬁrmer.
In the Med/Bsea we have seen a rush of cargoes enter the market this week with both cross-Med and ex-Bsea being active. Rates across the board have picked up 10 points or so, and the tonnage list is tighter than it has been in a long time. However, Suezmaxes are now coming into play, as well as Aframaxes open in the North, so it will be interesting to see how long owners in the Med can keep an upward pressure on rates.

## Capesize
Full speed ahead for the big ships as congestion keeps being a signiﬁcant factor supporting already very healthy fundamentals.
Spot scene hectic as Australian miners struggle to ﬁll early slots, particularly for iron ore. Average daily earnings up 25 pct w-o-w to come in at USD 75k, a level not seen for half a generation. Atlantic spot is close to becoming short of tonnage as number of ballasters is limited and hardly enough to satisfy needs of miners, power plants and steel mills - resultant fronthaul values up to
USD 100k/day, China-Brazil-China round up 30 pct to USD 65k, and pushing. Short-term outlook very bull, supported by ﬀa's.
Considerable period interest but considerable gap in bid/oﬀer for ﬁxed-price deals - whilst around 10 ships taken by major players during last 5 trading days for about 12 mos duration at indexed rates.
## Panamax
The ﬁrm market continued into this week with rates continuing to ﬁrm up in both hemispheres. However, this took a turn mid-week with tonnage building up in the North Atlantic. P1A is currently priced in the mid 30’s while Paciﬁc rounds are trading in region 36/37k levels. The period market has been fairly active with a Kamsarmax reported done at USD 31,500 for 10/12 mos.
## Supramax
The upward continue its rally also this week followed by big sizes, however not at the same pace. We can describe market as well balanced with some less activity due to upcoming Golden Week in China. USG market is improving and rates slowly moving up.
Supramax ﬁxing around USD 35k pd and Ultras able to reach USD 40k pd for Atlantic trade. For trips to East, owners are able to achieve close to USD 50k pd - MV Ym Advance (63,509/2019) del USEC 1/15 Oct trip grains rdl China USD 50k. Market in ECSA is stable with limited ﬁxing reports. Tess 58 was rumoured to ﬁx USD 43k pd for one trip to Med. The Continent and Med market is more active this week with improved demand for scrap and clinker orders surfacing. Trip from Continent to USG/ECSA reporting to ﬁx around USD 40k pd, while scrap paying premium around USD 42k pd. Far Eastern markets show resistance despite the pre- holiday mood, and the indexes are seeing only small gains. Supramax delivering North China ﬁxed at USD 34k pd for a trip via the
Russian Paciﬁc port to China, while Ultramax delivering South East Asia able to obtain USD 41,500 pd for a trip via Indonesia to
China. Australian trip paying low USD 40k pd on Ultramax and backhaul to the Atlantic around USD 35k pd.

## Gas
Chartering
### EAST
The East Baltic benchmark jumped up on the back of last done ex MEG at USD 44 Ras Tanura/Chiba which was concluded earlier this week. Seeing that the 'pure' owners are inclined to send their vessels in ballast towards the West in search for higher earnings, it may have a knock-on eﬀect on the MEG market. Of a vessel line-up consisting of about ten vessels (give or take) left open for the balance of October, about half of these are relets. The market is awaiting the release of the Saudi CP for November as well.
Something which normally, until it is out, reduces activity.
### WEST
In the western hemisphere, eyes are slowly but surely turning towards November enquiries and subsequently, what's in store. The typhoon currently passing the Paciﬁc Ocean seems not to have any severe impact to the shipping supply both in the MEG and in the USG. End October has during the course of the week seen a handful of relets appearing on the horizon, however, these vessels have yet to aﬀect the market signiﬁcantly and the last concluded Houston/Chiba rate was in the very low USD 80's oﬀ early
November dates. October has about six or seven vessels left and seeking employment opportunities and hopes are that after the
US stats are released today, there will be an increase in activity.
