Fearnleys Weekly Report
Week 36 - September 08, 2021
## Tankers
## Comments
## VLCC
The MEG market remains a frustrating hunting ground for owners, as the expectation of any improvement in rates remains on the horizon. Charterers able to source tonnage at last done levels in the low WS 30's MEG/East, and avoiding any rush to ﬁx. There remains much of the 3rd decade to cover however (25-30 cargoes?) and we already over 100 cargoes for September, so plenty of ﬁxing to be done in the short term. The Atlantic remains busy, but again, rates yet to move as ballasters content to escape a stagnant East market if possible. Some owners preferring to keep things short, as they realize that if you ﬁx long now, that is your
Q4. Much debate among market players as to when the much vaunted rate improvements will come, but we still a little early for that right now.
## Suezmax
There's a perception that Suezmax's always follow "big brothers" lead as soon as V's show any signs of life. Yes, if the maths permit it, we can see interaction, but the core issue for Suezmax right now is the East. Namely, its low enquiry volumes, therefore, ballasters continue to clip out Med and Wafr stems on a daily basis. A case in point being that the majority of vessels passing
Rondo now set a course directly for Wafr without even considering ballasting to deviation point Galle. The East list is so overloaded with unemployed ships that owners with larger ﬂeets aren't even looking to maintain ﬂeet balance. They are rolling the dice on
Western markets that still have decent enquiry levels. Unfortunately, this strategy becomes a law of diminishing returns and will continue to cannibalize Atlantic basin rates.
## Aframax
The Nsea/Baltic Aframax market experienced yet another disappointing week. The combination of an abundance of available tonnage coupled with somewhat reduced activity, in the current ﬁxing window, are keeping rates at bottom levels. Earnings are very close to nothing or even negative for some owners trading in this area. Unless, owners ﬁnd better alternatives elsewhere, we expect the next week to remain quite similar to the previous. The Med/Bsea market also continued trading sideways at bottom levels this week. Bad weather and potential disruptions in Libya have created a little bit of excitement, however these elements have yet to develop into ﬁrmer rates. A few owners have an optimistic eye on the cargo program out of Bsea for October, so it will be interesting to see how healthy this program is once revealed.

## Capesize
Rates are still coming oﬀ, but activity is decent. Various c5 ﬁxtures in the mid 12s and on c3, Vale have been ﬁxing a handful of ships this week just sub USD 30 pmt. Levels are strong after all, and another improvement is expected.
## Panamax
This week we have experienced a weaker sentiment in both hemispheres. The Atlantic demand has declined and tonnage building up. We do however see some good rates achieved for the shorter Baltic rounds and also out of Black Sea.
The P1A is currently yielding mid 20k’s. In the Paciﬁc, we see rates also easing but not to the same extent as in the Atlantic. Paciﬁc rounds are being concluded in the low 30’s.
## Supramax
It’s questionable whether Supras will continue to fall over the next weeks notwithstanding downwards pressure from the larger segments, as there are several factors lending strong support such as vessel demand from the biggest load countries in Asia have rebounded strongly since the bottom in early August and activity in the South Atlantic are almost back to this year’s highs, in addition lobal congestion is persistent around all-time highs.
From the Atlantic, USG building up tonnage due to recent hurricane, where trips to Far East were discussed around USD 40,000 and around USD 29,000 for trips back to Cont. From ECSA to Med, Supra was ﬁxed around USD 37,000. From the Paciﬁc basin nickel ore runs from Indonesia to China ﬁxed around USD 35,000.
Vsl open in Taiwan for trip via Indonesia redel WCI covered at USD 34,000. Some fertz runs via MEG to WCI closed around USD 50,000 dop Mesaieed. Steel runs via SAFR to Far East with vsl open in Bangladesh ﬁxed around USD 34,000.

## Gas
Chartering
### EAST
Market East of Suez currently ﬁnds itself in a bit of a waiting mode prior to the next round of acceptances. Those with vessels on
September dates in the East are hoping for some late running vessels or some last-minute Indian cargoes to avoid too much waiting time on their vessels. Any remaining potential September spot cargo in MEG would be much sought after by those with vessels open in this window hence competitive freight should be on the table.
### WEST
Activity is halting in the West VLGC market. Not only is there a mismatch between bids and oﬀers in the VLGC freight market just now, but there is an inherent lack of willingness to pursue what would make sense to conclude, simply due to what the arbitrage allows and last done ﬁxture ex USG is too far apart. Freight and product will at some point ﬁnd its feet again, but it seems time being that freight has to give. There are three vessels still left for the balance of September and ﬁrst half October counts about ﬁfteen or so VLGCs, meaning that currently, it is somewhat balanced and in control of largely two pure owners. Furthermore, we are yet to hear of any cancellations for October.
