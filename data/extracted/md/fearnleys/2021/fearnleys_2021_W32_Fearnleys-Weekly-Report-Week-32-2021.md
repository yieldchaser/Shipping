Fearnleys Weekly Report
Week 32 - August 11, 2021
## Tankers
## Comments
## VLCC
We remain mired, deep in the Summer doldrums. So far in the region of 90-95 ﬁxtures reported ex MEG for August and we already nearing the end of the month laycan wise, suggests a quieter month after a relatively busy July. As such tonnage has been building up at Fuj and this coupled with many deals concluding oﬀ market, where charterers pick a victim, owners are struggling to build up any steam. With the Atlantic quiet, and those that do ﬁx, involving tying in tonnage for lengthy trips while the hope of market improvement ever lingers, it is a quandary for owners. Worth noting the CNBC report that The White House is calling on OPEC+ to boost oil production in an eﬀort to combat climbing gasoline prices, as oﬃcials from the Biden Administration spoke with representatives from Saudi Arabia and others this week.
## Suezmax
So far this week we have seen an increase in activity in almost all areas. Both North Sea, West Africa and Brazil has been rather busy, which is much needed help for desperate owners. The Brazil export is probably the most reliable one, with Petrobras quoting a Suez cargo almost every day now. Tonnage list in the West on the other hand is still not a pretty sight for owners, but hopefully if this activity remains we can see it thinning out going into next week. In the East, we also see increasing activity, although much of the ﬁxing is happening under the radar. Tonnage-wise it is looking somewhat balanced, and going into next week we could see some more resistance from owners.
## Aframax
It was another lacklustre week for Aframaxes trading in the Nsea and Baltic area. On the back of limited cargo activity coupled with a longer tonnage list, charterers managed to push rates even lower from a level that was already perceived as a “bottomed out” market. Going forward, we expect the market to move sideways which only give marginal returns, if any at all. In the Med/Bsea we have seen a bit more activity on the cargo side compared to last week, however as in the North the activity levels are still nowhere close to satisfying the amount of prompt ships. We also expect the Med/Bsea market to trade sideways going into next week.

## Capesize
Market has been fairly quiet over the last week as many market participants have been on holiday. That said, Brazil has been very active with miners picking vessels coming in ballast from Far East to carry their iron ore to China. We therefore see an improvement on this route of almost 7% while the overall basket is up by 2%. As of now, the number of Capes able to load Brazil for end August and September is very limited and expectations of further increase in levels is strong. While the Brazil segment sees a solid increase, the West Australia route has been slow over the last week, bottoming on Tuesday with the segment slightly up today marking w-o-w down by about 65 cents.
## Panamax
The Panamax market is continuing its ﬁrming tendency in both hemispheres. We see fresh mineral demand in the Atlantic, but also grain shipments have picked up, especially from Black Sea. Charterers book tonnage in SE Asia and Indian Ocean in order to cover their grains out of Black Sea. P1A is currently priced in the low 30s while fronthauls are yielding almost 50k. In the Paciﬁc we see rounds being ﬁxed at region 30k.
## Supramax
Another positive week for the market with BSI pushing up. More activity from US Gulf and ECSA pushed rates up. The ﬁxtures reported from the US Gulf higher than “last done”. TCT from Mississippi River to Med-Cont being ﬁxed at USD 35,000 pd on
Ultramax and TCT to Far East at USD 45,000. Black Sea and Mediterranean market are extremely hot, and rates are pushing as high as USD 48,000’ to West Africa, and for trips to Far East owners are asking close to USD 60,000. Continent is tight on tonnage supply and charterers paying up. Indian Ocean and MEG region remains extremely strong, with period rates being done close to
USD 40,000 and trips from India to China ﬁxing at USD 45,000. Inter- Paciﬁc coal voyages are paying around USD 31,000-33,000.

## Gas
Chartering
### EAST
September ﬁxing is now starting to ﬁnd its pace in the Middle East with the release of Adnoc acceptances on Tuesday evening. In addition, we understand there is a handful of ﬁrst decade Australia cargoes in the woodwork, as well as an FOB tender from QP that was awarded yesterday. This month's last round of acceptances from Aramco are only expected to be published earliest on
Sunday meaning quite a few charterers still need to wait for their dates. That said, charterers with a ﬁrm program at hand might consider making a move before high 40's levels potentially won't be on the table anymore.
### WEST
The market has been far more active this week with rate levels, till now, hovering the mid-USD 80’s/ton for the transpaciﬁc run and mid-USD 40’s for the transatlantic run. Slowly, but surely, freight rates are picking up and entering into the mid/high USD 80's/40's with focus on second and third decade September cargoes. At these levels, ships are trading at low/mid and mid/high USD 30,000’s per day, respectively. Panama delays have come oﬀ to some extent, down to about six days each way at time of writing, however, it remains to be seen for how long this trend will last. If this does persist, we may see an opening for a further increase in freight rates going forward.
