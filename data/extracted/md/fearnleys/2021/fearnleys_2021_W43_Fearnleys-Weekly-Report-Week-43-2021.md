# Fearnleys Weekly Report - Week 43, 2021

**Week 43 - October 27, 2021**

source: `corpus/01-brokers/fearnleys/2021/fearnleys_2021_W43_Fearnleys-Weekly-Report-Week-43-2021.pdf`  |  pages: 1

## Tankers

### VLCC

It has been a couple of weeks with steady increase in rates for the VLCCs. Driven initially by an active West market where charterers with long haul voyages to cover tapping up eastern ballasters, with ships open in the Atlantic hard to come by. East of Suez also busy but with a position list filled with vintage ships or ex dd, the rates are not going through the roof quite yet. However, with owners’ sentiment strong, the rates are expected to increase as we move further into mid November laycan ex MEG. With the count now up to 50+ deals done, there is a lot of wood left to chop before we reach December, so upside potential in the days ahead.

### Suezmax

Regardless of the perceived backward steps in Wafr, the key indicator to observe is the gentle momentum in the VLCC market, meaning that owners who fixed Td20 at a discount to last done are, perhaps, short-changing themselves. Furthermore, second decade Wafr should still have a solid chunk of enquiry to come, and that despite some softer fixing, this market should push on again. Td20 has added circa 30 points in the last 3 weeks alone, suggesting it would be a brave punter who bets against this same scenario being replicated three weeks from now. Black Sea delays are up to 8 days north bound, and as we head into November this is likely to increase which will see risk-averse charterers demanding Canakkale cancelling 10-12 days prior to laycan. Td23 trades minimum WS 47.5-50 whilst MEG/East is WS 77.5-80 with a flat feel.

### Aframax

The Aframax market in the Nsea/Baltic area finally ticked up a few points on the back of increased fuel enquiry coupled with a healthier Baltic crude export program. Going forward, we expect rates to firm further supported by surrounding markets offering an alternative for those owners willing to ballast out of the area. Owners with ships in the Med/Bsea finally have a market to get excited about as rates pick up at 25-30 points across the board over the course of the week. This is due to several reasons, but mainly because cargo activity has been healthy and at the same time delays are increasing in Turkish Straits. In the week the come we expect this firm trend to continue.

## Dry Bulk

### Capesize

The market has fallen sharply the last week as the amount of available tonnage in the East has increased and charterers holding back taking advantage of the negative sentiment. The West Australia rout with iron ore to China is down by 28%, now giving owners a time charter hire of 31,850 per day. The iron ore rout from Brazil to China is down by 26%. The overall basket is down by 34% indicating that the more illiquid routs has come off harder than where there is a more constant flow of cargo. This might indicate that there have been a lot of panic in the Capesize segment, and time of writing there is some signs of support, though still very nervous.

### Panamax

This week the Panamax market has not shown any clear direction, but the underlying sentiment is negative. Rates are coming off in both hemispheres, although not to the same extent as the bigger Capesizes. P1A is currently trading around the 36k mark, while in the Pacific we see rounds being concluded at around 37/38k. The period market is currently lacking activity, much affected by the FFA market.

### Supramax

Both physical and FFA followed the larger sizes south. Biggest fall in USG where less fresh orders and some reassessing of levels. ECSA balanced, where sugar runs to Indo around 45+500bb. From Continent to Mediterranean usual scrap runs fixed around USD 36,000. Trips to USG with steels around USD 40,000. Far East big drop in Indo India rounds now flying around USD 30,000. On the period front, the Bulk Manara (55,692/2010) open Koh Sichang 3 November was fixed for minimum 3 months trading at high USD 30,000s. If the average seasonal pattern repeats perfectly towards year-end, average earnings will correct to the low 30,000s per day before rebounding slightly until year-end.

## Gas

### Chartering

#### EAST

With Diwali coming up next Thursday, Indian PSUs seemingly have been keen to sort out most of their November programs in advance of the festivities, and 6 out of the 9 spot fixtures for November so far has been Indian fixtures. As freight ideas in the West approaching mid 90s on the back of a flurry of fixtures ex US last night the East/West diff is widening. East freight should eventually also see the effects of a tightening market West of Suez as those owners who have the option to seek cargoes in US are more likely to do so now. A theoretical 50 RT/C (almost 3 dollars above last Baltic) versus a 95 H/C still results in a more than 6 dollar West premium before factoring in Panama waiting.

#### WEST

The West VLGC market has been very active so far this week, and first decade December fixing is well underway with still uncovered cargoes early in the month. End November only has a couple of vessels left open, and in all likelihood, these will be absorbed rather soon. Early December is tight! Freight rates are steadily increasing from mid/high USD 80’s and up towards mid USD 90’s if all sub-fixtures are confirmed. Yet again and for the market to sustain, we are reliant upon not overcooking the goose.
