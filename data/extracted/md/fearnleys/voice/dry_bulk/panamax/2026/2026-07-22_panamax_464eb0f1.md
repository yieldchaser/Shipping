## Market Commentary

The Panamax market has drifted softer through the week, with sentiment turning cautious across both basins as the index eased and owners found bids reluctant to follow their ideas. In the Atlantic the tone has been subdued – transatlantic business is holding together rather better than fronthaul, but the constraint is one of cargo volume rather than tonnage, and a build-up of ballasters arriving from the East has left forward positions in the South under pressure and the basin trading two-tier, with prompt units still commanding a premium. The Pacific has been the weaker of the two, as thin fresh grain and coal enquiry out of North Pacific, Australia and Indonesia has allowed prompt tonnage to accumulate and charterers to sit back and bid below last-done. Period remains the steadier corner of the market, with one-year business still concluding, though a backwardated forward curve and firmer bunkers on Middle East tensions are keeping owners measured rather than assertive. We look for a sideways-to-easier tone into next week, with the eventual ramp-up in new-crop grain out of the US Gulf and South America the most likely source of support later in the quarter.
