## Market Commentary

EAST A few cargoes are being worked in MEG and some have been placed on subs so far this week. Despite some activity including FOB tenders and a few Indian bound cargoes, length remains for June positions with availability widely spread across the month. We also continue to see more owners sending ships West on speculation. Nevertheless, the question now seems to be where we will find the freight floor as earnings are about to slide below break-even levels. WEST Freight rates West of Suez have continued to come under some pressure this week. With Houston/Chiba voyages now some $40pmt cheaper than they were a month ago, waiting time is considerably cheaper than it has been for much of the past year. This might be one factor in why owners have continued to dig their heels in and have been reluctant to be too competitive on freight in recent weeks. Consequently, it seems that traders have had more success in reducing their freight exposure in June, with owners looking longer freight as the market’s focus turns towards July. While owners’ decision to slow steam ships will help to stretch the fleet in the short term, ultimately there is still the sense that rates have further to fall. The extent to which this will happen will depend on how aggressive owners choose to be when the next round of fixing commences.
