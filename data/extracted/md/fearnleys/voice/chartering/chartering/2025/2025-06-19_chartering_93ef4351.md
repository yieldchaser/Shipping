## Market Commentary

EAST Since the first attacks on Iran last Friday we have so far only registered one single spot fixture intended to load inside of the Middle East Gulf. Aramco acceptances are now finalized, and whether this will flush out cargos from the Middle East Gulf right away, or if we will continue to see Charterers on the fence remains to be seen. So far only one uncovered cargo is being worked in the Middle East and we are just one and a half week away from July. WEST The West VLGC freight market continues to be closely tied to the state of the arbitrage, which remains a primary driver of rate direction. Recent weeks have seen periods where rate momentum has been more pronounced in the MEG than in the U.S., and this trend has continued into the current week. At present, the highest level fixed stands at USD 128 basis HCvP, despite RT/C levels trading in the USD 80–90 per metric ton range—indicating a spread of USD 12,000–14,000 per day between the two markets. For July loading, owners and relets fixing in the West seem more focused on locking in 80–90 day round voyages at firm levels, rather than chasing the shorter 20–40 day voyages prevailing in RT/C. To date, 24 fixtures have been reported in the USG, with only 6–8 ships remaining prompt for the balance of the month. This increasingly tight tonnage list points to possible upward pressure on rates if more freight demand emerges.
