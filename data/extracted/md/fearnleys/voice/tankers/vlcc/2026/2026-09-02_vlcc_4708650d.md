## Market Commentary

The situation in the Strait of Hormuz is becoming more precarious by the hour as the US-Iran conflict has intensified again. Several tankers have been hit, and reports suggest that a fully laden VLCC has been abandoned by its crew. The possibility of a major oil spill would of course be a natural disaster but will also bring more focus on the condition of ships which do make it through and their safety when it comes to performing ship to ship operations. A number of discounted Somo tenders never made it through the strait resulting in some failures of fixtures done in the last couple of days at inflated rates. With that some of the immediate pressure has eased slightly, but the position list still remains wanting and rates are unlikely to go much down any time soon. The Atlantic position list is even more challenging, and the likes of oil major Petrobras reportedly only had 1 offer yesterday for a Brazil/China enquiry by the deadline, on and ex d/d ship which wasn’t workable. There’s also reports of another Brazilian export cargo working off the market. Add to that a couple of charterers sniffing ex West Africa and a tender or two USG/Eastbound and it’s a perfect “storm”. Arguably the Suezmax market has seen a drop lately and will eventually cap the upside for their bigger sisters.
