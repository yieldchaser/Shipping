## Market Commentary

**MEG:** ADNOC released their May acceptances with some delays reported, this was inevitable as most lifters nominated basis preferred early month lifting because of the heavy backwardation from May to June. Qatar Petroleum is reported to have sold a May 31 to Jun 1st lifting of 46/0 at June CP plus $11-12 to a trading house. Premium for May FOB liftings varies widely pending on the different loading laycans, from mid high teens to single digits, the latter the cheaper. At the time of writing, May Propane CP is traded at $505 and Butane assessed $15 higher, with June Propane CP is at $29 less. Freight market ex MEG remains tight in the front month.

**Baltic**

 kept it’s upward momentum up this week, which eroded the margins on delivery basis, with lifters tending to sell FOBs at premium to cash premium instead of bringing the cargo into the East themselves, unless they have their own shorts to cover.

**FAR EAST**

: It was another hectic week with robust buying inquires, especially so for front the market against only a few offers. Although Houston channel has resumed operation, minimum 7-10 days are still expected to clear the backlock loading at some terminals. Therefore, the front tightness extends into 2h May or even 1h June. Off window there is talk circulating that a 23k Propane cargo for delivery during 1h May traded at May FEI plus $60, while 1h/2h May swap value is at $20. On Thursday a 22/22 cargo for 1h May delivery were bid at May FEI plus $70, and bids for 23/0 for 1h May and 2h May was at May FEI plus $55 and plus $30 respectively, no offers for 1h May delivery was seen and 2h May sellers was at 10 dollars higher. Chinese importers are also exploring cargoes for May, however limited cargoes are shown and the few that are is asking high $60ish or even higher, this is due to the higher FOB premiums and freight. Some importers have shifted buying requirements from 1h May into 2h May, hoping they can secure cheaper cargoes, this doesn’t look to be the case yet, especially when there are outstanding trader shorts competing for the same cargoes. Though Propylene appears weak and has squeezed PDH margins, this is unless it is an integrated downstream units. Compared to high premium on “regular” tons, Iranian origins appear rather attractively priced and April/May imports is expected to be maintained or exceed the March volume which was at 500k tonnes.
