## Market Commentary

Cash premium for Dec lifting FOBs ex AG soared up especially for evenly split grade, due to tight spot supply, coupled with robust Indian buying demand. Two oil majors were reported cancelled their Dec FOB AG buy tenders. No fresh deals were reported after last deal into India which was concluded at CP plus teens level. One producer trading arm was bidding in window for Dec FOB 2/2 at $490, which equates to Dec CP plus mid-teens, yet to entice offers. Indian IOC issued buy tender for monthly one evenly split cargo for Q1 delivery, either FOB AG or CFR India. Market participants are awaiting acceptance to know the full scheme, especially if any ratio/quantity adjustments from Saudi Aramco. By the time of writing, Dec CP was traded at $480 for C3 and C3/C4 at $10. Arbitrage for US exports to Asia narrowed sharply this week, as Lst/Fei for Dec and Jan once traded at below $150. However, due to the softening freight, terminal fee for Dec liftings improved to 6ish cpg and a couple of spot deals reported traded at around such level. A flurry of cargoes changed hand in Asian market, supported by strong demand from both heating and petchem segments. Supply tightened for 1h Dec delivery as Japanese and Korean importers are actively buying, together with traders covering shorts. Cash differentials for 23/0 parcels surged up into positive premium, vs last week’s deep discounts, with CFR 1h Dec being bid at Dec FEI plus $8 while 2h Dec at Bal Nov FEI minus $0.5, which equates to Dec FEI plus $3.5. 4/0 parcel for Dec delivery basis Chiba was heard traded at Dec FEI minus mid-single, and similar parcel basis Ningbo for 2h Dec delivery was bid at Dec FEI plus $3.5. Two PDHs have awarded their buy tenders to secure non-US 4/0 cargoes for Dec delivery at Dec CP plus high 60s basis Schina and Echina respectively. One cracker canceled their buy tender seeking 1h Dec delivery propane into Ningbo as limited offer, while the other managed to procure a 4/0 for Dec at Dec FEI plus $1.
