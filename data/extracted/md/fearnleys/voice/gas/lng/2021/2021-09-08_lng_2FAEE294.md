## Market Commentary

It has been pleasing to see that spot market activity picked up this week with FOB tenders appearing alongside firm requirements. Commodity prices remain strong yet perhaps surprisingly given cargo strength and increased freight activity, freight rates struggle to interrupt the depressed trend that currently overwhelms the market. The lack of prompt cargoes sees a sizable relet fleet open and interested in short localised business. As laycans drift into Q4, late October and November in particular, rates for long-haul voyages do rise significantly and we can expect the delta between BLNG2 and BLNG3 to widen in the coming weeks as winter gets priced in. Moving East, the thirst for vessels has improved yet insufficiently to absorb all open tonnage. Modern tonnage is likely to move West on their own accord in hope for Atlantic business. Those who remain East for spot Pacific requirements still have to compete with sublet vessels with pockets of availability which are dampening freight levels. LNG shipping availability may however tighten as we look ahead with the current future arbitrage opportunities. Yard prices hold firm, yet no new newbuilding transactions have been recorded.
