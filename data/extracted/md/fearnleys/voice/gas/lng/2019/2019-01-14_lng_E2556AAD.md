## Market Commentary

We are half-way to January and it does feel like the shipping market has taken a breather. We are only seeing limited new activity, which together with a number of redeliveries East of Suez, has caused our short term availability to look rather long, especially in the Pacific. As a result, any new piece of shipping business has gained great interest from the Owners, which has consequently put pressure on rates. As a matter of fact, it is starting to look like the correlation between the LNG shipping and cargo market has restored, making it more challenging to see any change in the short term shipping sentiment without a boost in the cargo side first (more cargoes or better arb’s). Although we are in the winter months, we get the feeling that without a solid cold spike, the “shoulder month season” might be coming upon us sooner than expected. That said, as we have learned in the past, the tide can change very quickly in the world of LNG and the players who can move just before the tidal change, will usually get the best deals. For a change, the shipyards had a rather quiet announcement week as no new LNGC orders were reported.
