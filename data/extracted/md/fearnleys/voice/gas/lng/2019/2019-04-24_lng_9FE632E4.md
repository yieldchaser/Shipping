## Market Commentary

Optimisation is the name of the game for those players with a fleet of LNG vessels. Portfolio players constantly optimise their fleets to charter in whenever it is more economical to do so, whilst spare vessels, usually with short term availability, are marketed for third party charter and this goes someway to explaining the number of vessels chalked up on the position list. Despite the number of chartering enquiries, rates remain depressed as competition remains high with shipowners eager to ensure that their vessels are moving. There are various cargoes available in the Pacific basin and they are set to keep chartering activity at a moderate level but some way off those previously witnessed. With new US volumes due to come online in the coming months and the hope of a warm start to summer, Owner's eyes are on charter hire rate levels with the hope of tightening in the shipping market. In reality this could be some months off but on the flip-side sublets are a high percentage of the availability and can vanish with little warning. Worldwide availability looks relatively balanced albeit oversupplied as intra-basin trade continues to be the overwhelming route choice. The Atlantic basin has been relatively quiet of late but we expect some vessels to be fixed given the ongoing good production out of North Africa. Spot trades from the Atlantic to the Far East are still a rarity with the abundance of cargoes from South East Asia and Australia providing any Far East LNG purchase interest with an obvious choice of source for any required cargoes.
