## Market Commentary

It’s back to the office after the summer holidays for some and with it the expectation that we ought to be leaving the quieter "shoulder month season" and seeing the influence of winter demand. This week has seen a lot of activity globally, both in the cargo and the shipping market as supply and demand tenders grab traders attention. October laycans are the focus with multiple spot vessel requirements in addition to term vessel interest, although with less vigor than last year. The Pacific has been the busiest region and this may mark a long awaiting increase in activity. The Atlantic market may also see an upturn, Freeport is producing its first cargoes, Cameron is up and running, as well is Corpus train two. It’s this cargo outlook on the back of a limited vessel availability list, albeit growing, that sees rates relativity stable despite low utilization. That said, this week we see a small drop in rates as prompt vessels start to lengthen availability lists. Looking further out, the curve appears to shallow as some Owners come forward to work on late Q4 requirements. Early days to predict where rates may end the year and we may yet we see a real tightening with the second half of November showing the most promise for Owners. Orders at the yards remain subdued with one order being reported at HHI whilst at the other end of the market two elderly vessels head for scrap.
