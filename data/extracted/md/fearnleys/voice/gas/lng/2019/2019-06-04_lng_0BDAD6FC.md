## Market Commentary

The LNG spot market continues to delight buyers and dismay sellers, with delivered cargo pricing slipping further in Europe and hitting a [3]-year low in the Far East. On-shore storage remains high and the East/West arbitrage is firmly shut, leading to fewer fresh requirements and the squeezing of trading margins. The prompt shipping market has in turn ceased strengthening and may be moving towards a shallow decline: the few recent fixtures completed suggest a slight drop in headline rates, albeit with overall structures that retain elements of positioning and repositioning. However, the view is quite different further out, as the relatively weak hands held by LNG sellers and shipowners today may herald a loaded deck tomorrow. The longer-term ton-mile story remains strong: the first cargo has left Cameron and Prelude is expected to follow suit imminently with more to come, and there is plenty of growth potential offered by an average voyage distance today that is down 10% against long-term levels. Add in to the mix a few other “ifs” and “maybes” that the present market has produced, such as the scope for floating storage if the market enters a contango or for reloads out of a full Europe if the arbitrage opens wide, and the firm and strengthening sentiment of owners towards longer-term charters makes sense.
