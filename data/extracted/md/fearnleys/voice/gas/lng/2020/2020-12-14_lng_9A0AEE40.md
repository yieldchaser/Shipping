## Market Commentary

The LNG market continues to stay boiling hot (ironically) as the Far East and the Middle-East delivered LNG prices hit new multiyear highs almost every day now, while short coverers and end-users are scrambling to find cargoes in the region. This in turn has caused the LNG shipping market to move from high-speed to hyperdrive, as every promptly available vessel gets taken in a heartbeat causing the global vessel availability to look very scarce indeed over the next month. Naturally, this tightness of available vessels have spiked up the charter rates, which have also reached levels that were last seen only during the peak winter of 2018. Furthermore, with new vessel requirements emerging this week to cover February liftings, we do expect this tightness to drag out a bit longer than we predicted just a few months back. That said, even if the first two months of 2021 look tight, we are still holding the view that vessel supply and demand will balance out before the end of Q1. On the newbuilding front, it was reported this week that Hyundai Heavy Industries have inked another four new LNG newbuilding contracts, two with Pan Ocean, one with Knutsen OAS and one with JP Morgan. All of these newbuilds are 174,000cbm and highly-equipped with XDF propulsion systems, and will be long-term chartered by Shell. With these new contracts, Shell’s total LNGC newbuilding tally stands at 18 modern XDF vessels, all being built by HHI.
