## Market Commentary

The flurry of activity in the Atlantic last week saw a modest increase in spot LNG charter rates, which has continued into this week as 2-stroke availability for December cargoes has all but disappeared. Portfolios keen to maintain or gain flexibility amidst uncertainty on optimal cargo destinations has tightened availability considerably and prompted several other requirements to emerge for later in January. It remains to be seen whether this tightness will lead to anything resembling a spike on freight or, as we are seeing for the moment, a slight uptick. Similarly, East of Suez sees a healthy amount of enquiries both from the Middle East and APAC with requirements still being worked at the time of writing. On the surface, the market remains oversupplied with TFDE’s but with independent owners holding most positions, Charterers who require vessels to reposition and seek flexibility on charter period have less bargaining power with rates already at relatively low levels. There is also a sense of convergence on multimonth and multiyear levels, although the delta is still a little wide for now. With owners and charterers travelling around for the festive period and possibly preparing for CWC, it’s likely we’ll see some progress on these medium term periods in the coming weeks.
