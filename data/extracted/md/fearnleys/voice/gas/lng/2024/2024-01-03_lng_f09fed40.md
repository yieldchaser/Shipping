## Market Commentary

It has been a quiet start to the New Year in the West, with a lack of firm requirements in the basin and general muted market activity following the public holiday period. This is typically the period when rates come off, although the lull could be short lived following the harsh weather that is prevailing through North West Europe, which has been and is expected to continue disrupting schedules. Moreover, with the ongoing challenges in the Red Sea, it will be interesting to see how shipping activity in the Atlantic responds and whether we see a response over the coming days as players seek prompt tonnage to address interferences. Such circumstances have the potential to disrupt the current softening market rates, given the shortage of modern vessels available in the West, especially if Owners withdraw tonnage amidst prevailing uncertainties. The picture in the East is quite different, as Owners finds themselves engaged with several firm requirements for tonnage across various sizes and specifications, whilst potential developing opportunities also draw attention. With vessel availability high East of Suez, there has been a corresponding lowering in charter rates. While the possibility of a slowing decline exists if the cold weather persists in North Asia and demand increases, the current trend is marked by heightened competition. On the term side, the past week has seen limited activity. However, as charter rates become more attractive to Charterers, we can expect to see increased interest in term vessel availability over the coming weeks. There were no reported LNG newbuilding orders this week, however, we note a USD 1.5 billion order for a FLNG unit at Samsung. LNG newbuild prices remain stable.
