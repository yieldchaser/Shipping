## Market Commentary

As players in the shipping industry resume their normal work pace following Gastech, the LNG shipping market continues to exhibit subdued activity, mirroring the trends of previous weeks. There are few transactions and ample vessel availability in both basins, alongside limited shipping demand. In the Atlantic, freight rates have remained flat, with no significant fluctuations observed and while several fixtures were reported last week, the arbitrage remains volatile. In contrast, the market East of Suez has experienced a more challenging week; activity has slowed due to a lack of firm requirements and owners competing for the various FOB cargo tenders, and so rates have softened. The multi-month term charter market has also remained relatively stable for another week, although there is a noticeable softening in sentiment primarily due to the increasing likelihood of a fairly flat winter. While several candidate vessels are available, charterers aren’t showing much interest at this stage. On the newbuilding front, Peninsula has signed an agreement with Hyundai Mipo to construct a new LNG bunkering vessel with a capacity of 18,000m3, expected to be delivered in 2027. Conversely, there have been no new orders for conventional LNGCs this week, despite indications of softening prices in the newbuilding market.
