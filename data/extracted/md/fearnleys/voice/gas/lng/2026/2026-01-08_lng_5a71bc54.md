## Market Commentary

Barely a week into 2026 and we have already seen some fairly drastic shifts. The unpredictable nature of geopolitics right now is raising all manner of questions, but even if we just focus on our more genteel LNG shipping market we will still have plenty to think about. Starting in the Atlantic, there has been a growing sense of a falling market with several vessels opening but very few new requirements. Charterers have generally been comfortable holding out in the circumstances, and some have been rewarded by securing much softer rates; however, the see-saw may already be tipping the other way with a number of fresh enquiries emerging and a much more balanced market, at least on paper. The Pacific basin has been a little more predictable, with downward pressure leading to a steady decline in fixing levels albeit from a lower base than in the Atlantic. The Middle East remains a more active region than has traditionally been seen, but with the build up of tonnage the premium for ballasting across is also coming under pressure. The question for many in these past weeks has been how the market will react to a weaker environment into Q1, and although it’s fair to say that things are cooling there is still some fight left in this market. The shipyards have also been very busy in closing out 2025, and we will provide a full summary in our next Quarterly Update – watch this space!
