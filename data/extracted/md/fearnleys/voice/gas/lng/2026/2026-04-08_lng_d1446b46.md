## Market Commentary

After several weeks of attacks, counterattacks and increasingly heated threats, the world woke up on Wednesday perhaps relieved to have woken up at all. If the present path of de-escalation is maintained, the energy and freight markets should return to normality but it will be a slow process and the impact and outcome on LNG shipping dynamics remains uncertain. This is a big "if" and the details will matter enormously as the negotiations unfold. In the coming weeks, much will depend on the demand profiles across different regions, especially while the Middle East production facilities slowly ramp back up. In the Atlantic basin, a lack of prompt firm requirements is contributing to softer rate sentiment, particularly as vessel availability builds through April and into 1H May. With limited liquidity, the market remains reactive, with participants trying to capture arbitrage opportunities or awaiting the next batch of cargoes. Although there have been few opportunities, the East of Suez has seen comparatively stronger fixture activity over the holiday period. This has created a competitive environment, particularly for owners of TFDE tonnage. Combined with a growing list of open vessels, sentiment has softened and players will be tracking markets carefully for clues as to how the market will develop as the ceasefire ensures.
