## Market Commentary

Last week we wrote of plummeting rates in the Atlantic, and this week we write of… plummeting rates in the Atlantic. While it was clear then that momentum was only going one way, the brakes have barely been applied as levels fell rapidly through the teens and now into the single digits. There are several more requirements within the traditional fixing window, but there are few signs of a recovery at this stage and competition remains fierce for each cargo. Our report also suggested that a reverse arbitrage could see some Asian cargoes heading into Europe and this has occurred, but it will probably not be a feature of the market going forward. The Pacific and Middle East regions are still trading at considerably higher levels than the Atlantic, and East of Suez there are TFDE vessels fixing out at higher rates than modern 2-stroke vessels are achieving in the Atlantic. All of this presents a challenging environment for owners; however, several 1-3 year fixtures have been concluded at levels that suggest a stronger second half of the year and should give encouragement to those with redelivering tonnage.
