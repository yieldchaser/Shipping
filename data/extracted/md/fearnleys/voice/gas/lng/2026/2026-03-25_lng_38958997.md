## Market Commentary

Recent weeks have seen the energy markets try to adapt in real-time to the chaotic situation in the Middle East, and while still nothing is resolved there is at least more stability in the prompt LNG and LNG shipping markets. The LNG shipping market has remained largely secondary to the commodity side, where swings have determined the direction of traffic although high rates have impacted the arbitrage form the US to Asia. There have been several fixtures at levels unthinkable just four weeks ago but the apparent easing of tensions has seen a softening of sentiment across the board, with prompt vessels competing more aggressively for employment. It remains to be seen whether the market is resilient enough to maintain levels above long term breakeven; however, in the short term it does appear that elevated cargo prices are supporting healthier freight levels. Looking further out in anticipation of an end to the conflict, the impact of the attacks on Ras Laffan are not yet fully understood but the most recent updates confirm a considerable loss in production for a number of years. The uncertainty in when or how the conflict will end, how will production be affected in the Middle East and the direction of tradeflows are causing headaches in pricing longer term charters, and although owners are there to lock in longer periods, the lack clarity is impeding activity in the multimonth space. Forward availabilities provide even larger and perhaps sharper headaches for charterers, who wrestle with the risk of remaining short versus locking in flexibility at a premium to their now-outdated forecasts.
