## Market Commentary

Plenty of chartering opportunities are coming to market yet the lack of available vessels has dampened freight activity this week. The result so far this week - only a handful of vessel are being fixed! The lack of availability, particularly on the prompt, likely means most potential requirements will pass without taking shipping, although those that do conclude will be at charter levels equalling 2023 previous highs. A wide spectrum of news stories emerged this week; reports of a power outage at Freeport saw concerns rise on the supply side, whilst seasonal bad weather effected European ports and the ability for vessels to discharge on time, potentially providing needed respite to terminals that remain almost at full capacity. Despite glimpses of colder temperatures appearing for brief intervals, high storage levels mean Mediterranean terminals are offering attractive reload parcels which seek homes further down the curve or in the East; however, Pacific cargo demand still appears limited and opportunistic. So, all-in-all there is plenty of uncertainty and control of tonnage remains valuable and priced at a premium. In the Atlantic, 170,000m3 plus vessels showing December availability could be viewed as “Unicorns”, and charterers are now looking to open vessels in the East for Atlantic loads as US gulf FOB tenders are offered to market. Not an easy exercise as rates in the pacific remain on par with those in the Atlantic. The next week will be an intriguing one for the LNG spot shipping market! Newbuilding prices rose this week on the back of two orders placed at HHI with delivery Q1 2028.
