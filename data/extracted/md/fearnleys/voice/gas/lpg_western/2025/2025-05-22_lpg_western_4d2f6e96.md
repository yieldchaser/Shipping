## Market Commentary

US The global LPG market is softening on all fronts and is led by the declines being seen in the US domestic market where propane, having rallied earlier in the month, seems to be succumbing under the weight of the latest EIA report. For the week ending 16 May total US primary stock levels rose by 2.7mmbbls to an overall total of 53.10mmbbls, or about 83% of last year’s total in mid May. As anticipated, the EIA data showed production levels at 2.833mmbbls/day (against a high of 2.852mmbbls/day and a 5yr average of 2.440mmbbls/day). Likewise, however, the export number revealed a decline which on its own was not surprising. Total exports were listed at 1.690mmbbls/day, which is still above the 5yr average, but well off the 2.335mmbbl/day overall high-water mark. This is thought to be the result of a host of parties switching export cargo ratios from full propane to 50:50 lots. That, in turn, is because of the US-led tariffs. Meanwhile, terminal fees on the Gulf Coast have been hovering just over cancellation economics as the arb from Asia remains depressed and netbacks – in consideration of robust, healthy freight levels – have been held down.
