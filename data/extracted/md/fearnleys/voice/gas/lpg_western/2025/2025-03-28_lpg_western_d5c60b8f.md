## Market Commentary

US In between the nearly rampant discussions of the potential implications of the various tariffs that have been proposed, the market is meanwhile discussing the reasons behind the ongoing and robust nature of the price of propane at Mont Belvieu. Inventories have drawn essentially as much as they’re going to this year, and the build season is commencing at present as weather-related demand has by now subsided. Nonetheless, MB propane continues to outperform, and is at present ~52% of WTI, which should theoretically indicate that the market is poised for decline, but as yet this is not the case. At just over 43mmbbls, overall US propane stock levels are below the 5yr average of 48.5mmbbls for this time of year, and while this is surely one of the reasons the pricing structure is so vigorous, it is the combination of low secondary and tertiary inventory levels which are being touted as supporting reasons – all while the influx of ‘outsider’ paper investment is bolstering market values. As for the US propane inventory forecast, there are emerging doubts that stock levels will reach the 5yr average of 91mmbbls by end October, and that sentiment is also a driver. A recent (and admittedly un-scientific) poll has estimates for 31 Oct stock levels clustered around the mid 80mmbbl level, which includes healthy production levels but also the anticipated gains that are expected in the overwater export sector that stem from the first (of several) announced marine expansions.
