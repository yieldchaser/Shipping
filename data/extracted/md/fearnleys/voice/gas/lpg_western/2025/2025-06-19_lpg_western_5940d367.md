## Market Commentary

US With the recent geopolitically oriented and tension-based gains seen in crude oil, which have brought WTI to its highest levels since January, propane itself has been quick to move in lock-step and has also now risen to its highest point since the first part of the year. Where that had allowed the relationship between MB c3 and WTI to reach as much as 56%, by now the differential has relaxed to a more palatable 45% in recent trading days. The EIA this week released its propane inventory data which showed a generally agreeable 1.5mmbbl build to an overall total of 67.50mmbbls as of 13 Jun. Production levels in the US remain at high levels (2.880mmbbls/day) while export volumes were off just a bit at 1.911mmbbls/day. This is believed to be due to recent weather-related issues which impacted the electrical systems at a couple of the terminals and caused single-digit days delays which are now being addressed. Based on 5-yr averages, Week 25 should see a build of ~1.6mmbbls, though the longer-term outlook over the summer build season rests in the hands of production rates and export volumes, some of which could be impacted by the weather.
