## Market Commentary

AMERICAS While an approximate 2.5mmbbl US propane inventory draw had generally been anticipated, the EIA this week reported a decline of 3.5mmbbls which brought the overall total stock level to 79.10mmbbls, or (just) 101% of last year’s total at the same time. The reported figures came on the heels of a corrected set of data which was revealed two weeks ago and at that time brought inventory levels down by 9mmbbls, a rather staggering drop. It should therefore be of little surprise that MB propane values have – over the same period of time – made their way up from the mid 60cpg range to the over 77cpg as of this writing, and most are of the impression that with a looming large-scale winter storm that is set to bring North American temperatures to sub-freezing levels over the next week should very likely result in yet still further pricing gains. Meanwhile, the market has been reeling from wildly volatile arb levels and fantastic declines in freight rates, which are the result of issues at the Panama Canal and, more recently, at the Suez Canal in the Red Sea. Terminal fees, all the while, have been resting around the mid 6cpg range for late Jan through late Feb business, and while a few transactions have been reported, it is generally a thinly traded market. NWE An offer in the TOT window has pushed the physical discount to CIF ARA -$8/MT for 20-24 January delivery. Continued offers in the TOT window for January deliveries continue to put pressure on physical values. Forecasted cold weather is expected to reach the region is keeping the demand picture positive. The relative low value of natural gas has Dutch TTF prices below CIF ARA propane and butane values on an MMBTU basis. Supporting North Sea LPG exports rather than rejecting LPG into the gas stream.
