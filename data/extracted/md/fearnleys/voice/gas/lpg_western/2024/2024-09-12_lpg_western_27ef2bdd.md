## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Q3 2024 has seen the MB non-TET propane price decline by 20cpg, while correspondingly a 21% drop in crude oil has been recorded over the same period. The ratio of propane to WTI has remained neatly in line and continues at around 42-44%. It might have been assumed that MB finally reacted to the weight of the propane inventory levels, but the evidence shows that propane is walking in lockstep with WTI values. As for the EIA report itself, as of 30 Aug a weekly increase of 1.1mmbbls was recorded, where the market was anticipating an approximate 1.5mmbbl rise, so slightly disappointing but not altogether unsettling. This puts US propane stocks on track to conclude the summer build season at ~102mmbbls. Production levels, say the EIA, are 2.702mmbbls/day, or just above both the quarterly and YTD annual averages. Export volumes, meanwhile, are also robust, and at 1.833mmbbls/day beat the YTD annual average of 1.728mmbbls by 105,000bbls/day, but most are of the opinion that exports are unlikely to trend higher until some of the capacity improvements commence. With regards the terminal fees, the market remains robust. Hot, in fact, as firm offers of 30cpg presently exist. Buy-side ideas, however, are in the ~27cpg range – and while this might be viewed as a standoff, there is legitimate logic and rationale from both sides of the spectrum. Freight is slowly waning; the arb is up and there is little product to be had other than via trading houses. From the buy side, however, 30 cents is a tough psychological barrier. NWE September is set to be the busiest month this year for imports into Europe, due largely to scheduled annual maintenance on the North Sea’s largest LPG export terminal, Kaarstoe which has sidelined the facility for last 3.5 weeks. In Q2 of this year, the facility exported just shy of 70,000mt/month, thus requiring added imports from the US to make up for lost volume. Elsewhere after multiple weeks of unmet offerings, a major petchem finally sold what’s thought to be the last of the September ToT cargoes, marking the conclusion of a very slow month in Window activity, and a turn in attention to a typically busier October. At present, October Pro/Nap is marked at $59 and CIF ARA is quoted at $530, up along with the rest of the market since Tuesday’s slide, but off $18 from MTD average.
