## Market Commentary

US: Cargo transactions concluded in recent days include a purchase at 21.50cts/gal, another at 21.75cts/gal, another at 22.50cts/gal and – the highest (as yet) – 23.50cts/gal. And while these lofty levels might seem extraordinary – and they are – they are supported by the arb to the East. Freight rates and robust Far East market values are the primary causes, since it is relatively cheap to transport product to the high-priced Asian markets. Freight, in turn, has softened as a result of the more readily available slots at the Panama Canal, though this is often referred to as a ‘wild card’ these days since it is uncertain if delays might again occur as they did last October. In the domestic market propane continues to track almost exactly with crude, at least on a percentage basis, and 42% was the average ratio during July. So, as crude prices fluctuate, so go MB propane values. Mont Belvieu propane concluded July at 78.25cts/gal – about 8% lower than on the first of July, and that compares neatly to the 8% decline in WTI pricing during the same period. Meanwhile, as to the propane inventory levels in the U.S., this week the EIA posted a 2.9mmbbl build, which caught most by surprise as expectations were well lower. In any case, overall US stocks are recorded as of 26 July to be 87.40mmbbls, or nearly 99% of last year’s end-July figure. Based on 5-yr average builds for August and September, it is calculated that US propane inventories will quite easily breach 100mmbbls, and likely stand at ~104mmbbls at the first week of October. Again, it is repeated that this level will be entirely sufficient for both domestic and international requirements. Perhaps too much, in fact.
