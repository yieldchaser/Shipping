## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Mont Belvieu propane market values declined by a nickel over the second half of April, and by nearly a dime since the front of April. Prices now are marked in the mid 70cpg range, but still a solid 39% of WTI (basis today’s $79.36/bbl value). Most of the market seem to agree that the weight of the (sometimes slowly) growing inventory levels will push prices down over the summer, but it appears now that a legitimate trend is developing. With regards the inventories, the EIA yesterday recorded what is perceived as a disappointing 200,000bbl build in overall stock levels (as of 26 Apr), where the market was anticipating something over/under 2mmbbls. Higher export volumes combined with (still) strong production levels are the culprits. With limited spot availability known to exist in June, the spot terminal fee market remains buoyant, with the last known deal done at just under 9cpg, but offers now seen at double-digits. Buy-side interest exists in the upper single digits. NWE Northwest Europe, now in decline and off the double digits, was unexempt from the crude-driven sell off of global LPG prices. Compounding the region’s downward pressure was the nearly 600,000 tons of LPG imported during April, and the return to service of Karstoe and Mongstad refineries in the North Sea. That said, with US propane under the most pressure at present, the LST/NWE arbitrage is open again, which will no doubt attract more US East coast FOB volumes to already well supplied Western European terminals.
