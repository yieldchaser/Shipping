## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Volatility is the key descriptor of the MB propane market this week. Crude strength and exterior influences such as hedge funds and price-shavers (i.e., not fundamental factors) have conspired and lifted values to near 80cts/gal, and propane/WTI ratios have reverted up to the low 40-percentile range. How long this will remain the case is as-yet undetermined, but the present situation was exacerbated by the data revealed by the EIA for the week ending 7th June which showed a rather paltry 1mmbbl week-over-week increase to a total of 69.80mmbbls, or 92.5% of last year. The market had generally been anticipating something nearer to a 2.5mmbbl build. Nonetheless, stock levels remain on track for an early-October total of right around 100mmbbls, which – based on current production and, separately, export levels – would be deemed sufficient. Meanwhile, terminal fees remain buoyant. 10cts/gal was fixed at least a few times, 11cts/gal was seen done, as was 11.50 and 12.00cts/gal. At present the remaining (and by now limited) avails are offered at 14cts/gal, presumably to close in the 13s. NWE European propane price realized gains this week following a reduction of imports from the North Sea due to gathering plant outages, increased steam cracking margins in the region and crude gains in the wake of OPEC’s meeting. Buyers have actively bid the window during four consecutive sessions for both propane and butane and while 250,000mts of LPG has already arrived in Europe in June (so far), only 30-40,000 metric tons is scheduled for the second half of the month, thus the moderately bullish sentiment in the NWE LPG market for now.
