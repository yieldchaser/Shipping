## Market Commentary

AMERICAS The EIA yesterday revealed a weekly build of 2.00mmbbls, bringing total US propane stocks to 92.10mmbbls, which is almost exactly the same as last year, but 135% of two years ago. On a Days of Supply basis, there are 121 days of supply as of last Friday’s reckoning, which is vastly greater than the 73-day average for 2024. At the risk of repetition, propane inventories in the U.S. are solidly sufficient and can easily accommodate all fashions of demand. It is calculated that US stocks will reach to between 102-105mmbbls at the first week of October, which would be the highest level in 10 years. The pricing shows Sep MB propane as presently trading at over 78cpg and at a ratio of 45% of WTI. There is very little pricing subsidence through Q1 2025 as the forward curve doesn’t allow for anything less than 78cpg until Mar of next year. Terminal fees, meanwhile, remain solidly robust – though there is a bit of fade into Q4. It is reckoned that Sep is effectively complete, as a mid-Sep 2:2 was fixed at a reported 21cpg. Oct avails are offered in the low/mid 20cpg range, while Nov is offered in the very upper teens for a 4:0. NWE In the first half of August, Europe's LPG market experienced a significant downturn, with prices sliding approximately $20 from August 1st to 15th. This decline was primarily attributed to steady supply from US exports and more consistent output from the North Sea. The East-West spread narrowed and While seasonal demand loomed on the horizon, the market remained in a lull, as almost half a million tons of LPG discharged at Amsterdam-Rotterdam-Antwerp (ARA) ports for the period July 15- August 15. However, as of mid week this week, the propane market in Northwest Europe has showed signs of stabilisation CIF ARA propane has risen to $572/metric ton, up by $6/mt. The large-cargo CIF ARA propane market maintained a steady assessment at +$21/mt to September quotes. Looking ahead, the European LPG market though well-supplied, could occur if maintenance is required in the North Sea or if temperatures drop.
