## Market Commentary

AMERICAS The Nov Gulf Coast terminal fee market has maintained much of its strength from Oct as current rates remain in the mid 20cpg range where several transactions are known to have occurred for all-c3 parcels. Mixed ratio cargoes are discounted and priced in the upper teens, but buy-side sentiment for Dec 2:2's is in the mid/upper teens range. Yesterday the EIA reported a 1.3mmbbl draw which was viewed as mildly surprising as most had anticipated a small build as a result of ongoing production strength and - in recent weeks - some export disruptions. Current overall US stocks as of 18 Oct stand at 101.8mmbbls, or 99.4% of last year's mid-Oct level. NWE While a handful of unmet window bids allowed large cargo CIF ARA propane values to rise during the first half of the week, a global slide in LPG prices which tracked the losses of crude resulted in the marking down of large cargo values by $8/ton to $594/mt where they stand as of Wednesday evening. Looking structurally at the curve, it will be difficult for importers to purchase and store propane due to significant backwardation, especially juxtaposed with belvieus modest contango. The propane / naphhta spread, now marked at -$50/ton, is nearing the levels of theoretical demand destruction for propane, due to thin margins, however several PDH plants including the new 437,000 ton/year plant that opened this year on the coast of Poland, have shown little signs of slowing down yet. As we approach the season of increased heating demand, it will be interesting to see how regional buyers bolster inventories, faced with such significant backwardation.
