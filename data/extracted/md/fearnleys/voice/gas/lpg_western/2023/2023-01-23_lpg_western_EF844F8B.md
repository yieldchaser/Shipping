## Market Commentary

AMERICAS The domestic US market has seen a series of factors enter over the past week or so which have collectively changed the shape of things. Propane prices themselves have been influenced upwards by the rising crude market, which bodes well for the overall economic outlook, as well, and helps things along. Additionally, terminal fees last week saw a sharp spike up to the low teens for Feb and to some degree Mar – but then the freight market took a sharp upturn thus putting pressure on fees and allowing them to relax to current levels of around 8.5 – 10.0 cpg for March. All the while the EIA inventory data for propane was released this week and showed a 1mmbbl decline to an overall total of 75.6mmbbls – still 130% and 140% more than the last two comparative years. The surprise, however, was in that the data revealed a 100,000bbl build on the Gulf Coast (PADD III) and simultaneously a 900,000bbl draw in the Midwest (PADD II). This has given cause for speculation that next week’s data might contain corrective data, though it might then be obfuscated by the combination of currently milder weather, as well as the expectation that a pair of cold-fronts are expected to reach down from the Arctic during Feb. Latin American demand levels have improved through the first half of the winter and formally issued tenders have recently been seen as a result. This is very likely due to the recent cold fronts which have penetrated to the south. NWE February CIF ARA propane values continue to strengthen through weak Brent and bearish US inventory figures. Two TOT window bids have been constant this week for 4-8 February deliveries. Which has kept the physical window trading at a +$3pmt premium to February CIF ARA. Though CIF ARA strengthened by $16.50pmt by close of business Wednesday, it will have to do more to tempt US cargoes away from the Asian market, which continues to be strong since reopening from its New Year holidays. Butane in the region has been weak and lacked large cargo discussions. Naphtha has been weak and butane remains assessed at 87.5% February naphtha, although when buyside interest arises this level is likely to increase rapidly.

**MED**

 The region has been quiet this week. Refinery workers strikes in France would usually hamper exports, for almost a year now refineries have been consuming most of their LPG for power generation.
