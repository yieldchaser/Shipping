## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Following the upwardly trending crude markets which are rising primarily due to pending production cutbacks in the Middle East, the Mont Belvieu propane market rose by more than 20cts/gal over the course of July with non-TET values presently pegged at around 73cts/gal. The reduced LPG output in the other primary supply region is putting the spotlight on the US and, predictably, spot FOB terminal fees have also increased – particularly since freight rates have stalled. Where just a short while ago September business was being talked in the mid single-digit range, notional terminal fee ideas rose quickly as an 8cpg transaction was quickly followed by a 10cpg deal, after which price ideas rose to as high as 12cpg – all for the mid/2H Sep market. Buyers are now known to be assessing values in the low 10cpg range, but little product is believed to be on offer. This is partially due to a pair of mechanical tie-ins which have recently been undertaken – one of which is related to PDH efforts, the other to a terminal capacity improvement project. Meanwhile, stock levels this week were recorded by the EIA to have risen by 1.3mmbbls for an overall total of 89.9mmbbls as of the end of the first week of August. The increase fell short of the market’s expectations, but nonetheless propelled stock levels nearer to eclipsing the 100mmbbl mark by the end of September. Over the past 5 years propane inventory levels have gained an average of over 15mmbbls during Aug and Sep which, if applied to the current 89.9mmbbl stock total, would put total US stocks at over 105mmbbls as of the end of Sep. NWE Approximately 150,000 mt of US Origin LPG are scheduled to arrive in NWE first half August on VLGC's, along with 70,000 mt on LGCs, along with a handful of MGC's also. All that to say, NWE is seemingly well supplied at present however the market still saw a bid for a September cargo in yesterday's window. It went unmet. European butane steadied after a sharp rise over recent weeks which saw prices at a 6-month high, however the arbitrage is expected to come in as blending season arrives.

**MED**

 This month will see the highest ever US origin imports into the region, totaling nearly 550,000 tons, and thus nullifying the need for more product at present. The bulk of the demand has been driven predictably by Morocco and Turkey, while Spain will import 140,000 tons also, contributing to the record levels.
