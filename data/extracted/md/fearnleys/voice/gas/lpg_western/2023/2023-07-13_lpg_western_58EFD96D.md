## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Few transactions are occurring despite the relatively robust arb. While one potential end-Aug spot re-seller elected to lift on its own account, another fixture was noted for end Aug but this likely nears the end of spot terminal sales for Aug. Price talk was in the 6cpg range. With a limited number of available vessels (and correspondingly robust freight rates) there is little that is available direct from the terminals through August. As such, terminal fees slope upwards as Q3 progresses. Where earlier in the summer low single digits were the standard, mid/upper single digits are on the horizon. The EIA reported Wednesday that propane stock levels in the US increased by the anticipated amount of 2.7mmbbls to an overall total of 83.8mmbbls, or 144% of the total achieved at the same time last year. The 5-year average increase for Week 27 was 1.584mmbbls, so this week’s rise – though expected – was quite a bit higher than average. At this point inventories are well above the high end of the 5-year averages and rising; not yet to the point where concerns of containment issues are being discussed, but nonetheless they are surely stout. NWE Imports to the region continue but with the East vs West spread at $77 in July and reduced demand in Europe this has the majority of July imports arriving on LGCs and MGCs. The continual bidding at a small discount for an end July TOT cargo has not been filled. The physical premium continues to be assessed at a small premium, +$3.5/MT to July CIF ARA. The netback to Europe remains workable for US imports, the main concern has been reduced demand through the rapidly falling cracker run rates. CIF ARA butane has been stable for some time at 58% July Naphtha, and though it remains heavily discounted to Naphtha, and propane, demand for C4 remains low.

**MED**

 Bids for a fully flexible ratio mid-August VLGC FOB tender from Algeria were due today. Which makes sense in a July market which is set to import 500,000MT of LPG. 150,000/MT of this heading to Port Said and likely on through the Suez Canal. Leaving 350,000MT in the region, the lowest volume for over a year.
