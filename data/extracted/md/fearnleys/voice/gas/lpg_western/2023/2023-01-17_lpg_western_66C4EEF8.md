## Market Commentary

AMERICAS In spite of a recent warming trend across most of the U.S., demand levels from both the domestic and offshore markets have given cause for propane inventory levels to decline steadily for over a month, beginning in the 2nd week of December at 90.3mmbbls and concluding the 2nd week of Jan at 76.6mmbbls, according to the most recently issued EIA data. A decline of 2mmbbls was recorded for the period from 6th to 13th Jan, where most had been expecting a draw of around a million barrels (or so). Stocks now are over 130% of last year, and more than 128% of two years ago. There is at this point little threat that stocks will be depleted over the balance of the winter period – rather, there have been recent questions related to potential containment issues later in the summer. Meanwhile, the overarching trend seems poised to continue as forecasts are now calling for a mass of cold air from the Arctic to envelope most of the U.S. at end-Jan / early-Feb and, furthermore, demand for cargoes in Feb (and in Mar) has been strong. Terminal fees have spiked sharply and for Feb transactions jumped from ~6cts/gal earlier in the year to the low/mid-teens at present. March is divided due to backwardation, but asking levels are fully double-digits in 1H March, and firmly in the upper single-digits for 2H March. LatAm demand has been seen improving, as the aforementioned cold weather has infiltrated to the South, giving cause for increased consumption levels and, thus, higher demand. NWE The February/March backwardation continues to steepen hitting -$42pmt at the close Wednesday with the outright January CIF ARA price the highest since August 31st. Imports to the region in January are expected to be around 650,000MT. The majority of this volume has come from the US where fog delays and a large draw and strong Eastern demand, are contributing to the strength in European values. Sustained cold weather is being felt in Europe, adding to demand. Butane is another story as a CIF ARA butane sits at 85% balance January Naphtha and few demand signals are being seen.

**MED**

 Activity in the region has been subdued as local demand remains low. There have been some recent delays to loadings in Algeria due to storms, causing minimal additional buying as the catch up is set to be quick.
