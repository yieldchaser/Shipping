## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Having reached over 83cts/gal at mid-month, propane values at Mont Belvieu have receded to 80.50cpg as of this writing. Recent crude declines from the mid-$82/bbl levels to the low $77/bbl levels now have not offered support, and – curiously - neither has the most recent EIA data which revealed a 700,000bbl reduction in stock levels. As of 14 April US propane stock levels were recorded by the EIA at 55.50mmbbls, down from 56.20mmbbls after record-level export volumes were made known. On that note, exports from the US were reported at 2.511mmbbls/day this week, up from 2.395mmbbls/day last week. For reference, at the same time last year exports were listed at 2.428mmbbls/day. Propane inventories are now 150% of the levels reported in mid-April last year, and 137% of two years ago at the same time. Meanwhile, terminal fees on the Gulf Coast have relaxed and for a full propane cargo talk now is centered in the mid-single digits mark. Mixed-ratio opportunities are available below that, but little is being accomplished. Latin American demand is presently satisfied, with no known requirements as contractual volumes are considered sufficient and Southern Hemisphere winter-related demand has not yet begun to surface. NWE An early May TOT offer in the window at a small discount reflects the CIF ARA April/May contango. Adding weight to the discount is the import program for April, with 250,000MT concentrated in the last decade. Propane naphtha spread has come in slightly but at $154/MT petrochemical buying will continue to drive demand through the summer. With US exports at record levels importers in Europe are not concerned about supply this summer. With the arb open further down the curve, some looking to lock in tons at attractive levels and hedge. Butane remains long in the region, with values directly tracking April naphtha downward at 78.5%.

**MED**

 The butane lull is keeping activity low as US tons continue to be offered into the region. North west Europe is experiencing C4 length though strong frieght rates are keeping the product in the region. French refinery strikes continue to hamper operations at Lavera.
