## Market Commentary

AMERICAS The EIA this week posted an inventory gain of 700,000bbls when the market was anticipating something on the order of +2mmbbls (the 5-year average weekly build is 650,000bbls). However, exports this month have been impacted by a recent electrical outage which caused compressor failure at one terminal, and mandatory government inspections at another, leaving many with the impression that overall export volumes should have theoretically declined and thus allowing domestic stock levels to grow at a greater rate. But the EIA data shows that export volumes over the 38th week were as high as 2+mmbbls/day, which is well contrary to what had been anticipated. Overall propane inventories as of the week ending 22 Sep stand at 101.4mmbbls, or 122% of last year, and 139% of 2021. The market still generally expects that the high point will likely be around 105mmbbls in the coming weeks when the traditional ‘build’ season concludes and winter-related demand commences. The price at Mont Belvieu should likely have been negatively impacted by the lofty inventory levels, but crude has taken control and as it is now in the $92-93/bbl range so propane prices at MB have seen recent price gains to the ~74cts/gal level. Meanwhile, based on a rapidly declining arb, terminal fees are in retreat from the high point of 15+ cts/gal (for Nov) and cargoes – few that they are – are being offered at just above a dime (10cts/gal) but buyers have the upper single-digits in mind – if a vessel is available. NWE Europe continues to manage to temp US volumes as constraints to taking a cargoes East, such as Panama Canal delays and tight freight keep Europe relevant. The October East/West spread around $120/MT not including the physical discount in the window, October CIF ARA minus $8/MT while the Ginga window is at November FEI +2/MT. The price difference has not stopped Europe importing 500,000MT in September and October likely to exceed this, with 260,000MT set to arrive in the first half of the month. Butane is at 82% October Naphtha, a large rise in outright value, as crude oil and naphtha saw large gains. In line with the strong level set for October CP and for the expected seasonal rise in demand.

**MED**

 Butane in the West Med moved up into the 96% spot Naphtha, on the back of strong bidding for a Lavera FOB. Much more typical ratio for the time of year. US Butane continues to be imported into Morocco. CP being set at $600/MT propane and $615/MT butane will continue to see split ratio US cargoes head for the East Med market.
