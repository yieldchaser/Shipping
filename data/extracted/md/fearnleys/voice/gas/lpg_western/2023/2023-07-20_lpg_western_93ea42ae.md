## Market Commentary

AMERICAS North American propane fundamentals are largely unchanged, and – considering the time of year – rather expected from a seasonal perspective. Growing inventories and limited demand are the norm. But the propane-to-crude relationship, which dipped down to the very low 30% range has begun to show signs of life and now has found a bit of life at the mid-30 percentile range. This is still historically low, however. Stock levels were this week reported by the EIA to have risen by 900,000bbls for a total of 83.8mmbbls as of 14 July. The expected increase had earlier been pegged at well over 2mmbbls. The minimal build – and 600,000bbl draw in PADD III) can in part be attributed to the current heat wave that much of the US is experiencing, and which makes it difficult to achieve the low temps required to quickly load vessels. Terminal fees for Aug have struggled to leave the 5cpg range, but talk for 1H Sep is in the 6s and negotiations for 2H Sep likely start at 7cpg. Very little is being accomplished, though, as cargo avails are limited in Aug and presently appear likely remain so in Sep. LatAm supply / demand is deemed balanced in that winter-related requirements thus far do not exceed previously termed volumes. NWE The TOT propane window has been quiet all week. Butane on the other hand has seen consistent bidding by a French oil major, which has rapidly pushed value up to 76.5% July Naphtha values. Raising butane/naphtha values from -$243/MT a week ago to -$139/MT today. Petchem plants are being idled or going on maintenance throughout Europe as ethylene and propylene demand remains very low. Feedstock LPG intake in July will be 425,000MT up from 395,000MT in June down from a high 650,000MT in May. The arb to Europe from the US is very much open though still well below going East.

**MED**

 The regoin will imports 430,000MT of LPG from the US in August, which will be the lowest monthly volume since last August.
