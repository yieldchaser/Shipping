## Market Commentary

AMERICAS The resiliency of MB propane prices is remarkable given that in spite of this week’s EIA inventory data which revealed a 2.2mmbbbl draw, it brought overall US stock levels to 93.7mmbbls, or 107% of last year and 134% of two years ago. This is without mentioning that the weather outlook for North America through the balance of December is calling for generally un-winter-like temperatures and for only limited levels of precipitation. MB non-TET propane is today valued at ~67cpg, having dipped to around 65cpg in mid-Dec. Crude values are providing support, but WTI prices are effectively unchanged over the balance of December, and propane export volumes are recorded this week at 1.812mmbbls/day – which is up from last week, but off from the more than 2mmbbls/day reported earlier in the month, and overall volumes are not expected to see a surge of any kind through the end of ’23. Meanwhile, terminal fees were seen at 7.50cpg for end-Dec – and while 1H Jan has seen little (or no) activity, 2H Jan values are off just a bit, with offers presumably in the 7cpg range, and lower for a mixed-ratio parcel. Otherwise, the ongoing issues at the Panama Canal and, more recently, in the Red Sea, have conspired to force overwater volumes to the longer, more expensive Cape of Good Hope route, which – when paired with a generally weak Asian market - has made for a difficult trading period and forced some participants to the sidelines. NWE Few VLGCs will go East via Suez due to the risk of attack reducing the number of large cargo offers into the region, by traders hoping to get their vessels back promptly. Import volume for December is expected to be around 370,000 MT, low for the time of year, with January import volumes projected to be similar. Though TOT bid for a mid-Jan delivery is supporting the market somewhat, the physical tons remain discounted, assessed at January CIF ARA -$2/MT. A repeated large cargo butane offer in the window has pushed values down from 92.5% balance December Naphtha to 82.5% today.

**MED**

 Higher than average temperatures in the region are keeping demand in check. The reduction of VLGCs transiting the region due to the risk of attack in Suez will see less transitory offers.
