## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Terminal fees for full propane cargoes from the US continue in the mid teens and show little sign of abating in the face of a now $300/mt arb to the East. Some have recently predicted that the arb will rise to as high as $315/mt, which only bolsters the notion that terminal fees might be maintained at current levels well in to Q4. Mixed ratio cargoes are believed available at a modest discount to all-propane cargoes, but there is little demand at present for anything other than 4:0 VLs. And while the arb is wholly robust, so are freight rates. In fact, the Hou/Chi route has today been established by the Baltic Index at $220/mt. Overall export volumes are generally steady, with 1.694mmbbls/day recorded by the EIA last week, and 1.547mmbbls in its most recent report, that for the week ending 01 Sep. As for inventories, the EIA reported a 500,000bbl week-over-week inventory increase for a current overall total of 96mmbbls. This is nearly 130% of the same time last year – week 35 – and nearly 137% over two years ago at the same time. This took the market by surprise as most had calculated an estimated 2.3mmbbl increase. That estimate was based on historical averages, current petrochemical consumption levels and export volumes over the past week. Over the past 5 years the average build for week 35 was 2.394mmbbls. NWE The region is finding it hard to attract trans-Atlantic VLGCs as netback values stand 5cpg below the East. The low demand environment due to reduced petchem run rates, is making it possible for traders to fill European demand on smaller size vessels. The October Propane vs Naphtha spread of $103/MT keeps propane the favoured feedstock, a bounce back from below $100/MT earlier this week. A TOT bid for end September arrival continues to be shown, with assessed value at September CIF ARA +$2.5/MT. The butane market has been quiet this week, keeping assessed value 83.5% September Naphtha.

**MED**

 Turkey imprted a record 418,000MT LPG in August. Another 400,000MT of LPG on VLGCs is aimed at the region in September already, although it is difficult to predict if the volume will stay in the region or head East. The Panama Canal delays and auction costs above $2million USD are pushing VLGCs through Suez on both laden and ballast legs.
