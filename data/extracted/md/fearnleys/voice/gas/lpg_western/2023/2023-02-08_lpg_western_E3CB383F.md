## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Netbacks remain robust - particularly from Asia - and have well supported strong activity levels. There is rising concern, however, that the combination of winter’s end and the virtual armada of LPG vessels currently headed East that the hurried pace seen so far this year could begin to falter. The most recent transactions have been seen as low as 9cpg and as high as 14cpg, with current bid/ask levels ranging approximately from 11-14cpg, depending on timing, location, ratio, etc. The bulk of March trades have likely been concluded, though a couple of interested parties remain. The EIA this week revealed a 4.3mmbbl draw in propane stocks, down from 73.20mmbbls last week to an overall total on 3 Feb of 68.90mmbbls. Current stocks are 144% of last year, and 134% of two years ago, giving cause for talk of historical high inventory levels. In the first week of Feb 2020 stocks were 4% higher than now at 71.82mmbbls, and likewise, early Feb 2016 totals were also 4% higher, which somewhat alleviates what are likely premature concerns as to brine containment later in the season. Those issues have arisen in light of strong domestic US production levels which are expected to continue for the foreseeable. NWE Weakness dominated CIF ARA last week, but that all changed this week after larger than expected US inventory draw saw Mt Belvieu and in turn CIF ARA spring back to life. The bid/offer spread in the TOT window was very wide, the bid equating to a flat price of $602pmt and an offer for the same dates at $800pmt. Unlikely to come together, even using Fearnleys! Trans-Atlantic cargo flows continue though the majority are term deliveries as any US spot cargoes in February and March cargoes have been snapped up to head East. The recent fall of European natural gas prices, as stocks remain high and winter cold but not extreme, will lead to an increase in LPG production from the North Sea. LPG production was reduced by around 35% when LPG was being spiked into natural gas. Butane remains assessed at 97.5% February Naphtha, with no window activity.

**MED**

 The arb to the region from the US is attractive, though no prompt cargoes are available to make the most of this opportunity. Dortyol down in the East Med reduces demand by a 2+2 VLGC every 10 days, though is not expected to remain down for long.
