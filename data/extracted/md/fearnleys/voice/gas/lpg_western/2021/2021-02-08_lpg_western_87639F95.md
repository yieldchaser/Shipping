## Market Commentary

AMERICAS The current US propane inventory situation can be likened to travelling down the highway in a car with a flat tire, a fuel gauge nearing empty and a dirty wind screen: Something will soon need to change. Speculation as to the US propane inventory data release this week was enthusiastic, but the matter has been settled (for now, at least) as a stock decline of 4.508mmbbls was reported by the EIA Wednesday morning and brought the total US propane inventory level to 51.545mmbbls as of 10 Feb 2021. Last year at the same time there were 71.822mmbbls in stocks, or a 28% decline YOY. Domestic production was off by 38,000bbls/day to 2.37mmbls/day while Res/Com demand grew by 714,000bbls/day to 2.204mmbbls/day. It has been cold, after all, and weather forecasts over the next week or so are calling for extreme temperatures over much of the nation. Exports (both overland and waterborne) of ~1.1mmbbls/day were recorded and seem to have been negatively impacted by a recent spate of fog along the upper Texas Gulf Coast. The weekly export average is around the 1.6mmbbl/day range. In turn, the Days of Supply has fallen by 4.5 days to 15.6 days. This is encroaching on historical lows, where the highest level recorded was in the 60-day range. PADD III (Gulf Coast) stock levels fell this week by 1.726mmbbls to 28.797mmbbls. This is significant, and additional attention should be paid any time the 30mmbbl level is breached. PADD III stocks have fallen this year by an average of 2mmbbls per week. At that rate – and in consideration of the massively cold weather at present – it is easy to calculate that Gulf Coast inventories will in early March be at (or near) 5-year lows. As of midday Wednesday, however, the Mont Belvieu market showed little, if any, reaction. Belvieu yawned, in fact, making the situation uncertain and unclear.
