## Market Commentary

NWE The lower than anticipated US inventory build pushed up Mt Belvieu propane and as a consequence CIF ARA propane prices. This has pushed propane/naphtha in NWE from -$2pmt Tuesday to Wednesday’s close of +$20pmt. Some petchem buying interest is said to remain due to traders hedging pro/nap at a a favorable spread. That said crackers are not running at capacity at the moment. CIF ARA butane has remained steady at 106% naphtha, the market is tight but quiet. The market has not followed the usual seasonal trends, LPG has been expensive throughout the summer. Soon weather related and gasoline blending demand will start to be seen. So the question for many European traders is when to start buying for winter?

**MED**

 Maintenance at Mohammedia storage which has taken out 80KT of it’s 200KT capacity has not had an effect on VLGC imports to the region. It is yet to be seen if discharge times will increase, or if stocks can be managed effectively. The rest of the region remains tight though there is very little interest in bringing in from the US as Mt Belvieu butane saw a $50pmt increase post US inventories being posted Wednesday. AMERICAS Mont Belvieu propane values continue to tick up on a daily basis, primarily on the back of concerns that inventory levels in the U.S. will be insufficient for the combined demand that is anticipated from the petchem sector, the winter period and – above all – overwater exports. Current valuations for non-LST Sep propane are in the $1.17/gal range. October bids were most recently seen at $1.20/gal. The EIA this week reported a 600,000bbl build in overall stock levels, but a draw of the same volume occurred in PADD III, the Gulf Coast – where the bulk of the exports occur. The overall total as of late Aug is ~73% of last year, and whether or not stock totals can make it to the desired levels is an openly discussed concern. There remain between 4-6 weeks in the traditional ‘build’ season and so much will be determined over the next month. Terminal fees remain tiered, with re-sellers offering at (or perhaps just below) a nickel and buyers – considering current economics – at levels well below that. The terminal owners/operators themselves are generally at yet another, higher level – particularly for full propane cargoes.
