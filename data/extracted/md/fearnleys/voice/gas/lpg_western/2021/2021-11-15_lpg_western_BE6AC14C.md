## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Massive volatility has overwhelmed the market this week, both prior to and after the EIA inventory numbers were released on Wednesday. Mont Belvieu propane closed Monday at $1.22/gal, saw massive inter-day price swings all day Tuesday and closed at $1.21/gal, then finally went a bit calm prior to the stock data Wednesday morning but then fell sharply in afternoon trade, only to rebound again and close at $1.19/gal. By Thursday morning $1.10/gal was seen in early activity, but then rebounded to $1.14/gal as of this writing. As of 12 Nov 2021 overall US propane stock levels were listed at 74.6mmbbls by the EIA, down by 200,000bbls from the week prior. This took some by surprise as a decline of some 1.0-1.5mmbbls was generally anticipated. PADD I built by a half-million barrels while Gulf coast stocks fell by 400,000bbls – not altogether surprising since export volumes are believed to be back at robust levels. Propane inventories in the US are now 80+% of last year and nearly 84% of two-years ago. All the while, US terminal fees have seen their fair share of volatility, though there remains a multi-tiered structure where the terminals themselves have purposefully priced themselves out. Re-sellers have been seen offering various ratio cargoes at between 7cts and 9cts/gal, though availability is becoming thinner through the balance of 2021. NWE The bids in the TOT window are not being hit, even it is thought 4 VLGCs 2 LGCs and an MG are expected to arrive in the region between end of November and early December. The bids have been shown at a premium to Naphtha values or at fixed prices. This week’s price swings have led to caution form traders and a fixed price is seen as an easier risk to manage. It is possible that VLGCs could consider re-routing to Asia if the East/West spread continues to widen from December +$61pmt. This uncertainty is keeping the arb to the region wide and the netback competitive. The Propane Naphtha spread has come in this week leading some traders to expect PDH buying to pick up and dramatically increase the regions demand. A butane offer in the window for early December jolted the quiet market and has just pushed values down from 110% to 105% Naphtha today on the lack of buying interest.

**MED**

 A full propane VLGC loaded Houston is heading to the region. The Butane arb from the US is open though no new cargoes have been heard making this trade. Morocco is yet to show additional demand above their existing term contracts. Though the arb is wide milder weather is yet to kickstart the Mediterranean market.
