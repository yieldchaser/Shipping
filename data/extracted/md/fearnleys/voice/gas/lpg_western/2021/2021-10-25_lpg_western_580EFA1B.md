## Market Commentary

AMERICAS The overall U.S. propane inventory build of 2.1mmbbls as reported by the EIA Wednesday for the week ending 22 Oct was welcome news and appears to have had the anticipated impact, at least sentimentally. The increase pushes stock levels to 75.7mmbbls, or nearly 77% of the levels achieved last year. The bulk of the build occurred in PADD III (Gulf Coast) where export volumes primarily occur. A typical month generally sees approximately 90 liftings, but total Oct volumes are expected to be down to around 75 liftings due to a host of buybacks, fog, and perhaps a bit more butane liftings (versus full-propane liftings). Meanwhile, as winter begins to approach, terminal fees have seen a bit of buoyancy as Nov and Dec resellers are seen at just above the 6cts/gal mark. The terminals themselves are around a penny above that, while Enterprise continues with its buyback scheme into Nov and Dec. Mont Belvieu propane values have steadily relaxed over the previous 10 days, having moved from 1.49/gal on 18 Oct to a current value of 1.36/gal today. NWE The TOT window went quiet Wednesday. After another build in Mt Belvieu inventories signalled to traders that there will be enough product to supply the North West European market in November. The VLGC being offered in the window was also withdrawn. No surprise with the East / West spread at +$51pmt. CIF AFA tracked the drop in Brent crude, leaving the propane naphtha spread at +$38/mt for November. The pro/nap spread remains in positive territory for Q1 2022. This combined with expensive natural gas for petchem’s power generation has traders questioning when demand from the sector will pick up. The inland propane market is picking up with trades for railcars occurring at strong premiums. Winter weather in the region is yet to bear it’s teeth, and the builds in Mt Belvieu have had a calming effect on the European market.

**MED**

 The coaster market in November was busy this week strong buying interest. This did not affect butane values in the region which slid lower with crude and naphtha. Large cargo discussions have moved on to December arrivals.
