## Market Commentary

NWE May propane vs naphtha remains above $100pmt which will keep cracking demand to a maximum. The pro/nap spread remains in triple digits until August. Many of the large cargo traders in NWE are covered for May.

**North Sea**

 gasfield maintenance is set to end June 3rd which would bring local production back. A number of traders are waiting for maintenance to end before buying additional tons int he hope that the CIF ARA will come off. The narrowing arb from the US is also keeping European traders quiet for the time being. This has been reflected in the TOT window which is assessed at CIF ARA +$7pmt for May, down from +$13pmt a week ago.

**MED**

 The sentiment was weaker in the West Med though butane was steady vs Naphtha. Demand levels were lower than anticipated. Though this has yet to be reflected in regional prices, butane was steady and propane showed some strength. AMERICAS Crude oil prices were down early this week as the API were forecasting an increase in crude oil stocks, while Mont Belvieu propane values continued to drift on the belief that domestic stock levels will remain sufficient. Looking towards the summer, the domestic US forward propane pricing curve is effectively flat through September. This is primarily due to the belief that crude oil production will continue to be healthy and promote production levels, which will need to exceed the combined demand levels of the North American res/com market, the US petrochemical sector and – most importantly – waterborne export markets which – according to the EIA – are at well-above average rates of 1.7mmbbls/day. In fact for this month, April 2021 we expect a record of 90 vessels loading. Meanwhile, the EIA this week were expected to report that propane stocks in the US built by something under a million barrels as of the week ending 16 April. It reported, however, a 74,000bbl draw – which included a half-million barrel decline in PADD 3 stocks. Total overall propane inventories as of 16 April are now 40.5mmbbls, which compares to last year’s 57.4mmbbls and the 2019 total of 51.8mmbbls. All the while a spate of petchem-related buying in Europe has kept the US export market moving at a robust clip as US terminal fees recently dipped to levels in the low 4ct/gal range for end-May, but are sharply higher for June volumes at asking levels of 6ct/gal.
