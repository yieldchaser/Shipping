## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Despite ongoing gains in the crude arena which have brought Brent to $84.06/bbl and WTI to $81.31/bbl, MB propane values earlier this week seemed relaxed to around $1.42/gal, only to rise again prior to the release of the EIA to current levels of $145.00/gal which was fixed just before the inventory data release. Expectations for this week’s inventory report – which was delayed by a US holiday on Monday – again ranged widely (and revealing ongoing uncertainty), from a draw of around a million barrels to a build of over a million barrels. Given the strong export volumes and pending winter demand season, the prospect of a draw seemed the more likely, and that turned out to be the case. For the week ending 8 Oct 2021, the EIA reported overall propane stock levels in the U.S. at 71.7mmbbls, which represents a draw of 600,000bbls. The components to that include a draw of 800,000bbls in PADD I and the build in PADD III of 300,000bbls. As there is little in the cold weather and ongoing strength in the export market this was eyed with some level of surprise. Terminal fees, meanwhile, though still generally range-bound, have inched up and offers for 2H November 4:0 cargoes are seen at 6cts/gal – December at the same. Buy-side interest remains limited. NWE The VLGC cargoes which were slated for the region have redirected East. Buying interest in the window did compete with the seller’s netback taking the cargo East. The window bid seen this week has pushed up physical propane value to November CIF ARA +$5. Although the European market is tight the news did not affect CIF ARA which fell this week in line with other commodities. Though inland demand has been picking up, cold temperatures are yet to hit the region. The volume of propane set to be exported in October and November from the US East Coast is down on September due to regional demand. These volumes usually find their way to the European market, further tightening the NWE outlook in Q4. Butane has been directly correlated to naphtha at 105% October.
