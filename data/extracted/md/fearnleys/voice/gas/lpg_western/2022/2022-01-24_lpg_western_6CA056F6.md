## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Continued cold weather for the Northern half of the Country has continued through January and is now forecast for the first half of February as well. This will continue to put pressure on the domestic inventories, however for now, the price continues to follow WTI and is not showing a premium. The wind went out of the sails, on the theory of there not being enough product to support large exports and weather demand, due to increased production forecasts for 2022, but it will be interesting to see if this changes if the cold weather lasts into the second half of Feb. This week propane inventories registered a seasonal and expected draw of 4.6 million barrels and we are expecting a similar number next week. Cargo FOB in the US Gulf are trading either side of 5 cpg for March with buyers ideas lower and sellers ideas higher. NWE Demand in the region is fairly balanced even as crude prices worked to firm LPG flat price. Europe remains well supplied for the balance of the January and into Feb as mild weather has done little to further demand. Chemical demand is moderate and propane's widening weakness in relation to naphtha could further insight the demand to the region for cracking. Further still, Dow plans to restart their ethylene cracker in Terneuzen by the end of the week. Struggles at the terminal began at the beginning of December, due to an issue with steam supply, but it is likely facility capable of absorbing 1.8 million tons / year should be back on line shortly. That said, weak ethylene margins could potentially act as a point of friction in the equation. As it stands, the propane naphtha spread sits at $100 pmt in the front month furthering the delta in favor of propane. Natural gas prices have softened, it has not been to a degree to absolve LPG from internal refineries where its being used as a cheaper alternative.

**MED**

 On paper, the appetite for LPG appears to be on the rise in the Med again, butane prices into Lavera saw double digit gains, along with an uptick in Moroccan demand, however no public cargos have changed hands yet.
