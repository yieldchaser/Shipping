## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Fears of recession and US governmental fuel-tax relief ambitions have caused crude to tumble, though propane has yet to react at corresponding rates. As such, the propane-to-crude ratio has increased from the low/mid 40-percentile range to 47% at present. Current Mont Belvieu propane for July is pegged at 120.25cpg while WTI is off by $2.25/bbl at $103.80/bbl. Wednesday’s WTI market closed at $106.19/bbl. Propane arbs, meanwhile, heavily favor the Asian markets, but buy-side interest does not appear to have fully materialized as Middle East export volumes appear to be satisfying demand levels there. The EIA propane inventory data was expected to have been released today (having been delayed due to the newly established “Juneteenth” holiday which was observed on Monday) but a systems issue has reportedly caused the EIA to postpone until next week at the usual Wednesday 9.30am CST time. Lastly, terminal fees crested earlier in the month in the low/mid 6s for July business but have receded for late-July / early August transactions to the mid 4cpg range. NWE Record imports to the region has closed the trans-Atlantic arb. With butane storages full and looking long in the region. Propane is looking long, with early July being offered in the window with any bids being shown in reply. Retail demand for LPG is low as to be expected with much of Europe experiencing temperatures of over 30 degrees. The petchem sector looks well supplied through 1H July and opportunistic sellers hoping to place spot tons in the market are getting worried, as record imports coincide with seasonally lower demand.

**MED**

 The East Med is long due to Yanbu butane coming into the region. While the west Med has been well supplied from the US, during this period of low seasonal demand. Additionally weakness in physical naphtha is further reducing butane value.
