## Market Commentary

AMERICAS An eventful week across the board as a number of market participants ascended on Miami as the year comes to a close, for a discussion on what to expect in the coming months. The answer. Uncertainty. While optimistic regarding recovering global demand, the timeline is very much up for debate. Freight too, has been the topic of uncertainty, as traders try to find direction while weighing the near 50 new builds coming to market during 2023, with growing exports and operational delays, that cut into availability. In the spot market, December FEI/LST arbitrage traded as wide as $231.00/ton on Thursday, but higher freight costs and Panama Canal delays have taken away the margins keeping speculative buying at bay. Presently, buyers' indications are in the mid 5cpg range, and a bit higher at Marcus Hook, while sellers are offering closer to 6.5cpg for December cargos. In Panama, delays stand at 10 days for southbound travel, and 19 north, which coupled with sky high freight can prove exceptionally costly for traders. On a Houston/ Chiba basis, freight was heard done as high as 192pmt earlier in the week but has since stabilized in mid to high 180's. On Wednesday, the EIA reported the first inventory draw of the season- showing stocks down approx. 300,000 barrels, where they now stand at 87 million barrels. The US Gulf accounted for the majority of the draw, 653,000 barrels, Conjoining with a draw of 404,000 barrels in the US East Coast (PADD1) . However, a large build in the midcontinent of 700,000 barrels mitigated a greater strain on stored barrels. The attitude around inventories is relaxed as the Gulf Coast levels are still 35 percent higher compared to this time last year. Exports were more or less flat week on week, totaling 1.4million barrels / day. While production saw a marginal decrease to about 2.5million barrels/ day. The draw, though surprising to the market, did little on the price of propane at Mt.Beliveu, which ended the session up only 0.25cpg on the day. That said, a forecast of Cold weather, not yet seen, in coming weeks, could kick off the price activity more typically related with winter in the US. NWE Multiple physical window bids and offers have been shown this week both on propane and butane. This pushed propane values to November CIF ARA +$6pmt, the strongest values since mid-July. Strong butane demand for blending is being seen in the region. With butane values at 94.25% November Naphtha, naphtha also being strong. The arb from the US has been improving, but with December East / West spread at +$97pmt all tons should go East. Although the potential of long waiting times at the Panama Canal or expensive auction fee to get through with reduced waiting has some traders looking to the West to stop their vessels being tied up for too long. The CFR market is active and will continue to be as freight is expensive and scarce making harder for traders to look FOB from the US and find freight.

**MED**

 Butane demand in the region is rising, though more to compete with demand from Northern Europe. As wintery weather is yet to be seen.
