## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Volatility has overtaken the crude oil markets which have been reeling from fears of a deepening global economic recession (not to mention a strengthening U.S. Dollar), the former causing governments to sharply raise interest rates in a bid to slow the economy. As such, WTI saw highs of over $122/bbl in June, but new lows were posted this week at $98.53/bbl. Correspondingly, the MB propane market has reacted, not only in terms of the flat price but as it relates to WTI on a percentage basis. Propane at Mont Belvieu is currently valued at ~$1.14/gal, or about 48% of WTI, where at one point the relationship had dipped in to the low 40 percent range. The EIA today released its findings on US propane stocks which, for the first time in some weeks, was expected to reveal a more accurate set of data. Most in the market were expecting a build but expectations ranged widely from under 1mmbbls to over 3mmbbls. As of 01 July 2022, however, the Overall Total propane stock level in the US is 54.9mmbbls, or 95% of last year, but 70% of two years ago. Terminal fees were earlier in June seen fixed in the upper 5cpg range, but buyers retreated to the low 4s until recently when the market turned and two separate transactions are widely believed to have been fixed for August at 5cpg. 2H 2022 values are at/above 6cpg. NWE Butane Storages in the region remains full and multiple cargoes are looking for homes with no luck so far. In the propane markets, Europe is on track to import close to 625,000 tons In July, (a new record high) , prompting a handful of unmet window offers, and a decidedly weak market sentiment to begin the short week. That said, LPG Avails from the North Sea will likely tighten further still, as liquids continue to be re-injected into the Nat Gas stream.

**MED**

 Butane is long in the region as traders look to the region to release some of the North West European butane pressure. Propane in the med appears long for the time being, as demand has quelled per usual at this point in the year. Sonatrach postings for July of $700 propane and $650 butane, will have done little to spur demand in the region. Demand above term supplies is not being seen.
