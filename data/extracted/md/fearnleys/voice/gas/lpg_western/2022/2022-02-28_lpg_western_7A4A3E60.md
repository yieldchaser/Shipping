## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Volatility has been the primary characteristic of the domestic energy markets this week, having been spurred on by events in eastern Europe, rising crude values, softening freight and widening arbs to both the East and the West. The result is that propane prices at Mont Belvieu rose by more than 12cts/gal over just this week to $1.53/gal. There remains, however, quite steep backwardation which extends well in to 2H 2022 and then in to the upper $1.20’s/gal. Meanwhile, terminal fees this week spiked hard and the few remaining March cargoes are known to have been fixed in the double-digits. Motivated buyers, sensing stronger demand in Europe because of the Russian advancements into Ukraine, emerged for March and 1H Apr volumes. It is anticipated that around 175,000mt per month of LPG formerly supplied by Russia requires replacement, and hence the enthusiasm. With regards the inventory levels of propane in the U.S., the EIA revealed an 800,000bbl overall weekly reduction which brought stock levels to 37.2mmbbls. The market took that in stride and essentially shrugged off the large decline in PADD 2 but pointed to the week-over-week increase in PADD III, the Gulf Coast, which saw a build of 900,000bbls. Cynics were prepared to wait for next week’s release for a ‘truer’ stock picture, as many believe there have been data errors in recent reports. NWE As the European LPG market tries to come to grips with the effects of the Russia-Ukraine conflict, supply concerns have come to the fore in an already tight market. This led to a scramble from European traders to find US FOBs as transatlantic March imports were expected to be around 120,000MT far below the 460,000MT imported in February. With FOB US bids rising on European trader interest March cargoes surfaced and most believe these additional exports were only made possible by the lower than expected draw in US inventories. At least 3 additional VLGCs were purchased for the European market which will calm some traders for end March and 1H April. The prompt market is short with the TOT window premium valued at March CIF ARA +$18pmt. The butane market is valued at 97% March Naphtha which is a strong value in this market and only for March tons, as the end of the month ends the gasoline blending season.

**MED**

 Butane values have hit eight-year highs, though offers are seen in the market but no bids. A butane heavy cargo from North West Europe is signalling arrival in Egypt in April. This week mixed ratio VLGC left the USGC bound for Turkey. Imports or high prices keeping bidders quiet.
