## Market Commentary

AMERICAS U.S. propane inventory levels this week increased by 1.1mmbbls for an overall total of 85.50mmbbls as of 07 Oct and pushing stocks to nearly 120% of last year, per the EIA. There are quite clearly sufficient volumes in tank to pose little threat to any domestic weather-related requirements this winter and to keep the exports rolling. Meanwhile, in the face of healthy freight rates terminal fees have seen an uplift and where before the bottom end of the range was in the 3cpg range, now the lower end of the range is in the 4cpg range. Sellers, meanwhile, have adjusted their ideas from the low/mid 4s to the 5 cpg range. Through it all, activity levels are still subdued but sentiment seems to be improving despite only modest temperature declines and very little crop drying demand. A Chilean tender was released overnight which calls for a propane-only LGC or a VLGC to be delivered from 5-12 December, perhaps on a 2-disport basis. Offers are due by 1pm CST on 13 Oct with the results anticipated by 10am CST Friday 14 Oct. NWE A deal in the TOT window was booked Wednesday setting the end October and early November levels at CIF ARA minus $2pmt. This was the third TOT deal in as many weeks, and we do not expect the market to slow down. Netback values west are at parity with the East, and with the Panama canal delays lengthening to 10 North and 9 days Southbound the additional cost with going East has many traders looking to place their cargoes in the West. Importers have been waiting to see clear signs of additional European demand, or to be offered something which fits their needs. So the arb is wide open and the market looks tight, but counterintuitively, it is a buyers’ market. European naphtha has taken a hit which has pushed the Propane / Naphtha spread to $93pmt down from above $100pmt earlier in the week. Large cargo butane remains at a strong 93.5% of October Naphtha, though French refinery strikes are likely to weaken these levels.

**MED**

 French strikes have dampened the mood in the West Med. The effects of these on the waterborne LPG market are yet to be seen on the market, as a butane offer from Lavera was withdrawn and no other bids or offers have been seen. The East Med remains quiet as additional winter demand is yet to be seen.
