## Market Commentary

AMERICAS A week-over-week propane stock build of 1.8mmbbls was recorded by the EIA as of 20 May, which puts this year’s inventory levels at 105% of the same time last year – though they remain below 70% of May 2020. The build was smaller than anticipated, but easily explained by the robust 1.6mmbbls/day export levels. Most are now anticipating that domestic US propane stock levels will reach “acceptable” levels by end Sep, and likely continue to build well into Oct. The NOAA, however, just issued its 2022 Hurricane Season forecast and is calling for 21 named storms and up to six major hurricanes this season. A typical year sees around 14 named storms and up to 3 major hurricanes. Depending on how things ultimately develop, both domestic production levels and export volumes could potentially be impacted, which would then affect overall stock levels. Meanwhile, terminal fees now seem a bit more fragile than in 1H May when levels of up to the mid 6cpg range were achieved. At present there have just emerged early signs of retreat, particularly from the buy-side, which was talking 5s for near-term business. NWE As it stands, June CIF ARA propane sits at $722/t, marking a negligible uptick on the day on the back of upstream brent utilization for diesel. However, the region appears balanced, rather than tight, and it is unlikely many new spot purchases will be made to meet new demand. While natural gas' premium over LPG would suggest an incentive to source it as an alternative fuel , the unwinding of seasonal demand and adequate refinery runs, coupled with healthy liftings from Marcus Hook, make for an agnostic attitude for NWE LPG end users.

**MED**

 The region has been well supplied by US cargoes in the past month due to the open butane arb. Supply to the region has been satisfied through the end of the month, with the only bright spot for demand being Turkey due to the high LPG vs Gasoline spread for autogas.
