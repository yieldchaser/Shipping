## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Having risen with crude, which was following the unfolding news of the Russian invasion of Ukraine, the MB propane market reached a peak of $1.63/gal on 8th March, only to then sink to the low $1.20’s/gal by mid-month. At that point the market turned again to the upside and was trading on an upwards trajectory towards $1.48/gal, only to reverse course today to current levels of $1.45/gal. In the background the EIA report for the week ending 18 March showed a modest build of 300,000bbls for an overall total of 33.6mmbbls (thus signaling the conclusion of winter), and which is 81% of the total achieved last year, and 56% of the total recorded two years ago. The small rise in stock levels is attributed to three factors: The decline of weather-related demand in North America, cargo buy-backs in March, and cargo cancellations in March (which when combined are reckoned at around a dozen or more cargoes). Domestic U.S. production, though healthy at 2.36mmbbls/day, is effectively flat on a week-over-week basis, but as well on a year-over-year basis. As to the trade, the steep backwardation continues well in to Q2. While March is effectively concluded, April European netbacks are more than $20/mt below March, and May is at least $10/mt below that. Thereon, the summertime netbacks flatten out, but as with all things LPG, crude is the ultimate driver and at this moment the headlines are the primary influence. Buy/sell terminal fee ideas, meanwhile, had at one point widened from the low/mid 5cpg range to as much as 12cpg during the height of the recent volatility, but have for now settled in a more modest range of 6-8cpg, generally speaking. NWE April CIF ARA is showing strength as the March/April backwardation comes out of the market slightly. A bid in the TOT window also increased the physical premium assessed at April CIF ARA +$33pmt. Though this is for very early April arrival rather than whole month. The arb from the US remains open and if you take into account the physical premium Northwest Europe should be drawing tons from the US. A sever with the European market the worry is that multiple cargoes will head to the region and very quickly shift the tight market into an oversupplied one. The issue has been finding US cargoes in the second half of March for arrival in early April. These cargoes were worked well ahead of time and to draw them to the region now would need them to be redirected from heading East, not impossible with the East not showing much strength. April butane also remains valued at a high level at 92% April Naphtha, though this is approached with caution due to lack of April butane activity so far.

**MED**

 The expected ramp up of buying pre-Ramadan has not materialised. VLGCs have been stuck in Moroccan ports causing delays since early March. The Chinook is now discharging C4 having arrived at Mohammedia on February 27th. This is now starting to affect coaster discharges into Morocco and is keeping the butane market in the region subdued.
