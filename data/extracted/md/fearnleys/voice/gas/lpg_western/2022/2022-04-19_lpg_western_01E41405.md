## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Bouyancy in the crude arena continues supporting LPG markets and propane values at Mont Belvieu remain above $1.30/gal for May. WTI as of this writing is up 2.5% at $104.63/bbl but largely dependent upon both the events related to the Russia/Ukraine war, but also Covid-related shutdowns, particularly in China. From the domestic US perspective, the EIA data released yesterday showed a build of 1.6mmbbls – just a bit more than anticipated – to an overall total of 37mmbbls, which is 91% of last year but 62% of two years ago. Stocks built on the Gulf Coast (PADD III) but showed a decline in both the East Coast and Midwest regions (PADDs I and II). Due to the lack of cancellations and ‘buyback’ cargoes, ongoing and robust export volumes in April were expected to have limited the build in Gulf Coast stocks, but instead the decline in domestic demand is being cited as the explanation. Terminal fees, meanwhile, range rather widely as buy-side ideas remain in the 4cpg area but sellers seem to content in the 6 cpg range. Reduced arbs and higher freight rates are contributing factors, as well. NWE The arb to the region has narrowed bringing trans-Atlantic netbacks into the high 3cpgs or low 4cpgs. The build in US inventories did not have an immediate bearish effect on MT Belvieu values and any moves were negated by strong freight. Houston-Flushing is now heard to be nearing $70pmts with no sign of coming off for May loadings. Propane vs Naphtha in the region continues to heavily favour propane with April, May and June Pro/Nap in the $140pmts. Though this figure has to be taken in context, with Natural Gas at $25mmBTU, making power expensive, and reduced demand for manufacturing due to supply chain issues clouding the picture. The spring weather in Europe has been very pleasant this year, so any additional inland demand has been due to larger geopolitical issues and crude oil price moves. Demand in the region remains and though urgent buying has not been seen since returning from Easter holidays.

**MED**

 The Med continues to be tight, butane in particular hard to find in the region. The arb from the US to the Med is open which will release some supply pressure as vessels STS into coasters in the region.
