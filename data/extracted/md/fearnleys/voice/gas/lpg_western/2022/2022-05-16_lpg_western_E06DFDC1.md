## Market Commentary

AMERICAS A surprise EIA report this week showed an unexpected and very modest 300,000bbl build for the reporting period ending 13 May, dashing hopes that a larger build would compel the Mont Belvieu pricing structure to move lower and allow for a widening of the arb. The market had been anticipating anywhere from a 1.0-3.0mmbbl improvement in stock levels (though most were clustered in the +1.9mmbbl range), but it would appear as though corrective data was utilized this week as the accuracy of the previous week’s data was considered rather dubious. Ultimately crude oil softness did allow propane to weaken, closing the day at $1.20/gal, off by nearly 2cts/gal from the day prior. At this point propane has fallen to 47% of WTI, having been sliding since Oct 2021. Terminal fees in the U.S. have been slowly rising, having been in the low 4cpg range only a couple of weeks ago and in the upper 4s/lower 5s range last week, only to eclipse the 6cpg level this week. Asking levels are now at least 6.5cpg, with some priced even higher, still. NWE The European prices lost ground after crude slid this week, this coupled with the less than inspiring US inventory build has narrowed the arb and hurt netbacks in this strong freight environment. That said in European summer the market tends to be driven by petchem demand. We continue to see crackers return from spring maintenance and fill demand from the US both FOB and CFR. With Pro/Nap at $149pmt petchem demand will continue to be at maximum. On the butane side not many tons have been openly traded which has left estimated values at 97% May Naphtha. This is seen by many as above true market value, and shows a wide open arb from the US which has many traders who would normally head East with 2+2 cargoes offering CFR in Europe. So far none of these trades have been heard done and without window deals the levels have not seen a correction. For the time being Europe is seeing good demand and though much of the prompt looks to be accounted for, we expect to see European petchems looking to buy CFR cargoes for June arrival.

**MED**

 There is a distinctly summer market feel in the region. With no new demand being shown the market is subdued although the arb is open. Much of the spot demand has been filled by US cargoes. LPG – Gasoline arb is making Autogas favorable in the East Med.
