## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Continued cold weather and increased exports are finally taking their toll on US inventories. Since last Summer, many warned that US inventories could not support exports and cold weather, and now those forecasts seem to be coming true. This week’s inventory report showed a draw of 3.9 million barrels bringing the two week total draw to almost 10 million barrels. Another large draw next week will really get alarm bells ringing. A large gathering of the domestic players met in San Diego this week for the OPIS Conference and whereby most agreed propane production was increasing, the amount was a matter of debate. We expect propane production to increase by 170,000 barrels however there are many expecting the number to be closer to 100,000 barrels per day. NWE The market is dominated by uncertainty and news of the Russian invasion. The European market is tight, but the arb remains closed from the US, and TOT window premiums are at $18.50pmt but so far have only sufficient to draw on cargo across the Atlantic in March. Europe will need to draw US cargoes destined for the Far East if the market is going to be able to fill current demand. With the March East vs West spread at +$53pmt this is not impossible. March/April backwardation stands at +$32pmt and prompt tightness has traders doing all they can to find March arrivals cargoes, both full propane and mixed ratio. Butane values have come off from 100% Naphtha to the mid high 90%s and is expected to continue to do so as naphtha surges with crude.

**MED**

 Butane demand in North West Europe is stopping the North South Mediterranean trade. An Algerian export cargo headed to US northeast which will help alleviate tightness in the region.
