## Market Commentary

AMERICAS The market was taken aback by the surprisingly large inventory draw posted by the EIA, which reflected a 5.9mmbbl decline in weekly stock levels to 41.9mmbbls. This compares to the overall total of 48.6mmbbls and 68.7mmbbls recorded over the last two years. In other words, 1H Feb propane inventories in North America are 86% and 70% of the previous two years, respectively. The market had generally anticipated an approximate 3mmbbl decline from the 4th to the 11th of February 2022. February price levels almost immediately spiked by over 5cts/gal, but March values were slower to respond, perhaps because the market is aware of around a half-dozen ‘buyback’ cargoes in March as well as the half-dozen (or so) cancellations which have been reported. Furthermore, recently rising crude values are believed to have likely improved field production, all of which appear to have conspired to limit the immediate upside – at least for the moment. To that, the North American weather patterns will continue to play a role, as there remain a couple of forecasted storms over the next 10-14 days which are meant to allow temperatures (and snow) to drop once again. That should likely, then, translate to additional demand pulls. The general sentiment, however, is that this week’s EIA data likely included corrective data (from previous reports) but that this week’s report overreached, in which case next week’s data could quite likely also contain additional corrective figures. NWE CIF ARA reacted strongly to the big draws in US inventories. The March price rose $27pmt on the day, most of that from when the draw was announced to close of European trading. Two bids were seen in the window with the high bid assessed a CIF ARA March +$10pmt. The European market is tight prompt all the way through 1H March, the February March backwardation widening to +$15pmt. The March East West spread has come in to +$54.5pmt and Eastern traders continue to look for homes for their cargoes in Europe. February Propane Naphtha spread is at $130pmt and continues to incentivise maximum petchem demand. Butane at 100% February naphtha with naphtha at 7-year highs. European traders are primarily interested 1H month US FOB cargoes, which can arrive in Europe in the same month as that of loading. For these they are the best FOB bidders in the market today.

**MED**

 Tight freight on larger pressurized vessels is hampering North South butane trade in the region. March trade discussions are yet to get going.
