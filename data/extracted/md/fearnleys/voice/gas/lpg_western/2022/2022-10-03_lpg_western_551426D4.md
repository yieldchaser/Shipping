## Market Commentary

AMERICAS The U.S. propane market seems to have settled into a lull as we are presently between seasons and at a time when domestic inventories are well stocked but weather-related demand has yet to commence. It is neither warm nor cold over most of the U.S., and things are presently calm. Export volumes are anticipated to increase in Oct. The EIA propane stock data was released and as of Sep 20 inventories reached an overall total of 84.4mmbbls, an increase of 1.6mmbbls over the previous week. The latest intel puts U.S. propane stocks at 117% of last year and 81% of two years ago. Nonetheless, as there is only limited demand at present there are likely to be additional – though modest – builds going forward, and as such even the current totals are considered sufficient to simultaneously accommodate both export requirements and weather-related demand. US terminal fees remain generally depressed, with buy-side interest most often found in the upper 3cts/gal range, with sellers looking for anything above the 4cts/gal level. In the meantime, very little is actually being accomplished and the market is in respite. Going forward, one factor to keep in mind is OPEC cuts combined with higher Crude prices, should open the door for increased US production, leading to increased propane production and lower propane to WTI ratio. NWE After an active start to the week in the European window propane discussions have quietened down. Leaving end October TOT cargoes values at October CIF ARA flat and 1H November cargoes valued November CIF ARA minus $2pmt. Although October propane naphtha spread in the region is -$109pmt, we are yet to see a rise in petchem demand. This low demand environment and, the lack of cold weather, is likely to have unsold November arrival cargoes look for a home in the East market instead of the West. The butane market remains active, with an open arb form the US. Though the one bid in the window at 89.5% October Naphtha for very early November arrival was well below the 98% bid seen earlier in the week.

**MED**

 The region has seen recent butane demand rise opening the arb from northwest Europe to Lavera. With northwest Europe also tight, US LPG is being imported to the region as the arb opens. A VLGC heading for Tangiers and a MGC into Sines in October, causing bids to disappear from the window.
