## Market Commentary

AMERICAS The domestic U.S. market has gone very quiet with activity levels almost nil. Crude fluctuations have forced some to the sidelines, but the poor arb during the heat of the summer is the primary cause. Buy side interest is also lacking. The EIA this week reported overall stocks at 65.7mmbbls, an increase of 2.1mmbbls which was slightly above the generally anticipated level, but which also brought current stocks to 100% of last year. Barring any unforeseen events, propane inventories in the U.S. will likely conclude September at around the 75 (plus) mmbbl range; likely sufficient given not only the lack of crop drying but the lack of cancellations and cargo buybacks. Both production and export volumes remain robust. Terminal fees are a bit subjective given the lack of activity, but ideas in the near term market remain in the mid 4s to mid 5s depending on location/ratio/timing, but move higher towards and in Q4. NWE A window bid pushed the large cargo market into a premium to spot CIF ARA price (+$2.5/t) for the first time since July. The downward pressure in the recent weeks was a result of a handful of factors- notably, record imports from the US, compounded by very low water levels in the Rhine, not allowing for inland distribution and thus leaving the ARA hub considerably long. Macro discussions around the region have been centered around how LPG's will be utilize this winter amid the ongoing Russia/ Ukranian conflict. Some participants believe the gas shortage will induce a migration over to LPG, bolstering demand and subsequently bulling up price, while others remain convinced that the infrastructure to move from gas to LPG's in a meaningful way is not in place, and would take time and considerable investment. This is not to suggest European prices will not rise this winter, but that they'd do so inline with typical, seasonal-related patterns as opposed to the Russian gas supply shock.

**MED**

 The Med remains very much long in both the propane and butane markets, and saw a drop of 30/ton midweek. The levels have effectively snapped shut the arbitrage, and no US cargos are currently aimed at the region.
