## Market Commentary

Sweeping sanctions on tanker tonnage has drastically altered the prospects of the fleet for 2025. Where in the first weeks of the year there was little TC interest, charterers are now rushing to secure ships on long period. Before the sanctions were introduced, it seemed that the overdue correction in values would have completely shifted the valuation story across the sector however, now the landing looks a lot softer than it did just a week ago. Values are still correcting for the time being, though it is now looking likely that they will settle higher than previously expected. That said, some key size and age ranges are still awaiting the benchmark transaction. One such benchmark has been set in the aframax sector, with AMAX AVENUE/ANTHEM (115K DWT SAMSUNG 2010/11) being sold en bloc at USD 79M, pricing the ships below USD 40M, with last done NISSOS DELOS (115K DWT SAMSUNG 2012) at USD 45.3M late last year. Things could quickly change however with the incoming presidential turnover in the USA next week. Lower activity in the dry market is typical for this time of year, and as we quicky approach Lunar New Year it is likely we will need to wait a further few weeks before we see this market come alive. Speaking of China, it is the most probably destination for older bulk carriers, with Chinese buyers account for at least half of the sales this week. Mirroring the tanker sector, we are still awaiting new benchmarks for 2025 across key ages. Eco tonnage will be of particular interest, with modern bulkers at the top of most shipowners’ wish lists. SALT LAKE CITY (171K DWT Daewoo 2005) selling at USD 16.2M to Chinese buyers has reset ideas on older Capesize, as not long ago a ship of this age/size would have bene asking in excess of USD 20M.
