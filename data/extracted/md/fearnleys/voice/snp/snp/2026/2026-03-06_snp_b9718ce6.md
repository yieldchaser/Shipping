## Market Commentary

Clearly, a lot has changed since the previous report last Friday. A long-predicted US-Israel bombing campaign on Iran commenced over the weekend to leave tanker owners (particularly crude owners) bracing themselves for heavy disruption. Most would have expected rates to rise, though few would have dreamed of the highs we are now seeing. A monstruous $696,803/day is being reported for scrubber fitted KALAMOS (317K DWT SWS 2010), which has comfortably set the record for highest rate recorded on a spot VLCC voyage. Shockwaves are being felt in the second-hand market, where already elevated price asks are shooting up in response to the new earnings norm, that is, if candidates are still being considered for sale. Sales reported this week are likely to be some of the last with a ‘pre-war’ pricing, and we would expect the number of sales reported to fall sharply as the market adjusts to what is going on. Time will of course tell in this very unpredictable environment. This week Scrubber Fitted Aframax GREEN ATTITUDE (112K DWT COSCO 2018) was sold for USD 70M. The dry market finds itself in a curious spot. A buoyant Q1 means that values are rising steadily, but sellers are disincentivised to part ways with tonnage. This is due to healthy rate predictions for the rest of the year and into 2027, and the fact that replacement tonnage is so hard to come by. Thus, if you sell your 15-year-old ship, yes you will receive a nice sum in return, but there is no guarantee you can find a 5-year-old equivalent to reinvest in, one would only be left with newbuildings as an option to deploy capital. Competition is fierce on any available candidates particularly quality tonnage (Japanese built units), with a rumoured 15 interested parties in SPIRIT OF HO-PING (82K DWT Tsuneishi 2011) which was eventually bought by Greek buyers for USD 19.4M. All this leaves dry with an ever growing list of buyers, and a seemingly shrinking list of candidates, something that will only influence values one way.
