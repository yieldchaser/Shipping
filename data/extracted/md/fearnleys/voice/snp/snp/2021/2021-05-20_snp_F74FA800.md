## Market Commentary

Stealing the headlines this week is the transaction announced by Frontline who has confirmed the purchase of six VLCC resales currently under construction at HHI. The vessels were part of an eight ship series originally contracted by Central Shipping with deliveries ranging from Q1 2022 until Q1 2023.  The aggregate purchase price stands at Usd 565.8m setting benchmarks for resale VLCCs at Usd 94.3m per unit. Current market environment seem to be validating the deal for all parties involved with buyers achieving relatively lower levels against current newbuilding offer and sellers booking a significant upside on the original contract price which is reportedly around Usd 90m per ship. Additionally these early contracts are offering prompter deliveries in a newbuilding environment that sees both pricing on the rise and slot availability for conventional tonnage slip into 2024. Elsewhere in the Tanker space we continue to see a sustained second hand activity for units built in mid-2000 across both the product and crude sectors with a steadily increasing buying enquiry suggesting upward pressure on pricing in the short to medium term. The Dry Cargo markets have seen a pick-up in activity on the capesize front with a number of buyers entering the market looking to secure additional tonnage. In terms on concluded business the Japanese controlled “AUSTRALIA MARU” (180k dwt, built 2012, KOYO) has been sold to Seanergy at Usd 33.5m. The vessel is both BWTS and scrubber fitted and is achieving levels in line with the upward momentum witnessed in the segment. Seanergy captured most of the snp activity on the cape front with the “AUSTRALIA MARU” being their fifth acquisition this year. Elsewhere Norden has sold their 2014 built “NORD HUDSON” built at NACKS for a price of Usd 21.1m which again represents a relative jump against last week’s comparable sales. To conclude we have witnessed some notable activity in the Handysize market with six vessels changing hands this week.
