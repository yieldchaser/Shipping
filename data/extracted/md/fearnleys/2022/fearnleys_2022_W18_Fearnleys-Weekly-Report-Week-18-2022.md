Fearnleys Weekly Report
Week 18 - May 04, 2022
## Tankers
## Comments
## VLCC
The week gone by has been a sluggish one with VLCC rates continuing to slide in all areas, now well established just north of WS 45 from both MEG and Wafr. Arguably major trading hubs have been oﬀ on holiday, with China and Japan only back tomorrow, but a well-stocked position list will not scare charterers as they resume ﬁxing the remainder of the April MEG program. The armada of ships ballasting West on spec when things were looking brighter has similarly tilted things in favour of charterers with Atlantic cargo to cover. All in all, bleak prospects short term.
## Suezmax
The Suezmax market is a little disjointed this week as the majority of the planet has taken some time oﬀ for some R&R.
Unfortunately, the short-term prognosis remains the same, at least this side of the weekend, with rates likely to trade sideways at best. TD20 has seen a couple of cargoes enter the fray in the 17-21 window suggesting second decade stems will be all but covered by the end of the week. With TD20 trading mid WS 70's, some charterers might see that as a "take" and start reaching forward with freight levels relatively low. In the East, there has been a trickle of activity for TD23 runs, with that particular route not giving much change from WS 45. MEG/East remains the ultra quite relative in this segment and for a modern, well approved vessel will trade at about WS 80 for a China run.
## Aframax
The Aframax market in the North Sea and Baltic started on a quiet note this week with more tonnage piling up due to less cargo activity. Going forward we expect rates to be under downward pressure. We might see some owners ballast away heading towards the Mediterranean which are showing some more promising trends compared to its neighboring North Sea market.
The Med/Bsea market has moved more or less sideways for most of the week with ships being ﬁxed at repeat levels. However, as talks of FM being lifted in Libyan ports is ﬂourishing this week there is a ﬁrm undertone among owners. At the same time we have seen a few stems being worked ex Bsea now, and we expect the ﬁrm trend to continue into next week.

## Capesize
Players back from holidays and activity up for the big ships - resultant average daily spot earnings improving slightly w-o-w to come in at tick over USD 19k. Fronthaul and transatlantic volumes plus levels, with only a few well-paying "exotic" exceptions, keep disappointing, with the describing TC-value Cont-Med to Far East being stuck at below USD 35k/day. The West Australia/China iron ore conference trade is stable/moderate in comparison, reﬂected by values for Paciﬁc rounds standing just about still at USD 24k/day. Bright spot being coal trades and particularly anything related to India - tenfolds of Capes done during last few trading days for Indonesia/India alone. Period interest remain signiﬁcant, with derivative values giving part support - representative ﬁxtures include 180,000 dwt built 2016 done for a year BCI ave5tc + 22 pct, 180,000 dwt built 2015 ﬁxed for a year at USD 31k, and 182,000 dwt built 2015 for 5 years at a reported USD 25,500/day.
## Panamax
A slow week, which was expected perhaps following holidays and in parts of the world the start of week. This said the Atlantic kicked oﬀ on a slightly ﬁrmer footing than previous with some talk of positional tightness in areas. Asia was said by some to have found a ﬂoor but with limited activity so far impacted by varying holidays it was less easy to call so far, according to sources.

## Supramax
Despite the big pressure on cargo supply from the Black Sea and Baltic, the market keeps ﬁrm sentiment.
ECSA and USG pushing rates up even with the ships ballasting from the Continent and Med does not change strong demand in those markets. Stable Paciﬁc market supporting good levels that owners asking.
Today's levels are in the low USD 30,000 pd for Atlantic RV on Supra with premium for Ultras. Paciﬁc RV are trading in the high
USD 20,000 pd.
Fronthaul Black Sea to Far East is like Continent to Far East ﬁxing in the low/mid USD 20,000 pd on Tess 58.

## Gas
Chartering
### EAST
With many of the market participants East of Suez out of oﬃce most of this week things are moving slow so far. Direction for the remainder of May ex MEG will take shape in the coming days - consensus around freight looking long and should come oﬀ a bit further. While the remaining spot volumes for May is yet to be seen, even with a healthy ending to the month, market tonnage is looking to be oversupplied.
### WEST
Players slowly but surely coming back from holidays. The Western freight market still looks strong and shows downward resistance in spite of missing activity. Last done ﬁxture was concluded at mid/low USD 120s Houston/Chiba which results in a USD 10 west premium versus the BLPG1 benchmark route. At the time of writing there have been a lack of product trade with Far East due to various holiday observances, but for now the most important question is how much demand there will be for vessels 1st decade in
June to tell where freight is going. Freight activity ex US has been quite subdued, only three US spot deals done so far in June, however, there is action looming.
