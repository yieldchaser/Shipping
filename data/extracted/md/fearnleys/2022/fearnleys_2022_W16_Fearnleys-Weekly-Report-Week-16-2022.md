# Fearnleys Weekly Report - Week 16, 2022

**Week 16 - April 20, 2022**

source: `corpus/01-brokers/fearnleys/2022/fearnleys_2022_W16_Fearnleys-Weekly-Report-Week-16-2022.pdf`  |  pages: 1

## Tankers

### VLCC

The April MEG program closed out with a healthy 140+ fixtures, so we watch to see what May has to offer. There is only 10 days left of the current month, so May should be gathering speed. As such, plenty of ships on subs, with no details attached as charterers seek to work from the shadows to keep activity private. The position list remains well stocked, and as a result, owners are struggling to keep hold of the pre-Easter gains, with rates slipping to the WS 50 level for modern well badged MEG/China.

Activity remains in the Atlantic with a number of cargoes looking ex USG at the time of writing. However, Suezmaxes are wobbling a bit as VLCC’s have been stepping on their toes for West Africa runs. With an armada of modern units ballasting west to seek their fortune, logic dictates that rates are capped in the short term.

### Suezmax

The Suezmax market has experienced some heady days recently with the wider, non-Russia market, sustaining long periods of strong freight rates. But, as with any volatile market, there are peaks and troughs and after such spectacular highs, rates are likely to correct downwards (moderately). Although the East market has seen a flurry of end/early cargoes, there just hasn't been enough activity to trim the list sufficiently to prevent a significant number of ships ballasting west. With this in mind, our call is that Wafr/East may threaten WS 160's levels whilst MEG/East on modern tonnage may dip under WS 110. TD23 has been all over the place recently but owners will do well to hang onto WS 60's, with even a WS 50's possibly on the cards.

### Aframax

Aframax rates in the North Sea came off slightly as expected due to lack of activity and dates moving forward. Baltic rates ex Russian ports is also under downward pressure as more tonnage willing to call Russian ports are emerging while awaiting further news about any possible further sanctions on Russian oil. Going forward we expect the market to move sideways with a softer undertone if we don’t see an uptick in activity.

In the Med/Bsea we have seen a quiet start to the week following the Easter holidays. The political diversion in Libya has resulted in several oil fields and ports closed as Force Majeure has been introduced. As expected, tonnage is also building up in the area and we have seen a downwards correction in rates. We expect the market to remain soft in the natural fixing window.

## Dry Bulk

### Capesize

The market improved slightly end of last week, but on thin volumes as big part of the industry was in holiday mood. However, as this week started the optimism was left behind, and owners started to chase charters requirements. We are therefore back to where we were last week with basically no change in levels, still lacking Brazilian iron ore and a dreadful oversupply of ships in the North Atlantic. On a more positive note, the volumes are still ticking in the Far East keeping the segment above operational cost at least.

### Panamax

An inauspicious start for the Panamax market following the holidays, the market was slow to get started according to sources. Asia appeared poised for a further push as sentiment remained positive with some seeing better bids but fixing activity had yet to get going thus far.

A distinct lack of fixing and activity to report on but the market seemed steady according to most sources. On the surface demand ex EC South America still appeared healthy and with a decent amount of mineral demand too, the early feelings were largely confident of a push.

Some representative fixtures this week: Bahri Grain (81,855/2014) Dammam 21 April fixing basis 4/6 months at USD 31,200 to ST Shipping. Orion III (76,602/2005) CJK 20/30 April going to Ming Wah basis 4/6 months at USD 22,000.

### Supramax

The Supra market in the Atlantic remains strong across all main routes. USG and ECSA are most lucrative areas with strong demand for Far East as well as across Atlantic trips.

Ultramax was reported fixing USD 33,000 pd deliver Miss River for a trip with grains Singapore-Japan. ECSA continued with good activities during Easter holidays, though very little was reported. The Supramax rumored to fix basis dely Brazil at USD 50,000 pd for TCT to Mediterranean Sea with grains. Furthermore, an Ultramax, 63’ dwt, delivery Chile for loading in Argentina at USD 40,000 pd for trip with grains to Morocco.

North Atlantic and Med markets followed similar trend with good activities, and we experienced a strong push on rates on certain trades. Trips from Continent to Med with scrap fixing in very high 20's dop on modern Supramax. The owners who are willing and able to do Russian ports is getting up to 3 times premium, reaching USD 80,000 pd for delivering Baltic for any direction within Atlantic.

Indian Ocean South Africa markets are stable with rates remaining strong both for backhauls and fronthaul trips. Supramax 58’ dwt open WCI was fixed at USD 27,000 pd for trip to Bangladesh with aggregates. A few Ultras were heard to be fixed around low USD 35,000 dop WCI with redely Far East. Far Eastern market were slow with noticeable rate reduction, for Tess 58 delivery China via Australia redly China, owners were accepting around USD 25,000 pd.

## Gas

### Chartering

#### EAST

Freight in the East remains firm and last done deal for non-India was concluded at USD 71 Baltic. The fixing window is still mainly concentrated on 1st half of May, but there is one FOB tender for last decade of May that has received some attention. Time of writing there are 2 Indian cargos being worked for fairly prompt dates, and as competition is rather limited, we expected these to be concluded at a premium.

#### WEST

The Western market continues to be strong with last done deal concluded at USD 122 Houston/Chiba range and USD 69 Houston/Flushing. Currently the freight is a bit stronger than the arb, but the vessel supply looks relatively tight so we expect the strong rates to maintain. The high demand we saw last month in Northwest Europe persist and the time of writing there are seven vessels heading this way so far in April. In all likelihood this will continue in the next months to come.
