Fearnleys Weekly Report
Week 32 - August 10, 2022
## Tankers
## Comments
## VLCC
A clever game played by charterers this week, as strong activity is kept in the shadows and vessels are picked oﬀ the list. We are up to 135 or so deals ex MEG for August, with a little under half of the 20-31 window to cover. Rates have ﬂattered to deceive with
MEG/East remaining pegged in the high WS 50’s. Owners should maintain some hope as we swing towards the Autumn loading program, as the list remains balanced and those requiring modern ships will ﬁnd them a little scarcer. A point of note, WS 65 achieved oﬀ very prompt dates, but given the lack of competition not a reﬂection on the market, and the vessel had been sat in the
MEG for a while, so the TCE was close to USD 0.
The Transatlantic activity increased with a number of ships ﬁxing USG/UKC at USD 3.3m, and we expect to see more of this trade going forward.
## Suezmax
The Suezmax market have been absolutely nothing to talk about the last week. Activity has been almost non-existing and the whole market have basically been waiting for a downward correction. This all changed yday with activity picking up in the USG on
Afras, in addition to V's looking a bit better. The USG market is more attractive for ships open Cont/Med and the only ship willing to look at Wafr will be eastern ballasters. All of this will limit available tonnage in these markets, hopefully keeping rates close to last done levels. In the MEG, it’s still rather quiet, except from short voyages into India. A lot of ships still looking Wafr or Bsea instead, so we expect the MEG market to remain around current levels.
## Aframax
The North Sea/Baltic market been moving sideways this week with rates for a cross UKC paying around WS 185 level. However, with quite a few Panamax enquires looking at Aframax tonnage due to a tight tonnage list on Panamax this we could see an upward pressure on rates moving into the 3rd decade ﬁxing window. The Med/Blsea market continued the downward spiral throughout last week. There’s been some under the radar activity this week, but not suﬃcient to support the prompt and available tonnage hence freight levels taken a further dip this week. We don’t foresee any real improvements for the balance of the week unless activity bounces back. However, some owners now ballasting away eyeing more promising returns in the USG.

## Capesize
After last week’s drop with an activity blackout, market have found some ground as all the miners are back again for West
Australia. However, though market is active and ﬀa bounced a bit the physical levels are not dramatically higher as there still is a lot of availed tonnage around as congestion has eased. The expectation for next week is some improvements as levels have been so hard smacked down, however with summer doldrums it’s hard to imagine a very big movement.

## Supramax
The Supra market in still lucking activity across all trading areas. Slow start of the week with declining sentiment. ECSA and USG are keeping steady momentous tough rates are not impressive. North Atlantic, Continent and Mediterranean markets suﬀering luck of inquiry.
Supramax dely Brazil TCT to Algeria ﬁxed at USD 25,000 pd. Trips from Continent to Med with scrap ﬁxing below USD 20's dop on modern Supramax. Trading in Russian ports still paying premium.
Indian Ocean and South Africa markets are stable with rates remaining like last done for backhauls and fronthaul trips. Supramax 58’ DWT open WCI was ﬁxed at USD 20,000 pd for trip to Bangladesh with aggregates. Far Eastern market is trading sideways with ﬁxture reports Tess 58 delivery Singapore - China range via Australia with ongoing rate around USD 20,000 pd, depending on delivery and vessel’s speciﬁcations.

Chartering
### EAST
With Singapore oﬀ for National day on Tuesday the market naturally took a small breather. So far just a few charterers have been showing interest in early September laycans, but Adnoc acceptances are just around the corner and we expect more shipping requirements to follow. As the cargo lineups for next month starting to take shape we will eventually also get a more accurate picture for the shipping balance into September.
### WEST
Last week we saw an uptick in activity after a couple of weeks with slow pace in the Western market. So far this week the pace of activity has not followed as we have only seen one ﬁxture concluded. Last done deal for August dates concluded at low 90s H/C while we see around 100 for September dates. There are still some ships left open in USG with soon two of them prompt open. The arb has narrowed a bit lately which has not been good for freight. A 1.75 million build would help widen it and maybe give freight some support.
