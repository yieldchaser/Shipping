## Tankers
## Comments
## VLCC
With a holiday in the US yesterday and London oﬀ Thursday and Friday for the Golden jubilee, expectations were for a busier period ahead of the Posidonia event that begins next week. While activity has been on the rise and bunkers have continued to ﬁrm, any resultant uptick in Worldscale rates has been somewhat subdued, as charterers ﬁght for every quarter point over last done.
Therefore, the result barely covers the higher bunkers costs to owners, and contributing to an overall negative on a TCE basis at the time of writing.
With some added political tensions in the Middle East (Iranian seizure of two Greek vessels) and a shortened work week ahead of a week that has many travelling, the question is will we see some further upward pressure develop, despite suﬃcient supply to cover demand?
## Suezmax
Last week, TD20 had the look and feel of a market that could trade well past WS 100, but by the time Monday rolled around there was a hint of a stall. A number of Wafr/long Mediterranean voyages traded up in dollar per day terms (over a TD20 run), but the market tends not to focus on numbers/maths and it was the lower WS rates that grabbed the headlines, ultimately forging market direction. The US Gulf remains the prime driver behind Atlantic rates and as long as cargoes and ships continue to trade in a concentric Atlantic-manner, we will continue to see further volatility. The East remains poor hunting ground for this segment, save for TD23, which provides a free ticket back to the Med perfectly positioning those owners who trade the Russian market. MEG/East needs testing but should trade circa WS 100.
## Aframax
European Union leaders agreed in principle on a sixth package of sanctions against Russia preparing to ﬁnalize a ban on almost 90% of all Russian oil imports by the end of the year. This means reﬁneries on the Continent will have to source oil in an even greater extent than before. As such we could see more intra-regional trade on some of the Nsea grades as more of the Urals could be shipped for East destinations. So far, the Aframax market across Europe is moving sideways and with some of the strategic export ports scheduled for maintenance we would need to see more volume before the market will improve.

## Capesize
A sharp drop in rates over the last week, with average 5 tc presently at 22,000 being down from 33,000 in one week. C5 is down from USD 14 to USD 12.50 and c3 is down from USD 36 to USD 31.50 pmt in this period. However, rates are on the rise again from earlier this week when c5 was concluded in the 11s. This may imply rates came oﬀ too much too quick. At the same time, forward values are keeping fairly stable with second half of the year still in the mid 30,000s.

## Gas
Chartering
### EAST
One producer has been driving much of the activity in the Middle East lately, and they still keep active into the beginning of this week. A persistently large disparity between East and West freight is tempting some traders to evaluate sending more vessels East, which could soften some of the pressure in the market towards the end of June. That said, we are seeing a distressed cargo in the works which could end up being ﬁxed at a large premium and have a certain impact on general sentiment for next deals.
### WEST
The Western market keeps up the pace and vessels are ﬁxed for July liftings ex US at rates which are slowly but surely creeping upwards. The shipping balance remains relatively tight and in control of few players and this is likely to continue as July ﬁxing moves on. With the observance of a US holiday at the start of this week and with an upcoming UK holiday Thursday and Friday this week, it is expected that the players in need for shipping coverage in the ﬁrst and second decade will bring some further action to an already hot market. Last concluded ﬁxture ex USG is around USD 140 Houston/Chiba and around USD 80 Houston/Flushing.
