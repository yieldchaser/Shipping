Fearnleys Weekly Report
Week 20 - May 18, 2022
## Tankers
## Comments
## VLCC
We sit between the May close out and the June stem releases in the MEG and as such, the surface looks quiet. Tonnage lists remain lengthy, and fundamentals point towards little change. However, with bunkers up close to USD 950 in Fujairah today, it is likely owners will have to push back, and at present there are rumors of undercover cargoes and owners holding the line. For modern ships in the MEG, it is WS 40+, if breaking 40 is the charterers aim, they will have to look at the ‘disadvantaged’.
In the Atlantic, tight and a little busier oﬀ prompt dates in Wafr, as more oil heads to the UKC, around the WS 47.5 level. Going East in the low WS 40’s. Crucial to the VLCC’s, Chinese reﬁneries are at their lowest utility since the start of the pandemic, but most of the news from China is becoming a little more positive.
## Suezmax
There continues be an overall ﬂat/soft feel to the Suezmax market this week, with the exception of possibly the US Gulf, which could start to uptick (modestly). TD20 has seen a trickle of activity but the main source of tonnage for this region, the Far East, continues to supply a never-ending supply of ships which keep chipping away at rates. We could see TD20 rates trade down to low
WS 80's if MEG maintains its lackluster performance. Thus far, the EU hasn't created a uniﬁed response to Russian crude oil exports which will keep a number of traditional owners away from the wider market, although Russian Baltic stems have gone a little quiet and may throw some ships back onto the regular list. TD23 is a falling market and may start to trade WS 30's as is MEG/China, which should trade WS 80'S.
## Aframax
The NSea took another hit last week as the market is dominated by relets and Suezmaxes for local runs. Supply of vessels is suﬃcient for the time being and with Baltic loadings becoming more complex owners are on the look for alternatives. Moving forward we expect market to remain weak and with owners ﬁghting to keep earnings above OPEX.
In the Med/Bsea, the market has come oﬀ further this week dropping another 15 points on cross Med voyages. The situation in
Libya is still unchanged, and we don’t see any imminent improvement in freight levels in the short run as it will take quite a bit of cargo activity to satisfy the number of prompt open ships in the area.

## Capesize
Market continues to ﬁrm as the appetite for vessels in the Far East continues. The fact that both West Australia and Indonesia have been very active over the last weeks is now making an impact on the nearby laycans out of South Africa which see limited amount of tonnage available. This together with improvements in the Atlantic indicates that market levels will go higher for the next week to come.
## Panamax
A further low-key week for the Panamaxes with the Atlantic showing further signs of easing with a distinct lack of transatlantic mineral demand piling pressure on the nearby positions. This despite decent numbers being achieved for the grain round trips. Asia witnessed a further slow week and lacking any real momentum especially in the South with Indonesia yet to show its hand this week.
Some representative ﬁxtures this week:
The BBG Fangcheng (81,629/2019) Gibraltar 21 May ﬁxed for a grain fronthaul trip at USD 41,000 with the rumor a grain house, but precise details remained under wraps.
The scrubber ﬁtted Ultra Margay (81,921/2020) Gibraltar prompt ﬁxed for a trip via US East Coast redelivery Gibraltar-Skaw at USD 34,000 with Oldendorﬀ, with the scrubber beneﬁt for charterers.
The scrubber ﬁtted Darya Preeti (81,981/2019) Nansha 19-21 May was placed on subjects for a trip via Indonesia redelivery India at
USD 31,000 with Norvic Bulk India.
## Supramax
Week started oﬀ ﬁrst with holidays in Asia and in addition shipping event in Denmark slowing down activity on all fronts. USG remained ﬂat with lack of fresh enquiries. 57,000 dwt seemed to be covered for US Gulf round trip in the higher USD 30,000’s.
Ultramax was ﬁxed basis delivery Turkey 15/20 May for 11/12 months trading at USD 29,000. Seemed it appeared less tonnage from
East Coast South America. From the East, some more activity on Indonesian coal although not too much reported. Utramax open
Lanshan was ﬁxed for a nickel ore run from Philippines to China at USD 30,000 but no further details came to light. 52,000 dwt was ﬁxed basis delivery Jakarta via Indonesia redelivery China at USD 29,000.

## Gas
Chartering
### EAST
At the time of writing Saudi Aramco is yet to publish their lifting dates for June, and we are hearing reports of some production issues causing the delay. First open vessels basis straight out charter in the Middle East currently only available from the 6th of
June onwards. While ﬁrst decade ﬁxing should be more or less spoken for by now, vessel balance for the rest of the month will only be clear once we have Aramco acceptances. With a west premium at around 6-7 dollars and a relatively thin position list we expect the market to remain ﬁrm in the near term.
### WEST
The western freight rates continue to rise and have now broken the 130-dollar mark for Houston/Chiba which we have not seen since the beginning of 2021. We do not see the rates decrease quite yet as the tight position list is currently dominated by pure shipowners and the arb remains wide. Fixing focus has now shifted towards the last decade of June with the majority of ﬁxtures this week done with 20-31 June laycans, but there are still a couple of vessels left in mid June and one in end May as well which we expect will ﬁnd suitable cargos. There have been approximately 30 spot ﬁxtures out of the USG/USEC so far in June, same as May, and we can expect this number to climb closer to 40.
