Fearnleys Weekly Report
Week 7 - February 16, 2022
## Tankers
## Comments
## VLCC
An interesting week on the front pages of the newspapers, but in the VLCC world, has been more of the same. The MEG market has sat in the void between February and March laycans, but even if cargoes were there to work, the sheer volume of ships waiting to take them out remains a concern. New lows, with a ﬁxture at WS 32-32.5 for MEG/China, after the charterer received 13 oﬀers. If you have a scrubber these returns are not good. Without a scrubber, they don’t bear thinking about. The Atlantic remains a little more interesting, with West Africa working mid-month and a 10-point premium achieved on a prompter laycan for Indonesia, but unreﬂective of any market swing, however. The USG has ticked up by a USD 100k or so per successive ﬁxture, but with bunker prices where they are, it’s the smallest of solaces for owners.
## Suezmax
Curiously, just as the situation in Ukraine looks like it’s slowly diﬀusing, the Joint War Committee has decided to add the Black Sea to their list of High Risk Areas. A few weeks too late maybe? Bizarrely, Black Sea rates have traded fairly ﬂat during this period, which makes shipping the sole outlier, in global markets, not to have reacted to the threat of conﬂict. Elsewhere, US Gulf /TA has traded 145kt x WS 65 which underlines that the Atlantic market doesn't have a backhaul trade anymore as everything has become fronthaul. In the East, there's a fairly steady feel as TD23 is likely to repeat last done levels as ﬁxing dates move into ﬁrst decade
March. MEG/East is still at the mercy of a very long list where rates will remain around the WS 60-62.5 mark for the balance of the week.
## Aframax
Yet another week in the Nsea/Baltic market without any positive changes to write home about. Some owners are desperately putting up some resistance, but slow activity and an abundant tonnage list neutralizes any attempts to push rates. However, a ﬁrm
USG/Caribs market is oﬀering an alternative for those owners willing to ballast. This might have a positive eﬀect on the Nsea/Baltic market. Till then, rates are expected to move sideways moving into the next week. Much of the same can be said about the
Med/Bsea market where there are enough prompt ships around to repeat the rate levels we have seen since last week. Any attempt from owners’ side to hold back on rates is replied by charterers taking ships out quietly oﬀ the market. Also, in the Med/Bsea we expect rates to move sideways in the short term.

## Capesize
Winter doldrums not yet over for the big ships, and spot values presently stuck at close to half that of Supras/Panamaxes. Average daily earnings ﬂat w-o-w at USD low 12k's as both major-volume trades West Australia/China and Brazil/Feast fail to meet expectations. Brazilian miners still battling weather-related production- and shipping delays, causing concern for a growing number of ballasters looking for March fronthaul alternatives - and owners discounting heavily quick Far East rounds in anticipation of better times to come. Only bright spot being the West Africa/Far East trades, compensating for part of the missing Atlantic commodity ﬂows. Period interest and activity lives its own life as most experienced takers well aware this is the time of year when bargains are secured. Levels reﬂect present forward views, and representative ﬁxtures include 180,000 dwt, built 2012, fetching
USD 27,300 basis 9-13 months and 179,000 dwt, built 2011, done for 19-22 months at USD 26,500.
## Panamax
Sentiment and overall views of the market in the Atlantic continued to be a little mixed this week when writing. APS ﬁxtures surfacing for the early tonnage undermining the bears view who reckoned there were some better numbers. Asia by contrast continued to hold steady with solid demand and little change in numbers exchanged.
Some representative ﬁxtures this week:
Min Sheng I (81,659/2012) Gibraltar 22 February for a Kamsar round voyage at USD 19,000 Oldendorﬀ.
The scrubber ﬁtted Ultra Serval (81,606/2020) San Ciprian 17/19 February for a trip via Kamsar redelivery Skaw-Gibraltar at USD 22,500 with the scrubber beneﬁt going to charterers.
RB Jordana (81,301/2016) retro sailing La Coruna 10 February was heard to have ﬁxed a trip via NC South America option US Gulf redelivery Far East at USD 29,500 to unnamed Chinese charterers.
TBN was said to have ﬁxed a 70,000/10 coal lift ex Hay Point to Praia Mole 1/10 March shipment dates at approximately USD 23.50 ﬁo with ArcelorMittal.
## Supramax
As the tension further escalated between Russia and Ukraine, the market reacts with intense volatility. Though strong demand for coal shipments on Indonesia-India routes pushes Asian market with high speed. Atlantic market has also shown improvement in main loading areas and pushed rates up between USD 3,000-5,000 pd from a week ago. Today's levels are in the low USD 20,000 pd for TARV on Supra and with few thousand premium for Ultras. Black Sea to Far East ﬁxing in the low USD 30,000 pd on Tess 58.
We see good number of fresh order out of ECSA with ﬁxtures been reported Supra USD 30,000 pd loading in Brazil for one trip with grains in the Cont/Med range. Trips from ECSA to EC India-China range ﬁxed around low USD 20,000 pd plus USD 900,000
GBB. We expect and foresee a further improvement in Atlantic Basin.

## Gas
Chartering
### EAST
After a slower than usual month for February ﬁxing with ‘only’ 13 deals concluded in the MEG, March has gotten oﬀ to a hot start and we currently count 10 deals ﬁxed (or still on subs) for 1st decade March loading. Nevertheless, last done rates still remain in the mid USD 40s Baltic region, as we are still left with a good variety of owners and traders relets even after more activity in market.
Going forward the focus will be on the pending Saudi acceptances, and there are also a couple of fob tenders from Middle Eastern suppliers for mid and end March.
### WEST
With freight rates seemingly stable for now we ﬁnd the West premium comfortably around USD 10 pmt before waiting in the
Panama Canal and last done ﬁxture concluded at USD mid/low 90s Houston/Chiba range. It seems that February and ﬁrst decade
March enquiries ex USG is more or less spoken for, and for the ﬁrst time in ages there is a prompt open vessel available in the US
Gulf. With that said, it remains to be seen if there are enough cargos for the vessels coming open ﬁrst half March in the USG.
