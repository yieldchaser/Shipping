Fearnleys Weekly Report
Week 11 - March 16, 2022
## Tankers
## Comments
## VLCC
With oil prices up, then down, it’s been a week where the bunkers dominate in determining the market levels, as opposed to any sentiment or supply and demand pressures. As owners refuel at the pumps, the calculated returns can dip dramatically after the (relatively) cheaper onboard bunkers are used up. But, as we see the bunker prices decrease, so does the rate. As for supply, by our count 180 vessels arriving Fuj in the next 30 days, speaks for itself, and with quoted MEG cargoes entertaining 15+ oﬀers, owner’s sentiment will suﬀer. The Atlantic, only really the USG providing much nourishment for the VLCCs.
On the ﬂip side, OECD inventories at close to their lowest for the last 10 years, these will need replenishing, once OPEC are able/willing to lift production. It is up to Western leaders to convince them to do so.
## Suezmax
Russian grades continue to command a premium over the regular market ex Baltic and Black Sea with the curious exception of
ESPO loadings from Kozmino. The wider market is still battling rising bunkers and falling freight with few signs of resistance other than some Indian market cargoes that have recently traded Wafr/India at circa 130kt x WS 88-90. Those with scrubbers have committed the dirty deed and allowed their consumption-advantage to be traded against them with Wafr/long East trading at WS 77.5-80. MEG/India enquiry has been particularly active, whilst conversely, long haul from this region has been quiet, allowing ships to recycle back onto the list quickly. MEG/East will trade WS 87.5-90 on modern whilst TD23 may start to threaten WS 30's if low cargo volumes persist.
## Aframax
We still see large premiums being paid for Russian port calls despite less reported activity over the last couple of days. Everyone is waiting to see if any stems get cancelled and how this will aﬀect the export program for both March and April. North Sea crude spot earnings came oﬀ signiﬁcantly last week and have been hovering around WS 120 levels due to erratic bunker prices. Going forward we might see rates in the North Sea improve as we expect even more VLCC stems ex WCN being lifted on Aframaxes.
Although the rates ex Bsea have come oﬀ this last week, voyages ex Russian ports are still giving owners a healthy return. In the
Mediterranean, we have seen the market soften further and we expect this to continue going into the weekend.

## Capesize
There is a large degree of uncertainty around the world, but all in all dry bulk rates have remained fairly stable; rates went from USD 18,900 on average to USD 21,800 from last week. C5 went from USD 12 to 12,3 on index, despite talks about USD 12,6 being concluded. The Brazil/ China round has been kept around USD 30 pmt level for some time (index value USD 30,5 one week back) and is presently at USD 27 pmt, also being last done by Vale who have been active in taking ships recently.
## Panamax
A softer week encompassed the Panamax market with little good cheer for owners to grab a hold of. Negative bids and tones across the board. Negative news and indexes have been the story of the whole week.

## Supramax
Despite the War in Ukraine continuing for the third week, the shipping industry are ﬁghting to get grips with the situation. The
Supramax market showed some good resistance, strong demand in the Paciﬁc made many owners leave Emed and ballast towards
Suez to obtain better rates loading in MEG, India, and Southeast Asia. USG and ECSA pushed rates further up. In Asia, the demand/supply was more balanced. The period activity decreased, with big gap between owners and charterers ideas, which are about a few thousands. The BSI-58 10T/C route closing yesterday just USD 100 below USD 32,000/day. South China trip via
Indonesia, E.C.India is up by, with ﬁxure reported close to USD 40,000/day, W.Africa trip via ECSA to N.China is USD 30,000/day, lower rates from Mediterranean to China/S.Korea is softer at USD 20,000/day. The big diﬀerence between the Atlantic average USD 15,000/day, while the Paciﬁc round voyages and back haul paus as much as USD 40,000/day.

## Gas
Chartering
### EAST
The momentum in Baltic has slowed down and for the ﬁrst time in 2 weeks the BLPG1 print showed a small drop. This is partly due to less activity and more trader relets appearing in market for 1H April, but also due to bunker prices seemingly ﬁnding some ‘stability’ compared to previous weeks. As a result of more upstream production, we have also seen a couple of spot fob cargos for sale from Middle Eastern suppliers. But so far this has not triggered any signiﬁcant activity on shipping yet, and we are (time of writing) still waiting for the Saudi acceptances for April.
### WEST
A couple of leftover March vessels are being targeted for some ﬁnal attempts to secure March FOB loaders at freight rates in the low USD 100’s Houston/Chiba and mid USD 50’s Houston/Flushing. First half April seems more or less done in terms of spot activity and all eyes are on second half April where there is a vessel requirement and FOB/CFR tender from an Indonesian major.
Under the current environment, Houston/Flushing is trading at a mere USD 6,500 premium to that of Houston/Chiba and thus far in
March there are six vessels already on the way towards North West Europe and it will continue to rise – that’s about the same number of VLGC liftings ex USG+USEC to NWE seen on average over the past three months. Although activity isn’t booming so far this week, we think there is deﬁnitely more to come.
