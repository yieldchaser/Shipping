# Fearnleys Weekly Report - Week 24, 2022

**Week 24 - June 15, 2022**

source: `corpus/01-brokers/fearnleys/2022/fearnleys_2022_W24_Fearnleys-Weekly-Report-Week-24-2022.pdf`  |  pages: 1

## Tankers

### VLCC

With the dust settled after another Posidonia, we catch up on deals done during the week. Plenty of AG ships on subs no details as rates struggle to make any headway, despite increasing fuel costs. AG/Korea sits at the ws43.5 level, a couple of points discount if you find yourself handicapped in any way. With June closing around 140 fixtures, volume is decent and Unipec already exploring July dates. Other sizes showing improvement, albeit heavily Atlantic driven, VL’s may follow suit, as a number plucked from the list in Rotterdam, Cape of Good Hope etc, with no details attached. Therefore a Charterer may find that as they go to the list for a ship, it will likely be thinner than it first appears. USG/Korea has edged up to $5.675m. With a tighter list, higher bunkers and firmer Smaxes, further Atlantic firming likely.

### Suezmax

Recent changes in the behaviour of the Atlantic crude oil trade has created it's own algorithm, with peaks and troughs revealing themselves to the market well in advance of them occurring. This algorithm comes with a caveat; as long as the East is quiet and keeps throwing ships back onto the Atlantic list, participants will have a degree of forewarning. We're in the middle of one of those upward trends now with Td20 trading at about W110-115 whilst the USG and Americas in general still look very interesting with Owners likely to make further gains. Vlcc's may provide a cap of sorts in the Atlantic but that segment may be tighter than anticipated. This optimism falls flat in the East where only Td23 provides sufficient enquiry to actually call itself a market. That route trades in the WS50's with further upside.

### Aframax

There is still a great demand from specific charterers for any vessels willing to load ex Russian ports. Despite some maintenance scheduled for some strategic export ports in the Nsea basin there is sufficient activity for both fuel and crude leaving other Owners in a position to push for a bit more than last done. Market looking a bit firmer.In the Med/Blsea we have seen a firming trend this last week with benchmark routes moving as much as +WS50-60 points w.o.w.With surrounding markets offering attractive alternatives and with charterers reaching and with long hauls in the mix - we expect the natural window to remain firm as lists looking tighter.

## Dry Bulk

### Capesize

Stable or tiny tick up at still mediocre and lower-than-expected levels for the big ships. Whilst the Brazil/China conference trade continues to disappoint in volume, transatlantic is showing signs of promise - average daily earnings for typically t/a coal up more than 20 pct to stand at usd 24k. India continues to be insatiable for indonesian coal, partly compensating for a slow West Australia/China iron ore trade not pushing capacity - value for pacific rounds stuck in usd 18-19k/day-territory. Slight improvements on other trades contribute to overall daily earnings putting on 5 pct to come in at just above usd 21k. Short-term forward values still in contango, and period capes much in demand - representative recent fixtures include 181000 dwt built 2014 done for 4-6 months at usd 35k.

### Panamax

A week of positive sentiment with talk abound of a floor being found in both basins. There was fresh fronthaul enquiry from the Atlantic in turn improving the bids, and along with this came talk that the trans-Atlantic rates being talked up, this has yet to transpire into physical fixtures, but sentiment remains firm for now. Similarly in Asia, plentiful fresh enquiry ex Indonesia appeared to lend support in the Southeast Asia region and surrounding area with rates seemingly finding a floor, the next week or so are an interesting watch.

### Supramax

Currently it seems the market will be in for a rebound, but there is no change to the fundamental story that demand growth remains below the rate of supply growth. The market usually climbs at this time of year, so the last couple of weeks’ drop should serve as a clear sign and warning that fundamentals are headed in the wrong direction for owners. The macro-picture is too weak. Just skim through the last months’ manufacturing and steel PMI readings across all major countries/regions and it seems very unlikely that demand will rise in the coming months. USG remained flat with little fresh reported. Supramax Dafeng was covered for a trip to South East Asia in the low usd 20,000s. Ultramax open Chitta was fixed for trip via Indonesia to China at usd 18,000.

## Gas

### Chartering

#### EAST

The BLPG1 index continue to come off, and as a result nobody seems to be in a rush to move on upcoming shipping requirements. The only players that have tested freight since last week has been a handful of Indian majors, but each of these deals have all been concluded at a discount to the previous deal. We are however seeing stronger upstream production, and many of the Middle Eastern suppliers are dealing with higher inventory of LPG. We therefore expect to see more spot fob cargos in the market over the coming weeks.

#### WEST

The same story applies for the West market. BLPG2 and 3 are coming off rapidly and will by the looks of it, enter double digit freight before activity resumes. About six fixtures have been concluded for July loadings ex USG/USEC, thus, there is plenty of room for spot activity, albeit at rates somewhere in between capex and opex.
