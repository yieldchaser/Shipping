# Fearnleys Weekly Report - Week 12, 2022

**Week 12 - March 23, 2022**

source: `corpus/01-brokers/fearnleys/2022/fearnleys_2022_W12_Fearnleys-Weekly-Report-Week-12-2022.pdf`  |  pages: 1

## Tankers

### VLCC

A general easing of bunker prices should have helped owners’ bottom lines over the last week, down close to USD 825/mt for VLSFO in Spore. However, any savings were quickly transferred to the charterers pocket as rates have dropped in accordance. Voyages MEG to the East sit in the mid 30s depending on route/quality of ship. Hats off to charterers who have gone into stealth mode and playing the “fear-card” to perfection, picking ships off left, right and centre under the radar and leaving an impression of an inactive market when the opposite appears to be true, as charterers plow into their April stems. West Africa, clearly influenced by tonnage from the East as rates to China down to a last done of WS 37. Elsewhere in the Atlantic there is some hope, as prompter dates in the USG provide small gains, up to USD 5.4m to Ningbo.

### Suezmax

Black Sea Suezmax rates have been one of the few shining lights in an otherwise flaccid market, and for obvious reasons. Therefore, when news started to filter through that the CPC SPM's had suffered serious “storm damage”, possibly hampering exports for number of weeks, a sharp intake of breath was likely taken by most Black Sea players. The good news is that only one SPMS is temporarily out of action whilst the remaining SPM is working normally. In the wider market, rates appear to be a little firmer in West Africa where an East run will trade WS 90's with TD20 trading mid WS 80'S. BOT/East is holding onto WS 90 by its fingertips whilst TD23 has done very well to hold onto WS 40's for a straight run. Kozmino/North China has seen a number of Suezmax's drawn into the mix as freights trade circa USD 1m.

### Aframax

Rates for the Nsea/Baltic Aframax market came off on the back of less activity. We still see quite high premiums for Russian trade despite a downward correction in rates. Some owners are not willing to fix current levels as the exposure for high bunker prices due to an unstable oil market is too high. Greater activity and firming rates in the USG/Caribs and the Euromed market might offer an alternative for those owners sitting with idle vessels on the Cont. Going forward we might see rates firm a bit. Med/Bsea: Over the course of the last week we have seen healthy cargo activity push the rates up about 20 points before it all slowed down again, and rates have come back down to where they were prior to the weekend. One to watch going into next week is further developments concerning Novo port and the CPC pipeline.

## Dry Bulk

### Capesize

Slowdown for the big ships on key routes, consequent average daily earnings losing 20 pct w-o-w to come in at bit over USD 17k. World steel production lower than for same period last year - and dominating West Australia/China and Brazil/Feast trades both remain at far from full speed. A Europe in need for substantial long haul coal volumes to fill the void of Russian material only partly compensates, mildening a soft tendency by taking ship capacity out of the ordinary Pacific and front haul trades. Growing concern over Mediterranean tonnage build-up as Black Sea exports, normally absorbing some 10 units/month, have come to a complete stop - transatlantic market in deep need of increased mineral volumes to maintain levels. Forward expectations and thus period interest nevertheless remain positive and far above spot - representative fixtures include 180,000 dwt, built 2015, done for close to 2 years at a handsome USD 32k.

### Panamax

A significant week of gains in the Panamax market when writing, largely Atlantic centric as activity for the transatlantic trips continued to thrive. Much of the activity and focus was grain led ex EC and NC South America with options to ex US Gulf and with bids further increasing against a tighter looking tonnage count, the front haul trip appeared to be impacted to this despite a lack of fixing. Asia continued to be reserved by comparison and largely flat aside from limited action ex NoPac with rates improving slightly over last done.

Some representative fixtures this week: Ursula Manx (82,561/2021) Cai Mep 2/3 April rumored fixed basis 5/6 months redelivery Worldwide at USD 32,000 to Cargill. YM Opus (81,863/2015) retro sailing Stade 19 March fixing for a trip via US Gulf option NC South America redelivery Skaw-Gibraltar range at USD 28,500 with Cargill. Navios Felicity I (81,962/2020) delivery aps EC South America 10 April was heard fixed for a trip redelivery Skaw-Gibraltar at USD 50,000. Frontier Leader (81,383/2013) retro sailing Hong Kong 21 March was placed on subjects for a trip via EC Australia redelivery India at USD 27,000. Amoy Sunny (76,598/2006) CJK end March secured by NS United for a trip via NoPac redelivery Japan at USD 26,500. Perseus (82,165/2013) Toyohashi prompt placed on subjects for a trip via NoPac redelivery Singapore-Japan with intention grains at a rate tick below USD 30,000. Boyang Garnet (75,764/2007) Rotterdam 23 March was fixed for a trip via NC South America redelivery Skaw-Gibraltar at USD 24,500 with Louis Dreyfus.

### Supramax

Continued strong demand for minor bulks, as preliminary estimates for this month shows record high loadings. Demand remained strong from East Coast South America for TA. Ultramax was covered from Recalada to Algeria at about USD 49,000. Clinker round from Egypt to Monrovia were fixed close to USD 30,000. Touch more reported from the Pacific basin. 57,000 dwt open Chittagong took USD 27,000 for a trip from EC India to China. Another 53,000 dwt open in Singapore was covered around USD 45,000 for a trip via Indonesia to China. From the North, 56,000 dwt open Bayuquan was finalized for a CIS Pacific round at USD 70,000. Some steels to USG from India paid owners USD 43,000 for a 58,000 dwt vessel open Haldia. Also a 52,000 dwt ship concluded business for trip via Thailand redel Haldia at USD 34,000 basis del Singapore.

## Gas

### Chartering

#### EAST

There’s been a handful of deals concluded or placed on subs for Middle East second decade April cargos this week, mostly in the high 50s freight range. East/West freight differential is again pointing in favor of the West and with less waiting in Panama (latest at 4 days both North and South bound). This will attract more ships away from the East. Foreseeing less vessels available coupled with VLSFO bunkers increasing some 6-7% since last Friday on the back of again rising crude - we expect freight to move back above the 60-dollar mark from here.

#### WEST

This week has already seen twice as many fixtures compared last week and the position list is now mainly dominated by pure shipowners. April cargoes are still in play and slowly but surely, early May is coming within fixing range. Although there have been over 30 spot fixtures for April thus far, we expect the last remaining handful of end April vessels to soon find suitable cargos. The last done deals ex USG/USEC were concluded at USD 100s H/C and low USD 50 H/F, and owners are attempting to pull freight upwards from here. Time being, the arb is widening and vessels supply looks relatively tight and in control of a few owners for the front of May and it seems freight will increase gradually as time progresses.
