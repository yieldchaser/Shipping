# Lion Shipbrokers Weekly Market Report — Week 43 (23 October 2026)

**Broker**: Lion Shipbrokers Limited (Athens, Greece)  
**Issue Date**: 2026-10-23 | **Report Week**: Week 43, 2026  

## Market Commentary

### Bulkers

The Baltic Dry Index (BDI) closed today at 2,660 points, up 149 points or almost 6% on last Friday's 
2,511 points, bringing a run of four consecutive weekly declines to an end. The recovery was led by the 
Capesizes, with the BCI gaining almost 17% w-o-w to 3,791 points, although the index eased slightly on the 
day. The Panamaxes remained under pressure, with the BPI losing a further 9% w-o-w to 2,109 points, while 
the geared sizes held their ground, with the BSI edging up to 1,887 points and the BHSI steady at 1,020 points, 
its highest level this year:

Sale & Purchase: Second-hand activity eased slightly compared with recent weeks, but values held firm in 
both the dry and wet sectors. In dry bulk, buying interest remained steady across all sizes with a clear tilt 
towards the smaller geared segments, where a broader choice of tonnage makes deals easier to conclude, 
while Japanese-built vessels continued to attract strong demand: the oh/box shaped handysize MOUNTPARK 
(38k blt 2016 Naikai) fetched $ 23.5 mill and the supramax YASA UNSAL SUNAR (55k blt 2007/Mitsui) 
$ 15.1 mill. In the larger sizes, the post-panamax MARITIME KING (96k blt 2011 Imabari) changed hands for 
region $ 19 mill basis forward delivery January 2027. In total, 11 bulk carrier sales were recorded, comprising 
1 post-panamax, 2 ultramaxes, 4 supramaxes and 4 handysize vessels. The tanker market stayed very active 
and firm, with prompt tonnage commanding a premium and Middle Eastern buyers paying top prices: the 
Suezmax ZOUZOU N. achieved $ 103.3 mill, above newbuilding parity, the VLCC VADIN fetched $ 117 mill 
basis prompt delivery Fujairah, almost double the price vessel was sold ($60 mill) just seven months ago in 
April 2026. In containers, buying interest continues to exceed the available tonnage, keeping values at 
elevated levels. 
For a detailed review and additional information regarding this week's transactions, kindly refer to our 
"representative sales" section.

### Demolition

Recycling activity remained thin, with only a handful of candidates circulating, mostly from the 
dry segment, as firm freight markets keep older tonnage trading; in Pakistan, two dry bulk demolition sales

increased demand for specialized vessels, while Bangladesh remains the most competitive destination amid

demand and renewed steel imports from Iran, while Turkey held steady despite slowing local steel demand. 
Looking ahead, possible sanctions relief for certain tankers could bring additional candidates to the market.
