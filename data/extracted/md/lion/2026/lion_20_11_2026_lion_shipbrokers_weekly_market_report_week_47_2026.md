## Market Commentary

Sale & Purchase: The second-hand market kept up its pace, with a further thirty vessels reported sold 
across all sectors (15 bulkers, 11 tankers, 2 containerships, 1 gas carrier and 1 multi-purpose vessel). 
Dry bulk accounted for half of the week's business, with Handysizes once again the most traded size and 
older Capesizes and Panamaxes finding buyers at firm levels. Tanker activity, by contrast, slowed from last 
week's pace as buyers and sellers took stock of the correction in freight rates, although the deals concluded 
show no sign of values giving ground. 
In the bulker sector, 15 vessels changed hands: 2 Capesizes, 1 Post-Panamax, 1 Panamax, 1 Ultramax, 3 
Supramaxes and 7 Handysizes. Korean buyers H-Line Shipping paid USD 39.7 mill for the Chinese-built 
Capesize EVER ABILITY (180,133 dwt, 2011 Qingdao Beihai/China), with her special survey freshly passed. 
The price level is in line with the USD 40 mill paid by Norwegian buyers in early October for the same-age 
GOLDEN MYRTALIA (177,979 dwt, 2011 SWS/China). This suggests that appetite for older Capesizes is 
healthier than it appeared a week ago.
In the Post-Panamax segment, the Japanese-built AURIGA OCEAN (93,549 dwt, 2008 Namura/Japan) was 
sold for USD 15 mill, a strong price for her age as it matches the level paid last week for the two-year-
younger, Chinese-built SHUN CHANG (93,322 dwt, 2010 Yangfan/China). 
In the Kamsarmax segment, the 2019 Oshima-built TAHO AUSTRALIA (81,788 dwt), which we reported last 
week as rumoured at region USD 38 mill, is now understood to have been concluded at USD 38 - 39 mill, 
level with the USD 38.5 mill achieved in early October by the same-age BABITONGA (81,770 dwt, 2019 
Tsuneishi/Japan). 
Vintage Panamaxes with favourable survey positions also attracted buyers: the ANASTASIA (75,331 dwt, 
2006 STX/Korea, SS due 01/2031) fetched region USD 13.2 - 14 mill, in line with the USD 13.6 - 13.8 mill 
paid at the end of October for the Japanese-built AMOY SUNNY (76,598 dwt, 2006 Imabari/Japan), 
although we understand from a trusted source that the ANASTASIA deal is an older sale that has only now 
come to light. 
In the geared sizes, the Crown 63-type Ultramax CARAVOS LIBERTY (63,301 dwt, 2013 Yangzhou

### Demolition

The number of reported recycling deals picked up, but the market remains short of 
conventional candidates: most of the business involved small units, damaged vessels and specialised 
tonnage, and prices were rarely disclosed. The largest unit was the 1977-built FPSO GIMBOA (33,700 ldt), 
sold on 'as is' Walvis Bay basis with delivery to the Indian subcontinent in buyers' option, while the 1972-
built mining vessel GRAND BANKS (6,369 ldt) fetched USD 610 - 612 per LT at auction 'as is' South Africa, a 
level that reflects her specification rather than the general market. Turkey took the MR tanker MINERVA 
VIRGO (11,110 ldt, 2006) together with the drone-damaged GOLDEN SAPLING and AROYAT, while the 
general cargo CHANG FENG HAI (2,343 ldt, 2006) was sold for recycling in China.
Price indications were more or less steady. Bangladesh softened but remains the best-paying destination. 
India recorded the largest gains, as recyclers returned from the Diwali holidays. Pakistan stayed quiet, with 
recyclers stepping back until prices soften. Turkey softened on reduced demand following an accident at 
one of the country's major steel mills.
