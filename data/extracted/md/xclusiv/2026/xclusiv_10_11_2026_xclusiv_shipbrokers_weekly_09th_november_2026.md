## Market Overview

### Editorial: Washington's Blue Wave, Shipping's New Crossroads

The November 3rd US midterm elections reshaped Wash- ington’s political landscape, with Democrats securing both the House and Senate, delivering a significant set- back to President Trump. Inflation, elevated energy prices and the prolonged conflict with Iran weighed heavily on voters. Although the new Congress takes office in January 2027, the outcome could increase pressure for adjust- ments in US foreign, energy and trade policies, with po- tentially significant implications for global shipping.

Oil markets responded sharply. On November 4th, Brent declined 8.7% to USD 94.62/barrel, while WTI fell 8.3% to USD 88.12/barrel, reflecting expectations of a possible de -escalation with Iran. By November 6th, Brent was head- ing towards a weekly decline of approximately 16%, po- tentially its steepest since 2020. Nevertheless, continued attacks on vessels in the Persian Gulf demonstrate that diplomatic expectations do not necessarily translate into im- mediate improvements in maritime security or energy supplies.

For tanker markets, the implications are particularly interesting and potentially contradictory. A gradual restoration of Middle Eastern exports could increase crude shipments, supporting VLCC and Suezmax employment. Equally im- portant is the potential restocking of commercial and strategic petroleum reserves following months of disruptions. Replenishing inventories could temporarily generate substantial additional cargo volumes, supporting tanker utilisa- tion and freight rates.

However, more oil moving does not necessarily mean greater tanker demand. Normalisation through Hormuz and po- tentially the Red Sea could shorten trading distances, while reduced congestion, waiting times and diversions would release previously constrained tonnage. This improvement in fleet productivity effectively increases available vessel supply without additional newbuilding deliveries. Consequently, an initial freight recovery driven by restocking and cargo availability could eventually face downward pressure as tonne-mile demand and vessel utilisation adjust.

Russia introduces another important variable. October's Washington–Moscow agreement facilitating Russian diesel exports, accompanied by partial sanctions waivers, demonstrated Trump's prioritisation of energy affordability, alt- hough actual US deliveries remain uncertain. Democratic control of Congress could encourage tougher sanctions and stronger support for Ukraine. Renewed restrictions might extend trading distances and reinforce shadow fleet activity, while diplomatic progress and sanctions relief could gradually restore Russian cargoes to mainstream tanker markets, potentially reducing tonne-mile demand.

Shipbuilding policy also faces uncertainty. Washington's ambition to strengthen domestic shipyards and challenge Chinese dominance retains bipartisan appeal, although implementation costs and effectiveness may face greater scrutiny. The proposed extension of the Section 301 sus- pension until January 9th, 2027, could provide temporary relief for Chinese-built tonnage. Nevertheless, uncertain- ty over future port charges could influence chartering flexibility, vessel liquidity, secondhand values and new- building preferences. Meanwhile, a Democratic Congress may challenge additional tariffs, although presidential executive authority limits immediate policy reversals. Any easing of US-China trade tensions could support containerised and dry bulk trade, while persistent restrictions would continue reshaping sourcing patterns and trading distances.

From an S&P perspective, owners and investors must distinguish temporary geopolitical freight premiums from sus- tainable earnings. Strong spot markets do not automatically justify elevated asset values when normalisation could rapidly increase effective fleet availability. Ultimately, the elections introduce competing forces rather than a straightforward bullish or bearish outlook. For shipping, the decisive factor is not simply whether geopolitical tensions ease, but how cargo volumes, trading distances and available fleet capacity respond.
