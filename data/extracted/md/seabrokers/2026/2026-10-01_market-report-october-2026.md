# OSV Market Round-Up

## GREEN LIGHT FOR JACKDAW BUT ROSEBANK APPROVAL STILL NOT GUARANTEED

The United Kingdom Government, at long last, has granted approval for Adura to commence production from the Jackdaw gas field in the Central North Sea. Prime Minister Andy Burnham has backed a "pragmatic approach" towards the UK oil & gas industry as we progress through the energy transition but it still seems somewhat farcical that final approvals have taken so long to be granted for the GBP 2.1 billion (USD 2.8 billion) development when the Jackdaw platform has already been installed and the development drilling campaign with the Valaris 122 jackup was completed the best part of 12 months ago. It would have seemed illogical to discard the substantial investment and carbon footprint from the extensive work that has already been undertaken at Jackdaw, for gas that will feed straight into the UK's domestic distribution network in a matter of weeks, in favour of a greater reliance on gas imports from overseas.

At peak production, Jackdaw is expected to increase the UK's domestic gas production by around 6%. The field is projected to supply gas for up to 11 years, contributing an estimated GBP 2.9 billion (USD 3.9 billion) to the UK economy and protecting hundreds of jobs.

Alongside the approval of Jackdaw, the UK Government has announced a GBP 2 billion (USD 2.7 billion) Energy Wealth Fund to reinvest North Sea oil & gas taxation revenues to finance renewable energy projects and to support the UK's transition to low-carbon power. The UK Chancellor John Healey announced the creation of the fund with the initial GBP 2 billion commitment from the Treasury, with future oil & gas receipts to be allocated to the fund to support renewable energy infrastructure, creating skilled jobs and economic growth across Scotland and the wider UK.

While the government has granted the go-ahead for Jackdaw production to commence, Adura is still waiting for confirmation on whether equivalent approvals will be granted for production from the Rosebank field west of Shetland. As Adura continues to progress its development drilling programme at Rosebank with Odfjell semisubmersible Deepsea Atlantic, there is still a small risk that this campaign will prove to be futile. According to recent media reports, the UK Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh is of the mindset that Rosebank production should not be approved. This perspective stems from the fact that the majority of UK oil production is exported due to limitations with the nation's refining capabilities. As Rosebank is primarily an oil field (90%), it is likely that the majority of Rosebank's oil will be sent via shuttle tankers to the likes of Antwerp, Rotterdam or somewhere similar rather than feeding directly into domestic supply, even though it will contribute towards European supply and may well return to the UK as refined product. Nevertheless, those same media reports have also suggested that the Energy Secretary may be overruled by the Prime Minister Andy Burnham and the Chancellor John Healey, who are both reportedly in favour of Rosebank approval. A final decision is expected before the end of 2026.

# OSV Market Round-Up

## NORTH SEA AHTS MARKET SOFTENS BUT PSV SUPPLY REMAINS LIMITED

**North Sea AHTS owners have experienced a rapid reality check in recent weeks with charterers appreciating far more stable trading conditions although PSV supply has remained limited on the spot market.**

The average monthly fixture rate for large AHTS vessels (>22,000 bhp) was GBP 43,794 (USD 58,746) in October. That is the lowest average in 2026 by a considerable margin. The next lowest monthly figure this year was in January when the average rate was GBP 90,283 (USD 121,108). The October figure was the lowest monthly average recorded since September 2025.

Supply levels have increased in recent months with the arrival of a long list of vessels following the completion of project charters in other regions. That has moved the market into a much more favourable state for charterers. However, there is still a fine balance between supply and demand so charterers would be well advised to avoid complacency.

On the flip side, PSV supply has continued to be limited on the spot market. We even had a scenario in October where Equinor was forced to charter the Siem Opal AHTS vessel for a "supply duties" fixture due to limitations on PSV availability. The notion of fixing up an AHTS vessel for supply duties would have seemed inconceivable a few months ago.

## HAVILA PSVs IN DEMAND IN NORTH SEA

Havila Shipping has had an extremely productive period with multiple North Sea E&P companies committing to term charters with Havila PSVs. In the UK, NEO NEXT+ has fixed up both the Havila Fanø and Havila Herøy on multi-year contracts. The vessels were initially chartered on 45-day firm contracts while longer term agreements were finalised. The PSV pair have now formally been fixed up to NEO NEXT+ on three-year firm contracts with three further one-year options available. The Havila Fanø (2010-built) and Havila Herøy (2009) are sister vessels that were constructed to the Havyard 832 CD design. In the southern sector of the North Sea, Peterson den Helder BV has exercised an option to extend its contract with the Havila Borg until September 2027; Peterson is holding further options to extend the charter by an additional 15 months thereafter. The Havila Borg, another 17 year-old Havyard 832 PSV, has been on hire to Peterson since August 2024. In Norway, Equinor has exercised one-year options on its contracts with both the Havila Charisma and Havila Foresight, extending their charters until December 2027 and January 2028. Both vessels have been on hire with Equinor (and predecessor Statoil) since they were delivered in 2012 and 2008 respectively. The Charisma is a Havyard 833L PSV while the Foresight is an MT 6010 MkII PSV.

## SEVERAL MORE PSVs RETAINED BY EQUINOR

In addition to the Havila extensions detailed above, Equinor has continued the process of prolonging its contracts with several more of the incumbent PSVs in its fleet. This continues a common trend that has been prevalent in recent months. To provide some recent examples, DOF has secured one-year contract extensions with Equinor for the Skandi Flora and Skandi Mongstad, Eidesvik has seen its Viking Avant extended until the end of 2027, Island Offshore has received a one-year extension for the Island Crusader, Skansi Offshore has picked up a one-year extension for the Kongsborg, and Østensjø Rederi has secured a six-month extension for the Edda Fram, prolonging this charter until April 2027.

## ROSS OFFSHORE CHARTERS PSV TRIO IN NORWAY

Ross Offshore, part of the AGR/Aqualis organisation, has chartered a trio of PSVs on behalf of Harbour Energy for operations in the Norwegian sector. Ross Offshore has selected the Aurora Galaxy from Aurora Offshore, the Energy Swan from Golden Energy Offshore, and the Viking Queen from Eidesvik to provide support for Harbour's upcoming drilling campaign with the Transocean Norge semisubmersible. The vessels are scheduled to go on hire to Ross Offshore in early January 2027 for an estimated period of circa 265-290 days. The Aurora Galaxy will be a new addition to the North Sea PSV fleet; she is currently working in the Mediterranean Sea offshore Egypt.

# CONOCOPHILLIPS CONSIDERS SALE OF NORWAY BUSINESS

Ekofisk Complex (c/o ConocoPhillips)

ConocoPhillips is considering the potential sale of its Norwegian operations in addition to the Teesside terminal in the United Kingdom following the receipt of an unsolicited offer for the linked North Sea assets. ConocoPhillips has confirmed that it is now reviewing the approach.

The prospective buyer and terms of the offer have not been disclosed, and ConocoPhillips has stressed that the assets will be retained if it is determined that the unsolicited offer does not meet its valuation threshold. Reuters has reported that analysts from Capital One Securities have placed a value of around USD 7 billion on the assets involved. ConocoPhillips operates the Greater Ekofisk Area in the Norwegian North Sea; its producing assets include Ekofisk, Eldfisk, Embla, Tor and Tommeliten A in addition to a range of non-operated assets.

## NORWAY PARTNERS ADVANCING NUCLEAR

A Norwegian-led collaboration including Vard and Island Offshore is preparing to develop and test an integrated nuclear propulsion system, targeting a first vessel deployment in either 2031 or 2032. The Marine Nuclear Propulsion System (MarNuPS) project, led by Emerald Nuclear Norway, will move from engineering studies to physical integration and testing, with a full-scale test facility planned at Stette near Ålesund in 2027. The project will combine Emerald Nuclear's 5 MW electric Gem microreactor technology with energy storage, power conversion, vessel power management, automation and bridge control systems.

## OSV Market Round-Up

## HAT-TRICK FOR PETROBRAS AT MORPHO WELL

Having initially encountered hydrocarbons at its Morpho exploration well in August, Petrobras has since encountered second and third oil-bearing intervals via the same well. Morpho has been one of the most eagerly anticipated exploration wells in recent years, carrying the potential to open a major new hydrocarbon province for Brazil. The Morpho well was drilled with Foresea drillship ODN II in the Foz do Amazonas basin near the maritime border with French Guiana in the Equatorial Margin off northern Brazil. Following well abandonment operations, the ODN II will be moved to the Potiguar Basin for an exploration well at the Mae do Ouro prospect. BP is also on a hot streak in Brazil, with hydrocarbons encountered at its Tupinambá exploration well in the Santos Basin; this follows last year's Bumerangue discovery.

In other developments, Petrobras has recently issued two new PSV tenders that contain multiple requirements. One of the tenders is for Petrobras' PSV 4500 category and the other is for the PSV 3000 category. Four-year firm contracts are on offer with commencement scheduled between June and October 2027. A 20-year age restriction has been applied.

## CHOUEST PARTNERSHIP FOR HORIZON MARITIME

The Chouest Group and the founders of Horizon Maritime Services (Horizon Maritime) have entered into a strategic partnership, including the acquisition of Horizon Maritime from CFFI and its lender HPS. This transaction will increase the Chouest Group's global coverage by enhancing its coverage across the Canadian maritime sector.

The Horizon Maritime fleet consists of the Horizon Arctic AHTS vessel, the Horizon Dragon PSV, the Polar Prince icebreaker, and three ice-strengthened truckable tugs (Horizon Aurora, Horizon Chinook and Horizon Glacier).

Horizon Maritime is also a joint venture partner of KOTUG International in KOTUG Canada which operates three vessels: the K.J. Gardner AHTS/OSRV and two RAsalvor 4400-DFM escort tugs (SD Aisemaht and SD Qwiy Aanitsa Sarah).

Dino Chouest of the Chouest Group has stated that "combining Chouest's global fleet expertise and technology with Horizon's local footprint across Canada's vast coastlines opens up incredible growth potential across Canada's East Coast, West Coast and Arctic regions."

# Feature vessel

## STAFFORDSHIRE

Bibby Marine has launched its new electric commissioning service operation vessel (eCSOV), which has been named **Staffordshire**, at Armon's shipyard in Vigo, Spain.

The eCSOV will now undergo further outfitting at the shipyard, including the installation of its 24.4 MWh lithium iron phosphate (LFP) battery system, ahead of delivery and a formal naming ceremony in 2027. Bibby Marine originally signed the construction contract in April 2024.

The vessel is designed around battery-first propulsion, with its energy storage system serving as the primary power source rather than supplementing conventional propulsion. According to Bibby Marine, this could reduce the vessel's operating costs by more than 40% in self-charging mode and by around 70% when operating solely on offshore charging.

Kongsberg Maritime and Corvus Energy are working with Bibby Marine on the vessel's propulsion and battery systems respectively, while Stillstrom is developing the vessel's offshore charging capability.

The eCSOV will be the fifth Staffordshire in the Bibby fleet. The first two were launched in 1894 and 1929. The third was built by its previous owners in 1950 and renamed Staffordshire when Bibby acquired it in 1964. The fourth was launched in 1977.

## UNION VEGA ORDERS TWO FIRM NEWBUILD PSVs

Union Vega Offshore, a joint venture between Union Maritime and the Vega Maritime Group, has placed a firm order for two newbuild PSVs to be constructed at the Fujian Mawei Shipyard in China. The contract comes with options for four further vessels to be built.

The PSVs will be built to the new Breeze Z 4409 design, giving them a length of 84m and a deck area of 900m². The vessels will be equipped with accommodation for up to 44 persons, and they will incorporate battery-hybrid propulsion and a moonpool. They will also be further prepared for the potential future installation of a 50-tonne Active Heave Compensated crane, making them suitable for light construction, geophysical and seismic support operations in addition to conventional platform supply duties. The first firm vessel is scheduled for delivery in 2028.

## EIGHTH SPP40 NEWBUILD PSV DELIVERED TO SPEC

Sinopacific Engineering & Contracting (SPEC) has accepted delivery of the CL SPEC Dancy from the Nantong CIMC Sinopacific Shipyard in China. The vessel is now being mobilised towards Nigeria. This is the fourth SPP40 PSV delivered to SPEC from this yard, and the eighth total vessel in the series, with the other four deliveries coming from the Nantong Rainbow Shipyard in China; at least three more SPP40 PSVs are being built for SPEC at Nantong Rainbow. All eight of the delivered vessels are either in West Africa already or mobilising towards the region. The SPP40 vessels have a length of 81.75m, breadth of 17.4m, deadweight of 4,000t and a deck area of 800m².

## ONE MILAHA DELIVERY FOLLOWED BY A LAUNCH

## ALESSANDRO VOLTA BEGINS SEA TRIALS

**Prysmian's new VARD 9 18-designed cable-laying vessel (CLV) Alessandro Volta has begun sea trials in Norway.**

VARD commenced construction of the CLV in September 2024 at its shipyard in Tulcea, Romania. She then mobilised from that facility to VARD's Søviknes yard in Norway for final outfitting.

The 192.8m vessel is equipped with three carousels with a combined 19,000t load capacity, with the system of tensioners able to sustain a maximum tension of 60t, and she is capable of conducting power cable lay in water depths of up to 1,500m.

# FIRST POWER DELAYED FOR EMPIRE WIND

Equinor has delayed first power from its 810 MW Empire Wind offshore New York until the first half of 2027, having originally been scheduled for 2026.

This is due to delays with the US-built vessels required for turbine installation. These include specialised US-built tugs and barges, still under construction, designed to transport turbine components from the South Brooklyn Marine Terminal to the 2026-built installation vessel Maersk Viridis, which arrived in Mississippi in August. The USD 7.5 billion development is currently 80% complete.

The remaining work on the development includes the installation of the project's 54 Vestas 15 MW turbines, a campaign now expected to begin in early 2027, several months later than originally planned.
