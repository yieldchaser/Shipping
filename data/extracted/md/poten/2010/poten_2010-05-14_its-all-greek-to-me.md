# It's All Greek to Me

As Greece's debt problems have blossomed from a national concern into a near existential threat to the European monetary union, and even beyond, the particulars of its economy and culture have come under harsh scrutiny. Likely facing a future of economic challenges and regulatory adjustments, Greece has an important asset in its storied shipping industry. Positioned to deliver another strong showing in 2010, shipping can provide an example to lead the nation's economy towards recovery.

One of the strategies credited with helping to insulate leading Greek shipping companies from financial chaos at home in recent years is an increasing focus on tankers and dry bulk carriers. While limiting their exposure to the harder‐hit container shipping market, Greek companies have played an important role in driving recent tanker markets. As shown in the charts below, the Greek‐controlled portion of the tanker fleet has grown over the past fifteen years, from 287 vessels in 1995, to current control over 642 vessels. As their portion of the market has grown, Greek companies have proven themselves to be among the most experienced and savvy operators in a notoriously volatile market; experience which could serve their homeland well during the years to come. ### Back in the Saddle

Financial trouble at home has not slowed Greek activity in the sale and purchase market this year, which has been particularly active after a lull during 2009's down market. Public and private Greek companies have reportedly both been among the most active in placing orders at Far Eastern shipyards this year. An examination of the orderbook of tankers ordered by Greek companies reveals that Suezmaxes have been the class of tanker favored by Greek owners in recent years. As shown in the chart below, 45 Suezmax vessels are expected to be delivered to Greek companies by 2013.

VLCC Suezmax Aframax Panamax MR While family money has been the traditional means by which Greek companies established their shipping presence, they have also led the way in public finance for the shipping sector. Of the 34 shipping companies across sectors to go public between 2005 and 2009, 20 were Greek. That leading role has continued this year, when about $740 million of the estimated $2 billion of total public shipping funds were successfully raised by Greek companies. Just as they were hurt by the sharp drop in world trade which occurred in the aftermath of the global financial crisis, these publically‐listed Greek shipping companies find themselves well placed to benefit from emerging signs of a recovery in world trade.

Greek shipping companies have undoubtedly taken an international approach to business and have benefitted from the market nature of shipping. Their ties to the global economy have kept Greek owners relatively insulated from their own domestic problems, at least for now. Expect these companies to thrive as positive sentiment in world trade continues; the real question is what does this mean for Posidonia?
