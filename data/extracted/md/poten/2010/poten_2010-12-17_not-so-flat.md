# Not So Flat!

It's that time of year again! The Worldscale Association has published the new schedule of nominal freight rates for oil tanker trades that will take effect in January 2011. The nominal freight rates, or flat rates, are intended to provide a standard of reference across various voyages for charterers and shipowners trading in the spot tanker market. The Worldscale market rate is merely a percentage of the published flat rate. Its level and volatility reflect the strength and weakness of the market on a daily basis. The chart below shows the change in flat rates for a selection of key tanker trades since 2007. The primary drivers of the change in flat rates are bunker prices and port charges. As can be seen from this chart below, flat rates are on the rise for 2011 by and average of 20% for the trades depicted. On a practical basis, however, the change in flat rate does not have a material effect on the earnings of shipowners and the total cost of freight to charterers as the Worldscale rate will adjusts to keep parity. More than anything else, this annual adjustment means that tanker market participants will have to recalibrate their sense of how much each Worldscale point means on each particular trade in dollars and adjust their commercial expectations accordingly.

The Worldscale bunker prices used in the determination of the flat rates are also shown on the graph. The Worldscale bunker assumption is an average of assessments of 380 cSt fuel oil between October 1 to September 30 provided by LQM Petroleum Services.
