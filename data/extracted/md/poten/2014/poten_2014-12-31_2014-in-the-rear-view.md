# 2014 In The Rear View

### Fig. 1: VLCC Rates (AG-Chiba / 270,000 tons)

### Update and Review of 2014 Tanker Opinions

The end of the year is often a time of reflection and subsequent planning for the new year ahead. In this last issue of this year we will review the current status of the most relevant topics that were discussed throughout the year.

Not surprisingly, the significant rise of U.S. crude oil production in recent years and the resulting changes in crude oil trade was discussed several times. The possibility of U.S. crude and condensates exports is an important factor, as it affects both the Jones Act market and the international tanker markets. Since the last time this was discussed (on October 17), there are no significant changes, condensates are still only exported on a limited scale and crude exports to Canada have remained steady in the second half of the year at about 350-400 Kbbl/d. However, this is a noteworthy increase over the 134 Kbbl/d exported in 2013. In November, BHP announced that they will export lightly processed condensates, as BHP determined that these qualify for export. This week, the U.S. government has clarified their definition of processed hydrocarbons that can be exported. This allows companies to 'Self Classify' processed condensates, similarly to many other export products. As a substantial portion of shale oil production is in the form of condensates, this would make more condensates available for exports after passing through a relatively simple crude distillation tower.

Suezmax trades saw more significant changes than any other tanker sector as their primary trade route (WAF- U.S.) was virtually eliminated. They found replacement employment in the growing WAF-Europe trades and managed to increase their market share in AG-India trades. Reduced imports from the Caribbean freed up more crude for exports to the Far East. This, combined with a reduction in VLCC based imports from the Middle East has lead to a tightening of VLCC availability for Caribbean exports to the Far East.

In the January 10 and May 16 Tanker Opinions, we discussed U.S. crude imports from Saudi Arabia and noted how resilient they had been given the growth in domestic production. The expectation was that Saudi Arabia would maintain market share through imports for the Motiva refinery as well as through their pricing policy. However, in the second half of the year, U.S. imports from Saudi Arabia declined to levels last seen during the 2008/09 financial crisis. The question is whether this is just a temporary decline or if these levels are going to be the new normal. Only time will tell.

Contango and floating storage was another topic that was discussed several times throughout the year, including last week. Since the last issue, Brent spot prices have declined even further and the contango between spot and 6-7 months into the future has widened by about $0.70 to over $5. We still believe that a potential contango-driven storage play can develop in the coming months, unless global demand increases dramatically and unexpectedly or oil production is curtailed, either through OPEC action or unexpected geopolitical events.

We believe that 2015 has the potential to develop into an exciting year and we wish all our readers a healthy and prosperous New Year.
