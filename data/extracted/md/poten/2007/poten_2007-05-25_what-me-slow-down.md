# What? - Me Slow Down!

A generation from now, energy scholars may search for clues on why, how and when American motorists' seemingly inalienable right to burn high-quality, cheap and plentiful gasoline eroded. Without the current emotional fog of blame-game antics obstructing their view, these researchers might wonder why the simple and effective fuel savings actions of driving slower or driving less or driving more efficient vehicles were not practiced more by today's drivers to relieve their burden of gasoline price.

Rather than promoting proven conservation measures, US politicians seem inclined to take a whack at cutting gasoline price by re- crafting an anti-trust law. If its present immunity is revoked, OPEC members might be sued for what US legislators view as a price conspiracy.

### Europe Sails to the Rescue

The 140 thousand bbls per day rise in imports between 2004 and 2006 represents the typical gasoline output of a 280 thousand barrel per day refinery. No new East Coast refineries, however, were built during this period. Shown on the graph below, to supply this incremental import volume, some 177 thousand bbls per day came from European countries to offset a 37 thousand bbl/day drop in imports from other countries. New higher quality standards for US gasoline likely prompted some countries, unable to meet these standards, to reduce their gasoline stock exports to the US.

### Suing for Relief

Lately the price pain to US motorists needed to entice extra gasoline-like stocks from foreign sources has become more and more burdensome. A form of relief from these gas pains has been touted as a price conspiracy suit against OPEC. Most industry experts, though, might consider this proposed course of action as being both largely symbolic and largely ineffectual. Hopefully, OPEC won't adjust their production targets if threatened by this type of action.

If a price conspiracy suit does come to pass, the ensuing litigation might see a long and convoluted journey through the courts. While price squeezed US motorists are made to wait for any lawsuit derived relief they will continue to pay up for gasoline - unless they slow down, drive less or scrap inefficient vehicles.
