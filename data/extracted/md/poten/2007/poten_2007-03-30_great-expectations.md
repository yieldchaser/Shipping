### Safe Seas

Both oil companies and shipowners are facing an increasingly strict regulatory environment over the next several years. Regulations regarding hull types and emissions are likely to impact the activities of owners and charterers alike. To its credit, the tanker industry has significantly improved its safety and environmental record. Given the amount of sea transportation that is conducted daily, the exemplary safety record of the industry in the few years is notable achievement.

### Future Project Planning

The project timeframe and investment horizon for major oil companies can very often transcend several decades. Similarly in the tanker market, the fleet development requires a long-term plan. Many owners and charterers are working to secure longer term time charters and are reacting to ensure that vessel supply is adequate for projects that are due to come online in the future.

With full shipyards and high capital costs, owners are forced to make chartering and fleet management decisions well in advance of the actual delivery of the vessel. The current vessel orderbook is over 1,000 vessels and represents between 30 - 40% of each of the current sectors with orders already starting to be placed into 2011.

### New Supplies

The increases in existing crude oil production areas around the world have created more demand for tankers on traditional trade routes and strong revenues for oil companies. The advent of new crude oil supplies have led to incremental ton-mile demand for tankers moving on new trade routes. For example, the Mediterranean and West African crude oil moving to the Far East has served to absorb significant amounts of available double-hull tonnage in the market. As new projects continue to be developed challenges will arise and the tanker market will be faced with new opportunities.

### Energy's Future

Oil companies have come under increased scrutiny from the public and governments to invest more in alternative energy sources. Many are working to test marketability and effectiveness of solar, wind and biofuels. Tankers are working to become compliant with the carriage of biofuels and the increase ethanol trades. But for the long- term, biofuels are likely to remain only a small part of the overall picture.

With several challenges facing the oil industry today- from regulations, to price movements to human factors- the crude oil, refined product and tanker markets remain subject to significant swings. But with focus on customer service and safety the top performers will be here to stay.
