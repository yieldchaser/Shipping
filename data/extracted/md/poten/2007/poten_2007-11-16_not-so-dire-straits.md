# Not So Dire Straits

As the days get shorter, one market that becomes of particular interest is the Black Sea. Overall traffic, restricted transit to daylight hours and weather can add to congestion and complicate trading and transit of the Turkish Straits. For example, on Monday of this week, the Bosphorus was closed due to heavy current. The chart below shows the northbound and southbound delays through the Turkish Straits, both the Bosphorus and the Dardenelles. The data shows that the delays are significant during the last months of the year and through the first quarter. With the winter season just around the corner, shippers and charters should be prepared to get in the queue.

### Relative Rates

The chart below shows the comparative spot market rates across several key Aframax markets in terms of timecharter equivalents (TCE). Since the start of October, Aframax rates on the benchmark trade of Caribbean to the U.S. have been in the range of $19,000 per day. The average rates for the same time period in the Black Sea have been nearly double that level, at $35,500 per day.

CAR - USG BSEA - MED X MED As would be expected, the trend for Black Sea rates closely mirrors that of the cross-Mediterranean (X MED) trade, but at substantially higher levels on a TCE basis. For the entire time series, July through this week, rates in the Black Sea have been 50% higher than those of vessels trading in the Mediterranean. Delays in the Black Sea can serve to significantly reduce the pool of available tonnage leading to spikes in charter rates. Expect to see increasing volatility in these rates through the duration of the winter.
