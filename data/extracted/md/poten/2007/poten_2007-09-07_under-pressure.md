# Under Pressure

In anticipation of the Organization of Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC) meeting next week, both the crude oil and shipping markets are busily trying to assess whether production quotas are likely to see movement. While changes in the stated production quota levels sometimes have little effect on the amount of crude oil seen in the market, decisions made by OPEC serve the important purpose of conveying the Organization's sentiments toward price and crude oil supply in general. Significant declines in production can have large effects on the tanker market, especially over a prolonged period of time. To speak in approximate terms, a 1 million barrel per day decline in Arabian Gulf exports would eliminate demand for 40 VLCCs assuming all of the barrels go West or 20 VLCCs assuming all of the barrels go East. Although this calculation is more extreme than what is taking place in the market today, the amount of cargos reportedly lifted out of the Arabian Gulf declined from 116 in July to 102 in August.

With crude oil demand forecasts staying firm for the balance of 2007 OPEC may respond to more pressure to produce. As far as the tanker market is concerned, any OPEC production increase will be linked to higher demand in key long-haul export regions, primarily the Arabian Gulf and West Africa.
