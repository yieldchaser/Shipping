# Not So Dismal Science

With the latest release of economic indicators, some economists are hinting that the United States could be headed for weaker times. Thanks to continued consumer spending, the US economy, among others, has been chugging along at relatively strong levels despite sustained high energy prices. However, cynics are beginning to ask, "Is this the beginning of the end?" While most data is subject to interpretation, the public's perception about the health of the economy is certainly changing. The Conference Board index of consumer confidence, shown in the chart below, declined last month to a level that is below the 2006 annual average. Understanding the intricacies of how consumer psyche drives hard economic data is challenging. One outcome that needs to be avoided is an economic down-turn as a self- fulfilling prophecy.

### Keep Working

At this point, the unemployment rate in America is at 4.5%, remaining within the lower end of the traditional "comfort" range of between 4-6%. If unemployment continues at manageable levels, then consumer spending and economic growth can continue. The Federal Reserve seems to be nervous about the increase in the Consumer Price Index (CPI) which has been steady since the start of 2007. Energy has seen a substantial increase of almost 7% since the start of the year, where other components such as food and beverage have increased only 1%. ### The Price of Energy

One interesting finding by the Conference Board is the decline in the proportion of people that plan to buy a home. It is suggested that high fuel prices are deferring many big ticket purchases in households - and apparently houses themselves. While consumers perceive their purchasing power as limited, they are likely to follow by changing their behavior.

One anomaly in the consumption data is the continued growth in gasoline demand that has seen an overall increase of 2.3% over the same time last year. While prices are already above their historic average levels for this early in the summer season, the high price of gasoline does not appear to be having a dampening effect on demand.

If consumers begin to believe that energy is expensive and that goods are too expensive, then there will be a natural contraction in consumption. This contraction will quickly contribute to further decline in economic growth. For the time being, there are no alarming signs that the end of the run is upon us, but the higher gasoline and food prices that are expected for this summer may further pinch consumption of other goods. The good news for the world economy, however, is that Americans love their cars and food.
