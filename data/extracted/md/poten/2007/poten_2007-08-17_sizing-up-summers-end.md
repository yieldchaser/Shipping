# Sizing Up Summer's End

For the past several weeks the spot freight market has been experiencing weaknesses. Regardless of market levels today, owners and charterers are still challenged with the task of making long-term strategic decisions. Although not a perfect indicator, the fundamentals of crude oil supply can offer a guide as to what can be expected of rates in times of uncertainty. The chart below shows the weekly U.S. crude oil stocks and VLCC AG to USG TCEs for 2006 and 2007. In general, TCEs have a tendency to decline when crude oil inventories are near their highest levels. Stock levels in combination with refinery utilization paint the plausible picture of increasing pressure to replace crude oil inventory when confronted with steady product demand.

### From Stocks to Rates

Shown below is a chart highlighting the tendency of time charter equivalents (TCEs) for VLCCs Arabian Gulf to US Gulf for 2006 and year- to-date 2007 versus their corresponding US crude oil inventory. TCEs and inventories are the weekly assessed. The chart shows that there is a relationship between the directional level of the U.S. crude oil stocks and freight rates - as stocks rise, TCEs tend to decline.

From a longer-term seasonal perspective, the anomaly came in the second-half of 2006. Whereas in other years rates tended to firm through the fourth quarter of the year, freight rates in fourth quarter 2006 (shown on the first chart) were decidedly weaker than those seen in the summer months. The pattern for 2007 to date seems to be more in line with historical seasonal patterns, with weaker rates in the second quarter. Perhaps this can serve as an indication that a stronger fourth quarter can be expected this year.

### The Missing Link

One element that links the degree to which stocks are depleted and the level of imports required is refinery utilization. The chart below shows the U.S. refinery utilization as a percentage of operable capacity for 2006 and 2007. At this point, the percentage of refinery utilization appears to be in line with 2006 levels, although actual operable capacity this year is higher. This trend suggests that utilization has the ability to creep above 90% in the next several weeks.
