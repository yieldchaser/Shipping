# High Stakes Energy Poker

Posturing and jockeying recently by supply-side energy market players created what some might construe as a high stakes game of poker. The US Government's cards propose a massive boost in ethanol output. US refiners' cards are their expansion plans for existing plants. These plans, always subject to checks for economic viability by refiners, now seem undermined by the proposed ethanol targets. Completing the game's threesome is OPEC's concerns that mounting talk of increased biofuels production will stifle refinery expansion and hence lessen the need for its members to supplement their production capabilities and their ability to supply crude oil. A dispassionate spectator of this game is left wondering which players are bluffing.

While the process to turn corn into ethanol has been practiced for centuries, the process for teasing it from cellulosic sources is still merely a laboratory curiosity. The idea of leaving no tree behind in the quest for fuel ethanol might have a long road to travel before an economically viable commercial process is ever reached.

Legislation that bets on the scale-up of unproven technology to provide 1.4 million bbl/day of cellulosic-based ethanol to the nation's fuel supply within 15 years may create risks. To some, this bet is tantamount to betting that people would walk on the moon within a decade of the first successful flight of a liquid-fueled rocket in the mid- 1920's. It is one thing to push technology to new levels of performance as this legislation would. But it is quite another thing to pin consumers' hopes of ample fuel by 2022 from a still-to-be commercialized method.

### OPEC's Mixed Signals

While OPEC contends that fossil fuels will remain the dominant energy source for the foreseeable future, any actions to reduce oil dependency seems to provoke this organization's concern. As the world attempts to diversify towards alternative energy sources, the in- roads of alternatives into oil's share of the energy mix seem inevitable - but slow to happen. The degree and pace of erosion of OPEC's market share, in part, hinges on the price spread between the cost of oil and the cost of tax-subsidized sources of alternative energy. If this spread widens because either oil's price rises or these tax subsidies increase, the use of alternatives will likely blossom.

### Sailing Through

Clearly, the stakes are high for planning new production capacity - in either the upstream or downstream energy sectors. For tanker owners, the stakes are high too in planning future fleets. Estimating the needs of vessel sectors is made more difficult by the way these three players sashay around the issues of crude oil and petroleum product supply. For instance, if the promise of cellulosic ethanol is not fulfilled, the equivalent of nearly five more daily MRs arrivals to the US could be needed to slake the nation's thirst for fuel.

Whatever the outcome of these three players' game of posturing and jockeying, part of petroleum's supply will be linked to its demand by tankers. But planning future vessel fleets in the face of uncertain domestic fuel supply can be stressful and it is not an exercise for the faint of heart.
