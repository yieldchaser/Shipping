# Bang for the Buck

The increase in vessel prices across all sectors has clearly raised the stakes for both shipowners and finance groups that are interested in asset plays. Shipping is an international business with various currencies changing hands. This adds another dimension of financial risk. Managing exchange rate risk is more of a concern today as the dollar continues to weaken against major currencies such as the yen, the euro, the won and the pound. And with most charter hire payments in US dollars, this can turn into a significant loss for some. Perhaps though the ones at greatest risk are the shipyards.

In dollar terms the price of a VLCC has essentially doubled from $69.3 million in January 2002 to a price of around $140 million today. Over the same period, however, the price of the US dollar in South Korean won declined by approximately 30%. While there is no doubt that prices have fundamentally increased, the magnitude of the increase has been buffered in some countries by the strength in their currencies.

A prolonged trend of US dollar devaluation could prove to be more challenging for shipyards as they work to balance costs. Labor and other costs incurred by yards are paid in local currency subject to their own set of inflationary pressures. With most newbuilding contracts in US dollars, it takes more US dollars to buy the same yen or won priced vessel.

It is difficult to see this situation changing. In fact our view would be that the dollar will continue its fall, further impacting shipyard profitability. With rates in the doldrums, tanker ordering will likely remain muted in the short-run, but exchange rates won't be enough to slow the roll that the market has been on.
