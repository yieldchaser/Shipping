# Ever Volatile Times

Life has always been volatile in the tanker industry. While market participants are accustomed to long-term changes in rates, volatility in daily rates is clearly increasing. An analysis of ten years’ worth of data suggests greater recent volatility of Worldscale rates for aframaxes in the Caribbean/USA trade. Below is a comparison between this voyage’s rates for the period 1997 to 2002 and those of 2003 through July 2007. The chart shows that the latter period’s average, low and high Worldscale rates were all greater than those of the prior period. To assess more exactingly, the standard deviation around the average – a proxy for volatility – is also increasing.

### Average Performance

The chart below contrasts the monthly average January to July 2007 Aframax Worldscale rate for this voyage to rates from 1997 to 2006. Through July of this year, average monthly rates appear to travel around their counterpart averages during 1997 to 2006. Although the 2007 rates have consistently been higher than the historic period’s lowest average monthly rate, they have not tested the historic high ones, either.

While the past may not necessarily be a guide for the future, it is interesting to note an improvement in the average monthly rate during the fourth quarter. This quarter also has wider swings – or volatility – between the monthly average lows and highs of rates.

### One Day WS Change

At WS rates of 105, modern vessels earn about $8,000 per day on a timecharter equivalent basis. This is far below the nearly $60,000 per day being earned earlier in the year. While there is no way to know if history will soon repeat itself, increasing rate volatility combined with the seemingly typical end-of-year increase in rates in the fourth quarter suggest this is a temporary lull.
