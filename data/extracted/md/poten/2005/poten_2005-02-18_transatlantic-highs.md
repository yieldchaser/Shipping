### What's Happening?

The Europeans have been "too" successful in inducing automobile owners to switch from gasoline to diesel fuel. European diesel fuel refinery capacity has topped out, diesel prices have risen, and the resulting price differential between the United States and Europe is greater than shipping costs. Arbitrage trading takes advantage of the lower cost of shipping eastbound (WS 160) than westbound (WS 270) because of the availability of product carriers returning to Europe in ballast.

### What's Going to Happen?

U.S. consumption growth will keep ahead of refinery creep and clean product imports from Europe will continue its measured growth, constrained only by the exhaustion of spare European gasoline refinery capacity. When this occurs, gasoline imports will come from more distant sources. The emerging shortage of diesel fuel is not going to change in the short term. As long as the price differential between European and U.S. diesel exceeds shipping costs, cargoes will move. But there is a limit to gasoil exports from the U.S. Gulf in terms of U.S. capacity to produce diesel fuel that meets higher European specifications. Overall things look rather bullish for handymaxes trading transatlantic; the highs have it!
