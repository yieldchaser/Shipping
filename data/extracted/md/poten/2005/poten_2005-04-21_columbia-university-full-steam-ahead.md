# Columbia University: Full Steam Ahead!

New York City has long been considered a financial epicenter and is home to the world's largest stock exchange on Wall Street, the NYMEX for commodity trading and a very active over-the-counter bond market. In recent years energy has taken a more central role in world affairs and New York City is once again playing a leading role. The Center for Energy, Marine Transportation and Public Policy at Columbia University (CEMTPP) has fostered the development of a burgeoning community of energy enthusiasts in both industry and academia. Located in Manhattan's Upper West Side, the Center has been at the forefront in providing a platform for serious research, analysis and education in the energy and transportation markets.

Formally started in 2000, the Center offers a concentration for graduate students who are interested in energy and transportation management and related public policy issues. The Center was created through the collective resources and efforts of the University, as well as the foresight of some industry leaders in the energy and marine transportation fields.

Since its rather humble beginnings with a small selection of courses and occasional seminars, the Center now offers some 15 courses in subjects ranging from the "Quantitative Methods in Energy Business and Policy Analysis" to "The Geopolitics of Energy." Additional course offerings focus more specifically on marine energy transportation.

### It Takes Industry Support Too!

In addition to the dedicated faculty that teaches the core coursework the curriculum is enhanced with industry executives. It is not surprising to see the head of a transportation group for a large oil company lecturing students about the intricacies of serving the needs of a complex worldwide refining system or the CEO of a large public tanker company sharing his views on the tanker market. Tanker owners, energy traders and bankers also grace the classroom with personal insights into the challenges of running their organizations.

Another component that has contributed to the success of the program is providing a forum to hear various views on energy issues. This past week, for example, the Center hosted three events that best exemplify its growing influence. On Tuesday night, the Center hosted "A Conversation with Lee R. Raymond," the recently retired CEO of ExxonMobil. CNBC's Maria Bartiromo interviewed Mr. Raymond. Her questions and his answers provided a cohesive picture of the positive outlook for economic growth and the energy market. The event was then opened to questions from the audience. A clear picture of the key success factors for growth at ExxonMobil emerged. Mr. Raymond attributed it to talented people within ExxonMobil, clear communication of objectives and the need to take a long- term view of the business.

On Thursday, Robert W. Haines, ExxonMobil's Manager for International Relations discussed ExxonMobil's outlook on world supply and demand trends through 2030.

On Friday, an all-day symposium was held focusing on one of the fastest growing energy consumers, China. The seminar entitled, "Feeding the Giant: The Domestic and Global Impact of China's Energy Needs" was held in conjunction with the Weatherhead East Asian Institute as the topic for the Second Annual China Symposium. The morning's discussions focused on economic growth and the impact on the global energy market, as well as issues discussing energy security.

The Center has stepped in and filled a clear need within the industry. For further information about the Center you can visit their website at www.cemtpp.org.
