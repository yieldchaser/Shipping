# Aframaxes in the Black (Sea)

With the exception of the VLCC market, rates on nearly all other large tanker sectors are up significantly from their month ago lows. Two of the more impressive increases occurred in the Baltic and Black Sea trades. For example, since last week, time charter equivalents (TCE) for Aframaxes trading from the Black Sea to ports in the Mediterranean increased by 174% to $74,500 per day. ### Get in the Queue

One driver of this recent upswing in rates has been the increase in the delays for transits through the Turkish Straits. It is typical that delays in the Bosphorus and Dardanelles increase in the winter months due to shortened daylight transit hours and weather. Currently, these delays are on average seven days in both directions. The chart below shows the weekly average delays for northbound and southbound transit through the two straits.

Northbound Southbound As delays mount, total voyage and idle time increases and vessel availability decreases. Charterers quickly work to find prompt tonnage and rates experience rapid increases.

### Turn Up the Volume

To compound the effects of the vessels delays, the Mediterranean overall has seen a higher degree of cargo liftings this month as compared to November and October. New crude oil exports from the BTC pipeline and increased production from traditional suppliers, such as Libya and Algeria, have contributed to the rise of the Mediterranean as a key crude oil exporting region. Further increases in production will take place in the coming year through the Ceyhan terminal in the Mediterranean and Black Sea ports.

### Winter Wonderland

In light of OPEC's decision to maintain current quota levels, production volume outlooks remain firm in the near term creating strong demand for tonnage in the Mediterranean. It is likely that delays in the Turkish Straits will worsen during the winter months complicating lifting schedules and adding volatility to rates. While the market is certain to recalibrate from today's levels in the coming months, increased pressure to find prompt vessels will remain an obstacle in the near term keeping upward pressure on rates.
