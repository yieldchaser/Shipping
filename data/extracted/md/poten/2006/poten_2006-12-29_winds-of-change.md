# Winds of Change

Although the tanker market is accustomed to everyday fluctuations in rates and regular movements in the freight markets, there are some trends that develop over the longer-term. In general, the sizes of tankers within traditional designations have gotten larger. These larger vessels have larger engines increasing fuel consumption, but also allowing vessels to travel faster. ### Keeping Up

In order to better reflect the changes taking place in the fleet, the deadweight designation for Handymaxes will be revised in January 2007 from 37,500 to 49,999 DWT to between 37,500 and 59,999 DWT. Panamaxes will simultaneously be reclassified as vessels between 60,000 and 79,999 DWT. This move will effectively reclassify approximately 70 vessels from Panamax to Handymax designation.

### Faster Fleet

The assumptions that are used in calculating the daily time charter equivalent (TCE) have also been updated. Despite larger size vessels in general, technologies have led to faster vessels, but with higher fuel consumptions. The table below displays the changes that will affect VLCCs.

At the end of the year, owners pay their expenses in cash. The incremental changes to the reported TCEs should provide a more representative benchmark for their expected cash flows.
