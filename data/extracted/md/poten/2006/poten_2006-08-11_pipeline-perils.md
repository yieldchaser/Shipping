# Pipeline Perils

U.S. crude oil supplies have been under scrutiny for several years as foreign imports have been on the rise not only to meet increased demand, but also to make up for declining production. The crude oil market experienced a bit of a shock this week when BP announced that production out of Prudhoe Bay, Alaska would be significantly reduced for pipline repairs.

The initial indications suggested of loss of 400,000 barrels per day of Alaskan North Slope (ANS) crude, but this was later revised downward to about 200,000 per day. Recent history has indicated it does not take much of a supply threat to send oil prices through the roof and stir tanker rates.

### Not Just a Drop in the Bucket

Although the outages from the Alaskan fields are smaller than the aggregate daily production from the U.S. Gulf due to last years hurricanes, the trade associated with (ANS) crude has unique attributes that are likely to have a ripple effect on the tanker industry.

There are already indications that atypical trading will be one of the fall-outs of this supply disruption. This week alone, several Panamaxes were fixed to carry crude though the Panama Canal from the Caribbean, and from the U.S. Gulf via lightering of VLCC's carrying middle east grade crudes.

### Fixtures To USWC

### Number of Fixtures

The graph above illustrates the number of fixtures with discharge zones on the U.S. West Coast (USWC) taking place between January of 2005 through today. The August 2006 fixtures are already higher than July's figures. In 2005 there were a total of 287 fixtures with discharge zones on the USWC, about 67 percent which were on panamaxes. 168 of the 287 fixtures in 2005 loaded on the West Coast of South America. Year to date 2006 there has already been a total of 159 fixtures with discharge zones on the USWC, 80 of which loaded on the WCSA. Not surprisingly, none of the spot market activity, until this week, included movements from the U.S. Gulf to the USWC. With the Alaskan pipeline operating at reduced capacity, it is assessed that the market will see an increase in the number of cargoes discharging on the USWC, particulary from the Middle East as the crude quality is similar to ANS.

### Don't Get Too Excited

One of the things that oil companies do well is crude oil and refined product system optimizations. The initial indication of 400 kbd lost production is already down to 200 kbd. We expect this project will be completed ahead of schedule and for the market to return to "normal", whatever that is these days.
