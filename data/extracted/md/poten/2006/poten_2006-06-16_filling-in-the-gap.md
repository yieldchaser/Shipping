# Filling In The Gap

While many countries have recently been struggling to maintain their crude oil production, the world has yet to truly be affected by a shortage in supply. There are several instances today where countries are unable to produce at peak levels. While much of the focus is on the cases where crude oil capacity has suffered, many countries have increased their production to satisfy global demand. The large consumers of crude, both East and West of Suez, have seen increases in demand in the past year despite rising prices. The Arabian Gulf has largely provided the supply to meet these demands and in recent weeks the rise in VLCC rates for voyages ex-AG has demonstrated the effects of these market conditions.

### Developments in Demand

The graph on the previous page shows the total reported VLCC spot liftings ex-AG for the first 6 months of 2005 and 2006. The discharge area for these liftings has been designated East of Suez, West of Suez or Red Sea. In comparison to the first half of 2005, total VLCC liftings in the AG have increased by about 8%, from 626 in 2005 to 674 in 2006, while the amount of cargo moved has increased 12%. When comparing just May and June, the number of scheduled liftings to the United States increased by 5 fixtures this year over last year to a total of 86, while those to the Far East have increased nearly 10%, from 101 in 2005 to 110.

The increase in the amount of cargo moving speaks to the higher vessel employment and an increase in ton-mile demand for tonnage. The spot market demand picture for VLCCs has been stronger this year in comparison to last year.

### Making it to the Market

Iran has a reported 20 million barrels in storage- roughly ten VLCCs. As has been the case in today's market, the incentive to keep crude in storage is largely the result of higher forward contract prices for crude as compared to spot prices. As long as the economics make sense charterers will hold tonnage for storage placing further pressure on supply.

The increase in the amount of cargo moved is the largest indication of a rise in demand overall for VLCCs this year. The combination of lower vessel availability in the AG and demand has created the upward pressure on freight rates. The use of vessels for storage creates an even tighter market as vessels that are anticipated to return to the AG take longer than expected. May and June of this year posted higher-than-average amounts of cargo moving out of the AG and with restricted production in other regions of the world, this trend is expected to continue as the economic incentive to reduce inventory does not yet exist.
