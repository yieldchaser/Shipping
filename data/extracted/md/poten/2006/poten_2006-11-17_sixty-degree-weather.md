# Sixty Degree Weather

As New York City continues to see unseasonably warm temperatures, visitors to town are not the only fortunate ones. Like many others on the East Coast, most New Yorkers are enjoying a respite from heating oil bills. With distillate stocks near historically high levels, product tanker demand has remained relatively weak. Handymax rates have experienced steady downward pressure in the Caribbean market in the past few weeks as demand for imported distillates and gasoline has subsided. The chart below shows the time charter equivalent (TCE) and Worldscale rate for St. Croix-New York (38kt) since the start of 2005. With daily earnings currently as low as $10,800 per day, there are certainly a few New Yorkers that are wishing for a cold snap.

### Well Stocked

### Replenishing Supplies

In the short-run it appears as though the United States is well stocked with distillates. However, as demand for distillates increases through the winter season, finding replacement supplies will now be even more of a challenge. Sulfur requirements for diesel products have further intensified the tight refinery situation. Traditional suppliers such as Venezuela, Aruba, Canada and the Virgin Islands will have to increase efforts to reduce the sulfur content of the products they ship to the United States.

Changes in regulations have the potential to create arbitrage opportunities, as regional imbalances of product specifications become apparent. From a shipping perspective, new trade routes for clean products may develop as a result if traditional suppliers become slightly disenfranchised.

With cold weather just around the corner, the consumption of distillates will quickly deplete existing supplies. With a set amount of domestic production capacity, the dependence on imports will likely return. There's still a long winter ahead, so be prepared to bundle up.
