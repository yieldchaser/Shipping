# Passing The Buck

As gasoline prices in the United States have begun their climb towards $3.00 per gallon, the chorus of industry bashing has been growing louder. What is impressive is how little knowledge is displayed about the oil industry and the underlying causes of today's relatively high prices at the pump. Perhaps even more disturbing is the list of potential "solutions" being offered to help mitigate the effect of higher prices.

### Short-Term "Solutions"

• A $100 tax rebate if the family income is less than about $200,000 per year • Eliminate the federal tax on gasoline (about 18 cents per gallon) • Delay implementation of new environmental regulations • Stop adding crude to the Strategic Petroleum Reserve

### Long-Term "Solutions"

• Impose a wind-fall profit tax • Open Alaska to more drilling • Provide large tax credits for buying hybrid cars And every congressman's favorite: launching investigations into price gouging.

### Crude Oil Costs Are Up

The graph below shows the NYMEX wholesale cash prices for gasoline and crude for the past 12 months and the ratio of crude price to gasoline. Guess what, since January the price of crude accounts for about 86% of the price of wholesale gasoline. Industry practice traditionally assumes that gasoline prices are dependent on the price of the crude from which it is refined. However recently, some industry experts are beginning to theorize that gasoline prices may actually be driving crude prices. The correlation between the prices was nearly 70% in March, and slightly lower than that in prior months. In the first 10 days of April the correlation was as high as 88%, but the last 10 days of the month saw a radical drop to only 20%.

### Pennies on the Gallon?

Understanding what makes up the retail price of a gallon of gasoline is the first step in solving the problem. Crude oil is not the only cost element. Taxes, distribution and marketing and refining are also components. Consider that almost half of the price of a gallon is driven by inputs other than the price of crude. For example in February prices averaged $2.28 per gallon: Gasoline Price Components (February 2006) National Average Taxes 20% $0.46 Refining 10% $0.23 Distribution & Marketing 11% $0.25 Crude Oil 59% $1.35 Price Per Gallon 100% $2.28 Figures from the American Petroleum Institute indicate that eight states in the US tax gasoline upwards of $.50 per gallon, while California, Hawaii and New York tax more than $.60 per gallon. The U.S. Department of Transportation estimates that the annual gas tax burden for every person in the United States (including children) amounts to $271. Congress' proposed $100 rebates (for taxpayers only) would therefore only partially alleviate gasoline tax "burdens."

### Supply Chain Squeeze

Changing environmental regulations that have increased the demand for ethanol have also contributed to supply tightness for gasoline. The effect on price was not unexpected in Washington.

The American public and congress is now playing the blame game with accusatory fingers generally pointed at "Big Oil," as the industry is often referred. Investigations into price gouging and the discussion of assigning windfall profit taxes is tops on the agenda of some Senators. Washington was aware of the effects the MTBE mandate would have on price and the impending market tightness due to increased demand in system with fixed refining capacity.

There are a variety of half-baked plans aimed at changing gasoline prices, most of which are punitive to oil companies or simply useless to consumers. In summary, between the effects of taxes and the creation of pollution standards, the power to affect gasoline prices and hence demand lies with the government. The difficulty is in finding the political will to actually make meaningful changes that might reduce demand. That won't be anytime soon so expect markets to remain firm as demand keeps going up.
