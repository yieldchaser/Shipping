# The Barrels From Brazil

### South America's largest producer boosts export trades

Amid all the fluctuations in global crude oil trades resulting from the fallout of the Covid-19 pandemic, the changes in Brazilian exports stand out. Brazil was on a path towards higher crude oil production and exports, but progress was disrupted by the outbreak of the Corona virus, which hit Brazil hard. However, the prospects for the Brazilian oil sector remain bright and (post Covid-19) we expect to see continued growth in export volumes on both Suezmaxes and VLCCs. In the short-term, however, while the country grapples with the virus, there may be some volatility around domestic demand and production, which will impact refinery intake and exports.

As can be seen in the first chart on the right, crude oil production in Brazil was relatively stable until mid-2019, ranging between 2.4 million barrels per day (Mb/d) and 2.7 Mb/d. Production picked up in the second half of 2019, when several new offshore oilfields came onstream. The key driver of this (and previous) production growth is the discovery and development of a prolific seabed formation known as the pre- salt. Pre-salt resources were first discovered by Petrobras in 2005 and production started in 2009. Now pre-salt represents most of Brazil's output. In 2019, output from pre-salt areas reached 634 million barrels, up 21.5% from the previous year and equivalent to 62% of Brazilian production, which exceeded 1 billion barrels for the first time last year. Further increases are expected. For 2020, the IEA forecasts Brazil's production to reach 3.05 Mb/d, up 5% from 2019. Output for 2021 should be another 8% higher and average 3.29 Mb/d.

All this production growth bodes well for the tanker market. Over the last five years, crude exports from Brazil have doubled from around 735,000 b/d in 2015 to 1.546 Mb/d in the first half of 2020. Despite Covid-19, Brazil has exported more crude so far this year as can be seen in the two charts comparing 2020 Suezmax and VLCC export voyages with volumes for the previous year. The Suezmaxes serve a relatively diversified customer base in both the Atlantic and the Pacific Basin, while the VLCCs are predominantly targeting China with a sprinkling of other Asian countries. Because Petrobras sells most of its crude on a delivered ex-ship basis, the growth in exports has propelled them up the Poten Largest Dirty Charterer Rankings. For the 1st half of 2020, Petrobras ranked 3rd overall, up from 11th in 2019 H1.

The IEA expects that production (and exports) will dip temporarily in the second half of 2020 as scheduled field maintenance, postponed from the first half of the year due to Covid-19, will resume from September. While this may temporarily slow chartering activity, long-haul trades from Brazil will benefit from higher production in 2021 and beyond.
