# Saudi Arabia The Kingdom Of Oil

### The influence of the OPEC oil powerhouse is significant

Saudi Arabia is the largest oil producer and most important member of the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC). Together with Russia, they largely determine the direction of the cartel. As OPEC's largest producer (and exporter) the actions of the Kingdom have a profound impact on the crude oil tanker market. Saudi Arabia also controls a large domestic refining complex. The vast majority of its refining output is exported and as a result, the Saudis are a major player in the international refined products trades as well. While the major energy developments involving Saudi Arabia are done within the OPEC (or now OPEC+) framework, we thought is it would be interesting to take a closer look at the crude oil and refined product flows of the Kingdom in recent years to highlight their importance.

A recent country analysis from the EIA summarizes the importance of Saudi Arabia for the oil markets: the country holds 15% of the world's proven oil reserves, it is the largest exporter of crude oil in the world. Another important fact is that Saudi Arabia holds the world's largest spare crude oil capacity. This gives the country the flexibility to quickly increase or decrease oil production and thereby have a significant influence on the global oil markets.

Saudi Arabia has used its market power several times in recent years. In March 2020, the Saudis were not able to convince Russia that significant production cuts were needed amid the rapidly spreading coronavirus pandemic. To maintain market share and convince Russia to come back to the negotiating table, the Saudi's used their spare capacity to increase production from 9.8 Mb/d in March 2020 to 11.6 Mb/d in April. As a result of this unprecedented output increase coinciding with rapidly falling demand, oil prices collapsed and the OPEC+ decided to cut production by 9.7 Mb/d starting in May 2020. The Saudi inspired production boost in April created a massive spike in tanker rates as vessels were needed for both transportation and storage.

In June 2020, Saudi production fell to 7.7 Mb/d, 3.9 Mb/d or 33% below the level two months prior. Since then, production has gradually increased, with the exception of February-April 2021, when Saudi Arabia temporarily cut production by an additional 1.0 Mb/d due to concerns over the impact of new Covid-19 variants and high global inventories. These cuts have since been reversed and as of November 2021, Saudi production returned to pre-Covid levels.

As can be seen in [Chart 1](file:///C:/Users/Dell/Github/Shipping/data/extracted/charts/poten/2021/poten_2021-12-10_saudi-arabia-the-kingdom-of-oil_chart1.png), production and exports fairly closely follow each other over time, and higher production typically means higher exports and tanker demand. However, there have been periods where this relationship did not hold. In September 2019, in the aftermath of the attack on Saudi Arabia's Abqaiq oil processing facilities, production declined by 1.35 Mb/d, while exports only declined 637,000 b/d. The Saudi's used inventories as a buffer. In May 2020, the massive production cut of 3 Mb/d was closely matched by an equivalent reduction in exports. However, the situation was different in the early months of 2021. While Saudi Arabia cut production by 1.0 Mb/d, exports only decreased by 500,000 b/d, drawing down inventories by 500,000 b/d to make up the difference.

Saudi Arabia is also an important refining center and refined product exports from the Kingdom support ton-mile demand for both medium-range and long- range product tankers. Product exports peaked in 2018 and have experienced a significant decline in recent years. Declining exports have coincided with a significant reduction in product imports. It may have been a policy decision to satisfy domestic demand with domestic supply rather than have high levels imports and exports at the same time. Saudi product imports have remained subdued since the beginning of 2019, but exports have started to recover after bottoming out at 500,000 b/d in February 2020. They are running at more than 1 Mb/d since April of this year. Good news for product tanker ton-mile demand.

Crude oil and product exports from Saudi Arabia have experienced significant challenges in recent years, but both are firmly on a path to recovery. Saudi Arabia will continue to be the tanker markets' main driver for decades to come.
