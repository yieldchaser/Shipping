# Stranded In Suez

### What will be the impact of the Suez Canal Closure?

On Tuesday morning, March 23, the 20,388 TEU containership 'Ever Given' ran aground in the Suez Canal, blocking all traffic through the canal. So far, efforts to dislodge the vessel with tugs, have been in vain. As we are approaching spring tide, chances might improve in the coming days, but some people are speculating it could take several weeks to free the ship. The Suez Canal is a vital link between the Atlantic and the Pacific. In the 2019/2020 fiscal year, an average of 53 ships passed through the canal every day. By now, a total of several hundred ships are waiting on both sides of the canal. Ships are starting to re-route via the Cape of Good Hope (around South Africa), rather than wait for the re-opening of the canal. While oil tankers are not the main users of the canal, for certain routes the canal is critical. This week we will take a look at which oil trades are most affected.

VLCCs rarely use the canal as they cannot transit in fully laden condition due to a combination of beam and draft restrictions. They sometimes use the canal in a so-called drop-and-pick move, where they discharge part of the cargo into the Sumed pipeline, which runs from the Ain Sukhna in the Red Sea to Sidi Kerir in the Mediterranean, transit the canal and reload in Sidi Kerir. However, in 2020 the volume of such moves has declined significantly; from about 5 voyages per month in 2019 to just 1.5 in 2020. VLCCs travelling from the Atlantic to the Pacific or vice versa normally use a route around the Cape of Good Hope, especially when freight rates are as low as they are now.

As the name already indicates, the primary crude oil tanker class using the canal is the Suezmax tanker. Suezmax tankers can pass through the canal in fully laden condition, and are often the most economical choice, if the canal is involved. The main trading routes that use the Suez Canal originate close to one side of the canal and have a destination close to the other side, so they maximize the time saved by using the canal. The main crude oil routes are from the Black Sea and Eastern Med to Asia and from the Middle East to Southern Europe. Overall, in 2020, an average of about 45 laden Suezmaxes per month passed through the canal.

Data from Lloyd's List Intelligence shows that in 2020, about 7 Suezmaxes per month travelled from the Middle East to South Europe, mainly from Iraq (6.8 voyages/month) to Greece and Italy. Another 3.7 ships sailed from the Middle East to the East Med, mainly to Turkey. Smaller volumes move from the Middle East to UKC and to the Americas. Some of these trades can relatively easily be replaced by using the Sumed pipeline. There are media reports that the pipeline operator has already approached traders with cargo onboard ships that are waiting.

The other main cargo stream goes in the opposite direction, from the Black Sea and East Med to Asia. About 10.7 Suezmaxes per month carry crude oil from the East Med/Black Sea to the Far East, mainly China (5.8) and Korea (1.5). Additionally, India is importing about 4.3 Suezmax cargoes per month from the East Med. Exports from Libya through the canal have increased again in recent months (3 in December) but are still below 2019 volumes (5 per month). Some Suezmaxes from the Americas and NW Europe also use the Suez Canal to travel to Asia.

It is going to be interesting to see how the Med market reacts now that it looks like the closure might take longer than originally anticipated. Suezmax rates have started to move up in the Med. Cargoes that were sold to Asian refineries might be re-sold to local refiners and be diverted. Refineries might be able to buy additional crude from Atlantic sources, but these will take some time to arrive. Cargoes from the AG could still reach the Med via the Sumed pipeline, although at added costs. This would also absorb tankers located in the Med that move the cargoes from Sidi Kerir to their final destination. As there will be less new tonnage coming into the area, local tanker rates could increase further in the coming days.
