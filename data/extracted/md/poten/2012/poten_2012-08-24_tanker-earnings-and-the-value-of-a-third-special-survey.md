# Tanker Earnings and the Value of a Third Special Survey

### Scrapping at 15 years may be required

Miserable dirty tanker earnings and strong marine fuel prices are placing unrelenting pressure on tanker owners. With the dirty tanker market clearly out of supply/demand equilibrium -- from both weak demand fundamentals and continued supply -- the market requires more drastic supply measures. Owners have begun to scrap 20-year old and younger vessels, as they become less marketable in a weak market, but they may have to consider the economics of paying for a third special survey, at 15 years of age, and then trading the vessel -- versus the scrap value of the vessel.

VLCC earnings AG-East are mired below zero This week, as shown in the following chart, VLCC spot TCE earnings crashed further into negative territory, as bunker prices approached $700/mt and as freight rates languished in a weak environment. The weak earnings do reflect the seasonal weakness associated with lower refiner requirements ahead of the Autumn maintenance season, and the FFA curve for the Baltic TD3 route for 260 AG-Japan does show a seasonal bounce, but the earnings outlook implies further earnings and cash-flow shortfalls for owners. The TCE earnings implied by the FFA curve and a backwardated bunkers forward curve, reach only $20,000/day by late 2013, below the breakeven levels for modern VLCCs including financing costs.

With this earnings environment, dirty tanker owners have embraced demolition as an investment alternative, and scrapping levels have rebounded in late-2011 and during 1h12. As shown in the following chart, dirty tanker demolition has exceeded 0.8% of the fleet in each of the quarters during 1h12, which is close to levels seen in compliance with the IMO-mandated phase-out of single-hull tankers in 2010.

Granted, these values are below the $23 million levels seen in mid-2011, but they may be high enough to question the value of paying a potential $4 million for the cost of a third special survey and trading the vessel to its next survey date. Examining a hypothetical fifteen-year old VLCC with a potential scrap value of $18.5 million (basis 42,000 ldt) and a $3.8 million third special survey cost, the owner would need to earn an average of $23,300/day over the next 2.5 years to achieve a 10% return on capital. This analysis also assumes that the vessel is valued near scrap at age 17.5 years, using the same $440/lt scrap price, and that it is 100% fully utilised post-drydocking.

Of course, a probable market recovery prior to early/mid-2015, when the vessel would be due for its fourth intermediate survey, should allow even 17.5 year old vessels to achieve values at a greater premium to scrap than those seen in this weak market environment. Using a similar analysis, a 17.5 year old vessel that could achieve $30,000/day prior to a likely demolition at age 20, might be worth $24 million in early- 2015. Assuming a sale price of $24 million for the hypothetical 15-year old VLCC in 2.5 year's time, the breakeven TCE would decline to $17,700/day. This average earnings ...breakevens would be more palatable $17,700/day Parting with cash requires faith in market prospects level might seem achievable to an owner loath to scrap a 15-year old, but as the earnings malaise lingers, the breakeven economics deteriorate. If stuck with low utilisations and earnings of $12,000/day for the next 12 months, then the breakeven TCE on the final 18 months of the holding period would rise to $22,200/day, assuming full utilisations and no idle time.

This still might represent a reasonable risk/reward trade-off for an owner, but parting with third special survey cash does require strong faith in a vigorous tanker market recovery, once supply ebbs. Negative earnings are not helping that faith.
