# Slowing Fleet Growth Makes a Reappearance

### sectors diverged on Clean and dirty tanker supply growth

Although the current macroeconomic environment is weighing on both the dirty and clean tanker sectors, the earnings performance of these two markets has diverged since 2010 from different supply trajectories. After single-hull removals slowed dirty tanker fleet growth during 2009-10, supply has accelerated from heavy deliveries, aggravated by the 2010 ordering burst. In contrast, the abrupt halt in clean tanker ordering that started in 2008 has flowed through the orderbook, slowing product tanker fleet growth. Continued declines in clean growth, coupled with regional refinery displacement, has underpinned the product tanker hypothesis for years, but weaker dirty tanker fundamentals and sustained supply growth have contributed to an increasingly-gloomy outlook for the sector. Slowing deliveries, continued demolition and restrained ordering, however, are suggesting further deceleration in dirty supply growth, which should drive the ultimate recovery in the dirty sector by 2014.

This annualised pace often influences near-term movements in tankers earnings, as large impulses of tonnage supply can overwhelm demand and shape market sentiment.

The sharp rise in VLCC and Suezmax growth during the summer coincided with a crude tanker market dealing with the loss of demand provided by the 1.5 mbpd global inventory build during 1h12. With 14 VLCC deliveries and 17 Suezmax deliveries during May through July, short-term growth hit 8% and 15% for VLCCs and Suezmaxes, respectively. Overall sector growth hit a 7.9% annualised pace, and earnings collapsed.

1q07 1q08 1q09 1q10 1q11 1q12 Following this impulse of tonnage supply, however, dirty tanker deliveries slowed and demolition picked up during the month of August, allowing growth to slow to a 3.8% annualised rate during 3q12. Still, dirty tanker demolition did experience its seasonal pattern of declining during the summer monsoon months on the Sub-continent, slipping to 2.4 mdwt in 3q12, after hitting 3.4 mdwt of removals in 1q12. Although the pace of VLCC demolition eased, Suezmax and Aframax owners maintained the demolition pace, with six Suezmaxes and five Afras leaving the fleet during 3q12.

Suezmax and Aframax scrapping has remained steady during 2012 Lack of elderly tonnage and market conditions pushing demolition towards 20 years Large number of dirty tankers facing costly 4th special surveys in 1q13 Absent a rate revival, these vessels should face the breakers soon This demolition response from the sustained earnings malaise is encouraging, as owners may finally address the supply/demand imbalance and take supply actions that would allow the sector to escape the 80% utilisation area that is keeping earnings near cash operating costs. One constraining factor on scrapping has been the lack of elderly demolition candidates, as the IMO single-hull regulations forced the removal of middle- aged tonnage. With a modern fleet and weak market conditions, however, 20-year old vessels have become untenable in the spot market and the average demolition age has rapidly declined from 25.6 years in 2008 to 20.1 years in 2012 to date.

As shown in the chart above, the number of dirty tankers facing fourth special surveys will jump to 3.2 mdwt in 1q13, with six VLCCs and four Suezmaxes due for this costly cash outlay. Seasonal demand and slowing fleet growth should allow a modest recovery in dirty tanker earnings during the winter, but absent a strong earnings surge, owners are likely to send these vessels to the breakers.

Of course, the dirty tanker supply/demand imbalance is so dire that owners will need a more aggressive supply response than the 2.5% of the fleet that next year's 8.0 mdwt of special survey candidates would represent. Still, the market is showing initial signs of rectifying the problem. During the month of August, the dirty tanker fleet actually shrank for the first time since 2010, and with the continuing runoff of the orderbook and slowing deliveries in 2013, continued supply discipline from owners could see the fleet grow by only 1% or so in 2013.

Dirty supply could slow to 1% by end-2013 Amidst the gloom, biggest forecast risk is to the upside Could the unloved dirty sector actually outperform in the future?

In fact, the greatest forecast risk to the current negative outlook for the dirty tanker sector is to the upside. Any meaningful recovery in aggregate demand and industrial production during 2h13, occurring during a slowing in tanker supply, could provide a strong utilisation and earnings response. Such a macroeconomic improvement may seem fanciful in this uncertain environment, but it remains an upside risk.

### Percent of Fleet

Dirty Fleet Clean Fleet Meanwhile, the inconceivable could even occur. As market participants reject the dirty tanker sector on its gloomy outlook, these orders have vanished. Conversely, as owners and financial investors embrace the product tanker investment hypothesis and the "eco- design" vessels so cheerfully offered by yards, clean orders are rising, particularly for MRs. So the slight uptick in the clean orderbook, shown in the above chart, is worrying. Currently, the clean tanker sector is enjoying the benefits from a pause in ordering, but the dirty sector may be the next to gain from this dynamic. The general disdain for the dirty sector and the ensuing supply response could provide a strong cyclical response in 2014-16, while sustained clean ordering may cap its earnings upside. Broad asset class revulsion almost always provides opportunities.
