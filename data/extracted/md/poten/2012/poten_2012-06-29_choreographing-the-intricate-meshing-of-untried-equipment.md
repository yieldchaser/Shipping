### Motiva's new 325 kbpd unit could be out until

A Delayed Launch Choreographing the intricate meshing of untried equipment during the commissioning of a new refinery sometimes experiences mis-steps. Motiva's troubled start-up of their 325 kbpd Port Arthur refinery expansion underscores this observation. Stung by equipment corrosion incurred during its initial operation, the new capacity now sits idle while the extent of damage is evaluated.

The incremental 8 MB of crude would represent an additional four VLCC cargoes over a couple of months prior to start-up. However, Vela, the shipping arm of Saudi Aramco, the 50% shareholder of Motiva, increased its number of AG-USG VLCC fixtures by a larger amount during 1q12. In fact, Vela's fixture volume rose from an average of 7.3 per month in 2011 to 11.3 per month in 1q12, or a difference of 12 cargoes during the quarter over the rateable volume of 2011.

Vela fixed eight extra cargoes in 1q12 over stock build needs Jump in fixtures during May reflected higher crude run rate from anticipated start-up Desire to cut crude stocks has slashed AG-USG fixture volumes AG-USG fixtures should recover once Motiva gets inventories to desired levels The apparent excess of eight VLCC cargoes over those required for the Port Arthur stock build suggest a few possibilities. Port Arthur could have increased the share of Saudi crude over the 55-60% average during 2011, or perhaps other Motiva refineries, such as its 235 kbpd Convent, LA unit might have taken some of the extra cargoes during 1q12. The most likely explanation is that other USG refiners imported additional Saudi volumes as part of the US and global crude inventory build ahead of Iranian sanctions and courtesy of Saudi Arabia's heightened 10 mbpd output.

The large jump in AG-USG fixtures in May would have partially reflected Motiva preparing for the continuous crude flow of 325 kbpd into the new unit, which would require an additional five VLCC fixtures a month, assuming 100% Saudi and other AG crudes into the new unit. Indeed, Vela increased its AG-USG fixtures to 17 in May, or five to six cargoes above its 1q12 average, so this pattern seems consistent with the anticipated start-up of the new unit. Overall, AG-USG fixtures rose by 12 cargoes in May over its 1q12 average of 20 stems, with additional activity from BP and KPC adding to the Vela sums.

Of course, Motiva Port Arthur's operating difficulties and subsequent shutdown has left the refinery with 50 days of crude inventory for its original 275 kbpd plant. The extra crude would represent roughly $700 million of incremental working capital, so Motiva would be keen to reduce stocks by lowering import volumes. In June, Vela slashed its fixture volume to five cargoes, which suggests very little, if any Saudi crude imports to Port Arthur. With the refinery traditionally running approximately 100 kbpd of domestic crude, a cut in AG imports would allow Motiva to bring crude stocks back in line within 45-60 days, assuming a 100-150 kbpd stock draw.

For tanker owners, the prospects of Motiva's expansion providing more trips for VLCC carrying oil from the Arabian Gulf into the US Gulf have temporarily faded. In fact, the need to cut Port Arthur stocks has cut into VLCC demand. Motiva should return to its 2011 import patterns towards the end of the summer, once it corrects its inventory levels, but any chartering boost from the expansion will have to wait until Motiva assesses the damage and repairs the new unit.
