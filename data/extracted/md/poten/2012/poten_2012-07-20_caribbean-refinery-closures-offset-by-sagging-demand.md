# Caribbean Refinery Closures Offset by Sagging Demand

### weak demand difficult

During the first quarter of 2012, the dual closure of Valero's 235 kbpd Aruba refinery and the remaining 350 kbpd of capacity at Hovensa's St. Croix facility offered the potential that the loss of this Caribbean refining capacity would provide a boost to product tanker demand. Long a source of clean product imports to the USAC, the loss of this short-haul supply would require imports from longer-haul locations for a region shedding its own refining capacity, so the hypothesis went. Although tonne-miles have risen sequentially, the continuing US product demand malaise has muted the effect.

As shown in the chart above, PADD1 clean product imports from Caribbean locations have plunged in early-2012, with the closure of the two refineries, according to the latest data from the US Energy Information Agency (EIA), which included April 2012 data. After averaging 220 kbpd during 2011, PADD1 clean product imports from Caribbean sources fell to 74 kbpd in 1q12 and 53 kbpd in April.

As illustrated in the chart on the following page, PADD1 clean product imports have slid steadily during the past several years, on weakening regional product demand, which has facilitated the replacement of lost Caribbean supply to the USAC. In fact, although PADD1 clean imports rose by 38 kbpd during 1q12 from 4q11 levels, European and Canadian supplies rose by 90 kbpd and 48 kbpd, respectively, more than offsetting the 107 kbpd sequential decline from the Caribs. With these shifts in sourcing, PADD1 clean product tonne-mile demand did rise sequentially during 1q12, as shown in the following chart. Regional demand during 1q12 jumped by 3.6 billion tonne-miles (btm), or 18%, from the depressed levels of 4q12, as a 3.5 btm jump in European tonne-miles more than offset the 1.6 btm loss from the Caribbean. The rebound in Indian gasoline blendstock imports added another 2.1 btm to demand.

### Sources: EIA, Poten

The initial data has supported the market hypothesis -- as the loss of Caribbean supply provided a sequential boost in tonne-mile demand -- but weak product demand has still left tonne-miles 3.9% lower yoy during the first quarter, despite a rebound from 4q11. PADD1 gasoline consumption - the key import demand driver - was 69 kbpd yoy lower during 1q12, and 80 kbpd lower yoy in April. PADD1 clean product imports rose from 705 kbpd in March to 816 kbpd in June, according to the EIA's weekly data, suggesting incremental demand, but TCE earnings on the Baltic TC2 route for 37,000 MT UKC-USAC have slid below zero in July.

Refiners may bring inventories back to more normal levels, but with gasoline demand hitting its seasonal peak in July and with Delta scheduled to restart its 185 kbpd Trainer refinery in September, refiners may have other avenues for inventory restocking.
