# Oversupplied Suezmaxes Fight for Market Share

### growth forced market Rapid Suezmax fleet share fight

Facing rapid fleet growth and limited organic volume from its traditional load regions, Suezmaxes have had to fight for market share from other dirty vessel classes. Continued deliveries of Suezmaxes have driven 9% growth in the fleet this year, overwhelming tepid demand growth in its traditional load regions of West Africa, the Black Sea and the Med. Instead, Suezmax fixtures have cropped up in atypical load regions - with higher AG liftings and a surge in Caribs-East cargoes providing the tonne- mile impetus to keep sector utilisations from crashing any lower than they have.

Rapid Suezmax fleet growth forced market share fight Fleet utilisations near 80% forcing earnings below operating costs Suezmax utilisations are under pressure, as shown in the chart of TCE earnings below. With earnings on key Suezmax routes currently well below cash operating costs, fleet utilisations are near or just under 80%, consistent with other dirty tanker sectors.

The reason for this share struggle has been the rapid fleet growth of the Suezmax sector relative to demand prospects. As shown in the chart on the following page, the delivery of 42 Suezmaxes during 2012 through August has swelled fleet growth to 9.3% year-over-year (yoy), with annualised growth of 13% during the past three months. In contrast, slowing deliveries and consistent demolition have dampened Aframax fleet growth to 1.2% yoy, while VLCC fleet growth is moving away from its 8% growth pace. Although the pace of VLCC fleet growth has punished earnings, VLCCs have enjoyed an organic demand benefit from increased AG production and from Asian crude import sourcing trends. The rising supply of light/sweet crude in the Atlantic Basin has tightened the Brent-Dubai crude price spread, which has encouraged Asian refiners to lift additional volumes of West African and other Brent-linked Atlantic Basin crudes, typically on VLCCs. Moreover, the continued demand from other Asian refiners for heavier crudes, in order to support growing upgrading capacity, has supported VLCC demand for Caribs, Mexican and Brazilian stems.

### yoy Percent Change

In contrast, the traditional load regions for Suezmaxes have not shown dynamic production and export growth. FSU production, for example, has risen by only 150- 200 kbpd yoy this summer, but higher Russian crude runs have kept exports roughly flat. The emergence of flows from the new Ust Luga terminal on the Baltic, offset by lower Druzhba pipeline flows, have kept Black Sea volumes modest, favouring Aframaxes in Baltic and leaving Suezmaxes to fight for share in the Black Sea and Med.

Meanwhile, continued growth in US tight oil output and low crude runs in the PADD1 USAC region have pressured the traditional Baltic TD5 Suezmax WAF-USAC route as well as Suezmax imports into the US Gulf. As shown in the chart on the following page, US imports of WAF grades have been in steady decline since late-2009, with imports almost 30% lower yoy during the three months ending July 2012. Although the 194 kbpd drop in USAC (PADD1) imports of WAF grades is consistent with lower crude runs in the region, the plunge in PADD3 (USG) imports is striking. Not coincidentally, the emergence of almost 500 kbpd of incremental light/sweet crude from the Eagle Ford shale and other Texas tight oil fields during 2012 has eliminated the need for WAF grades for PADD3 refiners. In fact, PADD3 imports of WAF crude plunged to only 386 kbpd in July, the lowest level in almost a decade. For the three months ending in July, WAF imports into PADD3 only averaged 555 kbpd, a 386 kbpd yoy drop, and consistent with rise in tight oil production.

1q04 1q05 1q06 1q07 1q08 1q09 1q10 1q11 1q12 Caribs-East AG-East AG-West One underlying demand driver may be the rise in Saudi Arabian production to 10 mbpd, in order to offset declining Iranian output under tightening sanctions. European and Med refiners, who have historically lifted Iranian volumes, may be taking higher Saudi and Iraqi volumes via Suezmaxes to meet their heavy/sour crude needs.

Meanwhile, the rise in Suezmax Caribs-East volumes may look tiny in comparison, but the tonne-mile effect has been substantial. The rise of this trade, as well the growing AG volumes, contributed to an estimated 10% yoy jump in Suezmax tonne-mile demand during 1h12, helping to absorb the surge in fleet growth. Of course, this was part of the overall 7% rise in dirty tonne-miles during the same period, benefitting from a 1.5 mbpd global inventory build during 1h12 on anxiety over Iran. With uncertain demand fundamentals and high crude stocks, the dirty tanker market is suffering from a normalisation in trade and sustained fleet growth. Continued Suezmax deliveries into 2013 suggest more market share competition with other sectors.
