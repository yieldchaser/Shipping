# Tightening European Diesel Market yet to Stimulate MR Demand

### European diesel prices are soaring, but Trans- Atlantic MR trade is not stirring

A series of refinery outages in the Atlantic Basin and anticipated seasonal maintenance have sharply tightened regional gasoil balances and have sent European diesel prices soaring. Typically, this would open up the trans-Atlantic arbitrage and encourage US Gulf diesel cargoes into Northwest Europe or the Med, stimulating MR demand. Unfortunately, the explosion and fire at Venezuela's 645 kbpd Amuay refinery, temporary USG refinery closures from Hurricane Isaac and the August fire at Chevron's Richmond refinery have all combined to remove supply and push diesel prices higher in the USG. With a closed arb and plentiful tonnage, MR freight rates on the Baltic TC14 route for 38 USG-UKC have languished. As Europe heads into its Autumn refinery maintenance season and as supplies come back on line in the USG, an reopening arb should stimulate activity.

Typically, this type of European refining environment and price action would open the arbitrage between the US Gulf and Europe, particularly given the impressive rise in diesel supply from USG refiners during the past two years. Unfortunately, the explosion and fire at Venezuela's 645 kbpd Amuay refinery, temporary USG refinery closures from Hurricane Isaac and the August fire at Chevron's Richmond refinery have all combined to remove supply and push diesel prices higher in the US Gulf. The US West Coast and the West Coast of South America are now attracting Asian mid-distillate supplies that would normally supply Europe, supporting European and Asian crack spreads, but also highlighting the tightening supply in the Americas. As shown in the chart on the following page, the spread between Northwest Europe and US Gulf diesel prices has remained below $4/bbl since May, which has made the arb challenging. Following the Amuay explosion and fire, the trans-Atlantic price differential fell to $0.59/bbl last week, closing the arb completely.

Ultimately, the diesel tightness in Europe will attract USG volumes, once USG refining operations return to full operation following Hurricane Isaac and as the Amuay situation stabilises. The current start-up of Delta's 185 kbpd Trainer refinery should also support US product balances, although the plant's expected orientation towards jet yields would likely cut into ULSD output.

Bad energy policy, happier MR owners Moreover, market observers are now suggesting that a release of light/sweet crude stocks from the US Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR), currently being considered by the Obama Administration, would boost diesel supplies on the USG and reopen the arbitrage. Although the political motivations behind an SPR release at this time are adding to the list of American energy policy miscues, MR owners could benefit from renewed diesel exports from USG refiners.
