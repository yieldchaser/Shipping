# Post-Sandy Focus Shifting to Distillate Markets

### After the gasoline lines, heating oil a concern

With the New York City area facing severe shortages of gasoline following Hurricane Sandy -- featuring the unwanted 1970s nostalgia of long lines at gas stations and now, odd-even day gasoline rationing -- the regional diesel and heating oil markets have not captured the same level of public attention. This week's storm that brought snow to the region, however, provided an abrupt reminder of approaching winter, and brought distillate inventories back into focus. In fact, New York City temporarily lifted the New York State requirement to burn heating oil with less than 15 ppm sulphur that began this summer, easing shortages. Despite the media focus on gasoline, the diesel and heating oil markets have had a profound effect on the Atlantic Basin MR market and should continue to influence its equilibrium post-Sandy.

5-yr Range 2012 2011 5-yr Avg PADD1 distillate stocks have not followed the typical seasonal build into winter, after which refiners draw down stocks during the peak demand months and during spring refinery maintenance season. Similar to its gasoline balances, PADD1 relies on inter- PADD transfers from the USG refiners of PADD3 for the majority of its distillate supply. PADD1 refiners only provide 25-30% of gross supply, with imports adding another 10%, as shown in the chart on the following page. With weaker distillate demand and higher pipeline transfers, PADD1 shifted to a net exporter of distillate during 2012, exporting a net average of 33 kbpd during 2012 through August, versus average net imports 44 kbpd in 2011. The balances are uneven across grades, however, with the region remaining a net importer of ULSD (<500 ppm) and 500 ppm heating oil, while exporting gasoil with 15-500 ppm specifications.

In retrospect, the export push by PADD1 refiners now looks premature, in light of Sandy's disruption. Distillate exports surged to 171 kbpd for the five months though August, a 47% yoy jump, while imports fell by 38% yoy, to 85 kbpd, during the same period. With two of the region's refineries still sidelined by hurricane damage, any expected year-end recovery in distillate inventories would require additional supplies from the USG and from imports.

PADD1 had traditionally relied upon Caribbean refiners for their distillate imports, but following the early-2012 closure of Valero's 235 kbpd Aruba refinery and the remaining 350 kbpd of Hovensa capacity, imports from the region have dwindled. This has left Canada to provide distillate imports, almost single-handedly, and the stability of this supply source has provided the overall decline in import levels. Essentially, the unwillingness to import from sources other than Canada, and the rise in PADD1 exports, have created today's inventory situation.

With this year's winter likely to return to seasonal norms, following last years' mild conditions, PADD1 refiners will need to rebuild stocks in the coming weeks. Even bringing total distillate stocks back to 41 MB by year end, or 35 days' supply, would require a 50-60 kbpd build during November and December. Facing potential distillate demand of 1.25 mbpd during these months and only 400 kbpd of refinery supply, following disruptions, PADD1 external demand would jump to 900 kbpd.

The most likely sources for inventory building are actually domestic. Inter-PADD transfers should rebound, as local terminal operators come back on line, with PADD3 pipeline receipts returning to the 750 kbpd seen before Sandy. The market has seen a smattering of Jones Act waiver requests, allowing foreign-flag tankers to haul product from the USG into the storm-battered area. These movements could provide another 25-50 kbpd of supply, while another possible domestic source would be the 75 kbpd of net transfers of distillate to PADD2, which operators could cut.

PADD1 to return to net importer Still, PADD1 would need to lose its net exporter status to bring inventories back to acceptable levels. Regional exports look set to decline from their recent 150-200 kbpd levels, which suggests that the consequent rise in imports could be less dramatic. Meanwhile, import sourcing could be challenging, with distillate stocks still tight in the Atlantic Basin, but the region might draw foreign supplies from longer-haul sources, offering some hope for owners.
