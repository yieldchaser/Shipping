# Naphtha Redemption Redux for LR1s

As we noted in this space in April of this year, a surge in Asian naphtha demand provided a brief respite for LR1 tanker owners operating along the benchmark Baltic TC5 LR1 rates have risen route. We likewise noted that the rate spike was unsustainable, and rate gains indeed sharply recently… subsided a few months later as naphtha demand growth was tempered. However, a recent increase in LR1 fixtures has generated a renewed surge in spot rates as LR1s have reached peaks of $19,100/day – up from $7,600 at the beginning of October. Spot rates should again subside as they attract increased tonnage from the supply-side of the market.

Naphtha demand, the main driver for TC5 rates from the demand side of the equation, has recovered throughout Asia since dropping off at the end of 1q2012.

Source: OECD, IEA

This demand growth is unsurprising. Recent economic activity and forward looking projections suggest Asian economies are moving in a positive direction. HSBC’s China OECD forecasts GDP Purchasing Managers’ Index is both on an upward trajectory and reflecting economic growth in much of Asia expansion for the first time in over a year. In addition, the OECD released 2013-2014 projections anticipating renewed growth in GDP expansion in China and Korea on Tuesday. Indeed, the Chinese economy is projected to grow at a clip closer to 9% by 2014.

### …yet naphtha crack spreads remain range-bound

Source: CFLP, HSBC

Economic growth since August supports increased industrial production and petrochemical demand, which in turn has driven aforementioned naphtha demand. Naphtha prices have rebounded as the naphtha crack spread (basis Dubai crude) is hovering near minus $5, up from its summer lows of minus $15. The spread is, however, bumping up against a recurring level of recent resistance, suggesting that demand is near its peak unless petrochemical margins widen, buoyed by macroeconomic growth.

### Long-term demand-side indicators are strong, but short-term rate spike should subside

Compounding the impacts of economic growth are low levels of domestic production of naphtha in Japan. Japanese refineries, running at relatively low utilization rates due to weak domestic demand for refined products, produce limited amounts of naphtha. As demand for naphtha has picked up, the pronounced need to import it has necessitated clean tonnage’s getting increasingly tight for the AG-East run.

Finally, extremely poor TC5 earnings at the tail end of 2011 and beginning of 2012 incentivized owners to explore other options such as moving out of the clean market and beginning to move crude cargoes. Poten data suggests that this move happened increasingly frequently in early 2012.

The numbers of AG-East naphtha fixtures on LR1s increased almost 40% from July to October, helping to significantly reduce available tonnage and strengthen freight rates. These movements have brought naphtha demand and pricing in-line with levels that are supported by underlying refinery and petrochemical facility economics. Improved indicators for Asian industrial production should also improve this calculus and in turn benefit the LR1 market in the long-run – assuming an unreasonable extrapolation of current spot rates does not lead owners to instinctively jump back into the market for new-builds – preventing the types of plummeting rates seen a year ago. The severity of the recent rate spike should be short-lived, though, as tonnage movements on the supply-side precipitate a reduction in rates.
