# IEA Trims OPEC Production Capacity Outlook

### 2013 by 1.07 mbpd

In addition to cutting demand estimates in its recent Oil Market Report, the International Energy Agency (IEA) sliced its forecasts for OPEC production capacity during 2013 by 1.07 mbpd since its December 2011 report. Baseline revisions and cuts in Iranian capacity, due to tighter sanctions, featured in the estimate reduction, which leaves AG capacity on a much slower trajectory. Combined with new refineries coming on line in the region, the new estimates imply a much lower level of crude export capacity available for VLCCs.

As shown in the chart above, massive swings in Libyan capacity, due to the loss of output during the 2011 crisis and subsequent recovery, dominate the recent OPEC capacity figures, but the deceleration in capacity growth among the Arabian Gulf (AG) producers is also notable. The region had seen solid capacity gains in 2009-10, with the start-up of the Al Kurais field in Saudi Arabia and continued Iraqi production increases, but Iranian declines are now pressuring capacity growth. In corrected figures, the IEA has removed 1.07 mbpd of OPEC production capacity for next year, to 35.32 mbpd, from the 36.39 mbpd forecast reported in its December 2011 update to its Medium-term Oil Market Report. The vast majority of this adjustment is from a 0.79 mbpd cut in Iranian capacity, plus another 0.44 mbpd of baseline revisions to Algerian and Qatari capacity.

This has left the AG region with only a 0.12 mbpd gain in capacity for 2013, as Iraqi and UAE capacity gains and the start-up of the Saudi Manifa field in late-2013 just manage to outpace Iranian losses. Estimates based upon the IEA's December update forecasts suggest that the AG could only see 0.71 of capacity growth during 2013-16, while total OPEC sustainable production capacity could rise by 1.78 mbpd during the four-year period. With three Saudi Aramco refineries with a total of 1.2 mbpd of distillation capacity potentially coming on line during the period, and with the UAE's 417 kbpd Ruwais project progressing, this level of AG production capacity growth would imply a 0.9 mbpd reduction in AG crude export capacity during the period.

Led by Saudi Arabia, the AG producers dominate the remaining OPEC spare production capacity, with 2.37 mbpd of current capacity, followed by West African cartel members, with 0.59 mbpd of spare capacity. With OPEC having overproduced by 1.7 mbpd over its 1h12 call on OPEC crude, in order to support substantial inventory building on anxiety over Iranian sanctions, the effective spare capacity has not been as tight as the 3.15 mbpd illustrated in the chart. The graph on the following page shows the level of OPEC spare production capacity as a percentage of global oil demand. During the booming oil demand of 2008, when OPEC was struggling to keep pace with a call on OPEC crude above 32 mbpd, the level of OPEC spare production capacity touched 3% of global demand. Crude oil prices surged to $150/bbl at the time, but a similar decline in OPEC spare capacity to 3.4% of demand did not elicit the same price response. The Brent crude oil price did manage to hit $125/bbl during the Spring, but the market remained over-supplied with large stock builds, during a period of weakening macroeconomic demand fundamentals.

The chart shows a gradual improvement in OPEC spare capacity relative to global oil demand, as the 1.78 mbpd of OPEC capacity additions arrive during 2013-16, but also as OPEC adjusts its production during late-2012 and 2013, to bring crude output nearer to the call on OPEC crude of 30.5 mbpd. Spare capacity surges again in 2015 due to a 0.33 mbpd drop in the call on OPEC crude, as projects in the US, Canada and Brazil come on line and boost non-OPEC crude supply by 0.97 mbpd yoy.

Slower development of non-OPEC crude projects or stronger-than-expected oil demand growth could push the call on OPEC crude higher, boosting oil tanker demand, but would also cut into OPEC spare capacity, bringing it back towards 3%. This pattern of constrained and then relaxed commodity supply chains is providing a useful price rationing process for the global economy, but an oversupplied dirty tanker sector still remains caught the middle.
