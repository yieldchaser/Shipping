# Distillate Inventory Impact Continues on Atlantic Coast

The recovery from last autumn’s Hurricane Sandy continues in some of the hardest hit areas of the Northeastern United States, and there are likewise lingering effects within Clean product market the petroleum products market in the region. Unseasonably high motor gasoline prices still recovering from effects of Hurricane are receiving considerable attention in the press as blending components for summer Sandy grades are in higher demand and contango in gasoline futures markets is narrowing. These high prices have come despite eroding gasoline demand in the area.

As noted in the aftermath of Hurricane Sandy (see 09 November 2012 Opinion), the US Atlantic Coast (PADD1) was primed for a recovery in distillate demand. Inventories already fell well below their usual levels in the second half of last year, not following their usual build before winter. The hurricane then aggravated these low levels and the need to rebuild inventories.

*Chart: USAC (PADD1) Total Distillate Inventories*

Source: EIA

The implication was that regional refineries were premature in beginning to export distillates, and PADD1 indeed reverted back to its status as a net importer of distillates shortly after the hurricane. While EIA data is pending, we expect that this trend will persist as inventory replenishment continues.
