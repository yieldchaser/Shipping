# Slumdogs of the Caribbean

The Caribbean market has been up and down, but more of the latter since the opening of 2009. Though for shipowners near and dear to the West Indies, the simple economics of supply and demand are playing a tougher game these days.

Aframaxes and Panamaxes have demonstrated the ability to earn over $60,000 and $50,000 per day; respectively, but have been in the sub-$10,000 per day range recently. The chart below shows the monthly average rates for Aframaxes and Panamaxes on the Caribbean - US Gulf trade. The historically volatile market has slid into a lull on the back of crude market fundamentals and subsequent lighter activity across the board.

Due to crude storage, lower refinery runs and less crude production in the region, the Caribbean has become a stagnant market with each part of the supply chain waiting for the downstream link to open up some demand. The current slow nature of crude movements has caused shipowners and charterers alike to slow down ship movements, sitting on cargoes with the ability to collect demurrage or take advantage of the spot to fixture crude price relationship.

### All's Quiet

The Caribbean tanker is quiet for now and is certainly at the mercy of the barrel price. Without the flow of crude oil greasing the wheels of the US refining system, stocks will continue to build. At some point the forward price structure must react to an amply supplied market. At such a point, the release of stocks into the system may cause the Caribbean market to once again be on the go.
