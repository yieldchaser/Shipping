# Testing the Limits

For now it seems for some owners the rosy days of 2008 when earnings reached unprecedented highs are just a fading memory. The prospects of negative timecharter equivalents (TCE) for shipowners are both overwhelming and unsustainable. While some companies have the financial fortitude to be able to bridge rough patches on retained earnings or with timecharters, others may not be so lucky.

For a few weeks now, the clean market has been staring into a deepening hole of sub‐par earnings. Softening demand across all clean product trades has left shipowners desperate for creative ways to employ their ships.

While waiting for the much anticipated seasonal uptick in gasoline demand, it now seems that it may take more than a quick activity spurt to promote a lasting recovery in the clean product tanker market. With weakening end‐user demand worldwide, it is difficult to see a rate recovery spearheaded by United States demand alone.

### Tough Decisions

As Worldscale rates continue to drop, owners trading in the spot market are rapidly approaching the razor's edge; the point at which it becomes uneconomic to move their ships. From a conventional TCE calculation perspective, based on round‐trip employment at 95% utilization, rates have been in the danger zone for weeks.

### Better than it Appears?

In reality the large fleets that trade in the MR sector, whether in official pools or within large trading companies, are quite skilled at navigating through rate environments. Through consolidation of commercial control, owners can usually manage costs more effectively. Bunkers purchased in bulk, centralized chartering operations and reduced overhead are ways that owners can protect the bottom line. But perhaps most important outcome of managing a large fleet is the improved flow of information resulting in greater ship utilization through triangulation opportunities.

The theoretical one‐way employment time charter calculation suggests that owners are achieving utilization levels above 100% in practice. By improving the laden to ballast ratio of the ship, the effective timecharter equivalent increases. Even in a falling Worldscale rate environment, prudent chartering decisions can help keep owner's heads above water.

While smart chartering practices can help stave off the realized effects of a weak freight environment, the increase of ship supply in a limited cargo environment will continue to limit the upside for freight rates.

Although a resurgence of cargoes in the gasoline trade to the United States is expected in the next few weeks, it will be up to owners collectively to manage the supply side of the market in the longer‐term. The removal and scrapping of older tonnage from trading, or the difficult decision to selectively remove tankers from the market for a short period of time, will help to curb the appearance of physical tonnage in the market - even if only psychologically.
