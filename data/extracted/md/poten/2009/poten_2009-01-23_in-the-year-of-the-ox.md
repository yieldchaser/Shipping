# In the Year of the Ox

Thank you to everyone who participated in our survey in the January 9, 2009 Weekly Tanker Opinion: Your Opinion. The results are in. We received many responses from all sides of the business that varied widely and even some that bucked the internal Poten trend. The charts below show our questions and tallied responses.

**Dusting off the Crystal Ball**

*Chart: The next investment bank failure will The economy in the United States will*

Banking

Based on the responses, there does not appear to be a lot of confidence in a quick economic recovery. Over 80% think that it will be at least a year to two years before we begin to see improvements. Additionally, many believe that the banking sector will continue to falter. Over half of the replies indicate people expect another bank failure within the next three months. Nearly 30% of people feel that there are no investment banking failures on the horizon, but it is difficult to interpret whether they believe this due to improved stability in the market, or so few banks are left to collapse.

The outlook for crude oil demand is evenly divided. Most oil company respondents think that crude oil demand will decline, while most shipowners believe demand to remain flat in the coming year. The view based on the majority of replies for the crude oil price question suggests that where the price of crude oil ends up is more or less irrelevant.

Despite rumors circulating about major oil company mergers, those surveyed believe the next consolidation in the market will be among tanker companies. As far as rates are concerned most people believe the average VLCC spot will be around $50,000 per day for 2009, in line with the ten-year average.

*Chart: The number of VLCC orders in 2009 will be:*

The majority of people believe that VLCC prices are likely to settle in the $100 - $124 million range in 2009, while some believe they will break below the $100 million mark. Despite the downward sentiment from levels seen earlier in 2008, this response suggests that many people believe there is a floor to asset prices. Generally, people anticipate between 10 and 20 additional orders booked next year, a stark contrast for the 100 put on the books in 2008.

### Interpreting the Numbers

Perhaps surprisingly, sentiments of shipowners and oil companies meshed in many cases. Each group thinks that the next bank will fail in the next three months, that the next merger will be amongst shipowners and that VLCC rates are likely to average $50,000 per day in 2009 – same as the Poten consensus. One divergence came as oil companies believe VLCC prices to end up below the $100 million mark.

So while they have similar sentiments at this point in the cycle, they stay far enough apart to keep it interesting.
