# Strength in Numbers

Making predictions about the direction of tanker rates is easy; getting it correct is the real challenge. Drawing on today's anecdotal support it is difficult to see a major recovery in freight rates in the near term. The initial step in formulating a view is identifying which factors could have an effect on the freight markets. A thorough statistical analysis allows for the consideration of many different potential factors and can aid in short‐term decision making. To simplify the evaluation and calculations, analysis was limited to one of the most transparent markets, VLCCs loading out of the Arabian Gulf.

But since Worldscale rates do not exist in a vacuum other factors were considered. After combing through 30 potential factors, the following had the strongest correlations: a) VLCC one‐year timecharter rates b) VLCC three‐year timecharter rates c) VLCC Scrap Value d) Spot vessel availability e) US crude oil stocks f) Crude oil price per barrel g) OPEC production level h) Bunker prices Putting it to the Test Although it is clear that Worldscale depends on different factors, the first method (autoregressive vector) sourced only historical Worldscale rates to generate an outlook. The analysis used 200 data points and the series provided very little correlation to the actual Worldscale data.

The next test consisted of several rounds of multiple regressions with statistical software to consider the eight variables as external influences. The variables that were initially found to be the most influential on the Worldscale rates were the last month's Worldscale rate, the number of ships available in the AG for the next month and the VLCC 1‐year time charter rates. Upon further drilling into the conditionality of these variables, it was concluded that the 1‐year time charter rates were not statistically significant in formulating a short‐term outlook.

In the final analysis, all eight variables were considered to be endogenous, acting upon each other as a system. When approached in this method, the variables produced a more closely correlated result to the actual rate progression. The one variable, however, that seemed to stand out as one of the most statistically significant was the spot availability of ships over the next 30 days.
