# The Road to Recovery

At the onset of the financial crisis last year, the fourth quarter of 2009 was targeted as the likely beginning of a recovery in the international economy. With the fourth quarter now upon us, things are, in fact, undeniably less bad than they have been. There are budding signs of life in the international economy. Can tanker freight‐rates be far behind? Despite some lingering uncertainty about the future, there is a sense that the tanker market may have already seen the worst.

In its Oil Market Report released last month, the International Energy Agency (IEA) indicated it expected worldwide demand to lift by 300 kb/d in the fourth quarter of 2009 ‐ the first such demand growth in the last five quarters. That marginal increase in demand could herald the early stages of economic recovery.

Improvements in both Chinese and US economic data were cited as the main drivers for the upward revision of the IEA's August projection of 2009 demand. Much has been made of the role that China has played in shifting the focus of petroleum and ton‐mile demand growth outside of the OECD. However, the OECD is now expected be the focal point of demand recovery in the fourth quarter. As shown in the table below, OECD demand is expected to increase by 900 kb/d in the fourth quarter of 2009 while non‐OECD demand contracts by 600 kb/d.

The system guarantees a profit for Chinese refiners and is designed to encourage them to maximize runs, presumably contributing to recent high levels of demand. The Chinese have also taken steps to fill their strategic petroleum reserves and the slowing rate of demand growth could be the result of this opaque effort reaching a new stage.

Outside of China, other non‐OECD regions are projected by the IEA to see that growth reverse or slow during the fourth quarter. The agency expects the Middle East to see a 600 kb/d decrease in demand from the third quarter which reflects the usual seasonal demand shift. Africa and Asian countries other than China could see only slight increases in demand.

### OECD‐ Back in the Saddle

Recent economic news may suggest things are improving in developed economies but it will take a while for the OECD to fully recover from the 2009's sharp drop‐off in demand. Nevertheless, the IEA sees a short‐term demand increase within both North America and Europe. The agency expects demand growth of 200 kb/d in each of those markets during the fourth quarter of 2009. Japan, Korea and the other countries that constitute the Pacific portion of the OECD are expected to see a larger cumulative demand increase of 500 kb/d in the fourth quarter. The fact that these countries are located far from the Arabian Gulf could help create additional VLCC ton‐ mile demand.

While supply and demand fundamentals dictate that the long‐term focus of demand growth will return to the non‐OECD countries, the short‐term improvements in OECD demand present a reason for some hope amid the discouragement that has prevailed for months.

Beyond lagging oil demand, the tanker market faces other challenges. The question of whether a 900 kb/d increase in oil demand during the fourth quarter will give a boost to tanker rates, especially those of VLCCs, really hinges on any growth in tanker fleet size. Notwithstanding the large orderbook, the challenge for owners will be attempting to manage fleet growth so as not to snuff out any recovery in rates brought on by ton‐mile demand.
