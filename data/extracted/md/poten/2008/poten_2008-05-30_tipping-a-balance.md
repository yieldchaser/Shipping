# Tipping a Balance

In tightly balanced tanker markets, any ripple in either short- term vessel supply or demand can potentially swing freight rates in the blink-of-an-eye. Lately, a tight Caribbean market saw Worldscale (WS) rates leveraged upwards by regional demand for vessels outpacing supply.

As the US began embarking on its Memorial Day holiday weekend, charterers pushed to fix their requirements ahead of this work-break even in the face of very limited tonnage lists. During the week preceding the kick-off of the May 23 rd holiday weekend, Caribs-up rates steadily added some 175 WS points or $70,000 per day more earning to peak at WS 410 or roughly $93,000 per day of Time Charter Equivalent earnings (TCE).

Although the average number of vessels available in the next 20 days remained relatively steady this past month, the real key to appreciating why rates rose quickly revolves around the dates upon which the vessels were actually available. With charterers fixing prior to a three-day long US holiday weekend, certain dates showed limited vessel availability. In a tight market, it can take only one more cargo than the ships available to make rates jump. For now, the market seems to have corrected somewhat by falling 60 WS points in the past two days (or a TCE earnings drop of roughly $20,000 per day). But this market continues to have surprises and its balance can still tip.
