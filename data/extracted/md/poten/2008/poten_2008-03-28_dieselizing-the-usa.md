# Dieselizing the USA

As US motorists continue to cope with their gasoline addiction, some are being lured to a different designer fuel by its enchanting, Siren-like offering of a lower per-mile cost. Driven by this - perhaps temporary - cost-benefit euphoria, more and more motorists are being enticed by vehicles igniting ultra-low sulfur diesel.

Illustrating the present per-mile cost advantage of diesel over gasoline, the chart below approximates these costs based on the wholesale price of these fuels in the New York Harbor area. Once distribution and marketing costs plus taxes are added, however, motorists would actually incur costs even higher than those shown for these fuels.

### Bringing the Diesel Home

The present moves afoot to expand US refining capacity also seem to be configured with an awareness of diesel's growing importance to US motorists. But if the demand for diesel explodes beyond the ability of these additions to satisfy, other supply measures would be needed. For instance, those existing refineries not yet fiddling with equipment to boost diesel might find merit in processing crude oils with diesel content's higher than those they presently run. Although typically higher in price, the net-back value of these usually distant, overseas crude oils to US refiners could warrant their purchase - if diesel prices remain strong. If the imported crude oil diet of US refiners shifts to meet higher diesel demand, tanker operators may see the traditional crude oil trade patterns into the US also shift.
