# Above the Average

The long-term health of the tanker market in general is driven by underlying fundamentals such as demand for crude oil and products, but the day-to-day relationship between supply and demand for ships drives rate volatility. The regional balance of available ships versus their demand can trigger sharp peaks and troughs in rates depending on requirements of particular trades. The trend over the long-term has been that of increased volatility in rates. The chart below shows the average monthly actual Worldscale rates for VLCCs Arabian Gulf to Far East (260kt) for all hull types. On average, rates have seen some upward movement in the fourth quarter, but 2008 has seen a decidedly different pattern. During the past ten years the rate floor has been steadier the upward end of the range appears to see a high degree of fluctuation.

While the highest month in 2008 was not above levels experienced in past years, the annual average without the peak month is about 7% greater than 2004 - a year many may consider to be the strongest.

The one metric of implied "volatility" is the differential between the annual average including the peak month and excluding the peak month. By this method 2008 comes in third behind 2004 and 2007. Another metric of volatility is the standard deviation of the rates which generally measures dispersion. Looking at the standard deviation, 2008 is the number one year suggesting that the highs and lows of the market are even farther away from average rate levels compared to other years.

Historically it has seemed that spot exposure in the fourth quarter of the year was the best way to capture market peaks. In 2008 however, those shipowners with any spot exposure at all have likely struck gold. In 2009 more vessel deliveries can be expected along with some near-term slackening in demand. The relaxed balance between supply and demand of ships may suggest that the market could see lower annual average rates versus 2008. But as seen in the past lower annual average rates can obscure the existence of very sharp market swings.
