# Workhorses of West Africa

Crude oil liftings in West Africa have kept tankers buzzing in 2008 and Suezmaxes in particular have been the beneficiaries of heightened activity. The chart below shows reported spot market liftings out of the region on a monthly basis in 2007 and 2008 by vessel size. The number of Suezmax liftings, with the exception of July, have been consistently higher in 2008 on a monthly basis than in 2007 and VLCC activity has been lower. The recent round of the Organization of Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC) production cuts potentially threatens the shipping markets if its members take the new rule to heart. West Africa, however, seems to be somewhat insulated from the quota cuts. For example, Nigeria is pumping below quota levels as infrastructure problems have already imposed a structural cap on production.

### Allure of the Atlantic

Limited opportunities for non-double hull ships in the Atlantic Basin tend to keep the tonnage lists in check, effectively reducing the pool of competition amongst owners. The chart below shows the assessed rates for VLCCs and Suezmaxes on a time charter equivalent (TCE) basis for liftings out of West Africa. Rates have averaged a shade below $100,000 per day for VLCCs and just around $60,000 per day for Suezmaxes.

Suezmax Average Since cargoes are stemmed as 130,000 MT (approximately 1 millon barrels) the market is attractive to both VLCCs and Suezmaxes. A differential arises from the required coordination to co-load two cargoes in order to fill a VLCC, often requiring two load ports and many times two different charterers. The balance in rates between the VLCCs and Suezmaxes in the region has remained pretty steady on a Worldscale basis during the last few years. However, the stable differential in Worldscale (WS) rates has resulted in a wide range in freight costs to charterers from $915,000 on average in 2007 with VLCC averaging WS 80, to almost $2 million on average in 2008 to-date as VLCCs average WS 136. Any psychology attached to maintaining a differential is sometimes not that clear cut.

While on the surface freight economics would tend to favor the VLCC, the practicality of sometimes complicated movements out of West Africa seems better suited to the versatility of Suezmaxes. Simplicity has its price.
