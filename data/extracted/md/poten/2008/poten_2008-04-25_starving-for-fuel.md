# Starving for Fuel

Blame! Blame! Blame! Blaming a product for a price spike or supply shortage in its associated markets in order to undermine its popular appeal seems to be a common technique employed by its detractors. Take for instance the recent clamor that the rapidly escalating production of so-called renewable biofuels is, in part, responsible for the recent steep rise in global food prices for the world's population.

While the chart below seems to suggest a clear correlation - at least in the US - between increased corn-based ethanol production and the rising price of corn, it is also possible that other factors might have contributed to corn's recent price rise.

### Truth in Advertising

Biofuel proponents claim that these fuels are renewable and environmentally-friendly. Maybe biofuels actually do embody these faddish marketing slogans, but until a rigorous, independent scientific and engineering audit of each biofuel's complete manufacturing process envelope is made, these claims will not be properly validated.

A biofuel should be rated by its manufacturing method's total impact on our environment. To judge a method, criteria such as its processing energy efficiency, green house gas balance, impact on land and water use, and its pressure on the price of food might be applied. Perhaps an assessment of whether or not a biofuel's production is truly environmentally sustainable is also warranted.

It is unlikely that a thoughtful person would support the use of biofuels if they knew that the use of such fuels is not only detrimental to the environment, but also potentially responsible for pushing food prices beyond the reach of many lower-income consumers. Until the net impact of biofuels is independently and objectively validated, it will not be known whether or not these fuels truly provide the benefits championed by their proponents. Moreover, until ratings are assigned, the biofuel industry will remain vulnerable to continued complaints and rumors of environmental and economic damage.

### Another Inconvenient Truth

With Governments world-wide mandating an expanding use of biofuels, more of these fuels seem destined to be carried by ocean tankers from producing to consuming areas. Contrasting this outlook for expanding sea borne trade is one that sees the growing perception of biofuels' detrimental environmental and economic consequences crimping their production. Tanker owners and charterers planning tonnage adequate to move any anticipated increase in biofuels' trade volumes should be alert and sensitive to any shift in either public or governmental perceptions of these fuels. Being a little clairvoyant - and cautious - wouldn't hurt them, either.
