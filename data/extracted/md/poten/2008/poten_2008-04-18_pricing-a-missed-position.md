# Pricing a Missed Position

Trans-Atlantic time charter equivalent (TCE) rates for clean MR vessels for most of this year were noticeably weaker than those during similar periods of recent years. Both February's and March's daily average TCE were below the lowest average TCE in those months during the last five years. Yet in the span of only a few days this week, TCE's for this route spiked some $12,000 per day to end at just over $34,000 per day on Friday.

Looking at the chart below, TCE rates have clearly taken a rather lackluster path in 2008. Their levels since the start of April hovered near the lows for rates during the period from 2003 through 2007.

### Figuring Fixture Activity

Sometimes sudden increases or decreases in fixture activity reported for a particular route provide a clue to as to why its hire rates suddenly shift, too. The generally lower fixture activity so far in 2008 compared to the same period of 2006 and 2007 supports the observation of 2008's lower freight rates. In the chart below, a buildup of reported fixture activity seems apparent since week 12 (mid-March) of this year.

The reported fixture activity this week - number sixteen - for clean MRs sailing trans-Atlantic from the UK Continent was about the weekly average of 2007. However, the introduction of many of these cargoes with very prompt delivery windows stimulated a TCE burst for this route. The freight rate jump was further impacted by replacement vessels capitalizing on scant availability of tonnage leading to this route's TCE spike from about $22,300 per day on April 16 th to $34,300 per day on Friday.
