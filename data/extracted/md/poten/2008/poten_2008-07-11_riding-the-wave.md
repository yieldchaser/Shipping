# Riding the Wave

There is no sign of shipowners having the summertime blues as the crude oil shipping bull market continues to rage on. In past years, the summer months have provided some relief to charterers able to capitalize on lower freight rates. But 2008 is certainly proving to be an exception. The chart below shows the time charter equivalent (TCE) for the VLCC voyage TD3 (Arabian Gulf - Far East). Today, market rates are assessed to be around $195,000 per day, as compared to this week last year where rates were hovering around only $35,000 per day. While there are a few precious weeks remaining before the turn of the season, there are several reasons to expect strong rates to prevail over the months to come. The supply and demand fundamentals are pointing to a tightening market over all, upping the stakes for further potential rises throughout the balance of the year.

### Demand Picture

Despite the public outcries against the price of gasoline, the tanker market has yet to see a decrease in demand. Overall reported spot market volumes to date are up over 2% in 2008.

### Worldwide VLCC Liftings

### Reported Spot Liftings

Total Liftings Estimated Liftings out of the Arabian Gulf in particular have seen some fluctuation in volumes month-on-month, but have generally been somewhere between 100 and 110 reported cargoes per month this year. This month, however, has seen an increase of westbound fixture activity from the Arabian Gulf to 23% of the total fixtures so far, well above the typical monthly average of 17%. The incremental westbound movements not only increase the ton-mile demand, but also create temporary displacements from the Arabian Gulf if the ships continue trading in the Atlantic basin.

### Supply Side

Perhaps the real surprise of the 2008 market has been the incremental growth (or lack thereof) to the VLCC fleet. While the orderbook at the start of the year showed a daunting 43 VLCCs, the conversion and removal activity has made the overall effect on the change in fleet size negligible. Owners of older or single hull tonnages have looked to capitalize on the strength of other shipping sectors such as dry bulk or offshore or simply taken advantage of the record scrap steel prices. According to estimates of conversion projects there is an estimated 50 VLCCs to be removed from the fleet for the full year 2008 resulting in a contraction of the available tonnage.
