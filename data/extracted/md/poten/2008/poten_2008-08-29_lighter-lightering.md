# Lighter Lightering?

The prospect of an additional offshore oil import platform in the United States Gulf of Mexico has sparked some discussion among shipowners, particularly those involved in the lightering trade. Generally speaking, the development of pipelines and other infrastructure can certainly upset the proverbial apple cart when it creates more competition for shipping. Texas Offshore Port System, known as TOPS, will be located offshore Freeport, Texas and is expected to have the capability of delivering 1.8 million barrels per day into a 160 mile pipeline system with the potential for further expansion beyond that. ### To Ship by Ship

Lightering occurs offshore when smaller vessels, generally Aframaxes, offload cargo from larger vessels in order to bring the cargo into port. Transportation costs associated with lightering can fluctuate in the range between $.40 per barrel to perhaps as high as $.80 per barrel depending on the conditions in the Caribbean Aframax market. The capital expenses associated with the TOPS are estimated at $1.8 billion. So far there are 750,000 barrels committed to the project and the operators are tasked with filling the remaining capacity in the system; something which may prove to be difficult. In order to entice oil companies to use the platform, the dollar per barrel transportation cost must be low enough to commit barrels to the project, yet high enough to generate a return for investors in TOPS. For now, the lightering business is safe, but there is potential for impact in the longer-term if the capacity of TOPS ramps up.
