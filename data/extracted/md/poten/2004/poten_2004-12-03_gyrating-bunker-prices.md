# Gyrating Bunker Prices

Bunker prices are an important part of overall costs for operating a tanker. A Vlcc consuming 90 tons per day and sailing for, say, 320 days per year will consume 28,800 tons of bunkers. At $175 per ton, the average price of bunkers for 2004 in leading bunkering ports, the annual bunker cost is $5 million per year or $14,400 per day for a 350 day operating year including days in port. Thus bunker costs are about double other operating costs (manning, insurance, maintenance, supplies). For tankers in the spot market, the price of bunkers is critical in determining profitability. As the following chart shows, the average price for 2004 varied among bunkering ports.

### Differentials Matter

Neglecting cargo intake, if the price of bunkers were the same throughout the world, then it doesn't matter where owners bunker. The differential in bunker prices among the leading bunkering ports in the world shows that it is not just the price of bunkers that is critical in determining profitability, but the managing of bunker purchases. Generally speaking bunker prices are higher in the Arabian Gulf than in Rotterdam and Houston. Most of the time this translates to a bunkering policy of buying bunkers in Rotterdam or Houston on a round trip basis. This practice not only minimizes bunker costs, but also maximizes revenue (the less bunkers on board a vessel at the load port, the greater the cargo intake as long as there are no other restrictions such as cargo availability, cubing out, etc.).

### There Are Exceptions

Oh were life so easy. Although this policy of bunkering for the round trip in Rotterdam, which maximizes cargo intake in the Arabian Gulf, would be a consistent winner since 2002, the same cannot be said about Houston. There were a few occasions when Vlccs seeking to fill their bunker tanks at Houston had to pay more than if they had done so in the Arabian Gulf. The last time this occurred was just last month when the price in Houston averaged $217 per ton versus $194 per ton in the Arabian Gulf for a negative spread of $23 per ton. The cause was a temporary squeeze in the Houston bunker market with less availability from refiners occurring just as a number of containerships showed up for refueling. Since then the price of bunkers in Houston has fallen to $166 per ton versus $183 per ton in Fujairah for a positive price spread of $17 per ton as availability of bunkers increased.

This phenomenon of the bunker price moving against an owner illustrates the need for owners to keep a careful eye on what's happening in the bunker market and to keep options open when it comes to bunkering. Spikes are natural when markets are tight, as seen this year in crude oil prices, tanker rates, and bunker prices. Spikes also create arbitrage opportunities such as loading a Vlcc in the Caribbean with a backhaul cargo of bunkers (fuel oil) to Singapore when the price spread widens sufficiently to cover the shipping costs.
