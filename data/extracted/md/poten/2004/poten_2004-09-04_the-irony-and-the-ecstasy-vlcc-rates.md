# The Irony and The Ecstasy VLCC Rates:

### Red Hot to Ice Hot

### The Irony and The Ecstasy

VLCC Rates: Red Hot to Ice Hot The last long weekend of summer is setting in today and ironically VLCC rates, normally known for their ability to rise after the three month summer lull have just started to fall. While VLCC rates have begun to cool off this month, they are still above historical averages. Between Russian oil company Yukos' financial woes, Iraqi rebel habits of pipeline sabotage and high worldwide oil production, VLCC rates have barely had time to catch their breath, to the benefit of owners. Average daily earnings for VLCC voyages West Africa to the US were $79,414 for July and $71,329 for August - not too hard to take even if rates are beginning to cool down.

### Strong Oil Demand

The International Energy Agency predicts that demand for oil will reach 82.5 million barrels per day in the 4th quarter of 2004, the highest growth rate in 10 years. The agency predicts that demand will grow by an additional 700,000 barrels per day in 2005. OPEC's output is currently at its highest output since 1990 at 29 million bpd. Worldwide oil production is also up by about 1.2 million bpd.

### Shifting Trading Patterns?

All these explanations on why rates should be robust are not supported by fixture activity, which, in general, has been declining in 2004. Even the key Arabian Gulf trade lacks luster.

### 2004 VLCC Fixture Activity by Load Area

Jan Feb Mar Apr May Jun Jul Aug 0 The next chart is compares the monthly number of fixtures between 2003 and 2004. Total fixtures through August totaled 1,235 in 2004, up 7% from 1,154 for the same period in 2003. That is one reason why rates are up this year - a greater number of liftings.

### Monthly VLCC Fixture Activity

Jan Feb Mar Apr May Jun Jul Aug 0 Fixture activity, while up overall, has declined in recent months on a month- to-month basis. This is not a sign of an overly robust market.

### What Does This Mean?

Yes, there are overall more vessels being fixed indicating higher levels of export. Yes, there is a great deal of anxiety in the market with Yukos and Iraq currently at the head of the list. But the apparent lull in fixture activity in recent months, the high price of crude as an inhibitor to inventory accumulation, and any progress in reducing the anxiety level of those in the oil business could mean an extension on the recent cooling of rates. With the coming of the winter heating season, the lull may not last very long.
