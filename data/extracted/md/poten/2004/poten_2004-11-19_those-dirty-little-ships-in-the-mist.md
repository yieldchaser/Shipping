# Those Dirty Little Ships in the Mist

All our professional lives we were taught that handymax tankers are built and make their profits in clean trading. Only those approaching the end of their useful lives trade dirty with worn out coatings whose replacement cost cannot be justified by the market. It was a sign that the grim breaker was nearing. Today it is quite different. Handymaxes can earn over $70,000 per day carrying dirty cargoes out of the Black Sea whereas handymaxes carrying clean cargoes out of the Caribs earn only half that amount.

### Why Is This So?

The answer to this question is congestion in the Dardanelles where transits of large vessels over 200 meters are restricted to daylight hours. With the days getting shorter, northbound transits are down to about 6-8 vessels per day. Smaller vessels can slip in and out while the big boys bide their time at anchor. Of course this means higher shipping costs for charterers of smaller handymaxes, but not after demurrage charges on the big boys are taken into consideration. Moreover a need for immediate delivery of a cargo may toss comparative economics out the window.

Current delay expectations northbound are about 6 days (4-5 days in the Dardanelles and 1-2 days in the Bosphorus) with about 5 days southbound. There are 19 tankers in the Dardanelles and 4 tankers in Marmara Sea awaiting permission to make northbound transits. For the southbound transit, there are 15 tankers (3 in the Black Sea and 12 tankers between the Marmara Sea and the Dardanelles) awaiting permission to proceed.

Before 2001 there were very few handymaxes fixed out of the Black Sea and 2002 saw a small step up. Since 2003, the number of spot fixtures has increased significantly, and continues to do so.

### WS Rate

The differential in WS points for handymaxes trading in the two load areas shows considerable variation over time. Whereas the differential in rates averaged 25 WS points over the entire period, it is obvious that the rate spread escalates greatly during the short days of winter when congestion becomes a significant problem in the Bosphorus. This is the time when handymaxes are most needed to sail in and out of the Bosphorus while their larger brethren languish at anchor.

Dirty trading in the Black Sea offers other inducements. Parcel sizes for Black Sea are 44,000 tons whereas parcel sizes in the Caribbean are about 37,000 tons. Larger parcel sizes combined with shorter Black Sea/Med voyages increase the daily TCE earnings rate for Black Sea trading. This might be the explanation of why the average age of handymaxes trading with Black Sea cargoes has declined from 13.3 years in 2001 to the current relatively young age of 5 years.

### Conclusion

Perhaps we shouldn't disparage handymaxes giving up the joys of clean living for a little down and dirty trading. After all tankers are for making money, not for keeping up appearances.
