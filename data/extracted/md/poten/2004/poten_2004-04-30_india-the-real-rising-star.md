# India The Real Rising Star

While China is taking center stage in the media for economic growth, India is moving to displace its diva counterpart. India's GDP rose by more than 10% in the fourth quarter of 2003 with a GDP growth rate of 7.2% expected for 2004, according to a recent edition of The Economist. Last month, the second most populous nation in the world reduced its 2003 $15 billion trade deficit by $3.2 billion. India exports light manufacturing products such as textiles and some agricultural products. Software is also moving into the export fold. The country benefits from foreign companies outsourcing information technology and financial analysis to Banglor in the south and Bombay in the west, also known for its "Bollywood" film industry.

But where tanker owners see green is in India's increasing demand for energy. Although more than half of India's energy consumption is coal, nearly all of which is domestically produced, the country's demand for oil is expected to increase from 2.0 million bpd to 3.2 million bpd by 2010. India's current domestic crude production is around 800,000 bpd and will remain flat, at best. The country then will have to rely on imports to meet the increased demand for oil that a growing economy will need.

### It Takes Tankers to Move It!

The increase in crude imports to India has caused an increase in tanker ownership by several large Indian companies. Great Eastern, Essar, Mercator and the government-owned Shipping Corporation of India (SCI) are becoming household names.

Each of these companies has added appreciably to its fleet via acquisition. Several have spent considerable sums upgrading their existing fleets including newbuildings. SCI is expecting to take delivery of two newbuild VLCC's next year and Essar recently added a double-hull VLCC. Great Eastern already has a good amount of double-hull tonnage. Look for this trend toward fleet upgrading to continue.

### Shooting Star

India's geographic location is a plus. As a nation close to the Arabian Gulf, it's a stone's throw to a major energy supply that doesn't require much tanker capacity. But oil imports will increase by 50% during the next six years, and will continue to be sourced from more distant locations. With sport utility vehicles becoming popular on the crowded downtown roads of the major Indian cities, oil demand will continue to grow. Tanker owners will be adding India to their list of stops on these lucrative trade routes.
