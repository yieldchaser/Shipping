### Oil Prices: Up

### What Gives?

This week's OPEC announcement that it would raise production quotas by one million barrels per day to help stave off high oil prices might have impacted actual production, but not the goal to lower prices. With every OPEC announcement to raise production, some other world event thwarts its intention to reduce oil prices. Could it be that world oil demand is so high that no matter what OPEC does it is like an echo in the Grand Canyon?

### Saudi Arabia: Pumping As Fast As They Can

Saudi oil production is already above its August production quota of 8.4 million bpd with an estimated production of 9.6 million bpd. Yet it is not filling the gap it once did. Saudi Arabia was once a bigger powerhouse in the industry providing nearly 16% of oil production to world demand. As seen in the graph on the next page, its contribution to satisfying world demand has declined somewhat since its peak in 1992. Moreover with a reputed capacity of 12.5 million bpd, its actual production has not exceeded 10 million bpd. One wonders if Saudi Arabia is at its peak production now.

### Are We At The Edge?

As world oil demand continues to grow, Saudi Arabia will be less able to contribute to satisfying demand. Next year, one million bpd of Caspian crude are presumed to come through the BTC (Baku-Tbilisi-Ceyhan) pipeline. Further gains are expected from North and West Africa and the Gulf of Mexico. But demand is also expected to grow at one million bpd per year. This seems to be a neck-in-neck horserace. It is possible that high oil prices are not so much a sign but the fact that we are straining the world's capacity to produce oil. If this is true, do not expect a significant drop in oil prices.
