# The Rearview Mirror

### Looking back at our 2023 projections

### The Rearview Mirror.  How Did We Do?

Almost exactly one year ago today, on January 13, 2023, we published our "forecast issue", a Weekly Opinion that listed what we thought could be the five key tanker market drivers for 2023 ("Our 2023 Crystal Ball"). In this issue we will review each of those projections and determine which ones we got right, which ones we got wrong and which ones we did not see coming.

Regarding the key developments that we missed; we have to start with the conflict in the Middle East. Nobody predicted Hamas's attack, Israel's response, and the Houthi's subsequently attacking vessels transiting through the Bab el-Mandeb straits. This had an impact on the market in the fourth quarter of 2023 and continues to be a factor this year. We also did not foresee the OPEC+ production cuts as well as the growing tension between Venezuela and Guyana.

Let's discuss our projections of one year ago and see what actually transpired: 1. China's oil demand will recover and give a boost to VLCC demand.

This forecast proved to be correct. China's oil demand did recover and VLCCs did get a boost during 2023. According to data from China's General Administration of Customs, China's crude oil imports hit an all-time high in 2023 as fuel demand recovered from a pandemic-induced slump despite economic headwinds. China imported a record 11.28 Mb/d of crude oil in 2023, 11% more than in 2022 and up from a previous record of 10.81 Mb/d in 2020. This growth did boost VLCC demand and rates. Earnings for regular (non-ECO) VLCCs with scrubbers increased from $37,500/day in 2022 to $50,750 in 2023 (+36%).

2. The volume of secondhand transactions will slow and demand for tanker newbuildings will start to pick up. The shadow fleet will underperform.

This projection was on the mark as well. After a booming 2022, the volume of secondhand tanker sales dropped 16% (582 transactions in 2023 versus 694 the previous year). The Suezmax and Aframax secondhand markets were most affected with declines of 33% and 25% respectively. Product tanker sales declined by 15%, while VLCCs eked out a small gain (+3%). Despite the decline in volume, the total value of the transactions increased by 11% as secondhand prices, which increased dramatically in 2022 remained elevated in 2023. The performance of the shadow fleet, which continued to grow in 2023, is hard to measure, so it is difficult to say if they did or did not underperform.

3. The product tanker market will outperform the crude tanker market Here we dropped the ball. Crude oil tankers outperformed product tankers last year. After a banner 2022, rates for product tankers (MR, LR1 and LR2) retreated by an average of 18% in 2023, even though they remained very healthy from a historical perspective. Earnings for crude oil tankers, on the other hand mostly improved. VLCCs were up the most (36%), while Suezmax rates increased by a more modest 10%. Aframaxes were the exception, with a 7% decline.

4. Crude oil exports from the U.S. will reach new records This was a home run. U.S. crude oil exports continued their strong performance from 2022 into 2023 and did indeed reach new records. In 2023, U.S. exports averaged 4.21 Mb/d according to the EIA, compared to 3.53 Mb/d in the previous year, an increase of 680,000 b/d or 19%.

5. Environmental regulations will have a growing impact on vessel operations This one is difficult to gauge. There are clear signs that environmental regulations are having an impact. An increasing share of newbuilding orders is for dual fuel or alternative fuel vessels and owners have been preparing for the introduction of new regulations and the implementation of the EU ETS. On the other hand, geopolitical issues largely took center stage again in 2024 with the Russian price cap, sanctions enforcement, the dark fleet and the Houthis receiving more attention than the latest environmental regulation.

Overall, we did not do too bad. Three projections proved to be correct, one was clearly wrong, while the outcome of the fifth forecast is up for debate. Stay tuned. Next week we'll publish our views for 2024.
