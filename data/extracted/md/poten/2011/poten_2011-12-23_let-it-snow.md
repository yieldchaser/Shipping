# Let it Snow

While the weather in New York is not yet frightful, rates this week have been quite delightful for product tanker owners trading tonnage in the Atlantic Basin! Time charter equivalent earnings on the Transatlantic trade from UK Continent to the US Atlantic coast rose to almost $22,000 per day this week, or over twice as high than the year to date average and about $2,000 more per day than VLCCs trading from the Arabian Gulf to the Far East. As we discussed a few weeks back, increased activity out of the US Gulf has been an important source of support for MR rates as PADD III refiners ship surplus distillate to markets abroad. This week thin availability of prompt tonnage emboldened product tanker owners to leverage higher rates as charterers sought to turn over inventories before the year end. Although the market appears to be cooling coming into the Christmas holiday, temperatures in the United State's largest heating oil markets are still unseasonably warm. If the weather turns bitter, it could very well keep the pre‐holiday rally in product tanker rates going into the New Year.

A year‐on‐year comparison of fundamentals suggests that there could be near‐term tightness in US distillate markets. This time last year total US distillate stocks stood at about 161 million barrels, or 43 days cover to meet the country's 3.7 million barrels per day (mbd) of demand. Fundamentals have been much tighter in 2011, particularly throughout the fourth quarter, as seen in the chart below.

Currently, US demand for distillate fuel stands at about 4.4 barrels per day, compared to 139 million barrels of inventory. At these levels, the United States has enough distillate to cover about a month of consumption, and much of the United States has yet to see the real start of winter.

Traditionally, distillate demand picks up significantly when Jack Frost comes knocking as more heating oil is required to keep businesses and households warm. The US east coast is the single largest market for distillate fuel oil, accounting for about 35% of total US demand from November to February of last year. Amid already‐tight fundamentals an abrupt drawdown in heating oil inventories could create requirements for replacement barrels while the comparatively high cost of Jones Act transportation can make it prohibitively expensive to source this product from Gulf Coast refiners. This could create prompt import requirements to meet demand. So long as opportunities in other markets continue to keep position lists lean, a scramble for tonnage amid wintry weather may create a perfect storm for tanker owners seeking high rates for Transatlantic business.
