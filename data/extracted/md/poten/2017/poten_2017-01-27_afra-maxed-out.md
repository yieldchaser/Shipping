# Afra Maxed Out

### Fig. 2: Aframax Ton Mile Demand by Load Area

### Dirty Aframax demand is up, but growth potential is limited

In 2016, global dirty (crude and dirty products) Aframax ton- mile demand increased by 1.1% compared to 2015 while the number of voyages increased by 1.9% (indicating a slight decline in average voyage lengths). This week, we will take a closer look at the drivers for these developments to see whether that holds any clues for 2017.

Data from Lloyds List Intelligence, that is used for this analysis shows that the shift in Aframax trades from the Atlantic basin to the Pacific continues. The main growth areas are in Asia, mainly due to increased Aframax exports from the Russian east coast port of Kozmino and growth in Indonesian Aframax trades. During 2016, Russian exports shifted away from Japan (down from 81 to 35) towards China (up from 138 to 203) and overall Aframaxes gained some market share from Suezmax tankers. Indonesian Aframax trades increased by 19% (80 voyages), but the ton-mile impact was limited because much of the growth was in Intra-Indonesian cabotage trade, reserved for Indonesian owners. The same applies to the growing coastal Aframax trade in China.

Russian exports were also a significant driver for growing Aframax trades in NW Europe. In 2016, Russia exported 871 Aframax crude cargoes out of the Baltic compared to 671 in 2015, however, this was partially offset by a decline in fuel oil cargoes (-51 cargoes). Imports into the Netherlands accounted for the bulk of the crude import growth, partially for refining in local refineries but mainly for transshipment to Germany, the largest European customer of Russian crude oil.

The above growth areas were offset by declining trades in the Mediterranean. In 2016, 1,601 Aframax cargoes loaded in the Med, 73 less than in 2015. Political unrest in Libya continued to negatively affect Aframax trades in the region, especially in the first 9 months of 2016, with a recovery in the last quarter. Exports from Azerbaijan on Aframax tankers were reduced as more of their exports were shipped on Suezmax tankers and on VLCCs (mainly to Taiwan). These declines were partially offset by growing Russian exports and larger volumes loaded at the Sumed Pipeline in Sidi Kerir.

Economic problems in Venezuela affected the Aframax market in the Caribbean/USG region. Due to operational problems at Venezuelan oil fields, crude oil exports declined by 7% in 2016. Venezuela uses Aframax and Suezmax tankers to supply regional customers in the US Gulf and to ship crude to storage facilities in the Caribbean. From here, cargoes are shipped on larger tankers to Asia. In 2016, Venezuela exported 669 Aframax cargoes compared to 760 in 2015. It is not clear if/when the situation in Venezuela will improve, but it is unlikely that shipments will recover in 2017.

One notable area of change, even though the volumes are small, is exports from the U.S. In 2015, crude oil exports went almost exclusively to Canada, with occasional condensate cargoes to Europe. In 2016, with the U.S. crude oil export ban lifted, trading patterns changed. Shipments to Canada declined by 60% while longer haul shipments to Europe and to Asia increased. Due to infrastructure constraints, U.S. crude exports are done almost exclusively on Aframaxes.

Total dirty Aframax ton-mile demand increased from 1,232 Billion ton miles in 2015 to 1,246 in 2016. Southern Europe is the region that provides the most demand for Aframax tonnage, followed by South East Asia, which is also the strongest growth region with 13% growth. The import region that experienced the largest decline in Aframax cargoes is Japan. Of the export regions, the Persian Gulf is a region with the strong demand growth, predominantly due to larger volumes shipped to Singapore.

We don't foresee a decline in Aframax employment for 2017, but we don't expect strong growth either because Russia (one of the main growth areas in 2016) is unlikely to grow further due to their commitments to join the OPEC cuts. Even if only half of the Aframax orderbook that is scheduled for delivery in 2017 is actually delivered this year, the Aframax crude fleet will still grow by 3%, which may be a tall order for the demand to match.
