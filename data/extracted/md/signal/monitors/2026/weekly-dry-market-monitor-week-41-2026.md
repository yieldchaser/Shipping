*Published on 14 October 2026*

## Spotlight of the Week | Steel


## Black Sea Steel Flows Weaken as Shipping Risks Rise


![Black Sea Steel Flows Weaken as Shipping Risks Rise](../../images/6acf827f36d156cdab088840_Screenshot 2026-10-14 at 14.23.34.png)

> **Figure 1: Black Sea Steel Flows Weaken as Shipping Risks Rise**  
> **Interactive Data & Source:** [Signal Ocean Platform](https://www.thesignalgroup.com/weekly-market-monitor/weekly-dry-market-monitor-week-41-2026) | [Local Asset](../../../../../../corpus/07-signal/images/6acf827f36d156cdab088840_Screenshot 2026-10-14 at 14.23.34.png)

**Recorded Black Sea steel loadings declined sharply in Q3 2026.** Volumes, including scrap, fell 65.6% y/y to **0.65 Mt** across 51 voyages, compared with 129 in Q3 2025. The year-on-year decline deepened through the quarter: July −28.0%, August −80.6%, and September −96.0%. September’s recorded volume totalled 20,690 t, comprising loadings from Turkey (12,750 t), Romania (4,970 t), and Ukraine (2,970 t). January–September volume reached **4.65 Mt**, down 31.0% y/y, across 314 voyages, down 34.3%.

**Russia and Ukraine account for most of the fall.** Russia accounted for 3.36 Mt, or 72%, of the 4.65 Mt recorded in January–September. Its recorded loadings fell to 31,000 t on 2 voyages in August, against 553,000 t on 25 voyages in August 2025, and **no Russian steel loadings were recorded from Black Sea ports in September within the selected Signal dataset, extracted on 13 October**, against 364,000 t in September 2025; the latest recorded Russian loading was on 18 August. Ukraine’s recorded steel cargoes fell 78.8% in Q3 to 62,940 t, with about 3,000 t in each of August and September. Ukraine’s September crude steel output was reported at 131,000 t, about 79% lower y/y, and on 25 September ArcelorMittal said it could not safely and sustainably restart Kryvyi Rih, giving no timetable.

**Voyage risk and reported insurance costs are rising.** Four merchant vessels were reported sunk or damaged in the Black Sea on 5–6 October. Two further cargo vessels were struck off Bulgaria on 13 October: HS Dignity sank and Chelsea 3 sustained damage. Responsibility for some incidents remains disputed. Hull war-risk quotations reported by BPG Shipping through Ukrainian Shipping Magazine on 9 October were 0.6–1.0% of vessel value before discount, compared with 0.2–0.5% a week earlier, equivalent to 0.3–0.5% where a 50% no-claims discount applies. A subsequent broker report on 13 October indicated increased enquiries for war-risk cover for Ukrainian Black Sea ports and Danube-bound voyages, without providing an updated premium range.

**Smaller bulkers face fewer steel cargoes and higher Black Sea voyage risk.** Small bulkers carried **91.0% of recorded Ukrainian steel tonnes** in January–September, while Handysize accounted for **37.2% of Russian steel shipments**, alongside Supramax at 43.9%. The reduction in Russian Handysize employment is substantial: recorded steel tonnage fell **53.4% to 1.25 Mt**, with voyages declining from **109 to 49**. Against a wider reference supply of **193 Handysize ballasters across the North Atlantic, Mediterranean and Black Sea**, the loss of Black Sea cargoes adds to downward pressure on employment and freight for vessels seeking business in the region. Fewer steel sailings narrow loading opportunities for small bulkers and Handysize vessels, while attacks on merchant shipping and higher war-risk quotations increase the costs and risks of regional trading. These vessels face a shrinking steel cargo base within a security environment affecting Black Sea shipping across cargo types.


## Supply & Demand | What Changed vs Week 40

**Ratios softened together; fundamentals split by basin.** All 4 demand-to-supply (D/S) ratios eased by 0.01–0.02, and Capesize moved to 0.99 from 1.01, so demand growth now trails supply growth across all 4 segment indices. That does not mean demand is falling: Capesize demand is +0.1% y/y against supply +1.0%.

**Rising vessel availability is weighing on Atlantic Capesize freight.** South Atlantic ballasters increased to **64**, compared with 48 in Week 40. On C3, forward vessel supply now exceeds expected demand from around **day 16, compared with day 30 previously**, bringing the supply surplus closer to the prompt loading window. C3 closed at **$33.29/mt, down $4.36**, reflecting the weaker Atlantic balance. In the Pacific, active C5 fixing was accompanied by a smaller rate decline of **$0.19 to $14.24/mt**, with forward supply remaining close to expected demand.

**Panamax — Rates turn higher as forward supply tightens.** In Week 40, firmer US Gulf demand failed to lift rates: all routes declined, and P5TC lost $313/day. In **Week 41**, the projected vessel shortfall widened: **P3 moved from surplus to deficit**, while P6 supply fell below expected demand from **day 3, compared with day 5 last week**. Activity increased across both basins, with transatlantic demand absorbing available tonnage. All routes gained, taking **P5TC up $464 to $21,813/day**, as the tighter forward balance was reflected in rates.

**Supramax — Asian gains offset Atlantic weakness.** In Week 40, vessel supply covered expected demand across all four forward cases. In **Week 41**, **S8 and S10 in Asia moved into deficit**, while US Gulf supply remained above expected demand and North Atlantic ballasters increased to **124, from 103 in Week 40**. Rates reflected this regional divergence: S2 gained $1,525/day, and S8 rose $650/day, while S1C fell $275/day as US Gulf fronthaul earnings slipped below transatlantic levels. Asian strength helped lift the **BSI to a 52-week high of 1,814**, despite an overall D/S ratio of **0.82**.

**Handysize — US Gulf weakens as South Atlantic gains ground.** In Week 40, strength centred on the Continent. In **Week 41**, subdued US Gulf demand and ample vessel availability pushed **HS4\_38 down $2,671 to $21,436/day**, while North Atlantic ballasters increased to **193, from 174 in Week 40**. The South Atlantic strengthened, with healthy cargo enquiry and tighter tonnage, lifting **HS3\_38 by $1,006 to $26,600/day**. In the Pacific, the forward balance was mixed: **HS6 moved from a projected vessel deficit to a surplus**, while **HS7 remained in deficit**.

## Key Takeaway

**Capesize led the downward freight correction in Week 41, while its D/S ratio fell from 1.01 to 0.99, leaving all four segments below 1.00.** Demand growth trailed supply growth across the four segment indices, and rising vessel availability added pressure: South Atlantic Capesize ballasters reached **64**, while North Atlantic Supramax and Handysize counts increased to **124 and 193**, respectively. Freight gains remained concentrated in routes with tighter availability: Panamax and Asian Supramax strengthened as forward supply fell short of expected demand, while South Atlantic Handysize benefited from stronger cargo enquiry and fewer available vessels.

**The loss of Black Sea steel cargoes adds to the pressure on smaller bulkers.** Small bulkers carried **90% of Ukrainian steel tonnes**, while Supramax and Handysize handled **43.9% and 37.2% of Russian steel shipments**, respectively, in January–September. Ukrainian mill stoppages and uncertainty over restarting Kryvyi Rih constrain the prospect of a near-term cargo recovery. Fewer cargo opportunities risk intensifying competition for employment as geared vessel availability rises, although the steel contraction’s contribution to the larger ballaster pool cannot be isolated. Attacks on shipping and higher war-risk premiums also raise the cost of the remaining Black Sea voyages.
