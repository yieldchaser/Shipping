## Market Commentary

### Supramax/Ultramax and Handy Markets Strengthen Across the Atlantic

Spot South Atlantic Supramax/Ultramax market continues to strengthen this week. Rates have risen across all routes, with some deals being signed significantly above previous levels. In West Africa, shipowners are expecting a similar upward trend, particularly in terms of shipments to India. Although the region's trading activity is lower than in ECSA, the overall positive sentiment in the South Atlantic is helping owners to maintain their freight ideas. By the end of the week, freight levels have added $1-3k daily and $1-4/t on all routes. For instance, shipment of 50- 55,000 t of soybeans or corn from Santos to S.China now costs low $50s/t. The Handy sector is still looking favorable for shipowners. The market is supported by stable cargo flows amid relatively limited tonnage supply. Shipowners continue to voice high rates, although charterers are sometimes hesitant to close deals, which results in a moderate number of contracts concluded over the week.

Supramax/Ultramax market is posting moderate activity in North Atlantic, with rates holding steady at last week's levels. Market participants forecast a significant increase in demand for Q4 and expect the steady uptrend to continue through the end of the year. The Handy sector is seeing robust cargo offer, but charterers are not very actively signing deals, as additional tonnage continues to enter the market for spot and prompt dates. Over the week, rates have nevertheless grown by $0.5-2k daily and $1-3/t. Thus, rates for transportation of 23-25,000 t of scrap (sf 55- 58`) from ARAG to Turkiye have increased to $48-49/t.

The Baltic & Continent area has started heating up thanks to brisk shipments of steel scrap, grain, and fertilizers. Shipowners are confidently pushing rates up across all key routes (up $2-4k daily and $3-5/t over the week). Charterers are facing serious difficulties in finding vessels for spot shipments and are increasingly chartering ships available in the Mediterranean Sea (even in its eastern part). To a large extent, this increased interest from charterers shipping cargo from North Europe allowed owners to confidently raise rates in the Black Sea as well. The fact is that a slight upturn in grain shipments from Romania and Bulgaria, as well as in shipments of minerals, fertilizers, and construction cargoes from North African and Turkish ports, has had a significant impact on rates given the extremely limited count of open vessels. Even in the absence of Russian and Ukrainian exports, shipowners have managed to raise rates by $2-3k daily and $2-5/t this week.

The APAC Supramax/Ultramax segment is fairly stable on the fundamental side. Owners still benefit from the moderate tonnage list, as well as lively exports of NOPAC grains. In SE Asia, the slowdown of Indonesian coal traffic to China is offset by Indonesia- Bangladesh exports, as well as shipments of SE Asian construction cargoes. As for the Handysize/ Handymax market, the tonnage list has inched up, but freights stay at previous levels.

### Supramax/Ultramax freight rates and dynamics on major routes

Supramax/Ultramax activity appears moderate in the Indian Ocean, with limited shipments of Indian ore and salt, while the market still lacks South African manganese ore. Freight levels have not changed significantly over the week.

Market participants forecast the North Atlantic cargo flows to pick up next week, with freight rates expected to start rising. The positive trend is also likely to continue in the southern part of the basin, as new grain offers are increasingly coming in.

Shipowners expect the North European market to firm up further, as there are currently no signs of a decline in cargo traffic. Grain exports from European and Russian ports continue perking up, which will support strong tonnage demand in the region, along with regular shipments of fertilizers and steel scrap. Meanwhile, the Black Sea market will hardly see a further upturn in cargo traffic; however, strong momentum from the Continent provides shipowners with every chance of raising rates slightly further.

Players' outlook is getting somewhat more cautious in the APAC region ahead of the upcoming Coaltrans events in Bali.
