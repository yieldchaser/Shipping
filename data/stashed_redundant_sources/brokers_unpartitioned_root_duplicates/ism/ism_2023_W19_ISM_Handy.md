## Market Commentary

Similar to the neighboring Panamax segment, the ECSA Supramax/Ultramax and Handysize markets are suffering pressure despite heavy grain shipments from Brazil. “Corn flows are increasing fast, but rates barely managed to stay at last-done levels as ballasters towards ECSA rapidly consume cargoes,» a broker mentioned.

Supramax/Handysize market under pressure in Atlantic, with better sentiment seen in Asia-Pacific region

Rates for transportation of 50,000 t of soybeans or corn from Brazil to S.China are still hovering at $41-42/t bss 1/1.

Quite similar picture can be seen in USG . The sentiment of Handysize and Supramax owners has improved slightly, but without any impact of freight levels. Mid-2H May tonnage list is growing quickly enough, balancing increasing cargo book.

The already low activity has shrunk to a near minimum on the North European and Mediterranean markets. Players note decreased number of new offers of fertilizers, coal and grains ex Russian ports, while shipments from Ukraine have been dead for the third consecutive week. Note that slowdown of coal shipments from Ust-Luga is primarily referred to problems with railway logistics.

Tonnage demand in both basins is supported only by shipments of European grain, which perked up slightly, as well as by traffic of construction cargoes from Turkey and the western Mediterranean. Rates have stayed at last week's levels in the Continent and the Baltic Sea, while BlSea & Med freights have dropped by another $0.5-1/t and $0.5-1k daily, which looks quite acceptable for ship owners, considering the fact that the tonnage list is more than three times longer than the total cargo book. Rates for spot shipment of 25-30,000 t of wheat from Constanta to SpanMed have dropped to $16.5/t bss 1/1 given 8000 sshex bends l/d rates.

The Indian Ocean Supramax/Ultramax market is also dominated by negative sentiment. Despite brisk shipments of construction cargoes from the Persian Gulf, ship owners are forced to make concessions to charterers amid a significant upturn in the list of vessels open in PG/ WCI. Rates have sagged by $0.5-1k daily and $0.25-0.5/t in the region. Freight levels ex South Africa are also sliding down amid an inflow of ballasters from ECI with ETA in late May. Meanwhile, the regional Handysize segment is stable.

As expected, the SE Asian and Far Eastern shipping markets have become more attractive for ship owners after protracted holidays in the region. Over the week, corresponding rates have risen by $0.5-1k daily and $0.25- 1/t due to an increase in cargo offer.

Next week, rates may still start growing in the South Atlantic, which will require higher pace of South American grain exports compared to the inflow of ballasters with ETA in late May, which looks realistic. The USG market

sentiment is also positive. «Cargo list looks solid towards the end of the week and we will likely see a strong market in the coming week,» a broker mentioned.

The North European market will hardly show any noticeable improvements next week. Players are pinning their hopes on increased scrap offer only closer to the end of May. The Black Sea market picture may change if the agreement on extension of the grain corridor operation is reached in the coming days. However, even the extension of the deal does not guarantee the increase in exports from Ukraine, because Russian representatives were actually sabotaging vessel inspections during the last few weeks, and it is difficult to say whether this situation will change even after the extension of the deal.

Further developments look ambiguous on the SE Asian market. Despite higher activity after holidays,

spot rates are reacting rather slowly due to long list of vessels accumulated in SE Asia during the holiday season. Moreover, shipments of Indonesian coal to China will most likely remain limited, as country's coal stocks are quite large. On the other hand, shipments of Indonesian coal to India may gain pace ahead of the monsoon season, which may support the market in 2H May.
