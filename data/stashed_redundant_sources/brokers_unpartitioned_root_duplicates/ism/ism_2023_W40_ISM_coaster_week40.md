## Market Commentary

Negative sentiment dominates virtually all the freight sections of the small-tonnage market in early October. Similar to the last few weeks, the Black Sea market remains the most attractive for ship owners, but even here charterers have managed to stop the rate growth amid

Pace of trade declining on small-tonnage market with growing list of open vessels, especially in EMed

the stabilization of cargo traffic from the Danube ports and EU countries. «Black Sea is apparently the only active area, however rates have inched up by another $1-2/t only for shipments from Russian ports like Novorossiysk and Tuapse,» a broker commented. Note that the resumption of navigation in the Sulina and Bystre after a 6-day downtime also played into the hands of charterers as queues for entry are gradually melting again, leading to increased tonnage list for 2H October. Note that the vessel list is growing in all regions. Thus, about 135 vessels of 2-12k dwt open in the Azov-Black Sea basin and Marmara Sea for the next couple of weeks, which is 25 units more than it was in September 20-25, while the Mediterranean Sea (from Gib to EMed) shows the most significant increase in the number of ships - up to 270 vessels against 170 a couple of weeks ago. With the shortage of cargo offers, rates stay under pressure in the Mediterranean Sea, with ship owners often having to make small concessions. Only fertilizers and agri

products are regularly offered for shipments in the region, but relevant volumes have decreased.

At the moment, transportation of 5-7,000 t of corn or soyabeans from Reni or Izmail to Alexandria costs $62-64/t, while rates for shipment of 3-4,000 t of corn from Constanta to Cyprus are still hovering at $28-29/t. Meanwhile, rates for shipment of 5-6,000 t of steels from Novorossiysk to Marmara have inched up to $22-23/t.

Meanwhile, the downward trend persists in the Azov Sea. «Demand for Russian grain remains limited in Turkey, with huge pressure on export prices,» an experienced Turkish trader commented. Despite still serious queues and delays in the Kerch Strait (more than 130 vessels waiting simultaneously for passage in + out), charterers manage to cut rates confidently. Freights for grain transportation have sagged by $5-7/t during the week, those for shipments of coal by $2-3/t. Shipment of 3,000 t of wheat from Rostov/ Azov to Marmara now costs low $50s/t.

Mediterranean Seas (excluding Ukraine) are 1.5 times lower than in 1H October last year and 2.5 times lower compared to 2021. Only premium shipments from the Danube ports under favorable conditions can bring ship owners as much as they used to get last year for shipments ex non- Ukrainian ports. The same is true for sea-river owners, who, although still able to earn twice as much as OPEX, are earning 30-50% less than in October 2022 and 2021.

In North Europe, the situation also remains rather pessimistic for owners. The regional cargo traffic remains inactive as for the traditionally peak period (there was no seasonal surge of activity), while freight rates are stable. «Same freight levels even though tick later in the autumn. Bunker's been going back and forth in 900s; thus no real change from that side either. As I see what will have the biggest impact is weather and delays caused by that,» an EU broker commented.

The Caspian Sea market is the only one which keeps climbing amid healthy demand for grain in Iran and lack of open vessels on the spot market. Freight rates have added some $1-4/t over the week.

If the cargo flow remains as limited in the Mediterranean Sea, the regional tonnage list will almost certainly continue to grow, which could trigger a decline in freight rates not only in the Mediterranean itself, but also on the neighboring Black Sea market. According to ISM, this development is quite probable considering the ongoing collapse of the steel market: the already weak demand for Russian square billets has decreased to a minimum in

Turkey and MENA countries after the introduction of an export duty in Russia; sales of Turkish HRC and rebar remain minimal. Demand for North African urea and Black Sea AN remains moderate in Mediterranean countries and Turkey. In addition, grain exports from Ukraine's deep-water ports are gradually gaining pace, which will inevitably affect the pace of Danube shipments, hence queues and the tonnage list in the area. In the Azov Sea, sea-river rates will most likely continue sliding down. Rates in North Europe can be affected only by possible deterioration of weather conditions, as there are no preconditions for an upturn in cargo flow.
