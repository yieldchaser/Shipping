## Market Commentary

Handy/Supra market: pressure persists in USG and ECSA, Continent remains flat, while the Black Sea faces mounting disruption

The Supramax/Ultramax market continues to weaken in both the USG and ECSA due to extremely moderate cargo volumes seen for 1H August. Market participants attribute the current lull to the traditional vacation season and expect demand to pick up no earlier than the end of August. At the moment, rates are falling across all trades, with most significant declines reported on transatlantic routes. Note that the Panamax market is putting additional pressure on the Ultramax sector, as it currently appears more attractive to charterers in terms of freight levels. By the end of the week, spot rates in the north have dropped by $1.5-2k daily and $1-2/t, while South Atlantic freight levels have sagged within $0.5k daily and $0.5- 1/t. Thus, rates for shipment of 50,000 t of soybeans or corn from USG to N.China have inched down to $64-65/t, while those from USG to Tarragona have decreased to $39-40/t. Handy rates also continue to decline, and there are no signs of a slowdown in this trend yet. Only a few cargoes are available on the market, while the number of open vessels keeps rising steadily. However, brokers note a localized shortage of vessels open for mid-August dates. Over the week, corresponding rates have decreased by $0.5-1k daily and $1-1.5/t in both parts of the Atlantic.

In North Europe, the market has stabilized at last week's levels for now. The bid/offer gap has widened, as expectations of market participants differ significantly. The Mediterranean market remains extremely difficult to analyze, as it essentially comprises three completely different segments. The so-called “non- premium” or “standard” market covering shipments from EU ports, Turkey, and North Africa is definitely not showing any growth, as the relevant tonnage list is quite long, and activity has now stabilized following a slight upturn in recent weeks. At the same time, the situation is entirely different in the segment for shipments from Ukraine. Although firm offers from local ports have become even scarcer than a week ago, the lack of vessels willing to call at Ukrainian ports is forcing traders to accept further significant increases in rates. At the same time, some shipowners' demands appear “obstinately high” to traders. In any case, sporadic deals are still being signed; short- haul freight levels have grown by $8-12/t, long-haul ones – by $12-18/t. For instance, charterers are ready to pay high $30s/t for transportation of 25-30,000 t of wheat or corn from POC to EgyptMed and up to $50/t from POC to Algeria. However, even these rates often do not guarantee charterers that they will receive counteroffers from shipowners. The situation is similar in case of shipments ex Russian ports, with the only difference being that rate growth is slower.

Freight rates are generally flat also in the Indian Ocean . However, shipowners are facing an increase in the number of vessels available off the Indian coast, as well as cautious sentiment in the South African sector.

Next week, rate downtrend may continue in the Atlantic Ocean amid a cargo shortage in all major exporting areas of the basin. Freights are expected to fall particularly sharply in USG and USEC, a trend that will most likely continue into 2H August.

Cargo traffic ex Baltic & Continent ports is likely to be gradually perking up in August. The expected shift of some Russian Black Sea cargo (and possibly some Ukrainian cargo) to the Baltic region will provide additional support to the local market. In addition, some positive impact can be expected from the recovery of Turkish demand for scrap and the start of the new grain season in Europe. As before, market developments in the Mediterranean region will depend on the military and political situation. In the absence of shipments from Ukraine and Russia, charterers have no chance of seeing higher rates ex EU, Turkish and North African ports.

The Supramax/Ultramax market prospects are uncertain in the Indian Ocean. Owners working with South African exports are still voicing TC levels that are too high relative to what current voyage business can support, and market participants expect those TC ideas to come down. Meanwhile, the Yemeni Houthis have announced the blockade on Saudi shipments in the Red Sea, with some tankers already forced to turn 'dark' in order to make it to India. While the impact on tanker trades is already apparent, it is yet to be seen whether such turmoil translates into a widespread avoidance of dry-bulk transits through Bab el-Mandeb.
