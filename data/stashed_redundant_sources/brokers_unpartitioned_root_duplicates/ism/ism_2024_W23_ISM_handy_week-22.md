## Market Commentary

The ECSA Handy market has firmed up thanks to increasing cargo offer for shipments from northern and southern Brazil with 1H-mid June laycans (both transatlantic, coastal and towards WCSA). By the end of the week, corresponding rates have risen by $1-2k daily

Optimism is getting back to ECSA Handy market, while Continent and Mediterranean sections still cannot find the bottom

and $2-3/t. Thus, rates for shipment of 25-30,000 t of corn from Upriver to Morocco have inched up to $44-45/t given 7,000x/5,000x l/d rates. Meanwhile, despite increased cargo offer for June laycans in USG, the tonnage surplus remains significant, which does not allow owners to push freight levels up.

Supramax/Ultramax owners have managed to stall rate cuts in both parts of the Atlantic Ocean. Similar to neighboring tonnage groups, owners were supported by brisker cargo traffic, especially on F/H routes. “Some more fresh cargoes seem to have entered the market for mid-late June, helping to improve the sentiment,” a broker mentioned.

Posidonia week and shipping dinner in Bergen have virtually killed business activity in the Mediterranean Sea and the Continent. Tonnage is building up both in EMed and the Skaw-Passero range, while fresh cargoes are very

few. It is still hard to say whether the market has reached its bottom this week, but current RV TCEs increasingly reach (and sometimes plunge below) the OPEX level, especially in the Handysize segment. Freight rates have dropped by another $1-2k daily and $0.5-2.5/t in both regions. Shipment of 30,000 t of clay from Chornomorsk to Ravenna is currently around $20/t, while rates for transportation of 25-30,000 t of fertilizers from Lower Baltic to Santos have sagged to $26-26.5/t.

In the Asia-Pacific region, Supramax/Ultramax rates have sagged by another $1k daily and $0.5-1/t due to a slowdown in Indonesian and Australian coal exports. Meanwhile, Handysize/Handymax freight levels are stable, although players already see first negative signs, particularly decreasing steel exports from the Far East. “I already see a couple of Handysize vessels on spot eager to find cargoes. I think tonnage supply is increasing,” a broker commented.

Ship owners share quite optimistic sentiment and expectations regarding further developments in the South Atlantic: “tonnage count looks much shorter for the coming few weeks and at the same time forward cargo bookings are creating some hope for owners,” a broker mentioned. Meanwhile, USG Handy rates are likely to stay under pressure, unless there is an outflow of tonnage to N.Brazil, where the market is gradually strengthening.

At the end of the week, the Mediterranean market has been stunned by news of a ban on wheat imports by Turkey from June 21 to October 15, but positive outlooks still prevail among players. There is no room for a further rate decline, and this time the new crop in Ukraine will come to the market a couple of weeks earlier than usual due to the dry weather, which means we may see a

noticeable upturn in wheat and barley exports in 1H July. Trade is likely to perk up slightly in North Europe next week, but rates are likely to stay at the bottom for the time being.

“Summer is coming and maybe China will import more coal due to bigger electricity requirement,” a broker shares his optimistic expectations on APAC Supramax/ Ultramax market, which sound legit considering decreasing coal stocks at Chinese power plants. However, upcoming Dragonboat festival in the country may add some negativity in a short run.
