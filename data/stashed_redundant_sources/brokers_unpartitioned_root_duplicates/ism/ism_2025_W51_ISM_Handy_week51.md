## Market Commentary

### Downward pressure on rates strengthened in all sections of Handysize/ Supramax market

The Supramax/Ultramax and Handysize shipping markets have expectedly declined in all parts of the Western Hemisphere ahead of the holiday weekend in a number of European countries, the US, and South America. A similar picture can be seen across all tonnage segments: virtually all December requests have already been covered, and many shipowners are actively considering cargoes with 1H January laycans in order to secure employment before the holidays. Meanwhile, charterers, if possible, do not hurry to enter the market with new requests or are aggressively pushing freight levels down when discussing January shipments, as they expect an even more noticeable decline in rates after the holiday weekend. “Activity easing across most regions, rates are declining in most directions as we move to holiday season,” a broker mentioned. As in ECSA and USG, rates in the Continent and Mediterranean Sea have dropped by $0.5-2k daily and $0.5-2/t on all routes by the end of the week. For instance, rates for shipment of 50,000 t of corn or soybeans from Santos to N.China have inched down to $45/t, while those from New Orleans have sagged to $58/t. Transportation of 30,000 t of wheat from CVB to the northern Adriatic now costs $17-18/t, however some traders already target even below $15/t for January dates.

The Asia-Pacific market also continues to decline given the slowdown in shipments of Indonesian coal,

as well as steel and fertilizers from CJK ports. Cargo traffic from Australia is decreasing as well. In the Indian Ocean, ISM sources note decreased exports of bulk cargo from PG and South Africa, as well as of ore from ECI. At the same time, the surplus of available vessels is becoming more acute, especially in the Indian Ocean Supramax sector. “The number of vessels off South Africa is extensive, while the ECSA market is coming off,” a broker adds. This week, freight rates have dropped by $0.5-2k daily and within $1/t.

Next week, Atlantic market activity is almost certain to decline even further, as most players will be absent from the start of the week due to the long holiday weekend in a number of countries. Freight rates are also likely to fall amid a noticeable decline in cargo traffic during the festive period.

Market participants agree that conditions will continue to deteriorate in the Supramax/Ultramax segments of both the Asia-Pacific region and the Indian Ocean. Moreover, sources believe that the market may only recover after the Lunar New Year.
