## Market Commentary

Low cargo offer keeps small-tonnage freights stable or on the decrease all across the market

Another week on the coaster shipping market is passing without a single note of optimism for ship owners. Particularly this applies to the Azov-Black Sea basin and the Mediterranean Sea. Given the extremely unfavorable conditions in the grain and metallurgical commodity sectors, the offer of these cargoes (which are traditionally driving the freight market) remains very poor. The Black Sea market has even seen some activity decline this week, and only the tonnage outflow allowed owners to restrain the rate cuts (short-haul freights ex Russian, Romanian and Bulgarian ports have sagged by $0.5/t on average, while levels from Ukraine stay at the bottom). For instance, rates for shipment of 5-6,000 t of corn from Izmail or POC to Mersin or Lebanon are still hovering at $21.5-22/t, while transportation cost from Bulgaria to Marmara has inched down to $13-13.5/t. “We receive only sub-sale requests from our clients with extremely low ideas, which become firm very very seldom,” a Ukrainian broker complains. “Even optimistic owners already feel desperate since there is no light at the end of the tunnel,” a Turkish broker commented to ISM. Meanwhile, the number of 6-12k dwt vessels open in the Marmara Sea and eastern Mediterranean has grown by about 25%, which has only increased the pressure on corresponding rates. However, ship owners refuse to make concessions considering a noticeable upturn in voyage costs amid switching to the use of more expensive ULSFO and MGO. Thus, transportation of 3-4,000 t of fertilizers from Tunisia to

Romania still costs $18/t, while shipment of 5-6,000 t of urea from EgyptMed to Romania costs $16-17/t.

In the Azov Sea, the sea-river market remains at the bottom, with owners increasingly laying up vessels until the start of the new grain season. A similar picture can be seen in the Caspian Sea, where even river navigation does not save owners. Another holiday in Russia only further aggravates the situation on the local market.

Negative trends prevail also in North Europe, with the Baltic Sea market staying the weakest one: spot cargo offers are literally sporadic in the area. However, we should note some decline in 2-4k dwt vessel count in the Baltic Sea (-11 units over the week). The Continent segment is showing slightly higher activity, but the tonnage list is much longer in the region (due in part to the outflow of vessels from the Baltic Sea), which even forces ship owners to make concessions. During the week, freight rates on all key routes have dropped by €0.5-1/t. For instance, the deal for transportation of 4,000 t of fertilizers from ARAG to Sweden (Gulf of Bothnia) is discussed at €17-18/t.

### and dynamics on major routes

Market players can hardly expect any noticeable activity growth in the Black and Mediterranean Seas, although some occasional recovery of sales of old crop residues cannot be ruled out. At the same time, there are no preconditions for increased activity in the segments for steel products and fertilizer transportation. In North Europe, trade will most likely again be affected by another industry event (this time the BreakBulk in Rotterdam), so owners can hardly count on any improvements in this region as well.
