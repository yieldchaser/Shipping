## Market Commentary

Black Sea and Mediterranean coaster markets firming up, while Azov sea-river sector weakens

Positive trends continue to dominate the small- tonnage shipping market, primarily in the Black Sea , where exports of Ukrainian grain are gaining pace. Note that the number of available vessels of up to 6k dwt is quite sufficient, while the shortage of 6-12k dwt fleet keeps getting more acute, with the situation in the 9-11k dwt segment looking particularly difficult for charterers. “We simply don't see 10k dwt vessels for 10-20 October laycans, no matter what our charterers intend to pay,” a Ukrainian broker complains. “Many traders are starting to widen their lot sizes or even try to split them into two small parcels in order to be able to find a vessel,” another broker adds. Despite the still moderate offer of other cargoes in the region (we can only note a slight recovery of fertilizer shipments), freight rates have risen by another $0.5- 1/t in case of small shipments and within $2/t for mini-bulkers of about 10k dwt. For instance, rates for shipment of 10,000 t of corn or soybeans from POC to Alexandria have increased to $28-29/t, while those for transportation of 5-6,000 t lots have inched up to $29-30/t. Despite a relatively flat tonnage list, the Mediterranean market also continues to strengthen gradually ($0.5-1/t up this week) thanks to increased demand from both local charterers and the Black Sea market. Note that average RV TCEs have finally reached the OPEX level in both basins, and even exceeded the OPEX by 20-40% in the 8-12k dwt sector.

Meanwhile, the Azov Sea market is by contrast showing some softening. Despite unfavorable weather conditions and still limited draft in Rostov and Azov (3.5-3.55 meters), a significant decline in offer of Russian wheat pushed freights down by $2-3/t. “Seems TMO has started increasingly selling wheat on the domestic market, with less demand for imports seen from Turkiye now,” an experienced trader commented to ISM. Thus, transportation of 3-5,000 t of wheat from Rostov/Azov to Marmara now costs low $50s/t. However, even current freight levels allow sea- river owners to feel much more comfortable in terms of earnings compared to owners of sea-going vessels. In the Caspian Sea and on the Volga river market, rates continue to grow steadily amid the shortage of open ships and stably high demand for grain in Iran.

The Baltic & Continent coaster market presents a mixed picture, with players' opinions varying greatly. Thus, the tonnage list has decreased significantly (by almost 30% in a week), but not all players attribute this to brisker tonnage demand; rather, they consider it a consequence of stormy weather and voyage delays. In terms of cargo traffic, new requests are coming in regularly, but activity is growing very slowly for October. Ship owners are trying to push freight rates up aggressively, but in fact, they have only managed to raise rates by €0.5-1.5/t, depending on the route.

### and dynamics on major routes

Next week, freight rates will most likely continue climbing up in the Black and Mediterranean Seas, as the corn export season is just getting started and wheat exports have not peaked yet. Ship owners will most likely manage to maintain positive momentum even with the still unfavorable situation on the market for finished and semi-finished steel products, as well as in the coal sector (demand for coal remains weak in Turkey and the EU). The market should be additionally supported by fertilizer traffic, in particular urea. Meanwhile, the Azov Sea market may continue to decline next week, as it was clearly overheated in late September and early October, and does not reflect the real picture of the region's key commodity markets. A further rate growth is quite likely in North Europe, although owners lack serious preconditions for this development, unless we take the seasonality factor into account.
