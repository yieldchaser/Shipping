## Market Commentary

Similar to the past few weeks, North Europe is the only region where small-tonnage owners are feeling the taste of approaching holidays and the traditional pre-holiday excitement (although not as high as usual). Limited spot tonnage list and persistent weather problems allowed owners to raise freight

No pre-holiday rush seen on small-tonnage markets of Azov-Black Sea basin and Mediterranean Sea

rates by another €0.5-1/t. At the moment, local ship owners manage to earn at least 30% above the OPEX when operating from EU ports and at least twice as much as OPEX when working ex Russian ports. For instance, the deal for transportation of about 10,000 t of fertilizers from St. Petersburg to Poland is negotiated at low €30s/t, which is equivalent to $10- 11k daily bss RV.

Other regions - the Mediterranean, Black and Azov Seas - are showing no hint of improvement for owners. The cargo flow remains extremely slack in the region, as a slight upturn in shipments from EU ports was offset by ongoing decline in fertilizer traffic from Egypt, as well as in grain shipments from Ukraine. Moreover, fixing activity has even decreased

in the Black Sea this week. “The gap in freight ideas for shipments of Ukrainian grain has even widened since grain charterers are under pressure of commodity market, while ship owners are not able to accept further cuts - neither economically, nor psychologically,” a Ukrainian broker commented to ISM. Freight rates have not changed much by the end of the week. Thus, transportation of 3-4,000 t of corn or wheat from Odesa or Izmail to EC Greece still costs low $20s/t bss 1/1, while rates for shipment of 5-7,000 t of steels from Novorossiysk to TBS are hovering at $15.5-16/t. Note that working from the ports of Romania, Bulgaria, Turkey or Russia, owners are still able to cover the OPEX and even surpass this level slightly under certain circumstances, but it is virtually impossible in case of shipments from Ukraine. In the Azov Sea, the cargo shortage has only got more acute during the week, as a decline in coal exports has just added to extremely poor wheat offer. Thus, sea-river rates have dropped by $2-4/t over the week despite adverse weather, noticeably longer delays

As for the Caspian Sea, the cargo traffic from Astrakhan has also decreased, with freights down by $1-2/t, amid new documentation requirements for grain exports.

In the next couple of weeks, we may witness some positional recovery in the Black and Mediterranean Seas, but it will hardly allow ship owners to push rates up. On the contrary, long tonnage list may force many owners to make concessions in order not to stay unemployed during the holiday period. In the Baltic Sea, most of the requests will surely be covered by the end of next week, and rates will also start sagging ahead of holidays. Note that some deals with January laycans are quite expectedly negotiated €1-3/t below spot levels (this mainly applies to grain shipments from Baltic ports and Denmark).
