## Market Commentary

Coaster owners avoid rollback of Black Sea & Med freights, while Azov market continues cooling off

Entering November, the small-tonnage market traditionally starts showing more bullish sentiment from ship owners. “In reality market activity looks steady, although owners increasingly repeat “Winter Is Coming” just like in “Game of Thrones” series,” an EU broker commented jokingly. Moreover, according to ISM estimates, as well as those of many brokers, market activity has even declined slightly during the week, especially in the Black Sea, where grain deliveries to ports have slowed down due to rains, forcing traders to postpone some shipments. The national holiday in Turkey is also having a negative impact on regional activity. Nevertheless, owners still manage to keep freight rates steady amid the continuing shortage of available tonnage in the Black and Marmara Seas. For example, less than 20 vessels of 4-8k dwt and less than 10 ships of 8-12k dwt are open in the Black Sea for the whole of November. Shipment of 5-6,000 t of soybeans or corn from Izmail or Chornomorsk to Marmara still costs $25-25.5/t, while the deal for transportation of a similar lot of wheat from CVB to the Italian Adriatic is discussed at $26-27/t. There is visually more available tonnage in the Mediterranean Sea, but local owners have even managed to raise rates slightly (by $0.5-1.5/t), bringing them closer to Time-Charter Equivalents seen in the neighboring region. Rates for transportation of 5-6,000 t of urea from Damietta to SpanMed have inched up to $30- 31/t, which is equivalent to quite strong $2.8-3.2k daily bss RV.

The North European market is again seeing a decrease in vessel count due to worsening weather conditions last week, as well as an increase in the number of COA-based contracts. At the same time, the cargo traffic remains rather slack in the basin as for the start of November. The bid/offer gap is widening, while fixing levels have still grown by €0.5- 1/t, according to ISM estimates.

Meanwhile, Azov Sea rates have been sliding down for the second week in a row. This time, grain freights have dropped by another $3-5/t, coal ones - by $1- 2/t. Even with this decline, regional TCEs for sea-river vessels are still about twice as high as OPEX and significantly exceed those for similar-size seagoing vessels in the Black Sea. Activity is also decreasing gradually in the Caspian Sea , although freight rates hold steady for now, as regional vessel count is very small.

In 1H November, activity will most likely be growing on the Black Sea small-tonnage market, primarily due to exports of new-crop corn. Owners working in North Europe count on at least a slight upturn in shipments of steel products and fertilizers as the holidays approach, but worsening weather conditions may actually become a more compelling argument in this case.
