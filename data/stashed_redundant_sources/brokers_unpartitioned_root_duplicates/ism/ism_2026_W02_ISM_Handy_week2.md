## Market Commentary

Handysize & Supramax market eases across all regions as USG sees a sharp downturn

The North Atlantic Handysize and Supramax/ Ultramax market has started the year with a sharp decline. “The market is notably weaker compared to the end of the year,” a broker mentions. “USG and USEC are empty in terms of cargo volume on T/A and F/H,” another broker adds. Shipowners are forced to ballast from USEC to USG in order to catch sporadic InterCaribs cargoes. Over the two weeks, Handysize and Supramax/Ultramax rates have dropped by $3-5k daily and $3-6/t on all routes. For instance, shipment of about 50,000 t of soybeans or corn from New Orleans to N.China now costs low $50s/t (compared to high $50s/t in late Dec).

Meanwhile, the South Atlantic market picture is more favorable for Supramax/Ultramax owners. Trade appears quite lively as for the post-holiday period (especially on F/H routes). The market in West Africa is also brisk, which is holding back the inflow of ballasters into ECSA. “Some post-holiday rate correction still happened, but very minor, especially if compared to USG,” a broker comments. Since the start of the year, freight levels have fallen by $1-2k daily and $1-3/t across all trades. However, the situation is not so optimistic for Handy owners. The shortage of cargo is quite acute in this segment, and rates have declined by $2-3k daily and $3-4/t on all routes. Thus, rates for shipment of 25-30,000 t of wheat from Upriver to Bejaia have dropped to $41-42/t given 7000x/5000x l/d rates, and to $47-48/t in case of 7000x/2500x l/d rates.

Handysize and Supramax market activity is recovering very slowly in North Europe . The total vessel count for January dates reaches about 100 units in both sectors, while cargo offer looks much smaller. A similar situation is developing in the Mediterranean basin , with the only difference being that the decline in rates ex Black Sea ports is somewhat restrained by increased military risks and a slightly reduced pool of owners willing to continue working ex Ukrainian and Russian ports. Charterers are pushing freight levels down quite aggressively. During the holiday period, rates have fallen quite significantly on key routes, namely by $1.5-4k daily and $0.5-3/t. Note that exports from Russian ports stay minimal (the Christmas holidays have only just ended in Russia). At the moment, transportation of 25-30,000 t of corn from POC to Alexandria costs $19-19.5/t, while rates for shipment of a similar lot of wheat from CVB to Alexandria have inched down to $16-16.5/t.

Despite a surge in exports of Indonesian coal and Far Eastern steel reported in the first half of the week, Handysize and Supramax/Ultramax rates have dropped by $1-2k daily and $0.5-2/t in the Asia-Pacific region over the two weeks, as the tonnage list still outweighs the number of enquiries.

### Supramax/Ultramax freight rates and dynamics on major routes

The Indian Ocean Supramax/Ultramax market has softened this week, primarily due to a slowdown in shipments of Indian iron ore, as well as a growing surplus of vessels available off ECI and South Africa. Corresponding freight rates have sagged by $1-2k daily and $1-2/t.

Next week, rates for shipments from North Atlantic ports may continue to decline, as the shortage of cargoes (especially fronthaul offers of US grain) will persist. Meanwhile, the southern part of the basin has every chance to see a rapid restoration of supply/ demand balance. In addition, the cargo offer for February dates may grow significantly: such requests are already increasingly entering the market.

Next week, we will hardly see any significant improvements in the Continent and in the Mediterranean region. Given the surplus of tonnage, shipowners will likely be forced to make further concessions, which is typical for January. Note that only the frosts forecasted for the Baltic and Black Sea countries in the coming weeks may somewhat slow down the rate decline.

Since the Asia-Pacific Supramax/Ultramax sector continues to suffer from a tonnage surplus, while trading activity has seen only marginal and positional improvements so far, it remains to be seen whether rates will recover to pre-holiday levels before the Chinese New Year.
