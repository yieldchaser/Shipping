In the Asia-Pacific region, demand for Supramax/ Ultramax tonnage is still supported primarily by regular shipments of Indonesian coal. Amid positive impetus from the Atlantic and a slight decline in tonnage list (due in part to increased idle time in the Panama canal), SE Asian Supramax rates have also grown by $0.5-1k daily and $0.25-1/t. Note that the cargo traffic from the Far East has grown slightly. Handysize rates have not changed significantly during the week amid an increase in vessel list, while exports of steel products from CJK to Europe and WAfr remain limited.

The cargo traffic from PG/WCI remains limited, but positive sentiment in ECSA section is pulling tonnage from the S.African region. However, relevant freight rates have not changed significantly this week.

The pace of freight rate growth may slow down in USG as the inflow of ballasters from anywhere within Atlantic is considerable. Nevertheless, the US has entered its peak season for grain exports, while the Mississippi River water levels return to normal and barge levels should normalize. The outlook for US exports is somewhat mixed (soybean exports are expected to fall by 15 million t compared to last year, while corn exports are projected to rise by 9 million t), but near-term positives are still expected. The South Atlantic market may strengthen further if USG keeps drawing attention from West African positions. «We expect this bullishness to continue going into the first half of December,» a broker mentioned. In the Mediterranean region and North Europe, the market may also continue

to grow gradually as demand for steel scrap remains healthy in Turkey, while demand for Turkish steel products has increased slightly. In addition, sales of European grain will surely be maximized ahead of holidays. Another deterioration of weather and navigation conditions in the Black Sea this weekend will also support the rate growth.

The Asia-Pacific Supramax/Ultramax market is likely to start weakening, followed by the already fragile Handysize segment, where the surplus of open vessels will become even more considerable. «Asia-Pacific market has topped out,» an operator commented. Players working in PG/WCI also expect the situation to worsen next week (there is still a shortage of cargoes for prompt dates in the area).
