## Market Commentary

Despite the peak of the grain season in South America, the Asian market has overtaken the status of the key driver of the Handysize and Supramax/Ultramax shipping segments. A gradual increase in coal exports from Indonesia and the Far East as well as in shipments

Asia-Pacific traffic is now key driver of Handy/Supra market, with negative sentiment prevailing in USG, Mediterranean and Continent

of nickel ore from the Philippines to SE Asia allowed Supramax/Ultramax owners to raise freight rates by $1-3k daily and $1-2/t. Despite limited offer of steel products for transportation from Far Eastern ports, Handysize owners have also managed to raise freight rates slightly thanks to decreased tonnage list in SE Asia. Thus, shipment of 40,000 t of coal from Samarinda to S.China now costs $15-16/t. In the Indian Ocean, Supramax/Ultramax rates have added $1-2k daily and $0.5-1.5/t due to brisker exports of iron ore from ECI as well as of coal and minor bulk cargoes from South Africa. In addition, the market regularly shows offers of fertilizers, minerals and aggregates for shipments from the PG to the Far East. Meanwhile, Handysize owners are so far unable to raise rates despite higher pace of trade. “We notice slightly longer tonnage list off WCI and Red Sea,” a local broker commented.

Positive trends prevail in ECSA Handysize sector. The market has firmed up amid increased grain offer for 1H May shipments on both transatlantic and coastal routes. Over the week, rates have grown by $1-1.5k daily and $2-3/t in the region. Similar sentiment dominates the Supramax/Ultramax segment: the number of cargo requests for transportation from ECSA and WAfr with May laycans has increased. Note that most ship owners are showing interest in working on front-haul routes, which leads to a noticeable upturn in transatlantic rates ($2-3k daily and $2-3/t up, while F/H levels have risen by $0.5-1k daily and $0.5-1.5/t). Rates for shipment of up to 50,000 t of soybeans from Santos to N.China have inched up to $46-47/t given 8,000 sshex bends l/d rates, while those for 50,000 t of corn from Santos to Tarragona have increased to $29-30/t.

In USG, Supramax/Ultramax rates have stabilized after last week's growth. There is no tonnage surplus in the region yet, while the cargo flow is stable. Meanwhile, the Handysize market remains under pressure of limited cargo offer and gradually increasing spot/prompt tonnage list. “Intercaribs shipments are relatively active, while T/As

are sporadic,” a broker mentioned. However, ship owners manage to keep rates stable for the time being. Rates for transportation of 25-30,000 t of corn from USG to ARA are still hovering at 25-26/t given 8,000 sshex bends l/d rates.

At the same time, ship owners are seeing no reasons for optimism in the Black and Mediterranean Seas as well as in the Continent. The main importers of European, Ukrainian and Russian grain insist on further price cuts, complicating the already difficult situation for suppliers to the limit. Given modest cargo flow and rather long vessel list, ship owners struggle to keep rates at previous levels in the Mediterranean Sea (only rates for shipments ex Ukrainian ports have dropped by $0.5-1/t, gradually losing their premium status). In North Europe, freight rates have declined by $0.5-1k daily and $0.5-2/t depending on the route.

In the Asia-Pacific region, players expect further strengthening of the Supramax/Ultramax market. “I think Supramax market will remain firm as Chinese coal imports

will remain active; however, not sure about Handy market,” a broker reckons, implying that solely lack of tonnage will not be sufficient for substantial gains in Handysize sector. Note that additional support for the Supramax market may come from a possible growth of coal imports by India ahead of the monsoon season.

Next week, ECSA rates are likely to show a further upturn as the cargo offer for May dates is growing steadily in all tonnage segments. “With paper back in the green, especially May, the feeling is firm in the South Atlantic,” a broker mentioned. Meanwhile, ship owners working in the

North Atlantic (especially Handysize ones) will be facing difficulties even keeping the rates stable amid slow cargo traffic. “Growing number of Handy vessels with spot/ prompt dates, especially of smaller sizes, will drive the market lower,” another broker mentioned.

Negative outlook prevails in the Mediterranean Sea and the Continent. The grain season is slowly coming to an end, with importers likely to continue to push for lower prices. Grain trade will continue to suffer additional pressure from uncertainty over additional export quotas for wheat in Russia and another tightening of inspections of exporters in Ukraine. There are no preconditions for any recovery of the regional steel export market. Demand for steel scrap remains minimal in Turkey despite positive expectations of European suppliers. Activity is likely to shrink to a minimum in the Black and Baltic Seas by the end of next week due to the Easter holidays in a number of countries. The fact that the market is increasingly receiving fertilizer offers for shipments ex Baltic ports in 2H May is the only positive news for owners, but it will hardly affect the overall market dynamics without an increase in other cargo segments.
