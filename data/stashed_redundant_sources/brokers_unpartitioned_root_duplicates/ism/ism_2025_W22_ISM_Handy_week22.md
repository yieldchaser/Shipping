## Market Commentary

### Handysize/Supramax rates under pressure of scant cargo offer all across the market

The Supramax/Ultramax and Handysize market sentiment is deteriorating in the Atlantic Ocean. Thus, new grain requests for shipments from Brazil and Argentina less frequently enter the market compared to last week (especially in the Supramax segment). Additional pressure on the ECSA market comes from the softening of the WAfr and SAfr sectors. In USG, tonnage demand has also started to decline after a solid upturn seen during the last few weeks. Charterers do not hurry to show up with new requests, feeling a gradual increase in tonnage list for June dates. Nevertheless, ship owners still manage to keep rates at last week's levels both in the North and South Atlantic. Thus, shipment of 25-30,000 t of wheat from Upriver to Algeria still costs very low $40s/t given 7000x/5000x l/d rates, and low-mid $40s/t given 7000x/2500x l/d rates.

The Baltic & Continent market has weakened again after some recovery. This is partly caused by holidays in the EU countries and the UK. In the Black Sea, activity expectedly stays minimal, as players are waiting for the new grain season. Spot freight rates are generally unchanged in both basins. “We see increasing number of requests from grain traders for July-August dates, but there is mostly reconnaissance, with still lack of firm offers,” a local broker commented to ISM and mentioned that forward indications from owners are so far quite modest, being only $3-4/t higher than

spot levels. For instance, spot transportation of 50- 60,000 t of corn from Ukraine to S.China is estimated by ISM at mid-$30s/t, while owners are seeking to get closer to $40/t in case of 2H July-1H August dates.

The Asia-Pacific Supramax/Ultramax market environment was further aggravated by the slowdown in Indonesian coal shipments (particularly to India, where the monsoon season has started). “We are already struggling to find an interest for owners for Indo-WCI shipments,” a broker claims. Owners are being forced to stay within the region and face growing tonnage list, given worsening situation in both the Indian Ocean and the Atlantic sector. Freight rates have sagged by $0.5-1k daily and $0.25- 0.5/t in the region. Meanwhile, Handysize/Handymax freight levels hold steady in the area. “The number of requests in FEast is roughly the same as last week,” a broker commented.

Things are worse for ship owners operating in the Indian Ocean given a noticeable increase in vessel count, a slowdown in exports of main cargoes from India, as well as in shipments of manganese ore from South Africa. Corresponding rates have dropped by $1-3k daily and $0.5-2/t by the end of the week.

### Supramax/Ultramax freight rates and dynamics on major routes

Next week, freight rates may go down across all tonnage groups and in all parts of the Atlantic Ocean, if the current cargo/tonnage imbalance stays favorable for charterers. In North Europe, only the expected upturn in June shipments of steel scrap from the EU to Turkey may help owners strengthen their positions at least slightly, but it will hardly be sufficient considering the shortage of grain and chemical cargoes.

Imports of main cargoes are likely to remain limited in the Indian Ocean, and this is not just a matter of weather conditions. Market chatter indicates that the Indian government may announce new tariff regulations on imports around May 31. As a result, the majority of traders (especially those working in the coal sector) are holding back.
