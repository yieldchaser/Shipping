Meanwhile, the ECSA Supramax/Ultramax market stays under pressure. «South Atlantic still sliding due to insufficient cargo replenishment, while tonnage list remains very long», a broker mentioned. imbalance got even sharper. «Sometimes we are compelled to work at literally OPEX levels,» an EU ship owner claims, meaning that actual TCEs for Handysize shipments sometimes do not reach even $4.5k daily. In contrast to the neighboring Mediterranean market, more pressure is put on voyage-basis rates in the North: «Depends if you do it on voyage or TCT, guess people are even more aggressive when it comes to book voyage biz,» a broker mentioned. Time-charter rates have sagged by $0.5-0.75k daily, while transportation costs of main cargoes have dropped by $0.5-2/t. Shipment of 25-30,000 t of steel scrap from ARAG or Avonmouth to Turkey with 6000x/5000c l/d rates costs $16-17/t. Rates ex Russian ports continue sliding down a bit more noticeably from week to week; thus, difference in rates between Russian and other regional ports keeps reducing, although activity here is slightly higher than in the Baltic or Continent.

In eastern hemisphere, Supramax/Ultramax and Handysize owners manage to achieve a slight rate increase only in case of shipments from SE Asian ports, while the Far Eastern and Indian Ocean markets are completely on the charterers' side. In SE Asia, time-charter rates have

risen by $0.5k daily due to the recovery of Indonesian coal shipments to India and stable cargo flow from Australia. Voyage-basis rates are virtually unchanged due to ongoing decline in bunker prices.

Despite regular shipments of steel products from Far Eastern ports, the local market is softening amid increased tonnage list. Freight rates have sagged by $0.5-1k daily and $0.25-0.75/t over the week.

In the Indian Ocean, Supramax/Ultramax rates ex Persian Gulf have also sagged by $0.5-1k daily and $0.75- 1.5/t as the return of players after the Eid al-Adha had no impact on pace of trade, which remains extremely weak.

Handysize rates have shown an even more considerable decline, namely within $1-1.5k daily and $1-3/t. Note that unfavorable weather conditions along Indian coasts add to the already negative market sentiment.

*** In ECSA, Supramax downward will probably continue next week despite lively grain exports from Brazil as the 2H July tonnage list will definitely not shorten. Meanwhile, Handysize owners are more optimistic, although the emerging positive trends in the region may be offset by the continuing ballaster inflow from the north. By contrast, Supramax/Ultramax owners working in USG will most likely manage to preserve the positive momentum and raise rates by at least another $1k daily.

In the Black Sea and the Continent, any rate bottoming out is possible only in case of a significant increase in demand for European and Russian new crop grain or the resumption and normalization of work of Ukrainian grain corridor. Probably, importers' interest will soon increase due to a further decline in wheat export prices, which, however, will strongly limit charterers' options and complicate negotiations on the freight market. For the time being, it is too early to state anything, as the surplus of tonnage remains very large in the region.

In the Indian Ocean, rates may stabilize after weather conditions improve along the Indian coasts. In SE Asia, ship owners put hopes on a possible upturn in coal traffic to China.
