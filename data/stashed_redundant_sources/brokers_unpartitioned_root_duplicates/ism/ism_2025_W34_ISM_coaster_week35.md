## Market Commentary

Azov sea-river sector starts cooling off after recent skyrocketing, while seagoing coaster market yet to show noticeable improvements

In contrast to the large-tonnage shipping sector (which can definitely be called dynamic, although not hot), the small-tonnage seagoing market remains slow, with minimal rate fluctuations. Moreover, the number of 2-12k dwt vessels open in the Black , Marmara and Aegean Seas, as well as in the eastern Mediterranean has increased by almost 10% by the end of the week and now reaches almost 200 units (the list of 8-12k dwt ships has risen most noticeably). Ship owners are unable to push freights up given still limited grain offer from Ukrainian and EU ports, as well as steadily dull exports of other main cargoes (primarily steel products and fertilizers). For instance, shipment of 5-6,000 t of grains (sf up to 50`) from Ukraine to EgyptMed still costs low-mid $20s/t, while transportation towards the Italian Adriatic is quoted at $29-30/t.

Even the Azov sea-river market (which literally soared during August due to long idle times and significant slowdown of fleet turnover) has started to show signs of cooling down this week. The fact is that insufficiently favorable export market conditions nearly halted sales of Russian wheat considering such high logistics costs, while gradually decreasing downtimes in Kerch and local ports allowed charterers to start lowering freight rates. However, spot tonnage list is so far limited in the region (especially if we take 3-4k dwt fleet), so owners manage to hold back the decline. By the end of the week, rates have dropped

by $0.5-2/t. Thus, rates for transportation of 3,000 t of wheat from Azov/Rostov to Marmara have inched down to $39-40/t.

Ship owners working in North Europe stay optimistic, hoping for seasonal market strengthening in the near future, but no significant improvements can be seen so far. Current cargo traffic is obviously insufficient to cover the regional tonnage list. Note that the total number of vessels open in the basin continues to decrease little by little, although the Baltic Sea vessel count has by contrast risen by about 20% (42 vs. 35 a week earlier). Despite sharpening standoff, owners have managed to achieve a slight rate upturn (€0.5-1/t up) only when discussing shipments with laycans after September 10.

Our forecasts for the Black Sea market remain unchanged - owners should not expect any serious increase in activity and rates until early October, when Ukrainian corn of the new crop enters the market. A tragic incident with a Ukrainian warship on the Danube (which was attacked by Russian USV) may lead to a slight upturn in owners' ideas, but it will hardly affect the local market significantly.
