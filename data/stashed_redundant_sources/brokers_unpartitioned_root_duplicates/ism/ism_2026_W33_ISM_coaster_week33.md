## Market Commentary

Coaster market: charterers partially reversed recent surge in Ukrainian freight rates, Mediterranean and Baltic face a long list of open vessels

Cargo offer continues to grow on the Black Sea small-tonnage market in the first  half of August, which primarily applies to shipments from the Danube ports of Ukraine and Romania. However, the share of “non-firm” cargoes is only increasing, with shippers of Ukrainian grain refusing to accept further rate upturns. Moreover, they are aggressively driving rates down. “Most traders push rates down; otherwise, they postpone sales and shipments, so owners start to accept slightly lower rates,” a Ukrainian broker told ISM. Despite a gradual decline in the number of available vessels (down from 67 to 60 units of 2-12k dwt), rates from Danube ports have dropped by $2-3/ t over the week. Thus, a couple of deals for shipment of 5-6,000 t of wheat from Izmail or Reni to Alexandria or Lebanon have been agreed at $62-63/t compared with $65-67/t a week ago. Cargo flows from ports in the EU, Turkiye, Russia, and the eastern Mediterranean appear relatively stable. For instance, transportation of 6-7,000 t of wheat from Bulgaria to the Italian Adriatic still costs mid $20s/t bss 1/1. According to ISM estimates, the rate growth from Russia has also stalled. “Actually, there is no market from Russian ports – most details are undisclosed, and fixtures are off the market,” a Turkish broker noted. Freights from EU Black Sea ports, as well as within the Mediterranean Sea, remain unchanged, while RV TCEs are close to historic lows as for mid-August.

The Azov Sea market remains non-existent. Meanwhile, activity keeps steadily growing in the Caspian Sea, as do corresponding freight rates (up another $4-8/t this week).

The Baltic & Continent market appears to have reached the peak of the “summer lull.” Thus, spot cargo offer remains extremely limited, while the vessel count has inched up since last week. Local shipowners are trying to keep rates stable, but spot deals are often being signed at lower levels.

The Mediterranean and Black Sea markets remain hostage to the mutual blockade of shipping between Russia and Ukraine. Even with a possible further increase in grain shipments from EU ports (including those transiting from Ukraine), as well as the recovery of steel and fertilizer traffic, the total cargo volume – excluding Ukrainian and Russian exports – will still be insufficient to push freight rates up.

In North Europe, market participants expect a gradual recovery in activity starting in early September – the number of new requests for grain shipments in September is already on the rise, which should help shipowners drive rates up. We may also see an influx of vessels from the western Mediterranean if the local market remains depressed.
