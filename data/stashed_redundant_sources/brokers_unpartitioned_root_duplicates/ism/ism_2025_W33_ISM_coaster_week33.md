## Market Commentary

### Azov sea-river freights rising further, while seagoing coaster market keeps moving sideways

Azov Sea has been the only hot spot of the small- tonnage shipping market for the third consecutive week. The accumulative effect expectedly worked on the local market: protracted idle times (due to inspections and weather conditions) resulted in a shortage of open sea-river vessels which is getting more acute day after day. Brisk shipments from Russian river ports are just adding to the shortage of tonnage. “With lack of open vessels around, many charterers with firm cargoes started to be nervous and accept higher rates, which was a trigger for owners,” a Turkish broker commented. Rates for grain transportation have risen by $5-7/t by the end of the week. Shipment of 3,000 t of wheat from Rostov/Azov to Marmara already costs $36-38/t. Charterers of other cargoes (in particular, coal) are not ready to compete with grain traders yet: corresponding indicative freights have added $2-4/t. Note that even with 5-6 days of idle time in Kerch, earnings of sea-river owners already confidently cover OPEX.

Meanwhile, the neighboring Black Sea , as well as the Mediterranean region, sees virtually no preconditions for higher activity and freight rates. Despite a slight increase in wheat demand in North Africa and the EU, the vast majority of shipments are being made by large-tonnage vessels. “Seems like a new grain season is walking past the coaster market,” a ship owner mentions sarcastically. “Most charterers are still fighting for every cent with sub-sale offers in hands and escape from the market in case there are

no suitable vessels,” another ship owner complains. According to ISM estimates, the number of new grain offers from Ukraine is even smaller this week. However, players report that there are charterers ready to pay $0.5-1/t more than a couple of weeks ago, which certainly inspires optimism in owners. For instance, some charterers are ready to pay mid-high $20s/t for shipment of 3,000 t of corn or wheat from the Danube to EC Greece. Meanwhile, rates for transportation of 6-7,000 t of wheat or soybeans from POC to Alexandria are still hovering at $23-24/t. In general, we can only note a slight recovery of steel shipments in the region, while other cargo flows remain dull (including fertilizer exports from Egypt). The tonnage list has decreased only in the 4-6k and 8-12k dwt segments, while other tonnage groups have even seen an increase in vessel count (especially the 2-4k dwt sector in the Black Sea).

The Baltic Sea market is witnessing a gradual recovery of cargo flow mainly due to an upturn in grain offer. Owners are so far unable to push rates up, as quite a significant part of these cargoes are not firm or have forward laycans, which allows charterers to reject higher ideas for the time being. However, the market is likely to be on the verge of a trend shift, and the bid/offer gap is widening. Besides, the tonnage list keeps getting shorter in the Baltic Sea. At the same time, the number of open vessels has again inched up in the Continent and the Bay of Biscay, which does not allow ship owners even to hint at an increase in rates.

### and dynamics on major routes

Despite the continuing growth of export prices for Ukrainian wheat, sales of small cargo lots are still rare and “creaky”. No market shifts are projected so far, therefore owners can hardly expect to push rates up until the start of the corn export season (i.e. until 2H September), especially considering the absence of any positive signs on other major commodity markets - metallurgical, coal and chemical. Meanwhile, positive momentum may continue in the Azov Sea, as the number of available sea-river vessels will be insufficient in the next couple of weeks. Ship owners will probably continue their attempts to push rates up in the Baltic Sea, though many players are inclined to believe that the region will see the upturn after the end of the vacation season, namely in early September.
