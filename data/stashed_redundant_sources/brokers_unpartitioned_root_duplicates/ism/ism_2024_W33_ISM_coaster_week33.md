## Market Commentary

Coaster ship owners are still unable to get freights moving in the Black and Mediterranean Seas. Moreover, many of them have already accepted the fact that activity will remain low and rates will be holding steady in the coming weeks, especially

Charterers successfully avoid rate upturns on small-tonnage market despite the progress of grain season

in the Black Sea. «We notice that margin between charterers' and owners' freight ideas is not 10-15% as during previous couple of weeks [when they tried to be more bullish], but only around 5-7% now; so, owners become more realistic according to market conditions,» an experienced EU broker mentioned. Slack exports of new crop wheat from Ukrainian and EU ports remain the key problem in the region. In the Mediterranean Sea, the cargo traffic has perked up slightly due to brisker shipments of steel products from Turkey and North African countries. However, the overall sentiment is not improving given minimal activity in other cargo segments (in particular, Egyptian fertilizers). Freight rates are unchanged. Thus, rates for shipment of 3-4,000 t of wheat or corn from Izmail to EC Greece are still hovering at $18-

19/t, while transportation of 5-6,000 t of steels from Nemrut Bay to N.Adraitic still costs $21-22/t.

The Azov sea-river market is looking quite sensitive these days considering small number of vessels open in the region (many ships got stuck in Egyptian and other EMed ports, while many others are involved in shipments on Kavkaz roads). At the very start of the week, the market stabilized and even started to sag a little bit, but later on a slight cargo traffic upturn has pushed rates up by $0.5-1/t. The Caspian Sea market has recovered slightly this week thanks to higher demand for Russian wheat in Iran, which provoked an increase in rates of $0.5-1/t from Astrakhan and Aktau as well as of $2-2.5/t from Volga ports.

Coaster owners working in North Europe show a little more optimism, although there are no signs of improvement in the area. The cargo/tonnage ratio remains unfavorable for ship owners, and spot market

The next couple of weeks will hardly bring much positive news for ship owners. Demand for Ukrainian and Russian wheat remains moderate in Mediterranean countries, while European grain is not withstanding price competition. As for the future corn harvest in Ukraine, farmers' fears are growing due to the prolonged drought. Owners should not count on support from the metallurgical and chemical markets either. Demand for Russian and Mediterranean square billets is still extremely low in Turkey, while exports of Egyptian urea are also minimal. Ship owners' success to raise rates by at least $0.5-1/t in such market conditions in the next 2-3 weeks could be considered a real victory.
