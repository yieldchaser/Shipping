## Market Commentary

An already weak trade on the small-tonnage freight market of the Black and Mediterranean Seas has slackened further after March 25. This even applies to such market drivers as Ukrainian grain and Egyptian fertilizers. The fact that the weather conditions had come to normal in

Small-tonnage market on softening mode in all sections except for Azov Sea

the Bosphorus boosted the average fleet turnover in the region and sharpened tonnage oversupply on the spot market. More than 230 ships with a deadweight of 2-6,000 t as well as over 120 vessels with a deadweight of 6-12,000 t are currently open on the section from Gibraltar to the Kerch Strait, almost 10% exceeding the fleet circulating there in the first half of March. However, shipowners continue to desperately resist any decreases in rates, and they so far manage to keep freights and estimated RV TCEs at the level 15-30% above the OPEX. Note that this is quite an inspiring outcome as for the end of March and early April, since owners rarely managed to achieve such results in this time of the pre-war years. At the moment, shipment of 10,000 t of wheat from Constanta to Italian Adriatic costs low $20s/t, while rates for transportation of 6-7,000 t of corn or soybeans from Izmail or Odesa to Alexandria have inched down to $33-34/t. Rates for shipment of 5-6,000 t of urea from Damietta to SpanMed are hovering

Azov Sea is the only region, where ship owners have managed to regain the initiative and push rates up. Positional shortage of sea-river vessels open for early April and continuing increase in wheat shipments forced charterers to accept the rate growth of another $2-3/t. The Caspian Sea market continues to sag amid sharpening cargo/tonnage imbalance. Transportation costs from Astrakhan have dropped by another $5-8/t, which is referred to normalization of navigation in the Volga-Caspian Canal. Rates for shipments from Aktau are generally flat this week.

In the short term, the market is likely to remain rather weak in the Black and Mediterranean Seas, and owners will focus on avoiding rate cuts. There are virtually no preconditions for an increase in trade in the basin; the situation is relatively positive only on the commodity market for fertilizers: demand for AN and urea looks healthy in European countries. Other commodity sectors are showing few reasons for optimism. Grain season is gradually ending, while demand for Black Sea and Turkish finished and semi-finished steel products is low in Mediterranean and North European countries. Increased coal prices in the EU have reduced the already weak demand on the spot market. Note that the current growth in the Azov Sea is unlikely to be long-lived. According to traders, the cargo flow will go down in a couple of weeks, followed by sea-river rates. Besides, the soon opening of navigation on the Volga-Don Canal may lead to an inflow of tonnage from the weakened Caspian Sea market to the Azov Sea. As for North Europe, many players are confident that activity in the North Sea will rather quickly recover

at least to the pre-holiday level. «However, this does not apply to the Baltic Sea market as the cargo flow will hardly increase in the area, while improved weather along with the end of ice campaign will widen the vessel list for April dates,» an EU broker concluded.
