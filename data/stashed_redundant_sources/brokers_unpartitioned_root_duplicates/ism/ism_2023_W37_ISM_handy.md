## Market Commentary

The Supramax/Ultramax and Handysize market continues to strengthen steadily in almost all freight sections for the third consecutive week. « USG continues on strong mode as the best paying location, even though the pace at which rates are increasing is visibly lower vs.

last week,» a broker mentioned. Amid vessel shortage in USEC, owners of carriers open in NCSA are increasingly considering cargo offers from the USA, which, by the way, plays into the hands of owners operating from N.Brazil. Speaking about the South Atlantic as a whole, players note still quite active offer of grain for September laycans, with additional support to the spot market still provided by growing shipments from West African ports. By the end of the week, spot F/H rates in the North Atlantic have increased by $1-1.5k daily and $1-3/t, while transatlantic levels have grown even stronger, by $2k daily and $2-3/t. Thus, rates for shipment of 50,000 t of corn from USG to ARAG ports have increased to $28/t given 10,000x/8,000x l/d rates, while those for transportation of 25-30,000 t of wheat from Upriver to Casablanca have grown to $37-38/t given 7,000x/5,000x l/d rates.

Excitement is growing in the Baltic & Continent , as well as in the Mediterranean and Black Seas . The cargo

traffic has grown slightly due to shipments of European, Russian and transit Ukrainian grain, as well as amid an increase in transportation volumes of fertilizers seen a week ago. This week has also seen an upturn in clinker shipments from EMed. The tonnage list is getting shorter both in South and North Europe. In these circumstances, ship owners continue to push rates up rather aggressively. However, they traditionally try to manipulate the market a lot. «We see plenty of overwhelmingly strong rates reported, but after some checking it appears they are not true and more like owners' ideas only,» an EU broker commented. This week, time-charter rates have risen by another $1-3k daily in both basins, while transportation costs of main cargoes have added $1-4/t. Transportation of about 30,000 t of wheat from Constanta or Varna to SpanMed now costs $20/t given 8,000 sshex bends l/d rates, while rates for shipment of 25-30,000 t of fertilizers from Lower Baltic to Santos have increased to $29-30/t.

The APAC Supramax/Ultramax market is also firming up due to a gradual increase in Indonesian coal exports to China and India, as well as given the lack of vessels open for late September dates. Note that the ballaster inflow from the Indian Ocean has decreased. Time charter rates

The local Handysize market looks much more balanced. Time-charter levels have inched up by just $0.5- 1k daily, with voyage-basis rates up within $0.5-0.75/t. «From Asia, again limited change,» a broker comments.

The Indian Ocean market is showing signs of a slowdown in shipments of South African coal. «Not much cargo coming up to support prompt tonnage in India, mostly for forward laycans like after mid-October,» a broker notes. Meanwhile, the shortage of carriers open off India's east coast persists. «Could tell tonnage supply off WCI is very tight. I did not think of taking tonnage from ECI and ballast to WCI before, until I saw it really happened,» another local broker quips.

Next week, upward trends may continue in the South Atlantic as Brazilian grains are still in good demand due to competitive prices. Expected strengthening of

neighboring Panamax segment also adds to ship owners' confidence. At the same time, the rate growth may slow down further in USG/USEC as the inflow of ballasters from other regions will be a restraining factor. There are no preconditions for market cooling in North Europe, as well as in the Black Sea. However, it will be rather difficult for ship owners to continue pushing rates up considering a noticeable pressure on grain export prices. Note that many players are looking forward to trial calls to Ukrainian Black Sea ports in late September-early October. If Ukraine manages to prevent Russian terrorism in its territorial waters and directly in its ports, the market may get a serious additional impulse in the form of Ukrainian cargoes (and not only grain).

The APAC Supramax/Ultramax market growth is gaining momentum, which is likely to continue next week. Restocking with Indonesian coal in China ahead of the Golden Week may continue to play into hands of ship owners. At the same time, lower demand for South African coal could negatively impact the entire Asian market. «These two weeks will be soft, but then up again,» a broker dealing with coal traffic ex SAfr expects. Handysize owners are pinning expectations on a recovery of steel shipments from the Far East. «Some said that more steels enquiry was now entering the market from the north which saw owners' expectations rise,» a broker echoes.
