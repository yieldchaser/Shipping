## Market Commentary

### Coaster owners still unable to raise freight rates

The start of a new grain season has so far brought only more aggressive ideas from small-tonnage owners who are tired of waiting for rates to rise in the Black and Mediterranean Seas. In fact, however, few charterers are ready to accept higher freight rates. The continuing surplus of open vessels is only playing into charterers' hands. According to ISM estimates, only the number of 4-8k dwt ships has dropped by 10- 15% over the week, while the list of small vessels and mini-bulkers of 8-12k dwt has by contrast increased significantly. Spot freight rates stay largely unchanged since last week, while rates on routes traditionally paid in euros have sagged by €0.5-1/t amid the euro's strengthening against the US dollar. For instance, transportation of 10-11,000 t of corn or soybeans from POC to EgyptMed still costs $17.5-18/t, while rates for shipment of 5-6,000 t of pig iron from Novorossiysk to Marmara are hovering at $16-16.5/t. Only sea-river owners working in the Azov Sea have managed to maintain last week's positive momentum and raise rates by another $0.5-1/t. Thus, shipment of 3,000 t of barley from Rostov/Azov to Marmara now costs $20- 21/t. However, even with a two-week upturn, owners rarely manage to cover the OPEX (often in case of long-haul biz only).

Meanwhile, the North European market continues to weaken. Despite a slight decline in vessel count in the Continent, dull cargo flows allow charterers

to lower freight rates, with the euro's strengthening being another market factor. Over the week, rates have inched down by another €0.5-1/t.

In the near future, ship owners will most likely continue to push rates up even more aggressively in the Black and Mediterranean Seas as the grain season gradually develops. Despite the fact that barley and wheat exports should indeed start perking up sooner or later, the overall situation looks rather complicated due to decent stocks in some importing countries (e.g., Egypt) and falling prices for Ukrainian and European grain. In North Europe, the environment will be challenging for ship owners, and freight rates may sag further as activity will be decreasing in the region together with the approaching peak of the holiday season.
