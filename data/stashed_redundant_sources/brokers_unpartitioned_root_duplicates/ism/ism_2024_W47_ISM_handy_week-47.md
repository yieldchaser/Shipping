## Market Commentary

The Supramax/Ultramax shipping market is weakening all across the Atlantic Ocean. Despite optimistic expectations of ship owners, primarily related to grain season in the USA and Europe, the pace of trade remains low both in ECSA and USG, as well as in the Continent and

Supramax market keeps softening in both hemispheres, while Handy sector sees improvements only in USG and Continent

especially in the Mediterranean Sea. Low export prices for wheat and corn, coupled with weak demand from major buyers (especially Asian ones) are putting strong pressure on freight rates. As a result, transportation costs from ports of above mentioned areas have dropped by $0.5-1.5k daily and $1-2/t on all routes. Thus, rates for shipment of 50,000 t of petcoke from USG to Iskenderun have inched down to $24-25/t, while those for transportation of 50,000 t of corn or soybeans from Santos to S.China have decreased to $38/t.

Situation is no better for Handysize owners working in the Black Sea, as well as in USG. Firm grain offers for shipments from Ukraine, Romania and Bulgaria are very few, while most of non-firm requests enter the market with very low ideas (traders are unable to voice higher rates considering the commodity market picture). An acute shortage of cargoes is a key challenge in the USG as well. “We continue to see vessels finding themselves in a spot

position with very few cargos left from USG for November dates,” a broker commented. Handysize rates have sagged by another $0.5-1k daily and $0.25-2/t in both regions. For instance, transportation of 25-30,000 t of corn or wheat from Constanta to SpanMed now costs $15-15.5/t, while rates for shipment from Ukraine amount to $20/t.

Meanwhile, players note some improvements in ECSA Handysize segment. Corresponding rates have started rising on all routes due to some upturn in grain shipments to China and India in small lots. Thus, Handy freight levels have added $0.5-1k daily and $1-3/t. Demand for Handysize tonnage has grown slightly in the Baltic & Continent as well thanks to brisker shipments of steel scrap, which allowed ship owners to keep rates stable, and even to strengthen positions a bit in some cases.

Despite stabilization of steel and fertilizer exports from the Far East, charterers have again managed to cut Handysize and Supramax rates in the Asia-Pacific region given extremely dull shipments of Indonesian coal and Philippine nickel ore. “SE Asian market is quite cold. There is very limited amount of fresh orders coming out,” a local broker commented to ISM. By the end of the week,

Downward trend continues also in the Indian Ocean. Thus, players note a slight decline in exports of fertilizers and construction cargoes from the PG/WCI area, while shipments of South African manganese ore remain sporadic. Meanwhile, the number of vessels open off the PG and R.Sea has grown slightly. Freight rates have dropped by $0.5-1k daily and $0.5-1.5/t in the region.

Next week, Supramax/Ultramax rates will most likely continue to grow in ECSA, as players so far do not expect any increase in large-tonnage grain exports from Brazil and Argentina for December dates. Meanwhile, the USG market may still see a gradual increase in activity as December approaches. “We wait for the market to regain a sense of balance next week and start moving up,” a broker mentioned. However, this applies more to the Supramax segment, while the USG Handy market is likely to remain soft.

Further rate cuts seem unlikely in the Black and Mediterranean Seas as the market has almost certainly reached its bottom already - such low late-November TCEs have not been seen in the region for a long time. On the other hand, ship owners can hardly expect freights to

rise, at least not until CFR prices for wheat and corn go up in the Mediterranean countries and Asia. The Continent market may witness a further gradual increase in cargo traffic as the holidays approach, but we can hardly project any traditional pre-Christmas rally this year.

In the Eastern Hemi, the expectations on Handysize/ Ultramax market are modest and the majority of players share a common opinion that the market will show yet another week of gradual decline or stability at best all across the board.
