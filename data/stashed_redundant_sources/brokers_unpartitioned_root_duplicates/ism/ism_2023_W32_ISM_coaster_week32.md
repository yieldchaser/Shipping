## Market Commentary

Despite all related difficulties and risks, the cargo traffic from Ukrainian Danube ports remains the most popular on the small-tonnage shipping market. The total list of vessels waiting for passage through Sulina already exceeds 110 units, and given continuing shortage of pilots

Shipments from Danube ports remain the most popular small- tonnage cargo flow all across the short-sea market

and limited capacity of Reni and Izmail ports, many ship owners are sure that waiting time will exceed 7-8 days agreed to be on their account. Corresponding freight rates continue to grow steadily, adding another $6-7/t during the week. Thus, rates for transportation of 5-7,000 t of wheat or corn from Reni or Izmail to EMed have inched up to $56- 58/t bss 1/1 given 7-8 days of waiting time and AWRP (if any) on owner's acct. At the same time, despite ongoing tonnage absorption, there are still plenty of available vessels competing for cargoes from other Black Sea areas, as well as for transportation from the Marmara and Mediterranean Seas, while demand is not growing much. Moreover, fertilizer shipments from eastern Mediterranean ports have slowed down; especially this applies to urea traffic from Egypt. Shipments of steel products and scrap also remain sporadic. ISM sources only note the quite unexpected upturn in shipments of wheat and corn from Turkey. Freight rates for transportation within the

Mediterranean Sea, as well as from Black Sea ports of EU, Russia and Turkey have not changed much over the week. Transportation of 10-12,000 t of wheat from Constanta to Tunisia still costs around $20/t, while shipment of 5-6,000 t of urea from Damietta to SpanMed costs $26/t bss 1/1.

Meanwhile, sea-river owners are raising rates little by little in the Azov Sea due to a further recovery of Russian wheat shipments, as well as amid stricter inspections of vessels passing through Kerch Strait that started last week.

Note that current earnings of owners of 5-12k dwt fleet in the Black and Mediterranean Seas are at OPEX level, being 2-2.5 times lower than in mid-August last year and 3.5-4 times lower than two years ago. TCEs for river- sea vessels of 3-6k dwt are also below levels seen in the previous two years, though not so significantly.

The North European freight market remains depressed. Only grain shipments continue perking up gradually, but they are still insufficient to change the overall market picture. «Basically no fresh cargo on the market, spot vessels all over Europe, some have been

standing still for over a week; steels are slow, not the season for ferts to be moving,» a local broker commented. Ship owners are struggling to keep rates at previous levels. However, even without calls to premium Russian ports, current earnings in the Baltic & Continent are still higher than in the neighboring Mediterranean basin and allow ship owners to earn 20-30% above the OPEX level if the vessel was not idle in dead spot, of course.

The Danube market will certainly continue to play the most important role in shaping the Black Sea sentiment; however, in the short term this cargo flow will hardly absorb enough vessels to allow ship owners raise rates in other regions as well. Moreover, recent news about possible resumption of shipments ex Ukraine's deep-water ports may again allow many grain traders to refocus on shipments of larger lots, which will cool down the Danube market in the long run and most likely will put a cross on the prospects of coaster rate growth in the basin as a whole. The fact is that there are still no other preconditions. The steel market looks bleak so far, while demand for European grain is growing very slowly; Russian wheat is in demand mainly in Asia. Shipments of urea, one of the Mediterranean market's main cargoes, will most likely remain brisk in the coming weeks. «It might even slow down a little bit further as the prices are quite high and importers prefer to wait if they can, so August and beg Sep should still be calm,» fertilizers charterers commented.
