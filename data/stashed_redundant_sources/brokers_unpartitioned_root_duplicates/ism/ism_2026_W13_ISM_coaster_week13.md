## Market Commentary

Turning tide in the coaster market: Azov softens, Black Sea plateaus, pressure builds into April

The small-tonnage shipping market continues to see a fierce standoff between charterers and owners, which results in minimal fixing activity due to a huge bid/offer gap. Owners continue to cite the incredible volatility of bunker prices; however, the number of firm cargo offers is actually decreasing, while tonnage is gradually building up for early April dates. In this situation, owners are unable to push rates further up in the Black Sea. For instance, shipment of 5-6,000 t of corn or soybeans from Ukraine to EgyptMed, Lebanon or Mersin still costs $37-38/t. Meanwhile, Mediterranean freights have still risen by $0.5- 1/t thanks to a slight upturn in shipments of steel products from Turkiye. Thus, rates for transportation of 5-6,000 t of steel products from Nemrut Bay to Tunisia have inched up to $28-28.5/t.

Note that RV TCEs declined by $0.3-0.5k daily in March: despite expectations, the increase in freight rates ultimately failed to offset higher bunker prices. “Same old story - owners try to compensate for voyage costs increase, but eventually they succeed only partially,” an experienced broker commented to ISM.

In the Azov Sea , freight rates have also dropped by $1-2/t despite generally solid Russian grain exports. Waiting times in the Kerch Strait have decreased from 15–20 days to 7–10 days, improving fleet turnover and increasing the regional vessel count. At the moment, shipment of 3,000 t of wheat from Rostov/Azov to Marmara costs $52-53/t.

The tonnage list has inched down in the Baltic Sea once again, although there has been no significant upturn in spot cargo volumes - a situation typically seen ahead of the Easter holidays in Europe. Many shipowners claim that their vessels are still fully booked for shipments of COA cargoes. At the same time, the cargo traffic appears flat in the Continent, but shipowners continue to push freights up across the entire basin due to high bunker prices. Thus, Baltic Sea rates have added another €0.5-1/t this week, while Continent freights have stayed at previous levels.

Next week, activity is almost certain to decline in the Black and Mediterranean Seas, as many traders have been refraining from signing new purchase and sale deals over the past couple of weeks, awaiting stabilization on the fuel oil market. Charterers will likely be able to drive down freight rates amid a growing tonnage list. Improved weather conditions in the Baltic and Azov Seas will also contribute to an increase on the tonnage side and a decline in rates.
