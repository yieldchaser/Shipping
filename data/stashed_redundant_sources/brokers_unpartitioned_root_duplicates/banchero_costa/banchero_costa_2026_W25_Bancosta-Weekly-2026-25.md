# COMMENT MARKET REPORT – WEEK 25/2026

# EUROPEAN UNION'S CRUDE OIL IMPORTS

After a marginal decline in 2024, when global crude oil loadings decreased by -0.2% y-o-y, things picked up in 2025, with full-year volumes increasing by +1.8% y-o-y. In Jan-May 2026, however, global crude oil loadings declined by -5.3% y-o-y to 851.1 mln tonnes, excluding all cabotage trade, according to vessels tracking data from LSEG. Exports from the Arabian Gulf plunged by -30.3% y-o-y to 249.5 mln t in Jan-May 2026, and accounted for 29.3% of global seaborne crude trade, down sharply from the historical norm of about 40%, reflecting the ongoing war in the Persian Gulf. Exports from Russian ports (including oil of Kazakh origin) edged up by +0.4% y-o-y to 95.1 mln t in Jan-May 2026, or 11.2% of global trade. From South America, exports surged by +35.0% y-o-y to 115.5 mln t. From the USA, exports increased by +18.3% y-o-y to 89.1 mln t in Jan-May 2026. From West Africa, exports decreased by -9.3% y-o-y to 66.3 mln t.

From ASEAN exports jumped by +20.3% y-o-y to 52.9 mln t in Jan-May 2026 (this includes trans-shipped Iranian, Russian cargoes). In terms of demand (measured by arrival at discharge ports), the top seaborne importer of crude oil in Jan-May 2026 was Mainland China, accounting for 21.7% of global trade. Volumes into China decreased by -6.6% y-o-y to 187.6 mln t in Jan-May 2026, from 200.8 mln t in Jan-May 2025. Imports into the EU27 increased by +2.5% y-o-y to 191.2 mln t, accounting for 22.2% of global trade. To ASEAN, imports decreased by -8.7% y-o-y to 106.7 mln t. Imports to India declined by -1.2% y-o-y to 99.6 mln t in Jan-May 2026. To South Korea, imports declined by -19.3% y-o-y to 46.7 mln t. To Japan, imports fell by -26.6% y-o-y to 35.7 mln t in Jan-May 2026. Imports into the USA up by +14.1% y-o-y to 53.9 mln t in Jan-May 2026.

**The European Union** is now again the world's second largest seaborne importer of crude oil, after having briefly overtaken China in 2022. Seaborne imports into the European Union (27) declined by -2.8% y-o-y to 460.1 mln t In Jan-Dec 2025, with the EU accounting for 21.6% of global seaborne crude oil imports. In Jan-May 2026, imports increased by +2.5% y-o-y to 191.2 mln t. About 9.4 percent of crude oil volumes discharged in the EU in Jan-May 2026 were carried in VLCCs, about 40.0 percent were carried in Suezmaxes, and about 48.2 percent in Aframaxes. Top ports in the EU in Jan-May 2026 were Rotterdam (42.4 mln tonnes of crude oil), Trieste (16.4 mln t), Gdansk (13.3 mln t), Fos (8.9 mln t), Le Havre (8.0 mln t), Wilhelmshaven (6.1 mln t), Augusta (5.5 mln t), Sarroch (4.7 mln t), Cartagena (4.6 mln t).

In terms of sources of the shipments, there has been understandably a bit of politically driven reshuffling in recent years. Seaborne imports from Russian ports (which also includes oil of non-Russian origin such as Kazakh oil), fell by -12.7% y-o-y in Jan-May 2026 to 21.7 mln t, still well below the 98.4 mln t recorded in the full year Jan-Dec 2022. Novorossiysk is still the single largest origin port for crude oil imports into the EU, accounting for 19.9 mln tonnes in Jan-May 2026. Russian ports have now fallen to the fourth largest source of seaborne oil to the EU, accounting for 11.4% of volumes in Jan-May 2026, behind the North Sea with 20.7%, North Africa (including Sidi Kerir) with 18.5%, the USA with 17.0%. Imports from North Africa (including from Sidi Kerir) decreased by -5.3% y-o-y to 35.4 mln t in Jan-May 2026. Shipments from the North Sea (Norway and UK) were up +12.0% y-o-y to 39.6 mln t in Jan-May 2026.

Imports from the USA surged by +29.0% y-o-y to 32.4 mln t in Jan-May 2026. Volumes from the Arabian Gulf declined further by -33.7% y-o-y to 6.2 mln t in Jan-May 2026. Shipments from West Africa to Europe were down by -9.4% y-o-y to 16.7 mln tonnes in Jan-May 2026.

## **China's coking coal imports set to rise as Shanxi mine disaster hits supply, traders say**

China's imports of coking coal, a steelmaking ingredient, are set to rise further this year after a fatal mine accident reduced domestic supply, traders said. Chinese importers have turned to producers such as Canada and Australia for delivery in June and July after 155 coal mines in the northern Chinese province of Shanxi, its largest coal producer, were shut for safety checks following the accident in late May.

## **China's copper smelters band together to strengthen hand in talks with miners**

China's top copper producers are seeking to add new members to an industry price-setting group, four sources with knowledge of the matter said, in a bid to strengthen their position in negotiations with miners over raw material supply. The China Smelters Purchase Team, a group of 16 major producers that traditionally sets floor prices for spot copper concentrate processing deals, invited at least six prospective new members to attend its quarterly meeting in Yantai in Shandong province on Wednesday, a fifth source said.

## **Iron ore industry sees a bright future, but green steel fades: Russell**

The most persistently bullish part of the commodities sector in recent years has been metals associated with the energy transition, such as copper, lithium and rare earth elements. Iron ore miners want their product to be added to the list. Strong demand growth for steel in South and Southeast Asia will keep demand for the raw material robust, and more than outweigh declining production in China and the Western world.

## **Steel industry *sounds alarm over* slow progress on green steel**

Delays to green steel projects are growing and government support is far short of what is needed, jeopardising the industry's drive to cut emissions, steel associations warned at an annual meeting in Singapore this week. About half of the world's planned green steel projects have already been delayed, while governments have committed just $20 billion of the $1.5 trillion needed to decarbonise the sector, according to the World Steel Association.

## **US fertilizer imports from Hormuz-affected ports fell to zero in May, data show**

U.S. imports of fertilizers from Middle Eastern ports affected by the Strait of Hormuz closure fell to zero in May, contributing to a **44%** year-on-year drop in overall crop nutrient imports, trade data provider Descartes Datamyne said on Thursday, in one of the first estimates of the Iran war's impact on U.S. farmers. The Middle East is a leading fertilizer production hub, and nearly one-third of the global fertilizer trade typically passes through the Strait of Hormuz, which has seen traffic brought to a standstill since the U.S. and Israel attacked Iran in late February.

## **India's rice stocks climb to record high; wheat inventories at five-year peak**

India's rice stocks in government warehouses rose 15% from a year ago to a record high for the start of June, while wheat stocks reached a five-year peak driven by increased farmer procurement, official data showed on Tuesday. Record rice stocks will help the world's biggest exporter increase shipments despite concerns over rainfall due to an El Nino weather pattern, while an improvement in wheat inventories will help the federal government tame any price spikes later this year by increasing open market sales.

## ****India likely won't export sugar for years as El Nino, ethanol squeeze supply****

India, once the world's second-largest sugar exporter, is expected to have little surplus for export for at least three more seasons as El Nino weather conditions threaten cane production and rising ethanol demand squeezes supply. The twin pressures are poised to keep millions of tons of sugar off the world market, tightening supplies for importers across Asia, Africa and the Middle East and supporting benchmark prices in London and New York.

## ****Indian soybean acreage expected to rise on higher prices, weak monsoon outlook****

India's soybean acreage is expected to increase this year as four-year-high prices and forecasts of below-normal monsoon rainfall due to El Nino encourage farmers to shift from water-intensive crops such as sugarcane and corn to the oilseed, farmers and industry officials told Reuters. Soybean is India's main summer-sown oilseed crop and higher output will help the world's biggest importer of edible oils to cap overseas buying of palm oil, soyoil and sunflower oil.

## ****Russian attacks could cut Ukraine grain exports by a third****

A rise in Russian attacks on Ukrainian seaports and vessels could cut monthly grain shipments by as much as a third and have left terminal operators facing mounting losses they say they cannot cover alone, officials and industry executives said. Ukraine, one of the world's major grain exporters, relies heavily on its Black Sea ports, which handle more than 90% of its exports. Source: Reuters / LSEG

# CAPESIZE MARKET

## ATLANTIC AND PACIFIC BASIN

The Capesize freight market endured a turbulent week, marked by a sharp mid-week correction in Atlantic rates before a strong recovery on Friday underpinned by firmer Pacific demand and rising freight derivative (FFA) values. The week opened sluggishly on Monday 15 June, with the Cape T4 index settling at USD 31,339/day. Activity was extremely muted, with limited urgency among charterers to conclude business. Some shipowners reportedly held back tonnage, preferring to monitor the **potential** bunker price implications of a prospective US–Iran peace agreement before committing to freight. The Atlantic basin bore the brunt of the mid-week sell-off. On Tuesday 16 June, the Tubarao–Qingdao route collapsed by USD 3.20/wmt in a single session to USD 31.75/wmt, as sentiment deteriorated sharply and the Cape T4 retreated to USD 29,270/day. Losses extended into Wednesday 17 June, with Tubarao–Qingdao easing a further USD 0.80/wmt to USD 30.95/wmt and the Cape T4 touching a weekly low of USD 28,250/day. A gradual lengthening of ballaster supply for second-half July Brazil laycans added to downward pressure on South Atlantic levels throughout the week.

The Pacific displayed considerably more resilience. The Western Australia–Qingdao route held firm at USD 10.75/wmt on both Monday and Tuesday, with active tonnage demand from Western Australia iron ore miners helping the basin find a floor earlier than the Atlantic. Recovery gathered pace from Thursday 18 June, as shipowners firmed their resistance ahead of a public holiday in China, lifting Western Australia–Qingdao to USD 11.45/wmt and the Cape T4 to USD 29,621/day. On Friday 19 June, Pacific momentum was sustained despite thin liquidity caused by the public holiday in China and Hong Kong, with multiple fixtures concluded by Western Australia mining majors at USD 12.25–12.30/wmt and the Western Australia–Qingdao rate assessed at USD 12.40/wmt on rising FFAs. By the week's close, the Cape T4 settled at USD 30,588/day. In aggregate, Atlantic routes weakened materially — Tubarao–Qingdao shed USD 3.80/wmt on the week to USD 31.15/wmt, and Saldanha Bay–Qingdao lost USD 1.55/wmt to close at USD 22.95/wmt — while the Pacific finished strongly higher, with Western Australia–Qingdao gaining USD 1.65/wmt over the five sessions.

# PANAMAX MARKET

## ATLANTIC BASIN

Atlantic activity remained relatively healthy this week, with ECSA continuing to generate the majority of fresh demand, although signs of softer sentiment began to emerge towards the end of the period. Grain cargoes continued to support fixture activity, though charterers appeared less aggressive than in previous weeks, resulting in a more balanced market. ECSA remained the key driver of Atlantic business. A 2013-built 81,687 dwt Kamsarmax fixed from Gopalpur for an ECSA/Singapore-Japan voyage at USD 22,000 per day, whilst a 2013-built 81,902 dwt vessel secured the same level for an ECSA round voyage. A 2012-built 75,946 dwt vessel fixed from Dahej for an ECSA round voyage, whilst a 2012-built 77,288 dwt Panamax concluded an ECSA/Singapore-Japan grain trip at USD 20,500 per day. Downward pressure on rates became increasingly evident, with fixture levels slipping from the stronger levels recorded earlier in the month. APS ECSA business attracted limited interest. The sole reported fixture involved a 2014-built 81,793 dwt vessel fixed for a grain trip to Skaw-Gibraltar at USD 35,000 per day. As the week progressed, owners faced increasing resistance on rate ideas.

In the North Atlantic, activity remained comparatively stable. A 2015-built 81,061 dwt vessel fixed from Ghent for a trip via the US East Coast with coal, redelivery India, at USD 30,750 per day, whilst a 2010-built 82,181 dwt vessel secured USD 27,000 per day for an NCSA grain voyage to Singapore-Japan. A 2012-built 81,641 dwt vessel fixed from Amsterdam for a US Gulf/Continent iron ore trip at USD 19,000 per day. Overall, Atlantic fundamentals remained supportive, although downward pressure on rates became increasingly evident towards the end of the week.

## PACIFIC BASIN

The Pacific market held broadly steady this week. Cargo volumes were not particularly heavy, though sentiment remained supported by a consistent flow of coal, grain and iron ore business across the basin. Downward pressure on rates became increasingly evident towards the end of the week. In Indonesia, coal demand remained relatively stable, though fixture levels continued to vary by destination and vessel positioning. A 2020-built 81,601 dwt vessel fixed at USD 19,000 for an Indonesia/India coal voyage, whilst a 2019-built 81,185 dwt vessel secured the same level via Indonesia with coal redelivery South China. A 2023-built 82,403 dwt vessel fixed at USD 17,000 for Indonesia/South China, whilst a 2017-built 81,129 dwt vessel concluded at USD 16,000 for an Indonesia voyage with East Coast Australia option, redelivery Singapore-Japan. In the NOPAC, grain cargoes continued to underpin activity despite a lack of significant fresh demand. A 2019-built 82,027 dwt vessel fixed at USD 20,750 for a NOPAC/Singapore-Japan grain trip, whilst a 2016-built 81,895 dwt vessel secured USD 20,000 for similar business.

A 2016-built 77,998 dwt vessel fixed at USD 17,000 for a NOPAC round voyage, whilst a 2018-built 81,824 dwt vessel reportedly achieved a rate in the USD 17,000s for a petcoke trip. Australian sentiment remained the strongest within the Pacific basin, supported by coal and iron ore demand into North Asia and India. A 2026-built 82,022 dwt vessel fixed at USD 24,000 for a North China/Japan iron ore voyage, whilst a 2015-built 79,489 dwt scrubber-fitted vessel secured USD 19,750 for similar business. A 2023-built 82,367 dwt vessel fixed at USD 20,500 for an East Coast Australia/India coal voyage, whilst a 2021-built 82,558 dwt vessel concluded at USD 19,500 for the same route. Australian business continued to provide the strongest support to Pacific earnings, particularly on voyages to India.

## EAST COAST SOUTH AMERICA

**ECSA** rates continued to rise, as evidenced across recent weeks for both Handysize and larger vessels. ## NORTH EUROPE / CONTINENT

The market held broadly in line with the previous week. # SUPRAMAX & HANDYSIZE MARKET

## MEG / INDIAN OCEAN

The Indian Ocean freight market for Ultramax and Supramax tonnage was broadly stable to slightly softer over the week, with activity described as subdued across both the Persian Gulf and South Africa loading areas. Fixing information was scarce throughout, with owners reluctant to concede on rates despite a steady build-up in available tonnage. Time-charter equivalent levels are understood to have eased by approx. USD 1,000–2,000/day week on week. Out of the Persian Gulf, enquiries were limited throughout. Limestone freight from Mina Saqr to Paradip ranged between USD 20.80 and USD 21.20/mt over the week, closing on Friday at USD 20.80/mt. Sentiment had firmed briefly mid-week following reports of a US-Iran peace agreement, which raised market expectations of increased cargo demand from the region; however, no fresh fixtures materialised. Out of South Africa, healthy demand from Richards Bay provided a degree of support to Indian Ocean rates. Coal freight to Port Qasim ranged between USD 21.10 and USD 21.70/mt, settling at USD 21.65/mt on Friday. A Bangladesh-bound coal cargo on late-July laydays was reported in the market towards week-end. No specific Handysize Indian Ocean assessments were available from the sources reviewed for this period.
