# COMMODITY NEWS – DRY BULK

## **China's Weak Steel Output and Strong Iron Ore Imports Signal Structural Shift**

China's steel production in April fell 2.8% year-on-year to 86.63 million metric tons — the weakest April figure since 2018 — with output for the first four months of 2026 down 4.1% to 331.12 million tons. Yet iron ore imports rose 8% in the same period to 418.6 million tons, with April imports at 103.9 million tons. The growing divergence is being described by analysts as a structural shift rather than a temporary dislocation. Port stockpiles monitored by SteelHome stood near record highs at 160.35 million tons as of 22 May. Analysts attribute the sustained import appetite to a gradual decline in China's domestic iron ore output, weakening ore grades, and precautionary buying by steel mills and traders concerned about future supply disruptions linked to the Hormuz closure. The front-month Singapore iron ore contract has been anchored in a narrow band around $105-$109/t for the past ten months.

## **Russia's Seaborne Grain Exports Double in April**

Russia's seaborne grain exports reached 4.9 million metric tons in April, up 100.1% year-on-year, with total season-to-date exports reaching 46 million tons, up 7.4% from the prior year. Exports via Black Sea terminals jumped 89% to 4.1 million tons. Baltic Sea shipments, serving new African and Latin American markets, rose 127.8% year-on-year. Caspian Sea exports to Iran held broadly steady at nearly 0.3 million tons, while Far East terminal shipments rose 21.5%.

## **Russian Wheat Export Prices Rise as Rouble Strengthens and Farmers Hold Back**

Russian wheat export prices climbed further this week, with 12.5% protein content wheat rising $5 to $245/t FOB for June–July delivery, supported by a strengthening rouble, limited domestic supply, and farmers holding back stocks in expectation of still-higher prices. New-crop wheat for July delivery was quoted at $242/t. IKAR estimates May wheat exports at 3.0–3.3 million tons, while Sovecon revised its forecast slightly downward to 2.8 million tons. A potential Turkish import ban — given Turkey's good domestic harvest prospects — is being watched as a key bearish risk for Black Sea prices.

## **India Cancels Soymeal Export Contracts and Starts Importing African Soybeans**

Indian traders cancelled 25,000 metric tons of soymeal export contracts for the first time since 2021 after domestic prices surged, pushing Indian soymeal offers to around $695/t FOB — up from $475/t a month ago — far above South American competition. India simultaneously booked 80,000 tons of soybean imports from African nations including Benin, Niger, Togo and Nigeria at $700–$760/t CIF for June–July shipment, with full-year imports potentially reaching a record 800,000 tons. The reversal in trade flows is expected to redirect Asian demand to South American soymeal suppliers.

## **EU Grain Crop Forecasts Cut; USDA Rates US Wheat at Record-Low Condition**

The European Commission cut its 2026/27 soft wheat forecast to 126.9 million metric tons from 127.3 million — down sharply from 135 million tons harvested last season — and also trimmed barley and maize forecasts. Separately, USDA rated only 26% of the US winter wheat crop in good-to-excellent condition, the lowest on record for this time of year since 1986, with approximately 70% of the crop in drought-affected areas. Corn planting was 86% complete and soybean planting 79% done, both trailing analyst expectations.

## **India Rice Exports Decline as Hormuz Disruption Blocks Gulf Markets**

India's rice exports in January–April 2026 fell 1.3% year-on-year to 8.39 million metric tons, with premium basmati exports declining 7% to 2.3 million tons as shipments to Iran, Iraq, Qatar and Saudi Arabia remain delayed. New deals are stalling as shipping insurance and freight costs have risen since the conflict began. India accounts for over 40% of global rice exports, and the disruption is exerting downward pressure on Indian domestic rice prices, already down more than 5% this year.

## **Brazil to Import More Wheat as Argentina Quality Issues Drive Diversification**

Brazil's wheat imports are forecast to grow to 7–8 million metric tons in 2026/27 as higher costs linked to the Iran war reduce Brazil's own crop area and quality issues in Argentina push mills to diversify sourcing. Brazil expects to import 1.0–1.5 million tons from non-Mercosur origins — primarily the US and Russia — at higher cost. Argentina's latest crop, while large at 28 million tons, includes significant volumes with insufficient protein for bread flour. Ukraine's grain rail deliveries to Black Sea ports rose 2.1% through 26 May, with 2.59 million tons exported in the first 25 days of the month. Source: Reuters / LSEG

# COMMODITY NEWS – CRUDE OIL & PRODUCTS

## **India Turns to Latin America and Africa as Middle East Supply Dries Up**

Indian refiners sharply redirected crude import flows after Hormuz disruptions severed access to most Gulf supplies. Imports from Venezuela, Brazil, Angola and Nigeria surged to fill the gap, while Iraq deliveries were skipped entirely in April. India's total crude imports held at 4.57 million bpd in April — down 15.5% from a year earlier — with the Middle East's share recovering to 45.2% via UAE and Saudi Arabia pipelines that bypass Hormuz. Russia remained India's top supplier at approximately 1.9 million bpd in May, with Venezuela rising to become the fourth-largest supplier.

## **Three Tankers Exit Hormuz with Transponders Off, Bound for India and China**

Two VLCCs and one LNG tanker exited the Strait of Hormuz with their AIS transponders switched off — masking their movements. The VLCC Eagle Veracruz, carrying 2 million barrels of Saudi crude, is headed for Sinochem's Quanzhou refinery (arrival ~16 June). The VLCC Nissos Keros, with 1.8 million barrels of UAE Das crude, is bound for Hindustan Petroleum's Visakhapatnam refinery (arrival ~3 June). COSCO-operated Hua Lin Wan left with Kuwaiti naphtha for Huizhou, China. The movements signal continued cautious efforts to resume tanker traffic through the strait.

## **US SPR Cargo Heads to Philippines — First Asian Shipment Since 2022**

A cargo of crude oil from the US Strategic Petroleum Reserve is heading to the Philippines, the first SPR shipment to Asia since November 2022, as the US works to redistribute emergency stockpiles to help the region cope with the near-closure of the Strait of Hormuz. Asia receives approximately 80% of its oil through the strait. The IEA's coordinated 400 million barrel reserve release is flowing at 2.5–3 million bpd and is expected to be exhausted by early August.

## **US Crude and Products Draw Down Sharply; Distillates at 23-Year Low**

US crude inventories fell 3.3 million barrels in the week ended 22 May, while gasoline stocks fell 2.6 million barrels and distillate stockpiles dropped 2.1 million barrels to their lowest level since May 2003. The SPR fell to its lowest point since April 2024. Refinery utilisation rose 2.9 percentage points to 94.5% as the summer driving season began, with total product supplied rising to 20.94 million bpd. Brent ended at $94.29/bbl on 28 May, off wartime highs but still 30% above pre-conflict levels.

## **Brunei and Oman Cash In as Non-Hormuz Exporters**

Small energy producers outside the Strait of Hormuz are reaping windfall gains. Brunei's crude exports hit 2.74 million barrels in April, up 51% year-on-year, while refined product shipments are expected at a multi-month high of 4.16 million barrels in May. Oman is forecast to ship a record 17.39 million barrels of refined products in May. Singapore jet fuel ended at $139.18/bbl — 49% above pre-war levels — and gasoil was up 52%, with both middle distillates outpacing the 30% rise in Brent. which averaged $4.49/gallon nationally on 27 May, versus under $3 before the war. High shipping rates and small fuel volumes transported have blunted the waiver's effect. California prices averaged $6.11/gallon. The White House said it was open to future extensions while critics noted the measure was failing to deliver meaningful relief at the pump.

## **IEA: Oil Investment Falls for Third Straight Year as Energy Capital Shifts**

Upstream oil investment is declining for a third consecutive year to below $500 billion in 2026, even as the Iran war has removed over 14 million bpd from global markets, the IEA said in its World Energy Investment 2026 report. Middle East oil and gas investment is expected to fall 1% due to damage, lower revenues and production stoppages across the region. Total energy sector capital flows will grow 5% to $3.4 trillion, but the vast majority is directed to renewables, gas and storage rather than oil. Coal investment will reach a 14-year high of $180 billion, driven by China and India. The structural underinvestment in oil supply raises concerns about the market's recovery capacity once the Strait of Hormuz reopens, particularly given that the IEA has warned of a supply red zone in July–August as strategic reserves near exhaustion. Source: Reuters / LSEG

# COMMODITY NEWS – LNG & NATURAL GAS

## **QatarEnergy Extends Force Majeure to Mid-August; Total 17 Cargoes Cancelled**

QatarEnergy notified Italian utility Edison of five additional cancelled LNG deliveries, extending its force majeure from early July to mid-August. In total, 17 cargoes to Italy's Adriatic LNG terminal are under force majeure, representing approximately 2.2 billion cubic metres of gas — around one-third of Italy's annual consumption. Edison, a unit of EDF, said it had replaced nine of the 17 cancelled cargoes by 25 March, primarily with US supply. The disruption halved Edison's first-quarter operating profit and prompted a cut to full-year guidance.

## **Australia's Draft LNG Domestic Reservation Rule Draws Industry Fire**

The Australian government released draft rules for its 20% Domestic Supply Obligation (DSO), requiring LNG exporters to reserve a fifth of any future contracted gas for the domestic market from July 2027. Industry reaction was strongly negative: Shell Australia warned the policy could cause oversupply, depress prices and deter investment in new exploration. The Santos-operated Gladstone LNG facility was identified as most exposed. Western Australia — home to Woodside and Chevron's major export facilities — is seeking confirmation its own 15% reservation policy will be compatible with the national scheme.

## **US and Thailand Accelerate LNG Supply Talks Post-Qatar Disruption**

The US and Thailand are in advanced talks on a long-term LNG supply deal between Venture Global and Thai state energy company PTT, negotiations that accelerated after Iranian strikes destroyed 12.8 million metric tons per annum — around 17% — of Qatar's LNG export capacity, with repairs expected to take three to five years. Thailand is Southeast Asia's biggest LNG importer and is actively displacing coal in its power mix. The two countries had agreed in October 2025 that Thai companies would purchase approximately $5.4 billion per year in US energy products including LNG.

## **Russia's First Arctic LNG Tanker of 2026 Departs Eastward Ahead of Schedule**

The ice-class LNG carrier Christophe de Margerie departed a Russian Arctic LNG 2 outlet and set sail east along the Northern Sea Route towards Asia — the first such transit of 2026, departing earlier than last year's first sailing in late June. Russia is promoting the Northern Sea Route as an alternative to the Suez Canal amid heightened Middle East security risks. The route can significantly cut transit times between Europe and Asia. Arctic LNG 2 is under US sanctions in connection with the Ukraine war, adding a layer of complexity to the shipment.

## **IEA: Global Natural Gas Investment to Hit 10-Year High of $330 Billion**

Global investment in natural gas projects is set to rise more than 10% in 2026 to $330 billion — the highest in ten years — as the Iran war accelerates diversification away from the Middle East, the IEA said in its World Energy Investment 2026 report. Oil investment is declining for a third consecutive year to below $500 billion. Coal investment will reach a 14-year high of $180 billion, driven by China and India. The gas investment surge is concentrated in LNG projects, reflecting the structural demand vacuum created by the Qatar outage and ongoing Hormuz disruption.

## **Asia Early Heat Wave to Drive Summer Coal and Gas Demand**

## **Surge**

Temperatures across major Asian cities are running well above seasonal norms weeks ahead of the official meteorological summer, with Seoul recording readings 13% above the long-term average since mid-May. Widespread air conditioning use is straining power networks across China, Japan, India, South Korea and Southeast Asia, with Vietnam, the Philippines and India all issuing power output warnings. Utilities are expected to step up coal- and gas-fired generation significantly, drawing down inventories that will need replenishment — adding a further bullish impulse to energy markets already disrupted by the Iran war.

## **Cheniere Accelerates Corpus Christi Expansion; Alaska LNG Project Gains Traction**

US LNG giant Cheniere Energy said Asian customers have significantly stepped up requests for additional LNG volumes since the Iran war disrupted Qatar's export capacity, and the company is accelerating completion of two new liquefaction trains at its Corpus Christi, Texas facility. Cheniere is the largest US LNG exporter. Separately, Thailand's PTT last year signed a 20-year agreement to procure 2 million metric tons per annum from Glenfarne's proposed Alaska LNG project, though no final investment decision has been made on a scheme requiring an 800-mile pipeline from Alaska's North Slope to tidewater. The combination of accelerating US capacity additions and new long-term Asian contracting interest — including the ongoing Venture Global–PTT talks — underscores how the Qatar outage is permanently reshaping long-term LNG supply agreements. Source: Reuters / LSEG

# CAPESIZE MARKET

## ATLANTIC AND PACIFIC BASIN

The Capesize segment posted a firm rally during the week ending 29 May 2026, with sentiment underpinned by a resurgent Pacific basin, weather-related disruptions in North China, and a belated catch-up in the Atlantic. The benchmark Cape T4 weighted average time charter equivalent climbed from USD 39,614/day at the close of 22 May to USD 45,176/day on 28 May, a gain of USD 5,562/day across the trading week. Strength was led by the headline C5 route (Western Australia–Qingdao), assessed at USD 16.70/wmt on 28 May, up USD 1.00/wmt week-on-week, while the C3 Tubarao–Qingdao route firmed to USD 37.70/wmt, up USD 1.40/wmt. The Saldanha Bay–Qingdao route advanced to USD 28.20/wmt, up USD 0.95/wmt. The Pacific drove early gains. A trio of Western Australian iron ore majors persistently sought prompt tonnage out of Dampier and Port Hedland, while several South Korean coal and iron ore tenders augmented the cargo book. Fixing intensified on 26 May, with multiple Capesize and Newcastlemax stems concluded in the USD 15.90–16.15/wmt range for early-June laycans, before fixtures pushed up to USD 16.50–16.75/wmt on 28 May for mid-June dates.

Persistent strong winds and fog at North China ports slowed discharging operations and worsened congestion, tightening prompt tonnage availability and reinforcing the rally. The Atlantic initially lagged owing to public holidays in the United Kingdom, the United States and Geneva, which thinned exchanges and left South Atlantic activity muted. Shipowners held firm on offer ideas while charterers refrained from refreshing bids. Momentum turned on 27 May, with several Tubarao–Qingdao Capesize fixtures concluded around mid-USD 37s/wmt, alongside a fronthaul stem from Ponta da Madeira to Taranto at USD 22.35/wmt for 15–24 June laycan. Bid-offer spreads remained wide into the week's close — indicated at USD 36.75 versus USD 38.50/wmt for end-June laycans — pointing to further upside potential. Freight derivative rates also strengthened, reinforcing positive market sentiment.

# SUPRAMAX & HANDYSIZE MARKET

## US GULF / NORTH AMERICA

**US GULF / NORTH AMERICA** Rates in East Coast South America (ECSAm) were slightly softer on Handysize tonnage, while larger units remained relatively stable despite slower momentum on fronthaul business. On Handysize, transatlantic rates from ECSAm to East Mediterranean were assessed at around USD 19,000 per day arrival pilot station basis on standard Handysize tonnage, with trips to Spain assessed in the mid-to-high teens per day arrival pilot station basis.
