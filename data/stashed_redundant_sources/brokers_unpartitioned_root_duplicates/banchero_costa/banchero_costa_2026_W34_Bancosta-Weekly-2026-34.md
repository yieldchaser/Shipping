# COMMENT MARKET REPORT – WEEK 34/2026

# AUSTRALIAN IRON ORE EXPORTS

2025 was another positive year for global seaborne iron ore trade. In Jan-Dec 2025, global loadings of iron ore increased by +3.5% y-o-y to 1,732.0 mln tonnes, based on AXS Marine vessel tracking data. 2026 has continued on a positive note, with export volumes up by +1.8% y-o-y in Jan-Jul 2026 to 978.3 mln tonnes. Exports from Australia increased in Jan-Jul 2026 by +2.3% y-o-y to 547.8 mln tonnes. From Brazil, exports increased by +1.2% y-o-y in Jan-Jul 2026 to 216.7 mln t. From Canada there was a -11.5% y-o-y decline to 30.2 mln tonnes. From South Africa volumes increased +2.8% y-o-y to 31.8 mln t. India saw an increase of +7.1% y-o-y in Jan-Jul 2026 to 17.2 mln t. From Norway exports increased by +2.7% y-o-y to 12.4 mln t. Demand is still driven primarily by Mainland China, which accounts for 75% of imports. Iron ore imports into China increased by +2.4% y-o-y in Jan-Jul 2026 to 729.2 mln tonnes.

Imports into Japan declined by -2.7% y-o-y to 52.5 mln t. To the EU, imports were essentially flat at 41.6 mln t. Volumes into South Korea were stable, -0.1% y-o-y to 41.3 mln t. Imports into Malaysia increased by +4.5% y-o-y to 15.5 mln tonnes. To Vietnam volumes were up by +15.3% y-o-y to 15.5 mln t. To Oman, volumes were down -28.7% y-o-y to 5.7 mln t, to Saudi Arabia down by -61.5% y-o-y to 2.5 mln t, to Bahrain -84.1% y-o-y to 1.3 mln t. **Australia** is by far the world's largest exporter of iron ore, with a 56.0% market share in Jan-Jul 2026, well ahead of Brazil's 22.1%, South Africa's 3.2%, and Canada's 3.1%. Export volumes from Australia have been relatively stable in recent years, with moderate increases. In 2020, iron ore exports from Australia surged by +4.1% y-o-y to 903.0 mln tonnes. This was followed by a -0.3% y-o-y decline in 2021 to 900.0 mln t, then a +0.8% y-o-y increase in 2022 to 907.2 mln t, a +1.4% y-o-y increase in 2023 to 919.8 mln t, a +1.1% y-o-y increase in 2024 to 929.8 mln t, and a +2.5% y-o-y increase in 2025 to 953.2 mln t.

In Jan-Jul 2026, shipments from Australia increased by +2.3% y-o-y to 547.8 mln t, from 535.4 mln t in the same period of 2025. The main iron ore export terminals in Australia are: Port Hedland (324.1 mln t loaded in Jan-Jul 2026), Port Walcott (95.1 mln t), Dampier (83.8 mln t), Ashburton (18.8 mln t), Geraldton (7.9 mln t), Cape Preston (5.7 mln t), Whyalla (4.1 mln t), Esperance (3.1 mln t), Koolan Island (1.5 mln t). The vast majority (79.9% in Jan-Jul 2026) of iron ore volumes from Australia are loaded on Capesize and Newcastlemax vessels (i.e. vessels in the 120-220k dwt range), with 14.3% on VLOCs, 3.8% on Post-Panamaxes, and 1.2% on Kamsarmaxes. Mainland China is by far the top iron ore importer from Australia, with a 84.0% share of shipments from Australian ports in Jan-Jul 2026. Exports to Mainland China from Australia increased by +1.1% y-o-y to 460.1 mln t in Jan-Jul 2026, from 455.0 mln tonnes in Jan-Jul 2025.

The second largest destination for Australian ore is now Japan, with a 5.6% share in 2026. Shipments from Australia to Japan increased by +5.3% y-o-y to 30.7 mln t in Jan-Jul 2026, from 29.1 mln t in Jan-Jul 2025. Exports to South Korea increased by +4.3% y-o-y to 30.6 mln t in Jan-Jul 2026. Volumes from Australia to Taiwan increased by +14.4% y-o-y to 8.5 mln tonnes in Jan-Jul 2026. To Vietnam volumes of Australian iron ore increased by +5.1% y-o-y to 7.9 mln tonnes in Jan-Jul 2026.

# COMMODITY NEWS – DRY BULK

## **China's easing steel output reflects divergent demand drivers**

The drop in China's steel output to the lowest this year in July fits the narrative that the world's second-biggest economy is struggling for growth momentum. But as usual the devil is in the details, with steel demand being uneven across several sectors of the economy. Steel output in the world's largest producer dropped to 76.93 mln t in July, down 3.6% from the same month in 2025 and the weakest July since 2017.

## **Rusal derailment in Guinea halts alumina exports, sources say**

A train operated by Russian aluminium producer Rusal derailed in Guinea's Dubreka prefecture over the weekend, cutting rail access to its Friguia alumina refinery, halting alumina exports and slowing production, three sources told Reuters. The train carrying alumina came off the tracks near Kagbelen on Saturday evening, injuring four people and forcing the suspension of rail traffic, company officials said.

## **China eases Iran war aluminium shock, but at a cost**

China has emerged as a significant swing supplier of aluminium to a Western market that is reeling from the loss of production in the Gulf due to the Iran war. The world's top aluminium producer has lifted exports of primary metal, alloy and semi-manufactured products (semis), helping cushion the impact on the global supply chain.

## **Aluminium giant Rusal swings to first-half profit on higher commodity prices**

Russian aluminium giant Rusal swung to a first-half adjusted net profit, driven by higher aluminium prices linked to heightened global volatility. The world's largest aluminium producer outside China, posted adjusted net profit of $196 million for the six-month period ended June 30, a sharp turnaround from a loss of $194 mln/year earlier.

## ****Greater regulatory scrutiny no bar to mining mergers, bosses say****

Mining bosses say that regulatory scrutiny of major mergers is increasing as governments pay closer attention to critical minerals and security of supply in the face of a volatile geopolitical backdrop, but they do not see the shift as a fundamental barrier to dealmaking. Executives at Glencore, Anglo American and Rio Tinto said after half-year results in July and August that antitrust reviews and national interest were becoming more prominent factors when assessing potential transactions, particularly where copper and other critical minerals are involved.

## **Global wheat buyers brace for supply squeeze amid Black Sea attacks**

Global wheat importers are bracing for tighter supplies as attacks on Black Sea grain infrastructure disrupt shipments, heightening food security risks for top buyers such as Egypt and Indonesia, while driving up prices. Benchmark Chicago futures have climbed more than 17% since the start of July, fuelled largely by a shortfall in Black Sea supplies, with physical prices making strong gains in rival exporters Argentina, Australia and the United States.

## **Russia and Ukraine's Black Sea grain exports grind to a virtual halt**

Attacks on shipping have shut down more than 97% of Russia and Ukraine's grain export capacity in the Azov and Black Sea basin, cutting off a major source of low-cost supplies and helping to drive up global prices. The two major grain exporters have intensified attacks on ports and shipping over the past month, leaving importers in the Middle East, Africa and Asia facing the prospect of sourcing grain from higher-cost suppliers such as Australia and the United States.

## ****Russian wheat export prices fall as shipments expected to reach 16-year low****

Russian wheat export prices fell last week, but some analysts said they regarded the prices as largely symbolic as shipments from southern ports have been virtually halted due to the escalating conflict with Ukraine. Export prices in the Baltic region — where the bulk of shipments are most likely to be redirected due to its ability to handle them — were significantly higher than in Novorossiysk, but changed little over the week.

## ****India weighs limited duty-free sugar imports to rein in record prices****

India is considering measures to bolster sugar supplies and rein in record prices, such as limited duty-free imports and curbs on the amount of stocks held by bulk traders, potentially allowing in overseas shipments for the first time in nearly a decade. Imports by India, the world's biggest sugar consumer, could support benchmark prices in London and New York, while helping New Delhi rein in domestic prices during the August to November festival season when demand rises.

## ****Ukraine wheat exports could slump to 5-10 million tons due to blocked ports, consultancy ASAP says****

Ukraine's wheat exports could decline to between 5 million and 10 million metric tons in the 2026/27 July-to-June season if its Black Sea ports remain blocked by Russian attacks, ASAP Agri consultancy said. Ukrainian seaports in the Odesa port hub effectively ceased operations at the end of July due to Russian drone and missile attacks on terminals and vessels. Source: Reuters / LSEG

# COMMODITY NEWS – CRUDE OIL & GAS

## **Iran threatens 45 tankers with fines, confiscation in Hormuz escalation**

Iran said it had blacklisted 45 tankers that had broken its rules for crossing the Strait of Hormuz, and would take action against any vessels transferring loads with them, escalating its threats over the key waterway six months into the war. The named vessels could be fined, detained and have their cargoes confiscated, according to an X post late on Sunday from the Persian Gulf Strait Authority, a new body set up by Iran to manage the strait.

## **China boosts imports of Russian crude, stymieing India's refiners**

One of the balancing acts that has prevented crude oil prices from spiking higher during the Iran war has been India buying record volumes of Russian crude. But it's getting more challenging for the world's third-biggest crude importer as top-ranked China resumes buying more from Russia amid the ongoing supply disruptions sparked by the conflict.

## **Iran grants permission for a number of Iraqi oil tankers to pass through Hormuz**

Iran has granted permission for a number of Iraqi oil tankers to pass through the Strait of Hormuz following repeated requests from Baghdad through various channels, Iran's state news agency IRNA reported on Saturday. IRNA said obtaining special permission for Iraqi tankers was one of Baghdad's main requests during Iranian parliament speaker Mohammad Baqer Qalibaf's visit to Iraq.

## **US refiners face historic stress test as global crisis looms**

U.S. refineries have operated near maximum capacity for the longest sustained period in over a quarter century, scrambling to capture windfall profits created by the Iran war. But history offers a cautionary tale: "Super refining" runs that push plants too hard can cause major failures. U.S. refineries' ability to sustain today's elevated operating rates for months, or potentially years, could mean the difference between an exceptionally tight global fuel market and a full-blown supply crisis marked by acute shortages, demand destruction and widespread economic pain.

## **The Hormuz crude volume debate masks the real shortage of refined fuels**

The crude oil market is currently engaged in the wrong debate over how much oil is actually moving via the Strait of Hormuz. It should be debating the constrained flows of refined products around Asia. U.S. Energy Secretary Chris Wright has repeatedly claimed that much more crude is getting through the disputed strait than vessel-tracking analysts such as Kpler can see.

## **Canada's pipeline ambitions hinge on uncertain output expansion**

Canadian pipeline firms are proposing billions of dollars in new projects despite oil sands companies being reluctant to commit to significant production expansions amid ongoing uncertainty around climate policies and long-term global demand. At least six different pipeline projects are underway or proposed in Canada, to move oil to the United States or to export markets on the Pacific coast.

## **Norway will drill in Arctic regardless of EU's position, says energy minister**

Norway will continue developing its oil and gas resources in the Barents Sea regardless of the European Union's support for a moratorium on Arctic hydrocarbon supplies, and no longer sees itself as Europe's "green battery", Energy Minister Terje Aasland told Reuters. Following Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine, Norway has become Europe's largest supplier of natural gas, meeting around 30% of gas demand of both the European Union and Britain.

## **US crude and gasoline inventories rise, distillate stocks fall, EIA says**

U.S. crude and gasoline inventories rose while distillate stockpiles fell last week, the Energy Information Administration said on Wednesday, while refiners cranked up utilization to capture higher fuel prices. Crude inventories rose by 4.4 million barrels to 428.8 million barrels in the week ended August 14, the EIA said. Crude stocks at the Cushing, Oklahoma, delivery hub fell by 1.3 million barrels in the week, the EIA said.

## **US to end summer blend gasoline requirement early in attempt to lower prices**

President Donald Trump's administration will end summer-blend gasoline requirements about two weeks early this year in an effort to boost the U.S. fuel supply and lower gas prices, the Environmental Protection Agency said on Thursday. The EPA said it would allow the sale of 10% ethanol, or E10, gasoline with a higher Reid Vapor Pressure (RVP) beginning September 1, about two weeks earlier than normal.

## **The Iran war energy crisis is just getting started**

The Iran war has pushed the global oil refining industry to the brink, signalling that diesel and gasoline prices may remain elevated for years. Deal or no deal, the global energy inflation shock is far from over. While oil markets adapted remarkably well to the abrupt loss of a fifth of global crude supplies from the Middle East during the conflict, workarounds for the refining industry have been far more limited. # CAPESIZE MARKET

## ATLANTIC AND PACIFIC BASIN

The Capesize market drifted lower through the first three sessions before rebounding firmly late in the week, closing modestly higher on the Pacific and Brazilian routes. Trading action was lethargic throughout, with persistent bid-offer gaps stifling fixture conclusion despite healthy cargo volumes in both basins. The Western Australia–Qingdao route (170,000 mt iron ore, plus/minus 10%) eased 20 cents/wmt to USD 13.95/wmt on 17 August, charterers remaining cautious over ship itineraries in the aftermath of Typhoon Dolphin even as three Western Australian miners sought tonnage. Losses extended by 45 cents/wmt to USD 13.50/wmt on 18 August, a mining major fixing Dampier–Qingdao at about USD 13.40/wmt, and by a further 40 cents/wmt to USD 13.10/wmt on 19 August as freight derivative (FFA) values swung either side of flat. Owners then resisted lower ideas, holding offers around high-USD 13/wmt, and the route rebounded 55 cents/wmt to USD 13.65/wmt on 20 August. It added 80 cents/wmt to close at USD 14.45/wmt on 21 August, up 30 cents/wmt on the week, as charterers showed greater urgency ahead of Typhoon Saudel's anticipated impact on Far East itineraries; a mining major took several Capesizes from Dampier to Qingdao for 5–7 September laycan in the low USD 14s/wmt.

Tubarao–Qingdao was unchanged at USD 35.80/wmt for three consecutive sessions amid a lengthening South Atlantic cargo list and minimal firm exchange, owners reportedly favouring Pacific employment despite a premium on South Atlantic round voyages. The route slipped 10 cents/wmt on 20 August before firming 25 cents/wmt to USD 35.95/wmt, up 15 cents/wmt on the week. Brazilian fixtures were reported at high-USD 35/wmt for 13 September onward and mid-USD 35/wmt for 12 September onward, while a North Atlantic fronthaul from Sept-Îles to Qingdao was heard at USD 43.25/wmt for 11–20 September laycan. Saldanha Bay–Qingdao lost 30 cents/wmt to USD 26.05/wmt on 17 August, held there midweek, dipped to USD 25.95/wmt and closed at USD 26.15/wmt, down 20 cents/wmt. A Newcastlemax was fixed Saldanha Bay–Qingdao at around mid-USD 25/wmt for 15–20 September laycan.

# PANAMAX MARKET

## ATLANTIC BASIN

Panamax rates in the Atlantic Basin softened through the week. North Atlantic faced the sharpest pressure from limited cargo enquiry and rising vessel availability, as ample tonnage weighed on both basins. US Gulf and US East Coast Panamax and Kamsarmax freights remained under sustained pressure. Owners increasingly chased bids and fixed below previous levels amid weak demand across routes. While broader dry-bulk markets drew support from geopolitical disruptions and higher tonne-miles elsewhere, the Atlantic segment lacked fresh stimulus. Limited grain and mineral fixtures kept sentiment soft, with owners competing for scarce employment. Overall, the week closed on a weaker note for Atlantic Panamax, contrasting the firmer tone seen in the Pacific.

## PACIFIC BASIN

The Pacific market maintained a steady pace this week, with consistent activity across the main loading regions of Indonesia, NOPAC and Australia. Rate levels remained broadly stable, with earnings continuing to vary according to vessel positioning, cargo and destination. Indonesia saw steady coal activity throughout the week, with rates showing a relatively wide spread. 74,632 dwt '13-built fixed at $16,000 for South China, while a 75k 2013-built achieved $14,100 for Singapore-Japan. Later in the week, the 75k '17-built unit reached $17,500 for South Korea, while 75,476 dwt 2014-built fixed at $14,500 for Singapore-Japan. At the lower end, a 76k '02-built fixed at $13,000 and 75k '13-built at $13,500, both for South China. Overall, Indonesian earnings remained mixed, with no clear directional trend, while vessel age and destination continued to account for much of the spread. NOPAC grain activity remained consistent, with early fixtures around the mid-$15,000s. 75,611 dwt 2004-built fixed at $15,500 for Singapore-Japan, while an 81k '11-built achieved $16,100 for the same destination.

Later in the week, 81,031 dwt 2016-built fixed a trip via NOPAC at $18,850, while the 82k '12-built unit achieved $17,500 for Singapore-Japan. Overall, the market remained stable, with the higher levels reflecting differences in vessel position and trip duration rather than a clear upward move. Australia remained active across both coal and grain business, with rates broadly holding around the high-$18,000s to $20,000. An 82k '18-built fixed at $18,000 for South China, followed by 82,177 dwt 2013-built at $19,000 for India. The 81,791 dwt 2020-built scrubber-fitted achieved $20,000 for South China, while an 81k '20-built fixed at $19,000 for Singapore-Japan and 77,888 dwt 2014-built at $18,000 for South China. Overall, Australian rates remained firm but essentially steady, with the scrubber-fitted unit achieving the upper end of the range.

# SUPRAMAX & HANDYSIZE MARKET

## MEG / INDIAN OCEAN

Indian Ocean Supramax and Ultramax sentiment stayed subdued over 17–21 August, limited cargo availability and ample prompt tonnage capping rates, though the east coast of India drew support from a firming Southeast Asian market later in the week. Outbound iron ore activity from the Indian coasts remained scarce and fresh orders thin. Out of the Persian Gulf, the limestone trade edged higher despite muted fixing information throughout. Freight to move 50,000 mt of limestone from Mina Saqr to Paradip on the east coast of India dipped to USD 17.20/mt on 17 August before recovering to USD 17.30/mt on 18 August and USD 17.35/mt on 19–20 August, closing at USD 17.40/mt, up 15 cents/mt on the week. South Africa opened under downward pressure but steadied. Freight to carry 50,000 mt of coal from Richards Bay Coal Terminal to Port Qasim eased to USD 20.40/mt on 17 August, then firmed to USD 20.60/mt, USD 20.65/mt and USD 20.75/mt by 20 August, where it closed, up 15 cents/mt on the week. Two 55,000 mt (plus/minus 10%) coal stems from Richards Bay Multi-Purpose Terminal to Pakistan International Bulk Terminal were worked, for 15–25 and 24–30 September laycan.

## FAR EAST

Pricing has reflected the positive sentiment, with both tankers and now bulkers well in excess of USD500 per LT/LDT. The vintage Indonesia controlled Handy tanker, namely the 2001 blt MT GREEN STARS (abt 8,274 LDT) which we reported sold last week at a price of USD500 LT/LDT we understand has been resold by the Cash Buyer into Pakistan at USD 552 per LT/LDT. While a vintage handy bulker, the MARIA (abt 6,140 LDT) has also been reported for a price in the region of USD532 per LT/LDT.

However a lack of tonnage continues to deny the market of any real activity and it remains to be seen whether these improved levels can persuade Owners to cash out their vintage tonnage.
