# COMMENT MARKET REPORT – WEEK 48/2022

# IRAQI CRUDE OIL EXPORTS

2022 has turned out to be a very positive year for crude oil trade, despite the surging oil prices and risks of economic recession. In the first 11 months of 2022, global crude oil loadings were up +8.6% y-o-y at 1,865.7 mln tonnes, excluding all cabotage trade, according to vessels tracking data from Refinitiv. This was well above the 1,718.3 mln tonnes in Jan-Nov 2021, but slightly below the 1,926.9 mln tonnes in the same period of 2019. Exports from Saudi Arabia are up +17.7% y-o-y to 332.3 mln tonnes in Jan-Nov 2022, above pre-Covid levels. Seaborne shipments from other AG countries are also up by +10.4% y-o-y to 471.0 mln tonnes in the same period, but still below pre-Covid levels. Exports from Russia have also increased by +11.2% y-o-y to 199.4 mln tonnes, slightly below the 206.6 mln t in the same period of 2019. From the USA, exports surged by +24.6% y-o-y to 149.8 mln t this year.

From West Africa, however, volumes are down -3.3% y-o-y to 154.6 mln t. From the North Sea, exports are also down by -1.7% y-o-y to 98.4 mln tonnes in Jan-Nov 2022. In terms of demand, seaborne imports into the European Union (27) increased by +12.5% y-o-y to 412.0 mln t in Jan-Nov 2022. Imports to India also surged +11.5% y-o-y to 203.7 mln t in the same period. Imports to China, however, declined by -5.2% y-o-y to 390.5 mln t, the lowest level since 2018. **Iraq** is the second largest seaborne exporter of crude oil in the Arabian Gulf after Saudi Arabia, the third in the world behind Saudi Arabia and Russia, and still just ahead of the USA. In Jan-Nov 2022, Iraqi ports accounted for 8.3% of global crude oil loadings. This only covers cargoes loading in Basrah (pretty much the only loading port in Iraq), and does not even include the exports which go by pipeline via Turkey, i.e. Kirkuk grade oil from Northern Iraq.

About 71 percent of volumes loaded in Basrah are carried in VLCCs, and about 28 percent is loaded in Suezmaxes. In the 12 months of 2019, Iraqi seaborne crude oil exports were as high as 176.3 mln tonnes. In 2020, however, due to limited global demand due to the pandemic, Iraq managed to ship just 151.7 mln t of crude oil, down -13.9% y-o-y. Things didn't really improve much in 2021, when seaborne crude oil exports from Iraq were further down by -1.3% y-o-y to 149.8 mln tonnes. This year, however, there has been a clear turnaround. In the first 11 months of 2022, Iraq exported 154.4 mln tonnes of crude oil, which was a +14.5% y-o-y improvement over the 134.9 mln tonnes in the same 11-month period of last year. This was also the best performance since 2019, when Iraq exported 161.7 mln tonnes in the first 11 months of the year.

In terms of destinations for the shipments from Iraq, it's still a head to head between Mainland China and India, with Europe in third place. In the first 11 months of 2022, Iraq shipped 47.2 mln tonnes to India, which represents a +3.1% y-o-y increase from 45.8 mln tonnes in Jan-Nov 2021, and also more than the 45.8 mln t in Jan-Nov 2019. India now accounts for 30.6% of Iraqi crude oil shipments so far this year. Iraq also shipped 46.1 mln tonnes so far this year to Mainland China, which is an increase of +7.1% y-o-y from 43.1 mln tonnes in the same period of 2021, but below the record 49.4 mln tonnes in Jan-Nov 2020. China accounts for 29.9% of Iraqi crude shipments this year. The third top destination is the EU, with 21.4 mln tonnes, or 13.9% of Iraq's total exports in Jan-Nov 2022. Shipments from Iraq to the EU surged by +67.8% y-o-y to in Jan-Nov 2022 from 12.7 mln tonnes in Jan-Nov 2021, but are still below the 21.8 mln tonnes in Jan-Nov 2019.

# CAPESIZE MARKET

## ATLANTIC AND PACIFIC BASIN

At the beginning of last week some positive signs were seen, but moving towards the end of the week the negative sentiment prevailed with the average of the 5TC routes settled at $12,598/d, which is a decline of $775 week-on-week. Quite limited activity with the market being very tight and quiet especially compared to the same period last year. Out of Brazil, C3 route was assessed at $19.006 per mt by the end the week. A premium was being offered for vessels able to load in mid-December dates, but there was not much cargo available. In the Pacific, the C5 route, West Australia to Qingdao remained active, ending at $8.98 per ton with the transpacif round voyage at $13,518 per day. Interesting to note that iron ore futures reached their highest in 23 weeks because of the support of China's steel producers for property developers which is a good sign for the future. As in the Pacific there was an increased supply with quite a number of vessels looking for cargoes, rates softened. Rio Tinto fixed a Capesize to lift their 170,000 mt +/- 10% iron ore from Western Australia to China at $8.10 per mt for a 19-21 December laycan.

FMG also fixed a Capesize for their 160,000 mt +/- 10% iron ore from Port Hedland to China at $8.10 per mt for a laycan of 17 December onwards. With the above, the freight rate for 170,000 mt +/- 10% iron ore from West Australia to China has been assessed at the end of the week at $8.10 per mt, down 90 cents from the previous week. In the Atlantic, limited activity was registered. With the current levels, owners are likely preferring to stay in the Pacific. A 203,500 dwt built 2007 was fixed for a 180,000mt +/- 10% iron ore from Sudeste to Qingdao for 27 December onwards laydays at $22.75/mt. Vale fixed a 180,000 dwt built 2008 for their 170,000 mt +/- 10% iron ore from Tubarão to Qingdao, end of December loading at a freight rate in the mid $21s per mt. A 180,100 dwt built 2004 was fixed by Glencore for a cargo of 170,000mt +/- 10% iron ore from Seven Islands to China, laycan 20-26 December at $25.75/mt.

The freight rate for 170,000 mt +/- 10% iron ore from Tubarão, Brazil to Qingdao, China was then assessed at a level of $19.5/mt. From South Africa, there was some fixing activity with steady rates compared to the previous week. The freight rate for a Capesize ship to move 170,000 mt +/- 10% iron ore from South Africa to China was assessed at $14.40/mt, a slight decrease of 10 cents from the previous week.

# PANAMAX MARKET

## ATLANTIC BASIN

As usual demand for mineral and grains during this time of the year is pretty strong and supply chases demand. Nice Kamsarmax units were fixed in the low/mid $20,000s/d going on fronthauls from USG and NCSAm. An 82,000 dwt built 2016 was fixed basis dely passing Gib 6/8 Dec for a TCT via USG redely Spore/Japan at $24,000/d. Transatlantic rates also had a positive week with rates went back in the mid teens. An LME open Gib achieved $15,000/d for a TA from USG. ECSAm activity was stable throughout the whole week. A 76,600 built 2003 sld Singapore 18 Nov was fixed for a TCT via ECSAm at $16,650 + 665,000 bb basis redely Spore/Jpn. From BSea many cargoes were cancelled and the tonnage list in E Med increased very rapidly, despite that a Kamsarmax was rumored in the mid/high teens for a trip from Ukraine to Far East.

## PACIFIC BASIN

Strategic move by many owners who decided to accept very low rates for backhaul trips in order to reposition their vessels in Atlantic. NoPac RV was pretty strong throughout the whole week. The highest levels recorded were around $13/14,000/d on very nice Kamsarmax units for sulphur. An 81,600 built 2020 open Kimitsu 4 Dec was fixed on subs NoPac RV at $13,250/d. On the other hand coal premiums were generally pretty low and smaller and older units were fixed on this trade and an 81,800 built 2003 was on subs for a TCT via Indonesia to S China at $10,000/d. Increasing demand was spotted from Australia, especially for coal to India, and fixtures were on a slightly positive trend around $12/13,000/d. Stagnant demand and dropping spot levels pushed many players to try and get vessels for short and long period and an 85,400 built 2020 was fixed at $15,500/d for 12 months.

## SUPRAMAX & HANDYSIZE MARKET

## US GULF / NORTH AMERICA

Demand was strong and tonnage supply tight, the feeling is that the market will go higher in the area. USG-F East remained stable around mid $20,000s/d both on Ultramax and Supramax whilst USG-Cont was in the low $20,000s/d.

## EAST COAST SOUTH AMERICA

The market remained active and rates stable. On Handysize a modern 33,000 dwt built 2010 was fixed basis dely Itaja for one trip via Santos to Continent at $22,000/d. A 31,000 dwt built 2005 was fixed basis dely Itaguai for one trip with grains to BSea intention Bourgas at $17,000/d. On larger units not a lot was reported a part from a 57,00 dwt fixed basis dely aps Recalda at $23,500/d for a trip to S Africa. A 48,000 dwt was done at $26,500/d basis dely dop Termisa for a trip with salt to US EC.

## NORTH EUROPE / CONTINENT

Due to a lack of enquiries and too many ships available the market in Cont and Baltic were still suffering with rates softening further. Charterers were aiming to fix Ultramax vessels for Cont to F East below the $20,000/d mark. Supramax rates for Cont-Med were in the low teens even if a 58,000 dwt was reported at $6,000/d basis dely aps baltic on the same route. The spread for Russian business compared to non Russian cargoes was shrinking and a Supramax was reported at $20,000/d basis dely ARAG for a trip via Russian Baltic to India.

## BLACK SEA / MEDITERRANEAN

The market in BSea was flat and stagnant again, very few cargoes, mostly not firm, characterized the trend of the last few days of the week pushing rates mostly downwards, but not in a nosedive. All routes and sizes suffered this situation and expectations point this direction given that forward cargoes for January have been seeing and fixing very low numbers. Handysize, 35,000/38,000 dwt, for trip F East were getting $19,000/d. TA tripto USG dropped to $12,500/d and $10,000/d was the level for ECSAm. The trip via BSea, basis dely Canakkale, to Cont was around $14,000/d level, the same level of CrossMed, but we believe fixtures have been done on private terms at lower level already. Supramaxe and Ultramax units had the same trend, or even worse: trip via BSea to Cont was around $14,500/d basis dely Canakkale, CrossMed probably hit the $14,000/d level already. Trip to ECSAm went down to $11/11,500/d and to USG to $13/13,500/d. Trip to F East went from $22,500/d to $21,000/d on Supramax and from $23,500/d to $22,000/d on Ultramax.

## **Iron ore shrugs off China COVID woes, focuses on stimulus: Reuters**

The iron ore market is choosing to focus on China's efforts to stimulate its property sector, rather than on rising concerns over the potential economic fallout from surging COVID-19 cases and public anger at efforts to contain outbreaks. ## **Easing war impact, rising supply set to make Asian thermal coal more affordable in 2023 - Platts**

With the impact of the Russia-Ukraine war on commodity markets softening as the new year knocks and several countries aiming to raise production, Asian thermal coal prices are expected to see a reasonable correction in 2023 given that supply concerns will likely take a back seat. Indonesia, China and India have all announced higher production targets for the next year as part of their larger goal of meeting domestic needs internally, which is expected to lessen the load on the Asian thermal coal trading market, according to miners, traders and buyers surveyed by S&P Global Commodity Insights.

## **Ukraine grain exports down 29.6% at 18.1 mln T so far in 2022/23**

Ukraine has exported almost 18.1 million tonnes of grain so far in the 2022/23 season, down 29.6% from the 25.8 million tonnes exported by the same stage of the previous season, agriculture ministry data showed on Friday. The volume included more than 6.9 million tonnes of wheat, 9.7 million tonnes of corn and about 1.5 million tonnes of barley.

## **Chinese farmers let cabbages rot as **COVID curbs disrupt sales**

China's vegetable growers are ploughing their produce back into their fields or leaving it to rot, as widespread restrictions to curb the spread of COVID cut off distribution channels and close markets. Thousands of tonnes of vegetables are unsold in central Henan province, one of the country's top producing regions, according to local media reports, charities and farmers, while farmers in northern Shandong province, another top growing region, are also unable to reach markets.

## **EU 2022/23 soft wheat exports up 3% on year at 13.89 mln tonnes**

Soft wheat exports from the European Union in the 2022/23 season that started on July 1 had reached 13.89 million tonnes by Nov. 27, up 3% from 13.45 million by the same week in 2021/22, weekly data published by the European Commission showed on Tuesday. The year-on-year increase has narrowed from 10% two weeks ago, although traders are expecting 2022/23 exports to get a boost in the coming weeks after talk of large French sales to China.

## **French grain exports from Dunkirk port at 10-year high**

Nord Cereales, which runs France's third largest grain export silo at Dunkirk port, has shipped its biggest volume in over 10 years for the first five months of the season, it said on Friday, illustrating a fast pace of French exports. Between the start of this season on July 1 and the end of November, Nord Cereales exported more than 1.2 million tonnes of grain, it said.

## **Mexico open to deal with U.S. on GMO corn as farmers demand clarity over ban**

Mexican President Andres Manuel Lopez Obrador said on Tuesday he is seeking a deal with Washington after the United States threatened legal action over Mexico's plan to ban genetically modified (GMO) corn in 2024. After meeting with Mexican officials on Monday, U.S. Secretary of Agriculture Tom Vilsack said Mexico's decree could violate the United States-Mexico-Canada (USMCA) trade pact.

## **Access to Brazil's Paranagua port blocked by landslides -authority**

Brazil's Paranagua port authority said on Tuesday that landslides caused by heavy rainfall blocked road and rail access to the port, the second busiest for grain exports in the country. In a statement, the authority said other port operations are normal as ships continue to unload and load products stored at the port's warehouses.

## **Funds forge most bearish CBOT wheat view since 2019, corn selling eases -Braun**

Chicago wheat futures are significantly lower than earlier this year when conflict broke out between top Black Sea exporters, though speculators' views have grown increasingly bearish despite an easing in prices. In the week ended Nov. 22, money managers increased their net short position in CBOT wheat futures and options to 53,402 contracts from 46,780 a week earlier. ## **EU tentatively agrees $60 price cap on Russian seaborne oil**

European Union governments tentatively agreed on Thursday on a $60 a barrel price cap on Russian seaborne oil - an idea of the Group of Seven (G7) nations - with an adjustment mechanism to keep the cap at 5% below the market price, according to diplomats and a document seen by Reuters.

## **G7 price cap on Russian oil kicks in, Russia will only sell at market price**

The Group of Seven price cap on Russian seaborne oil came into force on Monday as the West tries to limit Moscow's ability to finance its war in Ukraine, but Russia has said it will not abide by the measure even if it has to cut production. The price cap, to be enforced by the G7, the European Union and Australia, comes on top of the EU's embargo on imports of Russian crude by sea and similar pledges by the United States, Canada, Japan and Britain.

## **OPEC+ keeps steady policy amid weakening economy, Russian oil cap**

OPEC+ agreed to stick to its oil output targets at a meeting on Sunday as the oil markets struggle to assess the impact of a slowing Chinese economy on demand and a G7 price cap on Russian oil on supply. The decision comes two days after the Group of Seven (G7) nations agreed a price cap on Russian oil.

## **OPEC oil output drops in November after cut pledged -survey**

OPEC oil output has fallen in November, led by top exporter Saudi Arabia and other Gulf members, after the wider OPEC+ alliance pledged steep output cuts to support the market amid a worsening economic outlook, a Reuters survey found on Wednesday. The Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC) pumped 29.01 million barrels per day (bpd) this month, the survey found, down 710,000 bpd from October. In September, OPEC output had been the highest since 2020.

## **U.S. EPA to propose boost in biofuel blending volumes, EV program - sources**

The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency will propose increases in the amount of ethanol and other biofuels that oil refiners must blend into their fuel over the next three years, two sources familiar with the matter said. The EPA will also seek for the first time to make the use of biofuels to charge electric vehicles part of the renewable fuel program, giving car makers like Tesla Inc the ability to generate tradable credits, the sources said. The EV proposal will add up to 1.4 billion new credits by 2025, they added.

## **British Gas launches 'Peak Save' energy reduction plan**

British Gas is launching an energy reduction plan for this winter that will pay customers for every unit of electricity they save compared to their normal usage, the company said on Thursday. The plan, called "Peak Save", will run between December 2022 and March 2023, and could save customers 100 pounds ($119.92) over the course of the winter, British Gas said in a statement.

## **Russia discussing 'gas union' with Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan**
