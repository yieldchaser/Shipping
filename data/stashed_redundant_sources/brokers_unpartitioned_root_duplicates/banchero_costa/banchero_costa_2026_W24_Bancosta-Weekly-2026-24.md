# CHINA IRON ORE IMPORTS

2025 was another positive year for global seaborne iron ore trade. In Jan-Dec 2025, global loadings of iron ore increased by +3.5% y-o-y to 1,732.0 mln tonnes, based on AXS Marine vessel tracking data. 2026 has begun on a firm note, with export volumes up by +3.8% y-o-y in Jan-May 2026 to 692.2 mln tonnes. Exports from Australia increased in Jan-May 2026 by +3.8% y-o-y to 386.1 mln tonnes. From Brazil, exports increased by +3.7% y-o-y in Jan-May 2026 to 149.2 mln t. From South Africa volumes increased +4.7% y-o-y to 23.0 mln t. From Canada there was a -10.6% y-o-y decline to 21.3 mln tonnes. India saw an increase of +0.8% y-o-y in Jan-May 2026 to 12.9 mln t. From Norway exports rebounded by +11.6% y-o-y to 9.1 mln t. Demand is still driven primarily by Mainland China, which accounts for 75% of imports. Iron ore imports into China increased by +7.1% y-o-y in Jan-May 2026 to 528.0 mln tonnes.

Imports into Japan declined by -5.1% y-o-y to 37.5 mln t. Volumes into South Korea increased by +4.3% y-o-y to 30.9 mln t. To the EU, imports declined by -2.3% y-o-y to 29.1 mln t. To Vietnam volumes were up +8.3% y-o-y to 10.9 mln t. Imports into Malaysia increased by +6.9% y-o-y to 10.8 mln tonnes. To Oman, volumes were down -34.0% y-o-y to 3.7 mln t, to Saudi Arabia down by -51.1% y-o-y to 2.2 mln t, to Bahrain -62.8% y-o-y to 1.8 mln t. **Mainland China** is by far the largest importer of iron ore in the world. China accounted for 75.3% of global iron ore imports in Jan-May 2026. Imports into China have increased +4.1% y-o-y in Jan-Dec 2024 to 1250.4 mln tonnes. They have further increased by +1.5% y-o-y in Jan-Dec 2025 to 1714.7 mln t, which was a new all-time record. As already mentioned, in Jan-May 2026, imports into China increased by +7.1% y-o-y to 528.0 mln t.

The vast majority (70%) of iron ore volumes into China in Jan-May 2026 were loaded on Capesizes (130,000-220,000 dwt), with a further 21% carried on VLOCs (over 220,000 dwt), whilst just 3% was carried on Post-Panamaxes, 2% on Panamaxes, and 4% on Supramaxes. The main iron ore import terminals in Mainland China are: Caofeidian (66.7 mln t discharged in Jan-May 2026), Tangshan/Jingtang (51.3 mln t), Lanshan (45.6 mln t), Ningbo/Zhoushan (43.6 mln t), Tianjin (36.5 mln t), Lianyungang (29.0 mln t), Dongjiakou (28.5 mln t), Rizhao (28.4 mln t), Huanghua (23.9 mln t), Fangcheng (23.0 mln t), Zhanjiang (22.6 mln t).

In terms of sources for China's iron ore imports, Australia remains by far the top source country, with a 62.4% share in Jan-May 2026. Iron ore imports from Australia to China in Jan-May 2026 have increased by +4.3% y-o-y to an all-time high of 329.5 mln tonnes, from 316.0 mln t in Jan-May 2025. The top loading port in Australia for iron cargoes to China is Port Hedland (195.4 mln t in Jan-May 2026). Brazil remains in second spot with a 20.8% share in Jan-May 2026. Imports to China from Brazil increased by +10.3% y-o-y in Jan-May 2026 to 109.7 mln tonnes, from 99.5 mln t in Jan-May 2025. However, this was still marginally below the 109.8 mln t in Jan-May 2024. The top port in Brazil for cargoes to China was Ponta da Madeira (38.5 mln t in Jan-May 2026). Volumes from South Africa to China declined by -4.9% y-o-y to 13.7 mln t in Jan-May 2026. From India volumes declined by -1.1% y-o-y to 12.0 mln t.

From Peru volumes increased by +28.0% y-o-y to 11.1 mln t. From Canada there was a +30.5% y-o-y increase to 8.4 mln t.

# CAPESIZE MARKET

## ATLANTIC AND PACIFIC BASIN

The Capesize market endured a decidedly bearish week, weighed down by a persistent tonnage overhang in the Pacific basin and a sharp sell-off in forward freight agreement (FFA) values that eroded sentiment from the outset. Physical fundamentals remained poor throughout, with ample tonnage supply consistently outpacing demand despite intermittent flurries of cargo enquiry, and trading thinned further as the week drew to a close. The Pacific bore the brunt of the weakness. The benchmark Western Australia–Qingdao iron ore run, assessed at USD 13.15/wmt on 8 June, shed value almost daily — falling USD 1.00 on 9 June amid a marked tonnage build, easing on 10 June as owners softened their resistance, and dropping a steep USD 1.10 to USD 10.90/wmt on 11 June, before settling at USD 10.85/wmt on 12 June. Across the week the route lost USD 2.30/wmt. Although mining majors lent intermittent support through prompt fixtures from Port Hedland and Dampier to Qingdao, heavy tonnage availability and easing congestion at Chinese discharge ports kept the basin firmly on the back foot.

The Atlantic proved comparatively resilient. The Tubarao–Qingdao run held at USD 36.25/wmt on 8 June, dipped to USD 35.60/wmt on 9 June, then firmed mid-week before easing marginally to USD 35.80/wmt on 12 June, a modest net loss of 45 cents. A backwardated Brazilian market, in which limited early-July tonnage commanded firmer numbers for prompt laycans, lent support, though wide bid-offer spreads and a mismatch across July loading windows curbed activity throughout. South African business was subdued, the Saldanha Bay–Qingdao route easing from USD 27.35/wmt to USD 25.60/wmt amid scant enquiry, with the bulk of the decline coming on 12 June. Reflecting the Pacific-led softness, the weighted Capesize T4 time charter equivalent index fell from USD 34,854/day on 8 June to USD 31,222/day on 12 June, despite a modest rebound on the final session. With FFA sentiment fragile and Pacific tonnage lengthening, the market closed the week under clear downward pressure, the Atlantic offering only a partial counterweight.

# PANAMAX MARKET

## ATLANTIC BASIN

Following the return to normal trading after Posidonia, Atlantic activity picked up noticeably, with a higher volume of reported fixtures and firmer sentiment, particularly on East Coast South America grain routes. The week started strongly, with a modern Kamsarmax (2019, 82,000 dwt) fixed from Rio Grande for a trip via East Coast South America with redelivery Passero at USD 24,000 per day, highlighting the strength of prompt Atlantic employment. The majority of the week's activity centred on East Coast South America fronthaul business. An 81,000-dwt Kamsarmax (2014-built) was fixed via East Coast South America to Singapore/Japan at USD 18,750 per day, whilst newer tonnage built between 2015 and 2023 secured between USD 22,000 and USD 24,000 per day for similar voyages. Older Panamax tonnage generally traded around the USD 20,000 per day level. Arrival pilot station business remained active. An 82,000-dwt Kamsarmax (2012-built) was fixed APS East Coast South America at USD 20,000 per day plus a USD 1,000,000 ballast bonus for redelivery Singapore/Japan, reflecting continued charterer demand for South American grain cargoes.

In the North Atlantic, rates held firm. A Panamax (2014-built) secured USD 19,000 per day on a North Coast South America to Far East run.

## PACIFIC BASIN

The Pacific market remained generally firm this week, underpinned by steady demand from Australia and NOPAC, while Indonesian activity presented a more mixed picture. Although overall fixture volumes were not particularly elevated, modern tonnage continued to command a premium in key loading regions. In Indonesia, coal demand held relatively stable, with fixtures concluded across a wide spread depending on vessel age, delivery position and redelivery options. A 2017-built 81,361 dwt vessel fixed at USD 22,000 for an Indonesia/Malaysia trip, whilst a 2011-built 79,393 dwt vessel secured USD 17,000 for Indonesia/Malaysia coal. A 2011-built 75,503 dwt vessel was fixed at USD 17,500 for Indonesia/South China, whilst a 2011-built 75,599 dwt vessel concluded at USD 20,000 for Indonesia/Vietnam coal. The spread between modern and older tonnage remained evident. In NOPAC, grain demand continued to underpin activity, particularly for prompt tonnage, though fixture levels showed signs of moderation compared to earlier in the month, sitting comfortably below the USD 20,000 benchmark. A 2016-built 81,056 dwt vessel fixed at USD 19,500 for NOPAC grain redelivery China, whilst a 2013-built 75,331 dwt vessel secured USD 17,100 for NOPAC/Singapore-Japan.

A 2011-built 79,158 dwt vessel fixed at USD 16,000 for NOPAC grain redelivery Singapore-Japan, whilst a 2011-built 83,482 dwt vessel concluded at USD 18,000 for NOPAC/South-East Asia coal. Australia remained the strongest performing sub-market within the Pacific basin, supported by continued coal and iron ore demand into North Asia, with modern Kamsarmax tonnage achieving mid-to-high USD 20,000 levels. A 2022-built 84,998 dwt vessel fixed at USD 27,250 for West Australia/China iron ore, whilst a 2020-built 81,982 dwt vessel secured USD 26,000 for Australia/Korea. A 2025-built 82,111 dwt vessel fixed at around USD 24,500-24,750 for East Coast Australia/Japan, whilst a 2024-built 82,427 dwt vessel concluded at USD 24,500 for Australia/South China coal.

Rates in East Coast South America showed solid upward momentum across all segments, with larger units pushing significantly higher. For Ultramax tonnage, transatlantic voyages from West Africa via East Coast South America to Continent/Mediterranean were around USD 20,500 per day, whilst fronthaul trips from West Africa via East Coast South America to China were around USD 26,000 per day.

## **NORTH EUROPE / CONTINENT**

The market was firm, primarily on larger units, whilst Handysize tonnage remained broadly in line with the previous week. # SUPRAMAX & HANDYSIZE MARKET

## MEG / INDIAN OCEAN

The Indian Ocean opened lacklustre, with little fresh enquiry on the Indian coasts and subdued Persian Gulf fixing, before sentiment firm mid-week on tight prompt tonnage and owners' reluctance to discount. Trading thinned again into Friday, though rates held firm on limited availability. From the Middle East, Persian Gulf activity stayed muted throughout. The Mina Saqr–Paradip limestone benchmark (50,000 mt) was broadly rangebound, easing from USD 21.45/mt to USD 21.10/mt over the week; a 55,000 mt limestone stem from Salalah to Visakhapatnam/Paradip was reported fixed at USD 16.75/mt, free of discharge port charges. The Gulf-delivery Supramax time charter equivalent softened to around USD 21,650/day, and the Ultramax to roughly USD 24,070/day, on weaker bunkers. South Africa was the standout. The Richards Bay–Port Qasim coal run (50,000 mt) rallied to USD 22.25/mt by 11 June before easing to USD 21.70/mt — a net weekly gain of USD 1.25/mt — amid a shortage of prompt tonnage. A 55,000 mt Saldanha Bay iron ore fines cargo fixed to Son Duong at USD 30/mt, while a Richards Bay–Mombasa coal stem emerged. No Handysize fixtures from these origins were reported.

## FAR EAST

It was a stronger week for the Ultramax and Supramax sector. Asia started slowly but progressed during the week with healthy levels for both backhaul and Pacific round voyages, though the market ended quietly with suggestions it may be peaking. A 63,000-dwt vessel delivery China was fixed for a time charter trip via Gulf of Aden to the Mediterranean at USD 26,000 per day; a 64,000-dwt vessel delivery South-East Asia was fixed Australia/Singapore-Japan range at USD 23,000 per day. For period, a 63,000-dwt vessel open China was fixed for five to seven months at USD 22,000 per day. The Handysize market also strengthened over the week, with sentiment improving and several fixtures reported above previous levels.
