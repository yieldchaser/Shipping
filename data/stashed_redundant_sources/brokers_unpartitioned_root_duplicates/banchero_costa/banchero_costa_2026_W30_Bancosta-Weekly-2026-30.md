# COMMENT MARKET REPORT – WEEK 30/2026

# BRAZIL'S IRON ORE EXPORTS

**1H 2026** was another positive period for global seaborne iron ore trade. In Jan-Jun 2026, global loadings of iron ore increased by **+1.9% y-o-y** to 836.7 mln t, based on AXS Marine vessel tracking data. This builds on the **+3.5% y-o-y** growth recorded in full year 2025. Exports from Australia increased in Jan-Jun 2026 by **+2.6% y-o-y** to 472.1 mln tonnes. From Brazil, exports rose by **+1.4% y-o-y** in Jan-Jun 2026 to 182.0 mln t. From Canada, there was a **-14.0% y-o-y** decrease to 25.2 mln tonnes. From South Africa, volumes increased **+0.6% y-o-y** to 26.6 mln t. From India, increased **+4.2% y-o-y** in Jan-Jun 2026 to 15.3 mln t. Iron ore import demand remains robust in China. Iron ore imports into China increased by **+6.1% y-o-y** in Jan-Jun 2026 to 638.4 mln t. Imports into Japan declined by **-5.8% y-o-y** to 45.1 mln t. To the EU, imports decreased by **-6.6% y-o-y** to 33.3 mln t. Volumes into South Korea were broadly flat, down **-0.3% y-o-y** to 36.1 mln t. Imports into Malaysia increased by **+2.6% y-o-y** to 12.4 mln tonnes. To Vietnam increased by **+17.1% y-o-y** to 14.0 mln t.

To Oman, volumes were down **-21.4% y-o-y** to 5.2 mln t, to Saudi Arabia by **-56.5% y-o-y** to 2.3 mln t, to Bahrain **-70.2% y-o-y** to 1.8 mln t. **Brazil** is currently the second largest exporter of iron ore in the world, after Australia. In Jan-Dec 2025, Brazil accounted for **23.0%** of global iron ore shipments, after Australia's **55.0%**. Canada was third with a **3.7%** share, followed by South Africa in fourth place with **3.1%** and India with **1.6%**. In Jan-Jun 2026, Brazil's share eased to **21.8%**, against Australia's **56.4%**, with South Africa moving up to third at **3.2%**, ahead of Canada at **3.0%**. In Jan-Dec 2025, iron ore exports from Brazil increased by **+5.9% y-o-y** to 398.3 mln t. In Jan-Jun 2026, growth slowed markedly, with volumes up just **+1.4% y-o-y** to 182.0 mln t. After a soft start to the year (28.6 mln t in Jan 2026, down from 37.8 mln t in Dec 2025), Brazil's exports recovered through the half, reaching 36.1 mln t in Jun 2026, up from 33.2 mln t in May 2026 and the strongest month of 2026 so far.

About 50% of exports from Brazil in Jan-Jun 2026 were loaded on VLOCs (including Valemaxes), about 46% was loaded on Capesize tonnage, about 3% on Panamaxes, about 1% on Post-Panamaxes and less than 1% on Supramaxes. Looking at major loading ports for iron ore in Brazil in Jan-Jun 2026, we have: Ponta da Madeira (69.4 mln tonnes), Tubarao (39.1 mln t), Sepetiba/Itaguai (38.2 mln t), Guaiba (15.6 mln t), Acu (12.8 mln t), Ponta Ubu (6.7 mln t). Mainland China is still by far the top destination for Brazilian iron ore, accounting for **72.8%** of Brazil's exports in Jan-Dec 2025 and **71.5%** in Jan-Jun 2026. Shipments to China increased by **+7.1% y-o-y** to 289.8 mln t in Jan-Dec 2025, and by a further **+1.9% y-o-y** to 130.1 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026. Volumes to Malaysia (which is mostly for transhipment to other Asian destinations) increased by **+2.8% y-o-y** in Jan-Dec 2025 to 19.7 mln tonnes, and by **+8.8% y-o-y** in Jan-Jun 2026 to 10.6 mln t.

Exports from Brazil to the EU declined by **-23.1% y-o-y** to 13.0 mln tonnes in Jan-Dec 2025, and fell a further **-21.4% y-o-y** to 5.8 mln tonnes in Jan-Jun 2026. Shipments from Brazil to Oman dipped by **-9.9% y-o-y** to 11.2 mln t in Jan-Dec 2025 and collapsed by **-59.9% y-o-y** to 2.4 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026, to Bahrain declined by **-10.3% y-o-y** to 9.1 mln t in 2025 and by **-67.8% y-o-y** to 1.5 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026 To Egypt shipments declined by **-16.2% y-o-y** to 3.9 mln t in 2025 but rebounded by **+77.7% y-o-y** to 2.9 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026, whilst those to Turkey increased by **+28.1% y-o-y** to 5.5 mln t in 2025 and by a further **+26.8% y-o-y** to 3.4 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026.

## **China iron ore imports set to rise this year as steelmakers make up for declining grade**

China's iron ore imports are set to rise for a third consecutive year in 2026, as steelmakers buy more to compensate for declining iron content and as Guinea's Simandou project boosts supply, analysts said. The top iron ore consumer's imports are likely to climb as much as 4% to a record of just above 1.3 billion metric tons, analysts said, helping prevent any oversupply-induced price drop.

## **Fortescue chair calls for fair negotiations with China**

Fortescue founder Andrew Forrest called for China and Australia to "always negotiate fairly," at an event in Perth, as the world's fourth-largest iron ore maker negotiates annual supply terms with its biggest customer. Global iron ore miners have faced increasing resistance from China's state iron ore buyer, China Mineral Resources Group in annual supply talks over the past year as China seeks better terms for its steelmakers.

## **Asia's thermal coal imports continue to rebound, except for India**

Asia boosted imports of thermal coal for a third straight month in July as major buyers such as China, Japan and South Korea bought more of the fuel to meet peak summer demand. Asia, which buys about 90% of global seaborne thermal coal, is expected to import 73.16 million metric tons in July, up from 70.31 million in June and also above the 71.04 million from July last year, according to data compiled by commodity analysts Kpler.

## **Guinea first-half bauxite exports hit record high on Chinese demand**

Guinea's bauxite exports jumped 15% to a record high in the first half of 2026, official data showed, driven by robust Chinese demand despite mounting pressure on smaller producers from rising fuel and freight costs. Bauxite is a feedstock for alumina, a key ingredient in aluminium widely used in the transport, construction and packaging industries.

## **Shipowners halt calls at Ukraine's Black Sea ports, wary of Russian strikes**

Shipowners halted vessel arrivals at Ukraine's Black Sea ports due to intensified Russian strikes, Kyiv said, as President Volodymyr Zelenskiy warned Moscow might escalate attacks in order to block the country's main corridor for grain exports. In recent weeks, Russia has pummelled logistics and port infrastructure in the southern Odesa region with missiles and drones.

## **Canada's crops in best shape in a decade despite extreme weather, monitoring firm says**

Even as floods, tornadoes and hailstorms hammer the Canadian Prairies, farmers are seeing the best crop conditions in a decade halfway through the growing season, according to EarthDaily's analysis of satellite and agronomic data. The extreme weather has not lessened, but a years-long drought that left thousands of farmers across the vast prairies struggling to produce a crop has eased after bountiful spring rains provided enough moisture for the crop to germinate. Warm summer weather has sped crop development.

## **Argentina seen overtaking Brazil in corn exports as Iran purchases fall**

Brazil is set to lose its position as the world's second-largest corn exporter to Argentina in 2025/26 after shipments to key buyer Iran fell sharply amid conflict there, consultancy Hedgepoint Global Markets said on Tuesday. Hedgepoint projects Brazil's corn exports at 43 million metric tons in 2025/26, up from 41.1 million tons in the previous season but below Argentina's estimated 45 million tons. The United States is the world's largest corn exporter.

## **Rhine river water falls again, freight vessels sailing part loaded**

The river Rhine in Germany has fallen again, with shallow water hindering cargo shipping and increasing costs for freight transport, commodity traders said on Thursday. Shallow water after this summer's heatwave and scarce rainfall means cargo vessels are only able to sail partly loaded on the river, increasing costs for cargo owners. Loads must be spread among several vessels, also increasing costs and creating an unwelcome extra expense for German industry currently showing signs of recovery.

## **EU sugar outlook dims as dry weather hits beet crops**

The outlook for the European Union's sugar production has weakened after a severe drought hit beet crops in France, Germany and Poland, the bloc's three largest producers, and after farmers sharply cut plantings amid slumping prices, growers and analysts said. Lower output should help European sugar prices recover, easing pressure on farmers and sugar makers, which also suffered from high costs and import competition. Source: Reuters / LSEG

# CAPESIZE MARKET

## ATLANTIC AND PACIFIC BASIN

The Capesize market staged a broad-based recovery over the week, reversing the previous week's losses as firming freight derivative (FFA) values, a sustained climb in bunker prices and improving tonnage demand lifted rates across all major iron ore routes. The week opened on a soft footing. The Western Australia–Qingdao route (170,000 mt iron ore) slipped a further 80 cents/wmt to USD 11.00/wmt on 20 July, weighed down by ample Pacific tonnage supply, declining FFA values and a thin cargo list. Sentiment turned on 21 July, however, with several participants declaring the market had "bottomed out" as bid levels firmed in both basins. The route then advanced steadily — to USD 11.10, USD 11.75 and USD 12.15/wmt — before a further 75-cent/wmt gain carried it to USD 12.90/wmt on 24 July, a weekly rise of USD 1.90/wmt. Persistent charterer–owner standoffs slowed the pace of fixing, but a surge in fresh Pacific demand and firm week-end owner resistance underpinned the advance. The Atlantic led the recovery.

The Tubarao–Qingdao route held at USD 31.95/wmt on 20 July before climbing on tightening ballaster supply for the August loading window and rising bunker costs, gaining USD 1.15/wmt on 22 July alone. It closed at USD 34.60/wmt on 24 July, up USD 2.65/wmt over the week, with offers heard in excess of USD 35/wmt for H2 August laycans. Fronthaul activity also firmed, with North Atlantic fixtures concluded from Sept-Îles and Pointe Noire. The South Africa route (Saldanha Bay–Qingdao) followed suit, easing to USD 23.50/wmt early in the week before rising to USD 25.40/wmt on 24 July, a net weekly gain of USD 1.90/wmt. Overall, the market closed on a distinctly bullish note, with elevated bunker prices and a lengthening Pacific cargo list expected to keep bid and offer ideas supported into the following week.

# PANAMAX MARKET

## ATLANTIC BASIN

The Atlantic market held steady over the week, activity again concentrated on ECSA fronthaul business while the North Atlantic saw a more selective flow of fixtures. Freight levels changed little overall, with modern Kamsarmaxes still commanding premiums over older tonnage. In the South Atlantic, an 82,000-dwt (2020) open Haldia fixed via ECSA, redelivery Singapore/Japan, at USD 22,750/day; an 82,000-dwt (2022) retro Singapore achieved USD 23,000/day on the same route; and an 82,000-dwt (2024) open Tuticorin fixed at USD 22,750/day. At the softer end, an 82,000-dwt (2012) retro Singapore was fixed at USD 17,500/day and a 75,000-dwt (2008) open Tuticorin secured USD 17,750/day, underlining the widening spread between modern and older vessels. In the North Atlantic, a newly built 82,000-dwt (2026) open Ghent fixed for two laden legs with Atlantic redelivery at USD 25,750/day, the first voyage intended East Coast Canada to Argentina. Elsewhere, an 82,000-dwt (2023) open Gibraltar fixed via NCSA, redelivery Spain/Portugal, at USD 22,250/day, while an 82,000-dwt (2014) open Bilbao was reported on subjects for an NCSA round voyage around USD 21,250/day.

Sources also indicated an 85,000-dwt (2016) open Gibraltar fixed a Colombia–Taiwan coal voyage at around USD 33,000/day, reflecting the stronger returns available for longer Atlantic employment.

## PACIFIC BASIN

The Asia-Pacific Panamax market opened on a firm note but turned progressively bearish through the week, weighed down by a persistently overtonnaged Pacific basin and thin cargo replenishment. Rates edged up on Monday 20 July despite stagnant trading and firmed to mid-week highs around 21 July, before sentiment deteriorated. Elevated spot tonnage, easing port congestion releasing further vessels for late-July dates, and a softening east coast South America market combined to pressure owners' ideas. By 24 July many owners of modern tonnage were electing to ballast rather than fix at depressed Pacific levels. On the key Australian coking coal routes, Hay Point–Qingdao peaked at USD 18.65/mt on 21–22 July before easing to USD 17.85/mt by 24 July, down from USD 18.40/mt the preceding Friday. Hay Point–Paradip traced a similar arc, slipping to USD 20.65/mt. The Indonesian thermal coal trade also softened, Samarinda–Guangzhou easing to USD 8.90/mt from a mid-week USD 9.30/mt. Fixtures included a 75,000 mt Hay Point–Dhamra/Haldia metallurgical coal cargo at USD 17.40/mt FIO.

Firmer bunker prices added to the negative tone, with participants awaiting clearer direction.

## EAST COAST SOUTH AMERICA

**EAST COAST SOUTH AMERICA** The ECSA market appears quiet, with few cargoes, so it seems still to be slowing despite relatively modest tonnage. ## NORTH EUROPE / CONTINENT

# SUPRAMAX & HANDYSIZE MARKET

## MEG / INDIAN OCEAN

Indian Ocean Supramax and Ultramax sentiment was mixed over 20–24 July, firming for much of the week before easing as tonnage gradually built up across the basin, leaving charterers better supplied than a week earlier. Rising bunker prices lent underlying support, while owners held firm fixing ideas, particularly for trips towards India. Out of the Persian Gulf, the limestone trade firmed steadily. Freight to move 50,000 mt of limestone from Mina Saqr to Paradip on the east coast of India rose to USD 17.55/mt by 24 July, from USD 17.05/mt on 20 July.
