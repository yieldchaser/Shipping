# Banchero Costa Weekly Market Report - Week 1, 2023

## Weekly Macro Insight: Indonesian Coal Exports

After a slow start in the first quarter, global coal trade has really picked up pace last year, and is now fully back to pre-Covid levels. In the full 12 months of 2022, total global seaborne coal loadings increased by +5.9% y-o-y to 1204.9 mln t (excluding cabotage), from 1138.3 mln t in the full 12 months of 2021, although still below the 1275.6 mln t in Jan-Dec 2019. As already mentioned, the worst was at start of the year, and the trend in recent months has been increasingly positive.

In 1Q 2022, global loadings were down -4.8% y-o-y to just 257.4 mln t, and down -20.3% from 1Q 2019. In 2Q 2022, coal loadings were a strong +8.5% y-o-y at 313.8 mln t, and down -4.1% from 2Q 2019. In 3Q 2022, shipments increased again to 317.2 mln t, up +6.3% y-o-y, and just -0.7% from 3Q 2019. In 4Q 2022, loadings were 316.5 mln t, up +12.9% y-o-y from 4Q 2021, and -0.6% from 4Q 2019. In Jan-Dec 2022, exports from Indonesia increased by +21.2% y-o-y to 388.9 mln t, whilst from Australia down -5.0% y-o-y to 340.3 mln t. Seaborne coal imports into the European Union surged by +33.9% y-o-y to 116.5 mln t in Jan-Dec 2022, whilst imports to India increased by +13.6% y-o-y to 203.8 mln t, and imports to China declined by -3.2% y-o-y to 234.7 mln t. **Indonesia** is the world's largest seaborne exporter of coal, accounting for 32.3% of the global seaborne coal market in 2022.

Export volumes from Indonesia were relatively depressed during 2020 and 2021, due to disruption from Covid-19 and from government policies favouring domestic consumption, but bounced back to an all-time record high in 2022. Total seaborne coal exports from Indonesia in the 12 months of 2022 reached 388.9 mln tonnes, according to Refinitiv vessel tracking data. This was up +21.2% y-o-y from the 321.0 mln tonnes of 2021, and +25.4% from the low of 310.3 mln t in 2020, and also +0.7% from the previous record 386.2 mln t in 2019. The vast majority of Indonesian coal exports are loaded in East Kalimantan and South Kalimantan (the island of Borneo), with some volumes also shipped from southern Sumatra island. The main coal export terminals in Indonesia are: Taboneo/ Banjarmasin (78.1 mln t loaded in Jan-Dec 2022), Samarinda (74.2 mln t), Tanjung Bara (32.1 mln t), Muara Banyu Asin (30.7 mln t), Balikpapan (27.5 mln t), Bunati (25.6 mln t), Adang Bay (21.8 mln t), Muara Pantai (21.4 mln t), Pulau Laut (15.3 mln t), Tarakan Island (9.7 mln t), Muara Satui (8.5 mln t), Bontang (8.1 mln t), Tarahan (8.0 mln t), Tanjung Pemancingan (7.8 mln t), Muara Sabak (5.9 mln t).

The majority (57%) of coal volumes from Indonesia are loaded on Panamax vessels, with 26% on Supramax/Handymax vessels, and just 15% on Capesize tonnage. Trade patterns for Indonesian coal exports saw significant shifts in recent years. China continues to be the top importer of Indonesian seaborne coal exports, accounting for 36.9% of shipments In Jan-Dec 2022, shipments from Indonesia to China were flat +0.0% at 143.5 mln tonnes, following a huge +38.6% y-o-y jump in 2021. About 22.6% of exports, or 88.0 mln tonnes in 2022, were shipped to India, representing an increase of +78.9% y-o-y from 49.2 mln tonnes in 2021, but still below the 91.5 mln tonnes shipped to India in 2019. Shipments to Japan have also rebounded, increasing by +19.2% y-o-y to 25.5 mln tonnes, with Japan accounting for 7% of Indonesian exports. The Philippines imported 23.9 mln tonnes of coal, up +27.2% y-o-y, with the Philippines accounting for 6%. Exports to South Korea increased as well by +27.8% y-o-y to 23.9 mln t.

Malaysia received 19.5 mln tonnes, an increase of +8.5% y-o-y. Last but not least, exports to Taiwan increased +16.3% y-o-y to 15.3 mln t.

# CAPESIZE MARKET

## ATLANTIC AND PACIFIC BASIN

The dry bulk market started the New Year with a sharp decline. As usual, dry bulk rates are softer at start the year and the decline is not a surprise as seasonality typically take shape during the first several weeks of the year. In view of the above, the decline in the first week of the year appears to be no surprise to the market due to the lack of activity and the seasonal effect, both being seen since the holiday season. The average rate of the Capesize 5TC routes had its sharpest fall on Tuesday, losing almost 30% from the last publishing day 10 days before. By the end of the week the market continued falling but at a slower pace closing the week at $12,543/d. Interesting to note that compared with the same period last year, the 5TC is about $7,000/d or 35% lower in value. The Atlantic market remained quiet and in the Pacific the West Australia to Qingdao trade recorded freights below mid $7s/mt during the week.

The market is expecting fresh demand this week which will likely determine in which direction Capesize rates are going to move before the forthcoming holidays of Lunar New Year. In Pacific, Rio Tinto fixed a Capesize to lift their 170,000 mt +/- 10% iron ore from Port Dampier to China at $7.10/mt for a laycan 22/24 January. In Atlantic, there was a bit more demand from both Brazil and West Africa. Classic was looking for a Capesize for their cargo of 180,000 mt +/- 10% iron ore from Itaguai to Qingdao for a laycan from 6 February onwards, but no fixture was reported. Swissmarine fixed a Capesize to lift a 170,000 mt +/- 10% iron ore stem from Nouadhibou to Qingdao for laycan 19/28 January at a reported freight rate of $19.25/mt. The freight rate for 170,000 mt +/- 10% iron ore from Tubarão, Brazil to Qingdao, China was then assessed at a level of $18.25/mt. Limited activity from S Africa although there was reference to an end January fixture at low $14s/mt level on the Saldanha Bay to Qingdao iron ore route.

The freight rate for a Capesize to move 170,000 mt +/- 10% iron ore from South Africa to China was then assessed at $14.00/mt.

# PANAMAX MARKET

## ATLANTIC BASIN

The first week of the new year was characterized by scarce activity due to a lack of demand. Spot market rates kept the negative trend of the end of 2022, but with a positive turn regarding FFA especially in Q2. In N Atlantic the activity was on a slightly negative trend. A nice Kamsarmax open Amsterdam was fixed for a single TCT via USG to Riga, redely Skaw around $16,600/d. An 82,000 dwt built 2019 open Gibraltar got $15,000/d for a TCT with Bauxite from Kamsar with Stade redelivery. An old Panamax was fixed for very low teens for TA. One of the highest fixtures reported this week was a modern Kamsarmax fixed at $23,000/d for a fronthaul from US EC. ECSAm fixtures were mainly done on aps basis + ballast bonus. Fancy Kamsarmaxes were able to get in the region of $16,250/d + 500/600,000 bb on fronthaul whilst older tonnage was getting $14,5/15,000/d. Some activity on dop basis was also reported and a 2006 Panamax open in India was fixed at $9,750/d for an ECSAm FH redely Spore/Jpn range.

## PACIFIC BASIN

The market has been very quiet all over the week. Demand for tonnage was very low and consequently rates fell very fast, especially from Indonesia the export volumes of coal were extremely low with very little enquiries quoted in the market, Tongli has been reported to have fixed a local Chinese vessel in the $4,000/d basis dely S China for an Indo RV. In Australia the main driver was the coal from the EC to India which was fixed around $8,000/d on Kamsarmax. Other fixtures were reported for Panamax/Kamsarmax at $7,000/d for E Australia-Japan and $9,000/d for E Australia-Taiwan. On NoPac grain exports, Dreyfus was reporetd to have fixed a Kamsarmax around $8,000/d basis dely Tianjin.

# SUPRAMAX & HANDYSIZE MARKET

## US GULF / NORTH AMERICA

With the holiday season a limited number of fresh enquiries emerged whilst the list of prompt tonnage got longer pushing rates South. An Handymax was fixed at $19,000/d with coal from Venezuela to Ireland and a 57,000 dwt achieved $18,000/d from USG to Med. A 61,000 dwt was fixed at $25,800/d basis dely Mobile to Spore/Jpn with petcoke and a Supramax achieved mid $21,000s/d with petcoke to Japan.

## EAST COAST SOUTH AMERICA

The market was slow and weak during the whole week. A 28,000 dwt was fixed basis dely N Brazil for a TCT to Morocco at $9,500/d. A nice and modern 32,000 dwt was fixed basis dely N Brazil for a TCT to E Med at $11,000/d. On Supramax there was just a shallow/modern 55,000 dwt fixed for a TCT with grains to Lisbon, but no further details emerged, rumors were around $16/17,000/d aps.

## NORTH EUROPE / CONTINENT

A very quiet market in N Sea during the festivities. During the first week of the year a Supramax was fixed on a TCT from Poland to USG at $7,000/d and an Handy from Baltic to Morocco got $8,000/d with sulphur. The general sentiment remains fairly depressed due to a tonnage list still too rich compared to the amount of cargoes available.

## BLACK SEA / MEDITERRANEAN

The BSea market kept a slow but inexorable decline mainly due to a lack of cargoes. After the holidays new, fresh cargoes appeared, but the increasing number of ship available pushed rates further down. Handysize 35/38,000 dwt to F East were getting between $12/13,000/d, TA trips to USG dropped to $8,500/d, to ECSAm to $7,000/d. The trip Canakkale via BSea to Cont went to $7,500/d and CrossMed to $7,000/d. Supramax and Ultramax, as usual, followed and dely Canakkale via BSea to Cont was fixed at $9/10,000/d, CrossMed around $9,500/d. Trips to USG went down to $9,500/d and to ECSAm to $8,500/d. The trip to F East was fixed around $16,000/d on Supramax and some $500/d more on Ultramax.
