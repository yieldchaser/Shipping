# COMMENT MARKET REPORT – WEEK 27/2026

# WEST AFRICA CRUDE OIL EXPORTS

After a marginal decline in 2024, when global crude oil loadings decreased by -0.2% y-o-y, things picked up in 2025, with full-year volumes increasing by +1.8% y-o-y. In Jan-Jun 2026, however, global crude oil loadings declined by -6.1% y-o-y to 1,016.4 mln tonnes, excluding all cabotage trade, according to vessels tracking data from LSEG. Exports from the Arabian Gulf plunged by -30.7% y-o-y to 299.7 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026, and accounted for 29.5% of global seaborne crude trade, down sharply from the historical norm of about 40%, reflecting the ongoing war in the Persian Gulf. Exports from Russian ports (including oil of Kazakh origin) edged up by +3.5% y-o-y to 117.1 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026, or 11.5% of global trade. From South America, exports surged by +31.5% y-o-y to 137.6 mln t. From the USA, exports increased by +20.5% y-o-y to 107.3 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026. From West Africa, exports decreased by -10.4% y-o-y to 78.2 mln t.

From ASEAN exports jumped by +14.7% y-o-y to 59.8 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026 (this includes trans-shipped Iranian, Russian cargoes). In terms of demand (measured by arrival at discharge ports), the top seaborne importer of crude oil in Jan-Jun 2026 was Mainland China, accounting for 20.5% of global trade. Volumes into China decreased by -14.2% y-o-y to 209.5 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026, from 244.1 mln t in Jan-Jun 2025. Imports into the EU27 up by +1.7% y-o-y to 227.6 mln t, accounting for 22.3% of global trade. To ASEAN, imports decreased by -9.7% y-o-y to 129.8 mln t. Imports to India declined by -0.3% y-o-y to 119.3 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026. To South Korea, imports declined by -18.5% y-o-y to 56.7 mln t. To Japan, imports fell by -22.5% y-o-y to 44.1 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026. Imports into the USA up by +8.9% y-o-y to 62.8 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026. **West Africa** as a region is now the fifth largest exporter of crude oil in the world, after the Arabian Gulf, Russia, South America, and the United States. It accounted for 7.9% of global crude oil exports in 2025.

Total crude oil loadings from West Africa in the 12 months of 2025 increased by +2.0% y-o-y to 175.2 million tonnes, according to revised vessels tracking data from LSEG. The trend had been negative for many years, and after a brief rebound in 2025 it has resumed. Exports from W. Africa decreased by -10.4% y-o-y in Jan-Jun 2026 to 78.2 mln t, from 87.3 mln t in Jan-Jun 2025. In terms of individual countries, the biggest exporters in the region are Nigeria and Angola. Nigeria exported 28.3 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026, down -13.1% y-o-y. Angola exported 24.7 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026, down -3.3% y-o-y. Other major crude oil exporters in the region are Congo-Brazzaville with 6.1 mln t in Jan-Jun 2026 (-2.5% y-o-y), Cameroon 4.9 mln t (+3.1% y-o-y), Gabon 4.5 mln t (-18.4% y-o-y), Senegal 2.6 mln t (-10.2% y-o-y), Ghana 2.1 mln t (-21.5% y-o-y), Benin 2.0 mln t (+17.4% y-o-y), Cote D'Ivoire 1.3 mln t (-51.7% y-o-y), Eq. Guinea 0.9 mln t (-38.0% y-o-y).

West African crude oil exports are split almost evenly between the two largest vessel classes: 49% loaded on Suezmaxes and 48% on VLCCs in Jan-Jun 2026, with 2% on Aframaxes. Trade patterns for West African crude oil exports tend to be quite long haul, with the majority of volumes going to Asia and Europe. Mainland China and the EU are now neck-and-neck as the top destination. The EU accounted for 25.5% of volumes shipped from West Africa in Jan-Jun 2026, marginally ahead of China at 25.0%. Exports from West Africa to China fell by -30.9% y-o-y in Jan-Jun 2026 to 19.6 mln tonnes — the lowest first-half volume in the record and well below the 36.3 mln t of Jan-Jun 2020. Shipments from West Africa to the EU declined by -16.1% y-o-y in Jan-Jun 2026 to 19.9 mln t. Another 14.6% of shipments were sent to the ASEAN region, 11.7% to India, and 2.6% to the USA.

# COMMODITY NEWS – DRY BULK

## **China restricts some Fortescue iron ore cargoes as talks drag**

China's state iron ore buyer has asked some domestic steel mills not to take delivery of certain portside iron ore products from Fortescue, industry sources said, the latest Australian miner to fall foul of Beijing's push to increase control over the market. China Mineral Resources Group (CMRG) notified some mills verbally that from July 15 they must not take delivery of portside cargoes of Fortescue's Super Special Fines and Fortune Fines, both of which are lower-grade iron ore products, five sources with knowledge of the matter said.

## **South32 to sell bulk of aluminium portfolio to Alcoa for up to $5.6 billion**

Australia's South32 said it has agreed to sell most of its aluminium assets to Alcoa for an implied enterprise value of up to $5.6 billion, as the diversified miner streamlines its business to focus on copper under a new CEO. For Alcoa, the deal expands access to upstream assets including bauxite, alumina and aluminium assets across Brazil, South Africa, and Western Australia where it can harness its proximity to South32, given both operate alumina refineries within a few hundred kilometres of each other.

## **Australia flags risk to iron ore price from China state buyer**

An Australian government report said efforts by China's state-backed iron ore buyer to cut costs for Chinese steel mills may push down prices in the medium term, in a rare acknowledgment that such activity could affect earnings. Australia is the largest exporter of iron ore, which it counts as its single biggest export-earner and a significant contributor to government revenue.

## **EU unveils new steel import quotas to protect its industry from overcapacity**

The European Commission unveiled quotas under the new system to limit duty-free steel imports into the EU, in a move designed to protect the bloc's steel sector and increase its capacity utilisation. Under the new rules, the European Union's annual tariff-free import quotas are slashed by **47%** to 18.3 million metric tons, while an out-of-quota duty of 50% is introduced for 26 categories of steel products imported into the EU.

## **Coal miners caught between India's surging demand and Australia's taxes**

Miners of coking coal in top exporter Australia face the dilemma of balancing expectations of strong demand growth from top buyer India against a royalty tax system they view as rapacious and punishing to investment. The high-quality fuel, also known as metallurgical coal, is used to make steel and Australia supplies around half the global seaborne market, with exports of 148.4 million metric tons in 2025.

## **China buys at least 5 cargoes of US soybeans for Sept-Nov shipment, traders say**

China's state-owned trader COFCO bought at least five cargoes, or at least 300,000 metric tons, of U.S. soybeans on Monday for shipment between September and November, two U.S. traders with knowledge of the deals said. The purchase volume could be even larger as more deals are finalized, the traders said. One of the traders estimated the purchase volume as high as 10 cargoes, or about 600,000 tons, with shipments from both U.S. Gulf Coast and Pacific Northwest ports.

## **Drought threatens French sugar output with no rain in sight**

A prolonged drought is threatening sugar output in France, the European Union's largest producer, with no rain forecast in key beet regions over the next two weeks, growers said, as concerns over European crops fuel a rally in sugar prices. White sugar has gained nearly 10% in the past week, hitting a 9-1/2-month high on Wednesday, also supported by the El Nino weather pattern in Asia. Prices had slumped to a more than five-year low earlier this year on hefty supplies, hurting sugar makers' profits.

## **StoneX lifts Brazil's 2025/26 second corn crop estimate**

The second corn crop in Brazil is seen at 107.5 million metric tons for the 2025/2026 season, consultancy firm StoneX said on Wednesday, increasing its estimate by 1.4% from a previous June outlook. The second corn crop, which is being harvested and represents the bulk of Brazil's production, is planted after soybeans are harvested in the same fields.

## **US farmers plant 3% fewer corn acres in 2026, 5% more soybean acres, USDA says**

U.S. farmers planted 3% fewer corn acres this spring than a year earlier, but the acreage is still the fourth-largest in 82 years despite concerns about soaring fertilizer costs, government data showed on Tuesday. Grain growers have struggled financially for the past four years due to low crop prices and high costs for inputs, which worsened as the Iran war drove up fuel and fertilizer costs as U.S. plantings began. ## **OPEC+ approves further oil output increase as Hormuz exports start to recover**

OPEC+ has agreed a further increase in output targets from August, the group said in a statement on Sunday, adding to global supply at a time when oil prices are falling due to the gradual reopening of the Strait of Hormuz for oil exports. The oil-producing group agreed during an online meeting to increase quotas by 188,000 barrels per day from August, on top of similar increases for June and July.

## **UAE crude output nears record following OPEC exit, sources say**

The United Arab Emirates raised its crude output to near record highs above 3.8 million barrels per day in June after it quit OPEC to escape production caps, two sources familiar with production data said on Monday. June's output was the highest since April 2020, according to Reuters estimates, exceeding levels seen before the Iran war and providing an early vindication of the UAE's decision to leave OPEC and OPEC+ on May 1 to free production from quota restrictions.

## **Saudi oil price cut unlikely to convince sated Asia buyers, traders say**

The biggest price cut in more than two decades for Saudi Arabian crude oil sold to Asia still leaves the grade more costly to lift than some rival Gulf supplies, curbing appetite for oil from the OPEC linchpin. The world's top exporter slashed the August official selling price (OSP) on Monday for its flagship Arab Light crude to $1.50 a barrel below the average of Oman and Dubai quotes for Asia, down $11 from the previous month. It also cut the OSPs for its other four grades by $11 a barrel.

## **Kuwait sharply boosts crude production in June after US-Iran deal, source says**

Kuwait's crude oil production rose sharply to 1.65 million barrels per day in June from 580,000 bpd in May, a source familiar with the matter told Reuters on Thursday, as the OPEC member boosts exports through the Gulf following the U.S.-Iran interim peace agreement. The jump in Kuwaiti output adds to signs that Gulf oil flows through the Strait of Hormuz are recovering rapidly following disruption caused by the Iran war, with stranded cargoes gradually clearing the Strait of Hormuz and exporters restoring production.

## **Russia's Urals crude prices in India hit 4-month lows on ample supply, rising competition, traders say**

Price differentials for Russia's Urals crude delivered to India in August have fallen to their lowest levels since late February amid ample supply and rising competition, three trade sources told Reuters on Wednesday. They said discounts for August Urals cargoes delivered to Indian ports on a DES basis had widened to below $7 per barrel versus the dated Brent benchmark, from about $4 per barrel just 10 days ago.

## **No Brent crude oil cargoes set to load in August, a first for global price benchmark**

No cargoes of North Sea Brent crude oil are scheduled to load in August as production steadily declines, traders told Reuters on Tuesday, marking a milestone for the global Dated Brent oil benchmark, which prices over 60% of the world's oil. "What is left of Brent is just a brand name of the most important crude oil contract in the world," said Adi Imsirovic, a Brent expert and veteran oil trader. "At some stage, Brent crude will disappear, but the contract is likely to remain for many years to come."

## **Iran exploring oil sales to Japan, buyers seek longer sanctions waiver, sources say**

Iran has begun talks with Japanese companies under a U.S. sanctions waiver allowing it to resume oil sales, though prospective buyers are seeking a longer waiver and reassurances about ship safety, three Iranian and Western sources said. The waiver, part of 60-day peace talks between Tehran and Washington, was issued on June 22 and expires August 21.

## **Canada, Alberta announce new west coast oil pipeline to boost exports**

Canada announced plans to build a new oil pipeline from Alberta to the Pacific coast, which would give the world's fourth-largest oil producer greater capacity to export to Asia and ease its reliance on the United States. Prime Minister Mark Carney made the announcement in Calgary alongside Alberta Premier Danielle Smith, whose government said construction of the 1-million-barrel-per-day pipeline would begin as early as September 2027.

## **US LNG exports to Europe decline as Asia prices surge**

For the first time in nearly two years, less than half of U.S. LNG exports last month went to Europe as stronger prices in Asia and record imports by Egypt diverted cargoes, according to preliminary ship-tracking data from LSEG. # CAPESIZE MARKET

## ATLANTIC AND PACIFIC BASIN

The Capesize market staged a firm recovery over the week, reversing the pronounced weakness that had characterised the closing sessions of June. Sentiment opened on a bearish footing, with rates grinding to multi-week lows before a sustained rebound took hold from midweek and gathered momentum into the close. On the key Western Australia–Qingdao iron ore run (170,000 mt, plus/minus 10%), the assessed rate slipped to a trough of USD 10.10/wmt on 29 June amid a severe shortage of fresh Pacific cargoes and forward-dated laycans that left charterers under no pressure to cover. From 1 July, however, the active return of iron ore miners across both basins tightened prompt tonnage demand and handed owners greater leverage to resist low bids. The rate climbed successively to USD 10.75, USD 11.45 and finally USD 12.25/wmt on 3 July, a weekly gain of USD 2.15/wmt, underpinned by rising freight derivative values. The South Atlantic told a similar story. The Tubarao–Qingdao route held at USD 27.80/wmt on 29 June, weighed by a lengthening ballaster list for second-half July loading, before firmer sentiment and replenished cargo volumes lifted it to USD 28.70, USD 29.45 and USD 29.95/wmt by week's end, also up USD 2.15/wmt.

Owners increasingly held off fixing in anticipation of further gains, while expectations of additional Brazilian liftings under contracts of affreightment for August lent further support. South Africa lagged the recovery amid persistently thin activity, though the Saldanha Bay–Qingdao route still edged higher from USD 20.45 to USD 21.20/wmt. The week thus closed on a distinctly bullish note across both basins, with tightening prompt demand, owner resistance to low bids and firming paper values pointing to continued positive momentum into the following week.

# PANAMAX MARKET

## ATLANTIC BASIN

The Atlantic market remained relatively active throughout the week, with East Coast South America (ECSA) continuing to provide the majority of fresh employment and supporting freight levels despite a moderate flow of reported fixtures. Demand for grain cargoes remained firm, allowing owners to maintain a positive stance, particularly for modern Kamsarmax tonnage. Early in the week, an 82,000 dwt vessel (built 2012) was fixed APS ECSA for a voyage to Singapore/Japan range at USD 20,500 per day plus a USD 1.05 million ballast bonus. An 82,000 dwt (built 2023) was also reported fixed from Tuticorin via ECSA to the Far East at USD 24,500 per day, whilst a 75,000 dwt (built 2012), open Ennore, was fixed via ECSA for Far East redelivery. A further 82,000 dwt (built 2022), open Pecem, secured USD 31,000 per day for a North Coast South America transatlantic voyage, reflecting the premium still available for prompt Atlantic positions. Towards the latter half of the week, activity broadened across North and South Atlantic routes.

An 82,000 dwt (built 2015), open Eemshaven, fixed a US Gulf– Skaw/Gibraltar grain voyage at USD 22,750 per day. A 75,000 dwt (built 2004), ETA Suez, was reportedly fixed for a Ukrainian loading to India at low USD 30,000s, basis delivery Port Said. A further 82,000 dwt (built 2012) was heard on subjects for a transatlantic voyage from Haldia, whilst market talk indicated an 82,000 dwt (built 2012) sailing from Mumbai fixed an ECSA grain voyage at around USD 20,000 per day, with additional prompt Indian tonnage securing South Atlantic employment.

## PACIFIC BASIN

Sentiment across the Asia-Pacific Panamax market was broadly rangebound to firmer through the week, though persistently ample tonnage and thin fresh cargo replenishment capped gains. Trading opened sluggishly on Monday 29 June amid short cargo lists, before rates edged higher around 1 July as Pacific sentiment drew support from a firm South Atlantic market and rising freight derivative values, with tonnage tightening as owners ballasted towards the east coast South America trade. Momentum faded into 3 July as the ECSA market appeared to hit a ceiling and activity thinned ahead of the week's close. On the key Australian coking coal routes, Hay Point–Qingdao firmed to USD 16.90/mt by 3 July, up from USD 16.05/mt the preceding Friday, while Hay Point–Paradip held around USD 18.90/mt. The Indonesian thermal coal trade also gained, Samarinda–Guangzhou rising to USD 8.00/mt from USD 7.65/mt. Fixtures included an 80,000 mt Dalrymple Bay–Visakhapatnam metallurgical coal cargo at USD 19.80/mt. Charterers maintained pressure on owners throughout, and participants retreated to the sidelines awaiting clearer direction into the following week.

Bunker prices were volatile, ending softer.

# SUPRAMAX & HANDYSIZE MARKET

## MEG / INDIAN OCEAN

Sentiment across the Indian Ocean basin stayed subdued throughout the week, with sparse fresh demand and thin fixing activity weighing on rates for cargoes originating from the Middle East and Africa. Out of the Persian Gulf, enquiry remained muted. The bellwether limestone run from Mina Saqr to Paradip softened steadily, easing to USD 16.95/mt by 3 July from USD 18.85/mt a week earlier — a fall of some USD 1.90/mt. Out of South Africa, activity slowed on a dearth of fresh July requirements, though the occasional enquiry surfaced, including a Richards Bay/Bangladesh coal stem for 21–30 July laycan. Coal freight from Richards Bay Coal Terminal to Port Qasim traded choppy but closed softer, at USD 21.75/mt on 3 July against USD 22.10/mt at the open. On the east coast of India, tight prompt tonnage lent rates a firmer floor mid-week, yet owners struggled to convert this into gains amid the wider malaise. The basin ended the week on a soft footing, participants awaiting a demand-led recovery.

The Pacific Ultramax and Supramax market remained slow with very few fixtures reported.
