# COMMENT MARKET REPORT – WEEK 28/2026

# EUROPEAN UNION LNG IMPORTS

Global seaborne LNG trade was increasing sharply until 2022, helped also by the events in Ukraine which forced Europe to diversify away from Russian pipeline gas. In Jan-Dec 2024, however, global shipments of LNG increased by just +0.2% y-o-y to 414.8 mln t, based on LSEG vessel tracking data. In Jan-Dec 2025 there was a strong rebound, with shipment volumes rising +6.0% y-o-y to 439.5 mln t. In 1H 2026, global exports edged up +1.3% y-o-y to 215.8 mln t, from 213.0 mln t in 1H 2025. The largest exporter of LNG is now the USA, which accounted for 29.6% of shipments in 1H 2026, followed by Australia with 18.5%, South-East Asia with 11.3%, and Russia with 8.4%. Qatar, hit by the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, saw its share drop to just 7.2%. In Jan-Jun 2026, the USA exported 63.8 mln tonnes of LNG, which represented a +23.1% y-o-y increase from 51.8 mln t shipped in 1H 2025.

Qatar exported just 15.6 mln tonnes in Jan-Jun 2026, down -62.2% y-o-y from 41.3 mln t a year earlier, as the closure of the Strait of Hormuz halted the bulk of Qatari shipments from March 2026 onwards. Australia shipped 40.0 mln tonnes in Jan-Jun 2026, up +1.2% y-o-y. From South East Asia shipments increased +9.1% y-o-y to 24.4 mln t. Russia shipped 18.2 mln tonnes of LNG in 1H 2026, up +20.3% y-o-y from 15.1 mln t in 1H 2025.

The European Union remains the world's largest importer of LNG, accounting for 24.3% of global LNG imports. In 1H 2026, the EU imported 52.5 mln t, down -1.7% y-o-y from the record 53.4 mln t in 1H 2025, but still above the 51.6 mln t in 1H 2023. The United Kingdom imported 6.1 mln tonnes of LNG in 1H 2026, up +7.2% y-o-y from the 5.7 mln t in 1H 2025, but still well below the 10.8 mln imported in 1H 2023. China imported 29.9 mln tonnes of LNG in 1H 2026, down -2.3% y-o-y from 30.6 mln t in Jan-Jun 2025. Japan imported 31.9 mln t in 1H 2026, down -4.4% y-o-y. South Korea imported 23.6 mln t in 1H 2026, down -4.0% y-o-y. India imported 11.9 mln t in 1H 2026, down -1.1% y-o-y. Let's now focus a bit more on Europe. In Jan-Dec 2024, imports into the EU corrected downwards by -18.0% y-o-y to 83.6 mln t.

In Jan-Dec 2025, imports into the EU rebounded strongly, up +25.3% y-o-y to 104.8 mln t. This was a new record high, above the 100.1 mln t imported in 2022 and well above the 59.7 mln t imported in 2021. As already mentioned above, in the first half of 2026, imports into the EU came in at 52.5 mln t, down -1.7% y-o-y from the record 53.4 mln t in 1H 2025. Top LNG discharge ports in the European Union in 2025 were: Rotterdam (11.7 mln t in Jan-Dec 2025), Zeebrugge (9.8 mln t), Dunkirk (9.4 mln t), Swinoujscie (6.3 mln t), Fos (6.2 mln t), Montoir (5.5 mln t), Porto Levante / Adriatic LNG (5.4 mln t), Eemshaven (4.6 mln t), Bilbao (3.7 mln t), Wilhelmshaven (3.6 mln t), Sines (3.3 mln t), Leghorn (3.2 mln t), Piombino (3.1 mln t).

In terms of sources for European LNG imports, the United States now account for a massive 59.3% share. In Jan-Jun 2026, the European Union imported 31.1 mln tonnes of LNG from the United States, up +5.0% y-o-y from the 29.6 mln tonnes in the first half of 2025. Shipments from Russia to the European Union increased by +12.3% y-o-y in 1H 2026 to 9.0 mln t from 8.0 mln t in 1H 2025. Nevertheless, volumes from Russia to Europe remain historically relatively steady, as they are above the 8.8 mln t in 1H 2024 and the 8.7 mln t in 1H 2022. Russia now accounts for 17.1% of the EU's total LNG imports. Imports from West Africa fell back by -12.5% in 1H 2026 to 4.8 mln t, though still above the 2.6 mln t low of 1H 2024.

# COMMODITY NEWS – DRY BULK

## **Aluminium supply shock revives long-idled Western smelters**

The supply shock caused by the Iran war is sparking old Western aluminium smelters back into life. The restarts are laden with political significance for Washington and Brussels, both equally desperate to reduce import dependency on a metal that is ubiquitous across a diverse range of industrial sectors.

## **Morgan Stanley expects smaller 2026 deficit for aluminium**

Morgan Stanley expects aluminium supply deficit to fall in 2026, and said the market is likely to move into a surplus from 2027. The bank now forecasts a smaller aluminum deficit of 1.1 million tons in 2026 versus the previous forecast of 1.8 million tons.

## **China's aluminium wire exports set to slow as tax-driven arbitrage window closes**

An unusual surge in China's exports of aluminium stranded wire looks set to unwind, industry sources say, as falling global aluminium prices erode the profitability of a trade that flourished during the supply shock triggered by the Iran war. Aluminium buyers across Asia and other emerging markets have been importing stranded wire from China to remelt into ingots or process into rods and alloys, while traders and Chinese producers have capitalised on a tax advantage and fears of supply shortages after the Iran war disrupted shipping through the Strait of Hormuz and damaged aluminium facilities in the Gulf, which accounts for about 9% of global supply.

## **China buys more US soybean cargoes toward 25 million-ton goal**

China bought at least five more cargoes of U.S. soybeans overnight, traders said on Wednesday, as Beijing steps up purchases to meet a White House-announced target of 25 million metric tons of U.S. beans annually. State grain trader COFCO bought the cargoes for September and October shipment at around $2.70-$2.80 per bushel over November Chicago Board of Trade soybean futures, three Asian traders told Reuters.

## ****Argentine farmers hold back soy sales despite higher prices, slowing dollar inflows****

Argentine farmers are holding on to their soybeans despite a rise in international prices, limiting a key source of foreign currency for the economy and complicating efforts to rebuild central bank reserves, a report showed on Thursday. By late June, producers had sold only about 40% of the 2025/26 soybean harvest, the lowest pace in a decade, while just 21% of the crop had been priced, the note by ActivTrades said.

## **USDA forecasts smallest wheat harvest since 1970**

The U.S. Department of Agriculture on Friday lowered its U.S. wheat production estimate in a closely watched monthly supply-and-demand report, after the agency last month projected the smallest harvested wheat area since 1877. The USDA lowered U.S. 2026/2027 wheat production to 1.536 billion bushels, above an average of analyst estimates for 1.525 billion bushels and below its June estimate of 1.543 billion bushels.

## **Russia halts Don-Azov channel shipping, affecting grain trade, after Ukraine attacks**

Russia temporarily stopped shipping through the Don-Azov Channel, a navigable waterway linking the Don River with the Sea of Azov, three grain export industry sources said on Friday. The move followed a Ukrainian attack on 13 Russian vessels in the Sea of Azov on Friday, including 10 tankers. Market analysts note that up to one-quarter of wheat exports from Russia, the world's largest exporter of the grain, pass through the Sea of Azov.

## ****Grain exports from Rouen port surge above 10-year average****

Cereal shipments from Rouen, France's main grain export hub, rose sharply in the season that ended on June 30, making it one of the best campaigns in the port's history and above the 10-year average, the port's operator said on Friday. The rebound followed a weak 2024/25 season linked to France's poor harvest, Haropa port said in a statement.

## ****EU soft wheat exports rise 8% in 2025/26, barley up 72%****

European Union soft wheat exports rose 8% in the 2025/26 season that ended on June 30 to 23.42 million metric tons, with Romania and France the biggest exporters and Morocco and Nigeria the top destinations, European Commission data showed on Tuesday. Romania exported 7.29 million tons of soft wheat in 2025/26, followed by France with 5.56 million, Poland with 3.28 million, Germany with 2.34 million and Lithuania with 2.28 million, the data showed.

## ****China's wheat output rises 0.6% despite flood risks across key crop areas****

China's winter wheat production rose 0.6% this year, official data showed on Friday, despite flooding challenges in several major agricultural regions in the country. Output from the world's top wheat grower increased to 138.95 million tons, up from 138.16 million metric tons a year earlier, data from the National Bureau of Statistics showed. # COMMODITY NEWS – CRUDE OIL & GAS

## **US reinstates sanctions on Iranian oil sales after LNG, oil tanker attacks**

The United States on Tuesday re-imposed sanctions on Iranian oil, as a U.S. official warned that Iran's attacks on vessels in the Strait of Hormuz were "wholly unacceptable" and would be met with consequences. Oil prices were up more than 5% following the announcement. The U.S. Treasury had authorized last month Iran oil sales until August 21 as part of the fragile agreement between Tehran and Washington. Tuesday's revocation cuts that wind-down period to an end date of July 17.

## **Iran oil stuck at sea surges as China's teapots turn to rival Middle East supplies**

Iranian oil supplies at sea are rising after Tehran ramped up exports during the interim peace deal with the United States, but sales have been slow as China's independent refiners have turned to cheaper crude from Iraq, the UAE and Qatar. The return of U.S. sanctions this week risks leaving Tehran with more cargoes searching for buyers just as shipments arrive in Asia.

## **Hormuz traffic slows to multi-week low as renewed US, Iran strikes raise safety risk**

The number of vessels transiting the Strait of Hormuz fell to multi-week lows on Sunday, shipping data showed, as renewed strikes between the U.S. and Iran and attacks on ships in the Middle East heightened safety concerns. Six vessels transited the strait on Sunday, ship-tracking data from Kpler showed, the lowest number in five weeks.

## **Strategic oil reserve buying set to support crude demand through 2028**

Governments are set to buy millions of barrels of oil through 2028 to rebuild emergency reserves depleted by drawdowns to plug a gap in global supply caused by the U.S.-Israeli war on Iran, analysts and officials said. This could boost demand for crude that would absorb some of the expected global supply surplus following OPEC+'s decision to increase output, they say.

## **US commercial crude stocks build for the first time since mid-April as exports soften**

U.S. crude inventories rose for the first time since mid-April last week as exports soften, while gasoline and distillate inventories fell ahead of the Independence Day weekend, the Energy Information Administration said on Wednesday. Crude inventories rose by 3 million barrels to 411.4 million barrels in the week ending July 3, the EIA said, after falling for 10 straight weeks.

## **Fuel markets flash supply crunch despite calmer oil prices**

Gasoline and diesel markets are signalling a fuel supply crunch despite relatively subdued crude oil prices, suggesting the energy shock from the Iran war may be far from over. Energy prices surged after the U.S.-Israeli war on Iran led to the effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz, which carried about a fifth of global oil supplies before the conflict.

## **China lifts fuel export curbs for July, sources say**

China has lifted refined fuel export restrictions for the rest of July and allowed a private refiner to resume shipments after a four-month halt, trade sources said on Wednesday, as the world's biggest refiner returns towards normal after disruptions from the Iran war. Zhejiang Petrochemical Co, majority owned by Rongsheng Petrochemical, has been permitted to export fuel in July, four sources briefed on the matter said, after it halted exports for more than three months.

## **Russia bans diesel exports to ensure domestic supply after targeted Ukrainian drone strikes**

Russia introduced a ban on diesel exports on Wednesday as part of a raft of measures to support the domestic fuel market after systematic Ukrainian drone attacks on oil refineries triggered gasoline shortages and price spikes. Drivers in many regions are facing hours-long lines to refuel, as intensifying Ukrainian strikes on Russian energy infrastructure squeeze supplies of diesel and gasoline.

## **Asia's LNG imports recover, drawing cargoes from needy Europe**

Asia's imports of liquefied natural gas (LNG) are poised to hit a six-month high in July while Europe's imports plunge to their lowest in nearly two years. While some of the strength in Asia is because of seasonal demand during the summer peak, the weakness in Europe underscores just how far the second-biggest consuming region is falling behind in building inventories for winter demand.

## **Qatari LNG tanker awaits salvage off Oman after projectile strike**

A Qatari LNG tanker remained stranded off Oman on Wednesday after a projectile strike sparked a fire in its engine room, though industry sources said the vessel's cargo appeared secure and the risk of an explosion was low for now. The attacks on the tankers prompted Washington to revoke a licence allowing Iran to sell oil and U.S. forces to strike Iranian targets overnight. Source: Reuters / LSEG

# CAPESIZE MARKET

## ATLANTIC AND PACIFIC BASIN

The Capesize market opened the week on a firm footing and, despite a mid-week lull, closed higher across both basins, with sentiment shaped throughout by tropical weather threats to Chinese discharge ports. Trading began strongly on 6 July, as rates surged in the Pacific and Atlantic amid anticipated vessel delays from typhoon-related disruption in South China and firmer derivative sentiment. The rally extended into 7 July on robust tonnage demand from Western Australian iron ore majors and higher South Atlantic fixtures. Momentum then cooled on 8 July, when rates eased amid lacklustre forward freight agreement (FFA) trading and growing caution over the approaching Typhoon Bavi; several South China ports, including Zhoushan and Xiamen, were closed in the wake of Typhoon Maysak. A modest rebound followed on 9 July on firmer Pacific miner demand, before activity turned subdued on 10 July, with rates flat to marginally higher as charterers front-loaded fixtures ahead of Bavi's expected weekend landfall. On the key Pacific run, the Western Australia–Qingdao (C5) iron ore route firmed from USD 12.65/wmt on 6 July to a mid-week peak of USD 13.30/wmt, settling at USD 13.15/wmt by 10 July.

In the Atlantic, the Tubarao–Qingdao (C3) run advanced, opening at USD 31.50/wmt and climbing to USD 32.95/wmt by week's end, underpinned by a well-supported South Atlantic cargo list and firmer overnight Brazil fixtures reaching around USD 32.75/wmt. North Atlantic activity was comparatively muted, though an indicative offer of USD 42/wmt was heard on Sept-Îles–Qingdao late in the week. South African business also strengthened, with the Saldanha Bay–Qingdao run rising from USD 22.30/wmt to USD 23.85/wmt; one mining major covered the route with a Newcastlemax fixture. Overall, the week delivered net gains on all three benchmark iron ore routes, with typhoon-driven scheduling risk the dominant theme underpinning owners' resolve.

# PANAMAX MARKET

## ATLANTIC BASIN

The Atlantic market remained reasonably active throughout the week, with ECSA continuing to generate the bulk of fresh employment and to underpin freight levels despite a moderate flow of reported fixtures. Sentiment held firm, with grain demand steady, allowing owners to maintain a positive stance and hold previous rate levels across most South American fronthaul business, particularly for modern Kamsarmax tonnage. Early in the week, an 81,000 dwt built 2014, opening Hazira, was fixed via ECSA with redelivery Singapore/Japan at USD 22,000 per day. An 83,000 dwt built 2010, opening Singapore, followed at USD 21,000 per day for the same run, while a 75,000 dwt built 2012, retro Maoming, secured USD 19,000 per day via ECSA to Singapore/Japan. Later, an 81,000 dwt built 2013, opening Krishnapatnam, fixed at USD 18,300 per day; an 82,000 dwt built 2011 achieved USD 21,250 per day; and a modern 82,000 dwt built 2022 obtained USD 23,000 per day, all for ECSA grain voyages redelivering Singapore/Japan.

North Atlantic activity was more limited, with few cargoes reported. Notably, a 60,000 dwt vessel was covered for a Santos-Gdynia grain shipment at USD 30.90 per mt, indicating Atlantic grain exports into Europe remain active despite quieter volumes.

## PACIFIC BASIN

The Pacific market remained constructive on steady exports from Indonesia, NOPAC and Australia. Sentiment was firm, though performance varied by loading area, destination and positioning. In Indonesia, coal held steady, with a wide return spread. A 79,105 dwt 2011-built fixed USD 11,000 for South China; a 72,893 dwt 1999-built and 76,022 dwt 2012-built both USD 13,000 for South China; and a 75,414 dwt 2012-built USD 15,000 for the Philippines. NOPAC grain remained supportive: an 82,012 dwt 2018-built fixed USD 17,250 for Singapore-Japan; an 82,000 dwt 2026-built USD 18,500 for China; an 82,202 dwt 2026-built scrubber-fitted USD 20,000 for Japan; and an 82,561 dwt 2007-built USD 15,250 for Japan. Fixtures indicate a stable market, stronger returns for prompt, well-positioned tonnage. Australia remained the strongest area on coal and iron ore demand. Higher levels: an 82,406 dwt 2023-built USD 19,000 for Japan and an 84,930 dwt 2022-built USD 19,250 for South China. Highest, an 81,999 dwt 2020-built scrubber-fitted, USD 23,000 via Koolan Island to China with iron ore.

The US Gulf market continued its good momentum on both Handysize and larger units. ## EAST COAST SOUTH AMERICA

**The Baltic indices showed a mixed trend.** Supramax routes from WAF via ECSA rose to USD 27,821 (from USD 27,604 on Monday) to North China and to USD 23,350 (from USD 23,075) to Skaw-Passero, while Handies on the Rio-Recalada to Skaw-Passero route dropped from USD 25,094 to USD 24,333. For the bigger sizes, Ultramaxes remained strong: a 63,000-dwt ex-North Brazil and a 63,000-dwt prompt ex-Recalada secured USD 34,000 and USD 33,000 to Med/East Med, while Ultra units open Argentina and Brazil fixed around USD 33,500 and USD 34,500 to Turkiye.

## NORTH EUROPE / CONTINENT

The market showed a positive tick across the board, rates firming on both sizes, though few fresh fixtures were reported this week. On Handies, trips to ECSA and USG/USEC remained the usual discounting destinations for repositioning owners. ECSA rates edged slightly higher, around last week's USD 8,000/day, while USG/USEC pushed more distinctly into the low tens (around USD 10,500/day). Regionally, Cont/Med held last week's levels, but with the Med firming, owners sought little premium that way; Cont/West Africa stayed steady. Cont/USG transatlantic repositioning also gained ground at USD 16,000–17,000/day.

The Mediterranean and Black Sea market had a very positive week; a lack of tonnage and a good flow of cargoes pushed the market up on all routes.
