# Banchero Costa Weekly Market Report - Week 21, 2026

## Weekly Macro Insight: China'S Crude Oil Imports

After a marginal decline in 2024, when global crude oil loadings decreased by -0.2% y-o-y, things picked up in 2025, with full-year volumes increasing by +1.8% y-o-y. In Jan-Apr 2026, however, global crude oil loadings declined by -3.3% y-o-y to 690.1 mln tonnes, excluding all cabotage trade, according to vessels tracking data from LSEG. Exports from the Arabian Gulf plunged by -23.6% y-o-y to 218.8 mln t in Jan-Apr 2026, and accounted for 31.7% of global seaborne crude trade, down sharply from the historical norm of about 40%, reflecting the ongoing war in the Persian Gulf. Exports from Russian ports (including oil of Kazakh origin) declined by -1.5% y-o-y to 73.8 mln t in Jan-Apr 2026, or 10.7% of global trade. From South America, exports surged by +29.1% y-o-y to 89.8 mln t. From the USA, exports increased by +8.9% y-o-y to 66.4 mln t in Jan-Apr 2026. From West Africa, exports decreased by -8.0% y-o-y to 52.5 mln t. From ASEAN exports jumped by +30.9% y-o-y to 42.7 mln t in Jan-Apr 2026 (this inevitably includes trans-shipped Iranian, Russian cargoes). In terms of demand (measured by arrival at discharge ports), the top seaborne importer of crude oil in Jan-Apr 2026 was Mainland China, accounting for 23.1% of global trade.

Volumes into China increased marginally by +1.0% y-o-y to 161.5 mln t in Jan-Apr 2026, from 159.9 mln t in Jan-Apr 2025. Imports into the EU27 increased by +2.0% y-o-y to 150.6 mln t, accounting for 21.6% of global trade. To ASEAN, imports decreased by -7.9% y-o-y to 85.0 mln t. Imports to India declined by -2.9% y-o-y to 79.0 mln t in Jan-Apr 2026. To South Korea, imports declined by -15.6% y-o-y to 37.0 mln t. To Japan, imports fell by -26.6% y-o-y to 28.5 mln t in Jan-Apr 2026. Imports into the USA up by +22.1% y-o-y to 43.3 mln t in Jan-Apr 2026. **Mainland China** is right now the largest importer of crude oil in the world, with a 23.1% share, once again ahead of the European Union's 19.3% share. In Jan-Dec 2025, imports into China declined marginally by -0.3% y-o-y to 506.5 mln tonnes. In Jan–Apr 2026, crude import volumes into China rose marginally by +1.0% y-o-y to 161.5 mln tonnes. About 77.2 percent of volumes discharged in China in Jan–Apr 2026 were carried in VLCCs, about 5.3 percent were carried in Suezmaxes, and 16.8 percent in Aframaxes. Main crude oil import terminals in China are: Dongjiakou (18.3 mln tonnes in Jan–Apr 2026), Ningbo/Zhoushan (15.3 mln t), Lanshan (14.3), Dalian (10.1), Beilun (9.2), Qingdao (8.2), Zhoushan (7.8), Tianjin (7.1), Huizhou (6.7), Zhanjiang (6.6), Longkou (6.4), Yangpu (5.7), Yantai (5.7), Cezi (5.5), Shuidong (5.2), and Quanzhou (5.0). In terms of sources of the shipments, much of China's oil imports arrives from the Middle East.

In Jan-Apr 2026, Russia overtook Saudi Arabia as the single largest exporter to China, accounting for 12.9% of volumes, narrowly ahead of Saudi Arabia at 12.8%. In Jan-Apr 2026, China imported 20.7 mln tonnes of crude oil from Saudi Arabia, down -17.3% y-o-y on the same period of last year. In the same period, imports from Iraq to China decreased by -39.9% y-o-y to 12.7 mln t, and from Kuwait by -59.0% y-o-y to 2.0 mln t. Volumes from the UAE to China decreased by -21.5% y-o-y to 7.5 mln t, and from Oman increased by +10.0% y-o-y to 12.2 mln t. Direct shipments from Russia surged by +49.7% y-o-y in Jan-Apr 2026 to 20.9 mln t. Imports from ASEAN (which includes some trans-shipment of Iranian and Russian oil) increased by +3.5% y-o-y to 19.6 mln t in Jan-Apr 2026, and from West Africa decreased by -6.6% y-o-y to 15.9 mln t. Volumes from Canada rose to 3.9 mln t in Jan-Apr 2026 from 2.9 mln t in the same period in Jan-Apr 2025.

# COMMODITY NEWS – DRY BULK

## **China Coal Imports Surge; Shanxi Mine Accident Risks Further Tightening**
China's seaborne thermal coal imports are running at approximately 22.63 million tons in May, sharply up from 16 million in April and the highest since January, driven by weaker domestic production. April coal output fell to 385.63 million tons from a record 440.62 million in March. A fatal accident at a metallurgical coal mine in Shanxi province — the worst in 17 years with 82 deaths — is expected to prompt safety inspections across thermal and coking coal mines, potentially tightening supply further.

## **Indonesia to Centralise Coal and Palm Oil Exports Through State Enterprise**
Indonesian President Prabowo announced all exports of thermal coal and palm oil must be channelled through a government-selected state enterprise from 1 June, citing concerns about under-invoicing. Indonesia is the world's largest exporter of thermal coal and palm oil. Markets reacted sharply, with Jakarta's stock index dropping 3.5%, as traders feared changes to pricing mechanisms and squeezed margins.

## **US Millers Import Polish Wheat as Domestic Prices Soar**
US importers purchased an estimated 120,000–350,000 metric tons of milling wheat from Poland in recent weeks, drawn by an exceptional price gap: US hard red winter wheat was quoted at $305–$323/t FOB Gulf ports for June shipment, while Polish 12.5% protein wheat was available at just $245–$254/t FOB. Traders confirmed the purchases are for 2025 crop wheat to be shipped June/July to flour mills on the US east coast — the first time in recent memory that US domestic prices have risen high enough to generate European import demand.

## **IGC Forecasts First Global Grains Output Decline in Four Seasons**
The International Grains Council kept its 2026/27 global grains production forecast unchanged at 2.414 billion metric tons, down 3% year-on-year — the first decline in four seasons. The IGC cut its world wheat forecast by 1 million tons to 820 million, while corn was held at 1.3 billion tons. Fertiliser trade disruptions from the Iran war have driven up input costs and reduced crop application rates globally. Global stocks are set to tighten 4% by season-end to around 615 million tons, though they would remain within the five-year average.

## **India Soymeal Exports Set to Halve to Four-Year Low**
India's soymeal exports are forecast to fall to approximately 900,000 metric tons in the 2025/26 marketing year, down from 2.02 million tons last year, after prices surged 47% in a single month. Indian soymeal is now offered at around $680/t FOB versus South American offers at $430/t. The loss of competitiveness has halted new export inquiries from Asian and European buyers who are switching to South American suppliers, redirecting soybean meal shipments from Argentina and Brazil toward Bangladesh, Nepal, Germany and the Netherlands.

## **Active Grain and Feed Tenders Across Asia and North Africa**
A wave of sovereign grain tenders this week signals sustained dry bulk demand. Algeria's OAIC purchased approximately 200,000 tonnes of milling wheat at $284–$292/t C&F for July–September shipment. Jordan issued fresh tenders for 120,000 tonnes of milling wheat (bids due 2 June) and 120,000 tonnes of feed barley. South Korea's FLC tendered for 69,000 tonnes of corn for September arrival, while MFG bought 135,000 tonnes of feed corn at approximately $272/t C&F plus a $1.50/t port surcharge for September–October arrival. Korean buyers had been delaying purchases hoping for a US-Iran peace deal; with no deal in sight, they are now moving.

## **Argentina Cuts Wheat Export Tax; Soy and Corn Reductions Follow**
Argentina will cut its wheat export tax from 7.5% to 5.5% from June. Economy Minister Caputo also confirmed a schedule to reduce soybean export taxes from 24% to 15% by end-2028, with soy oil and meal cut proportionally. Corn export taxes will decline quarterly over the same period. Argentina is the world's largest exporter of soybean oil and meal and the third-largest corn exporter. The progressive reductions are expected to encourage greater planting and unlock additional export volumes — a positive long-term supply signal for commodity traders.

## **Germany Opens Indonesian Wheat Market; Egypt Plans Global Grain Hub**
German wheat exports to Indonesia — which imports around 11 million tons of wheat per year — will be permitted from June following a new phytosanitary agreement, opening a major new outlet for European supply. Separately, Egypt announced plans to develop a global grain trading and storage hub, with modern elevators, processing facilities and pricing benchmarks developed in partnership with Russia, targeting buyers across the Middle East and Africa. Source: Reuters / LSEG

# COMMODITY NEWS – CRUDE OIL & PRODUCTS

## **US Extends Russian Oil Sanctions Waiver as Brent Tops $112**

US Treasury Secretary Bessent reversed an earlier decision and granted a further 30-day extension of the sanctions waiver permitting purchases of Russian seaborne crude and petroleum products, responding to requests from energy-vulnerable countries unable to source Gulf oil due to the Hormuz closure. The extension covers Russian crude loaded on vessels as of 17 April. Brent rose around 2.6% to close above $112/bbl, having briefly dipped on false reports that the US was considering lifting Iran sanctions during peace talks — a report swiftly denied by a US official.

## **Chinese Supertankers Break Through Hormuz with 4 Million Barrels**

Two Chinese VLCCs exited the Strait of Hormuz carrying approximately 4 million barrels of Iraqi crude — the most significant tanker breakthrough since the waterway closed. The Yuan Gui Yang, chartered by Sinopec's Unipec, loaded 2 million barrels of Basrah crude on 27 February and is due to discharge at Shuidong Port around 4 June. Sinochem's Ocean Lily loaded Qatari and Iraqi crude in late February/early March and is expected at Quanzhou Port around 5 June. A third VLCC, Yuan Hua Hu, also exited with 2 million barrels bound for Zhoushan, as Chinese-flaged ships cautiously test the transit route Iran has ordered vessels to follow.

## **China State Refiners Slash Throughput by Over 1 Million bpd**

Sinopec, PetroChina, CNOOC and Sinochem have collectively cut crude throughput by more than 1 million bpd since the outbreak of the Iran war. State refiner crude runs fell to approximately 8.35 million bpd in May, down from 10.17 million bpd pre-conflict. China's total crude throughput in April dropped to 13.3 million bpd, the lowest since August 2022. Surging crude prices and weak fuel demand compressed state refiner margins to about 3,200 yuan per metric ton in late April. Sinopec has sharply cut Saudi crude loadings in May and plans no Saudi cargoes in June.

## **US Crude Stocks Fall 7.9 Million Barrels; SPR Withdrawal Sets Record**

US crude inventories fell 7.9 million barrels in the week ended 15 May, more than double analyst expectations, while crude oil releases from the Strategic Petroleum Reserve set a weekly record at 9.9 million barrels. US crude exports climbed to 5.6 million bpd as global buyers seek non-Middle East barrels. Gasoline stocks fell 1.5 million barrels with summer driving season approaching and pump prices at their highest level in four years. Brent was trading around $107/bbl. Analysts warned that continued inventory drawdowns ahead of summer could become a serious problem.

## **Ukrainian Drone Strikes Cripple a Quarter of Russia's Refining Capacity**

Ukrainian drone attacks have forced virtually all major oil refineries in central Russia to fully or partially halt operations, with affected capacity exceeding 83 million metric tons per year — roughly 25% of Russia's total refining capacity. The Kirishi refinery (20 million mt/yr) has been fully shut since 5 May. Affected plants include refineries at Moscow, Nizhny Novgorod, Ryazan and Yaroslavl, accounting for over 30% of Russia's gasoline output and about 25% of diesel. Russia had already imposed a gasoline export ban running from April through July.

## **OPEC+ to Raise July Quotas by 188,000 bpd Despite Continued Disruption**

Seven core OPEC+ members are expected to agree a 188,000 barrel per day hike to July production quotas at their 7 June meeting, though actual delivery remains constrained by the war. OPEC+ total output fell to 33.19 million bpd in April from 42.77 million bpd in February, with Gulf producers alone losing 9.9 million bpd. The group's only members with genuine spare capacity — Saudi Arabia, Iraq and Kuwait — remain impacted by Hormuz disruptions, making any quota increase largely symbolic in the near term.

## **IEA Warns Oil Market Could Enter "Red Zone" by July–August**

IEA Executive Director Fatih Birol warned the oil market could hit a "red zone" in July–August as peak summer demand collides with sustained Middle East supply loss and depleting strategic reserves. The Iran war has removed over 14 million barrels per day — the largest oil supply crisis in history. The IEA's coordinated 400 million barrel strategic reserve release is flowing at 2.5–3 million bpd, implying supplies will be exhausted by early August. Brent was trading around $108/bbl. Birol said the single most important solution remains full, unconditional reopening of the Strait of Hormuz. Source: Reuters / LSEG

## **Equinor Warns European Gas Stocks Risk Becoming Critical**

Europe could face a critical gas storage shortfall if Hormuz disruption continues for another one to three months, Equinor warned at the Flame energy conference in Amsterdam. European gas caverns are currently just above 35% full — well below a seasonal norm of around 50% — and the EU requires 90% storage by October–December. Equinor's Senior VP for Gas Trading said that even if the war stopped immediately with Hormuz fully reopened, storage would reach only a "tight" 75%; if the closure continues one to three months more, the situation becomes critical. Dutch TTF was around €50/MWh, having peaked at €74/MWh in March. Equinor estimated that prices of €60–70/MWh could reduce gas-to-power demand by around 10 billion cubic metres, providing some market self-correction.

## **Third Qatari LNG Tanker Transits Hormuz Bound for China**

**Third Qatari LNG Tanker Transits Hormuz Bound for China** A third Qatari LNG tanker, the Al Sahla (211,842 cubic metres capacity), transited the Strait of Hormuz heading for China's Tianjin LNG terminal with an expected arrival of 14 June, as a Qatari negotiating team arrived in Tehran to work toward a peace deal. The transit occurred nearly two weeks after the first Qatari cargo passed through the strait under an Iran-Pakistan arrangement. Hormuz LNG transits remain highly erratic: Iran had previously halted two Qatari tankers that had been officially authorised to pass, and each new transit is being closely watched as a barometer of whether commercial LNG flows might resume.

## **Four US LNG Vessels Head to China After Trump-Xi Summit**

Four LNG vessels were sailing from the United States to China, marking the first direct US LNG cargoes bound for China during Trump's second term. The ships — the Umm Al Hanaya from Cheniere's Sabine Pass and three vessels from Venture Global's Plaquemines plant — are expected at China's Tianjin port between 15-28 June. Trump stated during his Beijing summit visit that China wants to buy US oil, LNG and other goods. China still applies a 25% tariff on US LNG — identified as the primary obstacle to full revival of bilateral LNG trade — but the shipments suggest a pragmatic thaw is underway.

## **Australia's LNG Sector Warns of Missed Opportunity Despite Supply Boom**

Senior executives at the Australian Energy Producers conference described Australia's LNG sector as both "uninvestable" and the greatest available growth opportunity — a contradiction reflecting policy uncertainty rather than lack of demand. Australia became the world's second-largest LNG exporter after Iranian strikes forced Qatar to halt production, with spot LNG for North Asia peaking at $25.30/mmBtu during the conflict. Industry leaders called for faster project approvals, regulatory simplification and fiscal certainty, pointing to the Beetaloo Basin as a world-class shale play. Without action, they warned, Australia risks stagnating and drifting into long-term decline despite unprecedented global LNG demand.

## **PetroChina Bets on Deep Coal Rock Gas to Reduce LNG Import Reliance**

PetroChina is investing heavily in coal rock gas (CRG), an unconventional source extracted from deep below China's coal basins using horizontal drilling and hydraulic fracturing, targeting output of 30 billion cubic metres per year by 2035 — more than last year's record shale gas output. China's 14 coal basins are estimated to hold 13 trillion cubic metres of technically recoverable CRG reserves. China produced 4.2 bcm of CRG in 2025. Successfully scaling this resource would significantly reduce Beijing's reliance on imported LNG, whose geopolitical and price risks have been exposed twice in four years — first by Russia's invasion of Ukraine and again by the Iran war. Source: Reuters / LSEG

# CAPESIZE MARKET

## ATLANTIC AND PACIFIC BASIN

The Capesize freight market endured a broadly softening trend through much of the week before staging a recovery on the final trading day, with the Pacific basin leading the rebound whilst the Atlantic remained comparatively subdued throughout. The week opened on the back foot. On 18 May, rates eased amid thin activity and a cautious tone driven by geopolitical concerns surrounding a potential escalation in US-Iran tensions, which raised the spectre of firmer bunker prices. The Western Australia–Qingdao route was assessed at USD 14.80/wmt, down USD 0.25/wmt from the prior week's close. Rates continued to soften on 19 May despite an uptick in Pacific cargo volumes, with a large number of vessels reportedly preparing to discharge at Far Eastern ports — suggesting a swell of available tonnage in the near term. The Western Australia–Qingdao assessment slipped to USD 14.65/wmt, down USD 0.15/wmt on the day. A recovery materialised on 20 May, underpinned by a healthy influx of Pacific iron ore cargoes and improved coal requirements, including South Korean freight tenders, which bolstered demand and prompted owners to firm their offer ideas.

The route was assessed at USD 15.25/wmt, up USD 0.60/wmt. In the Atlantic, the Tubarao–Qingdao rate softened to USD 36.40/wmt whilst Saldanha Bay–Qingdao fell sharply to USD 27.45/wmt. Markets were broadly stable on 21 May despite weaker freight derivatives overnight, with Pacific trading eventually concluding several fixtures in the late Asian session. Western Australia–Qingdao was assessed at USD 15.20/wmt, down marginally by USD 0.05/wmt. The week closed strongly on 22 May, with Pacific rates surging as Western Australia iron ore miners maintained robust tonnage appetite. A mining major fixed two Capesize vessels from Dampier to Qingdao for 7–9 June laycan at USD 15.50–15.60/wmt. Western Australia–Qingdao was assessed at USD 15.70/wmt, up USD 0.50/wmt. Atlantic markets were assessed unchanged: Tubarao–Qingdao at USD 36.30/wmt and Saldanha Bay–Qingdao at USD 27.25/wmt.

## PACIFIC BASIN

The Pacific market traded on a firmer footing throughout the week as healthy coal and grain demand absorbed prompt tonnage and sentiment gradually improved. In Indonesia, consistent coal demand into South China and India alongside a tightening prompt list sustained a positive tone. Modern eco-tonnage commanded premiums, with several fixtures in the mid-USD 20,000s/day range. An 82,000-dwt vessel (2026) fixed at USD 26,000/day for a North Pacific/Singapore-Japan grain trip — the basin's highest. Australian conditions remained healthy, underpinned by steady coal and iron ore demand into China, Japan, and India, though sentiment softened towards week's close. In the Indian Ocean, sentiment improved on stronger steel-related cargo demand and tightening tonnage availability.

Period and long-haul business remained active, with low-to-mid USD 30,000s/day reported basis arrival pilot station for trans-Atlantic grain employment from East Coast South America.

# SUPRAMAX & HANDYSIZE MARKET

## US GULF / NORTH AMERICA

The US Gulf market remained firm, particularly in the Supramax and Ultramax segments. An Ultramax was heard fixed at around USD 31,000/day for a coal trip to the East Mediterranean. A modern shallow-draught 63,000- dwt vessel was also fixed at around USD 30,000/day for a trip to the Mediterranean. Handy activity was limited, with few fixtures reported. A 34,000-dwt vessel was heard fixed for a scrap trip to the East Mediterranean at USD 14,500/day, whilst a 36,000-dwt unit was fixed at USD 18,000/day for a trip to the West Coast Central America.

## EAST COAST SOUTH AMERICA

Rates in East Coast South America saw no remarkable movement across handy or larger vessel classes. On Handysize, transatlantic rates from East Coast South America to East Mediterranean were assessed at around USD 20,000/day arrival pilot station on standard Handysize tonnage, whilst trips to Spain were assessed in the high teens. For Supramax tonnage, transatlantic rates from West Africa via East Coast South America to Continent/Mediterranean were assessed at around USD 17,500/day, with fronthaul to China assessed at around USD 23,000/day. On Ultramax tonnage, the equivalent transatlantic was assessed at around USD 18,000/day, whilst fronthaul from West Africa via East Coast South America to China was assessed at around USD 23,500/day.
