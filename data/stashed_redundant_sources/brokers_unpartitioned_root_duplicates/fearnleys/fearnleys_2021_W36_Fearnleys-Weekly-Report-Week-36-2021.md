## 01 Tankers

### Market Commentary

#### Vlcc

The MEG market remains a frustrating hunting ground for owners, as the expectation of any improvement in rates remains on the horizon. Charterers able to source tonnage at last done levels in the low WS 30's MEG/East, and avoiding any rush to fix. There remains much of the 3rd decade to cover however (25-30 cargoes?) and we already over 100 cargoes for September, so plenty of fixing to be done in the short term. The Atlantic remains busy, but again, rates yet to move as ballasters content to escape a stagnant East market if possible. Some owners preferring to keep things short, as they realize that if you fix long now, that is your Q4. Much debate among market players as to when the much vaunted rate improvements will come, but we still a little early for that right now.

#### Suezmax

There's a perception that Suezmax's always follow "big brothers" lead as soon as V's show any signs of life. Yes, if the maths permit it, we can see interaction, but the core issue for Suezmax right now is the East. Namely, its low enquiry volumes, therefore, ballasters continue to clip out Med and Wafr stems on a daily basis. A case in point being that the majority of vessels passing Rondo now set a course directly for Wafr without even considering ballasting to deviation point Galle. The East list is so overloaded with unemployed ships that owners with larger fleets aren't even looking to maintain fleet balance. They are rolling the dice on Western markets that still have decent enquiry levels. Unfortunately, this strategy becomes a law of diminishing returns and will continue to cannibalize Atlantic basin rates.

#### Aframax

The Nsea/Baltic Aframax market experienced yet another disappointing week. The combination of an abundance of available tonnage coupled with somewhat reduced activity, in the current fixing window, are keeping rates at bottom levels. Earnings are very close to nothing or even negative for some owners trading in this area. Unless, owners find better alternatives elsewhere, we expect the next week to remain quite similar to the previous. The Med/Bsea market also continued trading sideways at bottom levels this week. Bad weather and potential disruptions in Libya have created a little bit of excitement, however these elements have yet to develop into firmer rates. A few owners have an optimistic eye on the cargo program out of Bsea for October, so it will be interesting to see how healthy this program is once revealed.

## 02 Dry Bulk

### Market Commentary

#### Capesize

Rates are still coming off, but activity is decent. Various c5 fixtures in the mid 12s and on c3, Vale have been fixing a handful of ships this week just sub USD 30 pmt. Levels are strong after all, and another improvement is expected.

#### Panamax

This week we have experienced a weaker sentiment in both hemispheres. The Atlantic demand has declined and tonnage building up. We do however see some good rates achieved for the shorter Baltic rounds and also out of Black Sea.

The P1A is currently yielding mid 20k's. In the Pacific, we see rates also easing but not to the same extent as in the Atlantic. Pacific rounds are being concluded in the low 30's.

#### Supramax

It's questionable whether Supras will continue to fall over the next weeks notwithstanding downwards pressure from the larger segments, as there are several factors lending strong support such as vessel demand from the biggest load countries in Asia have rebounded strongly since the bottom in early August and activity in the South Atlantic are almost back to this year's highs, in addition lobal congestion is persistent around all-time highs.

From the Atlantic, USG building up tonnage due to recent hurricane, where trips to Far East were discussed around USD 40,000 and around USD 29,000 for trips back to Cont. From ECSA to Med, Supra was fixed around USD 37,000. From the Pacific basin nickel ore runs from Indonesia to China fixed around USD 35,000.

Vsl open in Taiwan for trip via Indonesia redel WCI covered at USD 34,000. Some fertz runs via MEG to WCI closed around USD 50,000 dop Mesaieed. Steel runs via SAFR to Far East with vsl open in Bangladesh fixed around USD 34,000.
