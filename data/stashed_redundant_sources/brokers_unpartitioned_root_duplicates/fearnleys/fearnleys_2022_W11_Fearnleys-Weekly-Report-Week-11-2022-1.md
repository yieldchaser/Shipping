## 01 Tankers

### Market Commentary

#### Vlcc

On the flip side, OECD inventories at close to their lowest for the last 10 years, these will need replenishing, once OPEC are able/willing to lift production. It is up to Western leaders to convince them to do so.

#### Suezmax

Russian grades continue to command a premium over the regular market ex Baltic and Black Sea with the curious exception of ESPO loadings from Kozmino. The wider market is still battling rising bunkers and falling freight with few signs of resistance other than some Indian market cargoes that have recently traded Wafr/India at circa 130kt x WS 88-90. Those with scrubbers have committed the dirty deed and allowed their consumption-advantage to be traded against them with Wafr/long East trading at WS 77.5-80. MEG/India enquiry has been particularly active, whilst conversely, long haul from this region has been quiet, allowing ships to recycle back onto the list quickly. MEG/East will trade WS 87.5-90 on modern whilst TD23 may start to threaten WS 30's if low cargo volumes persist.

#### Mediterranean

Although the rates ex Bsea have come off this last week, voyages ex Russian ports are still giving owners a healthy return. In the Mediterranean, we have seen the market soften further and we expect this to continue going into the weekend.

## 02 Dry Bulk

### Market Commentary

#### Capesize

There is a large degree of uncertainty around the world, but all in all dry bulk rates have remained fairly stable; rates went from USD 18,900 on average to USD 21,800 from last week. C5 went from USD 12 to 12,3 on index, despite talks about USD 12,6 being concluded. The Brazil/ China round has been kept around USD 30 pmt level for some time (index value USD 30,5 one week back) and is presently at USD 27 pmt, also being last done by Vale who have been active in taking ships recently.

#### Panamax

A softer week encompassed the Panamax market with little good cheer for owners to grab a hold of. Negative bids and tones across the board. Negative news and indexes have been the story of the whole week.

Some representative fixtures this week: YM Pacifico (81,812/2015) sailed Hamburg 12 March was rumoured fixed for a trip via US East coast redelivery China with an intended cargo of petcoke at a rate in the region of mid USD 30,000's with charterers Uniper.

Star Amethyst (82,123/2009) retro sailing Haldia 3 March was fixed for a trip via EC South America redelivery Southeast Asia USD 26,750 to Omegra.

The Doric Armour (81,800/2018) Huangpu 15 March was placed on subjects by Messrs Cobelfret for a trip via Bunbury redelivery Continent at USD 30,000.

MBA Future (82,181/2009) Nagoya prompt was said to have fixed for a trip via NoPac with petcoke redelivery Japan at USD 30,000, but little else emerged.

#### Supramax

Despite the War in Ukraine continuing for the third week, the shipping industry are fighting to get grips with the situation. The Supramax market showed some good resistance, strong demand in the Pacific made many owners leave Emed and ballast towards Suez to obtain better rates loading in MEG, India, and Southeast Asia. USG and ECSA pushed rates further up. In Asia, the demand/supply was more balanced. The period activity decreased, with big gap between owners and charterers ideas, which are about a few thousands. The BSI-58 10T/C route closing yesterday just USD 100 below USD 32,000/day. South China trip via Indonesia, E.C.India is up by, with fixure reported close to USD 40,000/day, W.Africa trip via ECSA to N.China is USD 30,000/day, lower rates from Mediterranean to China/S.Korea is softer at USD 20,000/day. The big difference between the Atlantic average USD 15,000/day, while the Pacific round voyages and back haul paus as much as USD 40,000/day.

## 03 Gas

### Market Commentary

#### West

A couple of leftover March vessels are being targeted for some final attempts to secure March FOB loaders at freight rates in the low USD 100's Houston/Chiba and mid USD 50's Houston/Flushing. First half April seems more or less done in terms of spot activity and all eyes are on second half April where there is a vessel requirement and FOB/CFR tender from an Indonesian major.

Under the current environment, Houston/Flushing is trading at a mere USD 6,500 premium to that of Houston/Chiba and thus far in March there are six vessels already on the way towards North West Europe and it will continue to rise - that's about the same this week, we think there is definitely more to come.
