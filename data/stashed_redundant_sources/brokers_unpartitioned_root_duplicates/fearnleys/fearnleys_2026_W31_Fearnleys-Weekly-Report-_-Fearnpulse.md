## 01 Tankers

### Market Commentary

#### Suezmax

Petrobras continue to face the brunt of the volatility that this market has to offer. They fixed 50 points down and 40 points up, all within a span of 2 weeks. Owners who are able to transit the Gulf of Aden, albeit with conditions have done well with being able to achieve WS 297.5 for Red Sea to Korea voyage. With the Strait of Hormuz and the Bab El Mandeb being tricky to transit, suppliers are now looking to off load the oil via the Sumed pipeline and load out of Sidi Kerir. Charterers in the USG seem to be taking some cover by taking ships on subs but not much has come to fruition as most of them have failed.

* CPC has been tested, with W430 on subs with only 3 offers being received, as owners are clearly pricing in the risk following the recent drone attacks.

#### Aframax

* On the other hand, we have a market quote for Med/Skorea that received 8 offers. Therefore, despite this elevated rate from the Black Sea, it seems unlikely to translate across the wider market or offset the bearish supply fundamentals, as the majority of owners will continue to avoid the Black Sea.

* We have plenty of vessels available in the prompt window for WAF, with tonnage either opening locally or sitting FOC from UKCM positions. In addition, towards the back end of the fixing window, we have a fleet of ballasters from the East heading towards the Cape.

* In terms of volume, there will be cargoes to move in the 2nd decade, but a downward test is anticipated before any congestion emerges.

* Even then, owners are expected to remain pragmatic given the aforementioned supply outlook. Owners will be banking on the re-emergence of CPC activity and improved safety assurances to counterbalance the bearish market sentiment.

#### North Sea

Market activity has been limited as we push into August stems with cargoes covering quietly with relets and some Suezmax in the mix. The front end of the list has some natural North Sea tonnage on it for the first time in a couple of weeks. There has been some prompter questions but natural window now likely heading toward the end of the first decade. Surrounding markets are steady / softer but still attractive for tonnage to ballast out of the area.

#### Mediterranean

Midway through this week and there has been hardly any local Mediterranean runs from the usual suspects. The tonnage list is long with the prompt West Mediterranean positions ballasting towards USG but equally the Red Sea/Arabian Sea vessels keep feeding Mediterranean with ballasters. Fundamentals are pointing towards a correction while the short-term inefficiencies and sentiment might help rates remain at significant high levels for this period of the year.

## 02 Dry Bulk

### Market Commentary

#### Capesize

Australia with two main active miners in play. Latest last done levels on C5 reported at low-mid USD 12 levels with the most recent fixture reported at USD 12.25 for mid August dates. Refreshed ideas today are at high USD 11s and low USD 12s for mid August. C3 Brazil/China and West Africa also saw softening this week, in part due to easing oil prices earlier in the week. Charterers took a more patient approach amid the weaker market levels with bids hovering around the USD 33s for late August dates and offers aiming in the mid-low USD 34s. Ideas moving to September are rumored in the USD 32s signaling potential further softening. Atlantic activity has remained subdued this week following limited fresh interest. Transatlantic stems saw a slight increase in cargo volume but levels are below owners' expectations. Charterers are hopeful for rate corrections before committing and owners continue to discount prompt vessels in order to secure employment. Transatlantic paper has increased marginally today with C8 transatlantic valuing USD 44,500 per day.

#### Panamax

The Panamax market softened over the course of the week, with the index easing and sentiment turning more cautious across both basins amid the seasonal summer lull.

#### Supramax

The Atlantic set the softer tone - transatlantic and fronthaul enquiry stayed thin, prompt tonnage was discounted to secure employment, and a widening gap between owners' ideas and charterers' bids kept activity subdued, even as the Continent and Western Mediterranean remained comparatively tight on prompt vessels. The Pacific was the more constructive corner late in the week, steadying off recent lows as North Pacific grain enquiry improved and charterers returned to cover weather-disrupted positions, though an ample tonnage list and still-elevated Chinese stockpiles capped the recovery. Period interest remained active, with charterers continuing to pursue Kamsarmax cover for one- to two-year employment, but bid and offer stayed apart and owners of modern eco tonnage were reluctant to chase the market lower. On balance we would characterise the tone as soft but stabilising into month-end, with near-term direction hinging on whether the Pacific's tentative firming can broaden and Atlantic cargo replenishment comes through.

Supramax/Ultramax market remained under pressure, extending its recent downward trend as fresh cargo enquiry stayed limited across most regions. Atlantic sentiment continued to soften, with increasing vessel availability weighing on in despite occasional stronger fixtures. Asia remains relatively quiet. Overall, the market remains cautious with little sign of a near-term improvement in the coming weeks. The Handy market was largely unchanged, with sentiment continuing to be driven by vessel positioning rather than any meaningful shift in demand. The Continent/Mediterranean remained balanced but subdued, while the Atlantic softened further as an expanding tonnage list outpaced fresh cargo volumes. Activity in the Pacific was generally quiet, with limited fixing keeping momentum subdued.
