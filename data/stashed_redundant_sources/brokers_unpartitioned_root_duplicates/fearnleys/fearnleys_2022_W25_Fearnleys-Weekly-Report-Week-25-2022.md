## 01 Tankers

### Market Commentary

#### Vlcc

Quite a turn around this week. Owners have the bit between their teeth and are looking to continue market gains over the last week or so. Driven by an active Atlantic market, with rates creeping up USD 200k for USG/East on Monday, it set the tone and the West Africa market followed suit (jumping to WS 50 Wafr/China), as only modern approved tonnage would fit the requirements. What had been a sluggish MEG market burst into life Tuesday, with Unipec grabbing what they could at the WS 45-46 level, leaving other charterers entering the market and owners looking to push (at the time of writing owners talking the WS 50's). Those charterers that can escape on handicapped/old can do so, but it remains only a few points discount. Plenty of cargoes still to work, so it remains all to play for during the remainder of the week.

#### VLCC

In the Atlantic, Aframaxes continue to lend physical/psychological support to Suezmaxes whilst VLCCs could, conversely, cap proceedings. For now, the bulls are winning as it's moving in the right direction with TD20 poised to make further gains. Another support mechanism for the Atlantic comes in the form of charterers choosing to pothole the market with multiple laycans being spread out over a wide date-range. This allows momentum to build and imbues owners with confidence to ask for more. In the East, MEG/West trade flows continue to increase which has naturally benefited freight rates, particularly as both 15 and 20 tonne crane vessels are in demand which helps thin the list for eastbound requirements. TD23 is likely to hit WS 70's whilst a China run won't give you much change from WS 102.5-107.5 on a modern ship.

#### Aframax

A few Aframax owners, who aim to fix premium business ex Baltic, are also considering fixing cross Nsea cargos as a time filler.

Despite this, the current Nsea market is looking very tight for end month loadings. Owners are with workable tonnage in position is rightfully asking for higher than last done rates. We see this firm Nsea/Baltic market trend continue going forward. The Med/Blsea market was active throughout last week with rates correcting up as charterers trying secure prompt tonnage to cover 3rd decade June/1st decade July requirements. Slightly different story this week as charterers been holding back and with lower cargo activity the lists now looking healthier, resulting in rates coming off again. We foresee a softer/stable market for the balance of the week.

## 02 Dry Bulk

### Market Commentary

#### Capesize

Markets are shaking as the fear of recession is increasing, which is also making a big impact on Capesize expectation as steel and iron ore prices are dropping. We see freight for West Australia-China, which is the biggest flow for seaborn iron ore, is down by 16% in two trading days and the average of all routs down 23%. It's also interesting to note that forward expectations of hire is down from 27,500 last week to 22,500 now.

#### Panamax

The market began slowly entering a new week, no holidays of note but the market had yet to find a clear direction with a mixture of views for next done deals. However, the majority felt rates in the Atlantic appeared relatively healthy so far with fundamentals largely unchanged from the end of last week. The market in Asia appeared at first sight a little sloppy, but yet again market players had yet to show their hands so a little too early to call the market.

## 03 Gas

### Market Commentary

#### LPG East

The Indian majors continue their high activity in the East with both a couple of deals and a cargo out for mid July dates with one more expected this week. A couple of other market players have also been making some movements. Focus is now shifting towards mid month dates as early July fixing seems to be as good as done, and we have almost seen a dozen deals for early dates with last fixture concluded at USD mid 70s for non-India and USD low 90s for India. With the latest activity considered we expect freight to remain firm.

#### LPG West

The western freight rates are on the rise again and we are starting to see freight enquiries for August dates. On the one hand, freight is gaining momentum in the end of July although for the vessels left open in the first half, the threat of waiting time is imminent. We have seen over 20 spot fixtures out of the USG/USEC so far in July and owners are now aiming around USD 110 Houston/Chiba and high 60s Houston/Flushing. With a handful of uncovered July and early August cargoes out there and the position list in the hands of few players in addition to the arb supporting today's freight we expect rates to at least maintain.
