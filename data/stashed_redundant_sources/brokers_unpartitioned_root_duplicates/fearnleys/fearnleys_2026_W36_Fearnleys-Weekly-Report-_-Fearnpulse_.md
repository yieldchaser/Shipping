## 01 Tankers

### Market Commentary

#### Aframax

sniffing ex West Africa and a tender or two USG/Eastbound and it's a perfect "storm". Arguably the Suezmax market has seen a drop lately and will eventually cap the upside for their bigger sisters.

#### VLCC

Despite Suezmaxes regaining competitiveness against VLCCs following last week's sharp correction, a front-heavy tonnage list and muted activity have continued to weigh on the rates in West Africa. Meanwhile, Aframaxes remain firmly in the driving seat across the Atlantic. That said, the improved economics of the Suezmaxes appear to be attracting renewed interest, with activity showing signs of picking up.

#### North Sea

After a long weekend activity has been slow on the pick up. Natural dates 8-10 window but with a number of relets around and stems a little thin charterers are able to take their time. US market looks to have bottomed and with Mediterranean market looking softer we may see drive to ballast towards the US again after a quiet few days on that front. Slow but steady.

#### Mediterranean

Activity stalled at the end of last week which has rebuilt the early part of the list. Activity has picked up again at the start of the week but the increased tonnage has changed the sentiment for now and charterers have managed to pushed the market down. Natural window pushing into the 2nd decade with tonnage to clear which in the short term may lead to some more weakening before we find the bottom.

## 02 Dry Bulk

### Market Commentary

#### Supramax

of September, we may see some pressure in the Atlantic for late September and early October laycans. Overall sentiment is bullish but cautious as limited fresh demand to absorb growing supply.

The Supramax market remained mixed and largely directionless, with activity picking up selectively across both the Atlantic and Asia. The Atlantic saw pockets of strength, particularly around the USG and South Atlantic, supported by steady demand for grains, petcoke and minor bulks, while Asia remained active but positional with varied tonnage availability between areas. Handysize trading was generally subdued, although fundamentals remained healthy in parts of the Pacific and South Atlantic.

The US Gulf continued to show relatively firm conditions despite limited fixing, while the Continent and Mediterranean markets remained softer. Overall, sentiment across both sectors remains cautious, with regional tonnage positioning and pockets of enquiry driving the market rather than any clear broader trend. There is good appetite for period with quite a few fixtures reported past weeks.

#### Panamax

The Panamax market has firmed steadily through the week, with the gains broadening across both basins as the index pushed higher over successive sessions. In the Atlantic the tone is constructive: transatlantic business remains well bid on the back of a replenished cargo book and a tightening nearby tonnage list, while fronthaul has been slower to follow, with owners holding above charterers' ideas on US Gulf and South American grain. The Pacific has been the stronger of the two, where a steady flow of Australian and Indonesian coal alongside North Pacific grain has left prompt tonnage short and charterers chasing owners' levels rather than the reverse. Period interest has picked up markedly, with a good spread of owners collecting for both short and twelve-month cover and bids increasingly obliged to follow last-done rather than set it, although the forward curve continues to price modest backwardation beyond the prompt. We expect the tone to remain sideways-to-firmer into next week, with the main question being how much of the recent move reflects genuine incremental demand rather than positioning ahead of the autumn grain programme.
