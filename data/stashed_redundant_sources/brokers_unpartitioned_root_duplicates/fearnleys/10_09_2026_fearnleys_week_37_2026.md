## 01 Tankers

### Market Commentary

#### VLCC

The VLCC positions list is now so tight that no one would be too surprised if we see the WS 400 mark breached for a Fujairah/East run off a prompt-ish position before long, crazy as it may sound. Speed blindness does not even begin to describe the current mood. However, the oil still needs to get out through the Strait of Hormuz and Iranians have increased efforts to stop that from happening. It's a fragile state of affairs. Atlantic/East rates have followed suit as refiners look for supply security regardless of higher oil prices. Ship values have also skyrocketed in tandem with the spot market with reports of newbuilding resales at USD 200 million- and 16-17-year- olds more than half of that. Fasten seat belts.

#### Aframax

The majority of the publicly reported West Africa cargoes have either fixed away or may have relets to utilise. As a result, we are likely pushing into the 27-30 September fixing window. However, despite this shift towards fixing closer to three weeks forward, there is little expectation that activity will slow, with adjacent sizes either already pulling their weight or primed to do so. Aframaxes are surging in the Mediterranean and, if the physical/paper dynamic is anything to go by, are expected to do the same stateside. In addition, and perhaps more significantly, the VLCC market is extremely busy ex-Fujairah and approaching our equivalent with the Atlantic also firming aggressively. Regardless of pricing, VLCCs remain under-fixed for the first decade of October ex-West Africa, and with this surge in Eastern activity there may be very few units physically capable of interacting with this window, which could inevitably lead to cargo splitting and increased Suezmax utilisation. To top it all off, CPC stems should be released imminently, and there are reports of attacks on terminal infrastructure and vessels, which will almost certainly result in higher premiums.

#### North Sea

As North Sea Aframax loading dates are now moving into the third decade of September, the list of available tonnage continues to thin. With neighbouring markets such as the US Gulf and Mediterranean offering attractive alternatives, owners are increasingly ballasting away from the North Sea. While rates remain steady for the time being, tightening tonnage availability could support a modest upward correction in the near term. Activity in the Mediterranean remains robust, with cargoes being quoted from virtually every export area and loading port. Rates continue to gain momentum, and with charterers actively seeking to secure tonnage for forward- loading programmes, the market is expected to remain firm with further upside potential.

## 02 Dry Bulk

### Market Commentary

#### Supramax

tonnage has owners staying strong with their offers, today seeing numerous offers in the USD mid 42-43 levels for late 2h October laycans. Bids for late October sit at high USD 41s with one fixture seen last night at high USD 41 levels, and another Newcastlemax took an early October cargo for USD 41. Sentiment is mixed moving forward as FFAs signal slight flattening overall, owners remain bullish, holding firm on their offers, operators are quietly watching the market suggesting potential upside risk if demand persists at current levels.

The Supramax/Ultramax market showed a slightly firmer tone during the week, supported by improving activity in the Atlantic and steady demand in the Pacific.

Owners remained selective, while charterers continued to show some resistance on rates. Overall sentiment is constructive but balanced. Recent gains suggest some tightening in the Atlantic, while adequate tonnage availability in Asia is limiting stronger upside.

#### Panamax

After several weeks of steady gains the Panamax market has levelled off, with the index easing gently over recent sessions from what now looks to have been an early- September high. The Atlantic accounts for most of the softening: prompt tonnage has built up in the North Continent while fresh transatlantic enquiry has slowed, leaving owners there with less leverage, though fronthaul remains the firmer part of the basin and owners continue to favour Atlantic employment over repositioning east. The Pacific has held its ground more comfortably, supported by Australian and Indonesian coal, North Pacific grain and a useful flow of additional cargo from split Capesize stems, but the urgency that characterised late August has faded and charterers are no longer chasing. Period remains active and, notably, better bid than the softening spot market would imply - business is still clearing at levels close to recent highs, with owners of modern tonnage holding their ideas firmly and the gap to charterers' bids widening rather than closing. Our own indicators argue for caution from here: shipment volumes continue to run below fleet supply growth, the South Atlantic tightness reading suggests the recent correction has further to run, and we would treat the current period market as an opportunity to secure cover rather than expect a renewed push higher.

#### Handysize

The Handy market remained stable, and even firmed up a touch, with a healthy balance between supply and demand. Activity was mainly focused on spot and short- period employment, with owners generally able to remain selective. Touch positive bias, provided grain programs and other minor bulk cargoes continue at current levels. Balanced tonnage availability should, however, keep freight gains measured.
