## 02 Dry Bulk

### Market Commentary

#### Capesize

This week markets are showing slight pressure with limited fixing today on Capesize across all segments, however markets are consistent on C3 Brazil/China with the main decline seen in Pacific C5. FFAs show slight positive sentiment in the long term although above average laden to ballast ratio of vessels heading toward the Atlantic gives a slight bearish outlook in the near future. This week sentiment has had a softer tone in the Pacific and today saw a decline on paper. Earlier fixing concluded around and just below the USD 14 range for West Australia/Qingdao. Today, two miners reported fixing at USD 13.4 yesterday and latest bids are mixed and around USD 13 to USD 14 for early September laycans. In the Atlantic, levels have remained consistent however South Brazil and West Africa to China remains subdued with limited bids and offers reported today. C3 Brazil/China sees bids and offer reported in low USD 36 levels with last done in high USD 34s on C3 for West Africa and another reported in the USD 37 range on Friday for a Newcastlemax.

#### Panamax

Panamax sentiment has turned more cautious this week, with the index giving back part of its recent gains and both basins trading with a softer bias. The Pacific has led the correction: prompt tonnage has accumulated across the North and South while fresh NoPac grain and Australian and Indo coal enquiry has been slow to absorb it, leaving charterers in control and the market still in search of a floor. The Atlantic has held up rather better, with ECSA fronthaul remaining the firmer leg and Continent tonnage still reasonably tight, although bid/offer have widened and fixing volumes are thin as charterers wait for the north to settle. Period discussion remains active across both Kamsarmax and Panamax, but owners are resisting the softer nearby curve and bids have yet to close the gap, so relatively little is concluding. We would expect a sideways-to-softer tone into next week, with any recovery contingent on a fresh injection of grain and coal cargo in the Pacific; in the meantime the Atlantic should continue to offer owners the better alternative.

#### Supramax

Supramax market remained broadly steady, with limited movement across most regions and only modest gains in the Atlantic. The Continent and Mediterranean continue to face pressure due to ample tonnage and limited fresh cargo input, while the South Atlantic remains more balanced but somewhat positional. In Asia, sentiment is improving, supported by better activity in NoPac and Southeast Asia, although this has yet to translate into significant rate gains.

#### Handysize

Handy market wise- another subdued session overall, with the Continent and Mediterranean remaining soft amid limited cargo volumes. The South Atlantic and USG were broadly stable, with little change in fundamentals. Asia remained the strongest area, supported by tighter prompt tonnage and steady cargo flow, although activity remained relatively limited.
