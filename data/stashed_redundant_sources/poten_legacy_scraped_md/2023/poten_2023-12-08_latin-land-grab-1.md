# Latin Land Grab 1

### Venezuela is eyeing Guyana's Oil Riches

### Latin Land Grab

On Sunday, December 3, Venezuelans voted in a referendum to annex Essequibo, a region of Guyana that borders Venezuela. All the oil fields that have propelled Guyana into one of the most exciting new oil producers of the last 5 years happen to be offshore Essequibo. President Maduro has created a new map of Venezuela, which includes the disputed area and has said that residents that live in Essequibo (which represents two-thirds of Guyana's land area) will be granted the Venezuelan nationality. He also approved oil, gas and mining exploration licenses and encouraged state oil company PDVSA to start right away. Not surprisingly, his counterpart in Guyana, President Irfaan Ali took a dim view of Maduro's referendum and actions. He called the Venezuelan moves "an imminent threat" and a desperate attempt". What is the background of this conflict and is there a chance that Venezuela will indeed get access to Guyana's offshore oil fields?

The conflict goes back to 1899, when a Paris-based arbitration panel ruled that Essequibo belonged to the British colony of British Guiana. Venezuela has long disputed that ruling because Essequibo was part of its boundaries during the Spanish colonial era, and the countries have unsuccessfully tried to resolve the conflict. After the referendum and the aggressive declarations from Maduro, an alarmed Guyana put its armed forces on high alert, notified the U.N. Security Council as well as the International Court of Justice. The United States and the United Kingdom have both come out strongly in support of Guyana. The U.S. Southern Command conducted joint military flight exercises with Guyana on December 7, which Venezuela labeled a "provocation".

While Maduro undoubtedly has an interest in Guyana's oil fields, his short-term aims may be to divert the attention of Venezuelan's population away from the dismal state of the economy, its sky-high inflation (360% in 2023) as well as the international isolation the country finds itself in. Maduro is up for re-election in 2024 and he has a formidable opponent in María Corina Machado, who was voted to be the opposition candidate by a large majority. Maduro may think an international diversion will increase his popularity.

The oil industries of Venezuela and Guyana are a study in contrasts. Venezuela boasts one of the largest oil reserves in the world, but its industry, which was nationalized in the 1970s, is in bad shape after decades of mismanagement and corruption and - in recent years - ever tightening sanctions. The country's crude oil production, which was still above 3 Mb/d back in 2009, has dropped to 0.77 Mb/d currently. In October of this year, the U.S. eased sanctions against Venezuela for an initial period of six months. In exchange, Venezuela's president Maduro promised to allow independent candidates to run in the presidential election next year. This promise looks rather hollow against the backdrop of his recent actions.

In contrast to developments in Venezuela, Guyana's oil industry has been a success story. A consortium led by ExxonMobil first discovered oil in the offshore waters of the country in 2015 and production started in December 2019. Many more discoveries have been made and Guyana currently produces (and exports) around 400,000 b/d from two FPSO's with a third FPSO already on-site. By the end of 2025 ExxonMobil and its partners Hess and CNOOC estimate to produce at least 800,000 b/d from four offshore production units, matching Venezuela's current output, with more to come. The future for Guyana appears bright.

In the (unlikely) event that Venezuela decides to go further than rhetoric and actually moves into Guyana, the oil production and exports from both countries will likely suffer. Sanctions will be reimposed (and probably tightened) and international oil companies will move their assets out of Guyana, crippling that country's production. International military intervention is possible and if Argentina's move on the Falklands and Saddam Hussein's invasion of Kuwait are any guide, Mr. Maduro will more likely end up losing power than strengthening his position.
