# Back Behind the Wheel

Memorial Day weekend in the United States signifies to most the unofficial start to the summer season. For those in the gasoline and tanker markets, this weekend symbolizes the kick-off to a period of high demand for petroleum products. The Automobile Association of America tracks the number of travelers driving at least 50 miles from home. During the Memorial Day holiday in 2008, the number of people was 31.9 million. The expectation for this weekend is for the number to increase to 32.9 million. While it may not immediately translate into a broader recovery in refined products demand internationally, expectations of increased travel can be interpreted as small signs of confidence by the American consumer. The chart below shows the historical prices of gasoline, both regular and premium, for the past few years. A weaker price environment at the pump will likely make it more palatable for the American consumer to fuel up.

The equilibrium between refining margins and shipping rates generally dictate whether the incremental barrel of clean product is supplied through higher domestic refinery outputs or via imports.

### Downward Demand

With the health of the overall economy starkly different from a year ago, gasoline demand is about 4% lower year-on-year. On a practical basis demand is even less when adjusted for hydrocarbon content due to ethanol mandates. The chart below shows weekly gasoline supplied in the market. Although gasoline demand is less than a year ago, volumes remain well within the 10-year range.

### 3-2-1 Crack Spread

2008 3-2-1 Crack Spread 2009 3-2-1 Crack Spread Refining margins are lower as compared to a year ago and the price strength favors gasoline rather than diesel. Since the crude oil freight component is adding less pressure to the bottom line, refiners may be tempted to ramp up runs seeking higher gasoline output. The challenge with higher refinery runs will be managing the seemingly unwanted byproduct diesel. Instead clean products traders should be called upon to source available finished products, namely gasoline, from other regions that have ample supplies and an attractive price arbitrage.
