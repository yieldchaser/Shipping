# Gyrating Bunker Prices

Bunker prices are an important part of overall costs for operating a tanker. A Vlcc consuming 90 tons per day and sailing for, say, 320 days per year will consume 28,800 tons of bunkers. At $175 per ton, the average price of bunkers for 2004 in leading bunkering ports, the annual bunker cost is $5 million per year or $14,400 per day for a 350 day operating year including days in port. Thus bunker costs are about double other operating costs (manning, insurance, maintenance, supplies). For tankers in the spot market, the price of bunkers is critical in determining profitability. ### Differentials Matter

Neglecting cargo intake, if the price of bunkers were the same throughout the world, then it doesn't matter where owners bunker. The differential in bunker prices among the leading bunkering ports in the world shows that it is not just the price of bunkers that is critical in determining profitability, but the managing of bunker purchases. Most of the time this translates to a bunkering policy of buying bunkers in Rotterdam or Houston on a round trip basis. This practice not only minimizes bunker costs, but also maximizes revenue (the less bunkers on board a vessel at the load port, the greater the cargo intake as long as there are no other restrictions such as cargo availability, cubing out, etc.).
