# Aframax Runup Myth or Reality?

With current weekly average Aframax rates at WS 400 ($60,000 per day), with several individual fixtures at record breaking WS 450 in the past two weeks, we might wonder whether shipowners are experiencing an extremely pleasing dream with an alarm clock about to go off or have rates risen to a "permanently high plateau?"

### Is It Vessel Availability?

Aframax availability in terms of vessel arrivals over the next 20 days has averaged 25 vessels since August with a present availability of 30 vessels. Hence a shortage of vessel availability cannot be an explanation for the high rates.

### Is It Fixture Activity?

The following chart shows that fixture activity in 2004 is slightly lagging 2002 and 2003.

### Aframax Spot Fixture Activity

Total fixtures in 2002 were 5,890, 5,870 in 2003, and an annualized estimate of 5,830 in 2004, down by less than 1%. Thus a rush to fix does not appear to be an explanation as to why rates are so high.

Is It Changing Trading Patterns? If fixture activity is not the reason for higher rates, then it may be a shift in trading patterns. The chart on the next page shows the distribution of fixtures by load area in 2002 and 2004 with the shift away from the Caribbean load area to the Arabian Gulf and N. Europe load areas.

The AG load area exhibits greater fixture activity on all three key trades: AG to the Far East (naphtha to Japan, crude to northern China), crude to India, and a combination of clean and dirty cargoes to Southeast Asia and southern China.

The other load area that has grown is northern Europe.

### N. Europe Load Area

An examination of the underlying fixtures shows that the most common trade is intra-regional shipping of North Sea crude cargoes, which is essentially constant. Growth is primarily in the longer-haul UKC to US trades to satisfy growing U.S. crude imports in light of stagnant Venezuelan exports.

So Are We In Dreamland? Obviously rates indicate strong demand for Aframax tankers. Underlying fixture activity indicates some increased demand in the form of shifting trade patterns away from short haul Caribbean crude to the United States to longer haul voyages of AG product and crude to Asia and North Sea crude to the United States. These shifts add to ton-mile demand, but not enough to push Aframax rates to record levels. Something else is happening to explain the reality of WS 450 because the explanation of high rates from increased demand in terms of spot fixture activity and shifting trade patterns is a myth. There has to be another explanation why Aframax rates are so high, but, like a good soap opera, you'll have to wait for the next episode!
