# CMA Conference BAM!

### Really "Kicking it Up a Notch

### CMA Conference

### BAM!

This past week, ship owners, charterers, brokers, One New Commodore and several past Commodores, as well as numerous maritime-related people gathered at the Westin Hotel in Stamford, Connecticut to press palms, exchange business cards and listen to peers speak at the Shipping 2004 conference hosted by the Connecticut Maritime Association. Referred to as the "Posidonia of America," this year's theme, "Kicking it Up a Notch!" a bit of an ode to a celebrity chef, was the group's 19th conference in its 20th year, and was named to recognize the recent record high earnings for ships of all kinds. The conference officially began Monday afternoon with a Dry Cargo Review, after CMA President Donald B. Frost welcomed the group. Rates in that sector, driven by China's demand for everything from coal to soybeans have shot way up. Among those presenting their visions on the market were Henning Oldendorff, chairman of Oldendorff Carriers GmbH & Co. KG and Michael McClure of Navios, who gave the shipowner's viewpoint on the booming dry bulk market.

After a quick break, the "Tanker Market - What is Next?" panel provided a general overview of global supply/demand energy changes affecting the market. Capt. Saleem Alavi, an analyst with DVB Nedship Bank, N.V., spoke first about global post-Iraq energy demand. He noted that a significant amount of money would need to be poured into Iraq's energy sector to bring it up to full speed. He pointed out several Russian pipeline projects that could have a profound impact on the tanker market. Finally he reviewed the hottest topic - LNG. It is expected that LNG will become less expensive and change the energy picture going forward mentioning that the US is expected to significantly increase its imports of LNG through at least 2025.

The shipowners' view was presented by Robert Bugbee, president & COO of OMI Corporation with his talk on "Lessons to Learn - A Shipowners's Viewpoint," and, the next morning, by Lars Carlsson, chairman of Concordia Maritime and Intertanko, with his speech, "The Fundamental Issues of our Day: Views from 35,000 Feet." These two distinguished speakers help to remind us that owners need to look not only at their day-to- day operations but also set strategic direction for their companies. As Mr. Bugbee astutely noted, referring to his competition, "There are many sharks in the water."

In summary, as shown on the next page, there are numerous reasons why tanker rates are so high. Whereas in the past there were significant gaps of around five years between major events, now it's more like five months. The congestion of events has caused enough confusion in the market to push rates to record levels across all international shipping markets.

### The New Commodore Honored

The highlight of the conference is the Commodore's Gala Dinner. CMA's 2004 Commodore Award recipient was Peter C. Georgiopoulos, chairman and CEO of General Maritime Corporation. In his keynote address, he noted that in his father's day no one made money in shipping. But in his day, shipowners are making money in shipping. Mr. Georgiopolous, the man who in 7 short years has built a powerhouse in the international tanker business said he was "humbled" by all the attention and honored to be recognized as this year's Commodore. The frenzy of the conference reflected the frenzy in the market. The program was quite thorough, thought provoking and well attended. Perhaps one day maritime industry types will refer to the event in Posidonia as "The CMA of Greece!"
