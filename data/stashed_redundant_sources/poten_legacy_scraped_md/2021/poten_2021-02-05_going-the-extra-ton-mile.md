# Going The Extra Ton Mile

### Covid-19 caused dramatic changes in crude oil trade

For many years, Poten compiled and published a ranking of the largest dirty spot charterers, based on reported spot fixtures. This annual ranking inspired us to list the largest crude and dirty oil trades in 2020 in a similar fashion. In the first table on the right, we show the 20 largest crude oil tanker routes for 2020 and compare them with 2019. We show the ranking in both million barrels per day (Mb/d) as well as million ton-miles per day (Mtm/d). The first metric (Mb/d) allows the reader to put the crude oil trades in the context of global oil demand, while the second metric (Mtm/d) is the most relevant for the tanker markets, because it also considers the distance over which the oil is moved. In addition to the first table with the ranking of the largest trade routes, the second table highlights the biggest changes, both increases and decreases from one year to the next. This analysis, which is derived from the APEX data of Lloyds List Intelligence, shows some interesting developments in 2020, many of which can be traced back to the Covid-19 pandemic.

First, let us look at the largest crude oil trade routes (for this analysis, we also include dirty products, like fuel oil in the crude oil trades). To nobody's surprise, the long-haul routes from the Arabian Gulf (AG) to the China Sea (covering China, Korea, and Taiwan) and from the AG to Japan are leading the pack, followed by West Africa to the China Sea and South America Atlantic to the China Sea. The top 4 routes in 2020, which represent 41% of the total seaborne crude oil trade, were also the top routes in 2019 (in the same order). Further down the list we see some changes. The USG to China Sea route is 5 th on the list (up from 6 th in 2019), trading places with the AG to SE Asia route. USG exports to China, Korea and Taiwan increased by 15.2% as U.S. producers were able to increase movements to these countries at the same time as the OPEC countries in the Middle East agreed to cut back production in an attempt to balance the oil markets in the aftermath of the Covid-19 outbreak.

The severe impact of Covid-19 on the oil and tanker markets is clearly demonstrated in the second table, showing the top 5 increases and decreases in Mtm/d in 2020 versus 2019. On top of the list is the Scandinavia/Baltic to China Sea route. Ton- miles on this route more than doubled, from 362 Mtm/d in 2019 to 843 Mtm/d in 2020. The collapse in European oil demand as a result of Covid-19 pushed record volumes of North Sea crude (and Fuel Oil) to Asia and the long distance of this voyage amplified the impact on ton-mile demand. Significant increases were also observed in two long-haul routes to SE Asia, one from South America and the other from West Africa. Another growth area was SE Asia to the China Sea, which, combined with the earlier point seems to indicate that a significant portion of the crude moved from Brazil and Venezuela was transshipped in SE Asia and ultimately ended up in China. It is interesting to note that ton-mile demand on the direct route from South America to the China Sea actually decreased 11%.

Other the other end of the scale, we also observed significant decreases in ton- mile demand in Japan, the US West Coast and North West Europe. The AG - Japan trade decreased 16% and the AG - USWC trade was almost cut in half. The reductions in demand in the U.S. and Europe were clearly caused by Covid- 19. Another country that was heavily impacted by the pandemic was India and that is why the South America to South Asia trade showed the fourth largest decrease (-40%). Given the distances involved, all these reductions had a material impact on overall tanker ton-mile demand.

Unfortunately, in 2020, ton-mile demand for crude oil tankers fell by 5% compared to 2019, largely in line with the decline in global oil demand. On the bright side, the expected increase in oil demand in 2021 should also translate into an equivalent increase in tanker demand.
