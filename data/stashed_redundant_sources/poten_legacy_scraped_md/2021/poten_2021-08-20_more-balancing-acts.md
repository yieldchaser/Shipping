# More Balancing Acts

### Aframax & Suezmax ton-mile demand not so bad

In our Weekly Opinion of July 30 th, titled " It is a Balancing Act " we looked at the VLCC ton-mile demand and fleet supply balance to understand the current market slump. As we discussed in that issue, VLCC ton-mile demand suffered from OPEC+ production cuts, which, combined with strong fleet growth caused the current rate malaise. This week, we will look at the smaller oil tanker markets.

The smaller tankers were affected differently from the VLCCs. OPEC producers took a disproportionate share (about 50%) of the global production cuts, even though they account for only about a third of the global oil production. OPEC oil production was 16% lower in Q3 2020 compared to Q4 2019 and about 67% of OPEC crude oil exports are using VLCCs, so it is not surprising that VLCC demand declined significantly.

As the top graph on the right shows, overall Suezmax ton-mile demand was much less affected than VLCC demand by the production cuts caused by the demand destruction from Covid- 19. Overall Suezmax Ton-Mile demand declined by about 77.2 billion ton-miles or 3.5% in 2020 compared to 2019. However, the impact was very uneven for different regions. Ton-Miles of voyages loading in the Mediterranean and the Black Sea declined by 171.8 Bln Ton-Miles, more than twice the overall decline, mainly due to a fall of Russian and Libyan exports, dramatically reducing long haul voyages from the Med to Asia. Demand from the Americas and the Middle East actually increased by over 100 Bln Ton-Miles while exports from West Africa, Asia and UKC had only minor declines. Global Suezmax Ton-Mile demand for the first half of 2021 is only 2.1% lower than in 2019.

If we look at the last 5 years, we see that Suezmax Ton-Mile demand in the first half of 2021 has increased by 11% compared to the first half of 2016. However, over the last 5 years, the Suezmax fleet has increased by 26%. The fleet growth was especially strong in the last 12-18 months with strong deliveries and a slowdown in scrapping.

Looking at the Aframax graph, which combines dirty and clean cargoes, we see that demand declined towards the middle of 2020 but has recovered nicely since then. In fact, 2021 H1 Ton- Mile demand is 3.2% higher than 2019 H1 demand was. In contrast to the Suezmaxes, the Med is the main growth area for Aframaxes. It might come as a surprise that the dirty Aframax trades have grown more than the clean trades.

Dirty Aframax Ton-Mile demand increased by 5.6% from the first half of 2019 to the first half of 2021. About three quarters of the total growth originated in the Mediterranean, and the main growth route (in Ton-Miles) were exports from Russia to the U.S., which more than doubled over the period. Intra-Asian trade also increased by about 5%.

Ton-Mile demand for clean cargoes moved by Aframax tankers (LR2s) is much smaller than for the dirty trades and demand was more or less flat when comparing the first half of 2019 with 2021.

The supply-demand balance for Aframax tankers has developed much better than for Suezmaxes as total Aframax (dirty and clean) Ton-Mile demand increased by 14.8% since 2016 while the fleet has grown by a similar 15.2%. Regrettably for Aframax owners, their freight rates are being dragged down by the other segments, so they don't get rewarded for their more disciplined approach.

In contrast to the VLCC market, where Ton-Mile demand has come off at the same time as the fleet has grown rapidly, the dynamics for the medium sized tankers are more positive. The Aframax market is quite balanced and the Suezmax market suffers mainly from excessive fleet growth, while the demand dynamics seem manageable. Unfortunately for owners, it is unlikely that these markets will really thrive before the VLCC market recovers as well.
