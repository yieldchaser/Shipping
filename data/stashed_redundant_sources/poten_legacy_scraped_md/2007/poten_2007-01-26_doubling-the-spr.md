# Doubling the SPR

In his State of the Union, President Bush declared plans to "double the current capacity of the Strategic Petroleum Reserve," (SPR) a move that would involve increasing government reserves to 1.5 billion barrels of crude oil. The timeline for completion of this "ramp-up" is by 2027.

The graph below shows the historic progression of SPR inventories since 1982. From 1986 through 2002 total SPR crude fluctuated only moderately between 500 million and 600 million barrels. From 2003 through 2006, however, the SPR inventory levels have increased and at present stand at just under 690 million barrels, equivalent to approximately 34 days of domestic oil consumption.

### The Planned Increase

The plan to increase the SPR to 1.5 billion barrels of crude by 2027 is set to start with the government purchasing 100,000 barrels per day beginning in the spring of 2007. After a few months of such purchases the government would re- assess its performance and the present crude price situation. The government would then evaluate when and where to buy the remaining crude needed to fill the SPR to the announced capacity. Filling the SPR to 1.5 billion barrels of crude would involve a net increase of more than 800 million barrels over the 20-year window the government has set out. This increase is therefore equivalent to approximately 110,000 barrels of oil per day through 2027. Though such an increase would represent just a fraction of the 5 million barrels of oil that the US produces domestically every day, as well as of the more than 10 million barrels of oil that the US imports every day, this increase is nonetheless quite significant.

Some sources indicate that the desired source for this SPR crude will be increased domestic capacity. Even if domestic sources do supply this additional crude, the tapering production of mature domestic fields and increasing demand levels should force import levels to increase by at least 110,000 barrels per day in order to fill the SPR to its desired level. Over the last 12 months, the largest sources of crude imports have been Canada, Mexico, Saudi Arabia, Venezuela, and Nigeria. Together these five countries have supplied nearly 70% of US imports over the past year.

### Affect on Tankers

Regarding the affect of this SPR increase (and the 110,000 barrel per day increase in import levels), we assume that further imports will continue to flow in proportion with crude imports over the last twelve months. Thus the percentages used in the table below match exactly with those on the pie chart on the previous page. In addition, we also assume that the incremental imports from these places will continue to be imported by the same ship sizes as charterers are currently using from these regions. For AG crude that means that imports will be almost entirely on VLCCs; for West African crude, an even split between VLCCs and Suezmaxes; for Caribbean crude nearly 100% on Aframaxes, and for Canadian crude almost all will be imported via pipeline. One can see that the annual increase in cargoes for each vessel size should be relatively low, by our logic.

The proposed increase in the SPR must still be approved by Congress before it can become reality. Once approved, though, US import demand could increase by 110,000 barrels per day at a time when supply of tankers of all sizes remains tight relative to demand. Even a small increase in crude cargoes headed to the US should push ton-mile demand higher.
