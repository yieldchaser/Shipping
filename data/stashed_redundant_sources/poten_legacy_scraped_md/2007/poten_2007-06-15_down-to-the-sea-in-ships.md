# Down to the Sea in Ships

It is generally agreed that the quality of petroleum shipping has improved greatly in recent years due to tanker owners' commitment to quality in combination with the stringent standards of charterers. While there have been changes in ship design in efforts to meet safety and environmental standards, one concern remains on the horizon. As the world fleet of ships continues to grow, some are apprehensive about the human element and the ability to crew ships. ### Staying Seaborne

In addition to maintaining high standards of vessel quality, owners must somehow attract and persuade officers into remaining at sea. This is increasingly difficult in an environment that involves the inherent and obvious risks of seafaring, long assignments and time away from families, as well as exposure to potential criminal liability. Meanwhile, career opportunities ashore are readily available to seafarers with less associated dangers and competitive salaries. In attempts to ensure that there are enough officers and crew members to serve their fleets, some forward thinking owners are even working to establish their own maritime academies. Given the large capital commitment to fleet expansion, operators will have to intensify efforts to make a career at sea an attractive option to the global workforce.
