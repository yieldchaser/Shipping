# A Long Way Back

### Oil demand recovery not expected until 2022

Last week, the three main oil market analysis agencies (International Energy Agency (IEA), OPEC and the US Energy Information Administration (EIA)), published their short-term oil market forecasts for 2020 and 2021. This week we will take a look at their outlook for oil demand recovery, after Covid-19 devastated demand in Q2, and what this might mean for the tanker markets.

The IEA estimates that global oil demand in the second quarter of 2020 declined to 83.2 Mb/d from 99.3 Mb/d in the prior year (-16.2%), as many countries announced restrictions to slow the spread of Covid-19. Transportation fuels, which represents the majority of oil demand, were especially hard hit by lockdowns as businesses closed offices and told people to work from home where possible and airlines cancelled many flights. While the restrictions hit jet fuel demand particularly hard (OECD Jet/Kerosene demand was down by 2.8 Mb/d or 60% in April compared to 2019), in volume, gasoline demand decreased even more (-3.6 Mb/d). Many countries started to ease restrictions in May and June and oil demand is gradually recovering. So far, some countries had to re-instate certain constraints to combat flare-ups of Covid-19, but those were generally more localized than the earlier restrictions.

The first graph shows the IEA regional breakdown of the oil demand outlook compared to 2019. The Asia Pacific region, which had the first cases and started lockdowns earlier, is expected to show a smaller decline than most other regions in 2020 and a quicker recovery in 2021. Europe and the Americas are expected to show a stronger decline at 10.7% and 9.7%, respectively. The IEA forecasts a stronger recovery in 2021 for Europe than for the Americas, while China's 2021 demand is expected to exceed 2019 and Indian demand will be very close to 2019.

The second chart compares the recovery paths forecasted by the oil analysis agencies. The chart shows the global oil demand for each quarter in 2020 and 2021 compared to the same period in 2019. As can be seen, none of the forecasts show a full recovery by the end of 2021. The EIA is the most optimistic for 2021 oil markets with an average decline of 1.08 Mb/d compared to 2019 while IEA is the most pessimistic with a shortfall of 2.89 Mb/d and OPEC is about halfway with -2.05 Mb/d. Additionally, the EIA has raised their 2021 forecast by 290 Kb/d compared to July (on more positive lockdown assumptions and higher petrochemical demand in China) while IEA lowered their outlook by 240 Kb/d, mainly due to prolonged weakness in the aviation sector. OPEC left their demand outlook unchanged from the July issue.

So, what does this mean for the tanker industry? We believe that the slow recovery of global oil demand will generally have a negative impact on the crude tanker markets in the coming quarters. This is reinforced by continued deliveries of tonnage for the remainder of 2020 and 2021 (some have been delayed by the impact of Covid-19).

Additionally, global oil inventories are still elevated. The IEA reports that OECD land-based inventories were at 4.8 Bln bbls (109 days of demand), compared to 4.5 bn bbls (94 days) a year earlier. Additionally, Lloyd's List Intelligence estimates that currently 272 Mln bbls of oil are in floating storage, compared to 116 Mln bbls a year ago.

Tanker scrapping has been very low so far this year, which is not surprising, given the generally high freight rates over the last 10 months. If the market remains weak, demolition sales will likely pick up, offsetting at least some of the deliveries.

One major positive factor for the market is the fact that the orderbook is declining quickly and very few ships are being ordered. After 2021, deliveries of new tonnage will decline dramatically, right when demand is expected to increase again, which could lay the foundation for a couple of strong years.
