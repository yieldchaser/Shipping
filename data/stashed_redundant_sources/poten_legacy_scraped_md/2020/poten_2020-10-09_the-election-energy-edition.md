# The Election Energy Edition

### Where do President Trump and Vice President Biden stand?

On Tuesday, November 3 rd , the citizens of the United States will go to the polls to elect a new president. The choice is between the Republican incumbent, Mr. Trump and his challenger from the Democratic party, Mr. Biden. Energy and energy policy play an unusually large role in this year's election and both candidates have starkly different views on the future of the oil industry. Who wins the election will likely have a significant impact on the oil and tanker industry in the coming years.

In his three and a half years in office, President Trump has been a strong advocate of the domestic oil and gas industry. Early on, his administration fast-tracked the approval of the Dakota Access Pipeline and gave the approval to advance the Keystone XL pipeline (although both pipelines continue to be challenged in court). Mr. Trump has also promoted oil & gas production (including fracking) across the board, reduced regulatory oversight and relaxed fuel efficiency standards. Mr. Trump has also announced the U.S. withdrawal from the Paris Climate Accord. On the foreign policy side, the U.S. has taken a hard line against Iran, Venezuela and China. The U.S. pulled out of the Iran Nuclear deal and re-imposed strict sanctions on the regime in Tehran. Similar actions were taken against Venezuela. Both countries are members of OPEC and their production and exports have suffered. The trade war with China has impacted crude oil exports to the country that continues to be the world's largest importer. The Covid-19 pandemic has had a significant impact on global oil markets and also led to a material decline in U.S. production, in particular from shale. However, the expectation is that, if Mr. Trump gets re-elected, U.S. shale production will likely recover, even though the rapid growth of the past may not resume. Under another Trump term, environmental policies and regulations will largely stay the same, as will tax legislation and foreign policy.

The Biden platform will bring big changes to the oil industry. Even though Mr. Biden has denied that he will ban fracking outright, his administration will most likely increase regulatory oversight, tighten environmental regulations (e.g. put limitations on methane emissions and flaring) and change the permitting policy and/or royalty rates on federal lands, where 25% of U.S. oil is produced. A Biden administration will also propose to increase corporate tax rates and to eliminate subsidies for the oil and gas industry as well as withdraw the approval of the Keystone XL pipeline. On the foreign policy side, the Biden administration will re-enter the Paris Climate Accord as well as the Iran nuclear deal (if possible). A president Biden may not be as tough on Venezuela and could also pursue a de-escalation of the trade dispute with China. Overall, Mr. Biden is more focused on addressing climate change and promoting renewable energy and reducing America's dependence on fossil fuels.

What will be the implications for the oil and tanker industry of a Trump victory versus a Biden win? Under a Biden presidency, the domestic oil industry, in particular shale, will face substantial headwinds. This could lead to significantly lower production and less exports. Canadian producers will also be impacted by the cancellation of Keystone XL. Removing or relaxing sanctions on Iran will increase movements from the Middle East, most likely to Asia, to some extent replacing U.S. sales. The situation in Venezuela is different. A removal of sanctions on the Maduro regime will not immediately have a material impact on production and exports. That will take time. Over time, a Biden administration will attempt to reduce the production and refining of fossil fuels aligning with similar trends in Europe.

The tanker industry will face a different world in the next five to ten years, regardless who will be in the White House, but the changes will come sooner under a Biden administration than with Mr. Trump at the helm. A Biden win will accelerate the transition away from fossil fuels towards more renewables. Lower oil demand and production from the U.S. (combined with similar trends in Europe) will increase the importance of the Middle East OPEC as a source of supply and Asia as the only driver of demand growth. This will dampen the recovery prospects for the tanker market in 2021 and beyond.
