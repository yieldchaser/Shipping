# Summer Doldrums

Recent weeks have seen earnings on benchmark tanker voyages threaten to break below operating costs amid an oversupply of vessels. The specter of vessel deliveries to come and indications of a slower economic recovery have led some to conclude from this vantage point that tanker markets will struggle for the remainder of 2010. This year's summer struggles certainly reflect the structural challenges facing the market, but it is important to keep in mind the seasonal influences which tend to drag the tanker market down during this time of year.

A number of factors can lead to a slowdown in the tanker market as August winds to a close. Extended summer vacations in worldwide shipping centers, combined with refinery maintenance and reduced demand for heating oil in Western markets, can contribute to a slowdown in import activity. This increases the supply of vessels seeking employment, typically producing a dip in voyage earnings during the third quarter. In each of the past three years, earnings for VLCCs on the benchmark Arabian Gulf - Far East voyage during the last week in August have been below average earnings for the second quarter and well below average earnings seen in the fourth quarter, as shown in the chart below.

### Seasonal Sensitivity

With the presumed peak of gasoline demand during summer driving season mostly behind them, refiners often choose to close for maintenance near the end of the summer, reducing demand for imports of crude oil. After reaching a three‐year high of 91.5 % in July, refinery utilization fell to 87.7% in the week ending August 20 th , as shown in the chart below. With declining utilizations set to slow vessel demand, tankers returning from storage duty have increased vessel supply to the detriment of owners.

### U.S. Refinery Utilization

Despite the understandable focus on demand centers of the developing world, seasonal demand patterns in Western markets are still very important factors in international oil and tanker markets. As such, there is reason to believe market conditions will improve as the year continues, even if demand growth is propelled mostly by countries outside the Organization of Economic Cooperation and Development (OECD). While it is becoming more difficult to remember the heyday of the recent past, those languishing in struggling tanker markets as August winds to a close still have good reason to hold on through these summer doldrums.
