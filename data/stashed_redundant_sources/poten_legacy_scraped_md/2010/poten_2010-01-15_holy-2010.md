# Holy 2010!

Just when it seemed like the tanker market was poised to hobble along for another year, spot market freight rates have again surprised everyone. Freezing temperatures across Asia, Europe and the United States helped set off a rally in tanker markets this week. While the challenges of continual fleet growth and uncertain economic recovery remain, 2010's strong start has certainly given a much‐needed boost to market sentiment.

As shown in the chart below, tightening vessel availabilities combined with an increased lifting program have helped lift VLCC rates significantly over the past five days on the benchmark Arabian Gulf - Far East (260 kt) voyage. The physical supply of vessels in position to take cargo requirements is a key factor in the direction freight rates take, and often acts to help narrow the gap between vessel supply and demand. As illustrated below, the next 30 day VLCC availabilities have been reduced, which has helped to prop up freight rates. Earnings on the voyage, which averaged $25,840 per day last year, have been pushed up rapidly over the past week to their current level of $86,900 per day.

### Let it Snow!

The seasonal increase in activity has been important, but inclement weather has emerged as the main driver of the recent strength in the tanker market. Tankers plying through seasonal rough seas and reduced daylight hours, especially those attempting to traverse ice‐clogged waterways, are more likely to encounter delays. A temporary closure of the Houston Ship Channel hindered tanker traffic into the US Gulf, while increases in the Bosporus Straits Delays have slowed exports from Black Sea suppliers.

Freezing Northern Hemisphere temperatures have also given a boost to heating oil demand, which is already risen enough to coax some of the distillate currently in floating storage into markets onshore. Rising heating oil demand has coincided with increased product imports from the United Kingdom into the United States, bringing with it an increase in rates on the product tanker market as well.

### East Meets West

While fixtures headed east in advance of the upcoming Chinese New Year have played an role in driving the rate spike, there are also some positive signs for Western demand within the recent resurgence of spot market rates. The volume of VLCC liftings from the Arabian Gulf are currently at 94 for January, above the one‐year average of 89 liftings per month but slightly below the number of liftings seen in January 2009. As shown in the chart below, western‐bound cargoes make up a bigger percentage of these fixtures than they have at any time since December 2008. This hints at signs of recovery in the West, but the steadiness of this trend remains unclear.
