# Russia Turns Up The Volume

### Crude oil exports to China are increasing

Russian crude oil production and exports have been in the news lately. It is being reported that Russia is shipping more crude to China and less to Europe. This shift in target market seems logical since the Chinese market is bigger and growing faster. This could lead to significant changes in tanker trading patterns and ton-mile demand. How big these changes will be and where they will impact the various crude oil tanker segments depends on a number of factors, such as which crude (if any) Russia is displacing in China and where Europe is sourcing the shortfall (if needed).

The most recent trigger for increased flows to China seems to have been the opening of an additional spur of the East Siberia Pacific Ocean Pipeline (ESPO) into China, doubling the capacity from 300,000 barrels per day (b/d) to 600,000 b/d. This second spur of the pipeline opened on the 1 st of January 2018. Chinese imports from Russia in 1Q2018 increased by 166,000 b/d compared to 4Q2017, which can be attributed to the increase in pipeline capacity. The ESPO crude flowing to China is a high- quality blend (34.8 degrees API gravity and 0.62% sulphur) that compares favorably to Dubai (31 degrees API and 2% sulphur). ESPO crude is also transported to the Far Eastern Pacific port of Kozmino to facilitate seaborne shipments. The crude coming out of Kozmino is sold to a wide variety of customers, predominantly in the Asia Pacific region. More than 50% of the Kozmino flows are also destined for China.

Russian exports to China have been growing for many years and flows are expected to continue to expand facilitated by the additional pipeline capacity. In 2016, Russia surpassed Saudi Arabia as the largest crude oil supplier to China. However, since Russia signed on to the OPEC production cuts in January 2017, there should be (in theory) no room to expand overall exports. When the OPEC deal was announced, Russia committed to a 300,000 b/d reduction in output. However, Russia has not always adhered 100% to its cutback target. In March 2018, Russia fell below 90% compliance with an output reduction of 260,000 b/d. Russian news agency Tass reported yesterday that the country's compliance improved to 95.2% in April with a production cut of 285,600 b/d.

Despite this less-than-perfect compliance record, it is clear that growing sales to China would need to be compensated for by reductions in exports elsewhere. Most analysts expect that Russia will be selling less crude to Europe, although early statistics showing tanker movements from the Baltic and the Black Sea do not seem to show that yet. Russia could also decide to export less through the Druzhba (Friendship) pipeline system to Eastern Europe and Germany. Unfortunately, we don't have access to detailed statistics on the flows through what is considered the world's longest oil pipeline and one of the biggest oil pipeline networks in the world. However, the International Energy Agency (IEA) regularly reports flows through the Druzhba Pipeline and throughput in 2017 and 2018 YTD seems to be stable around 1.0 million b/d. Given that pipelines are typically the cheapest modes of transportation, we expect that the Druzhba throughput will remain relatively stable at current levels.

Assuming that Russian crude oil exports to China will continue to increase, it is likely that exports to Europe will indeed be reduced. One possible scenario would be for Europe to import more Middle Eastern crude, which would most likely arrive in VLCCs (if going to Northern Europe) or Suezmaxes (Mediterranean Europe), replacing current Baltic and Black Sea moves on Aframaxes. More U.S. shale oil could arrive in Europe as well, but this crude is much lighter (and has a lower distillate yield) than what Europe typically imports from Russia and is therefore more likely to replace barrels from Nigeria and Libya. The immediate impact of increased pipeline movements from Russia to China on the tanker market in the Pacific is limited since China's demand growth exceeds the import growth from Russia.
