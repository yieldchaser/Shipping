# Tanker Supply Cloud Has A Silver Lining

### The orderbook may not be as scary as it looks

We recently updated the age profile statistics for the tanker fleet. The situation as of January 1, 2017 is shown in Figure 1. At first glance, this is not a pretty picture. Based on the delivery schedule of the current orderbook, deliveries of new tankers in 2017 will be at the highest level since 2009, which came at the end of the tanker market "super cycle". While, it is difficult to see how the market can absorb such a large orderbook without further pressure on tanker rates and prices, there may be some mitigating factors to consider.

Let's look at the current fleet profile and orderbook in more detail to see if there are differences by segment. On the crude tanker side, the Suezmaxes stand out: if all Suezmaxes that are scheduled for delivery this year leave the shipyards on time, we will add 10.1 million deadweight (mdwt) to the fleet, well above the 2009 record of 7.1 mdwt, and two and a half times total 2016 deliveries. The recent high-water mark for VLCCs was 2011, when 65 new units hit the water (20.1 mdwt). This year, the yards are scheduled to deliver 15.9 mdwt (21% less than 2011, but 11% more than last year). Aframax & LR2 deliveries in this year (9.4 mdwt in total) are likely to fall well short of the record of 2009 (10.5 mdwt), but the composition is radically different. In 2009, the deliveries were primarily crude vessels (75%), while the orderbook of Aframax-sized vessels to be delivered this year is dominated by the coated LR2s (61% of the total). This shift towards coated vessels is even more pronounced in the Panamax/LR1 segment. Over the last 5 years, this vessel class has become almost exclusively a product tanker segment. Deliveries in this segment peaked in the 2005-2008 period with deliveries in excess of 3 mdwt in each of these years. Expected deliveries for 2017 are a much smaller - but still sizeable - 2.4 mdwt.

In the smaller product carrier segments, the orderbook for delivery in 2017 is more moderate, compared to earlier years. For MRs, peak delivery years were 2008 (125 vessels) and 2009 (134 vessels), equivalent to more than 6 mdwt in each year. The advance of the 'ECO-vessel' triggered another delivery boom in 2015 (105 MRs). Compared to these years, the 77 vessels scheduled for 2017 look relatively modest. The one segment where the orderbook is truly low is the Handysize fleet. These smaller product tankers seem to have fallen out of favor compared to the larger MRs. Only 18 are expected to be delivered in 2017.

The main mitigating factor when considering the above fleet statistics is the fact that we are comparing actual deliveries in the past with scheduled deliveries in the future. History has shown that there can be significant discrepancies between scheduled and actual deliveries, especially in periods with a challenging rate environment, as illustrated by Figure 2. Delivery delays and order cancellations picked up considerably after the tanker market dropped in 2009. For the period 2005-2016 we have tracked the level of actual deliveries as a percentage of scheduled deliveries and the variations by segment and by year are significant. VLCCs have the highest average delivery ratio (80%), ranging from a high of 100% in 2006/2008 to a low of 53% in 2013. The high delivery ratio of VLCCs is not surprising. The orderbook data is more reliable, since VLCCs are mostly built at large, reputable yards and owners are typically well capitalized. Product carrier orderbook statistics, on the other hand, appear less reliable and these tankers a low average delivery ratio (around 50%). Even during the strong freight markets of 2005-2008, only 65% of the vessels that were reportedly on order were delivered. In the 2010-2012 period, the delivery percentage fell to 25%, reflecting delays, cancellations and shipyard closures following the financial crisis (in addition to inaccurate data). The delivery percentages of the other segments are between 62% (Suezmaxes) and 76% (Aframaxes).

Based on the historical variability of vessel deliveries and its apparent correlation with the freight market, 25-40% of the crude oil tanker orderbook and up to 50% of the product carriers scheduled for 2017 might not be delivered this year. The orderbook remains high, but the outlook for tanker supply in 2017 is better than a cursory look suggests.
