# Tanker Wars 2.0

### Tensions rising in the Arabian Gulf

Two oil tankers, the 110,000 dwt LR2 Front Altair (built 2016) and the 27,000 dwt product tanker Kokuka Courageous (built 2010) were attacked in the Gulf of Oman on Thursday morning local time. Both vessels were carrying petrochemical feedstock, the Front Altair naphtha destined for Taiwan and the Kokuka Courageous methanol to Singapore. While both vessels were severely damaged, the crews of both ships are safe and unharmed. The immediate impact of the incident was rather predictable: oil prices jumped as did gold and war risk premiums for tanker voyages into the Arabian Gulf. Tanker owners and charterers alike are observing the situation before they commit to more cargoes and ships in the region. The U.S. has said that it will consider providing naval escorts for commercial ships traveling through the Straits of Hormuz, the chokepoint at the southern point of the Arabian Gulf. According to Lloyd's List Intelligence, an average of 16.8 million barrels per day of crude oil was transported through the Straits of Hormuz in 2018. Significant volumes of refined products, as well as LNG, also use this route to export markets. Accusations are flying as to who may be responsible for the attacks and, not surprisingly, the accused parties are denying involvement.

The longer-term implications for the tanker market are dependent on what happens next. Geopolitical tensions have already risen to a very high level and further attacks and/or proven involvement of "state actors" (i.e. Iran) could result in a significant escalation of the conflict. Yesterday's incident followed similar attacks on four tankers (including two Saudi vessels) off the United Emirates' Port of Fujairah on May 12.

Many people have referred back to the Iran-Iraq "tanker war" of the 1980s as an example of what could happen if the situation escalates. In 1981, Iraq initiated attacks on ships steaming to and from Iranian ports at the northern end of the Arabian Gulf. In 1984, Iraq increased the rate of its attacks and expanded the geographic scope to include the oil-loading complex at Kharg Island. This caused Iran to initiate its own attacks, making the tanker war a two-way affair. According to data from the U.S. Naval Institute, Iraq initiated 43 attacks on ships in the Arabian Gulf prior to 1984. In the period from 1984-1987 another 408 ships were attacked, 168 by Iran and 240 by Iraq. Although the vast majority consisted of oil & product tankers, other vessel types were attacked as well.

Iraq's objective of the tanker war was to provoke Iran to retaliate with extreme measures, such as closing the Strait of Hormuz, which would bring American intervention. However, Iran limited its attacks to Iraqi shipping, leaving the Strait of Hormuz open to general passage.

The superpowers did eventually get involved. In 1987, the U.S. launched "Operation Earnest Will", where the United States Navy offered to provide protection to foreign tankers reflagged and flying the U.S. flag. In 1988 Iraq and Iran agreed to a ceasefire.

Obviously, the current situation is different from the mid-1980s. There is no armed conflict between Iran and Iraq or any other Middle Eastern country. In the 1980s the main destination for the oil from the Middle East was the U.S. and Europe. Now more than 80% of exports from the Arabian Gulf are headed for Asia, in particular China. This raises the question of how the Chinese government will react to potential supply disruptions from the region since they have a lot at stake. However, there are also similarities. The regional rivalries between Iran and countries like Kuwait and Saudi Arabia are still there. The government of the U.S. and Iran eye each other with suspicion. And last but not least, the tanker market, which was in the middle of the worst depression in its history in the mid-1980s, is again in dire straits.

The hope is that reasonable minds prevail, and a further escalation of the conflict is averted. Tanker crews should not be put in harms way and be used as pawns in a geopolitical chess game.
