# Crude Prices Climb While Spot VLCC Activity Falls Why?

### Crude Prices Climb While Spot VLCC Activity Falls

### Why?

According to the Associated Press and Reuters, the reasons for oil bouncing around $66 per barrel are: Ø World oil demand at peak levels with little spare capacity.

Ø Worries over potential supply disruptions.

Ø Closure of the U.S. Embassy and consulates in Saudi Arabia as a precaution against possible terrorist attacks.

Ø Iran resuming uranium conversion activities that might prompt the EU and the United States to seek UN sanctions against Iran.

Ø Rebel attacks on oil pipelines in eastern India (no report on damage, if any).

Ø Fear of refinery breakdowns such as a weekend fire at Sunoco's refinery in Philadelphia that interrupted operations plus about a half-dozen other unexpected outages at U.S. refineries.

Ø Drawdown of U.S. gasoline inventories (in the midst of the peak driving season, what's the surprise?).

Ø Possible hurricanes developing from tropical storms in the Gulf of Mexico that could affect offshore oil field output or onshore refinery operations.

Ø A growing sense of fear that something not included above will occur.

Ø Some combination of the above. Strained Production? The explanation for high oil prices often cites strained production. If so, we would expect VLCC fixture activity to be up; but as seen on the next page, fixture activity has declined from year earlier levels.

Gasoline 2000 2001 2002 2003 2004 2005 Current 0 Maybe We Are Looking in the Wrong Place Maybe the responsibility of high crude oil prices lies with hedge funds loading up on crude oil futures, which cause near-term crude future prices to jump. This then becomes the new marker for OPEC cash sales. In other words, traders are driving up the price for near-term futures, which then drives up the cash market, which then spurs traders and hedge fund managers to take even larger positions in futures, which, in turn... well you have the picture of a positive feedback system. Suppose this bubble of speculation bursts and oil prices come down? This will encourage oil consumption, oil imports, tanker demand as reflected in fixture activity, and last, but not least, tanker rates, which is more to our liking.
