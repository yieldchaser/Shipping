# Big Game Hunting

Now that the year has closed on the strongest tanker market in a generation, the time has come to reflect upon those charterers who helped make it so. We know from reviewing past years activity that about 25% of the charterers account for about 80% of the activity. 2004 was no different. Excluding failed fixtures (a surprising 20% of activity) the Poten Spot Fixture Database recorded 10,491 fixtures. Some worldwide spot market highlights VLCC fixtures up 7% from 2003 level of 1,768 to 1,889; Suezmax fixtures roughly equal year-on-year with 2100 fixtures in 2004; Aframax also equal to 2003 levels at 4,651;

### Reflecting decreased trading opportunities due to EU regulations on the

carriage of fuel oil, the Panamax sector fixtures declined from 1,235 to 975 for 2004; Dirty Handymax fixtures were up in 2004 by about 100 fixtures to 428 as the above noted Panamax cargoes generally went on these smaller size vessels.

### Who's the Biggest Charterer?

As in the past, Shell maintains the top ranking as the largest spot charterer of crude oil and fuel oil vessels. A total of 235 different charterers fixed at least one ship, but as noted below, the top 20 companies account for just over 50% of the activity. Although the overall total of reported spot market fixture activity is essentially unchanged, with more crude oil actually moving, it is clear that more cargoes are moving under contract arrangements between charterers and owners.

Which Is the Most Popular Ship Size? Aframaxes - the largest dirty tanker fleet and the workhorse in terms of flexibility and short voyages were once again the most popular in the spot market. Aframaxes made up 44% of 2004 spot voyages with Suezmaxes registering a distant second at 22%.

VLCC charterers - Major Oil Companies and Governments Lead With 1889 fixtures the VLCC spot market lagged the Suezmaxes by 10% even though the fleet is significantly larger - mainly due to the long AG-USG voyages that employ the big ships for months at a time. ### Adam

Much the Same as Last Year 2004 saw record tanker rates and fixing activity in the spot market just like 2003. In addition, the main charterers have remained the same with the major integrated oil companies leading the way as usual. So it seems another year brings more of the same and with more and more industry consolidation these days - 2005 examination of "Who's the Biggest?" may see the biggest players just get bigger.
