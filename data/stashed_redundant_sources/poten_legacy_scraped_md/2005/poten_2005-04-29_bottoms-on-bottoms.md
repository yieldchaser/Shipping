# Bottoms on Bottoms!

### Transporting Fuel Oil from Europe to the Far East

This week's report is based on a speech by Larry Axelrod of Poten & Partners on Transporting Fuel Oil from Europe to the Far East . The speech was made at the IPE/Confidence Conference on Fuel Oil and Heating Oil - Processing, Trading, Transportation in Antwerp, Belgium, on April 21-23.

Russian Fuel Oil Exports Skyrocketing Russian seaborne exports of fuel oil from Baltic and Black Sea ports have nearly tripled from 15.8 million tons in 1998 to 43.2 million tons in 2004.

Baltic Sea The surge in fuel oil exports stems primarily from the substitution of natural gas for fuel oil in Russia. Over 70% of Russian fuel oil exports are from the Baltic Sea, with key exporting ports being Ventspils (Latvia), Klaipeda (Lithuania), Tallinn (Estonia), and St. Petersburg. Floating storage off St. Petersburg allow loading of small cargoes into larger sized vessels. Ship-to-ship transfers offshore the UK or Denmark or loading in Rotterdam allow VLCC shipments to the Far East. Black Sea loadings take place from Novorossiysk and Tuapse (Russia) and Odessa (Ukraine). Floating storage is available offshore Kavkaz (Russia) and Kerch (Ukraine) for larger shipments. Chinese Fuel Oil Imports Skyrocketing What is exported must be imported somewhere. Somewhere today is China, whose economy has not only been booming, but at its present rate of growth will double every seven years.

While all tanker classes have benefited from the rising movement of fuel oil from Russia to China, the proportion of cargoes carried in larger-sized classes has been growing. For instance, VLCCs carried 39% of the volume of fuel oil in 2002 and 54% in 2004.

### And of the Future?

As long as China's economy keeps growing, even at a more subdued rate, fuel oil can be expected to continue to move in large quantities from Europe to the Far East over the coming years.
