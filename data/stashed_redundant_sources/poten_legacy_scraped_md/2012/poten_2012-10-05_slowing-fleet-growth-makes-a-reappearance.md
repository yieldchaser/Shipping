# Slowing Fleet Growth Makes a Reappearance

### sectors diverged on

Although the current macroeconomic environment is weighing on both the dirty and clean tanker sectors, the earnings performance of these two markets has diverged since 2010 from different supply trajectories. After single-hull removals slowed dirty tanker fleet growth during 2009-10, supply has accelerated from heavy deliveries, aggravated by the 2010 ordering burst. In contrast, the abrupt halt in clean tanker ordering that started in 2008 has flowed through the orderbook, slowing product tanker fleet growth. Continued declines in clean growth, coupled with regional refinery displacement, has underpinned the product tanker hypothesis for years, but weaker dirty tanker fundamentals and sustained supply growth have contributed to an increasingly-gloomy outlook for the sector. Slowing deliveries, continued demolition and restrained ordering, however, are suggesting further deceleration in dirty supply growth, which should drive the ultimate recovery in the dirty sector by 2014.

1q07 1q08 1q09 1q10 1q11 1q12 Following this impulse of tonnage supply, however, dirty tanker deliveries slowed and demolition picked up during the month of August, allowing growth to slow to a 3.8% annualised rate during 3q12. Still, dirty tanker demolition did experience its seasonal pattern of declining during the summer monsoon months on the Sub-continent, slipping to 2.4 mdwt in 3q12, after hitting 3.4 mdwt of removals in 1q12. Although the pace of VLCC demolition eased, Suezmax and Aframax owners maintained the demolition pace, with six Suezmaxes and five Afras leaving the fleet during 3q12.

### Suezmax and Aframax

scrapping has remained steady during 2012 This demolition response from the sustained earnings malaise is encouraging, as owners may finally address the supply/demand imbalance and take supply actions that would allow the sector to escape the 80% utilisation area that is keeping earnings near cash operating costs. One constraining factor on scrapping has been the lack of elderly demolition candidates, as the IMO single-hull regulations forced the removal of middle- aged tonnage. With a modern fleet and weak market conditions, however, 20-year old vessels have become untenable in the spot market and the average demolition age has rapidly declined from 25.6 years in 2008 to 20.1 years in 2012 to date.

### Dirty supply could slow

to 1% by end-2013 In fact, the greatest forecast risk to the current negative outlook for the dirty tanker sector is to the upside. Any meaningful recovery in aggregate demand and industrial production during 2h13, occurring during a slowing in tanker supply, could provide a strong utilisation and earnings response. Such a macroeconomic improvement may seem fanciful in this uncertain environment, but it remains an upside risk.
