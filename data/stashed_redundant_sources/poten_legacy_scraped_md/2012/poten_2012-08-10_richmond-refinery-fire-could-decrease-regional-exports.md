# Richmond Refinery Fire Could Decrease Regional Exports

### shift Latin American

The fire at Chevron's 257 kbpd Richmond, CA refinery on Monday evening has removed 12% of the state's distillation capacity, boosting clean product prices and prompting market speculation that increased product imports could bolster MR demand. Although the refinery situation remains unclear, the continued operation of secondary units and available spare capacity on the US West Coast suggest that a reduction in exports to the west coasts of Mexico and South America could balance the markets, potentially supporting MR trades out of the US Gulf as replacement supply.

### Richmond refinery fire

likely to curtail exports, shift Latin American sourcing On Monday evening, a fire broke out following a diesel leak near a pump at the Richmond refinery's sole crude distillation unit (CDU). Chevron shut down the CDU, but the other units were unaffected and remain operational. As shown in the chart below, the price response from the blaze was immediate, with San Francisco and Los Angeles prices of gasoline surging by about 11% (30¢/gallon), while jet fuel and diesel jumped by about 7% (10¢/gallon) in the succeeding days. Chevron has not indicated when the CDU will restart or at what capacity the remaining units operate.

In addition to Richmond, Chevron owns and operates a second refinery in California, the 269 kbpd El Segundo facility in Los Angeles. Both refineries account for about 25% of California's net installed refining capacity.

As shown in the chart below, PADD 5, which includes the states of California, Alaska, Arizona, Hawaii, Nevada, Oregon and Washington, has been net long diesel during the past few years, following shortages prior to the financial crisis. Diesel exports reached 76 kpbd in 2011 on the subsequent surpluses generated from reduced demand. PADD 5 has traditionally been net short gasoline, requiring large transfers of gasoline blending components from PADD 3 and finished gasoline transfers from PADD 4.

The region remains net short jet fuel, following large shortages prior to the crisis that included imports hitting 115 kbpd in 2007. With jet fuel demand now 16-17% lower from the peak of 507 kbpd in 2007, jet imports and exports have been nearly at parity during 2008-11, with small PADD transfers supporting the regional balance.

### California's

gasoline exports varied between 15 kbpd and 30 kbpd from 2008 to 2011. The bulk of the gasoline moves to Mexico and Canada on MRs.

California's jet fuel exports declined from 13 kbpd in 2008 to 5 kbpd in 2011. The bulk moves to Canada.

California's diesel exports varied between 32 and 65 kbpd from 2008 to 2012. More than half of the diesel exports moved to Mexico and Chile on MRs.

Depending on the Richmond refinery's typical clean petroleum product yields and its share of California's production, the state could potentially increase its product imports to meet demand, typically sourced from North Asia or Canada. Still, refiners may be more likely to cut exports of gasoline, jet fuel and diesel to the west coasts of Mexico and South America. In the short run, shipments from the US Gulf Coast could supply these regions, to replace the product typically sourced from PADD5, providing a potential boost to the MR market from both a tonne-mile demand perspective and by temporarily positioning vessels into the Pacific Basin, and away from the USG and Caribs markets.

### Californian refiners

would likely cut clean product exports.

Still, alternatives to higher product imports could come into play. With the CDU at Richmond disabled, refiners in California would likely benefit from the momentary jump in the prices caused by the perception of diesel, gasoline and jet fuel shortages. PADD 5 refinery capacity utilizations have hovered above 80% during 2009 to 2011, suggesting that the remaining fourteen California refineries could increase their utilization rates to meet any drop in production and to take advantage of elevated margins. Moreover, with the Richmond's catalytic cracking and hydrocracking units still operational, the refinery could source additional Vacuum Gasoil (VGO) feedstock for the units, to help meet product demand. With traditional VGO sourcing for PADD5 from the Caribbean and Algeria, additional VGO imports could support dirty Panamax demand.
