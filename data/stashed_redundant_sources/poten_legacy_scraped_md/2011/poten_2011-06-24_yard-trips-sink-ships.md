# Yard Trips Sink Ships

Public companies, private equity groups and new entrants to the shipping markets convened at various finance conferences this week bringing both enthusiasm and raising valid questions about the long‐term prognosis for recovery in tanker freight rates. Recent comments made to the press and Wall Street have created some cause for concern as well‐established tanker shipping companies seem to appear somewhat cavalier about the oversupply situation. While we appreciate the confidence in the resilience of the market, tanker shipping companies should be cautioned that the overall demand climate seems well satisfied by the developing fleet for the near term.

As discussed a few weeks ago in Cover Your Asset , depressed markets can present lucrative opportunities to investors seeking to place capital, as long as prices are low and recovery is near. Low asset prices and interest rates present tempting opportunity for owners and investors, who bank on the play of inflation and the time‐value of money to justify investments. Shipping is a notoriously cyclical market, and an owner's relative success is largely a factor of timing (and luck). History shows that newbuilding activity tends to follow upticks in spot market earnings. Last year saw a burst of new orders, particularly in the Suezmax sector, following an uptick in spot market earnings in Q4 2010 and Q1 2011, as seen in the chart below.

However, looking at what has happened in the Suezmax sector over the past two years underscores the risk of relying too heavily on spot market trends to direct investment decisions.

### Bubble Trouble

Suezmax earnings took a nosedive with the rest of the tanker market in the third quarter of 2009, averaging only $8,300 per day on the West Africa to US Atlantic coast trade in the months between July and September of 2009. Earnings rallied over the first and second quarters of 2010 to average over $29,500 per day, a rate about 20% less than the long term historical average at the time. An ordering frenzy ensued. Over 50 newbuilding orders were reported in the sector over the course of 2010, the vast majority of which were booked between March and July. The timing of these orders suggests that the newbuilding market of 2010 was being led by an apparent upward trajectory of spot market rates.

Itchy trigger fingers may have seriously impeded a speedy recovery. Prior to these newbuilding orders, global demand estimates suggested a relatively quick rebound of Suezmax fleet utilization, defined as the ratio of demand to supply, as seen in the chart below.

### Hold Your Horses

Although it seems players may have gotten accustomed to the current freight rate environment, revenues are not stable enough just yet to make asset plays at their behest. Despite encouraging signs, it is still a weak market, and it is in the best interest of shipowning participants to nurse it back to health. The short‐term horizon of the public markets and the influx of other people's money have the potential to derail freight rate recovery. Sustained improvement will require owners take a longer view when contemplating new investments and consider carefully the repercussions of delivering new vessels into an adequately supplied market.
