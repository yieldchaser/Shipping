# Bunker Mentality

As tanker spot‐chartering rates have experienced a mild recovery in recent months, equity analysts and industry pundits alike have eagerly anticipated a rebound in tanker company stocks to little avail. For the past couple years, the steadily‐declining market capitalization of some of the biggest names in the business has come as no surprise given the general state of the freight environment. A wide variety of factors both material and psychological affect the performance of tanker equities in what appears to be a stag‐flationary economic environment in the United States. Rising bunker prices in conjunction with an apprehensive investing public are making upward movement in tanker equities difficult. As stock prices represent a forward view of the market, it may suggest Wall Street is guessing tanker earnings will remain range‐bound.

Recent stock price action has done little to engender positive sentiment, even in the face of tanker earnings coming off recent lows.

### Hunker Down and Bunker

The most compelling fundamental reason for weakness in the tanker company performance today is the dramatic rise in bunker costs. As outlined in Poten's March monthly Fuel Oil opinion The Squeeze is On , the price spike in bunkers has drastically eroded the slim profitability that ship owners were experiencing for the better part of the last 6 months. On the benchmark VLCC Arabian Gulf to Far East voyage, it is estimated that at $630 per metric ton, bunkering costs equate to 60% of total freight. This percentage of revenue has climbed significantly in recent months on the back of higher bunker prices, and compares with figures as low as 36% seen in June 2010. With margins being squeezed by rising bunker prices, it stands to reason tanker stocks will continue to face downside pressure until prices ease or tanker rates rise accordingly to truly compensate for the higher price. IFO 380 Marine Fuel Bunker Prices (Fujairah) Poten Tanker Freight Index Although ship owners attempt to naturally adjust charter rates to maintain levels of profitability, spikes in bunker prices can be difficult to capture given the drawn‐out nature of tanker charter negotiation and the corresponding time lag. This holds true particularly for un‐hedged owners who agree to a spot‐voyage deal basis going market rates and bunker prices only to find that, post‐fixture, their realized bunker costs can be up to 5% higher than what they initially estimated. This hidden cost is difficult to compensate for and a stealth threat to owner's profitability in the short term.

Tanker earnings are not the only factor that affect tanker sector equity prices, but they are an important fundamental factor in trying to determine which direction stock prices will move. Balance sheet maintenance, hedging, and internal investment decisions can all affect individual tanker stocks differently in spite of similar macro‐economic conditions. Investor sentiment also plays a significant factor in determining equity prices and can add a degree of irrationality to valuations. After all, as the saying goes, markets can remain irrational longer than you can remain solvent. Whether current valuations of tanker stocks are irrational (to the upside or downside) is anyone's guess, but inflationary fundamental headwinds still affect profitability in the sector and seem to indicate the path higher may remain difficult still.
