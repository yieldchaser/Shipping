# In‐dia for the Long Haul

The high crude oil price environment seen in 2011 has raised concern that inflationary pressure could interfere with the pace of global economic recovery. Some developing economies have attempted to control runaway inflation by limiting the price of fuel sold to domestic consumers, but just over a year ago, India began phasing out its subsidies for transportation fuels. The Finance Ministry acknowledged that the removal of subsidies would likely bring about an increase in inflation in the near term. However, this liberalization along with a tightening in quality has facilitated marketing to the United States and could increase demand for long range product tankers over the next few years.

The change in India's energy policy was implemented to help reduce India's $5.5 billion oil subsidy bill and boost profits at India's state‐owned refiners. While it was estimated that India's state‐owned refiners saw more than $20 billion in losses from the price controls in 2010 alone, the nation's private refiners have been able to shift their focus to international markets. ### The Rest is West

In addition to taking steps to make fuel prices more market driven, India has also implemented product specifications similar to the Euro III and Euro IV standard, ostensibly as a way to improve air quality and adhere to stricter environmental standards domestically. The tightening of quality has facilitated marketing of Indian transportation fuels to consuming nations in Europe and the United States on long range product tankers, which offer greater economies of scale for these long‐haul voyages compared to smaller MR sized product tankers.

The increase in reported long range (LR) tanker fixtures out of India has not been as dramatic as to consumption centers east of Suez, however the developments in trade patterns are noteworthy. ### To USA

To UKC European markets are better suited to absorb these larger quantities of clean products, as fewer ports along the eastern United States seaboard have terminal facilities large enough to accommodate LR2 sized product tankers and their cargoes. Clean product shipments on long range product tankers are most often transshipped via large terminals in the Caribbean, such as St. Croix (Hovensa).

Recently Bahamas Oil Refining Co. International Ltd. (Borco) announced that they have awarded a $40 million contract to construct 14 oil storage tanks in Freeport, Bahamas. Completion is set for second‐quarter 2012 and will add about 3.5 million barrels to their existing 21.6 million barrel complex. This beefing up of import infrastructure suggests that Indian transportation fuels are likely to continue making their way to pumps in the United States, as long as India's refiners have the extra barrels to spare.
