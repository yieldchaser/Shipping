# Uncle Sam's Slight of Hand

The move this week by President Obama and his administration to release Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR) crude oil should be seen as nothing more than a political stunt. Although an immediate short‐fall in supplies worldwide was touted as the impetus, the decision to release more barrels from the SPR was more likely aimed at quieting oil prices than satiating short‐term demand requirements. The tapping of 30 million emergency barrels raises questions about the motives, timing and most importantly, the accountability of the United States government with respect to oil policy and the use of the SPR as a tool to manipulate markets.

In theory: "if hurricanes or other unexpected physical conditions disrupt either crude oil imports or domestic production, the Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR) is ready to make replacement oil available to the extent approved by the President and/or Secretary of Energy." 1 At 727 million barrels, the SPR located in Louisiana is the largest stock‐pile of crude oil worldwide and holds enough oil to cover US consumption for roughly 38 days. On the back of the oil embargo in the early 1970's, the SPR was established to mitigate disruptions in physical oil supply and help ensure that United States' livelihood and economy would not be beholden to natural and man‐made disasters.

As shown in the chart below, total crude oil (including SPR) and refined product stocks are currently 4% lower than the all‐time high. For perspective, the peak level of 1.8 billion barrels is enough to cover the island of Manhattan under 10 feet of crude oil. Of the total stocks, the SPR has accounted for 40% on average since 2000.

The United States and other OECD economies combined would sell an incremental 60 million barrels from emergency reserves. The stated rationale behind the coordinated release was the 1.2 million barrel per day loss of Libyan production. As would be expected, benchmark crude oil prices dropped $4 per barrel on the news. However, over the past week crude oil prices have recovered to levels seen prior to the announcement.

The digestion of the news by the market highlights how perception can often become reality. Demand for refined products in the United States remains weak. The chart below shows refined product supply, an indicator of consumer demand, since 2007. Since reaching over 21 million barrels per day just four years earlier, demand for refined product has dwindled to about 80% of peak levels. Mandated fuel ethanol inclusion in the gasoline pool further exacerbates the losses in demand as the actual petroleum content is lower today.

### Life, Liberty and the Pursuit of Price Control

If demand in the United States called for additional production of gasoline, refiners produce as prices would warrant. Instead refiners are running at levels of 88% utilization nationally and a paltry 72% on the East Coast.

The slackened demand domestically for incremental crude oil and refined products makes it difficult to see how SPR barrels will be eagerly absorbed by the market. As SPR crude oil from the Gulf Coast is tendered on the basis of pipeline, vessel and barge delivery, it would seemingly point to a change in marine transportation demand for the Jones Act ships. Also, larger international tankers could petition for waivers to trade coastwise in the United States. On a practical basis, strategic petroleum reserve barrels will likely end up moving on inland tows and ocean going barges.

The American taxpayers should be aware of the possible ulterior motives of elected officials in Washington and whether they will soon be on the hook to pay for re‐stocking of reserves. Gasoline prices in the United States are high by historical standards because crude oil prices are high by historical standards; not because quantities are scarce. The plot of Washington to manufacture a dip in oil (read: gasoline) prices was foiled by the markets. Welcome to America where everything is for sale: crude oil, gasoline, and even re‐election.
