# Tanker Prospects 2012 ‐ 2016

The past year has been characterized by much pessimism. Freight rates have remained well below breakeven levels for many ship owners and frequently have been insufficient to even cover operating expenses. Crude oil demand is at all time highs and geopolitical unrest has further complicated the availability of crude oil. It would be expected that the confluence of these two factors would result in significantly improved freight rates. However, tanker newbuildings ordered prior to the global economic downturn continue to deliver into the market, resulting in an oversupply of vessels and consistently low freight rates. Rising costs are further complicating matters for tanker operators.

In general, industry participants are bracing themselves for more hard times ahead. Understanding current market drivers and potential wildcards is critical to staying afloat during these trying times. In this context, Poten & Partners is pleased to announce the release of its annual outlook Tanker Prospects 2012‐2016.

### Economy of Scaling Back

Even amid significant economic and energy consumption growth in emerging markets, the fragile state of mature economies has hindered a full scale global recovery. Lagging growth continues to pressure demand for petroleum in these regions. At present, the International Energy Agency (IEA) expects that combined crude oil consumption in the United States and the major European economies will erode by 400 kbd by the end of 2011 and will continue to fall an additional 190 kbd in 2012, as seen in the chart below.

Europe 5* China Total World As petroleum demand contracts in the United States and Europe, the health of the tanker market depends on emerging market economies, particularly those in Asia. China was responsible for 37% of the growth in crude oil demand in 2010 and is expected to account for over one third of total demand growth in the year to come. Loss in volumes to demand centers West of Suez are taking a toll on tanker demand. However, the diversification of crude oil sources and increased participation of Chinese companies in global crude oil trade lends support to overall ton mile development.

### Refining Matters

Price divergence among similar grades of crude oil has challenged the economic landscape for refiners dependent on Brent‐priced streams. The premium of the Brent crude oil price benchmark to West Texas Intermediate (WTI) has risen to an average of $18 per barrel since January 2011, as seen in the chart below.

5 Year Average This wide price spread has resulted in uneven refining prospects, particularly for some North American companies dependent on processing imported light sweet grades. This year saw Sunoco announce plans to divest itself of its Pennsylvania refineries, and ConocoPhillips has already begun to idle its Trainer, PA facility. The combination of these events could create a potential loss of almost 700 kbd of structural capacity on the US Atlantic coast. However, Motiva's planned expansion is anticipated to add over 325 kbd of refining capacity to the US Gulf coast by 2015.

If approved by the US government, construction of the Keystone XL pipeline could bring Canadian oil to PADD III refiners by 2013, possibly displacing crude oil import volumes to the US Gulf coast from abroad. Perhaps surprisingly, the relatively lower price of WTI along with lackluster domestic demand for clean products has enabled the United States to become a significant exporter. Developments in crude oil sourcing and pricing are likely to heavily influence petroleum trade flows over the medium term.

Tanker Prospects 2012‐2016 will provide medium term outlooks for the VLCC, Suezmax, Aframax, Panamax, and product carrier segments, incorporating the factors mentioned above among others.
