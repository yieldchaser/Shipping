# Pakistans Promise

### Another South Asian country is gaining prominence

Until recently, Pakistan was not a country that received a lot of attention in the oil and tanker markets. However, that has started to change after Donald Trump returned to the White House. To be clear, Pakistan's oil demand is relatively modest and is not a major player in the tanker markets, but there have been changes in recent months that caught people's attention and have raised Pakistan's profile. In this week's Tanker Opinion, we will discuss these developments and whether they could make a difference to the oil and tanker markets in the future.

Pakistan's oil industry is rather modest. Up-to-date information on its proven oil reserves is unavailable, but 10 years ago it was reported to have reserves of around 350 million barrels, ranking the country 52 nd in the world. Oil production peaked in 2015 at 95,000 b/d and is currently estimated to be below 70,000 b/d. No new discoveries have been made since 2010. Oil demand in Pakistan is estimated to be around 500,000 b/d. Pakistan has five main oil refineries with a combined capacity of around 450,000 b/d, although actual output is likely much lower. Pakistan's oil markets also face a "smuggling problem". Significant volumes of cheap Iranian gasoline are smuggled over the border into Pakistan. The Pakistani government tries to clamp down on this illegal trade, since it undermines domestic production and legitimate fuel imports. It also impacts government revenues, since the smuggled fuel is sold without taxes. However, it is difficult to combat this illegal trade, because the low price of Iranian gasoline incentivizes smuggling, especially in border areas.

Over the last 10 years, Pakistan's seaborne crude oil imports have mostly ranged between 150,000 and 200,000 b/d. In 2025 YTD, seaborne imports averaged 175,000 b/d. Pakistan relies primarily on two key suppliers: Saudi Arabia and the UAE, who combined supplied 95% of Pakistan's crude since 2016 (Chart 1).

So far this year, things have started to look up for Pakistan, to a large extent because of a closer relationship to the United States under President Donald Trump. Pakistan has a strategic location in Asia, close to the Middle East, bordering Iran, Afghanistan, India and China. In recent years, many Western countries, including the US have courted India, historically Pakistan's archrival. However, since the brief conflict with India in May, relations with the US improved. Pakistan acknowledged Mr. Trumps role in defusing the conflict and its army chief had lunch with Donald Trump in June. The US did a trade deal with Pakistan, limiting their tariffs to 19%, while imposing 25% tariffs on India, which was later raised to 50%. The US and Pakistan are rebuilding ties to boost trade and investment. In August, following the announcement of the trade deal, President Trump made a statement that Pakistan and the United States will work together to develop their "massive oil reserves". While this appears an overstatement and there is little proof of such undeveloped reserves, it does reinforce the warming relationships between the two countries.

One concrete result of these improved relations has been the recent purchases of US crude oil by Pakistan's largest refinery. The 156,000 b/d Cnergyico refinery, located on Pakistan's southern coast, has so far purchased two Suezmax cargoes of WTI from Vitol. The first cargo is expected to arrive in October and second one in November. If the economics make sense, Cnergyico may import one cargo per month. Imports are being handled by the refinery's Single Point Mooring (SPM) facility in deep water offshore. While the current SPM can handle Suezmaxes, there are plans to expand the capacity to VLCCs. This is in stark contrast to current import patterns, which rely almost exclusively on Aframaxes (Chart 2).

If the changes in import patterns will become permanent, this will add significantly to Pakistan's ton-mile demand. Further growth is possible since Pakistan is planning to invest billions in expanding refining capacity. A rapidly growing population (Pakistan is already the fifth most populous country in the world) would definitely support higher oil demand in the future.
