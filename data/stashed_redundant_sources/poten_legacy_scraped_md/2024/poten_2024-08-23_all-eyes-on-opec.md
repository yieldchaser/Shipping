# All Eyes On OPEC+

### Awaiting OPEC's October production decision

Several months into the Covid-19 pandemic, OPEC+ was faced with a collapse in oil demand due to widespread lockdowns. So, in April 2020, the cartel agreed to a production cut of 9.7 million barrels per day (Mb/d) representing about 10% of global crude oil output. In July 2020, OPEC+ reduced the cuts to 7.7 Mb/d. OPEC's actions stabilized the oil markets and when oil demand started to recover from the pandemic, they started to gradually increase output again. However, continued increases in non- OPEC output, geopolitical turmoil and a slowdown in oil demand growth forced OPEC to reverse some of the output increases. In April 2023, OPEC+ surprised the market by announcing additional cuts, including a voluntary (supplementary) cut by Saudi Arabia on 1 Mb/d. These cuts have been largely maintained until now, but OPEC+ has indicated that some of the production restrictions will be relaxed by the end of September. OPEC+ has announced that 2.2 Mb/d of cuts will be gradually phased out on a monthly basis from October 2024 through September 2025. In this Tanker Opinion, we'll discuss the likelihood that this actually happens.

OPEC+ has indicated in recent statements that they are indeed considering bringing back some of the crude that they removed from the market in 2023. However, they also said that they could pause or reverse their decision subject to market conditions. In any case, there are a number of factors that the cartel will have to take into consideration: Oil prices, oil demand, non-OPEC supply and the geopolitical situation.

The level of oil prices is critical for virtually every OPEC+ member. Most of the producers in the group are highly dependent on oil revenues for their balance of payments. Countries like Russia, Venezuela and Iran, which are all subject to various levels of sanctions are even more sensitive to oil prices and, as a result, tend to be particularly hawkish. Everything else being equal, adding more oil to the market would put downward pressure on oil prices. OPEC+ would like to prevent this. From the second half of 2022 onwards (after the oil price spike caused by the Russian invasion of Ukraine), oil prices have been relatively stable, mostly ranging between $70 and $90/barrel for Brent. Most OPEC+ members have shown remarkable production discipline and the ones that regularly exceed their quota (Iraq, Russia, Kazakhstan and the UAE) have promised to limit their over-production. The only countries that have the capacity to increase output are Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Kuwait and Iraq. The UAE and Iraq already produce above their quota and Kuwait's spare capacity is relatively limited. This leaves Saudi Arabia, with 3.0 Mb/d of sustainable spare capacity, in the driver's seat.

Global oil demand growth has been slowing in recent quarters, with Chinese oil demand in particular decelerating more than expected. This must give OPEC+ producers pause. Ironically, some of the countries that have increased their market share in China are the same ones that are not subject to (Iran) or do not adhere to (Russia) the OPEC+ quotas.

Non-OPEC production has been growing steadily since the oil industry came out of the pandemic. The U.S., Canada, Guyana and Brazil in particular have increased output in recent years, eating into OPEC's market share. For next year, further non-OPEC growth is expected. This is bad news for OPEC. The IEA forecasts non-OPEC production to increase by 1.75 Mb/d in 2025 significantly exceeding expected demand growth of 1.0 Mb/d. This means that there is no room for OPEC+ export growth in the short- to medium term.

Does this mean that OPEC+ will not increase output? Not necessarily. OPEC has surprised the market before, and they forecast higher demand growth than the IEA. However, it is more likely that, if they start to roll back their production cuts, it will be very slow and in small increments, so as not flood the market and undermine oil prices. For the tanker market, this means that owners should count on an additional boost from OPEC+ this winter.
