Key View
Import losses narrowed sharply to -3.97 yuan/mt and the board rose a second day; pre-holiday
restocking has started, but hot metal is still falling
The biggest change this period is on the cost side. The average imported ore margin rose from -13.18 to -3.97 yuan/mt, a 9.21-
yuan improvement, and none of the three reasons SMM gives sits in demand — lower Australian freight, a stronger yuan against
the dollar and a narrower seaborne premium. C5 Australia→China freight has fallen for three straight sessions, from 18.34 to USD
16.37/mt; lower landed costs improve import economics directly and explain why USD quotes rose broadly today (Indian fines +2.59%,
BRBF +1.08%) while most yuan spot prices held flat. On the demand side the first positive signal of this decline has appeared: SMM
states that some mills have begun restocking ahead of the holiday and that overall iron ore demand still supports prices. Together with
the DCE contract rising for a second session to 710.5 yuan/mt, this rebound rests on firmer ground than the past two days. It should not
be overstated, though. The same material notes mill losses deepening, end-use demand still weak and blast furnace maintenance
increasing; as of Sep 16 hot metal at 2.3991 million mt and the operating rate at 88.93% remain in a downtrend, and there is no new
print to overturn that. Inventories offer a structural warning: while the 10-port total drew down 1.1059 million mt for a third straight
week, the entire draw sits in fines (-1.5908 million mt) and concentrates (-641,200 mt), whereas lump built 1.0688 million mt in a
single week, up 11.26%, with pellets also higher. Lump building while lump prices hold flat looks like a structural increase in arrivals,
but if it persists it will first compress the lump premium. Structurally high grade remains the strongest end: the indices rose for a second
session, the 65% port spot index added 3 yuan to 838 and the high-low spread widened another 2 yuan to 192 yuan/wmt, a
fourth straight session of widening and 9 yuan up from 183 on Sep 14. On balance prices look caught between the two, trading in a
range — SMM's view too. How far the rebound runs depends on whether pre-holiday restocking is large enough to offset falling hot
metal, while the biggest downside variable remains whether last week's shipment surge (+8.59%) turns port stocks into a build once it
lands.

Market Commentary · Imported Ore
Futures & Spot
The board extended its recovery. The DCE most-traded I2701 settled at 710.5 yuan/mt, up 1.5 yuan (+0.21%) from Sep 16 and
higher for a second straight session; the SGX most-traded contract was at USD 95.44/dmt (as of Sep 16, -0.16%). Most Qingdao
Port grades closed flat: PB fines 673, Mac fines 676, Newman lump 873, SSF 535, Carajas fines 840 and PB lump 868 yuan/wmt;
BRBF rose 5 yuan to 733 yuan/wmt, the only clear gainer, while Newman fines slipped 2 yuan to 678. USD prices moved
higher across the board — Indian fines 69.03 (+2.59%), BRBF 100.35 (+1.08%), Carajas fines 115.58 (+0.57%) and SSF 72.16
(+0.46%)/dmt. The ranking differs from the yuan quotes, mainly because the yuan strengthened against the dollar on the day.
Driver
This period's driver sits on the cost side rather than in demand. SMM lists three reasons for the margin recovery: lower
Australian freight, a stronger yuan against the dollar and a narrower seaborne premium. C5 Australia→China freight
has now fallen for three straight sessions, from 18.34 down to USD 16.37/mt; lower landed costs improve import economics
directly and reduce the need for traders to hold out on price — which is also why USD quotes rose broadly while most yuan spot
prices held flat.
Demand
The first positive signal of this decline has appeared. SMM states that some mills have begun restocking ahead of the
holiday and that overall iron ore demand still supports prices. The negatives have not gone away, though: SMM's survey
shows mill losses deepening, end-use demand still weak and blast furnace maintenance increasing. As of Sep 16 the blast
furnace operating rate across 242 mills was 88.93% (-0.15 ppt) and daily average hot metal 2.3991 million mt (-3,700 mt), still in a
downtrend, with no new print this period. SMM therefore sees prices caught between the two and trading in a range, with
import margins staying in loss until end-use demand genuinely improves.
Inventory &
Supply
The 10-port series updated to Sep 17: total inventory 101.9125 million mt, down 1.1059 million mt WoW (-1.07%) and a third
consecutive weekly draw. The product mix, however, has clearly split: fines fell 1.5908 million mt (-1.91%) and concentrates
641,200 mt (-7.38%), so the drawdown is concentrated in fines, while lump built 1.0688 million mt against the trend, a
11.26% weekly rise, and pellets added 57,300 mt (+3.38%). Lump building while PB lump and Newman lump prices held flat
points to a structural increase in arrivals rather than weaker demand. Other weekly data still stand as of Sep 11: 35-port inventory
143.49 million mt (-420,000 mt), daily outbound volume 3.235 million mt (+90,000 mt), imported ore stocks at 242 mills 74.24
million mt (-440,000 mt) with 19.3 days of consumption; global shipments 37.4058 million mt (+8.59%), Australia 20.7583 million
mt (+10.18%) and Brazil 7.8627 million mt (-7.62%).
Cost & Margin
The biggest change this period. The average imported ore margin rose from -13.18 to -3.97 yuan/mt, a 9.21-yuan
improvement that SMM attributes to lower Australian freight, a stronger yuan against the dollar and a narrower seaborne
premium. The two routes keep diverging: C5 Australia→China fell another USD 0.75 to 16.37/mt (-4.38%), down for a third
straight session, while C3 Brazil→China recovered USD 0.18 to 41.97/mt (+0.43%, both as of Sep 16). C3's rebound lines up with
BRBF leading the gains and Carajas fines holding firm — Brazilian route costs still support Brazilian grades. SMM expects import
margins to stay in loss until end-use demand genuinely improves.
Market Commentary · Domestic Ore
Prices
The SMM China Domestic Ore Composite Price Index still reads 845.94 yuan/mt (as of Sep 11, +0.67%), with no new print this
week. Regional quotes have turned lower recently: Shandong 64% alkaline concentrate is down to 816 yuan/mt ex-mine and
Tangshan 66% concentrate to 935–940 yuan/mt delivered, dry basis and tax inclusive.
Supply &
Demand
Supply stays tight: concentrator run rates remain low, state-owned operations largely consume their own output and private
concentrate supply is short. On demand, deepening mill losses keep buyers pressing hard on domestic concentrate prices,
though the start of pre-holiday restocking puts something of a floor under essential requirements.
Outlook
Imported high grade has strengthened for two sessions, and if that holds it will support domestic concentrate on relative value.
With mill losses deepening and end-use demand weak, however, domestic ore should stay soft and rangebound near term. The
next print of the national composite index is worth watching and should reflect the past fortnight's regional pullback.
