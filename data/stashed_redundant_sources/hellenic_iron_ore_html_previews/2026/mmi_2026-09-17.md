## Market Commentary

The biggest change this period is on the cost side. The average imported ore margin rose from -13.18 to -3.97 yuan/mt,a 9.21- yuan improvement, and none of the three reasons SMM gives sits in demand — lower Australian freight, a stronger yuan against the dollar and a narrower seaborne premium. C5 Australia→China freight has fallen for three straight sessions, from 18.34 to USD 16.37/mt; lower landed costs improve import economics directly and explain why USD quotes rose broadly today (Indian fines +2.59%, BRBF +1.08%) while most yuan spot prices held flat. On the demand side the first positive signal of this decline has appeared: SMM states that some mills have begun restocking ahead of the holiday and that overall iron ore demand still supports prices. Together with the DCE contract rising for a second session to 710.5 yuan/mt, this rebound rests on firmer ground than the past two days. It should not be overstated, though. The same material notes mill losses deepening, end-use demand still weak and blast furnace maintenance increasing; as of Sep 16 hot metal at 2.3991 million mt and the operating rate at 88.93% remain in a downtrend, and there is no new print to overturn that. Inventories offer a structural warning: while the 10-port total drew down 1.1059 million mt for a third straight week, the entire draw sits in fines (-1.5908 million mt) and concentrates (-641,200 mt), whereas lump built 1.0688 million mt in a single week, up 11.26%, with pellets also higher. Lump building while lump prices hold flat looks like a structural increase in arrivals, but if it persists it will first compress the lump premium. Structurally high grade remains the strongest end: the indices rose for a second session, the 65% port spot index added 3 yuan to 838 and the high-low spread widened another 2 yuan to 192 yuan/wmt, a fourth straight session of widening and 9 yuan up from 183 on Sep 14. On balance prices look caught between the two, trading in a range — SMM's view too. How far the rebound runs depends on whether pre-holiday restocking is large enough to offset falling hot metal, while the biggest downside variable remains whether last week's shipment surge (+8.59%) turns port stocks into a build once it lands.
