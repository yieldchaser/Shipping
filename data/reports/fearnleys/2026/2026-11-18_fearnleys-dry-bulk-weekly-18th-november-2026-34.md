## Capesize/Newcastlemax

The model below consists of factors like the global ballast/laden vessel ratio, West of Singapore ballasters, and a couple of other AIS indicators. Below the charts are tables showing where the model sits currently. It is only at extremes that these models give very clear suggestions about the near-term outlook. 

Comment from last week *"Still, it's worth pointing out that the bearish signal given last week will be "live" next week; at the same time, the bullish signals given in mid-October will no longer be in play (2 to 4 weeks is the timeframe the models are good at predicting)."* The rally that started in mid-October is likely to take a break at least now. The models are in neutral territory, so they do not suggest anything clear at the moment. 

In the last 6 weeks, total South Atlantic shipment volumes were up 17.5%% compared to the corresponding weeks last year, continuing the strong trend. Brazil iron ore exports growth remains negative, but Bauxite flows have returned to the exceptional growth rates seen a few months ago - up 60% when comparing the previous six weeks to the same six weeks last year.

## Panamax/Kamsarmax 

Shipment volume growth continues to be lower than DWT supply growth. Through week 46, growth was 2.6%, compared to DWT supply growth of 4.7%. Over the last 10 weeks, volume growth was 0.3% compared to the same period last year.  

Both the Pacific and South Atlantic indicators suggest the market rebound is over, and that we will trend sideways over the coming weeks. The same goes for the coal futures curve indicator. We notice a bit more activity on fronthauls, maybe as a result of the weaker Brazilian Real. 

**Top left chart:** Shipment volumes were up 2.4% year on year through week 46. In the last ten weeks, volumes were down 0.1% compared to the same period last year. Supply growth was 4.7% at the latest reading. 
**Top right chart:** The 2-month lead of the coal futures curve suggests the market could slowly trend upwards in the next two months.
**Bottom left chart:** The indicator suggests this rebound could soon be over. 
**Bottom right chart:** The indicator suggest the P6 rebound could soon be over.

## Supramax/Ultramax 

Through week 46, shipment volume growth was 2.5% year-on-year, whereas over the last 10 weeks, growth was 1.8% compared to the same period last year. DWT supply growth continues at 5.5% year-on-year.  

The AIS model has returned to neutral territory, but the extreme readings given a few weeks ago suggest sideways at best over the coming weeks. 
Industrial metals prices have dropped significantly recently, which reflects that global industrial activity in slowing down. 

**Top left chart:** Weekly shipment volumes, 2026 ytd vs 2025. 
**Top right chart:** Supra/Ultra AIS model. Extremely high readings suggest the market will fall in the near term, and extremely low readings suggest the market will rise. 
**Bottom left chart:** The industrial metals index is composed of copper, aluminium, zinc and nickel, and weighted according to market value. The 9-month change of this index has a high correlation with the 9-month change of the Ultramax 1-year TC rate, and leads by about 3 months. 
**Bottom right table:** This table shows the average statistical outcome following given readings of the model.

## Handysize

Through week 46, shipment volumes grew by an estimated 0.4% compared to last year. DWT supply growth remains at 3.2%. The market remains firmer than the weak headline figures suggest. In the near term, the softening of the Ultramax segment suggests the Handysize market will soon start falling. This potential fall is now overdue based on the usual lag of roughly 1 week between the Ultramax and Handysize segments. 

**Top left chart:** The ratio between Rio-Recalada / Skaw-Passero (HS3) and SEA / Singapore-Japan (HS5) is usually a good predictor of tops and bottoms of the HS3 route. A higher ratio suggests a market top, and a lower ratio suggests a market bottom. 
**Top right chart:** Through week 465, Handysize shipment volumes were 0.4% higher year on year, declining from the 3%+ figures seen in the last months. DWT supply growth was 3.2% at the latest reading. 
**Bottom left chart:** The correlation between the S11TC and HS7TC is over 90%. The correlation is even higher when the S11TC index is shifted forward a few days, which means that movements in the S11TC index often lead the movements of the HS7TC. 
**Bottom right chart:** The change in the price of copper is usually an accurate leading indicator for asset values.
