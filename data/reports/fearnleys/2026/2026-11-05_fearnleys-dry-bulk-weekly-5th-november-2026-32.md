## Capesize/Newcastlemax

The model below consists of factors like the global ballast/laden vessel ratio, West of Singapore ballasters, and a couple of other AIS indicators. Below the charts are tables showing where the model sits currently. It is only at extremes that these models give very clear suggestions about the near-term outlook. 

The momentum model has entered bearish territory, whereas the composite model remains in neutral territory. As written over the last weeks, the bullish signals given through mid-October are "live" through the third week of this month. However, as the models have turned more bearish now, further upside from here seems less likely. 

In the last 6 weeks, total South Atlantic shipment volumes were up 8.8%% compared to the corresponding weeks last year, continuing the improving trend. Brazil iron ore exports growth has been negative for seven straight weeks (down 14% yoy over those seven weeks), but Guinea bauxite exports growth has increased over the last few weeks (up 23% yoy in the last 6 weeks).

## Panamax/Kamsarmax 

Shipment volume growth continues to be lower than DWT supply growth, with a slight worsening seen over the last few weeks. Through week 44, growth was 2.8%, compared to DWT supply growth of 4.7%. Over the last 10 weeks, volume growth was 2.5% compared to the same period last year.  

The near-term indicators suggest the market will rebound. The three indicators all have different suggestions as to the duration of this rebound. The Pac indicator says just one week more, the SATL indicator says two weeks, and the coal futures curve says for another month. Worth noting that neither seasonality nor fundamentals suggest a strong rally.

**Top left chart:** Shipment volumes were up 2.8% year on year through week 44. In the last ten weeks, volumes were up 2.5% compared to the same period last year. Supply growth was 4.7% at the latest reading. 
**Top right chart:** The 2-month lead of the coal futures curve suggest the market could slowly climb for another month. 
**Bottom left chart:** The indicator suggests this rebound will be short-lived, only for another week. 
**Bottom right chart:** The indicator suggest P6 could rise in the next two weeks, then soften.

## Supramax/Ultramax 

Through week 44, shipment volume growth was 3.2% year-on-year, whereas over the last 10 weeks, growth was 2.5% compared to the same period last year. DWT supply growth continues at 5.5% year-on-year.  

The ballast/laden vessel reading remains at a level which suggests further upside is limited in the near term. The reading now is comparable to July this year and September/October/November last year. Further, the spread over the Kamsarmax market is the highest since S11TC index inception in May 2023. 

**Top left chart:** Weekly shipment volumes, 2026 ytd vs 2025. 
**Top right chart:** S11TC vs the Ballaster/Laden vessel ratio. A lower ratio indicates a looser market balance, and vice versa. The index and the ballast/laden ratio have diverged from the index over the last month and a half
**Bottom left chart:** The industrial metals index is composed of copper, aluminium, zinc and nickel, and weighted according to market value. The 9-month change of this index has a high correlation with the 9-month change of the Ultramax 1-year TC rate, and leads by about 3 months. 
**Bottom right chart:** We made an AIS based momentum model. Extremely high readings are usually followed by market corrections, and vice versa.

## Handysize

Through week 43, shipment volumes grew by an estimated 0.6% compared to last year. DWT supply growth remains at 3.2%. The market remains firmer than the weak headline figures suggest. In the near term, the softening of the Ultramax segment suggests the Handysize market will soon start falling. 

**Top left chart:** The ratio between Rio-Recalada / Skaw-Passero (HS3) and SEA / Singapore-Japan (HS5) is usually a good predictor of tops and bottoms of the HS3 route. A higher ratio suggests a market top, and a lower ratio suggests a market bottom. 
**Top right chart:** Through week 44, Handysize shipment volumes were 0.6% higher year on year, declining from the 3%+ figures seen in the last months. DWT supply growth was 3.2% at the latest reading. 
**Bottom left chart:** The correlation between the S11TC and HS7TC is over 90%. The correlation is even higher when the S11TC index is shifted forward a few days, which means that movements in the S11TC index often lead the movements of the HS7TC. 
**Bottom right chart:** The change in the price of copper is usually an accurate leading indicator for asset values.
