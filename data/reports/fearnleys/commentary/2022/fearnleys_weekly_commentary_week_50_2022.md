### Capesize Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2022-12-13 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

No excitement, and very mixed picture, but overall slightly improving for the big ships - average daily spot earnings up 5 pct w-o-w to come in at some USD 14,500. Far East remains a disappointment for owners, with volumes essentially limited to the West Australia/China iron ore trade and consequent transpacific values falling more than 25 pct to USD 11k/day. ## 2. Tanker Sector (Crude & Products)
### VLCC Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2022-12-13 | **Subtype:** Tank Weekly Comment | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

After a period of steady decline, the VLCC market suddenly metamorphosed Thursday last week. A large number of ships had been quietly picked off under the radar and then Bahri swooped in and fixed half a dozen ships in one go. As a result, every other charterer with uncovered enquiries then toppled in one on top of the other, and MEG/East rates jumped 10 WS points in the space of a handful of fixtures. The frenzy that followed has left the MEG position list wanting for the balance of December, although the fixture count indicates that the month’s program is largely covered. The Atlantic has seen a lot of fixing/failing/re-fixing with rates flatlined, and a weakening Suezmax segment it is likely to cap any upside for their bigger sisters.

## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2022-12-12 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The headlines this week have been all about the cold weather that has persisted in the Northern Hemisphere, corresponding to the draw down in LNG stocks especially in Europe. It has been a good reminder that Mother Nature tends to switch the thermostat rather quickly and although most of the countries have been prepared for this, it has still caused sudden spikes especially on electricity prices across the old continent. This has in turn killed the LNG arbitrage for now, but at least the contango is looking rather firm into Q1 2023. On the LNG shipping side, most of the subletters that have tonnage have been carefully assessing the situation during the last few days, and consequently the spot market looks rather balanced, although it is to be said that all the spot opportunities are now for loadings past the new year. On the other hand, the term market seems to be holding firm with further reports emerging on term fixtures concluded with deliveries as far as Q3 next year. On the newbuilding side, it was a quiet week with no new orders confirmed, but the yards report tender activity and continue to be comfortable with the current orderbooks and future outlook.

The ongoing build in PADD III continues to impress due mostly to robust production levels but aided by both Panama Canal-related shipping delays as well as a recent bout of fog- delays on the Houston Ship Channel.   In any event there has so far been only limited cold weather demand across the U.S., but now a couple of prominent forecasters are calling for a series of Arctic fronts to sweep across the nation over the Christmas and New Year period, perhaps marking the beginnings of the traditional demand season which will likely be viewed in a “better late than never” context.  NWE The TOT Window continues to be well bid for end dec and early Jan deliveries into Europe. Inland demand is increasing, demand for railcars is up and European storages are drawing down.
