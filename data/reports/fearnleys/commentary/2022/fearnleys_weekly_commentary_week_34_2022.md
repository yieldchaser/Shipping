## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2022-08-23 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

As we’ve watched LNG prices rise to previously unimaginable levels this week, someone reminded us that in April 2020, JKM was trading at US$1.83. Watching JKM breach the $55 mark this week serves as an expensive reminder of how much has changed in this period, but it’s worth noting that not everything is more expensive. In fact, shipping is relatively inexpensive at present. In April 2020, a spot cargo from Australia to JKTC would have cost about 25 cents in shipping which equated to approximately 13.5% of the total cargo cost. At present, and basis our rates this week, the Unit Freight Cost is $1.50 which means that shipping accounts for less than 3% of the total cargo price; a 500% decrease since April 2020 if you will. The purpose here isn’t to agitate for a rise in rates of course, rather it’s to point out the dilemma that the market faces in that almost all tonnage is controlled by Charterers yet their risk vs reward ratio for chartering out has never been less attractive. Freeport LNG’s announcement of a small delay to their restart should free up some October intra-basin tonnage, but with big ships proving harder to secure than a cargo slot into Europe, the question now is, how long until the bidding wars for the ST ships begin? No new orders again this week but then perhaps we will see a Gastech inspired flurry in a few weeks’ time.
