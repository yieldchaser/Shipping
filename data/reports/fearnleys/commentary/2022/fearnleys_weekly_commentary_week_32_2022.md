## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2022-08-09 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

We observed last week that an unusual feature of this current market is the sheer number of prompt spot cargoes. Well, for better or for worse, this behaviour has now been normalised and so it is only natural that we released another August loading cargo requirement today. Of course, some traditional Asian Owners still adhere to the four to six week fixing window and accordingly they have stoked the fire in the September spot market with a brace of Australian cargoes loading on the same day (albeit from opposite ends of the country). Whilst this activity is welcome news for Owners, one noteworthy complication at present is the difficulty that off-takers are having securing cool down slots. The spread between warm and cold tonnage is unsurprisingly attractive at these LNG prices but with terminals holding firm to their presentation requirements (cold and ready to load) Charterers are faced with a limited pool of suitable vessels to pick from and in turn we have now comfortably moved into the 6-figure territory (Round Trip) for inter-basin cargoes. With a handful of term requirements still being pursued, sentiment has definitely turned bullish across the board and we are witnessing a narrowing of the delta between spot and term rates. No new orders to report this week; in fact, Q3 has been pretty lacklustre in this department, with a grand total of 4 new contracts inked to date. Notwithstanding this, Qatargas slot allocation is keeping concerned parties busy.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2022-08-08 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS The domestic U.S. market has gone very quiet with activity levels almost nil.  Crude fluctuations have forced some to the sidelines, but the poor arb during the heat of the summer is the primary cause.  Buy side interest is also lacking. The EIA this week reported overall stocks at 65.7mmbbls, an increase of 2.1mmbbls which was slightly above the generally anticipated level, but which also brought current stocks to 100% of last year.  Barring any unforeseen events, propane inventories in the U.S. will likely conclude September at around the 75 (plus) mmbbl range; likely sufficient given not only the lack of crop drying but the lack of cancellations and cargo buybacks.  Both production and export volumes remain robust. Terminal fees are a bit subjective given the lack of activity, but ideas in the near term market remain in the mid 4s to mid 5s depending on location/ratio/timing, but move higher towards and in Q4.  NWE                                                 A window bid pushed the large cargo market into a premium to spot  CIF ARA price  (+$2.5/t) for the first time since July. The downward pressure in the recent weeks was a result of a handful of factors- notably, record imports from the US, compounded by very low water levels in the Rhine, not allowing for inland distribution and thus leaving the ARA hub considerably long. Macro discussions around the region have been centered around how LPG's will be utilize this winter amid the ongoing Russia/ Ukranian conflict. Some participants believe the gas shortage will induce a migration over to LPG, bolstering demand and subsequently bulling up price, while others remain convinced that the infrastructure to move from gas to LPG's in a meaningful way is not in place, and would take time and considerable investment. This is not to suggest European prices will not rise this winter,  but that they'd do so  inline with typical, seasonal-related patterns as opposed to the Russian gas supply shock.  MED The Med remains very much long in both the propane and butane markets, and saw a drop of 30/ton midweek. The levels have effectively snapped shut the arbitrage, and no US cargos are currently aimed at the region.
