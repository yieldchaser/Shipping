## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2022-01-12 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Rather surprisingly, the second week of the year hasn't delivered much new activity to the market. The LNG spot shipping market has remained very quiet, with only a handful of ongoing requirements being worked, most of which are cargo tender related. Furthermore, availability for prompt tonnage is increasing in both basins, mainly due to lack of arbitrage opportunities that have led to many charterers being long on freight. We don’t see this situation changing anytime soon either, since it seems that especially Korea and Japan have sustained enough LNG inventories to cope through the remainder of the winter and thus the main Far East buying activity depends on China’s demand. This has led to a real hammering on the spot rates, as those looking for tonnage have seen a large number of vessels offered – mainly from subletters. The clear bright side for Owners is the healthy activity and gradually reducing availability in the term market, which has kept term rates steady especially for the more modern and larger LNGCs.  The newbuilding market also remains active as amongst others, Maran Gas Maritime added to the fresh tally for 2022 with a pair of LNGC newbuilds from DSME. Yard capacity is now quite limited for any new orders with delivery in 2025. In addition, market participants are eagerly waiting for the final decision from the European Union antitrust regulator with regards the DSME and HHI merger, which might get vetoed by the EU according to the latest rumours.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2022-01-10 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS  The combination of colder temperatures across North America and healthy export volumes over the holiday period have given cause for a robust draw in propane inventories, which recorded a 3.3mmbbl decline for the week ending 7 Jan.  This weighs against the 66.00mmbbl level recorded one year ago, and puts current stocks at just under 95% of last year at the same time. Additional winter weather is on the near-term horizon, as well, and forecasters are calling for below-average temps and significant snowfall over the balance of January.  The EIA’s Days of Supply calculation as of 7 Jan stands at 39.9, versus 38.6 days one year ago.  The current supply rate compares to the 5 year average of 47.92 days for the first week of January. Meanwhile, recent terminal fee levels have been noted as trading within a very thin range, with full-propane deals recorded at 6cts and mixed-ratio parcels being fixed at around 5.75 within the last trading week.  More recently, though, offers to sell mixed-ratio cargoes are seen at 5cts/gal, and full-propane lots in mid/2H Feb are presently being marketed at the “high 5s”. Mont Belvieu prices have all the while been slowly climbing, and started Dec at $1.03/gal but as of this writing have reached over $1.18/gal, supported – in part – by both the weather, the healthy export volumes and the nearly endless gains in the crude sector.  WTI is off at present, but remains well above $82/bbl.  NWE Demand in the region remains strong and is sufficient enough to absorb the over 400,000 MT of LPG set to be imported to the region in January. Winter weather is not severe but still generating good demand. Whereas chemical demand is getting back to maximum as Terneuzen is now back online. The propane naphtha spread at -$36pmt in January favouring propane, as Naphtha strengthened with recent rise in crude oil. The large import volume in January has calmed the market which looks relatively balanced. That said a persistent bid in the TOT window for January 22-26 delivery window has yet to be taken out after being shown all week and improving values, now valued at Jan CIF ARA +$2.5pmt. The window will need to improve to tempt further US volume as the arb narrowed significantly since the large draw seen in US inventories. ## 4. Sale and Purchase (S&P) & Corporate Activity
### SnP Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2022-01-14 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

While SnP markets are gradually finding their rhythm post the holiday season, this new year opens with no particular surprises with unchanged sentiment across both sectors. Dry cargo continues to draw healthy amounts of buying interest on the second hand front with freight continuing to provide support for potential buyers looking to take further positions in order to joy of these sustained returns. Supramax sector captures the bulk of activity with a handful of vessels changing hands at values which for now remain broadly in line with the end of year transactions. Saying that there have been signs of ongoing negotiations where bids have come at a discount to last done with potential buyers hoping to take advantage of the slight corrections in the chartering markets and this quieter seasonal period. Sellers however remain committed to their targets leading to a flat pricing curve.   With Tankers closing the year with a widely shared sense of disillusion about a market recovery that seems to become increasingly evasive; most market players have started the year with caution. This subdued sentiment can be felt in part in the thinning level of enquiry with buyers taking a wait and see approach rather than going to meet an ever-stagnating offer. This being particularly apparent on the modern front as opposed to older tonnage where pockets of activity can be seen with a number of region 10 year old product carriers changing hands.
