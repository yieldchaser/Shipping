## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2022-02-02 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

With the Lunar New Year upon us, activity across basins has remained limited this week, maintaining high levels of global vessel availability amongst Subletters and applying sustained downward pressure on spot rates. JKM & TTF LNG prices continue to be in flux however spreads between indexes have been diminishing towards parity in recent days. This has partly been a result of Lunar New Year but also due to milder European winter weather and increased gas flows from Russia, levelling off European gas prices to almost match those of Asia. That said, with geopolitical tensions remaining elevated in the region and Asia coming back online later this week, the window to opportunity remains ajar for now. Term shipping activity has similarly slowed however rates continue to be in markedly healthy condition and relatively unchanged in anticipation of business-as-usual later this week.   On the newbuilding front, TotalEnergies’ announcement to restart Mozambique LNG within 2022 has more or less mopped up any remaining LNG berth availability at shipyards through to 2027, further tightening an already tight LNG newbuilding market. Requests for earlier slots have been heard quoted at considerable premiums while anything thereafter, from 2027 onwards, have been marked up accordingly. No new orders have been reported this week.

### Chartering Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2022-01-31 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

EAST The Eastern freight rate has taken quite a big hit with the last done India fixture below USD 50 Baltic equivalent while the Chinese New Year festivities keeps being the main focus. As much of the Asian countries slowly return from Spring Festival celebrations, more spot activity is eventually expected. Freight in the East is getting very close to breakeven levels for most Owners, typically this could lead to more resistance towards further downside on freight rates. That put aside, the Middle East Gulf had a very strong month in January lifting-wise with over 60 liftings. Compared to recent months this is the highest number with a good margin. Approximately 70% of the liftings is heading to either China or India. A third of the liftings in January are spot fixtures. At the time of writing we count 11 spot fixtures out of the MEG in February.  WEST The US continues to see healthy exports with 85 liftings in January, seven of them LGCs. This is the second strongest month compared to the last four, only behind November which saw 91 liftings in total. In January, 67% of the liftings sailed East and 33% sailed West – the lowest East percentage compared to the previous 4 months. A strong crude price is usually affecting the arbitrage in a positive way, but with the current cold weather in the US and draws on inventories, propane and natural gas prices are being pushed higher and effectively limiting arb trades. This means there is very little interest in discussing shipping at current freight rates of around $100 east. Shipping has so far come off around $20 for a US/East voyage, but at the same time shipping is also being hit by rising bunker costs and waiting time in the Panama Canal makes scheduling, causing more ships to run late for laycans.
