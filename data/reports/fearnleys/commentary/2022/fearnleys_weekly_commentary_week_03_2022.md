### Capesize Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2022-01-18 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Big ships still suffering from the typical January dip after a very brutal start of the year. Crucial milk route Brazil/China in limbo as seasonal weather factors significantly delay iron ore mining and exports, spot volumes remarkably low. Average daily earnings down more than 40 pct w-o-w to come in at around USD 10k. Far East activity, mostly represented by the West Australia/China trade, not sufficient to absorb tonnage as they come open – inter-pacific values consequently shaved off some 30 pct to stand at USD 8k, still better off than the much longer China-Brazil-China round which has lost almost 45 pct and come in at USD 7k. With 2021 average values coming in at USD 33,333 and fundamental outlook for 2022 remains positive, good period tonnage keeps being very much in demand – latest representative fixture appearing 181,000 dwt, built 2011, delivering Far East March for about 12 months done at USD 27k.

### Suezmax Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2022-01-19 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

You'd be forgiven for thinking that oil prices, presently at eight year highs, had parted permanent company with the physical freight market. ## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2022-01-18 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Continuing the trend from the previous week, the ongoing lack of spot chartering activities caused a further free fall for the spot LNG vessel charter rates as demand for spot LNG ships have been muted so far for this week. There is now a high number of vessels available in both basins seeking spot charter opportunities. More and more players are indicating fleet availability, of which a vast majority are controlled by traders, portfolio players and projects with spare shipping capacity to sublet. This may be linked to reduced amount of trading opportunities in the market as demand for LNG post winter evidently tapers off and a soft purchase interest at current LNG prices. Despite the current soft sentiments in the spot market, the term charter market remain relatively strong and charterers continue to issue requirements to charter vessels for one year or multi-years.  It is a different vibe in the newbuilding market with its continuous ordering activity streak. Mitsui OSK Lines (MOL) reportedly confirmed an optional order for a newbuilding 174,000m3 LNG vessel at Daewoo Shipbuilding & Marine Engineering (DSME) with the expected delivery of end-2024. NYK Line have announced the signing of a long term charter between France LNG Shipping (joint-venture company between Geogas LNG SAS and NYK) and EDF LNG Shipping SAS, with an order of a newbuilding 174,000m3 LNG vessel for 2025 delivery.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2022-01-17 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS US propane stocks are in generally better condition owing to robust domestic production and fewer ‘buybacks’, and despite healthy exports.  Expectations as to this week’s EIA report again ranged rather widely, this time from a draw of 1.5mmbbls to as much as 3.5mmbbls.  Ultimately a draw of 3.7mmbbls was recorded, but the larger-than-expected draw was met with a rather lackluster response and the numbers were rationalized and explained as having been generally in line with sentimental expectations.  Nonetheless, market values did begin to climb after the release of the EIA figures and were marked at $1.1875 before the lunch hour on Thursday.  Domestic US propane stocks have risen to within 2% of the level achieved last year and as of mid-Jan 2022 stand at 58.70mmbbls. Terminal fees have seen a bit of weakness, having started the year at / around 6cts but coming off of those levels to the 5.75cpg level more recently.  Traders were keen on 5.00 cpg for March ideas, but sellers thus far have resisted.  The terminals are priced well above.  NWE Demand in the region remains decent though slightly more bearish as 2 offers and a discounted bid emerged in the window. That said, the region seems equipped to absorb 400,000 MT that has or will be delivered into the region this month. Winter weather has been tepid, however forecasts are now predicting a dip back below seasonal average which could possibly stoke demand.  Chemical demand is moderate and propane's widening weakness in relation to naphtha could further insight the demand to the region for cracking. That said, weak Ethylene margins could potentially act as a point of friction in the equation . As it stands, the propane naphtha spread sits at -$94.25 pmt in the front month- or the widest its been since May. Natural gas prices have softened, it has not been to a degree to absolve LPG from internal refineries where its being used as a cheaper alternative.    MED The Med remains balanced, with muted activity. While the arbitrage for butane has widened on paper (US Butane has come off a bit following the drive-heavy holidays, along with some crude related CIF ARA gains) logistical constraints have hampered any opportunistic trade in the region.

## 4. Sale and Purchase (S&P) & Corporate Activity
### SnP Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2022-01-21 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Despite it be a quiet week in the snp market with a shrinking number of sales to report, headlines were stolen by the reported ongoing discussions between Scorpio and Hafnia for the acquisition of Scorpios LR1 fleet. The deal would comprise of 12 LR1 product carriers built in 2016 and 2015 with all units being scrubber fitted. It would mark a continuous consolidation in the Product space which has been led by Hafnia over the years through a number of large scale transactions. A move that seem to be welcomed by most market players who see it as a stamp of confidence in a market that has been falling short of expectations.  In terms of concluded transactions this week, the dry markets have seen a handful of vessels changing hands notably in the Supramax segment which continues to capture the bulk of activity. Momentum seem to be subdued compared to last quarter’s frenzy, mainly due to market players finding their bearings in this new year and identifying the next investible sector.
