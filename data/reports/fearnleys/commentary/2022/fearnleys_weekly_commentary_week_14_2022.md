### Capesize Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2022-04-06 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Levels for the big ships dipping further as slow port turnaround/increased congestion remains insufficient to compensate for markedly lower mineral volumes traded compared to same period last year. Daily average earnings down 10 pct w-o-w to come in at USD 12k. Pain most acutely felt in Pacific, where values for rounds have fallen by 45 pct over last 5 trading days as fresh coal and in particular iron ore activity has been uninspiring. Transatlantic rates are steady in comparison, but with relevant quantities too insignificant to make much difference. Fronthaul trades remain a reflection of main Brazilian miners being far behind production and export guidance. Market generally (still) very much in contango, with keen interest in period candidates of virtually all size and colour - exemplified by vintage unit 171,000 dwt, built 2003, recently done for rest of 2022 at USD 25k.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2022-04-04 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS  Since the end of March the Mont Belvieu propane market has seen intermittent declines which have been interrupted by small spurts of buoyancy, though all told the market has seen more softness than not.  This is owing to the combination of declines in winter-related demand and that crude oil has been coming off in recent days. Inventory levels in the U.S. have for two consecutive weeks risen, though rather modestly. Current stocks are posted by the EIA at 34.4mmbbls, or about 86% of last year and 57% of two years ago.  The market is beginning to think about how robust exports over the summer will match against slowly rising production levels, and calculating a required average build of 2mmbbls over the coming 5 or 6 months in order to reach ~80mmbbls by end-Sep / early Oct.  Convention wisdom is calling for a traditional tug of war between the two factors, and through the duration of the coming summer. Terminal fees on the Gulf Coast, meanwhile, are presently seen on offer at 6.00-6.50cpg, though a more enthusiastic seller was seen in the upper 5cpg range.  Buyers, however, have generally retreated to sub-5cpg levels unless a cargo is in need of being fixed immediately.    A spate of fixtures was reportedly accomplished over the past few days, and market intel indicates those parcels were concluded with a 6-handle.  NWE US crude oil inventories, rather than LPG publishing a big build caused a rapid drop in global crude oil values. This kept traders out of the TOT window and pushed outright CIF ARA values down over $30pmt. The transatlantic netbacks have been squeezed all week. Weakness in the European market has been partly attributed to the shift to the summer market and partly due to industrial demand expected to be reduced by planned maintenances. Production in the region has not increased as LPG continues to be spiked into the natural gas stream where possible. The continued question over whether European countries will put sanctions on Russian Energy, which would turn the market on it’s head. For the time being the crude moves and warmer temperatures coming in are keeping the market fairly quiet.  MED The Med remains tight, particularly propane, as strong winds hamper Algerian loadings. The commencement of Ramadan will keep the market quiet in the near future.

A week dominated by a tanker market that seem to have finally caught up with the long drawn expectations of a market upturn. We have witnessed rates going from strength to strength in all major sectors establishing a positive sentiment across the board. A sentiment supported by news of further consolidation with the announcement of the merger between Frontline and Euronav creating the largest tanker owning company in the market comprising of 69 VLCCs and 57 Suezmax.
