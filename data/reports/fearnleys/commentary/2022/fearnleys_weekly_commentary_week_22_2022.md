## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2022-05-30 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Over the last few weeks, the LNG shipping market have seen consistent gains.  Firm sentiments and rate ideas were predominately driven by the West of Suez as more requirements were seen for delivery into Europe.  Lack of vessel availability for June to 1H July in the Atlantic have spurred spot rates to break the six-figure barrier for the larger vessel sizes, which are highly sought after.   The growing demand for modern tonnage is holding sturdy and anticipated to continue for the remaining of the year as US exports increases.   Charterers with limited flexibility on their requirement have seen fewer options and often with a significant premium attached.  Discussion on longer term charters are ever-present as offtake agreements especially from the US are being secured.  With the ongoing geopolitical issues and gas supply disruptions, FSRU/ FSU related LNG projects have also gain traction with more engagements than previous years as Utilities, National Oil companies and governments prioritize energy security.  There are no new orders reported this week, nevertheless, shipyard capacity continue to diminish as demand for Newbuilds from all other shipping sectors continue to push LNGC newbuild deliveries further into the backend of 2026.  One LNG vessel arrived for recycling this week, which is the first for the year 2022. In 2021, 7 units were recycled.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2022-05-30 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS The EIA data for the week ending 27 May was revealed today and showed a 3.3mmbbl increase over the week prior.  Current overall stocks stand at 49.6mmbbls, which is nearly 103% of last year and just under 70% of two years ago.  Concerns as to stock levels at the conclusion of the summer are beginning to dissipate, though any forecast at this point would likely be premature. Meanwhile, propane values – as a result of the larger-than-anticipated inventory increase – are trending down, having peaked before the end of May at $1.25/gal and now posting at below $1.22/gal.  At present propane is 45% of WTI, having been in decline against crude almost consistently since Q4 2021. Terminal fees in the near-term have softened, with buyers clustered around the 5cpg level but sellers holding on for values nearer to 6cpg.  The second-half of 2022 has been more healthy, with a slew of transactions occurring at between 6.00 and 6.50cpg.  NWE The June CIF ARA propane price came off 11 dollars prior to the holiday closure, and presently sits at $744/t. A tug of war persists as North Sea product remains tight, however ample exports from the US have kept demand satisfied and the muted window activity illustrates the regions balance, and  it is unlikely many new spot purchases will be made to meet new spot demand. Looking farther down the calendar, the ongoing Ukrainian conflict has some participants concerned regarding supply shocks in the coming winter. Natural gas continues to trade at premiums to  LPG, incentivizing its utilization as an alternative fuel source when/ where logistically feasible- But for now, given that it is summer, exports from the US East Coast, coupled with sparce supply available from the North Sea are proving sufficient.    MED The region has been well supplied by US cargoes in the past month due to the open butane arb. Supply to the region has been satisfied through the end of the month, with the only bright spot for demand being Turkey due to the high LPG vs Gasoline spread for autogas.
