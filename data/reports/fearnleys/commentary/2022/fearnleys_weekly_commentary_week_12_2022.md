## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2022-03-23 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The trend in the charter market is set to continue from the previous weeks where demand for LNG vessels for a term charter has increased with various charterers issuing requirements seeking to secure tonnage over a period. The driver has certainly been security of LNG supply, given the recent geopolitical situation, however vessels are still expected to be in demand by charterers in anticipation of this year’s trading for winter, regardless of the ongoing events in Europe. These recent events have only added further demand to the already short tonnage situation for LNG vessels. As more vessels are secured by charterers on multi-year periods, the list of available modern vessels with length are diminishing over time, increasing owner’s expectations on the term charter rates. Meanwhile the spot market is relatively long with available vessels in both basins. Shipowners and subletters have been quite willing to propose competitive terms to secure spot charters during these “shoulder months” of April and May.   On newbuildings, an order has been reported at Daewoo Shipbuilding & Marine Engineering (DSME) with two (2) ME-GI propelled vessels, with specifications including reliquefaction unit, air lubrication system and shaft generators. These vessels are expected to be delivered at the very end of 2025.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2022-03-22 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS Having risen with crude, which was following the unfolding news of the Russian invasion of Ukraine, the MB propane market reached a peak of $1.63/gal on 8th March, only to then sink to the low $1.20’s/gal by mid-month.  At that point the market turned again to the upside and was trading on an upwards trajectory towards $1.48/gal, only to reverse course today to current levels of $1.45/gal. In the background the EIA report for the week ending 18 March showed a modest build of 300,000bbls for an overall total of 33.6mmbbls (thus signaling the conclusion of winter), and which is 81% of the total achieved last year, and 56% of the total recorded two years ago.  The small rise in stock levels is attributed to three factors:  The decline of weather-related demand in North America, cargo buy-backs in March, and cargo cancellations in March (which when combined are reckoned at around a dozen or more cargoes). Domestic U.S. production, though healthy at 2.36mmbbls/day, is effectively flat on a week-over-week basis, but as well on a year-over-year basis. As to the trade, the steep backwardation continues well in to Q2.  While March is effectively concluded, April European netbacks are more than $20/mt below March, and May is at least $10/mt below that.  Thereon, the summertime netbacks flatten out, but as with all things LPG, crude is the ultimate driver and at this moment the headlines are the primary influence. Buy/sell terminal fee ideas, meanwhile, had at one point widened from the low/mid 5cpg range to as much as 12cpg during the height of the recent volatility, but have for now settled in a more modest range of 6-8cpg, generally speaking.  NWE April CIF ARA is showing strength as the March/April backwardation comes out of the market slightly. A bid in the TOT window also increased the physical premium assessed at April CIF ARA +$33pmt. Though this is for very early April arrival rather than whole month. The arb from the US remains open and if you take into account the physical premium Northwest Europe should be drawing tons from the US. A sever with the European market the worry is that multiple cargoes will head to the region and very quickly shift the tight market into an oversupplied one. The issue has been finding US cargoes in the second half of March for arrival in early April. These cargoes were worked well ahead of time and to draw them to the region now would need them to be redirected from heading East, not impossible with the East not showing much strength. April butane also remains valued at a high level at 92% April Naphtha, though this is approached with caution due to lack of April butane activity so far.   MED The expected ramp up of buying pre-Ramadan has not materialised. VLGCs have been stuck in Moroccan ports causing delays since early March. The Chinook is now discharging C4 having arrived at Mohammedia on February 27th. This is now starting to affect coaster discharges into Morocco and is keeping the butane market in the region subdued.

### SnP Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2022-03-25 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

With a market still trying to find its bearings in light of an ever-changing geopolitical situation and a week that is cut short by several national holidays, we close this Friday with a relatively subdued snp sentiment. Despite already closing one month into the conflict, the situation as expected remains highly volatile with sanctions and their impact on global trade representing a constant moving target for market players trying to identify alternative trading patterns. An uncertainty that is as usual felt the most in the SnP markets where potential buyers and sellers prefer to apply a wait and see approach before committing to any long-term decisions. This becomes particularly apparent in the tanker space where the gradual rate recovery should in essence provide enough support for buyers to start meeting the offers at hand but where we are still experiencing some reserve as to whether this gradual "recovery" has legs.  Dry markets continue to see very high levels of enquiry and as such liquidity remains relatively high in most sectors with Supramax and Ultramax leading the charge with a handful of vessels changing hands and signs of a number of ongoing negotiations. Levels are therefore well supported across the board with some segments seeing an appreciation in values, a trend that should be set for the foreseeable future.
