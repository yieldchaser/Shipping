## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2022-06-15 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

With the buzz of Posidonia behind us, we are rattled back to reality by the recent Freeport LNG explosion and a 40% cutback of LNG flows from NordStream 1, due to technical issues, further tightening an already stretched LNG market and releasing a flurry of prompt LNG vessels, many only until recently committed, back unto the market. In turn, while LNG prices across the forward curves of Asian and European indexes rise in the wake of these events, vessels’ spot rates across both basins plummet, bringing us back to levels not seen for some weeks. However, with a fair few requirements still out there in the Atlantic and Pacific, and in anticipation of increasing demand from the East as we inch towards winter, we can expect spot rates to maintain some level of support and buoyancy going forward. On the term side of the equation, a similar story can be told as 1-Year term rates soften off the back of current events. That said, this is driven by prompt availability discounting the coming weeks. Subject to Freeport start-up, the market remains structurally tight and the prompt discount vanishes for later deliveries.  On newbuildings, two new orders have been reported this week from a Greek Head Owner as vessel prices continue to tick upwards, and with the rise in US interest rates the impact on newbuild charter rates is likely to be significant.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2022-06-13 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS The EIA report on Wednesday showed a modest build of just 1.6mmbbls for an overall propane stock total of 51.8mmbbls as of 10 June 2022, which is about 94% of last year’s total and 73% of the total achieved two years ago.  It’s also well below the average for the second week of June, which is +2.12mmbbls.  The market had been expecting something between 2.8-3.2mmbbls (with a couple of noteworthy outliers who were calling for a “flat” or “minimal” builds).   On the whole, propane values over the last week have declined from 124.50cts/gal to current levels around 118.00cts/gal.  All the while, netbacks were seen in decline as re-sale terminal fees have shrunk to ~4cts/gal.  However, by the close of business on Wednesday there appeared promise as the arb showed signs of improving – opening, even.  Furthermore, a series of offers appeared this week and supplies seem plentiful despite the three known July cancellations on the Gulf Coast. Meanwhile, demand in Latin America appears to have been satisfied via a spot sale and a tender award, though the re-opening of storage caverns near Pajaritos in Mexico may allow for some volumes to move in that direction.  Current consumer demand levels in Mexico are seasonally limited, however.  NWE  An end June propane bid continues to be show in the TOT window, supporting the market. The value was equal to July CIF ARA +$7.5pmt. With the increased Marcus Hook exports and the majority of these tons flowing to Europe, it was expected this bid would have been hit rapidly. The summer mood continues in region as storages begin to fill up. Butane in particular is long with storages full at all major demand centres. Term supply and European traders picking up Marcus Hook tons has blocked the hopes of Eastern placing some of their length in Europe. The July Propane Naphtha spread in Europe is at $98pmt and demand from petchems remains good.
