With the current global events taking centre stage and infiltrating our daily lives, it is no different for the VLCC market, as unprecedented bunker prices bite hard on owners returns. On a TCE basis, we are essentially back in the pre-invasion low WS 30s. Bumping along the bottom. Sentiment played its part at the beginning, driving rates upwards, but now, reality bites.

### Aframax Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2022-03-08 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

With most Aframax owners not willing to call Russian ports as the geopolitical situation is still very volatile, we have a two-tier market in the Nsea/Baltic area. The owners fixing out of Russian ports fix these cargoes at a very high premium compared to other areas in the North Sea. As more owners who normally would be fixing out of the Baltic market is looking at Nsea cargoes, this market came under downward pressure and rates have come off significantly. Bunker prices are still extremely high due to the current oil price, so we expect the market to stay around current levels for the time being. Much the same can be said about the Med/Bsea market with Bsea deals receiving a healthy premium vs cross-Med fixtures where the trend is softening by the day. We are expecting more of the same in the week to come.

## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2022-03-09 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Global energy prices have once again been at the beginning of most conversations this week with record levels seen for both oil and gas. Energy supply concerns, along with uncertainty and a genuine lack of tonnage available for term, sent that particular part of the market into overdrive. Term rates increased across the market with a scramble to secure modern tonnage at the back end of last week and continuing through this one. With TTF pricing where they are and more cargoes headed into Europe, tonne miles will be reduced, but tonne time may increase. However, tonnage availability may increase around the Atlantic basin. This could soften the Atlantic regions spot market in the near future despite the upward trend we are currently seeing on term rates. That being said, ships that are being taken in for term have reduced availability for the remaining enquiries now being discussed. The newbuild market remains active with another 3 new vessels ordered for delivery in 2025 from Hyundai. These vessels will be fitted with the ME-GA engine with a cargo capacity of 200,00cbm. We may also welcome another yard to the list of LNG shipbuilders as it looks like Jiangnan in China are working towards their first large LNG vessel orders.

## 4. Sale and Purchase (S&P) & Corporate Activity
### SnP Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2022-03-08 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Liquidity in the dry SnP markets is back at levels witnessed through most of last year with twenty-five units changing hands this week. The underlying macro environment is providing a relative degree of support on the freight side as mentioned last week. Rates that are driving buyers to increase their bids in order to secure additional tonnage. Most of that activity is captured by the Kamsarmax/Panamax and the Utramax/Supramax markets with seven and eight vessels being sold in those respective segments. Worth noting that sellers of Chinese built candidates are the ones more willing to transact with prices deemed to have reach relative heights while potential sellers of Japanese designed vessels are counting on the sharper drive in values usually experienced for quality designs in such rising markets.  Pick up in activity in the tanker space as well, with most market players expecting values to take turn off the back of the recent relative increase in rates. Although earnings remain limited in most sectors due to increased bunker costs, buyers are still willing to go meet the offer, expecting the current situation to have further positive impact on rates which will translate in higher second-hand values. The VLCC sector continues to see a number of units changing hands with a number of ongoing negotiations suggesting even further activity in the near future.
