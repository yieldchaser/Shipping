## 2. Tanker Sector (Crude & Products)
### VLCC Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2022-03-30 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Working the VLCC market at present, can seem like being uninvited to a party and resorting to looking through the windows. While other markets ebb and flow, ours remain somewhat subdued. However, for owners, some optimism should remain. At the time of writing, there are plenty of cargoes looking for transport. A pickup in activity (72 MEG cargoes, and we only fixing mid-month laycans) and many owners slow steaming is thinning the position list in both Atlantic and Fuj, (albeit mainly the modern-Approved-scrubbered vessels).  With the MEG paying up to WS 42.5, some charterers may look to older/handicapped, and of those, plenty remain. The Atlantic is the realm of those who can – a premium of USD 200+ for low over high sulphur bunkers pricing out the unscrubbered. It feels the VL’s in need of a little trickle-up economics.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2022-03-28 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS  Crude oil market volatility, driven by headlines related to both Covid-19 issues in China and, separately, the war in Ukraine, has pushed most participants to the sidelines as the two issues have created opposing forces which – on alternating days – has caused WTI and Brent values to rise and fall at significant rates. Mont Belvieu propane, meanwhile, continues to ebb and flow consequently, having peaked on 08 Mar at $1.63/gal, only then to fall sharply to $1.22/gal by the 15th of the month.  That’s when an upward trajectory was resumed and a peak of $1.47/gal was seen, only then to fall again to $1.395/gal at present. The arbs have remained positive, however, as offshore values have mostly kept track all the while, though at present arbs have narrowed. Terminal fees, meanwhile, are loosely in the 6-7cpg range, depending on timing/ratio, etc., with a couple of outliers on either side.  One transaction to an end-user was reported for a late April 4:0 in the upper 6’s, another mixed-ratio parcel for April was said placed in the low/mid 6s.  NWE Competing macro factors in the oil market have made for a volatile week in Europe. NWE LPG prices came off considerably (11 dollars / ton) on back of Brent weakness, and large cargo activity was fairly quiet. That said, insecurity of supply surrounding the ongoing conflict in Ukraine, along with considerably cold temperatures across the region would theoretically stoke demand- however, its assumed that scheduled arrival of 1h April propane cargoes will sufficiently absorb the additional demand. The lack of strength of NWE price could also be attributed to the maintenance on as many as 5 steam crackers- that would otherwise be favoring propane- given the current discounts to naphtha.   MED Very little activity in Med LPG trade as the market mostly assessed the newly posted Sonatrach prices, that came in at an 8 year high following the strong ramp up in crude prices this month. Sonatrach posted prices of $870/ton $930/ton for propane and butane respectively.
