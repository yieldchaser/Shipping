## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2022-11-16 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Upon us we have yet another stagnant week of spot activity, with rates dug-in and entrenched at record highs. With many requirements in the East & West still uncovered and portfolios still thirsting for any ship with a LNG tank on its back, it’s clear that rates aren’t going anywhere anytime soon with the ongoing lack of sublet availability and market liquidity. However, with Freeport facilities yesterday reporting continued delays in their anticipated start-up into early 2023, perhaps Off-takers might now be able to shed a smidge of shipping length to relieve some of this market pressure.   Shifting focus to the term market, availability from 2023 and beyond continues to get picked off week on week, further stoking term rates for all remaining vessel classes. With fewer 2-Strokes from Independent Owners than fingers on one hand, the focus for many term-takers at present are TFDE’s as headline rates for those few modern vessels maintain their hefty price tag premium. With that in mind, we marginally adjust our term shipping rates upward in view of continued demand while keeping spot rates constant. On the Newbuildings front this week, yard pricing remains robust as Minerva Gas has reportedly won contracts from ExxonMobil for two LNGC’s with anticipated delivery in 2026.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2022-11-15 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS As of the first week of Nov US propane inventories had eclipsed the 5-year average by some 5mmbbls and reached an overall total of 87.7mmbbls.  Last week saw a modest 400,000bbl draw, but this week – which was anticipated to realize a draw of around a million barrels – saw a build of 100,000bbls.  In effect, the market is in the ‘shoulder’ period, late though it may be. In short, as of 11 Nov the US inventories stand at 87.8mmbbls, or 118% of last year and 94% of two years ago.  This propels the current stock level to around 5mmbbls above the 5-year average (82.6mmbbls).   The immediate explanation as to why stocks increased this week is that (for starters) winter is later to commence than usual, but also includes missed laycans due to shipping delays as well as reduced load rates over the past couple of weeks at one of the Gulf Coast terminals; the combined impact being the modest build.  Spot terminal fees now range rather widely, from the upper 5cpg level to over 7cpg, depending on timing / ratio / location, etc., but little is being accomplished due to shipping-related anomalies which stem from the fantastic delays presently being seen at the Panama Canal as well as the general lack of weather-related demand so far this season.  NWE The physical window has calmed down after early week excitement due to reported LPG production curbs at Kollsnes in the North Sea. Since the arb has not seen any marked change, though buying interest has come off. Attributed to the continued mild winter and unchanged petchem demand. The East / West spread remains near $110pmt for propane, so unsold US Gulf Coast loading in 2nd half November and 1st half December are not considering selling in the West. Butane demand remains strong primarily for blending and lies 94% naphtha.   MED The mild weather continues to keep demand in the region well supplied by term deliveries.
