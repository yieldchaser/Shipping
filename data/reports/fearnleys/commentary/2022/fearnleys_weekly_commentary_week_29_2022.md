The market remains supported for both basins, but it is missing expected spark to guide rates to next level, therefore we need to see a continued flow of cargo moving forward. ECSA rather quieter for end July and/or mid-August loaders, with tonnage counts still high as weeks of low Pacific market forced the owners to ballast south towards Singapore, therefore rates remain at best flat hovering around USD 18,000 daily.

## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2022-07-20 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

As the UK and Europe battle all that these current record temperatures are bringing, there’s no need to remind our Northern Hemisphere readers that we’re in the midst of Summer. Liquidity has dried up and the holiday season continues unabated. Nonetheless, there is definitely an undercurrent of repressed uncertainty about what might be in store when everyone gets back to their desk. NS1’s impending restart marks the first time many of us have read about the travel plans of a broken Turbine in the trade press but come tomorrow, all eyes will certainly be on East to West gas flows. Given that European volumes used for winter are currently being drawn down in some countries, the race to refill before the heating is turned on just got a little more intense. The Atlantic shipping market is displaying similar behaviours as protagonists weigh the weakness on the prompt with the need to ensure security of supply (i.e., tonnage) over winter. To this end, we see a massive disconnect between spot and term rates which makes life tricky for both Owners and Charterers at this exact time. In the East, we’ve seen a few requirements emerge this week which, as part of the usual churn of activity, sees the Pacific market retain its premium to the West. No Newbuildings to report this week (which is itself newsworthy) but as prices continue their ascent, there’s no sign of demand easing up in this segment yet.
