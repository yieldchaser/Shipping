## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2022-08-01 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

With TTF sitting comfortably above $60/MMBtu, it’s not surprising to see Charterers paying up for 2-Strokes. But with spot loadings fixing at a fraction of the Term rates, there’s a big spread to be bridged between now and Winter. Of course, geopolitical uncertainty is high and given the dollars at stake with every cargo, it’s arguably sound risk management to go into Q4 comfortably long. For those Owners who can monetise their length, however, recent reports of $200k pdpr deals in the 2-Stroke segment should provide the necessary incentive to optimise. Speaking of which, optimisation on the prompt is alive and well with an interesting development this past week being the emergence of very prompt cargoes in both basins. This has provided some excellent “keep cool” voyages for owners but also suggests that: i. projects are trying to squeeze out additional cargoes, and ii. portfolio players see real value in fine-tuning their schedules. If we see enough of these cargoes emerge, spot rates will firm again but for now, the only guaranteed strengthening comes from the yards, with Newbuild prices up again this week.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2022-08-02 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS Arbs have suffered in recent weeks, primarily due to the influence on the LPG pricing structure by the declining crude market, and where at the start of July WTI was over $108/bbl it is now below $90/bbl and still volatile.  Propane values, meanwhile, have fallen from $1.21/gal to $1.09/gal over the same period. Relatively strong inventory gains have also been a factor in that propane stocks have risen to 63.6mmbbls as of end July, or 96% of last year’s level.  However, current stocks are notably just 71% of the 86.7mmbbl inventory level achieved two years ago.  Given the heat and dry climate across North America, crop drying should not be much of a factor which should then, in turn, allow stock levels to gain a bit more traction. With the inventory goal being somewhere near the 80mmbbl range, there is a 16.4mmbbl deficit at present, and to reach that target level a weekly build of 1.82mmbbls over the next 9 weeks will be required.  If the timeline is shifted to mid-Oct, then a 1.49mmbbl weekly build would suffice, and for an end-Oct deadline a 1.26mmbbl weekly increase would be needed. The August lifting roster is nearly complete with only a small number of potential cargo slots remaining.  Sep, however, is beginning to emerge and where the Aug terminal fee values are placed at/below 5cpg, Sep is perhaps at/above 5cts, at least for the first half of the month.  2H Sep is pegged in the mid 5s at present.  Q4 strips were talked in the 6cpg range. A full propane VL tender was issued last week for 2H Sep CFR Chile and was awarded at a price deemed to be ‘well below’ last year’s level.  NWE The TOT window in Europe has been quiet recently, without any bids or offers. August import volume is increasing, with term volumes starting to trickle in from the US.
