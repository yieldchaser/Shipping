## 2. Tanker Sector (Crude & Products)
### VLCC Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2022-03-02 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Although VLCC rates have all but doubled in the week gone by the MEG position list is still in need of thinning for the market to move on from pure sentiment to a fundamental change. Baltic and Black Sea aside, rates in general have gone up because owners have been unwilling to fix and sitting on their hands. **Date:** 2022-02-28 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS  Volatility has been the primary characteristic of the domestic energy markets this week, having been spurred on by events in eastern Europe, rising crude values, softening freight and widening arbs to both the East and the West.  The result is that propane prices at Mont Belvieu rose by more than 12cts/gal over just this week to $1.53/gal.  There remains, however, quite steep backwardation which extends well in to 2H 2022 and then in to the upper $1.20’s/gal. Meanwhile, terminal fees this week spiked hard and the few remaining March cargoes are known to have been fixed in the double-digits.  Motivated buyers, sensing stronger demand in Europe because of the Russian advancements into Ukraine, emerged for March and 1H Apr volumes.  It is anticipated that around 175,000mt per month of LPG formerly supplied by Russia requires replacement, and hence the enthusiasm. With regards the inventory levels of propane in the U.S., the EIA revealed an 800,000bbl overall weekly reduction which brought stock levels to 37.2mmbbls.  The market took that in stride and essentially shrugged off the large decline in PADD 2 but pointed to the week-over-week increase in PADD III, the Gulf Coast, which saw a build of 900,000bbls.  Cynics were prepared to wait for next week’s release for a ‘truer’ stock picture, as many believe there have been data errors in recent reports.  NWE As the European LPG market tries to come to grips with the effects of the Russia-Ukraine conflict, supply concerns have come to the fore in an already tight market. This led to a scramble from European traders to find US FOBs as transatlantic March imports were expected to be around 120,000MT far below the 460,000MT imported in February. With FOB US bids rising on European trader interest March cargoes surfaced and most believe these additional exports were only made possible by the lower than expected draw in US inventories. At least 3 additional VLGCs were purchased for the European market which will calm some traders for end March and 1H April. The prompt market is short with the TOT window premium valued at March CIF ARA +$18pmt. The butane market is valued at 97% March Naphtha which is a strong value in this market and only for March tons, as the end of the month ends the gasoline blending season.  MED Butane values have hit eight-year highs, though offers are seen in the market but no bids. A butane heavy cargo from North West Europe is signalling arrival in Egypt in April. ## 4. Sale and Purchase (S&P) & Corporate Activity
### SnP Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2022-02-28 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

As we end another week of escalating conflicts in Eastern Europe and as sanctions on Russian interests unravel, we continue to feel its effect on all shipping markets. Confusion around which specific entities are being sanctioned has paved the way for a number of tanker owners to proceed with Baltic and BSea voyages offering substantial risk premiums for Russian liftings. The majority of players however are opting for a "self-sanctioning" approach with refiners and traders shying away from Russian product. Should the situation remain or worsen we expect these soaring regional rates to continue their climb and provide wider support for freight across all geographies. The sale and purchase markets have seen a pick-up in activity in the VLCC sector with 5 units changing hands this week, breaking an extensive stretch of inactivity in the segment. A strengthening buying interest that is driving buyers to re-evaluate their bids to go meet a thinning list of available candidates in hope to capture the gains that are currently witnessed in the chartering markets.  In a relative contrast to the volatility experienced by Tanker players, the Dry sectors are continuing to joy of steadily strengthening markets. A strength that can be felt through the most recent SnP transactions that have seen vessels achieve levels in excess of last dones. A dynamic that is particularly well illustrated by the sale of the 2012 Mitsui built MV "ORCHID" which achieved Usd 22.6m.
