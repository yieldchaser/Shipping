## 2. Tanker Sector (Crude & Products)
### VLCC Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2022-02-23 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

A small surge of enquiry in Wafr saw a WS 37.5 logged eastbound and WS 41 reported to WCI yesterday, with potential for more as cargoes remain uncovered. ## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2022-02-22 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Last week we noted considerable uncertainty and no reason to have a different view this week, geopolitical news needs to be under constant watch, not that this is particularly profound but now is a particularly sensitive time. On top of this backdrop, spot cargoes prices rose due to weather driven eastern demand amid colder weather in China and intensified competition with Europe for cargoes, something that could very easily continue unabated. This view could explain the rise in share prices of Asian shipbuilders and shipping firms which rallied, likely on the expectations that European countries may seek more seaborne energy sources on news that Russia’s Nord Stream 2 pipeline’s approval process has been halted.   On the prompt, tonnage remains abundant as a high percentage of vessels remain contained in their respective basin leading to persistent lower than average voyage distance and thus additional capacity remains plentiful. Multi-month activity remains active with several live tenders being worked and bidding interest in vessels remains. This week we increase the multi-year levels supported by a few fixtures yet despite this rise, the bid/ask spread oscillates depending on vessel type and delivery timings, plenty of nuances that need to be understood yet clarity should start to reduce these as we get closer to Q3 and charterer preferred delivery timings near.   In the new build market, one reported transaction this week with Hyundai Glovis booking a 2H2024 slot that is rumoured to be on the back of a charter. 2024 is all but sold out whilst 2025 follows suit with all slots reserved and as such the newbuilding price continues its ascent.

## 4. Sale and Purchase (S&P) & Corporate Activity
### SnP Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2022-02-21 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Following weeks of escalation - the growing fears shared by most over the past few weeks have unfortunately become a reality with Russia’s military operations currently underway in Ukraine. A volatile geopolitical environment with undeniable ramifications across all markets which have met Russia’s aggression with large scale economic sanctions. Measures that have yet to be fully announced and which implications remain to be seen; an uncertainty that has left the tanker markets with an already tighter list of available tonnage. Adding to an already skewed fleet positioning east of Suez leading to rate gains in the Atlantic. Baltic Aframax rates have also surged with most owners deciding to hold back unless they see a considerable premium from charterers. We expect these firming chartering markets to soon translate into a pick in SnP activity with potential buyers finding enough support from these improved rates to pull the trigger on future acquisitions.  Dry cargo players are of course also endeavouring to digest the effect that these recent events will have on their market; direct impact is as expected felt in the Black Sea where Ukraine has officially halted its operations in several of its ports including from Yuznhy where force majeure has been declared. A disruption in exports for several key commodities coming out of the Black Sea that will have to be substituted should the situation persists. The tonnage list in the region is of course following suit with owners shying away from Black Sea fixtures. Similarly, we expect this tension on rates to provided support to an already steady value environment which could be mean further gains for Dry cargo assets across all segments.
