### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2022-06-06 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS Propane market levels at Mont Belvieu continue to drift, though rather inconsistently, having peaked in the second week of March at 163.75cpg, and closing most recently at ~123.00cpg.  Conversely, WTI has been trending up since early December and overwater LPG exports remain robust – the combination of which might normally lead to a firm pricing structure.  The conclusion of winter demand and rising stock levels, though, have colluded and allowed values to decline.  Meanwhile, propane as a percentage of crude has been drifting, as well, as Q4 2021 levels were in the 80-percentile range but this week are flirting in the low 40% range. LatAm demand was revealed this week as a primary buyer issued a tender for 33-44,000mt for delivery from 3-9 August.  Offers are expected by 1pm on Thursday 9 June and are to remain valid until Noon EST on Friday 10 June. Terminal fees have reached a plateu of sorts, with buyers and sellers at/above 6cpg, though out-month 2022 transactions are known to have occurred at 6.00cpg, 6.25cpg and 6.50cpg. The EIA this week showed a 600,000bbl build to a total of 50.20mmbbls, or over 93% of last year’s total.  Expectations ranged from 2.75-3.25mmbbls, so the weak build was seen as disappointing – and at least mildly bullish on a short-term basis.  NWE The region looks well supplied with a large volume of trans-Atlantic cargoes arriving in the second half of June. Counter intuitively the TOT window premiums remain for 2H June delivery at June CIF ARA +$5pmt. A stronger premium than the June / July backwardation of $2pmt. Crude continuing to climb has kept the propane / Naphtha spread above $120pmt, supporting industrial propane demand in the region. The majority of US East Coast exports continue to come to Europe, and these exports are growing rapidly. Butane has been steady at 91% Naphtha, though this level has not been tested recently in a fairly stagnant Northwest European butane market.   MED The region is well supplied through term contracts and no additional demand has been seen. Although a fire at Fawley refinery in the UK is expected to tighten butane supply to the Med.
