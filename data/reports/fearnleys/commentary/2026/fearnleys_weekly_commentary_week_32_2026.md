## 3. Gas & LNG Markets

**Date:** 2026-08-05 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Atlantic rates have come under considerable pressure, with a growing availability list chasing relatively few requirements and competing not just against each other but also against aggressive FOB structures. The impact on spot rates has been dramatic, to the extent that the Pacific basin now commands a premium for the first time this year. The market East of Suez has also had to contend with excess tonnage, but so far rates have been supported by the premiums charterers are prepared to pay for larger, more modern vessels. There is more likely to be further downward pressure in both basins, as Atlantic sublets try to accommodate their own backstops dates while Pacific vessels are at risk of warming up; unless more cargoes emerge to fill the gap between now and the early-September requirements, the list of ships requiring cool-down threatens to grow substantially. There have also been several multi-month fixtures, spanning periods through this winter out to the end of next year, at rates that suggest a muted winter season. There has been some discussion as to whether the winter freight market will be buoyed by higher LNG prices and thus attractive LNG trading dynamics or weighed down by soft fundamentals; the signs today suggest the latter trends may dictate the coming months.
