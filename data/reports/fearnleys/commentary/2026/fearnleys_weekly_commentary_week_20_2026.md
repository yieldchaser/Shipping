## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2026-05-13 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Some significant LNG shipping developments in the Middle East over the past week, as both QELM and ADNOC vessels are reported to have transited the Strait of Hormuz on laden voyages. This marks the first LNG cargoes loaded within the Gulf to exit via the Strait of Hormuz since conflict began in late February. While this is not a signal that regular LNG transits are on the horizon, and the scope of the agreements (if any) between the counterparties and Iran are unknown, it gives some hope that Qatari and Abu Dhabi volumes are not beyond reach.   In the East, sparse May cargoes coupled with ample supply has led to hot competition across the tonnage classes for prompt requirements. Owners that are able to call Qalhat are somewhat insulated from the quieter SEA market, and continue to enjoy a steady stream of opportunities to load Omani volumes at premium freight rates. Requirements are emerging for June but currently these are stacked towards the second half of the month, giving owners the dilemma of fixing forward and absorbing idle time, or waiting and risking unemployed vessels. Requirements out of the US East Coast and especially West Africa have been able to entice tonnage to reposition from the East, offering attractive utilisation and a rate premium to Pacific cargoes. While Kitimat’s schedule remains uncertain, Barossa loadings are expected to be resume, which the market hopes will boost liquidity East of Suez.   The Atlantic market has proved very stubborn, with rates remaining tightly bound in the mid-$90k/d range when accounting for vessel specifications. The requirement list has been a little lumpy though, with relatively few cargoes in early June set against an extensive list from mid-June onwards. We may therefore see a little more variability as charterers face more or less competition for individual requirements, but the fundamentals and in particular the situation in the Middle East are showing no signs of change in the coming weeks.   Following the trend we have seen through the past few weeks, multi-month coverage through Q3/Q4 remains of interest but charterers have eased back on taking on tonnage. The bullish sentiment of the major portfolio players has trickled through the market and we do see some additional subletters hoping to lock-in utilisation, and this may explain the “wait and see” approach among charterers now.
