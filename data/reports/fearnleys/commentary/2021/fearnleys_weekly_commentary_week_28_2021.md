### Panamax Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2021-07-13 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

It would seem the “summer market” has hit both hemispheres the last week, both on physical and FFA. The slight flow of fresh requirements in the North Atlantic is also seeing competition from larger vessels cutting size, hence the positive effect of the fresh requirements seem to be minimal. ## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2021-07-14 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

After a couple of relatively quiet weeks in the spot market, we are seeing more requirements for August and September laycans both East and West. Nevertheless, fixing levels have continued their slight soft trend as sublet tonnage competes for these cargoes in an effort to optimise their portfolios. Cargo market remains volatile at firm levels with European and Asian markets competing for the cargoes and destination changes coupled with some production issues out of the US may encourage more sublet opportunities on the short term. For the ones keeping an eye for term tonnage, despite the slow down in activity this week, freight levels remain robust as Independent Owners with remaining availability are weighing different opportunities. Important to note again that tonnage options are slimming as most tonnage has shifted hands and been incorporated into portfolios, which will play a key factor on how the market develops in the months to come. In the Newbuilding markets, prices keep firming for all types of tonnage, including LNG ships. For new orders, Capital Gas has exercised another option and added one more vessel to their latest pair of speculative orders a couple of weeks ago. Vessel is expected for delivery late 2023.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2021-07-12 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

NWE The physical market is assessed at August CIF ARA + $8pmt and the CIF ARA Large butane quotes were at 95% balance July Naphtha. Both strong physical discussions due to the fact that no August arrival cargoes have been heard heading to North West Europe from the US so far. With crude oil coming off and CIF ARA tracking Brent faster than Mt Belvieu is tracking WTI this is unlikely to change in the near future as the arb narrows. August Pro/Nap spread at -$37pmt will keep petchem demand up and they will have to source the product from somewhere, which should in turn widen the arb.  MED Butane market in the region has been strong these past days. Lack of supply from the US as well as demand enquiries is fueling strength. Tightness has been the theme of the summer in the region with values doing just enough to keep supply coming but not yet breaking out.  AMERICAS The domestic US propane market received this week’s EIA inventory report with a lackluster response, as the 1.6mmbbl week-over-week build was generally in line with expectations.  As of 09 July domestic US propane stock levels were listed at 59.60mmbbls, or 74% of the levels recorded at the same time last year, 80.30mmbbls. The pricing structure did very little to react afterwards and remained at about the 1.10/gal range, but overnight trading in Europe and Asia saw values in those markets decline, and with the US propane-to-WTI percentage at 64%, expectations are for MB prices to soften, particularly since WTI is now in retreat.  Terminal fees are tiered, at this point, with a recent resale having occurred at a reported 4.625/gal for mid-Aug.  Asking levels are now in the 5cts/gal range.  The terminals themselves, however, remain at 6cts/gal (or above) as the Ethane/Propane pricing differential continues to dissuade the production of Low-Ethane propane, the export-grade product derived from the American spec, HD5, which has a higher ethane content.

## 4. Sale and Purchase (S&P) & Corporate Activity
### SnP Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2021-07-16 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

In the tanker space we have witnessed some interesting transactions just before the holiday season kicks in and we see a continued lift in values on the older and bigger tankers. Olympic managed to squeeze out an extra bonus when they sold their M/T Olympic Legend (2003, Samsung, 309k dwt) at Usd 31.5 mill. No attenuating circumstances such as BWTS or scrubber that could have condoned this higher price even more so considering that she is due for docking later this year. It’s a good Usd 2.5 mill up from Maran’s sale of similarly aged VLCC only a couple of weeks back. A continued interest for modern tankers is proven yet again by JP Morgan’s move who have confirmed this week the purchase of 2 x 114k dwt LR2 resales from CMB. Although the vessels were originally caught up in the Hanjin Subic liquidation at building stage, they were subsequently completed in South Korea and have now achieved Usd 55 mill each. A relative strong price given the original yard and history of construction but one that underlines the current newbuilding markets with yards uninterested to go meet the tanker demand having taken on a flurry of more competitive container orders on their orderbooks.  Perhaps the first week within this past month that most dry players have had the time to digest more recent activity with only a handful of sales to report. As previously reported the Cape market saw a number of units being placed for sale over the past weeks which are now changing hands. Namely the scrubber and BWTS-fitted “BULK DENMARK” (2010, Koyo, 181k dwt) which was picked up by Thenamaris at Usd 31m. JP Morgan also invited offers on their 2012 HHI built Capesize “TRUE ENDURANCE” earlier this week after having up to 10 parties registering their interest.
