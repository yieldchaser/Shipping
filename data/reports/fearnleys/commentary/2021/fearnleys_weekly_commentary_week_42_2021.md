## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2021-10-20 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The LNG market remains at historically high levels while the freight market continues to climb in fits and starts, with a definite break now appearing between the higher levels seen in the Pacific basin and lower levels in the Atlantic. This is an unusual situation heading into winter, and may be the result of fewer ships travelling on the West-to-East conveyor belt when Europe fought for USG cargoes. There are relatively few firm requirements for this time of year and not many vessels being actively marketed, and combined with fewer clean fixtures there is some uncertainty as to where rate levels will go from here. On paper at least, charter rates and TCEs could top the record levels seen last winter; however, it only takes one sublet with just the right availability seeking to mitigate idle time and costly boil-off for those theoretical returns to take a big hit. On the longer term, several charterers are already making a move for 2022 tonnage. Again, this is unprecedented for this time of year and all points to a very tight market in the coming years. There have been no newbuilding orders this week, however yard slots are few and far between due to activity in other shipping segments.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2021-10-18 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS A primary U.S. propane inventory increase of 1.9mmbbls was recorded by the EIA for the week ending 15 Oct, surprising most of the market which had been anticipating either a modest draw or a small build.  Total stocks have reached 73.6mmbbls, or 75% of both last year and two years ago.   On a Days of Supply basis, there continues to be a deficit against last year, and stocks on that basis are still below the range of the last 5 years, hence the ongoing concerned focus related to the winter demand period as it relates to the continuing robust export volumes.  Although there has begun to be cooler weather in North America, there have yet to be any real storm systems which might mark the true commencement of winter.   Cargo availability continues to be split, with offers being seen from the trading community, while still being limited from the terminals themselves.  Terminal fees reflect this and for Nov spot trade is presently focused at just under 6cts/gal.  NWE The well documented news that LPG is being spiked into the LNG stream by producers has rapidly improved CIFA ARA values. Though TOT window offers have been generating the most discussions. Dow and Vilma both offering in the window was seen as bearish. Dow is expected to receive term tons and is not running at 100% so are looking to make an achievable profit on tons they have in the region today. For traders the arb is wide open, netback values from the US are around 9cpg for November for US liftings arriving in North West Europe in the same month. Raising the question whether selling in the TOT window can make more sense than waiting in the Panama canal to sell at a discount in the East. The question is whether these offers will push the window into a discount, or if the European market with less locally produced tons, can absorb the offered tons and more heading into this winter market?   MED The quiet market and prevailing tight supply environment continues. Moroccan public holidays adding to the lack of trade discussions. No winter weather in the Mediterranean region aiding the wait and see trader attitude.
