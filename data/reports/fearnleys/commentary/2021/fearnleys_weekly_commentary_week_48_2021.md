## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2021-11-30 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Following a flurry of sublet activity and market volatility in LNG these past couple of months, it appears we may finally be peaking and on trajectory to softer markets. Although Asian LNG prices remain relatively elevated in recent days off the back of stronger than usual European gas, it seems shipping rates have plateaued in part due to growing spot availability from subletters keen to get a taste for record high shipping rates. Optimization has surely been key to lucrative trading reported, with most sublets seen from portfolio players and traders dominating the vessel list. On term shipping, given a fair few vessel redeliveries to Head Owners are expected within Q1 of 2022, several multi-month and short term enquiries appear to be on hold with Owners and Charterers as they evaluate the market and consider their approach. That said, we still do see and expect continued activity on the term market as Charterers seek coverage through 2022, with some even issuing requirements earlier than expected.  On the newbuilding front, it was an active week with Qatari, Asian and Greek Owners all adding to their newbuilding orderbook in Korea.

**Date:** 2021-11-29 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS The sky is probably NOT falling but it sure feels like it. Lack of weather across the US has caused propane prices to crash from a 1.41 per gallon a month ago to 97 cents per gallon today.  The inventory situation, which a month ago looked as though the US would not have enough product to cover first quarter export demand,  is no longer an issue. Talk of 10 percent production growth, the lack of weather and falling Asian demand,  have all contributed,  but the bottom line is, we are not running out of product. The weekly number showed a draw of 1 million barrels which had little effect on the market.  Terminal  fees have continued to fall with Jan cargos bid at 4.50 cpg and sellers at just under 6.  NWE  The European market seems to be the only one which knows what winter is! CIF ARA continues to show relative strength in the context of FEI losing over $50pmt today and Mt Belvieu propane dipping under the dollar mark for the first time in months. Europe is feeling winter weather already and is forecast to have coldest winter in 30 years. But that is not the whole story as PDHs started buying again in December and January, ramping up imports from the US. And local supply continues to be around 36% less than usual as LPG is spiked into the natural gas stream. The arb East coming in has the December East/West spread at +$32 and more US loading VLGCs looking to come to the region. A seen by today’s window offer of a full propane VLGC cargo for end December arrival at December CIF ARA flat. This offer was basis 1 port with EU qualifications for buyer and equivalent to an offer of CIF ARA +$5pmt for a TOT stem.   MED The market is calm in the region and it seems buyers are waiting to see offers before showing their hand. Cold weather is being felt and demand is increasing, although this is yet to translate to action in the market.
