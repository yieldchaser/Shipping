## 2. Tanker Sector (Crude & Products)
### VLCC Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2021-06-23 | **Subtype:** Tank Weekly Comment | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

A little more was expected of this week, as the July MEG stems hit the shelves. However, under the radar and drip-feed tactics continue. OPEC+ remain in discussion for further easing on oil output cuts, as prices rise on the expectation of continued demand recovery. These price increases of course having a detrimental effect on VLCC owners bottom lines, as bunkers soared to close to the USD 540/mt level in Singapore. A grim reality is that some owners on ships lacking scrubbers can only make sense of voyages if they already have cheaper bunkers on board.  The result is perhaps a glimmer of improvement when we look at worldscale rates, as MEG/East improves a point to ws32.5, Wafr/East at the ws34 level, but the TCE’s on a particular voyage likely lower, as owners battle to keep voyage calculations in the black.

### Aframax Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2021-06-23 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Nsea/Baltic Aframax rates increased significantly after several weeks of remaining at close to zero return levels. ## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2021-06-22 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Although the maintenance and drydocking season is well underway it doesn’t appear to affect the shipping market much. Spot rates continue to build on the previous weeks’ gains, supported by term discussions and a strong product market due in part to warm weather across Europe and some problems with European pipeline supply. There are available vessels particularly in the east, but it must be noted that most of those offering today are subletters and the tonnage could quickly be withdrawn if the cargo market strengthens further or the East/West arbitrage increases. We’ve seen two orders for multiple newbuildings this week: one from COSCO and PCI firming up three options at Hudong-Zhonghua, and one from Capital Gas at HHI. The Cosco tonnage is due for delivery at the end of 2024 and early 2025, whiles the Capital Gas tonnage is due for delivery by the end of 2023.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2021-06-21 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

NWE Window bidding failing to find seller has raised CIF ARA paper value as well as pushed the physical window premium to near CIF ARA July +$10pmt. All tons arriving to the region are accounted for by crackers, leaving 1H July tight. The arb from the US remains unworkable as Mt Belvieu strength continues off the back of strong crude and lower than expected inventory build. So although the netback has increased and CIF ARA July is getting close to 3cpg, it is still more than 1cpg below the FEI netback. For the time being the market is set to remain tight.   MED There has been butane selling interest in the region, though buyers are keeping quiet. Petchem demand in the West Med is strong, VLGCs coming in and no surplus product being resold.  AMERICAS                                                                                                                                                                                Market values for July propane at Mont Belvieu have risen to $102.75/gal (as of this writing), riding the combined wave of support from both the crude oil sector as well as the most recent propane inventory data released by the EIA. In that report the government revealed a 1.046mmbbl increase in primary propane stocks in the U.S., bringing the overall total to 56.240mmbbls, which compares to last year’s total figure of 71.331mmbbls, or a 15.091mmbbl deficit YOY. WTI, meanwhile, was marked at $73.08/bbl for August, having been spurred by U.S. gasoline futures which – in turn – were being pushed by a bullish inventory drawdown. Terminal fees in the U.S. are presently in the 4.50-5.00cts/gal range, with offers seen on the higher end of the range and bids at the lower end, and more than a penny lower on the East Coast. Meanwhile, in an interesting turn of events not seen in well over a decade, Enterprise this week marked itself as a buyer of August Enterprise cargoes (any dates, any ratio) and is now known to have purchased a cargo from a term customer.  Additional purchases of similar variety are anticipated.
