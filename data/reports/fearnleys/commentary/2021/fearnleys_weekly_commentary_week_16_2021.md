## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2021-04-21 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

With the intensity of the LNG shipping market last weeks, one could have perceived this to be the pre-winter months of previous years when comparing the amount of chartering enquiries, the increasing LNG prices and the firming charter rate levels. While the market corrected itself last February after a winter with high gas demand, it was expected that replenishment of LNG inventories would take place over the subsequent summer months. Hence, the market were preparing itself for maybe a more active summer. Vessels are now being fixed on term charters as a regular occurrence, not just to ensure shipment coverage over the winter of 2021, but also in anticipation of a more active summer. With this activity and LNG market setting, greatly reducing a charterers potential idle time over the next 12 months charter period, Charterers are able to justify to move quickly to secure tonnage. Available modern vessels are depleting fast in both basins as spot and term charters are being concluded.

MED The sentiment was weaker in the West Med though butane was steady vs Naphtha. Demand levels were lower than anticipated. Though this has yet to be reflected in regional prices, butane was steady and propane showed some strength.  AMERICAS Crude oil prices were down early this week as the API were forecasting an increase in crude oil stocks, while Mont Belvieu propane values continued to drift on the belief that domestic stock levels will remain sufficient.  Looking towards the summer, the domestic US forward propane pricing curve is effectively flat through September.  This is primarily due to the belief that crude oil production will continue to be healthy and promote production levels, which will need to exceed the combined demand levels of the North American res/com market, the US petrochemical sector and – most importantly – waterborne export markets which – according to the EIA – are at well-above average rates of 1.7mmbbls/day. In fact for this month, April 2021  we expect a record of 90 vessels loading.   Meanwhile, the EIA this week were expected to report that propane stocks in the US built by something under a million barrels as of the week ending 16 April.  It reported, however, a 74,000bbl draw – which included a half-million barrel decline in PADD 3 stocks.  Total overall propane inventories as of 16 April are now 40.5mmbbls, which compares to last year’s 57.4mmbbls and the 2019 total of 51.8mmbbls.  All the while a spate of petchem-related buying in Europe has kept the US export market moving at a robust clip as US terminal fees recently dipped to levels in the low 4ct/gal range for end-May, but are sharply higher for June volumes at asking levels of 6ct/gal.
