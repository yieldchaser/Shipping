## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2021-06-08 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Activity in the spot market remained fairly muted this week. With firm commodity prices & freight rates, discussions around ship positions, cargo trades and optimisation of both were taking place, but few deals were concluded. However, the Far East thirst for 3Q deliveries are now dragging vessels from the Pacific into the Atlantic for US-sourced cargoes back to Asia. Spot Pacific requirements, offering owners shorter charter durations, would normally warrant a premium in these circumstances, but sublet vessels with pockets of availability have dampened this effect. LNG shipping availability may however tighten as we look ahead. With the current arbitrage opportunities, there is a clear risk for more and longer voyages West to East. Scheduling may then become challenging with potential waiting time in Panama versus taking the long route via Cape.  With yard prices firming across shipping segments, the newbuild market saw 2 interesting orders being concluded. JOVO place an order for a rare mid-sized 79,800cbm vessel at Jiangnan. Over at KSOE (HHI) 4x 200,000cbm LNGC were ordered and these vessels are apparently to be fitted with the new ME-GA two-stroke engines.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2021-06-08 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS The EIA posted a weekly propane build of 5.5mmbbl as of the week ending 04 May, bringing overall US stocks from 48.151mmbbls to a current level of 53.650mmbbls.  The market was very surprised, having anticipated a build of not more than about 2mmbbls – on the high side. Prices reacted almost immediately to the unexpected build and declined from the low 95cts/gal level in to the mid 93ct range, though a slow resurgence was noted in the early afternoon hours.  24 hours later the market was bid at 95cts again.  This, however, may have been because buyers stepped in during the moment of pricing weakness as well as the strong crude oil markets.   While there remains quite a lot of skepticism as to the validity of the EIA data, it is generally believed that higher-than-anticipated butane exports, previously canceled cargoes, the reclassification of Gulf Coast propane inventory and healthy production levels are the reasons behind the increase. Terminal fees, having been in the low single digit levels, have this week seen a resurgence and are now seen in the 4cts/gal range.    In South America, continued buying demand from Brasil continues to provide a floor for the FOB markets, with Petrobras buying one to two spot cargos per month.

## 4. Sale and Purchase (S&P) & Corporate Activity
### SnP Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2021-06-10 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The Dry cargo markets have most definitely regained the headlines this week with freight rates continuing on their unhindered rally supported by a flurry of snp transactions who have become tribute to the overwhelming positive market consensus that these renewed rates have settled for the foreseeable future. On the Kamsarmax front, JP Morgan invited offers on the 3rd of June on their sisterships “PEAK PEGASUS” & “PEAK LIBERTY” (82k dwt, 2013 / 2015, Tsuneishi Japan). Sellers invited up to 10 outright buyers to bid and both vessels have now been committed enbloc at Usd 23.75m & Usd 26.75m respectively. To put levels in perspective, same owners disposed of the 2013 built sistership “PEAK PROTEUS” in May at a price of Usd 21.5m marking an increase of approximately 10% in less than a month. This positive trend is spreading across all sectors, even more so this week in the Supramax segment where the Japanese controlled “DONAU K” (58k dwt, 2012, Kawasaki) achieved a price of Usd 19.6m marking a substantial jump in values considering that only two weeks ago the same age sister ship “PACIFIC HERO” was sold at Usd 18m. With a buying enquiry showing no signs of slowing down and a number of underbidders left to try and secure alternative units, we expect additional tonnage being placed for sale by sellers looking to capture the ever increasing value offered by these improving markets.  Very little to report on the Tanker front this week with persistent subdued rates holding any potential buyers from going to meet the ever thinning list of potential sale candidates. Most activity being found in the mid 2000 built crude carriers where we continue to witness signs of ongoing activity suggesting further sales in the near future.
