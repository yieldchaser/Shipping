## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2021-08-11 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

A fairly quiet week for LNG shipping after the slight revival of activity last week. Those vessels that did secure business fixed at rates slightly higher than last done, predominantly East of Suez. However, the feeling is that Charterers expectations are unlikely to be met especially as the fixing window approaches September, and current indications are showing increase. The limited tonnage in both basins continues to be a cause for concern for those with requirements, sublets continue to trickle back into portfolios as winter approaches and schedules are finalised. That being said, reduced spot activity across both basins could see pockets of availability compete. Winter and 2022 coverage remains of interest for some players, and sentiment remains strong for this business as expectation for a healthy LNG market holds. No new LNG orders have been recorded this week, but levels are firming up.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Eastern Market
**Date:** 2021-08-09 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

MEG  Another volatile week with swap numbers surging up. At the time of writing, Sep Propane CP was assessed at $679, up by $25 WOW and Butane maintained at $7 cheaper. Sep/Oct CP was traded at flattish level. Qatar Petroleum was reported to award their sale tender for early Sep lifting full propane at discounts to CP, or FEI netback equivalence.   FAR EAST A handful of 2h Sep delivered propane parcels were traded this week. However the cash premium inched down to flattish level to FEI, with buyer biding at discounts whilst seller asking low-mid single digit to FEI.   Off window a plenty of tenders are floating, mainly for evenly split cargos. India IOC tendered to buy one cargo for Sep delivery, while HPCL issued similar tender to cover demand for Oct delivery. Indonesia PERTAMINA issued two term buy tenders, with one for at least one cargo for monthly delivery in 2022, and the other for at least one US lifting from Jan to June. Taiwan CPC also issued tender to buy a 11/11 parcel for 21-30th Sep delivery.   Comparatively, off window inquiries for propane parcels reduced as several PDH have scheduled maintenance during Aug into Sep, partly triggered by the eroding margins. Despite such, one Chinese New Petchem importer, with 900kt/y PDH capacity, issued buy tender to procure two full propane cargo for 2h Sep and 1h Oct delivery into Qingdao respectively.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2021-08-09 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

NWE  The Northwest European market remains tight as minimal arb cargoes have been directed to the region in August and for September. The pro/nap spread has come in to -$4pmt which is reducing petchem demand. Holiday mode in the region is not the whole story as cargoes from the US are uneconomic and the region cannot survive without imports. Leading to the TOT window premiums to cover base seasonal product needs.   MED The region remains in a tight product environment. US arb cargoes not coming ti the region has led to buyers paying up only when necessary and excess demand being talked.  AMERICAS All eyes are on US propane inventories, as the EIA yesterday surprisingly reported a draw of 700,000bbls in overall US propane stocks, notably a million barrel decline along the Gulf Coast (PADD III).  US propane stocks are now 73% of the level achieved last year at the same time (89.3mmbbls) and 80% of those from two years ago (82.6mmbbls).  Export volumes – despite the rising pricing structure – are the culprit, and as of this writing there appears little end in sight.   Mont Belvieu propane market values topped $1.00/gal in late June, having eclipsed the $0.90/gal level at the end of May.  Presently MB propane is pegged at over $1.13/gal for Sep barrels, and expectations are calling for even higher levels to be achieved as crop drying demand should soon commence, and the likelihood of realizing a comfortable inventory position prior to the onset of winter is fading.  Re-sale terminal fees are now between $5.00-5.25cts/gal, within which range the last known transaction occurred.  Meanwhile, Enterprise continues with its buyback scheme, and is offering $4.625/gal for September barrels.
