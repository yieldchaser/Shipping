## 1. Dry Bulk Sector
### Capesize Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2021-04-14 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Big ships doing well. Average daily earnings up 16 pct w-o-w to come in at just over USD 26k. Main driver being continued solid steel and iron ore margins, and major miners are busy covering slots by the dozen to meet industry demand. Lion’s share has been on West Australia, but Brazil again picking up speed with resultant triple contribution to tonne-mile. Coal finding new trades due to political deadlocks, substantial congestion in a number of main ports and ongoing quarantine/crew change restrictions all add to reduced fleet efficiency and upward pressure on rates. Limited period activity, as expectations seem to exceed ffa values supporting well in excess of USD 25k on model type tonnage for balance 2021.

## 2. Tanker Sector (Crude & Products)
### VLCC Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2021-04-12 | **Subtype:** Tank Weekly Comment | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

As April stems close out, the MEG has warmed up for modern tonnage. It may not be the green shoots of a full recovery, but welcomed by owners nonetheless. Despite this, owners will look to keep this bump rolling.

## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2021-04-14 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

We are witnessing a rare scenario, as the start of Q2 2021 has been rather exciting compared to previous years.    LNG Shipping remains positive as fixing levels across both basins continues to increase day-by-day, and Owners with prompt availability in the Atlantic or those able to work 2H May US loadings are likely to see considerable interest on their ships.  The lack of availability in the West, strong gas demand and decreasing inventory levels in Europe are just a handful of reasons why sentiment is holding firm.     While the LNG market continue the steady upward trend, several Owners are still prioritizing utilization, longer period businesses instead of spot charters. This could mean more inclination from Owners to work more competitive rates at present for longer duration such as multi months to short term time charters.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2021-04-12 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

NWE The big story in the region is Propane Naphtha spread going out to -$105 pmt for May. The Mt Belvieu CIF ARA arb has been widening since Wednesday. Although as the arb to Europe widened by about $10pmt so too the cash premiums in the TOT window decreased by $10pmt. The wide pro/nap will keep demand in the region high. The Mt Belvieu butane arb to NWE is above that of propane, which will contribute to maximum LPG demand.  MED The Med has been quiet in the wake of global propane moves and the region remains well supplied.  AMERICAS Crude continues to march upwards, with May WTI improving this week to over $63/bbl and Brent is now approaching $67/bbl.  Conversely, however, the Mont Belvieu propane market remains in retreat as non-TET values at Mont Belvieu have been in decline for the last 10 days, having most recently reached  77.50 cents/gal.   The most recent price reduction comes on the heels of the EIA report which revealed that propane inventories in the U.S. had risen by 1.016mmbbls from the 2nd April to the 9th April to an overall US total of 40.588mmbbls.  One year ago, in the first week of April 2020, stocks were recorded at 56.780mmbbls.   Domestic production of propane rose modestly from 2.299mmbbls/day last week to 2.313mmbbls/day at present, remaining robust as compared to the year-ago rate of 2.061mmbbls/day.   Overwater exports remain healthy despite a half-dozen cancellations, and terminal fees reflect the sentiment, having moved from below 4cts/gal to current asking levels of 6cts/gal.  Improved interest from Europe on the basis of the Propane/Naphtha spread and a widening F/E arb – which is enhanced by reduced waiting times at Panama – should bolster the US-to-Asia trade and assist in maintaining healthy US waterborne trade volume.

Momentum continues to build in the tanker SnP markets with an increasing number of transactions materialising this week. Also increasing activity across most product segments as seen by East Med’s purchase of SOCATRA’s two HMD resales. Both vessels deliver in 2022, and the price is reported at Usd 35.5 mill each – presumably on novation basis. Even for LR1s which normally is a pretty illiquid class we see more deals surfacing.  The dry markets seem to have met some relative stability following the steep appreciation in values witnessed over recent weeks. Most reported transactions are now falling in line with last done, consolidating benchmarks across sectors. Saying that, buying enquiry remains strong with sign of ongoing negotiations however a constant stream of tonnage being placed for sale seems to be keeping a lid on competition and contributing to the abovementioned flattening in prices – famous last words.
