## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2021-09-22 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

It has been yet another week of surging gas prices both in Europe and in Asia, causing a lot of speculations around how long this gas shortage can last and what will be the ultimate ramifications out of it. Naturally, this has been the hot topic this week in Gastech, as it is the first time in a very long time since we have seen a large (physical) industry gathering! Regardless of the striking headlines that the LNG cargo market has been causing lately, it has not reflected into more LNG shipping activity. On the contrary, it has been another lackluster week in the spot market, with only a handful of fresh requirements. We can perhaps partly blame the Gastech or multiple public holidays in the Asia region for this week's quietness, but the fact is that the surprisingly high gas prices in Europe have led trade-flows to be more intra-Atlantic heavy than anticipated causing majority of industry players to have sufficient length in their current LNGC fleet. However, looking past the relative weakness of the prompt shipping market, the view of the winter market still remains tight or at least extremely volatile, with no certainty on how availability might look like during the peak winter. On the newbuilding side, market is still keeping active and shipyards have their hands full with various ongoing discussions.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2021-09-20 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

NWE A quiet market was exacerbated by the draw in US inventories. Traders expecting a strengthening price reaction. This did not materialise immediately as the strength already priced in to the market. What this means for North West Europe is that it will be very difficult to attract imports to the region. The East West spread from the US at +$53pmt for October and widening for later months not tempting traders with the freight diff at $40pmt. Propane Naphtha spread at +$63pmt for October is keeping petchem demand at rock bottom for the time being. With weather in the region above seasonal averages it’s a gamble when weather related demand might shift the market to require imported tons. A butane deal done in the window set the C4 market at 101.75% October Naphtha.  MED The mood in the region is that the market is tight and it will only get tighter. Right now questions regarding available cargoes are being asked though firm price discussions are still lacking. The market is yet to see any winter demand though the general strength is keeping everyone on their toes.  AMERICAS The EIA this week reported that US propane stocks fell by 500,000bbls over the one-week period ending 17th September.  This compares to the 97.9 and 95.4mmbbl totals recorded during the same week last year and in 2019.  Inventories are now 71% and 74% of the levels achieved in each of the two previous years.   As regards terminal fees, there are multiple tiers which depend on the composition of a particular cargo, and whether that cargo is sourced from a re-seller or direct from one of the terminals.  The terminals themselves are offering full propane lots, but at rather steep levels: 7cts/gal in the highest case, but this underscores the ongoing issue of the Ethane/Propane pricing differential (among other factors), as well as the perceived expectation that terminal fees are being more closely managed.  Split ratio parcels are offered at progressively lower rates, some under 6cts/gal – and perhaps with some room for negotiation thereafter.    Resellers, meanwhile, do not have the same E/P pricing considerations (as they relate to the production of LEP from HD5) and can let full propane lots go at around 5cts/gal, depending on timing/location.  Buy side ideas, meanwhile, reside at (or, more likely, just under) the 4cts/gal level, but interest levels have generally been pretty sparse, particularly from end users.
