## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2021-04-07 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

LNG shipping market activity has picked up right where it was before the short Easter break as new spot requirements emerged from both basins boosted by the strengthening LNG cargo prices, which are currently rendering the threat of US cargo cancellations to minimal at least in the near term. Furthermore, there are handful of ongoing term-requirements, which together with the fresh spot requirements, are giving available Owners plenty of options to choose from. Accordingly, spot rates are cautiously climbing upwards as vessel availability shortens, however the cap between spot and term rates still stays relative wide as expectations of stronger market conditions for the second half of the year persists. It definitely feels like the overall shipping market is a bit more volatile this year and this uncertainty might not disappear anytime soon. Notwithstanding the recently launched long-term tenders from a few big industry parties, there were no new LNG carrier orders placed this week.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2021-04-06 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS At least “a few” LPG cargo cancellations are known to have occurred in April, while yet another is very likely to be cancelled prior to the conclusion of this week.  All in, perhaps 5 or even 6 VLGC’s will be removed from the April roster, which should allow up to 3mmbbls to remain in the domestic system.    Propane stocks as of the week ending 02 Apr were reported by the EIA to have increased by 325,000bbls, bringing overall US inventories to 39.572mmbbls – some 20mmbbls below the 59.027mmbbls seen at the same time last year.  The EIA also reported Wednesday that the Days of Supply of propane in the US fell from 28.9 to 26.6 days as of 2nd April, well off from the 51.5 days of supply recorded a year ago.  Meanwhile, res/com demand in the US is relatively robust at 1.46mmbbls/day as of 02 April, up from 1.356mmbbls/day at 26 March.  This compares to the April 2020 demand rate of 1.146mmbbls.   US petrochemical sector consumption of propane, though, remains below par at ~225,000bbls/day, still weak after the storm which occurred over a month ago.   Spot terminal fees have seen a bit of weakness as the arbs have closed in recent days, with talk of a deal between traders having been heard at 4cts/gal for May.  Term business, however, likely resides 2 cents above that.  NWE A Tuesday deal set the physical window premium at April CIF ARA +$13.5pmt for last decade delivery. This softened the demand for prompt propane tons and was seen by most traders in the region as the end of large cargo buying for April. Petchems and end users seen as being well covered through the month. The post Easter, pre Ramadan period which expected by some to be a time of high activity never materialized. The recent drop in Mt Belvieu did not have a marked effect on the arb to Europe as CIF ARA softened in tandem.  MED Some action was seen in the MED with 4KT butane offered in the window. Which put pressure on outright butane values. There remains very little propane demand in the region.
