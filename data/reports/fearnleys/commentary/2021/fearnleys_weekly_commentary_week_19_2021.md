## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2021-05-11 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Firstly, to those celebrating Eid – Eid Mubarak. National holidays have been plentiful recently and these “interruptions” could be seen to affect the rhythm of the market, yet LNG prices continue to rise throughout the curve and point to continued improvement in the underlying market. It’s impact on freight is very noticeable, owners remaining bullish with this bullishness being built on the evidence of persistent multi-month enquiries. As mentioned last week the activity in the multi-month market is likely to reduce due to the lack of supply, unless we see a step up in levels to encourage further sublets into the market as owners. Looking at availability generally, there appears to be a systemic shortage of availability within the independent owners fleets.  Although some vessel available is starting to appear in all basins from portfolio players, the prompt available remains stable with adequate yet not overbearing availability. Looking further out to June and Q3, availability is far improved and adds pressure to rate increases. Despite this the start of Q3 looks stronger than usual for LNG Prices and shipping activity.  In the newbuild market - no new orders have been seen this week yet the newbuild price continues its steady climb.

**Date:** 2021-05-10 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

NWE CIF ARA propane was weaker than crude, pushing pro/nap higher into the $100-$110pmts. Large cargo butane value in the region was down now at 79.5% Naphtha. The region has been well covered for May demand mainly through imports from the US East Coast. Traders in the region are now monitoring the North Sea Maintenance and expect it to come back on time, June 3rd. With bank holidays in much of the region Thursday, many traders have bridged the long weekend and will reassess the market next week.  MED The butane market in the region is seen as under pressure. Due to split ratio US cargoes heading to the region to discharge their butane. The only offer seen in the window was an FOB meeting Moroccan spec which was withdrawn before the close. Both the propane and butane have lacked the strength.  AMERICAS If it’s not one thing it’s another. Asian and NWE CFR prices rose enough to what should have made cargos from the US workable, only to have freight go up further and faster, pushing the Arb back into cancellation economics.   The domestic propane inventory build of 2.5mmbbls allowed the market a sigh of relief as stocks moved up from 41.5mmbbls to 44.0mmbbls as of 7 May 2021.  The East Coast (PADD 1) saw a decline of 300,000bbls (from 3.8 to 3.5mmbbls) but given the volume of overwater exports that have been – and are still – occurring, this is not surprising.  PADD 3, however (the Gulf Coast) saw a build of 2mmbbls, from 24.8 to 26.8mmbbls.  Ostensibly this can be the result of either fantastic production levels, reduced export volumes or some combination thereof.  The EIA, however, reported that production in the first week of May declined to 635,000bbls/day, down from 1.353mmbbls/day – and neither figure seems believable, since the much-discussed cancellations did not occur in the first week of May, but are expected in 2H May.  As to June cancellations, there are believed to have been around a half-dozen, but in at least a couple of cases there were corresponding re-purchases (at lower rates), which thus allows for a modest “net” cargo cancellation total (so far) of 3.  Rumors of an additional 3-4 were noted,  but remain unsubstantiated. On the buy side, only the Brazilians seem to be continued spot buyers.   Meanwhile, terminal fees are believed to have been fixed in the low 3cts/gal range for June. As of this writing price ideas for 2H 2021 are talked above 5cts

**Date:** 2021-05-14 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The holidays are over in most regions, and activity is picking up again. Zodiac have scored on their 4th VLCC from Xihe, with the purchase of M/T JIN HUA SAN (317k dwt, blt 2009 SWS) at USD 36.8 mill. This is $2,5 mill up from the sister M/T TAI SAN (also SWS 2009) that was done back in October last year. Still – these forced sales are not representative for the general market, and with the growing interest we expect prices to continue climbing. As we all know, there is still not much support to get from the physical markets but increasing steel prices have an impact both on the NB prices and demolition values, and as such pulling everything up. These tendences are evidenced with two 2004 built VLCCs sold - M/T STARLIGHT VENTURE (291k, blt 2004 HHI) at USD 30.6 mill and M/T NEW CENTURY (299 dwt, blt 2004 Universal) at USD 31 mill which clearly show the trend. An easy reference here is DHT’s sale of their three 2004 models only a month ago. Despite their scrubbers and Cap 1 rating the price obtained was a fair bit lower at USD 29,6 mill (on average) each. We would guess the buyers of these could score a nice profit by flipping as the scrubbers should give a further premium compared to the most recent deals. The fuel spread seems to continue to widen. Okeanis Eco Tankers’ LR2s – all 115k, built Hyundai 2015 and with scrubbers have been widely discussed in the market. SOCAR was pointed out as the buyer, but this week it surfaced that Torm managed to pull it together at the end. A nice deal for both parties it seems, and the official price is USD 40,25 mill each. With the shipyards leaning back, and the general good interest, we expect prices to keep moving north.  Another week with dry sales of all sizes and new benchmarks set. Notable sales include the 2012 Japanese built Kamsarmaxes “SPRING AEOLIAN” and “TANGERINE ISLAND”, both sold for region USD 21m each. This compares with the 2012 Japanese built Kamsarmax “KINOURA” which was sold for USD 18m end of February, and illustrates clearly the development. With the increased prices and competition on Japanese vessels, we also witness a firming trend on the value of Chinese tonnage. Sister vessels “PEDHOULAS FARMER” and “PEDHOULAS BUILDER” (2012, Zhejiang Ouhua, 81k dwt) have been reported sold for USD 22m each. The scrubbers and BWTSs fitted on both have a clear effect, but this is still a strong substantial jump compared to the previous 2012 Chinese built Kamsarmax sold, “EVERGLORY” end February at USD 16m. We see an increased amount of interest from buyers for around 10 – 15-year-old bulkers, and with the steep increase of the demo prices along with the firm dry market, we expect a continued rise across all sectors and age in dry.

### Chartering Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2021-05-10 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

EAST Fixing out of Middle Eastern ports saw a spike in freight over the past week as vessel availability has been scarce in the first days of June. Eastern premium is consequently now in the high single digits. Some vessels are now also looking set to run late for their laycans which could further tighten the early parts of June. Looking a bit further into the month we see a handful of potential relets and together with the usual owners positions the direction will be decided by actual volumes for the month as a whole.  WEST The market has moved on, very much in the direction as reported last week. It’s a bit of an odd one, as the arb does not support the current spot freight at all, which under “normal” circumstances would dictate cargo cancellations and drops in freight levels. But not this time around. We have only seen a handful of cancellations so far, and although we might see a few more, there are also an increasing number of cargoes coming out from the East Coast. This, in combination with dry-docking and other fundamental inefficiencies, have actually pushed freight up. We cannot say the market is active, and it’s only the ones having to lift that is actually fixing, others seem pretty happy sitting on the fence so far. Freight rates for the transpacific route currently trades in the very high 30s per day, a clear discount to the East still, which as of yesterday where traded in the mid/high 40’s per day.
