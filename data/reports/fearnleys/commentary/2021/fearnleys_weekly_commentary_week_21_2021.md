## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2021-05-26 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Sentiments for the spot market have certainly dropped as the recent spot fixtures were done at lower levels against the recent highs. More vessels are being shown as available to the market, following redeliveries from previous month’s string of fixtures. Players with spare tonnage capacities continue to seek opportunities for sublets in both basins, although these vessels have a limited available period of about one to three months. These sublets constitute of great proportion of the available tonnage list. Looking a little further out to July, and there are signs the current dip may be short-lived. There appear to be more requirements on the horizon especially out the US Gulf, and these may test how tight the market really is, with or without the sublets.    Despite the reduced sentiments in the spot market, the charter rate expectation for term employment, i.e. multi-months over the winter, one year and multi-year charters remain firm and intact, with little indication that rates for such a duration would soften based on the expectations from shipowners with the remaining available vessels for the period.   No orders reported this week, price indication for newbuilding LNG vessels are continuing its gradual upward trend.

AMERICAS Most of the domestic US market players and observers were calling for a small propane inventory build this week; perhaps something under a million barrels, with most generally clustered around the 650-750,000bbl range. In fact, however, the EIA data revealed a decline of nearly 400,000 bbls from 44.4mmbbls last week to 44.1 mmbbls.  PADD I stocks fell from 3.9 to 3.7mmbbls, PADD II showed an improvement from 11.2 to 11.5mmbbls, while the Gulf Coast fell from 26.3mmbbls to 25.7mmbbls – all during a time when record-level exports are believed occurring. Propane stocks in the US are now squarely at minimum tolerances, with the Days-of-Supply tally running at 18.09 days, where the previous 5-year low (which occurred in 2018) was pegged at 19.83 days.    Prices reacted immediately, moving up within minutes from ~85cts/gal at Mont Belvieu to over 87cts/gal before lunchtime.  Netbacks, meanwhile, which were already suffering to the point of cancellations – seven of which are believed to have occurred for June – may be sent over the tipping point, though no additional cancellations were immediately revealed.  Terminal fees, presently offered at 3cts/gal, reflect the difficult arbs.

## 4. Sale and Purchase (S&P) & Corporate Activity
### SnP Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2021-05-28 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

In the Tanker market, most focus is still on the older vessels, but we sense that Chinese purchase interest is cooling off a bit and the last week has seen fewer transactions concluded. Recent changes in the Chinese import policies (quotas) seems to have an effect and there is a significant drop in purchase enquiries with Chinese addresses. If this continues, we could expect an effect on the pricing of the older units, but on the other hand do strong steel prices play an important role also here, with a continued push on the demolition prices. New Shipping are reported to have sold their M/T “NEW DIAMOND” (299k dwt, 2000, Mitsui) to demo/cash buyers GMS for USD 22 m – or $564,50/ton (gross). It’s important to note that M/T ”NEW DIAMOND” and the likes are among the lightest VLCCs built in that period, and whilst these have LDT in the region 38500-39000 tons, the larger (and heavier) Koreans of same age often are in the region of 43000 tons and some pushing towards 48000 tons. To put things in context – we are approaching USD 25 m demolition prices on some VLCCs, and hopefully more will follow Mr. Polemis’ example.  We continue to see Dry bulk asset prices catching up with the chartering market. This week, Enesel Group backed Valhalla, decided to take profit on some Ultramaxes with the sale of 2 x DACKS resales being delivered 2022 for USD 29.2mil each to Genco. This sale is sure to set a new benchmark for resales on both Chinese and Japanese built Ultramaxes. Furthermore on handies, M/V “SIDER SYROS” (38k dwt, 2016, Minaminippon) was sold at firm USD 20.7 m to Orient shipping, which demonstrates the strength of the market and will set another strong benchmark for Japanese built handies.
