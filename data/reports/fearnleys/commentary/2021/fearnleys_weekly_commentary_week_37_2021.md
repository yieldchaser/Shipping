### Suezmax Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2021-09-15 | **Subtype:** Tank Weekly Comment | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The East Suezmax market took a backward step this week when a modern ship fixed MEG/East in the low WS 50's. ## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2021-09-15 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

While cargo prices strengthened and remained strong this week, the high LNG prices have so far not translated to any strengthening of the spot charter rates - hence, the correlation between cargo price movements and charter rates is yet to be seen. However, a vast majority of the available LNG vessels for spot charters are controlled by portfolio players and traders. As sellers’ cargoes are offered with the requirement to procure spot shipping, they are more often than not, lifted by buyers with available vessels for further trading and filling up short positions given the ideal LNG trading market conditions. The market is also not flushed with a long selection of vessels as the availability list is relatively short.  Meanwhile, the term charter rates for 1 year period remain steady. Charterers’ interest in security over vessel availability for a term period continue to be a priority for various players.    Newbuilding prices continue to climb as Mitsui OSK Lines (MOL) confirmed the orders for upto four (4) LNG vessels at Daewoo Shipbuilding & Marine Engineering (DSME) on the back of charters with Novatek Gas & Power Asia. The 174,000m3 LNG vessels, fitted with ME-GA engines for propulsion, along with a shaft generator and an onboard reliquefaction unit, are expected to be delivered in 2024.

MED The main import countries continue to look for product. Tightness in the East Med looks less severe in October than September, with sufficient volume heading for Turkey. West Med demand is present though not urgent, with Morocco looking for small volumes of butane.  AMERICAS Propane supply is now of concern to most parties, since the EIA inventory data revealed a bullishly paltry 700,000bbl build for the week ending 10 Sep, and which brought overall total stock levels to 70.8mmbbls, or 73.6% of last year.  Naturally, the pricing structure at Mont Belvieu is responding and has moved up to over $1.30/gal.  Inventory draws were recorded in both PADD I and PADD III, indicating that export volumes – despite the recent hurricane which landed near Houston and a bit of mid-month terminal downtime – remain robust.  To further illustrate the point, Gulf Coast inventories are 33.2mmbbls, down from 54.9mmbbls last year – a nearly 40% reduction over twelve months.  The ‘bright’ side is limited to the lack of crop drying in North America, but with a La Nina climate forecast on the horizon, winter – and any corresponding demand that comes alongside– looms.  Terminal fees in the US remain tiered, with spot buy-side interest at sub-4cts/gal levels, and spot re-sellers at/below a nickel.  The terminals themselves - depending on the ratio - have a stair-stepped system ranging from the low 5ct range up to more than 6.5cts/gal.
