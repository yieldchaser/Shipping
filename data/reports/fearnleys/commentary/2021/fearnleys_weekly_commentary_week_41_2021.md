## 1. Dry Bulk Sector
### Capesize Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2021-10-12 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Fundamentals generally unchanged whilst spot rates adjust - average daily earnings down 15 pct w-o-w to come in at a still very healthy USD 75k. Chinese, Indian, and partly European energy crisis with consequent desperate need for coal at moment not sufficient to compensate for a sudden drop in prompt iron ore volumes. Only one major miner active on key route West Australia/China, with value for Pacific rounds in turn losing almost 20 pct to stand at USD 65k. Similarly, but slightly less dramatic for the Brazil/China conference trade, where transactions few and far between. Congestion, however, remains significant - and number of ballasters at a historical low, leaving a delicate supply/demand balance where tonnage actually workable keeps being very limited. Operators remain very keen on any available period candidate, last representative fixtures include 176,000 dwt, built 2010, done for about 12 months at USD 33k.

## 2. Tanker Sector (Crude & Products)
### VLCC Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2021-10-13 | **Subtype:** Tank Weekly Comment | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The VLCC market has been threatening to improve for the past month or so, but with a number of false dawns so far. Charterers have found a cheaper vessel, a relet, or a date re-arrangement and the market has continued sideways. At the time of writing, we are seeing quite a build up of Atlantic cargoes. Some may be tender business or rumour, but there is no smoke without fire. The MEG, quieter by comparison, is still unfinished for October and we are closing in on a healthy 120 cargoes for the month. We understand 8 offers in to a Formosa cargo, but modern 'unhandicapped' vessels are likely to steer well clear. With bunkers heading North of USD 600/mt, owners will need to push the market just to maintain current TCE’s. Higher numbers look likely in the near term, but charterers will be using all the tricks in their armoury.

## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2021-10-13 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

We’ve seen yet another week with gains on LNG Spot rates, confirming the trend over the last few weeks. That said, it’s on a small trading volume and in such an illiquid market there is considerable uncertainty and volatility. There are relatively few spot cargoes requiring shipping as compared to previous years, and most of the independent tonnage is sold out. Current availability is controlled by subletters, and those can and does disappear quickly as schedules change so it is a risk going short shipping right now. Furthermore, most of these sublets are restricted on period as they have their own scheduled cargoes further down the line. We are certainly set up for a very interesting winter! This week we’ve added the four Qatar Gas ordered ME-GA’s at Hudong.

AMERICAS Despite ongoing gains in the crude arena which have brought Brent to $84.06/bbl and WTI to $81.31/bbl, MB propane values earlier this week seemed relaxed to around $1.42/gal, only to rise again prior to the release of the EIA to current levels of $145.00/gal which was fixed just before the inventory data release.    Expectations for this week’s inventory report – which was delayed by a US holiday on Monday – again ranged widely (and revealing ongoing uncertainty), from a draw of around a million barrels to a build of over a million barrels.    Given the strong export volumes and pending winter demand season, the prospect of a draw seemed the more likely, and that turned out to be the case.  For the week ending 8 Oct 2021, the EIA reported overall propane stock levels in the U.S. at 71.7mmbbls, which represents a draw of 600,000bbls.  The components to that include a draw of 800,000bbls in PADD I and the build in PADD III of 300,000bbls.  As there is little in the cold weather and ongoing strength in the export market this was eyed with some level of surprise.  Terminal fees, meanwhile, though still generally range-bound, have inched up and offers for 2H November 4:0 cargoes are seen at 6cts/gal – December at the same.  Buy-side interest remains limited.  NWE The VLGC cargoes which were slated for the region have redirected East. Buying interest in the window did compete with the seller’s netback taking the cargo East. The window bid seen this week has pushed up physical propane value to November CIF ARA +$5. Although the European market is tight the news did not affect CIF ARA which fell this week in line with other commodities. Though inland demand has been picking up, cold temperatures are yet to hit the region. The volume of propane set to be exported in October and November from the US East Coast is down on September due to regional demand. These volumes usually find their way to the European market, further tightening the NWE outlook in Q4. Butane has been directly correlated to naphtha at 105% October. ## 4. Sale and Purchase (S&P) & Corporate Activity
### SnP Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2021-10-14 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Following a series of headline announcements this week, the tanker space has for once joyed of most of the limelight and as such we thought we will give tankers the center stage of this weekly digest after being overshadowed by overwhelmingly active dry cargo markets. On Monday, Euronav publicly announced the acquisition of 5.5% of its stock by Fredriksen controlled entities. SEC filings have indeed confirmed that the Cyprus based Famatown Finance, a company beneficially owned by Mr. Fredriksen, has now increased its shareholding stake to 9.8% in the Belgian stock-listed owner Euronav. The move seems to be a welcomed sign for most tanker players as it firmly consolidates the current growing anticipation for a substantial market upturn. As mentioned throughout these previous weeks this growing sentiment was often made apparent by an ever increasing level of buying enquiry with owners struggling to acquire additional tonnage from a continuously thinning supply of sales candidates. In any case we expect this renewed momentum to spur several other parties into action which should see a strengthening of bids across the board in an effort to go meet the rising expectations of any potential seller. Other notable news in the product sector, is the sale of the two modern sisterships ECO LOS ANGELES & ECO CITY OF ANGELS (50K DWT HMD 2020) which were bought by Norden at Usd 36.5m each. The sale was made with the balance of the current charter to Trafigura attached, details of which remain undisclosed at this stage.  Volume of transactions in the dry snp markets remains unhindered with 20 reported sales this week. The larger segments have seen a rise in activity with two modern VLOC being sold to Eastern Pacific shipping, namely the STELLA BELLA & STELLA IVY (250k dwt, Qingdao Beihai, 2016 & 2015) which have respectively achieved Usd 60m and Usd 58m basis a 5 years charter attached to BHP. Another three Capesizes built from early to late 2000s have changed hands providing additional support and benchmark for future transactions in the space.

EAST Since our Shipping Weekly report last Wednesday, the RT/C Baltic benchmark has climbed just a few cents shy of three dollars.
