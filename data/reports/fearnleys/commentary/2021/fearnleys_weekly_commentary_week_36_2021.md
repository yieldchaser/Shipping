### Suezmax Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2021-09-08 | **Subtype:** Tank Weekly Comment | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

There's a perception that Suezmax's always follow "big brothers" lead as soon as V's show any signs of life. Yes, if the maths permit it, we can see interaction, but the core issue for Suezmax right now is the East. Namely, its low enquiry volumes, therefore, ballasters continue to clip out Med and Wafr stems on a daily basis. A case in point being that the majority of vessels passing Rondo now set a course directly for Wafr without even considering ballasting to deviation point Galle. ## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2021-09-08 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

It has been pleasing to see that spot market activity picked up this week with FOB tenders appearing alongside firm requirements. Commodity prices remain strong yet perhaps surprisingly given cargo strength and increased freight activity, freight rates struggle to interrupt the depressed trend that currently overwhelms the market. The lack of prompt cargoes sees a sizable relet fleet open and interested in short localised business. As laycans drift into Q4, late October and November in particular, rates for long-haul voyages do rise significantly and we can expect the delta between BLNG2 and BLNG3 to widen in the coming weeks as winter gets priced in. Moving East, the thirst for vessels has improved yet insufficiently to absorb all open tonnage. Modern tonnage is likely to move West on their own accord in hope for Atlantic business. Those who remain East for spot Pacific requirements still have to compete with sublet vessels with pockets of availability which are dampening freight levels. LNG shipping availability may however tighten as we look ahead with the current future arbitrage opportunities. Yard prices hold firm, yet no new newbuilding transactions have been recorded.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2021-09-06 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

NWE Surging Natural Gas prices have been dominating the headlines. In NWE this has led to refineries using more propane as fuel, reducing local production. CIF ARA propane and butane both reached seven year highs, the strength largely driven by CIF ARA following Mt Belvieu. The price strength has not been enough to keep trans-Atlantic cargoes flowing. With less than 100KT of imports from the US in August, the lowest volume in over a year. Propane is at an $8 premium to naphtha in September increasing to $30 in December. This is set to reduce already low petchem run rates to a minimum this winter. The low demand environment in this Autumn shoulder period is the reason a North Sea loading propane VLGC is set to be exported, a sign the supply floor has been reached. Cold weather will be the remedy to jolt the subdued NWE market into action!  MED The region remains tight in the East Med, with Turkey looking for volume and the lack of imports form the US are causing concern. The West Med has been quiet though traders are worried about buying for winter. With cold weather around the corner how expensive will the product needed for winter become.  AMERICAS The release by the EIA of propane stock levels in the US was delayed due to the American Labor Day holiday on Monday, but was eagerly awaited today as the domestic market continues to precariously hinge on inventory levels.  Prior expectations varied widely, with speculation from one perspective calling for a modest draw and from another a build of up to 2mmbbls.  Ultimately the EIA reported that an overall build of 800,000 barrels, though a draw of 100,000 draw was noted in PADD III, the Gulf Coast.  Stocks are – as of 3 Sep – 72% of the total achieved in 2020.  Ultimately the stats are considered generally bullish. Meanwhile, the pricing structure continues at elevated levels, but with winter-demand looming and propane at 71% of WTI the number of those predicting even higher levels seems to be growing.  This is based on the expectation that stock levels – such as they are – can not sustain both a cold winter and heavy export volumes.  That has led to concern as to where the low point of stocks will be next Spring, and where prices will reside at that point. Terminal fees, all the while, have been sagging and for October are now seen ranging from sub-4ct bids to the mid/upper 4s on offer.  Very little is being accomplished.
