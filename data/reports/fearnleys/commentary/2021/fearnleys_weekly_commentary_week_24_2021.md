## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2021-06-16 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

LNG shipping rates, especially for term, continue to firm up with week-on-week gains across both basins.  While the Pacific is fairly quiet on the spot market, demand for shipping remains high in the West with several requirements to work on.  Owners with vessel availability are still very focused on either 1-2 spot laden legs or term period very close to 1 year + but nothing in between.  Charterers, on the other hand, prefer to secure vessels up till end Q1 – early Q2 next year.   Hence, it remains challenging to bridge that gap between Charterers preference to cover winter and Owners preference to secure longer period.   Furthermore, the current spot cargo prices have also fueled strong demand for ships which in part explains Owners stance at the moment. So the question remains on everyone’s mind: To fix at the current rates and duration or take “the wait-and-see approach”?   There are no new orders reported this week and newbuilding prices remain stable, with a firm undertone.

AMERICAS A weekly EIA propane stock build of 1.544mmbbls was met with trepidation as the previous few weeks have been riddled with corrected data.  And while there remains the expectation that this week’s data contains yet more of the same, it was perceived as directionally accurate. There remain concerns as to the ability of US stocks to build to sufficient levels, but to move from present totals of 55mmbbls to a desired level of 75mmbbls over the space of the next 16 weeks requires a weekly average build of just 1.25mmbbls, which (from a historical perspective) is entirely achievable.   With just one exception, the mid-Jun to mid-Oct average build rate has increased each year over the past 5 years, moving up from 1.3mmbbls in 2016 to an average weekly build of nearly 1.7mmbbls last year.  Therefore, it is well within reason that US stocks will rise to sufficiently comfortable levels.   The pricing structure, meanwhile, reacted to the EIA data in a bullish manner, moving from ~95cts/gal to over 96cts/gal.  By Thursday morning the market had realized additional gains and was traded at 96.5c/gal. Terminal fees have been waffling in the low/mid single-digits, with East Coast transactions occurring just under 4 cpg , and a recent Gulf Coast deal was reported at the “low 4’s” per gallon.
