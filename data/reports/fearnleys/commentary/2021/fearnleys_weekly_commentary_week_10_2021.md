## 2. Tanker Sector (Crude & Products)
### VLCC Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2021-03-10 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Once again, another week of higher bunker prices and ever lower Worldscale rates taking its toll on owners returns. Coupled with the disappointing outcome of last week’s decision by OPEC to essentially maintain the status quo and not inject the expected 1.5m barrels into the global supply chain, certainly compounds the misery. In a market awash with Oil Company relets, whose desire to keep the vessels moving and thus minimize losses, applies pressure on the independent owners to compete. Quiet mutterings of warm layup remain mutterings at present, but with continued current conditions, these may increase in volume. Certainly if you lack a scrubber. But we should not forget that there remains plenty of optimism for the summer and beyond, as the world (and our market) hopefully returns to a form of normality.

## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2021-03-09 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Spring is a time of renewal, and having endured a torrid few weeks we do see some encouraging signs for LNG shipping. After falling from record highs to unsustainable lows, rates appear to have bottomed out and if anything may be starting to firm again. The main drivers are on the demand side, as a flurry of spot and term enquiries hit the market this week with more to come. The cargo market is also encouraging, with forward pricing in both Europe and Asia considerably higher than last year. Back to the shipping market though, and the supply side remains challenging due to prompt availability and a number of sublets joining the fight for spot and term business. It’s too soon to report green shoots, but owners and charterers will be watching carefully in the coming weeks. There were no newbuilding orders placed this week.

AMERICAS The last few weeks have seen US propane inventories decline at surprising rates, but the accuracy of the data has been questioned since the devastating storms, a national US holiday, intermittent fog, regional production outages, massive (but short-lived) domestic demand and petrochemical downtime have each played a part in shaping the market.  This week, though, both market sentiment and the EIA data indicate that things seem generally back on track since the government reported a week-over-week decline of just 47,000bbls – effectively an even draw – and leaving total US propane stocks at 41.199mmbbls.  Some expectations were calling for a draw of 3 (or more) million barrels, while at least one observer was calling for a 1mmbbl build. We like to remind our customers the monthly eia inventories are much more accurate than the weekly’s.  Propane values at Mont Belvieu reacted with mild weakness, not the kind that might be associated with the end of winter drawdowns, and remains in the low 90’s/gallon.  Arbitrage economics did improve, however, and while export volumes have been limited as of late, at this point the terminals are all up and running at prescribed rates.  We are calling the export market for 2 Q Bid 5.50 and offered 6 cpg.  NWE The prompt European propane market has been weak, and demand for refined products in remains low. ## 4. Sale and Purchase (S&P) & Corporate Activity
### SnP Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2021-03-08 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The strong physical dry bulk market has been picked up by capital market participants with rising asset values and continued strong rates translating into higher share prices and premium pricing to NAV, which in turn may tempt other listed companies to follow Belships example of raising capital for growth. We had another week where we saw the values increase across the line. Seanergy acquired Capesize BULK SWITZERLAND (2010, Imabari, 181k dwt) at Usd 25.6 mill, which is up from the sale of COLOGNY (2011, TESS CEBU, 180k dwt) at Usd 22.9 mill a month ago and will set a new benchmark for 10 year olds. Other notable sales include LADY GIOVI (2007, Mitsui, 82k dwt) reported sold for Usd 16.1 mill to Castor Maritime. For comparison the one year younger Oshima built Kamsarmax FORTUNE RAINBOW was sold for Usd 14mill three weeks back. Another interesting sale was EASTERN EDELWEISS (2012, Hantong, 57k dwt), which was liquidated in bank sale in June last year for Usd 8.1 mill, now resold for Usd 11.8 mill.   Although activity in the tanker markets remains limited in terms of completed transactions, we see an increasing number of buying enquiries seeking to take advantage of the current correction in values and position themselves for the upcoming market rebound which seems to have become the general consensus among most market players. Many buyers were in the running for ex Xihe owned VLCC CHANG BAI SAN 2012 SWS built which ended up being sold to Pantheon for Usd 44 mill. In the clean sector, the two sisterships LEOPARD SUN (2013, SPP, 50k dwt) and LEOPARD MOON (2013, SPP, 50k dwt) have now been sold at Usd 21.4 mill each setting a well needed benchmark for units of such vintage considering that the last comparable sale dates back to November. We expect volume of sale to remain limited in the short term with sellers choosing to stand their ground therefore widening the existing bid offer spread.
