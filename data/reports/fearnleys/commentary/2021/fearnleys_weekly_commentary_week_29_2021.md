## 2. Tanker Sector (Crude & Products)
### VLCC Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2021-07-21 | **Subtype:** Tank Weekly Comment | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The week gone by has yielded little of note in the VLCC market, with rates sliding sideways and ships largely trading close to or at negative returns. The only positive news in this summer doldrum is bunker prices going down, courtesy of dropping oil prices. Barring some Chinese “scheduling”, some oil company internal programming and a tad of under the counter fixing the August MEG program has yet to kick off in earnest, but as and when it does tonnage supply is more than sufficient to cover demand. ## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2021-07-21 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Only a handful of spot LNG shipping fixtures concluded and placed on subjects last week and not enough to move the market, at least on the prompt basis. There were a few enquiries this week for possible charters subject to execution of LNG cargo trades, optimisation and tender awards. However, a relatively quiet week and not as intense as compared to the previous month. Despite July being the month of summer holidays traditionally with reduced LNG demand, fundamentally the cargo market is not weak, with firm support on LNG prices.  The list of available vessels is short with only eight open ships across the world with a few more coming open in August. Hence, the market can change in an instant, not just due to enquiries for charter-ins/outs in the upcoming weeks, but the timing of which these possible charter enquiries would be issued could be a strong catalyst. Assuming a few requirements or vessels are released at the same time (i.e. same day or a few days apart) and with similar vessel delivery terms, this could lead to a perception that the market is tighter/softer than it actually is. Charter rate levels may easily move quickly in these scenarios.   LNG vessel newbuilding prices continue the uptrend with commodity prices and currency exchange rates moving unfavourably for the shipyards. The continued high demand for newbuilding ships across all sectors add further pressure to the raising price for LNG vessel newbuilds.    We wish you a blessed Eid Al-Adha Mubarak!

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2021-07-19 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

NWE  The physical market in the region is assessed at a small discount to August CIF ARA. A bid and an offer were seen in the window, with the price difference to wide to bridge. The market is starting to settle back into a normal rhythm after the crude correction. Now that both Brent and WTI are trading above $70pmt propane/naphtha spread in August is at -$36pmt in the region. Keeping LPG demand from petrochemicals healthy. This being said the window offer was form a petchem company. The reasons for the resale is said to be receiving terminal congestion rather than demand reduction. For the time being the market remains tight with little volume set to be imported from the US in August.  MED Tightness prevails in the region. It seems like the buyers have outweighed sellers for the whole year so far. Mixed butane in the West Med is tight and at 96.6% Naphtha putting it at a premium to North West European coasters.  AMERICAS The nearly universal reaction to the news of this week’s EIA report, which showed a weekly build of 3.1mmbbls, was one of surprise.  Most had anticipated a more modest build of 1.6-1.8mmbbls.  But the Gulf Coast showed a build of 2.2mmbbls, putting the total US propane inventory level at 62.7mmbbls as of 16 July.  This compares to the 82.4mmbbls reported last year, and the 75mmbbls seen the year prior to that – and leaves current stock levels at 76% and 79% of the last two years, respectively.  The MB propane market did not immediately react to the EIA data with much enthusiasm in either direction.  Pre-EIA prices were flat at 106.00 cts/gal, and only a modest decline to 105.75cts/gal was noted in the hours after the data was released.  By Thursday morning, though, crude upticks had positively influenced the market and the prices were again above 107.00 cts/gal.  Otherwise, the buyback scheme embarked upon by Enterprise continues.  It is believed that Enterprise has bought back 5 (or maybe 6) cargoes in Aug and remain a buyer for Sep, as well.  Whether or not those same cargoes are then re-sold will then (somewhat) dictate the EIA’s inventory build rate, which will then have an impact on the pricing structure.  Terminal fee ideas from the buy-side are in the 4.50-5.00 cts/gal range, while resale market expectations are in the 5.00-5.50cts/gal range.  The terminal owners themselves, still under pressure from the E/P spread issues, have priced themselves at higher levels.
