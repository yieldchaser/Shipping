## 2. Tanker Sector (Crude & Products)
### VLCC Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2021-07-05 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Clouds of uncertainty prevail as OPEC struggle to finalise any agreement. Oil prices remain high and so do the resultant bunker prices, but sentiment and the market staying the same. Savvy chartering, fixing deals off market has robbed the Owners of the ability to choose where to employ their ships. Rates remain in the low ws30’s AG/China and those ‘handicapped’ ships, be it age or ex dd have had to show numbers in the ws20’s to secure employment. In Wafr, despite signs of a stand from Owners, Charterers were able to secure ws33.5 to China. Therefore, Owners returns can only be positive on a scrubber fitted ship, but even then well below operating costs. Like a mirage in the desert, the improvement in the market seems to keep moving away from Owners. A positive OPEC decision however, and the mirage may become reality.

## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2021-07-07 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Multi-month activity dominates the focus of most discussions which continue to successful conclude despite the lacklustre prompt market.  Yet it should be noted that freight levels remain bullish compared to historical levels so it is not surprising that a temporary correction to the downside is likely as available tonnage looks for utilisation through July and early August. Beyond is less certain, yet most views agree with a contango into September. However, views do differ on how deep winter will eventually be. Regardless of individual views most are in agreement that the lack of independent tonnage continues to be an overwhelming factor and weighs heavily on decision making. The cargo market has been volatile this week and may encourage a reorganisation of portfolios and a flurry of activity as cargo destinations are altered, something that would be welcomed by those with prompt availability. The number of desponent owners with availability on the prompt continues to increase in the east yet west remains sparse, should intra-Atlantic flow increase then this should lead a balancing of the available tonnage globally. In the new build market Owners continue to order to lock in newbuilds before prices escalate further yet it does impact the number of speculative orders available for charter from 2024.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2021-07-05 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

NWE The first propane offer for a month was seen in the TOT window Wednesday, amid continued offers. The physical premium is $6.5pmt to July CIF ARA and the lone offer has not dampened buying interest in this tight market. Butane market in the region remains stroing at 95.5% July Naphtha. Though the arb from the US has improved significantly competition from Asia is ever present and spot fixtures to Europe are yet to be heard. The all time high outright values of LPG in Europe prompted a cargo to be sold from Stenungsund storage this week, an anomaly for the time of year. Another factor would have been the tight margins of producing Ethylene and propylene making re-exporting the cargo preferable.   MED Crude coming off has prompted an opportunistic rise in butane demand. The market in the region is bullish though not as strong as North West Europe.  AMERICAS Last week’s EIA report spawned a propane pricing surge which continued unabated through the midpoint of this week when profit-taking is reported to have allowed market values to relax.  Where a high point of $1.15/gal was reached, values just prior to this week’s EIA data release are pegged at 1.06/gal.  As might be anticipated, many have taken cover on the sidelines, but a few transactions were noted. However, the pricing differential between ethane and propane seems to be as much of a factor in the limited activity levels as anything else.  The terminals themselves can offer spot product and are clustered around asking levels of about 6ct as the cost of extracting the ethane from the propane (in order to convert HD5 propane to HD2 (or LEP) has become prohibitive. Meanwhile Enterprise, continues with its ‘buyback’ program and remains a buyer of any-date, any-ratio cargo from its terminal.  Thus far a generous handful of parcels are believed to have been sold back to Enterprise in August, and talk is now heard as to September buybacks.  Current economics favor the Eastbound trade, though in each case margins are at or below 4cts/gal, thus bringing notional terminal fees to around the 3.00 – 4.00 ct/gal range, though in one case a buyer reportedly panicked when the market spiked earlier this week and paid a significant premium.  The EIA data release today – which was delayed due to U.S. Independence Day holiday – revealed a surprisingly low 500,000 barrel build, which was immediately met with jeers and mockery, as well as the early prediction that next week will see a significantly larger build.
