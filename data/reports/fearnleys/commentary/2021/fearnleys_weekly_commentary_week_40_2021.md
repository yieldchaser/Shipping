## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2021-10-06 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The LNG spot shipping market has exceeded expectations this week. The lack of workable vessels available and a couple of uncovered requirements, predominately within the East of Suez have firmed up “Owners” sentiments going into winter.  The strong product prices driven by the lack of supply and competition for LNG cargoes between both basins have also given more room and margins for freight.   History have shown how illiquid and volatile the LNG shipping market is and although most Charterers have taken measures earlier this year to cover shipping, an open West-East Arbitrage, continuous rally of spot LNG prices and potential Panama canal delays can trigger more appetite for spot tonnage. With the current cargo prices, being long shipping may be a preferred position.   We recorded another 4+2 new building orders this week and the total tally for the year is now at 52 large LNG vessels.

**Date:** 2021-10-04 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS  Volatility is the keyword in the Mont Belvieu LPG market this week.  Intra-day price swings of up to a nickel have been recorded, leaving many on the sidelines.  Crude and natural gas strength have led the way, pausing only for profit taking. Propane stock levels in the U.S. continue to be the primary focus, though there remains a steady eye on both export volumes and ‘buyback’ numbers at EPD, of which there have been a solid 7 (if not 8) for Oct. Weather-related demand in North America will no doubt compete with export volumes over the Q4 ’21 and Q1 ’22. Spot terminal re-sale values are in the sub-6cts/gal range for full propane lots in 2H Nov.  Buy-side interest is hovering at above a nickel but uncertainty is so far keeping the two sides at odds.  NWE Two VLGCs set to arrive in the region have pushed the physical cargoes to a $4.5pmt discount to paper. The propane naphtha spread for September averaged +$45pmt. This lead petrochemical users taking in 325,000 MT, to the lowest monthly intake for 15 months. The October Pro/Nap spread stands at $119pmt today, and some petrochemical users are still taking tons. Due to it being more economical to run a minimal rates than fully shutting down. The draw in Mt Belvieu inventories will keep the arb to Europe closed and with the East/West spread at $51pmt for October and rising further down the curve we do not expect to see many more tons imported from the US in the near future. Butane in the region continues to be assessed at 105% October Naphtha, though this translated to a $15.5pmt drop in value as naphtha crashed.  MED The regions is nervous about winter supply. With production issues in Algeria reducing propane output and the arb to the US closed, traders are wondering where they will find supply this winter. For the time being the market is on the tight side and traders are being cautious not to get caught in short supply if demand picks up.
