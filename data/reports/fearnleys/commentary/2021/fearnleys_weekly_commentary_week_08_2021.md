## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2021-02-24 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

It has been a flat week for the LNG Shipping markets. Sentiment continues to dip further with limited activity and soft demand for shipping. There were requirements to be worked, however the impact on rates remain minimal. The continuous increase in prompt availability across both basins, potential cargo cancellations from US projects, constant decline in product prices as we move closer to Q2 and a closed West – East arbitrage window did contribute to the lack of shipping opportunities. Multi month and short term inquiries seem to be put on hold as Charterers take the “wait and see” approach to further evaluate their shipping portfolio. Nonetheless, we do expect some interest in the coming weeks or months as Charterers look to take advantage of the current market conditions. Utilization is imperative and this could mean more inclination from Owners to work competitive rates now for longer duration to brace the shoulder months. On Newbuilding activities, Maran Gas seem to have added to their order tally at Samsung.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2021-02-22 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS Last week’s winter storm halted hydrocarbons production in the Permian Basin of West Texas.  Simultaneously, feedstock deliveries to the chemical sector were stopped as that system shut down, as at the same time millions of barrels of Raw Make production was lost.  All that while the Houston Ship Channel was closed. The weather has now abated and the various systems are being returned to service much more quickly than anticipated.  Most are indicating that the lost time will be caught up with by the end of the month (if not sooner).     The EIA on Wednesday reported that propane stocks in the US drew by a surprisingly high 5.2mmbbls as of the week ending 19 Feb.  The market had assumed a draw of between 3.0 and 5.0mmbbls.  The prospect of fog remains a possibility, and with that comes the potential impact on LPG vessel transits which could limit export volumes and, therefore, inventory draws.  Meanwhile, WTI strength should foster production levels and allow propane stocks to begin recovery in Q2. Terminal fees are on the Gulf Coast are bid at 3.5cts/gal, with offers at/above 4cts/gal.  Meanwhile, though, an East Coast March fixture is believed to have occurred at 5.25cts/gal.  NWE Strength in the front of the market remains although CIF ARA has not tracked the rapid rise in Mt Belvieu. The cold weather in the US affected East Coast exports leading to a lower than anticipated volume of LPG finding it’s way to NWE from 1H March Marcus Hook. This in turn has pushed up the CIF ARA window premiums which are assessed at CIF ARA March +$11.5pmt for a 1H March TOT stem. March Propane/Naphta spread in the region is now -$44pmt the widest spread so far in 2021.  MED  Has been quiet of late and with the US delays having a knock on effect and causing some supply uncertainty.

### SnP Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2021-02-24 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Crude spot market remains sad and depressing, but another modern VLCC was sold this week when Hunter announced the exit of its 1,5 year old HUNTER ATLA (delivered Sept 2019). A price of USD 84.5 mill paid by ADNOC is considered firm by some market players, but in our books it is a fair level – especially in light of the recent pricing from leading shipyards. Generally speaking, there are not many sellers of modern tonnage in this space today. Still, we see an impressive number of takers for older crude tankers, and prices keep up fairly well.  Given the scrap-levels on offer, the poor earnings in the (conventional) market and the increasing bunker prices we had expected more older ships sold for demolition. There is a strong demand in the product market and both MR and LR2 tonnage in decent condition and age do get a lot of attention.  The dry markets continue to display strength. Supramax rates are up excess 40% the last week, breaking USD 20k/day on Wednesday for the first time in a decade. Even crazier, the historically less volatile handies are also on fire where the Baltic has recorded over a month of daily consecutive gains and are now at excess USD 19k/day. There are a lot of bidders on available ships for sale and we expect values to quickly follow suit. Also this week we see a lot of activity on region 10yrs old Kamsars, and again the values are increasing slightly from last week. Less transactions for the modern Eco units, but that is not due to a lack of buyers. There are many owners scavenging the market for Eco units in fear of future IMO requirements, but there is simply very few units for sale. The only one this week was the BULK HERO (61k dwt, Universal, 2016) sold at at USD 20.6mill, which will represents a new benchmark on the 5yrs old Ultramax.
