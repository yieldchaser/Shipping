## 2. Tanker Sector (Crude & Products)
### VLCC Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2021-12-08 | **Subtype:** Tank Weekly Comment | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

As we drag ourselves to the end of 2021, Q4 has been a major anti-climax. ### Suezmax Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2021-12-08 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Just when it looked as if Q4 was turning into a total wipe out for owners, the Atlantic has granted them some relief. There are pockets of resistance in regions where laycans are being worked on a very prompt basis, which has caught some charterers by surprise. The US Gulf has been the main driver with tight lists and firmer rates enticing ships to ballast from the Western Atlantic. This has shorn the West African and Black Sea lists of a few ships and hindered the ability of early Wafr cargoes to repeat last done, resulting in firmer rates. The long East list is still of concern and with conversely lower enquiry levels, there is the probability that large numbers of modern ships, looking for employment, will set a course West. Td20 trades WS 67.5-70 firm whilst Td23 is WS 30 and flat.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2021-12-06 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS Contrary to expectations, the EIA propane inventory for the week ending 3 Dec revealed a 600,000bbl increase to an overall total of 73.3mmbbls, or nearly 84% of last year at the same time.  As such, it remains to be seen as to whether a corrected set of data might be released in the coming weeks. The market this week had been predicting a draw of ~1.25mmbbls, the difference thus leading to even closer scrutiny of the data.  Overall export volumes are off the high’s seen earlier in the summer, and the buyback scheme at Enterprise remains underway so the 900,000bbl build in PADD III (the Gulf Coast) can generally be rationalized once higher production levels are additionally considered. Meanwhile, the pricing structure appears – for the moment at least – to have settled at around the $1.00/gal level, having fallen rather steadily from the $1.52/gal seen during the first week of Oct.  Terminal fees have softened, as well, and now range from a low just under 5cts/gal to a high of just over 7cts/gal for spot business. As it remains generally warm across North America, the market seems increasingly dependent upon two factors:  Weather (or the lack thereof) and petrochemical consumption rates – both in the US and abroad.  NWE The TOT window continues to be assessed at a small discount to December, with late December and early January looking well supplied. Through the recent global price swings the arb to Europe has remained robust. The only weakness in the market was caused by disrupted operations at Terneuzen, which continues to undergo maintenance to try to fix the issue. With December pro/nap now -$63pmt petchem demand will remain high and we expect the Terneuzen cracker to come back online as quickly as possible. Weather related demand has been rising and cold temperatures across much of the region are keep railcar demand high, as well as retail sector demand. The arb to Europe has been competitive with the East market for a month now, though FEI strengthening is causing traders wait and see if CIF ARA strengthens in line and continues to draw US imports.   MED The market looks balanced and winter demand has not exceeded tons already programmed to arrive in the region. Colder than average seasonal temperatures are yet to spread further south, and prompt demand lacking.
