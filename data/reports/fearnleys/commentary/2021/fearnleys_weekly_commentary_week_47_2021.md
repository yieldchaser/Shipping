## 2. Tanker Sector (Crude & Products)
### VLCC Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2021-11-23 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The VLCC market continues at a sluggish pace, with rates yet again under pressure. First decade of December MEG stems seems all but a wrap, leaving behind a number of ships seeking employment. The Atlantic has yielded little to help out, with only a few US export cargoes to show for it and Wafr cargoes all but absent. Daily earnings are yet again below OPEX even for the best of them. The US have said it intends release millions of barrels of oil from strategic reserves in coordination with China, India, South Korea, Japan, and Britain, to try to cool crude prices after OPEC+ repeatedly ignored calls for more crude, which doesn’t bode well of an increase in ocean going freight short term. However, longer term same reserves have to be replenished, showing a glimmer of light at the end of the tunnel.

### Suezmax Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2021-11-23 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The outlook for the Suezmax market remains unchanged with all load zones trading flat/soft. With VLCCs fighting to hold onto every point, the chances of a mid-winter Suezmax revival appear dim. Western charterers have become spoiled when it comes to the sheer choice of vessel for their long-haul cargoes. ## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2021-11-24 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The LNG market continued to build on last weeks gains, predominantly driven by the East with robust spot LNG prices delivered into Asia and the availability of shipping to fit enquiry still lacking. The emergence of new requirement seen last week saw high level discussions firm into sub-fixtures quickly as rates showed no sign of retreat. Expected tender news for demand in Asia will give more direction to the commodity market moving forward, but with availability of vessels currently light this is unlikely to have any effect on shipping rates in the spot market. The surfacing of more sublet availability probing for enquiry into January hasn’t put the dampeners on the market further out either, and as the fixing window stretches well into 2022, rates are maintained. The regional imbalance continues as rates in the Pacific remain at levels not yet achieved in the Atlantic. Though with so few ships in the basin, and the possibility for more volume to have to remain within the Atlantic, we could see this flip/reverse at any time. The potential for a prolonged Winter, Owners continue to ride the wave, with no signs of a change on the horizon.    Despite no newbuild orders being placed this week, prices remain firm and yards remain busy with plenty of enquiry and interest being recorded.

## 4. Sale and Purchase (S&P) & Corporate Activity
### SnP Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2021-11-23 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The positive sentiment witnessed in the tanker markets over the past couple of weeks is starting to translate into transactions with a number of buyers picking up tonnage. This dynamic is led by the product sector which has seen eight vessels changing hands across an array of vintages. This pick up in activity doesn’t however mean that buyers go meet higher price expectations but rather sellers taking advantage of an increased buying pool to dispose of vessels that have failed to attract any interest throughout these subdued markets. This is particularly apparent for vessels that are 10 years and older which are seeing a notable pick up in buying enquiry. We therefore expect values to continue to move sideways with a number of similar vessels being placed for sale.  Dry cargo continues to see a slowdown in activity with bids coming at levels below last done In hope to capture the value offered by the recent correction. Corrected values that also put pressure on transaction already in places with signs of a number of deals failing to materialize.

### Chartering Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2021-11-22 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

EAST Since our last weekly update, we have seen several spot deals concluded in the Middle East throughout December. It is evident that freight ideas have increased on the back of this activity, and last done deals range from mid-high 60s Baltic (depending on the business). With increased uncertainty on exact waiting to transit through the Panama Canal, we have started to see more owners sending ships back West via Cape or Suez. With new rules for Panama transits coming into effect shortly we expect this trend to continue, and this ultimately means that more ships will be positioned for Eastern business potentially creating more competition.  WEST Fixing focus is slowly but surely shifting to first decade January as the number of December cargoes in need for shipping diminishes. Owners’ freight target has been relatively persistent at, or around, the USD 120 per ton mark, however, a handful of relets seems content with somewhat lower rates than that. We expect activity to resume relatively soon and that the market tightness will persist well into first quarter next year. Although the product market is indeed a large decision maker for the market direction and activity level, the inefficiencies in discharge ports and Panama will contribute to an exciting start to the next year.
