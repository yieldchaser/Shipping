## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2021-06-01 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

It has been another slightly quieter week from LNG shipping perspective, which is somewhat surprising considering that the LNG cargo market continues to thrive as LNG prices done for deals into Europe and into the Far East elevate higher. Most of the ongoing shipping activity is now focused on July deliveries and we have witnessed a modest uptick on rates in the Atlantic basin, since some of the sublets have disappeared from the availability list. However, it has been a different story in the Pacific, as we continue to see further softening on rates, as prompt availability continues to build up and there hasn’t been enough requirements to significantly reduce this. Few of the independent Owners have noticed this as well, and have therefore decided to start ballasting their prompt tonnage towards the more promising Atlantic market. Evidently the spot market continues to stay volatile with its rather unpredictable movements from one week to another, but the big picture seems to be holding strong which is clearly illustrated by the term rates remaining firm. On the newbuilding front, it was a noticeably busier week, since amidst the strengthening yard prices, it was reported that SHI have secured an order for two modern 174,000cbm LNG carriers for an Oceanic shipowner and additionally it was also announced that DSME has signed a contract to build one 174,000cbm LNG carrier for Hyundai LNG Shipping which will go to Repsol under a long-term charter.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2021-06-01 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS  The 4.1mmbbl build in propane stocks for the week ending 28 May was instantly met with both elation and contempt, and resulted in an almost immediate decline in Mont Belvieu propane values (to 89.75cts/gal for Jun), but also speculation as to whether next week’s data release would include corrected data. On the former point, and after some weeks of disappointing build levels, propane stocks in the U.S. were in rather desperate need of showing signs of improving.    In any case, at 48.2mmbbls as of the 28 May, domestic US propane stocks still pale in comparison to levels of a year ago when 67.3mmbbls were reported, a 19.1mmbbl deficit YOY.  The generally accepted comfort level for pre-winter propane stocks is in the 65-70 mmbls range, which will require an average weekly build over the next 21 weeks of sbout 1 million barrels per week which we believe is very achievable.  Meanwhile, terminal fees have been seen and reported in the 3.5-4.0 cpg range for June, with asking levels in July tentatively within the same range for the Gulf Coast and a penny cheaper for the USEC.

### Chartering Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2021-06-01 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

EAST Rates have continued to drop in the East as we are yet to see any real activity in June, while still being left with a healthy amount of open tonnage. One trader was reported fixing a ship close to usd 50 Baltic for mid-June load, but it remains to be seen if this level will be repeated for current fixing window (last decade of June). Meanwhile we are starting to see more Indian enquiry in the market, and we also expect there to be uncovered cargos left from other players before we can close the month off. But it is still likely that some of the ships open for June will have to take waiting time and opt for July cargos after we start seeing more acceptances come out for next month.  WEST The market has continued its somewhat slow pace from last week, as the charterers have more-or-less concluded their June lifting program, and both owners and charterers are getting ready for the next fixing window. We have already seen some vague inquiries for July, but so far nothing done. A market call cargo for first decade July loading, has attracted the attention of some owners, and are currently ongoing. There are only less than a handful of ships avail for June loading, and still some uncertainties on available ships for early July, so owners’ ideas have kept rather stable. As such, and with a market somewhat in the doldrums where the arb is allowing for far lower freight rates than the owners are willing to consider, we have as usual under such circumstances seen some proposals and requests for Baltic related deals. Last done at time of writing, is an end June lifting with freight calculated at an average July Baltic average. We have had another week with a discount for the transpacific route compared to the BLPG1 route, whereas the transatlantic route, although not as liquid, is trading at a healthy premium.
