### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2021-06-28 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

NWE  The market remains tight with strong petrochemical cracking demand dominating buying. July trading has been picking up though not many cargoes are being traded in the window as they are being snapped up before making it to the market. Butane is at 92.5% of July Naphtha, very strong for the time of year particularly as naphtha and crude are strong. Mt Belvieu strength is keeping the trans Atlantic arb firmly closed for the time being. Many traders expect this to change due to the continued strong petchem demand.   MED Sonatrach posted it’s July prices at $595pmt & $605pmt for C3 & C4 respectively, surprisingly strong, but then so was CP. This was a bit of a shock in the region, though it did not dampen trade discussions as the market remains tight. With crude rising today and butane trading at 90% of Naphtha the high SH postings will be taken in the market’s stride.  AMERICAS Non-TET market values for propane have risen from 92cts/gal at the beginning of June to 114 cts/gal as of this writing – a 24% increase.  But the last 5 trading days have seen the sharpest gains, having moved up by over a dime during that time. This is because of inventory-related fears in the U.S. market, which were compounded by this week’s EIA report that revealed a one-week build of just 1.239mmbbls and brought the total overall US propane stock level to 57.479mmbbls as of 25 June.  This compares to the Year Ago level of 74.599mmbbls. The ongoing concern – both from domestic and international market participants - is that propane inventories will be insufficient to simultaneously accommodate domestic demand (including potential crop-drying requirements), petrochemical feedstock demand, and export demand.  Indeed, the Days of Supply were recorded at 66.5 on 25 June, where at the same time last year there were 80 Days of Supply in stocks.  Meanwhile, cargo cancellations for August are being talked with additional vigor, and as of today we have seen at least 4. However please keep in mind we are not quite sure how to calculate the Enterprise buybacks and will they be traded on or simply put back into the inventory.  Export cargo terminal fees, meanwhile, have crept up slowly and quietly, having now loosely congregated at around the 4.50 - 5.0cts/gal range for the US Gulf.

We expect new benchmark in the Supramax sector with the “BULK COSTA RICA” (2010, NACKS, 58k dwt) inviting offers today.
