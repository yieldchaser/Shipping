## 2. Tanker Sector (Crude & Products)
### VLCC Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2021-01-18 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

2021 has kicked off with a whisper, with reduced Middle East Gulf volumes and a tonnage build up. Rates have gone from weakness to weakness, and for the first time in years we have seen fixtures concluded yielding negative returns – in other words owners paying for the privilege – and risk – to carry crude oil from A to B. From a slim January program the tonnage overhang into next month is greater that seen for a long time, not helped by ships coming off storage and quicker turnarounds. The Atlantic has been a saving grace, in a manner of speaking, although rates for the longer haul voyages have also given way with correspondingly lower earnings, barely showing positive returns. Alas, with February being a short month, and Chinese New Year celebrations to bout, we do not foresee a pickup any time soon.

## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2021-01-19 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

This week has been met with slight trepidation as portfolio players and traders alike take a moment to reassess the trading patterns of their portfolios. Record-breaking LNG cargo prices have retreated from their peak yet vessel availability remains tight through February, and those with availability are holding firm against offering a discount. When reviewing March and beyond the picture changes as seasonal effects take hold, but levels remain elevated year-on-year. Panama continues to cause headaches to scheduling and at the time of writing the number of vessel trading from the Atlantic to the Pacific via Suez or the Cape instead of via Panama is markedly up. The situation in the Pacific is slightly different with the onset of increased availability starting to materialize at the back end of the availability list, but as the fixing window is far prompter the full effect of this will not be seen for some weeks! Newbuild market remains subdued with no reported newbuilds this week.

On the tanker market we have seen notable activity with three modern VLCC’s sold this week setting some much needed benchmarks for units of similar vintage. Following its purchase in November of TRF HORTEN built 2018 at HHIC-Phil at Usd 72m, Delta Tankers of Greece has now acquired the one year older sistership HUDSON for Usd 71.5m. Newbuilding prices have certainly passed their bottom spurred by a combination of increased steel prices, strengthening local currencies vs USD and a strong influx of large container vessel projects.  Dry SnP markets continue to joy of high volumes of transactions with vessels changing hands across all sectors. Pricing-wise it seems that most market players are still looking to digest the recent appreciation in values that we witnessed last week with buyers looking to figure out whether this is indeed the start of a sustained upward trend in values rather than a one off glitch in what has been until now a relatively flat market.
