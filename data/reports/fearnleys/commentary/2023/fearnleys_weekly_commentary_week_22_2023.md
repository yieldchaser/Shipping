## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2023-05-31 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

LNG as a fuel is back in vogue this week. Those shippers fortunate enough to control DF cargo ships are inundating their bunker suppliers with fuel stem requests. As one established LNG bunker provider put it, “Christmas has come early this year”. It’s a stark reminder for those of us on conventional chartering (who have been bemoaning the collapsed Contango) that every cloud has a silver lining. Like the owners of LNG fuelled container ships, price sensitive buyers have switched back to sea gas in recent weeks, helping to repair some of the demand destruction our chosen commodity has suffered over the last few years. Even though this exacerbated buyers’ market is unlikely to survive the next winter, this current period of competitively priced restocking in both basins will go some way to restoring LNGs role in the fuel mix. So, whilst this may not be a great time for those who are long product and / or shipping, it is a fantastic market for those buyers who can purchase at current spot market levels. Given where those prices were in August last year, it seems only fair that the pendulum has now swung so far in this direction.

**Date:** 2023-06-01 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS Having commenced the month of May at ~70cts/gal, Mont Belvieu non-TET propane values drifted most of the month to ~62cts/gal when - until the last few days - a sudden, sharp decline occurred.  Current market values are in the 55cts/gal range, a 22% decline over the 30 day period.  This is due to a combination of weighty inventories and depressed crude prices – which themselves fell from $75.76/bbl at the front of May to $68.24/bbl as of this writing, or a 10% decline.   Crude itself is suffering from lofty inventories, lengthy on-the-water stocks and larger-than-expected production levels – particularly from Russia.  Adding to the mix is limited demand, which in no small part can be attributed to the lackluster economic development in China.   With regards to propane stock levels in the US, the EIA had been expected to rise from 66.70mmbbls (as of 19 May) by ~2.2mmbbls for a total of 68.90mmbbls (as of the 26th of May), but – in fact – a 4.00mmbbl increase was recorded, bringing total US stocks to 70.70mmbbls, equal to the 5-year high.     Meanwhile, as to terminal fees, market levels have not wavered much over Q2 and bid/ask levels primarily remain in the 5cpg range.  There is some improvement going in to Q3, but shipping rates – which are astonishingly high – are the limiting factor.  NWE Petchem demand has slowed due to reduced downstream demand.
