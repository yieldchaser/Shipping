## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2023-01-11 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

It seems premature to declare that winter is over, so we definitely won’t do that. It is hard to ignore the traffic jam of tonnage that’s worked its way onto the availability list this past week, though. The net result is a sizeable correction on spot rates and given that sentiment drives all markets, there has been a softening in the term numbers too. Available Newbuilds remain stubbornly impervious to the warm European winter, however, with one Owner reportedly securing a brace of fixtures at record levels (on Subs) to an Atlantic project for mid-2024 delivery. Returning to the here and now, there’s a spate of tenders and bilateral discussions to keep the prompt market turning but with an increasing number of relets competing for each cargo, this is looking more like a Charterer’s market. At least for now. A cold snap (like the one forecast for late January in NE Asia) or a post “zero covid” demand surge in China or indeed any hint of European reinjections failing to keep pace with the level required, will change the mood again. In fact, this is likely the new normal that we contend with this year (and beyond?) - increased volatility borne out of a hypersensitive market that has re-learned the hard way that LNG is first and foremost about security of supply. This rings true for the ships as much as it does for the molecules. If you don’t have a vessel, you can’t get the product to where it needs to go and whilst the availability list currently provides charterers with some degree of comfort, in a relet dominated market it doesn’t take much of a nudge for portfolios to reabsorb this length.   No Newbuild orders to report this week but prices continue their ascent as demand remains strong.
