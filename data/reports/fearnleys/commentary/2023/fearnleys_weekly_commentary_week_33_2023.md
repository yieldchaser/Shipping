### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2023-08-17 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS  WTI values have seen a $2/bbl decline since the start of August, and propane values have also weakened over the same period – from 68cts/gal on the 1st to below 62cts/gal at present.  The ratio of WTI to propane values, though, has weakened from just over 35% to 32%, perhaps signaling that MB propane values have room to again advance in the near-term.   Terminal fees, meanwhile, have made remarkable gains this month and moved from somewhere in the 6cts/gal level to 8cts/gal, then to 10cts/gal, followed by a 12.5cts/gal deal and – finally – a 15+ cts/gal transaction that was recorded earlier this week.  Asking levels have correspondingly increased, but buy-side interest at this point remain in the lower end of the double-digit range.   As for inventory levels, the EIA reported this week that stock levels as of 11 Aug had built by 700k bbls to a total of 90.6mmbbls.  The week-over-week increase was well below the 2.2mmbbl 5-year average build for the 32nd week, but inventories are now 133% of the same time last year and 135% of two years ago. Many are now rightly anticipating that propane inventories in the US will handily eclipse the 100mmbbl level by the beginning of October.    As the excessive heat of the North American summer continues and ocean water temperatures continue to post at record levels the threat of tropical storms and/or hurricanes has now intensified.  NOAA forecasters have just recently increased their Atlantic hurricane season predictions to ‘above normal’.  NWE  The region saw a significant rise in imports month on month, from 425,000MT in July to 582,000MT expected in August. Surprising as the netback to Asia has been considerably stronger during this period. The August Propane / Naphtha spread rose $10/MT to $126/MT, and remains the preferred feedstock. Though with many petchem plants on maintenance, shutdown or running reduced rates due to weak olefins markets feedstock demand fluctuations do not represent a huge volume. The TOT window is assessed at a double digit discount to September CIF ARA, public bids and offers have been scarce. Butane continues to be strong now at 87% September Naphtha as refinery demand beings to increase to meet adequate vapor pressures before blending season.  MED  The East Med has been buoyed by the wide arb from the US. Morocco importing 115,000 MT in the first half of August already, 60% of US origin. In the West Med Turkey has been importing a large volumes of LPG, and continues to look for prompt tons. As the summer driving season is keeping autogas demand high.
