### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2023-04-27 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS Domestic US propane demand has naturally tapered as the winter requirements have subsided, but export volumes are now regularly at record-level, whether overland (rail, truck and pipe) or – overwhelmingly - by oceangoing vessel.  In spite of that, however, propane inventory levels rose sharply over the week ending 21 April, having been recorded by the EIA at 58.9mmbbls, up from 55.5mmbbls the week prior.  A build of that size might lead to the expectation of a lower pricing structure, but non-TET propane at Mont Belvieu has so far hardly budged from its 77cpg perch.   With regards terminal fees, there was earlier in the month a flurry of activity but things have slowed considerably as freight costs have risen and – rather correspondingly – terminal fees have softened to near-cancellation levels.  Presently offers are seen in the 5cpg range, but bidders are increasingly scarce and priced at least a penny less.     Latin American demand should begin to see some improvements relatively soon as Southern Hemisphere winter demand is poised to increase in the coming weeks and months.  NWE  The region continues to receive a large volume of US imports arriving in the end April timeframe with some cargoes slipping into early May timeframe. This volume is additional to term deliveries and is keeping the region well supplied, with cargoes waiting to discharge into petchem importers. So even with the pro/nap at $153/MT for May we do not see any additional prompt demand. The is hope the larger than expected US stock build will improve the arb to Europe, and whilst the East vs West spread is at $77/MT in May the West will have to close that gap before attracting further spot tons. A large butane volume is set to be exported from Marcus Hook in May. Butane value in Europe is 76% April Naphtha, that said, large cargo lots have not been actively traded and few importers are looking for additional volume.   MED The region continues to see offers of trans-Atlantic tons. Which has kept open discussions quiet.
