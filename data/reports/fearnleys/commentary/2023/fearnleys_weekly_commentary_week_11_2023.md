## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2023-03-15 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The spot LNG shipping market has remained stagnant, with limited activity reported. While there are a few pending requirements, most of them are within the East of Suez and are more than 6 weeks out. Presently, there is a surplus of prompt vessel availability compared to the requirements in the market, with vessels opening in the Atlantic still commanding lower spot rates than those in the Pacific.  A potential window of opportunity for the East – West Arbitrage could arise as the spread between TTF and JKM spot prices shows signs of reversal, with JKM now commanding a marginal premium to TTF. In the term market, charterers remain focused on securing tonnage for winter coverage, and there is continued interest in long-term charters. On newbuildings, Maran Gas has reportedly ordered two 174,000 cbm LNG carriers at the DSME yard, with delivery expected around mid-2027.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2023-03-15 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS  The historically improbable propane inventory build of 900,000bbls recorded yesterday by the EIA puts overall stocks at 61mmbbls as of 10 March, surprising the majority of market participants.  Current stocks are 183% of last year, and 148% of two years ago and are now effectively equivalent to the highest second-week-of-March stock level since 2015.  Since then there have been only two occasions when U.S. propane inventories have built – the other being around half of yesterday’s increase.   Propane stocks for the period from 2015-2023, when plotted, reveal a high, a low and a 5-year average, and current stock levels are now on par with the highest part of the range.  With no support from falling crude oil prices – and the fact that winter-related demand has by now subsided – Mont Belvieu propane values have sharply declined from the 90cpg levels seen in early March to today’s 68cpg, which continues to decline.   As for terminal fees, a rather wide range was established this week for April cargoes, with a 6.50cpg deal being transacted, but also an 8.25cpg deal.  Latin American demand was covered via both spot purchase and tenders this week, one of which was recorded as a typical spot purchase, the others via tender, which were awarded both for a one-off parcel as well as a term contract.  NWE Credit Suisse needing a $54 billion dollar loan from the Swiss Central Bank shook the LPG market. As many LPG trader’s LCs are issued by the bank. With the prompt market well supplied traders in the region are happy to sit back and watch CIF ARA track crude oil values. Leaving the majority of US cargoes, even US East Coast VLGC exports, heading East. Further down the curve the arb looks healthy and workable, and many are questioning what the LPG and Gas markets in Europe will look like later this year? As the conflict in the Ukraine looks no closer to resolution. Butane in the region had not been bid or offered for some time, and an offer in the window correct butane values from 95% March Naphtha down to 88%.  MED The region is well supplied for the last vestiges of winter, though if butane weakness in NorthWest Europe continues it is likely those tons will find a home in the West Med. The East Med has been active as Turkey continues to see higher LPG demand as it recovers from the devastating earthquake coupled with cold temperatures.
