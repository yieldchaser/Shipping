## 2. Tanker Sector (Crude & Products)
### VLCC Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2023-02-07 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

We are entering another familiar cycle in the VL market, a pattern seen a number of times in 2022. The Atlantic (mainly USG) activity picks ups, rates jump, and the rest of the market is dragged along with it. The price for a VLCC USG/Ningbo has climbed USD 100k on successive fixtures, currently sitting at USD 8.4m. With West Africa/East also positive at the WS 60 level, modern vessels will ballast West purely for the richer hunting grounds not found in a lagging MEG market. As always however, the risk of dilution remains.  East of Suez (TD3C) rates creeping up in the low WS 50’s, and owners will be hoping for a pop in rates to match Western returns, but the February MEG program is close to completion and it's a short month also. However, the position list looks more balanced than for some time, as many ships turn left at Rondo.

## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2023-02-06 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Last week the spot market lacked activity as the market returned from Chinese New Year, thankfully, this week has kicked into action with multiple vessels being chartered in both basins and more requirements continue to flow in. The East in particular has seen activity pick up and March is starting to stack up with laycans offering owners a selection of requirements to work on.  Freeport remains a question mark and tonnage remains on standby. In the meantime, Egas tenders continue to be the focus of those mostly affected by the reduced Freeport production as they seek to source replacement volumes and the portfolio disruption adds to chartering activity in the Atlantic.   Multimonth demand continues to attract attention as further sublets seek interest through summer with some success, whilst those offering winter coverage find new homes through competitive bidding processes as Charterers who had been holding off as sentiment softened become keen bidders. As highlighted last week, the focus remains on vessels that can be worked through winter, and the yearly rates have started to rise accordingly.   For another week, there hasn’t been reports of any newbuilding orders but prices remain firm.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2023-02-08 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS Netbacks remain robust - particularly from Asia - and have well supported strong activity levels.  There is rising concern, however, that the combination of winter’s end and the virtual armada of LPG vessels currently headed East that the hurried pace seen so far this year could begin to falter.    The most recent transactions have been seen as low as 9cpg and as high as 14cpg, with current bid/ask levels ranging approximately from 11-14cpg, depending on timing, location, ratio, etc.  The bulk of March trades have likely been concluded, though a couple of interested parties remain.  The EIA this week revealed a 4.3mmbbl draw in propane stocks, down from 73.20mmbbls last week to an overall total on 3 Feb of 68.90mmbbls.  Current stocks are 144% of last year, and 134% of two years ago, giving cause for talk of historical high inventory levels.  In the first week of Feb 2020 stocks were 4% higher than now at 71.82mmbbls, and likewise, early Feb 2016 totals were also 4% higher, which somewhat alleviates what are likely premature concerns as to brine containment later in the season.  Those issues have arisen in light of strong domestic US production levels which are expected to continue for the foreseeable.  NWE Weakness dominated CIF ARA last week, but that all changed this week after larger than expected US inventory draw saw Mt Belvieu and in turn CIF ARA spring back to life. The bid/offer spread in the TOT window was very wide, the bid equating to a flat price of $602pmt and an offer for the same dates at $800pmt. Unlikely to come together, even using Fearnleys! Trans-Atlantic cargo flows continue though the majority are term deliveries as any US spot cargoes in February and March cargoes have been snapped up to head East. The recent fall of European natural gas prices, as stocks remain high and winter cold but not extreme, will lead to an increase in LPG production from the North Sea. LPG production was reduced by around 35% when LPG was being spiked into natural gas. Butane remains assessed at 97.5% February Naphtha, with no window activity.  MED The arb to the region from the US is attractive, though no prompt cargoes are available to make the most of this opportunity. Dortyol down in the East Med reduces demand by a 2+2 VLGC every 10 days, though is not expected to remain down for long.

## 4. Sale and Purchase (S&P) & Corporate Activity
### SnP Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2023-02-07 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The ageing tanker selloff continued this week with MARAN AQUARIUS (320K DWT 2005 DSME) and SEAHERO (306K DWT 2006 DSME) each fetching over USD 50M. It is easy to see why, when ships of this size and age have almost doubled in value over the last eighteen months. Clear premiums for Ice class units are still very visible in the market with the sale of scrubber-fitted Ice 1A LORELEI (73K DWT 2007 STX JINHAE) for USD 33.5M, compared to both HAFNIA COLUMBIA/KRONBORG (76K/74K DWT 2007 DALIAN/NEW CENTURY) each selling for USD 22M.    Meanwhile in the dry market, timing appears to be key as market players look to position themselves as values seem to be bottoming out. China’s post CNY recovery has been slower than usual, in part to people enjoying their first in person CNY since the pandemic began, but also due to decreased liquidity. This will likely be remedied by additional stimulus in March, which could lead buyers into action. In the highly competitive Japanese Ultramax space, scrubber-fitted NORD POTOMAC (63K DWT I-S SHIPYARD 2016) sold for USD 27.75M setting well needed benchmark in the segment with the most buying enquiries. Over in the Supramax sector, MILOS (56K DWT 2010 COSCO Zhoushan) sold in the high 13s, and Navios unloaded the SERENITAS N (56K DWT 2011 YANGZHOU GUOYU) for USD 12.5m.
