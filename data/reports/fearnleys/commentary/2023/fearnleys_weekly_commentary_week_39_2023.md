## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2023-09-27 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

It has been a rather surprising week on LNG pricing since we have seen the paper market forfeit most of the contango opportunities until December, which is likely driven by high gas inventories and an early forecast of milder than average temperatures for the start of the winter in Europe. This has created some uncertainty with regards to what might happen to the LNG shipping rates, since LNG prices are still high enough to avoid any cargo cancellations keeping supply intact, but on the other hand, we might see fewer floating opportunities and more available tonnage, unless of course we start seeing congestion at various disports. Throw into the mix the constantly fluctuating arbitrage and we a have proper conundrum on our hands! On the prompt however, we are seeing a bit of softening on the spot rates in both basins, and with only limited prompt opportunities to be worked, some vessels might see some idling ahead. We are seeing some charter opportunities for November and even for December, but these laycans are still a bit far out, and proposed levels are staying relatively strong amidst this uncertainty. On the newbuilding market, we have not recorded any new orders in the past week, but yards are keeping busy finalising the paintjobs in order to deliver a legion of shiny newbuilds for Q4 2023 and Q1 2024.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2023-09-28 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS  The EIA this week posted an inventory gain of 700,000bbls when the market was anticipating something on the order of +2mmbbls (the 5-year average weekly build is 650,000bbls).  However, exports this month have been impacted by a recent electrical outage which caused compressor failure at one terminal, and mandatory government inspections at another, leaving many with the impression that overall export volumes should have theoretically declined and thus allowing domestic stock levels to grow at a greater rate.  But the EIA data shows that export volumes over the 38th week were as high as 2+mmbbls/day, which is well contrary to what had been anticipated. The market still generally expects that the high point will likely be around 105mmbbls in the coming weeks when the traditional ‘build’ season concludes and winter-related demand commences.   The price at Mont Belvieu should likely have been negatively impacted by the lofty inventory levels, but crude has taken control and as it is now in the $92-93/bbl range so propane prices at MB have seen recent price gains to the ~74cts/gal level.   Meanwhile, based on a rapidly declining arb, terminal fees are in retreat from the high point of 15+ cts/gal (for Nov) and cargoes – few that they are – are being offered at just above a dime (10cts/gal) but buyers have the upper single-digits in mind – if a vessel is available.
