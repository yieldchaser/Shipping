## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2023-04-12 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

After a week in which much of the world enjoyed a long festive break, LNG players were “welcomed” back with muted market activity and some lacklustre macroeconomic news. The LNG sector itself remains flat, with delivered prices in both basins showing little sign of recovery through the rest of the quarter; nor is there even the hint of an arbitrage opening up in the near-term. The impact on prompt shipping has been predictable, and in fact the renewed competition among disponent owners East of Suez reduced the regional disparity in rates. It’s not all doom and gloom though: this week saw several term fixtures concluded at strong levels, implying a degree of confidence in the second half of the year and beyond. However, away from LNG the IMF has lowered their projections for global growth in 2023, especially for advanced economies, and this may impact demand for LNG. If we’re again looking for a silver lining, then slower growth across the globe may lead to a lowering of interest rates and thus an improvement in the financing terms for shipowners and project developers. Good timing, as we await another round of FIDs.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2023-04-13 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS Mont Belvieu propane values have risen over the past couple of weeks from a low point of 70cts/gal to current market values which have well eclipsed 83cts/gal.  This is due to the combination of inventory drawdowns at MB and the recent surprise announcement that OPEC will cut production by up to one million bbls/day.  That news led to a $5/bbl increase in the WTI price which, in turn, gave lift to the MB pricing structure.  That buoyancy remains intact as propane closed most recently at 83.625cpg.  Going forward, domestic US liquids production is expected to outstrip demand (which – as evidenced by the half-million barrel inventory build yesterday - is clearly beyond the winter boundary by now), and inventory levels have now begun to build.  The strength and rate of stock growth, though, is contingent upon exports remaining true to the forecast that they will continue to be robust.  Terminal fees dropped from early March when values in the low teens were being seen, and bottomed out in the mid single-digit range before beginning to recover.  Current terminal fees for May are focused around the 6cts/gal level for a full propane cargo, while 2H 2023 values are pegged at/above 7cts/gal.  NWE April CIF ARA propane has continued to strengthen throughout the week with a late April TOT bid going unanswered. This prompt demand surprised the market as an 8 month high of 560,000MT will be imported in April. This strength has been largely due to maximum petrochemical demand with will continue as long at the propane naphtha spread remains wide, -$188/MT in April. Natural Gas prices remain low so we expect to see an increase in the region’s production which has been ramping up since March. Butane has been weak since the end of blending season with netbacks values for US C4 in around Mt Belvieu flat.
