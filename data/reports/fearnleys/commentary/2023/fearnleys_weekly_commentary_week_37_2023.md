## 3. Gas & LNG Markets

**Date:** 2023-09-13 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Following the resounding success of the Gastech conference in Singapore, LNG shipping is riding a wave of renewed optimism. Industry players have returned to their offices around the world with fresh insights and a collaborative spirit, and are now directing their attention towards chartering in preparation for the impending winter season and supporting ADP processes for next year. We are at the time of year when floating cargoes may start to emerge, and there is a contango through to December and with storage levels high there is also operational storage on the table. Risks to the downside include geopolitical factors (or industrial strikes) that could disrupt supply chains and influence pricing, although as we saw last year in particular such factors can reduce volume but higher LNG prices can produce a frenzy to secure tonnage. As the LNG shipping landscape evolves, stakeholders are closely monitoring these dynamics to make informed decisions and seize opportunities in this dynamic sector.  This week saw no newbuilding orders, with the persistently limited availability of shipyard slots and the ongoing strength in newbuilding prices.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2023-09-13 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS In a rather classic case of the market becoming “overdone”, spot terminal fees for 1H October rocketed upwards in late August and early September, reaching heights not seen for some years as a couple of transactions were noted above 15cpg.  Asking levels went even higher but the headwinds that accumulated alongside such levels became too great – particularly in the face of rising freight costs and delays associated with the lack of rain at the Panama Canal.  As such terminal fees for 2H have declined into what is now deemed to be the low ‘teens’ range.  The timing of shipping (and availability), though, remains a concern.  Meanwhile, 1H Nov is presently offered at 11cpg and bid in the 10s – still rather high from a historical perspective, but generally supported by the Eastern arb.   All the while the EIA reported on Wed that propane stock levels as of 08 Sep increased by 3.1mmbbls to 99.1mmbbls – well above the 5-year high point of 97.380mmbbls, 127% of last year, and 140% of two years ago.  There exists at this point no reason to believe that overall US propane inventory levels won’t reach 105mmbbls by the end of the month, and well positioning the market for what is increasingly expected to be a colder-than-average winter.   Otherwise, the persistent high heat in the US South and Midwest regions may have finally begun to abate, but nonetheless the production and transportation systems across the nation continue to struggle, perhaps most notably a recent power outage which caused several days lifting delays at one of the terminals.  Current estimates are calling for a full roster within the month of September, though.
