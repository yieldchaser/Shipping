## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2023-05-03 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The LNG shipping market has seen a quiet week resulting in low demand for spot charters. However, there is interest in securing tonnage for longer periods, as more Charterers are seeking to prepare for the upcoming winter season. While spot rates have remained unchanged in all basins, multi-month and multi-year period deals have been observed, with more expected to follow. This trend is likely to reduce term availability and increase sentiment for term charters. It is always a question of where winter spot rates will be, and this will eventually be factored into longer-term rates as Charterers look to compromise on longer periods at the right price to cover this winter. At present, many Independent Owners are reluctant to fix for short periods and are instead enticing Charterers with better rates for longer-term charters. However, for now, some are struggling to find a bridge between the spread, but it is expected that either parties will bite soon. Despite the current subdued state of the spot LNG shipping market, the increased interest in longer-term charters suggests a positive outlook in the coming months. On the newbuilding front, prices remain firm with reports this week that an Owner have secured two 200,000m3 LNG carriers at around US$277M a vessel from Hyundai Heavy Industries for delivery in late Q4 2027.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2023-05-04 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS Three significant events have occurred over the course the past week:  First, crude oil values declined by $4/bbl, mostly on economic fears based on the recent large bank closures (and the ongoing fear of additional problems).  Second, propane prices at Mont Belvieu softened by up to 5cts/gal as a direct response to the crude oil weakness.  Third, terminal fees have weakened as buy-side interest has retreated.  Current June offers are seen at 5cpg but buyers remain agnostic, even when shown that there is plenty of room for negotiation.   Meanwhile in rather a surprising fashion, the EIA reported that propane stocks as of the week ending 28 April actually fell by 600,000bbls to 58.30mmbbls from 58.90mmbbls.  This leaves current stock levels at 143% of those recorded last year and 140% of end April 2021.  This week’s data release is widely viewed as a correction from the week prior when a 3.4mmbbl stock increase was published.   If – in fact – that is the case then presumably the traditional ‘build’ season should resume with next week’s report.  In any case, at this point stock levels of propane in the US are well above the 5-year average and remain closer to the 5-year high’s, which in some cases has become worrisome in the sense that brine containment issues later in the season are now becoming a consideration.  NWE Large import volumes continue, around 600KT in April and 350KT set to arrive in 1H May. Despite the drop in crude LPG values the propane naphtha spread remains at a healthy -$144/MT in May. The spread continues to keep petchem demand at high levels which is driving this spring market. Additionally production in the region is increasing, though re-injection into the natural gas stream continues. The butane market has been quiet over the last few weeks and continues to be valued at 76% May naphtha which has lost significant value this week in line with crude.   MED  The butane market has come off relative to naphtha with decreasing seasonal demand.
