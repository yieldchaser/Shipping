## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2023-04-20 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Despite national holidays being enjoyed around the globe, activity in the LNG cargo market and subsequently the shipping market has remained buoyant, with a combination of anticipated shorts and FOB tenders leading to a number of fixtures being registered this week. Activity in the Atlantic saw a marked improvement with a flurry of fixtures for 1H May being concluded and prompt vessels continue to see interest with ships being worked firm as we issue this weekly publication. Although the shoulder months still have some weeks to run, rates appear to have found a floor. Should activity continue in the same vein, we can expect rates to stabilise and tick upwards in the comings weeks, yet it is perhaps important to reiterate that there is a big discrepancy in the level of activity and interest between the desirable 174,000m3 tonnage and anything smaller. It is not to say that smaller tonnage is irrelevant, but its marketability needs careful consideration in the Atlantic whilst in the Pacific, owners are offered more relief due to a greater variance in parcel sizes.   The newbuilding price sees another small increase, yet no new buildings were reported this week.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2023-04-21 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS Having reached over 83cts/gal at mid-month, propane values at Mont Belvieu have receded to 80.50cpg as of this writing.  Recent crude declines from the mid-$82/bbl levels to the low $77/bbl levels now have not offered support, and – curiously - neither has the most recent EIA data which revealed a 700,000bbl reduction in stock levels.   As of 14 April US propane stock levels were recorded by the EIA at 55.50mmbbls, down from 56.20mmbbls after record-level export volumes were made known.  On that note, exports from the US were reported at 2.511mmbbls/day this week, up from 2.395mmbbls/day last week.  For reference, at the same time last year exports were listed at 2.428mmbbls/day.  Propane inventories are now 150% of the levels reported in mid-April last year, and 137% of two years ago at the same time.   Meanwhile, terminal fees on the Gulf Coast have relaxed and for a full propane cargo talk now is centered in the mid-single digits mark.  Mixed-ratio opportunities are available below that, but little is being accomplished.  Latin American demand is presently satisfied, with no known requirements as contractual volumes are considered sufficient and Southern Hemisphere winter-related demand has not yet begun to surface.  NWE  An early May TOT offer in the window at a small discount reflects the CIF ARA April/May contango. Adding weight to the discount is the import program for April, with 250,000MT concentrated in the last decade. Propane naphtha spread has come in slightly but at $154/MT petrochemical buying will continue to drive demand through the summer. With US exports at record levels importers in Europe are not concerned about supply this summer. With the arb open further down the curve, some looking to lock in tons at attractive levels and hedge. Butane remains long in the region, with values directly tracking April naphtha downward  at 78.5%.  MED  The butane lull is keeping activity low as US tons continue to be offered into the region. North west Europe is experiencing C4 length though strong frieght rates are keeping the product in the region. French refinery strikes continue to hamper operations at Lavera.
