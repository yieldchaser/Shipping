## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2025-02-12 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The Fearnley LNG team has gathered in Oslo for our annual strategy session, just as the market is serving up a smorgasbord of topics to address. For the bread and butter, a fresh surge in requirements has emerged, particularly in the West and the Middle East, and will keep the attention of a lot of owners and subletters. We have seen a slight uptick in spot charter rates for more prompt business, and although we may expect some downward pressure to return later in the month these fresh enquiries could support rates. Appetite to charter in modern tonnage in the Middle East also remains strong, leading to slight increases whilst activity in the Pacific Basin is more muted at present. The excess of modern tonnage has not led to many term charters on fixed rates yet, but there is now at least one ship marketed for multi-months in an otherwise illiquid market.  There are also growing rumours of vessels being considered for lay-up, and less formally there are now a sizeable number of ships idling (including older vessels nudged aside by charterers taking in more modern spot tonnage) - it now appears to be a question of how many ships will be laid up and for how long. For those further along their life cycle, there is fresh uncertainty on scrappage given news of a vessel being rejected by buyers. And going full-circle, news that Hanwha Ocean had committed two 2027 slots to their own shipping arm may point to a slight softening on prices in the near term although this could quickly reverse if certain requirements for large numbers of vessels firm up.  Finally, for those joining the seminar hosted by Fearnley LNG and Wartsila in London next Tuesday, we look forward to seeing you for an engaging afternoon!

US Volatility is the key word at the moment, though maybe “uncertainty” comes in as a close second.  Crude is bouncing around, the weather is having a say, and while the EIA data this week was not entirely surprising, MB propane continues to ride at higher-than-expected levels.  Fog has invaded the US Gulf Coast and, in the morning hours at least, been a factor for over a week while – simultaneously – the Northeast US has been cold and is presently downright bitter.  This has kept MB propane values elevated and waffling at (or just under) 90cpg since the start of Feb.    Meanwhile, the EIA this week released its findings which were felt to be in line with expectations.  Current overall stock levels of 58.90mmbbls is over 103% of last year (though around 89% of two years ago).  Production levels in the US this week were recorded at 2.609mmbbls/day, or just about average on a YTD basis.  However, calendar 2024 averaged 2.669mmbbls/day, so 2025 – mostly because of freeze-offs in the Permian – is off to a slower start.    Terminal fees, all the while, were felt to be rising and asking-levels rose to the low double-digits (though not quite the ‘teens’) but the last known propane transactions are thought to have occurred in the low 10cts/gal range.  Buyers at this point, faced with a bounty of choices for March FOBs have their ideas set in the upper single digits.
