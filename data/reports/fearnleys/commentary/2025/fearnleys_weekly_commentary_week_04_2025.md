## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2025-01-22 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The start of President Trump’s second term in the White House has dominated news this week, even eclipsing the promising developments out the Middle East. The reversal of a freeze on new U.S. LNG export applications is the key talking point for LNG shipping, although this has been widely expected and only likely to influence the market on a long term view. Elsewhere, the welcome agreement reached between Israel and Hamas for a temporary ceasefire and the resulting potential resumption of LNG traffic through the Red Sea could affect LNG tradeflows in the near term. However, weakening supply/demand shipping fundamentals have overshadowed the “closure” of the key chokepoint since transits came to a halt in 2024, hence a change of fortunes due to its reopening seems unlikely.   The current negative Arbitrage has exacerbated oversupply on the prompt driving rates in the Atlantic down further this week. The few fixtures recorded cleaned away most of the US Gulf requirements, and with few opportunities on the horizon those with tonnage scrambled to fix at levels considerably lower than previous weeks. The Pacific market also saw a concentration of activity around mid-February dates, although the market could easily drift lower again as a growing number of independent owners and portfolios show length through the first quarter.  Term discussions continue to be largely muted with the exception of index linked structures, which have proven a catalyst in an otherwise stagnant space. It has remained difficult to bridge the bid/ask spread for fixed rates, and many participants are increasingly comfortable working to indexed rates.
