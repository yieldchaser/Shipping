**Date:** 2025-04-02 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

As many market participants return from Eid holiday celebrations, the LNG shipping market is experiencing a shift in rate sentiment in the Atlantic, whilst limited new activity in the Pacific and Middle East has kept rates relatively stable in these regions. Following a comparatively tighter second half of April and a period of rising rates in the west, the focus has now shifted to May laycans, where softening rate sentiment is emerging. Several recent fixtures have been concluded at lower levels than those reported last week, which can be partly attributed to a clearer picture of vessel availability as see ships gradually redeliver from existing charters, and partly due to a slowdown in the number of firm spot requirements. With an uncertain arbitrage and tonne-mile demand currently impacted by more to intra-basin trade, this trend may persist. We have seen how quickly the market can turn though, and considering the current geo-political environment caution is the name of the game for both owners and charterers. On the multimonth side, despite ongoing interest from Charterers looking to secure coverage across the summer and winter periods, the bid-offer gap remains, thereby stalling fixture activity for these periods. Meanwhile, the newbuilding market remains quiet, with no reported new orders this week.
