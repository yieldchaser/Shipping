## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2025-03-20 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The LNG shipping market continues on a more positive footing this week amidst healthy levels of activity worldwide. Several short positions in the Pacific have led to a fresh flurry of fixtures East of Suez with rates firming to close the gap on the gains saw in the Atlantic last week. The crush on mid-April tonnage in the U.S. has been eased somewhat with several FOB sales, suggesting there are ships being held back that can relieve the pressure on freight, albeit pushing the squeeze onto the cargo side. There aren’t many signs of the upward pressure easing in the coming weeks, although the number of FOB sales does suggest a soft ceiling to rate levels for now, at least.  Multimonth availabilities with flex through the remainder of the year are beginning to look a little thinner, with several period enquiries being worked for up to 12 months with signs of tightening. Markets have also been made for longer term periods through to 2030, although the difficulty for term charterers covering forward positions lies in the sharp increase in rates and sentiment for the final three years of the decade. For mid-term periods, during what appear to be the weaker years fundamentally, the bid/offer spread for most parties remains wide so most mid-term interest reaches out to 2026 or 2027 to capture the lower rates seen during those prompt periods. There has also been plenty of activity in the FSRU market with Egypt, Iraq and Jordan looking for near-term (2025) start-up. Limited supply is keeping rate elevated and a niche-relet market suggests we may see deals at firm levels over the coming weeks.  Finally, projects news has also been overwhelmingly positive this week with news of further loan support for Mozambique LNG as well as the reinstatement of FERC approvals for Rio Grande LNG and Texas LNG.
