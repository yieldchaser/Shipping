## 3. Gas & LNG Markets

**Date:** 2025-05-21 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

As many market participants gather for the World Gas Conference in Beijing this week, one may be forgiven for expecting market activity to come to a halt as in previous years, but the LNG shipping spot market is a different beast in 2025. We have perhaps seen a downturn in fresh requirements building over the past week in the Atlantic; however, fixing activity remains quite brisk for June cargoes, although increased competition among owner and sub-letters has erased some of the gains seen over the past couple of months. East of Suez, several prompt positions have found employment though there remains an overhang of modern tonnage likely to reposition to the Atlantic, and a further decline in rates has been observed for June dates. Overall though, this past week has felt more like a drift downwards rather than a serious fall.   Rates for multimonth periods covering the coming Winter remain higher than prompt spot levels, and with interest for such periods building there is some consensus of late-Summer or Winter uptick. Longer tenures through 2026 are largely flat though, and the following Winter is still proving tricky to price for owners and charterers.    Whilst unconfirmed, there is growing noise around further large-scale Newbuilding orders for deliveries before the end of the decade. There have been very few new orders this year and prices have eased, but Shipyards sit comfortably with long forward orderbooks and this latest news may alleviate downward pressure on prices.

**Date:** 2025-05-22 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

US The global LPG market is softening on all fronts and is led by the declines being seen in the US domestic market where propane, having rallied earlier in the month, seems to be succumbing under the weight of the latest EIA report. For the week ending 16 May total US primary stock levels rose by 2.7mmbbls to an overall total of 53.10mmbbls, or about 83% of last year’s total in mid May.  As anticipated, the EIA data showed production levels at 2.833mmbbls/day (against a high of 2.852mmbbls/day and a 5yr average of 2.440mmbbls/day).  Likewise, however, the export number revealed a decline which on its own was not surprising.  Total exports were listed at 1.690mmbbls/day, which is still above the 5yr average, but well off the 2.335mmbbl/day overall high-water mark.  This is thought to be the result of a host of parties switching export cargo ratios from full propane to 50:50 lots.  That, in turn, is because of the US-led tariffs. Meanwhile, terminal fees on the Gulf Coast have been hovering just over cancellation economics as the arb from Asia remains depressed and netbacks – in consideration of robust, healthy freight levels – have been held down.
