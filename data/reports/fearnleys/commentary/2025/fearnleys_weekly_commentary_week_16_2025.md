## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2025-04-16 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

While US tariffs were eating up all the headlines a week ago, the LNG freight market kept calm and quietly carried on. We’ve seen a little bit of a switch-around this week though, with a back-down in the full implementation of tariffs allowing some calm to return to the wider economy while here in LNG shipping, there are fresh challenges emerging. In the near-term, fewer requirements were already resulting in increased competition among owners and sub-letters and leading to softening rates. On top of this, reports of production issues in Asia will not be welcomed by those that are long shipping. The most modern tonnage does still remain well above the levels seen earlier in the year, but rather than the Atlantic market pushing up to match the “Pacific premium” we’ve seen in recent weeks it is the Pacific market that is coming down to meet Atlantic levels. There are also major developments announced or being considered by national and international bodies that will need careful review, such as the additional USTR port fees and the outcome of the MEPC 83 meeting, both of which would have a considerable impact on shipping. The latter in particular has provided some clarity on the anticipated implementation of GHG fuel intensity regulations, but it is a little too early to really understand the implications.  There have been no newbuildings announced this week.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2025-04-17 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

US Uncertainty rules and predictable mayhem has ensued as a result of the ongoing tariff-related actions by the US government.  The markets are uncertain as to how to proceed as there is no known clear path at this point.  FOB USGC buyers almost instantaneously opted for as much butane as possible (per their respective contracts) and date-swaps have been common.  Meanwhile, though – and despite it all – MB propane values reacted by rising and thus squeezing arbs, thereby placing pressure on netbacks, which have declined and resulted in the lowest terminal fee levels since late 2023.  As if that were insufficient, the EIA data this week was a surprise to most participants, many of whom were calling for a .500-1.00mmbbl inventory gain but were met with a 1.3mmbbl reduction to an overall US stock position of 44.40mmbbls, or about 80% of the same time last year.  This is believed to be the result of higher exports (1.966mmbbls/day, up from 1.739mmbbls/day) and mildly lower production of 2.744mmbbls/day (versus 2.752mmbbls/day the week prior).  There is some speculation, however, that the increased butane liftings (at the expense of propane) will next week result in lower propane exports, and thus a build – though at this point it is difficult to gauge the level that stocks might build.  Whether or not the tariffs that will (or won’t) be imposed will (or won’t) include NGLs or LPGs remains a very hot topic that is being discussed and bantered by all.  The only sure thing at this point is that nothing is certain.
