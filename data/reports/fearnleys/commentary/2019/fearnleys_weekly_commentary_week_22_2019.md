## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2019-05-29 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

We are a few days away from June, which will mark the beginning of the Northern Hemisphere summer months. Although we are only reaching the opening of the summer ‘’peak’’ demand period, it is already proving to be a quite different setup for this summer in the LNG market compared to same time last year, predominantly driven by the sluggish cargo demand and ample supply, pushing the LNG prices close to annual lows. This seems to have caught up with the prompt spot LNG shipping market too as we have recorded only a handful of fresh requirements across the basins, halting the hike of the freight rates for now. Shipping availability has also increased, as we have seen a number of sublets appearing, possibly putting some pressure on the spot freight rates in the upcoming weeks. It is quite a different story on term requirements however, as owners are still holding on to their strong views about the second half of this year, supported by the fact that we see a steady flow of interest for multi-month charters from different LNG players and expect more LNG volume to come online. On the newbuilding front, Hyundai Heavy Industries confirmed late last week to have secured an order for two 180kcbm LNGC newbuilds, increasing the order tally for 2019 to a total of 22.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Eastern Market
**Date:** 2019-05-29 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

MEG: The highlight of the week was the announcement of Saudi Contract Prices for June of $430 for Propane and $415 for Butane. At the time of announcement, this was more or less in line with expectations. However, as the week have moved on, it become obvious that there are/were substantial unsold FOB volumes still to be placed. These volumes are from producers and from traders as well Some of these have now been cleared but at large discounts to CP, ranging from high single digit to high teen’s. This price dive has been aided by the continued strength in the VLGC freights. Arriving in the office Monday morning, we were presented with a near contango situation for the balance of the year but this quickly changed whereby backwardation has once again reared it’s ugly head, especially so for Butane. It is reported that Iran LPG export volumes this month will reach 550 kt, or more.   FAR EAST: It has not really been an active trading week, tough some deals were concluded. A WAF origin cargo was sold into East China for 1st half July arrival and a window deal for 23 kt c3 1st half June at June FEI plus 8 was done. These deals are more or less representative of the market developments this week. While these deals point to a CP netback of lower than minus 20, it should not surprise anyone that this downward pressure on CFR values more than likely will continue for some time. The realization that slowing increase in demand from the 2 biggest import markets, combined with continued strong Iranian exports and new volumes from US, Canada and Australia, mean that Eastern markets are very well supplied.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2019-05-29 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The US propane inventories showed an unexpected drawdown today of 100,000 barrels. This drawdown was completely unexpected and very far from the consensus view of a build of 2 million barrels. Under normal circumstances,  this would have caused the propane market to spike upwards but with crude falling so fast and so hard it meant that propane would not fall as much and propane did firm compared to WTI closing today at 39 percent of WTI. General belief is that that this is a correction from prior weeks and that next week should once again show a build of close to 2 million barrels. Bottom line is that there is enough inventory to support, domestic cracking, retail and exports for the foreseeable future. In other news US terminals are continuing their catch up from last months delays. Enterprise expects to be caught up in early June and Targa by late June. In Canada, Alta Gas completed their first export from their new facility at Prince Rupert Island, British Colombia. They had a few normal start up delays but overall the terminal performed well and the spec was perfect. All going well Alta gas hopes to have 30 VLGC propane cargos a year from the new terminal.
