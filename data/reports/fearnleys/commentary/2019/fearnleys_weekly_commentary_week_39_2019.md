### Capesize Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2019-09-25 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Underlying fundamentals remain good, but road again bumpy for the big ships and 12 pct of spot value shaved off in 5 trading days. Average daily earnings still at a handsome USD 29k/day and general sentiment remains positive. The Atlantic is the brightest spot, down 10 pct w-o-w to USD 35/day for rounds, whilst the high-volume Pacific round is down almost 19 pct to come in at below USD 22k/day. Huge differences seen between the basins, as units pile up to do quick Far East rounds at discounted levels. IMO 2020 compliance and/or installing of scrubbers again being blamed for regional imbalances. This is this set to continue for the next few months, and unless Australian spot iron ore + coal export volumes increase the Pacific will continue lagging in earnings. Period interest healthy, recently exemplified by 1st generation 207,000 dwt/built 2008 reportedly done for around USD 22k basis about 12 months.

## 2. Tanker Sector (Crude & Products)
### VLCC Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2019-09-25 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

After a couple of weeks with frenetic activity for the VLCCs, and rates rising sharply for all the major VLCC-routes, things have quietened down considerably. A few days with charterers holding back has created an increased uncertainty where the market is heading. Earnings are still healthy, and many have elected to accept rates sub last done levels simply to secure the present earnings. Upcoming holidays in the East may pull more charterers to the market prior coming week-end, which is the hope and expectations out there. In the meantime, rates have corrected down all over, however nothing dramatic. Expectations among owners remain firmly in place and we may see a market for the balance of the year with sharp up-and-down turns.

## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2019-09-25 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Another Gastech is behind us, this time leaving many of the visitors with an overflowing impression, quite literally. The LNG market has continued to be flooded with cargo tenders and subsequently shipping requirements, diminishing the prompt vessel availability across all of the basins to the lowest levels we have seen for a long time. Sublet owners, who recently contributed to the excess prompt availability, have become more cautious and started to withdraw some of those positions while increasing their rate ideas significantly. Charterers in need for a flexible cold spot tonnage, especially in the Pacific, have noticed that in order to fix the vessel they have to pay substantially more than a few weeks ago. That said, the Atlantic side has not been as active as the East, and whilst vessel availability is clearly tight, we have to be wary of the limited number of fixtures done in the region. As typical to our LNG industry however, sentiments can swing from one side to another within a week, but at least for now, LNG shipping seems to have hoisted the sails once again. This week we have no LNGC newbuilding orders to report.

Traders have been busy securing US origin cargoes to cover their shorts in the SE Asia markets and active bids and trades have been shown up in the window all of this week. FOBs ex MEG for specific dates are being bid at October CP plus from small single digit to teens premium, depending on the ratio and loading dates. 23kt Propane for 2h October delivery has been traded a couple of times at Oct FEI minus $3 to $5.   India’s IOC and BPCL have issued buy tenders consecutively to cover front shorts for October delivery but it seems they were unable to secure prompt availabilities. Indonesia’s Pertamina is reported to have secured an October delivery of equal ratio at October CP plus low mid 40s and one similar cargo for November delivery at high 40s, an additional tender for an evenly split cargo for 1h November delivery into Teluk Semangka remain uncovered.    Taiwan’s FPCC is reported to have awarded their buy tender for a full propane cargo with delivery mid Nov at Nov MOPJ minus high 60s, Hanwha Total is also reported to have award their private tender at November MOPJ minus 80s for 23k propane delivered early November.   Another hot issue this week is that on Wednesday US levied more sanctions against several Chinese companies and individuals. It has aroused players’ attentions in the forward market into China, especially when China is the biggest single destination for absorption of Iranian oil and gas.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2019-09-23 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS  The draw of 1 million barrels in US inventories was seen as a correction from the previous large builds and strengthened Mt Belvieu prices. The strength was not dramatic and only slightly affected netback values. The arb remains wide open and as we approach winter there is very little to no chance of cargoes being cancelled form the US at these $170pmt October US/FEI arb values. Traders are now back to trading around a month prior to laycan having done deals further out in the wake of the Saudi attack news. Recently seen demand for US butane has been very strong and traders are now looking at a bidding war to try to prise away already programmed tons. This has been attributed to historic seasonal US trade for gasoline blending in the winter, leaving international traders to fight against a historically good domestic trade and making butane availability tight.
