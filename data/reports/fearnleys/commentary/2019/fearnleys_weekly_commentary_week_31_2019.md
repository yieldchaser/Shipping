## 1. Dry Bulk Sector
### Capesize Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2019-07-31 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Two weeks ago the 5TC index was at 33,000 USD pd. Since then it has fallen off a bit and has stayed around 27,000 USD pd the last week. As we see it the main reason for the correction is that the fleet has repositioned in a more balanced way with the Atlantic basin filling up. The amount of vessels heading to the Atlantic has resulted in a more cautious outlook amongst market players reflected by August FFA being priced at 23,500 USD pd. However, cargo volumes remain at healthy levels so it is hard to believe in a dramatic market fall the next weeks.

**Date:** 2019-07-31 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The market has taken a breather in both basins, and the summer-boom has taken a rapid turn to the red. However, the sentiment in the market is still positive for the longer term, and many expect rates to continue the increase or at least stabilize going forward. ### VLCC Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2019-07-31 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Although the long term optimism remains strong, the VLCC market has continued to tread water in the week gone by, with little or no upward traction. However, the owning community has drawn a line in the sand each side of the W40 mark Meg/ East for unrestricted tonnage, pending on the voyage in question, terms and conditions. There are of course discounts to be had, with plenty of more vintage- and/or ex d/d's or ex scrubber retrofits etc. available, but even this class has shown unwillingness to drop below the mid W30 level. ## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2019-07-31 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The LNG prices, particularly the North East Asian prices have been relatively low, reportedly due to limited demand from the end-users. Despite the flat LNG prices, there has been more activity in the LNG ship chartering market. Vessels have been recently fixed for single voyages and multi-months charters for August and September deliveries. Charter rates have been relatively firm, despite owners facing stiff competition for each and every chartering requirement. All eyes are on the fixing levels for September cargoes as these are currently being negotiated. These would clearly indicate the direction the shipping market is moving as we approach Q4 where traditionally the increasing LNG demand due to winter may lead to a number of players seeking to play the contango.   Despite the strong sentiments for LNG vessel newbuilding activity recently, no new orders were reported this week. However, this year is due to be an interesting one as we expect the yard slots to be taken up in due course.

### Chartering Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2019-07-29 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

WEST For much of this week, the prevailing sentiment was that Western VLGC freight rates were coming off; Houston/Chiba rates weakened to $105pmt, while bids were lower than that on the follow. However, an influx of 3rd decade cargoes rumoured to be in the market was used as a reason by some to offer higher rates again. There are many factors determining whether or not these will be achievable: notably, how many of these cargoes are firm and what levels owners with open vessels at the front of the month want to target compared to those with ships at the back. Arguably the most important factor, though, will be how the arb responds to US stats today.  This could be crucial in helping players decide whether to firm up freight requirements.   EAST Even though current fixing window has shifted towards 2H August in the MEG, we still see available shipping length from Owners and Traders in 1H August. This has yet again added some pressure on rates, and last done deal was reported in the mid/high usd 60s Baltic. In view of the present physical contango situation we do not expect to see any remaining cargos before 20-30 August onwards, although there were talks that some of the MEG suppliers might have spot fob tons available for sale.
