## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2019-04-24 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Optimisation is the name of the game for those players with a fleet of LNG vessels. Portfolio players constantly optimise their fleets to charter in whenever it is more economical to do so, whilst spare vessels, usually with short term availability, are marketed for third party charter and this goes someway to explaining the number of vessels chalked up on the position list. Despite the number of chartering enquiries, rates remain depressed as competition remains high with shipowners eager to ensure that their vessels are moving. There are various cargoes available in the Pacific basin and they are set to keep chartering activity at a moderate level but some way off those previously witnessed. With new US volumes due to come online in the coming months and the hope of a warm start to summer, Owner's eyes are on charter hire rate levels with the hope of tightening in the shipping market. In reality this could be some months off but on the flip-side sublets are a high percentage of the availability and can vanish with little warning. Worldwide availability looks relatively balanced albeit oversupplied as intra-basin trade continues to be the overwhelming route choice.     The Atlantic basin has been relatively quiet of late but we expect some vessels to be fixed given the ongoing good production out of North Africa. Spot trades from the Atlantic to the Far East are still a rarity with the abundance of cargoes from South East Asia and Australia providing any Far East LNG purchase interest with an obvious choice of source for any required cargoes.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Eastern Market
**Date:** 2019-04-23 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

MEG: Following the rise in crude price, May CP price has surpassed April CP, now being $20 higher at 535$ at the time of writing. May/June CP is still in heavy backwardation but continues to narrow from the previous $46 to $28 within this week.  Freight has continuously soared, whereby eroding the potential CFR profits.  FOB lifters without ship length, are keen to resell their FOB’s rather than bringing the CFR volumes to the Far East themselves.  May FOB’s, especially with lifting in the last decade of May, is reported to have been concluded and mid/high single digit discounts to May CP.    FAR EAST: The rally hasn’t ended, and prices are edging up by a day by day basis. Until this Wednesday 23k propane for 1h June delivery was traded at June FEI plus $30.  Cargoes for 2h May delivery is being bid at May FEI plus $50 and no offers seen.  May FEI/CP has narrowed rapidly from minus $20 to near flattish during this week, partly because the tightness in front month for Far East market and also because positions are being rolled into June.  Until backlog delays are cleared thoroughly from US, we will likely see the Eastern tightness extending into 1h June.  Chinese importers have more or less finished their May buying program and are now looking at their June schedule. Recent deal premium levels has varied from $50’s level to $70s on May CP linkage, subject to cargo delivery time range.  PDH margins have been squeezed by higher Propane costs and weaker Propylene demand, we do however expect demand from this sector will stay stable as most units have substantial downstream linkage, yielding higher value products.  Indonesia’s Pertamina was reported to have awarded their two buy tenders on FOB basis, one middle May for lifting ex AG at May CP plus mid-single digit premium and one late May at CP minus.  FPC has awarded their buy tender for 44k propane delivered to Mailiao during 1-10 June at June MOPJ minus high 40s, with 6 offers received. Some players reckon it’s higher than expectations as compared to a window deal on the same day, while others assessed higher value for cargo delivered early June, plus higher freight costs.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2019-04-23 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

We are starting to see a clearer picture of the volume of exports lost to the delays on the Houston ship channel. Exports were curbed by approximately 200,000 barrels per day for 45 days. Keeping 9 million barrels of LPG in the domestic US market. With this in mind some traders found it surprising to see Mt Belvieu prices rise after a slightly smaller than expected, but still a build of 1 million barrels in US inventories. We expect to see maximum exports from US terminals for the next few weeks as the catch up continues,  with the normal lifting schedule to be resumed at all terminals by the beginning of June. South American demand continues its seasonal increase. Importers are having to pay hefty premiums to draw tons to the region to compete with the CFR market in the East.
