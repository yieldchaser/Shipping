## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2019-04-10 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

This week brought most of the LNG players back to the grind after last week’s meetings and networking in Shanghai. Activity seem to have “hit the ground running” with a firmer sentiment - from what could have been the lowest level of year 2019  with regards to the intensity of the LNG shipping activities. While the Chinese players dominated the showcasing of their LNG capabilities in the LNG19 conference, this week everybody joined in (traders, exporters, importers alike) and chartered vessels from all basins. This has resulted in some vessels finding the way out from their idling positions and we see a number of sublet vessels that were available, being withdrawn from our availability lists to do internal trades, further reducing the list of tonnage seeking employment.  Despite the recent activities, headline rates remain stable, but with the likelihood of improvements on the ballast bonus figures going forward. We expect full round trip economics for the shipping costs to maybe come before the headline rates improve.  On the newbuilding front, Maran Gas Maritime and NYK again have hit the headlines with both ordering yet another 174,000m3 LNG vessel respectively, with deliveries from DSME for the former and Hyundai Samho for the latter, to be ready within 2022. NYK’s order is reportedly on the back of a long term charter of up to 20 years including optional extension periods.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Eastern Market
**Date:** 2019-04-08 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

MEG: ADNOC released their May acceptances with some delays reported, this was inevitable as most lifters nominated basis preferred early month lifting because of the heavy backwardation from May to June.    Qatar Petroleum is reported to have sold a May 31 to Jun 1st lifting of 46/0 at June CP plus $11-12 to a trading house. Premium for May FOB liftings varies widely pending on the different loading laycans, from mid high teens to single digits, the latter the cheaper.  At the time of writing, May Propane CP is traded at $505 and Butane assessed $15 higher, with June Propane CP is at $29 less.  Freight market ex MEG remains tight in the front month. Baltic kept it’s upward momentum up this week, which eroded the margins on delivery basis, with lifters tending to sell FOBs at premium to cash premium instead of bringing the cargo into the East themselves, unless they have their own shorts to cover.    FAR EAST: It was another hectic week with robust buying inquires, especially so for front the market against only a few offers. Although Houston channel has resumed operation, minimum 7-10 days are still expected to clear the backlock loading at some terminals.  Therefore, the front tightness extends into 2h May or even 1h June. Off window there is talk circulating that a 23k Propane cargo for delivery during 1h May traded at May FEI plus $60, while 1h/2h May swap value is at $20. On Thursday a 22/22 cargo for 1h May delivery were bid at May FEI plus $70, and bids for 23/0 for 1h May and 2h May was at May FEI plus $55 and plus $30 respectively, no offers for 1h May delivery was seen and 2h May sellers was at 10 dollars higher.  Chinese importers are also exploring cargoes for May, however limited cargoes are shown and the few that are is asking high $60ish or even higher, this is due to the higher FOB premiums and freight.   Some importers have shifted buying requirements from 1h May into 2h May, hoping they can secure cheaper cargoes, this doesn’t look to be the case yet, especially when there are outstanding trader shorts competing for the same cargoes.  Though Propylene appears weak and has squeezed PDH margins, this is unless it is an integrated downstream units.  Compared to high premium on “regular” tons, Iranian origins appear rather attractively priced and April/May imports is expected to be maintained or exceed the March volume which was at 500k tonnes.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2019-04-08 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

FOB discussions in the USGC remain thin. The fog and benzene spill caused havoc and delays which will affect USGC loadings well into May. Traders with cargoes loading from terminals on the Houston ship channel are still unsure of the exact number of days delay they will incur. Halting all FOB trade discussions in the area due to laycan uncertainty. This has led to Marcus Hook going from the ugly duckling, during the well documented ME2 commissioning problems, to the belle of the ball today. Demand for East Coast US tons is at a premium and Marcus Hook has not disappointed. Set to export 9 VLGCs in April and we believe 7 VLGCs per month onwards. Marcus Hook is having a global impact on export cargo flows. The build of 1.2 million barrels although expected surprisingly pushed MT Belvieu up to 42% WTI from 39% the week prior. Latin American demand has been strong, with 80 000 MT more imported into Mexico alone in the 1st quarter 2019 compared with 1st quarter 2018. South American incremental seasonal demand continues, a recent US VLGC tender is rumoured to be heading that way.

## 4. Sale and Purchase (S&P) & Corporate Activity
### Chartering Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2019-04-08 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Both east and west of Suez the VLGC markets have rallied during the course of the last two-three weeks. The fact is, we have witnessed a ten dollar increase in the BLPG index and further jumps are looming as the May acceptances are not out yet from MEG suppliers. Early May vessel availability is seemingly tight in the MEG and as such there is a feeling of anxiety in the market in case May cargo dates are accepted in the beginning of the month.   In the Atlantic basin, there have been a number of fixtures for laycans up to fifty days ahead, something which in turn is creating challenges with regards to vessel scheduling. Actually, owners currently hold back from further fixing activity simply because there is too much uncertainty. As the US terminals are catching up with the delays and things normalize, the inevitable question is, how long will these rates sustain.
