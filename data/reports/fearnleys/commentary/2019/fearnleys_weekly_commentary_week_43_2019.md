## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2019-10-22 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The strong market continued this week as rates remained in the solid six-figures with three way economics, but there are signs that the sharp shock to the market may be easing. Much of the recent bullish sentiment was driven by a flurry of activity coupled with a very tight shipping market in the East; however, the easing of US/China sanctions and vessels redelivering into the spot market will likely reduce those pressures, and owners’ focus may now pass to the West. The West/East arbitrage, which has long been closed, is showing signs of life while tonnage in the Atlantic has all but dried up. There is potential for ballasting from the East and also the release of portfolio vessels, which have already been marketed albeit with restricted availability and a tendency to be withdrawn as internal requirements evolve. On several fronts then, the rollercoaster rides on. There were no new orders placed for newbuilds.

## 4. Sale and Purchase (S&P) & Corporate Activity
### SnP Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2019-10-22 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The S&P activity is picking up in the tanker market. Rates having stabilized at lower, albeit still very firm levels, should bode for more predictable price levels in sight.  However, as we speak, the bid/ask spread is still quite wide with determined buyers running the risk of chasing after shadows in pursuit of particularly modern crude tankers. Rumors abound that a Korean VLCC resale has been committed at region usd 107 million on fairly long subjects.  The jury is still out as to what a brand new VLCC on water/with prompt delivery is worth, given a straight-forward sale. In any event we have for sure passed the usd 100 mill mark and as such we are moving into a pricing territory decoupled from newbuilding replacement costs.    This at a time when yard prices are soft. Based on history this delta would have resulted in increased newbuilding contracting but that’s far from the case now.  Two factors go a long way in explaining this – owners are uncertain about the longevity of the earning spike and even more importantly, owners are nervous and uncertain about the right propulsion system for the commercial life-span of a newbuilding.  MOL has the enviable position of soon inviting offers for their 2007 built VLCC PHOENIX VANGUARD after a previous deal at region usd 39 mill failed to materialize. The price will for sure be higher this time around.      Interest and transactions in the product space are also increasing with several first generation eco-spec MR’s for sale.  Grindrod Shipping for instance today invited inspections of its 2 x SPP 2013 built MRs LEOPARD MOON and LEOPARD SUN. We expect this vintage MR’s will command a price in the very high 20’s.  Following its recent purchase of two 2016 built Capesizes, JP Morgan is now inviting inspection of its TRUE ENDURANCE built 2012 HHI. The Chinese cabotage market is picking up and we are seeing interest for Tier II Supra- and Panamaxes. More ships ending up in the domestic market should have a positive impact for the overall market balance for these sizes. Once imported, they usually leave the international market for good as there are considerable costs related to importation.
