### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Eastern Market
**Date:** 2019-09-17 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

MEG:  The incident in Saudi Arabia over the weekend turned the whole market upside down. Tuesday late night Saudi Aramco finally released their October liftings acceptances, this was with only around two weeks delay being reported and minimal cuts heard, this soothed the market sentiment somewhat.  October CP initially surged to $450, up by $100 compared to the previous Friday, only to later plunge to $400 after the acceptance announcement.  We still believe that with the impact of such incident, October CP will hover above the $400 level. In addition, the prompt demand from especially India and Indonesia, means Butane undoubtedly will be maintained at $10-15 higher. At the time of writing, October CP is traded at $410. However, the front strength has slightly changed forward CP swaps into a backwardated structure.  Qatar Petroleum awarded their mid October lifting of full Propane to a Chinese importer at October CP plus small premium.    FAR EAST:  The supply disruption in Saudi has helped support the US origin price. 23k Propane for 1h October delivery was traded at October FEI minus $15.5. A similar cargo for 2h October delivery is being bid at October FEI minus $12 versus offer at minus $5.  With ample supply from the US, October FEI/CP has narrowed to a $5 spread, makes US origin tons even more attractive to Japan and Korean importers.  The US-Feast arbitrage is still wide open, and players are keen to secure equal ratio cargoes ex US, either to cover shorts in own system into SEA, or for better optimization on cargo origin swap.  Off window market, discussions was in a stalemate situation. VLGC freights are increasing due to friction in the MEG and potential increase in additional war risk premium etc.  Meanwhile, some lifters are cautiously programing bearing in mind the Saudi delay in liftings and continually monitoring the loading schedules out of the MEG before taking further decisions.    There are still some buying inquiries for October delivery tons, not only from China but also prompt demand from India and Indonesia.  Some traditional importer still have room to take in middle October delivery Propane heavy cargoes, while the others are now exploring November delivery tons.  Indonesia’s Pertamina issued another buy tender, this time to buy an even ratio cargo to be delivered into Teluk Semangka during flexible October dates which is closing this Friday with validity till following Monday. India BPCL has issued prompt buy tender for a 22/22 delivered during end Sep into early Oct arrival.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2019-09-17 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS: After a larger than expected 2.9 million barrel build inventories have  gone over 100 million barrels for the first time since November 2016. These stocks levels are set to keep Mt Belvieu prices around or below the 33% WTI mark where they are today and thus competitively priced in global markets. After a flurry of interest in US FOBs due to Saudi supply constraint worries led to a jump form 7 cpg done Friday 13th September to 10 cpg done on Tuesday 17th September. Now that the dust has settled it seems the Saudi attacks will have less of an impact on their exports than originally expected and demand for prompt US tons has reduced. However,  in our view, the vulnerability of the Middle East experienced last weekend,  will allow US exporters to remain at or very near capacity for the for see able future.

## 4. Sale and Purchase (S&P) & Corporate Activity
### Chartering Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2019-09-17 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

EAST: The East market has been in a standstill ever since past week incidents in Saudi Arabia that created a lot of uncertainty. Aramco October acceptances were finally announced last night, and to some peoples surprise it seems that most of the lifters still got nominations within October. However there were a lot of delays as expected, and this has created a vacuum in the market whereby we still have open ships for 01/10 Oct dates, but not that many cargos to fix. We therefore expect more ships to be sent back to the West, which is still also trading at a premium compared to the Eastern market. We also expect next fixing window in the East to slowly move towards 10/20 Oct owns, and Owners ideas are currently in the low/mid $60s excluding awrip.   WEST: This week has been a game of two halves: prior to the weekend, the market seemed content to wait to see the direction things were going after lengthy delays in the east and as such there was little in the way of fixtures. After the commotion in the middle east over the weekend, players took a day to try to assess what the damage was and what the subsequent impact on the market would be. On one hand, bunkers and crude prices soared, which was one factor in the strengthening of western rates. On the other, a flurry of fixtures has left vessel availability looking much shorter in the West. It will be interesting to see what this means for western rates moving forward
