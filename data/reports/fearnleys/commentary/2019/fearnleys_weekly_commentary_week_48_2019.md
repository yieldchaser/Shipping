## 2. Tanker Sector (Crude & Products)
### VLCC Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2019-11-26 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

With VLCC rates having gone from strength to strength as last week went by, things looked rigged for further improvement form the get go on Monday. However, activity was slow as the day progressed, and players were taken by surprise at a ws130 concluded for a MEG/Thailand run late in the day – a good 15-20 point jump on last done levels, with nothing in between. Arguably it was based on a min 14.5 knots laden speed for a shorter type voyage, but nevertheless it immediately added fuel to the fire. However, charterers kept their cool, and come Tuesday managed to convince owners to accept ws115-117.5 deals for MEG/China voyages, yielding daily returns in the USD 90’s/day. With the Thanksgiving tomorrow, and the Christmas party season kicking off in London next week, charterers and owners alike possibly feel a sense of urgency to get things done – and a gradual strengthening is likely more sustainable than the boom/bust scenario we saw not too long ago. Current earnings are not to be sniffed at, and It’s also important to remember that waiting days have become correspondingly expensive.

## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2019-11-26 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

It has been another relatively quiet week, which is slightly surprising considering we are close to the peak winter period historically speaking. On the cargo side, we are seeing a handful of supply tenders, but it seems that these are not creating a massive interest in the market, again highlighting the looming global oversupply of LNG. However, it might as well be that with the current low-price LNG environment, fewer players are willing to take an extra cargo on their books for a small risk/reward ratio so late in the fiscal year and rather focus on how to decorate their Christmas tree this winter. The few shipping requirements that has been worked this week have proved that there is indeed downward pressure on the rates in both basins, albeit the situation in the Pacific looks more challenging for the owners than in the Atlantic, where the vessel availability is still very tight. That said, by all means it is not all doom and gloom for the rest of the year, since we are yet to see how the winter weather will develop, and as we have witnessed before, things can change in a heartbeat in the world of LNG. From the newbuilding side, Samsung Heavy Industries secured an LNG carrier order worth of USD 1.5 billion for a yet undisclosed Eurasian shipowner for delivery by Q3 2022.
