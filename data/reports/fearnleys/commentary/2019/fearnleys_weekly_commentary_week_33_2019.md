## 2. Tanker Sector (Crude & Products)
### VLCC Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2019-08-12 | **Subtype:** Tank Weekly Comment | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

After a busy last week the VLCC activity has slowed down whilst waiting for the September MEG program to be confirmed. However, being in the twilight zone between monthly programs is unlikely to cause much suffering, with bunkers still trending down and decent TCE’s returns at $30k/day or above. There has been some evidence of “profit taking” for a date advantage, but there little change in sentiment. Where possible, charterers are still targeting the older and/or restricted class, which has settled at the very high W40’s for MEG/east cargoes, while modern ships still command rates in the low to mid W50’s.  Overall it’s a holding pattern at the moment, but we do expect charterers to get cracking on September as soon as they can. A firm foundation and a tighter than normal position list could well see further improvements once it gets going in earnest.

It’s been yet another monotonous start to the week for the LNG shipping market with limited requirements seen.  The national holiday(s) in Asia earlier this week did contribute to a slow start, however trade deals may have also not materialize due to the current softening of LNG prices. There were a few sell tenders which could have developed into shipping requirements, but again they were mostly awarded to Traders/ Portfolio player with length in shipping. Despite this, sentiment and rate expectation remain stable as we move into September. As for multi month charters, we did hear favorable numbers on the last deal concluded and we still do encounter some strong multi month interest from a handful of charterers looking for coverage this winter.  On the Newbuildings, Tsakos Energy Navigation have order up to two vessels (1+1) from Hyundai Heavy industries last week.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2019-08-12 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS: The market was battered down after inventories on Wednesday after showing a higher than expected build of 3.2 million barrel build for the week ending August 9th 2019. This has left US inventories at 86.5 million barrels, 24% above the same week last year. The way US production is going has lead traders to believe US inventories will exceed 100 million barrels of propane before draw season begins. It has also lead to traders believing that draw season will begin later than the historic October 1st date. US FOB discussions are still above cancellation levels, but with delivered markets looking very weak and discounted, traders are wondering where they can place the tons. The question is if Mt Belvieu propane continues to be cheap, around or below 30% of WTI, will the good value manage to sustain 1.3 million barrels per day or above of exports from the US through winter?

## 4. Sale and Purchase (S&P) & Corporate Activity
### Chartering Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2019-08-12 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

EAST: It has been a relatively quiet week in the East so far with public holidays in the Middle East, Japan and Singapore. We are still left with prompt open ships in August which continue to add pressure on rates, and more Owners have decided to ballast their ships towards Cape on the back of stronger freight markets in the West. Aramco September acceptances are also expected soon, and as per usual we expect to see more activity on freight once those dates are known.   WEST: In contrast to the last few months, this week has seen the Western VL market flip into a premium relative to the East. There have been two main drivers behind this particular market move. On one hand, a handful of fixtures in the West have absorbed much of the slack in vessel availability. On the other is the composition of ownership of the remaining open vessels, with the vast majority being controlled by the largest few shipowners. Consequently, H/C via P voyages are now being fixed at around $110pmt – some $10 higher than the equivalent Baltic. It will be interesting to see whether Western rates continue to strengthen and to what extent this might influence the balance of the fleet across East and West.
