## 2. Tanker Sector (Crude & Products)
### VLCC Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2019-02-11 | **Subtype:** Tank Weekly Comment | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Another week with rates for the VLCCs under pressure and therefore corrected down somewhat more. There is however a feeling that there is support at present level and the ‘bottom’ may have been found. Those with modern ships, spurred by in influx of fresh business ex USG, have taken a slightly firmer stand. Therefore, WAfr/East rates may edge up slightly, though not yet done. MEG/East remains another story as the list is still overpopulated by ‘mature’ ladies or ex drydock. Therefore a turnaround here is probably a bit down the road and a very active start of the March program required.

## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2019-02-11 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

IOC terminal commissioning was hotly contended this week and is welcome demand and marks the opening of the first LNG Terminal on the East coast of India. This new arrival occurs just as OLT announces that the LNG Toscana Regasification Terminal in Italy is working at almost 100% after less than one year from the introduction of the new allocation mechanism via auction, a very clear indication that trade routes has shifted significantly. Signs of Spring can be seen in Europe which is welcome relief from the dark days of winter but offers less rest bite for owners. The position list could be described as blossoming but that doesn’t give correct connotations for its effect on the market and charter rates which continue to soften. Its weak across the board – all basins and all duration take a hit except for 3 years and longer. Its clear that the prompt market is being underpinned but lackluster demand but the bigger picture remains relatively stable, more LNG due to be produced, the only question mark here is will it be in time for winter 2019/2020. Second-hand prices and NB prices remain stable, for now, but ongoing discussions between the Korean yards could see a significant change in the ownership structure of the 1st tier LNG yards, which may impact NB prices and in turn Charter rates.

## 4. Sale and Purchase (S&P) & Corporate Activity
### Chartering Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2019-02-11 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Shipping activity in the East have been lackluster with very few enquiries to report. Most lifters ended up getting delayed for their March acceptances ex-ADNOC, and hence we are also seeing some additional shipping length from Traders in March. We are still awaiting final March acceptances from Aramco, which is usually needed to kick start activity. The Baltic VLGC Index, which has been moving downward for quite some time, turned around upwards last week and has continued in the same direction this week, although at baby steps. The latest freight levels equates to a time charter equivalent rate of around USD 250,000 per month. The Western VLGC market has not really come to life this week. A couple of fixtures done nevertheless, and done at levels, far above what has been concluded in the East. The levels done represent a T/C/E of around 500 to 550,000 dollars per month.
