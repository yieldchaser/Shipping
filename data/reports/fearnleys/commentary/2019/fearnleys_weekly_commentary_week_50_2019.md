## 2. Tanker Sector (Crude & Products)
### VLCC Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2019-12-09 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Mid-end last week the VLCC market was searching for direction with rates all over the place, but finally found a consensus this week, climbing quickly from ws95 to ws110-112.5 for MEG/eastbound cargoes before levelling off. Current levels are yielding earnings in the USD 80k’s per day when burning the more expensive compliant 0.5% VLSFO and upwards to USD 100k per day if scrubber fitted. Waiting days are thus equally expensive, moving the focus more on loading dates than pushing rates further. The West/East trade has picked up again as well with a number of US export cargoes, spreading the fleet and in turn supporting all load areas. Short term we expect the market to move sideways, with smaller discounts possible for good dates or longer voyages. The position list is thigh in the front end, but opens up as we move into January, hence the preference for longer employment.

## 3. Gas & LNG Markets

**Date:** 2019-12-11 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

We are only in the second week of December but with the reduced activity it feels as though the holiday mood may have already hit the market. As overall availability has increased slightly, spot rates continue to soften from recent highs although so far both headline levels and voyage economics remain broadly positive. Therefore, the delicate market balance continues to hold, and could yet tilt further towards charterers or swing back in owners’ favour,  especially if the build-up of vessels waiting to discharge at certain terminals leads to prolonged floating storage. ### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2019-12-09 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS: The Mt Belvieu propane price fell to 37% WTI after bearish inventories. A surprise build of 1.7 million barrels was seen when market expectations were for a draw of 2 million barrels for the week ended December 6th. This is very much a sellers’ market for US FOBs with many vessels available to lift and buyers queuing up to buy any tons which become available. These market factors have led to another week of extremely high exports of 1.538 million barrels per day. We expect these record high export figures to continue as wide arbs, and freight rates from the US under pressure, means traders are trying to squeeze every possible ton out for export. The last week has been warmer in the US and domestic demand has come off by around 1 million barrels per day. Once winter rears it’s head in the US the questions is how large will the weekly draws be and how quickly will Mt Belvieu react narrowing the arb?

## 4. Sale and Purchase (S&P) & Corporate Activity
### SnP Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2019-12-13 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Despite sustained period of healthy earnings in the tanker chartering markets, it seems the upcoming seasonal holidays are gradually slowing down potential S&P interests. The firm spot markets continue to support Sellers quest for premiums over last dones in order to conclude any potential sales. Saying that, a couple of Buyers are willing to go and meet sellers expectations in order to secure prompt spot tonnage, particularly on the region 10 year and older vintages. This is well illustrated by the sale of the Zodiac controlled JUPITER SUN (ABT 115K DWT SASEBO 2007) which was sold at USD 28.7m. This is the second vessel sold by Zodiac to this buyer, worth noting that the Jupiter Sun is scrubber-fitted hence the premium against the same age sistership Virgo Sun which was sold last month. Regardless both levels represent a significant  jump from last concluded transactions.  With signs of ongoing negotiations for tanker units of similar age we expect a number of Buyers to take positions before year end. The majority of eyes are now however, focused on 2020, patiently waiting to assess the actual impact that the new regulatory constrains will have on the markets.    New revised benchmarks are being set in the Dry SnP markets with three deals being done across segments. The Capesize GOTIA (ABT 178K DWT SHANGHAI WAIGAOQIAO 2012) was sold at Usd 23m, the Kamsarmax MENARO (ABT 81K DWT JIANGSU JINLING 2016) achieved Usd 22.75m while the Dolphin 57’ design Supramax BAOJI (ABT 56K DWT XIAMEN 2013 C4x36 T) was sold at Usd 11.6m. These levels illustrate particularly well the subdued sentiment we have been witnessing from potential Bulker Buyers which, as indicated by the abovementioned sales, has driven values down by 5 to 10% since the summer.  This negative trend can also be demonstrated by the lack of Japanese built vessels changing hands with Sellers preferring to withdraw their units rather than following this downward direction.
