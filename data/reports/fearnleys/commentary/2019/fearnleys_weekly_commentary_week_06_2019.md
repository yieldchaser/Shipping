## 1. Dry Bulk Sector
### Capesize Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2019-02-04 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

With the Chinese New Year celebrations taking place this week, the dry bulk market is as expected extremely quiet. Freight rates are presumably at the lowest for the year over all, and number of spot ships is increasing every day. On top of this, the dry bulk community is watching the developments in Brazil carefully, and what the actual consequences will be in terms of reduced output. Time being it is estimated that 40 million tons of iron ore pellets will be impacted, but this is an estimate only and the length of the period is unknown.

## 2. Tanker Sector (Crude & Products)
### VLCC Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2019-02-04 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The activity for the VLCCs, particularly in the days prior to the Lunar New Year holidays, remained active but as the holidays started in earnest things slowed down worldwide. Ships in all categories remain in ample supply and hence charterers take it easy. Rates have been and are under heavy downward pressure with earnings dipping below USD 20k/day for even the most economical ships. The ‘last bastion’, the USG/East rates have also come down, shaving off abt USD 1 mill from last done levels. All in all, subdued and under pressure for now with few bright spots on the horizon.

### Aframax Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2019-02-04 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

As expected, Aframax rates in the N.Sea and Baltic came under downward pressure this week. The downward correction was mainly due to lack of activity and a build-up of available tonnage in the area. As it will take some time to absorb the prompt available tonnage, we expect the market to stay soft till we reach 3rd decade fixing window. A rather quiet week in Med and Bl.Sea. Owners have been more reluctant to ballast away from the area in the back of seeing alternative markets taking a hit, with Carib-USG dropping around ws45 points and USG-TA dropping ws25-30 points in a week’s time. We have seen a slight downward trend on straight cross Med voyages while Bl.Sea stems continued to trade sideways. Expect the market to remain steady/soft in the coming week with the support of a healthy tonnage list.

## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2019-02-04 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

LNG spot market activity remains muted as Lunar New Year festivities take place in most parts of Asia Pacific and with no arbitrage between the basins. Rates are continuing their descending trend on the back of limited requirements in all basins. In the East, availability keeps growing and although it would seem as a better place to be for an Owner, a few independent Owners are repositioning their open vessels to the Atlantic. In the West, availability is slowly growing as Independent Owners’ positions are joining the existing available sublets. But as a few projects will be commissioning cargoes during Q2, some more activity is indeed expected in the US Gulf. On other news, it is very encouraging for the LNG market to see another major US project getting its FID – Congratulations to the Golden Pass promoters. Yards may continue to be busy building LNG vessels. Second hand prices remain stable and there were no Newbuilding transactions recorded this week.  In the meantime, we wish everyone a Happy Lunar New Year!!

## 4. Sale and Purchase (S&P) & Corporate Activity
### Chartering Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2019-02-04 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

With Chinese New Year celebrations going on, the market has remained rather slow all over the field, although some ships are being fixed somewhat under the radar. Recent deals concluded in the West indicates a further drop in returns to some USD 12-13,000 per day for the transpacific route and about USD 14-15,000 per day on the transatlantic route. The position lists cannot be described as very long, and the largest Owners are close to being sold out for February loadings in the US Gulf. So, a handful of fixtures may enable the market to tighten. The Owners with ships coming open in February will try hard to push freight upwards, so if any cargoes left for Feb, we might see US/East freight rates back up towards the mid USD 50’s due to this shorter shipping length. As the US market is seeing more shipping availability for 1st half March, ships fixed for early March cargoes are currently trading at a discount to the ones loading during 2nd half February. In the East, the market is sliding sideways, with little or no action to report, and the Baltic index is currently returning levels below OPEX which establish a significant “premium” in the western market on some 18-20% depending on trading route.
