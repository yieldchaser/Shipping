### Capesize Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2019-10-01 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The BCI5TC index has dropped 15 % week-on-week, with the most pronounced decline seen the in the Atlantic (down 30% week-on-week). The recent market correction is attributable so a seasonal slowdown in iron ore demand (August often marks an intermediate top before fresh highs for the year usually comes in December). Coupled with this, there has been too many vessels in ballast towards the South Atlantic, with this number rising from 126 at the start of September to 169 at the latest reading.  However, the last few days there has been an increase in fresh requirements that has supported the market, with the BCI5TC stabilizing at slightly above 24,000 USD p/d. It remains to be seen whether this will be enough to turn around the negative trend.

## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2019-10-01 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Sentiment remains high with Owners, and the freight rate indications being exchanged continue to climb despite the fact that limited clean fixtures are out in the market to support it. Current availability is the key driver with very few, if any ships being marketed in the Atlantic for the whole of October, and also limited vessels available in the East. Additionally plenty of enquiry, plus recent FOB activity is likely to create even more shipping opportunities than were currently seeing. Those players with a larger fleet size, and the ability to free up some of their vessels at the right time and location, are the ones likely to fix at solid levels or secure cargoes at attractive levels. We also saw a change in direction for several vessels that were headed into Europe from the US and now have switched their destination to “East of Suez”. What could have been a short trip leaving tonnage open in the Atlantic, has now dragged more ships out of the basin which could tighten the market further. The recent fixing activity from the Pacific seems to have been short spot trips, delivering at similar dates, and could cause an influx in tonnage opening in the East all at once but we will have to wait and see if options are exercised which could split redeliveries up a little. Weather also playing a role on future availability and dates. No newbuilding LNGC orders reported this week.

Sellers of 23kt propane for 1h November delivery are asking November FEI minus $3 to minus $7, while bids heard  at minus $10 level.  The FEI/MB Arb is still wide open, this despite the rising freight and Far East discounts.   This week both India’s IOC and Indonesia’s Pertamina have retendered to buy equal ratio cargos, one is for full month Nov delivery into Ennore and Haldia, and the other is for 6-8th Nov into Teluk Semangka. The result is yet to know but we trust the IOC tender will have high chance of getting awarded because of the greater flexibility in delivery timing.  Despite their holiday’s, Chinese importers are still sniffing for November delivery cargoes, mainly heavy propane ratio as this is for winter heating and petchem use. Late last week one full Propane cargo for early Nov arrival was reported sold at November CP plus low mid 60s basis 1 port E.China, one major China based importer was reported to have resold a 23/0 2h October delivery at October CP plus close to $70 to a PDH user. ### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2019-09-30 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS: The expansion at the Enterprise Terminal has now been completed. The debottle necking completed,  the tie ins made,  and yet the cargos are not there. At one point we were told this expansion would add 175,000 barrels per day, or 10 cargos per month, to the export slate. The reality seems to be an increase of one or two cargos per month. Enterprise stated the product and capability are now there for the increase but the scheduling is where the problem lies and the extra cargos still seem to be well in the future. The propane inventories ended the summer over one hundred million barrels and a level that should lay to rest any fears on product tightness over the winter of 2019/2020. In fact with ever increasing production the idea of a tight product market in the US seems like ancient history.
