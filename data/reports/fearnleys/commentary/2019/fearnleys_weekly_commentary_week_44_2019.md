### Supramax Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2019-10-29 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Supramax rates dropped across all borders. Atlantic market with surplus of tonnage available and lack of cargoes, resulted in a sharp drop in USG rates. Med- W.Africa clinkers fixing USD 13/14k per day, which is about USD 3k less from previous week. The limited cargoes from EC India resulted in a drop in the rates, as well as WC India and S.Africa rates experienced a further drop with Supramax, getting around USD 13k/d + 300k bb, compared to much healthier rates earlier this month. SE Asia has seen steady activity, though rates were under pressure. Ultramax reported fixing USD 14,500/day for a trip via Indonesia to China. Tess 52 delivery North China for Indo coal back to China obtaining USD 9k. We expect the market and indexes to drop further.

## 2. Tanker Sector (Crude & Products)
### VLCC Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2019-10-29 | **Subtype:** Tank Weekly Comment | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Despite things appear a bit quiet, the volumes continue at regular levels for the major VLCC routes. The difference is that most is done quietly, well under the radar. This may indicate that the present levels are ‘acceptable’ to both parties, and hence rates appear steady and flat for now. Some had expected rates to corrected down much sharper than they have but there is still both optimism and resistance among owners. The MEG is however a bit over supplied with ex dry dock and also with some newbuildings which have fewer takers, but if the demand from the Atlantic continues, the ships with ‘needs’ may have found their own support level for MEG/East. For the market to firm from present levels we would need increased volumes for all the major VLCC routes, something many are both hoping and expecting to happen as we embark on the actual winter months.

## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2019-10-30 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Some cause for a pause in the chartering market as sanction news and its effects dissipate and chartering requirements pull back from the recent sprint.  This news couldn't come soon enough for some, yet a couple of prompt cargoes remain effected by its wake and those holding the cargoes continue to weigh up the choice between taking a ship at year highs or selling FOB at slightly better numbers. This challenging market has seen some creativity which is great to see. There aren’t too many Picasso’s out there and the LNG chain doesn’t provide a white canvas backdrop to go crazy but there are still some individuals/companies making grounds with ideas that should be common place. Creativity does face a challenging compromise, in a tight market the chartering equivalent to a silent assassin normally comes out on top but right now chartering departments are just one cog of a complex string of adjustments that takes time to align. This inability to move quickly carries its own risks. The result is lots of activity, questions and requirements and bigger differences between gains and losses.   The biggest movement in shipping economics this week is to the ballast bonus sentiment whilst further out one year and multi-year rates take a slide, thus rates relatively stable this week but overall economics are starting to drift downwards. New build prices are stable yet under pressure whilst second hand prices continue their downward trend.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Eastern Market
**Date:** 2019-10-31 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

MEG: Saudi Aramco has set the November Propane CP at $430 and Butane CP at $445, each up by $10 from October price.  The Propane CP is in line with latest swap value, yet some end users/importers reckon it is higher than expectation. Also, the spread between Propane and Butane is wider than latest trade which was at $10.  At the time of writing, December CP is traded at $410, $20 less than the just announced November CP. December FEI/CP spread is traded at around $35, partly driven by the sustained strong freight.  Qatar Petroleum is reported to have awarded their December lifting of a full cargo Propane at December CP plus a small digit premium to a Chinese Major.   FAR EAST: A flurry of deals was concluded in the window this week. 23kt Propane for 2h November delivery was traded at November FEI plus $14, and one equal ratio cargo for end November delivery into Chiba was done at November CP plus $77, which equates to November CP plus $5 on FOB netback.  Off window discussions on Propane for December delivery are also active with a few trades concluded at December FEI plus mid-high single digit during mid-week. However, the levels currently under discussion are weakening.  One S.China PDH operator issued a buy tender to secure a 1-10 December delivery of 23/0, the result is yet to be known by the time of writing.  Other Chinese importers are cautious and want to think twice before taking spot cargoes in December before CP is set, importers also se sluggish demand from domestic customers combined with surplus supply from refineries.  Nevertheless, importers still expect strong rising demand and are starting inquire for next year’s term supplies but some discussions are still pending with uncertainties on US-China tariffs and strong freights in 2020.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2019-10-31 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS: The lower than expected draw in US inventories of 100,000 bbls took the total inventories to 99.8 million barrels. The draw was bearish against expectations but Mt Belvieu went up. Last week the inventory number was bullish and Mt Belvieu went down. Go figure, but with that said the first real cold weather of the season has arrived and this domestic demand may be the real reason for the domestic price strength. This has coupled with rising freight rates has brought netback values down and traders FOB bid ideas down into single figures for December. We seemed to have reached a stalemate of sellers not willing to go below double figures FOB premiums and sellers not willing to pay up. Cargoes have been being worked a long way in advance to the usual 30-40 days prior laycan due to tight freight. The question is who will blink first for the few remaining FOB cargoes available this year? Buyers paying up or sellers coming down?

## 4. Sale and Purchase (S&P) & Corporate Activity
### SnP Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2019-10-29 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Hartree is reported to have sold a brand new DSME built VLCC for close to usd 103 mill to Korean buyers.  This means, as expected, that we have broken the usd 100 mill mark for such ships able to give prompt delivery. We believe the number of buyers willing to pay these numbers is still thin on the ground but nevertheless this is clearly a new benchmark. As such a similar spec resale Suezmax able to give prompt delivery with scrubber onboard should be worth region usd 70 mill. Offers are invited next week for the 2007 DSME built VLCC PHOENIX VANGUARD and we understand close to ten buyers are outright. Older crude tankers are in demand as seen by the sale of VLCC’s ASTRO CHALLENGE and ENERGY R / POWER D built 2002 and 2003 which falls in line with the recent Ridgebury deal for same vintage.  Also we see much interest for MR’s across the board but modern eco ones have yet to see any sales activity.  We see a continuation of the decoupling between the rates and the values on dry. The explanation that most point to is the sluggish period rates caused by the FFA curve being in steep backwardation, which in turn is caused by bleak macro outlooks. Another explanation is that the number of bulkcarriers for sale is still large which goes a long way in explaining why values are best case still moving sideways.
