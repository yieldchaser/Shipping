### VLCC Weekly Comment

## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2019-06-19 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The spot shipping market appears to have become subordinate as Charterers have their eyes on the autumn-winter market and in some cases multiyear coverage. Winter coverage often ends up in discussions for a 1yr TC vs the requested 6 or 9 months TC, due to pricing and owners reluctance to take «shoulder months» risk. It will be interesting to see how these year and multiyear discussions pan out over the next few weeks.  Last week was relatively active East of Suez,  but with good availability of vessels, rates remained stable. Markets were quieter West of Suez, but availability within this basin is very limited.  «News of the week» was that Anadarko has taken FID on Mozambique for 12,8 MTPA which could easily translate into a requirement of about 12-16 vessels.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Eastern Market
**Date:** 2019-06-20 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

MEG: Saudi Aramco released their July acceptance over the weekend, with some Butane liftings being advanced. It is reported that the producer is offering equal ratio cargos for July lifting with ample Butane stock. July CP swaps remained flat most of the week, until Thursday afternoon when it rose to $360, shadowing the crude rebound. Not sure if freight has reached the ceiling at $80 but the continuous increment on shipping costs have squeezed the margins, more so when considering current weak demand from India and China. Buyer are asking mid-teens or even higher discounts on FOB.   FAR EAST: Plenty of deals were concluded in the window during this week. 23kt propane for 2h July delivery was traded at Jul FEI plus 3-6$, which equates to July CP plus low mid 50s’. FEI/CP still maintains at 50ish, thanks to the soaring freight. Off window inquiries into China are quite sluggish, thus the spread between US and Non-US origin has been narrowing quickly.   Apart from window deals, a handful of tenders are ongoing. SP Chemical awarded their buy tender to one Chinese major importer at July CP plus 60s for 23k propane, SP Chemicals are reported to be looking for another cargo for end July delivery, for use in their cracker. Following that, Dongguan Juzhengyuan was also reported to have secured Propane from the largest Chinese importer at July CP plus 50s. FPCC awarded their buy tender for 11/11 during 16-20th July delivery into Mailiao at July MOPJ minus mid 80s. Indonesia Pertamina has closed their term tender for 2 full cargos for delivery January to December 2020, result is yet to known.  Having said this, the majority of spot demand is driven by petchem for Propane, buying interest from traditional importers is pretty slim, with only one Chinese importer reported to have purchased an 11/11 cargo at July CP plus around 50$.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2019-06-20 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The US supply keeps on coming! Gaining 3.4 million barrel build last week was slightly (around 1 million barrels) above trader expectations. These larger than expected builds and general bearish sentiment in Mt Belvieu has not lead to higher terminalling fee discussions. Skyrocketing freight rates have kept product netback values around 9cpg US-FEI based on flat price. Which in itself is bullish for prompt delivered markets as the arbs are supporting strong freight values from the US. There is trepidation around terminalling fees. Buyers not willing pay… and seller not willing to buy… through flat price values. The trader sentiment is that f it makes sense on paper then it can work. With the outlook for Mt Belvieu prices bearish through high supply. The question is: Does bearish Mt Belvieu lead to a wider arb, and what freight level can it maintain?

## 4. Sale and Purchase (S&P) & Corporate Activity
### Chartering Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2019-06-17 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

East market remains tight as all 1st decade July positions was cleared immediately after Saudi acceptance came out. Now people take a short breather as the Baltic is approaching $80 pmt, after a climb of almost $10 pmt during just one week. The markets further direction will be dictated by inquiries in the middle and second half July, which is yet to hit the market. Looking back at the Baltic history, around $80 was the previous ceiling in 2008, 2011, 2012, 2013 before super bullish cycle came in 2014-2015. Now we are in the time whether this ‘historic’ ceiling works this time again or not. One thing that is different this time is that the previous market movement was made without neo-Panama canal opening and big US export. This time around, the market is mainly driven by widen arb, it would be interesting to see how far Freight is going to go.  Focus in the West VLGC market is on last decade July which is closing in on being wiped out in terms of shipping avails. Thus, in a relatively quiet week in the Western VLGC market, owners look content to see what direction the market will take and seem somewhat hesitant to commit to fixing simply due to some vessel’s discharge schedules are not firm yet. It will be interesting to see whether the Baltic Houston/Chiba equivalent also will face resistance in the coming weeks if the BLPG index flattens out or even starts sliding. As a direct consequence to the market direction over the past few weeks, is time charter requests. We see increased interest in longer-term TCs, and at the same time, we note owners being reluctant to lock in fixed numbers at current levels… We have also seen some activity on the newbuilding scene for the VLGCs, a couple of orders have already been reconfirmed, and at the current moment the Orderbook counts 37 ships. In addition some 6-8 ships are very close to being reconfirmed.
