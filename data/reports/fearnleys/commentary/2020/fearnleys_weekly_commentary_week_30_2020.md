### Capesize Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2020-07-22 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Levels for the big ships spiraling downwards on a combination of modest iron ore/low coal trading volumes and a derivatives market being sold by numerous players lacking faith. Fundamentals, however, remain robust in comparison, and question is when levels will again find a floor and rates again start to pick up. ## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2020-07-22 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The shipping market has seen plenty of activity this week, with Australian exports generating interest in the east and US Gulf enquiries picking up in the west. Fixing levels aren’t yet showing sharp increases while the tonnage is there to meet demand, although ships are slowly trickling into employment and sublets are being reabsorbed back into their portfolios. While most firm activity has been east of Suez, the Atlantic market is being watched carefully; after a very weak summer, expectations of an improving LNG trading environment appear to be bearing fruit as early estimates suggest September US Gulf cargo cancellations are down considerably. With players seeking winter coverage and the window for September spot chartering approaching, we could see sentiment firm up in the basin. No Newbuilds have been ordered this week but interest in projects and small scale seems to be restarting, with the Philippines moving forward with FSRU talks, Mozambique LNG project getting financed and the Qatar expansion progressing. As parts of the world re-emerge from lockdown and some limited cross-border travel returns, a sense of normality is returning. Wherever you are in the world, stay safe and we hope that your 2020 will perk up soon enough.

## 4. Sale and Purchase (S&P) & Corporate Activity
### SnP Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2020-07-24 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Another week that has seen a flurry of activity in the dry markets with numerous vessels changing hands and an increasing number of modern quality tonnage being made available for sale. The Capesize segment seems to gradually be following suit with some owners deciding to take advantage of the buying momentum witnessed in the smaller sectors by putting a couple of units onto the market. Namely the Japanese controlled “PACIFIC ENDURANCE” (2011/Koyo/180kdwt) and the sistership “JUBILANT EXCELLENCE” (2013/Koyo/180kdwt) –  both owners are inviting inspections on their respective units and understand that 5 buyers are lined up to get onboard the 2011 built unit while the “JUBILANT EXCELLENCE”  will be made available for inspection while in DD in China beginning of August. Elsewhere the 5 year old Ultramax  “ALAM MULIA” (2015/Iwagi/61kdwt) has been sold by Malaysian owners at Usd 18.8m. This sets a much needed benchmark for modern Japanese built Ultramax considering that the last sale dates back to February this year. The levels achieved are more or less in line with recent sales in the sector reaffirming the premium that Japanese built condone against their Chinese and Korean counterpart, even with Japanese design in China. This becomes particularly apparent when compared the sale of the” DEVONGATE” (2014/Dacks/61kdwt) which achieved Usd 16m earlier this month.  In the tanker markets second-hand activity remains very much subdued across all sectors with very few sales to report. A striking lack of activity on the modern front has lead a number of buyers to  explore the Newbuilding route where shipyards are striving to become competitive against a stagnant and much less flexible second hand value environment. As such there are signs of a number of ongoing discussions taking place across China and Korea for orders ranging from MRs up to VLCCs.
