### Capesize Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2020-09-02 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Rates softening somewhat for the big ships, despite very punchy iron ore prices and subsequent volumes. Further surprising is that the accumulated effect of close to zero fleet growth, countless ship-days lost due to crew change delays, typhoons, substantial congestion and a large number of ships waiting off China since months to discharge Australian coal has yet to turn spot sentiment positive. Average daily earnings down 7 pct w-o-w to come in at USD 17,600, the Atlantic being the area holding up best despite labour issues in major coal exporter Colombia. Going forward, optimistic projections much attributed to seasonally increasing trade flows on the Brazil/China conference route. Period activity limited as ffa's give little support for levels anticipated/asked and number of definite candidates low - only a couple of conclusions for around 12 months duration on BCI index type tonnage in the USD low 15k's recently done.

## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2020-09-02 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The list of open tonnage continues to diminish, firing the shipping market in both basins just as the fixing window approaches the fourth quarter of the year. The Pacific and Middle East continue to see more activity through a combination of bilateral discussions and tender business, while the Atlantic is seeing higher rates and longer fixtures. Despite the slight difference in trading environments, Owners will be heartened by more opportunities and improving economics in both basins. That said, there is some uncertainty in the prompt LNG market in the west as we await the full impact of the recent hurricanes that battered the US Gulf terminals; early signs are that the damage is less than was feared although there are some cargo delays and cancellations, and we wait to see how this effects the shipping market. We show further rate rises this week, but the familiar story of a relatively weak cargo market and players lifting with their own tonnage is keeping the market in check. There are flickers of a strengthening LNG market, and if that takes hold shipping will follow. The sale and purchase market remains active but transactions are limited as buyers and sellers engage in pricing discussions. There have been no newbuilding orders placed at yards this week

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2020-09-01 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS: The weekly EIA propane inventory report  showed an unexpectedly high build of 4.389mmbbls, from 90.833mmbbls for the week ending 21 Aug 2020 to 95.222mmbbls as of 20 Aug 2020.  The data also revealed a YOY increase of 2.366mmbbls when comparing the Aug 2020 figures to the Aug 2019 data.  Most of the build occurred in PADD III (Gulf Coast) and is reckoned to be the result of lifting delays as related to the passing of Hurricane Laura.  On that, the only terminal to be directly impacted – and which continues to suffer closure – is the LoneStar facility at Nederland, TX.  Where the Houston Ship Channel and Freeport, TX terminals were temporarily closed (and briefly, at that), the Category 4 storm made landfall only slightly East of the Sabine River and caused significant damage in its wake, and left the LoneStar terminal out of commission for approximately a week.  Front-month Mont Belvieu values are presently hovering in the 49ctgs/gal range but then rise through the balance of the year, only tapering, presumably, on seasonality factors at end Q1 2021.  With US stock levels having risen so sharply at the end of August the sentiment is one of cautious optimism in that it appears at present to be sufficient volumes to satisfy both domestic res/com and petrochemical-related demand and export volumes.  But with near record-level exports seen in August, and concern of production declines, the underlying concern of insufficient stocks lingers and is thus supportive of the pricing structure for the next 4-5 months.
