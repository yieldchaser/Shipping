## 2. Tanker Sector (Crude & Products)
### VLCC Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2020-06-08 | **Subtype:** Tank Weekly Comment | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The VLCC market failed to capitalize fully on the upward momentum from Friday when a W65 was logged MEG/China. Numerically activity has been healthy, but most have been Chinese “COA” liftings and a few program deals, leaving little to choose from for independent owners. The owning community’s fighting spirit still remains high, however, and a W60 concluded midweek – on an oil company relet to bout – was against the tide and shows strength. There are still discounts to be had from the vintage fraternity, but a W53.25 replacement fixture MEG/Vietnam on a ’03 built unit is not a giveaway either. Rumours of a mass US sanction listing of owners having traded from Venezuela failed to materialize, but the threat of same has cautioned players and partly aided current rate levels. Continued OPEC+ cuts through July does not indicate a turnaround any time soon, but daily VLCC earnings in the high $40k’s per day is nevertheless miles ahead of those of their smaller sisters, struggling to cover OPEX.

## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2020-06-10 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

General news flows in recent weeks have had an air of pessimism but the mood may be changing, with countries reopening and an expectation that things are starting to look a little brighter and the same can be said for the LNG industry. Despite low product prices and LNGC rates, the industry continues to its forward progress. The 19.2 billion US dollar shipbuilding deal agreed last week by Qatar Petroleum with three South Korean yards offer Owners a well needed distraction from today’s spot market.  New areas and sectors of demand is also bearing fruit, with further LNG bunkering vessels being launched.  And whilst operating on the open seas has seen new challenges raised through the restrictions on movement of personal and quarantine measures, MAN dual-fuel engines passed one million operating hours this year, which reflects a level of experience and operating history that should offer improved reliability and a greater understanding of the technology meaning smoother sailing for Owners and Charterers alike. All we need next is a sustainable arbitrage to reduce what is a slightly bulging availability list.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2020-06-09 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS: Following a flurry of July FOB cargo sales at the beginning of the week, the US/ Asia arbitrage narrowed Wednesday following an unseasonable draw in Inventories. The  US Gulf made up the entirety of the draw (1.3 million barrels) as the Mid continent built. As it stands, US inventories now rest at a similar level to this time last year, though the sentiment of considerable Q3 and Q4 product tightness remains throughout the market. With that said, two US producers echoed a similar feel this week in that barrels redacted last month, following cutbacks due to tanking crude prices have reappeared due to the historic May rally. A handful of July cargos remain unsold. Traders and terminals alike continue to target terminalling fee’s at or above 5cpg to move, while others hope to roll cargos back. In South America, a Tender was issued for 44,000mts of propane for an end July arrival.
