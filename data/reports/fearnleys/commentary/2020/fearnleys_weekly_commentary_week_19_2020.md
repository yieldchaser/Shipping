## 2. Tanker Sector (Crude & Products)
### VLCC Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2020-05-06 | **Subtype:** Tank Weekly Comment | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

One sentence that could sum up 2020 is of course that at one point in recent weeks, 1 loo roll was worth more than a barrel of crude oil. But another may be that owners became too focused on oil trading, thereby over estimating the shipping market - whereas their counterpart suffered from the opposite. They should probably both have stuck to what they are good at. As much as the owning community rode the upward trend too far, Oil companies and traders alike can't seem to give it away quick enough on their recently acquired relets. If nothing else they practice what they preach when it comes to keeping rates under pressure - albeit wearing the wrong hat. We appeared to have found a floor at W59 eastbound from both MEG and Wafr, repeated a number of times during yesterday's session, only to find Exxon having lowered the bar further by fixing their ship at W57 for a MEG/Korea run. Probably not a big enough drop to make Charterers continue the recent re-negotiation trend, but will nevertheless be the new benchmark to beat. Batten down the hatches.

## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2020-05-04 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

It has been a slightly quieter week as we have been enjoying several public holidays across the globe (namely Labour Day holidays and the Golden Week in Japan) giving the markets a short breather. Moreover, based on the news this week, the Covid19 – situation might find a bit more light at the end of the tunnel, as various countries have slowly released their plans for lifting some of the ongoing restrictions. This is cautiously positive news for the global economy and energy demand, but the road to recovery is still very uncertain. The same fundamentals apply to the world of LNG, where the supply overhang is still very much a fundamental problem causing bearish views for the rest of the year for both cargo volume and shipping. That said, we have seen a bit more interest in multimonth TC-periods as Charterers’ and Owners’ ideas on expected rate levels have narrowed. This might be more speculative than purely demand driven, but nonetheless seeing more term activity is a good sign. In the spot market, we are recording a small softening on the rate levels this week, as the ongoing charter interest, which is mainly driven by various spot cargo tenders, is not enough to compensate for the lengthy vessel availability list. Finally, there has been no new large LNGC orders reported this week (and orders for first 5 months 2020 are down by 12 units compared to 2019). With a strong US dollar, low interest rates and limited new orders from other shipping segments, there could be a risk of softening newbuilding prices.

### Chartering Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2020-05-05 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

EAST: This week had a quiet start with several Asian countries on public holidays. Come mid-week however we saw an uptick in activity with a handful of cargoes either worked or placed on subs. Looking at the positions list, May looks tight – but on the other hand it’s not looking like there is a lot of uncovered cargoes for the rest of the month as we start to look ahead towards June dates.  WEST: Bearish sentiment continued West of Suez this week, with the threat of cancellations given poor US-Japan netbacks never far from players’ minds. While it’s unanimously agreed that freight is weaker than the 3-digit Houston/Chiba rates seen not long ago, limited liquidity has made it difficult to say exactly where the market should price tonnage. East and West have nearly been at parity for some time, which means it's also not immediately obvious which direction the fleet will sail. These uncertainties may pave the way for more deals to be done on a floating basis.   Earnings have remained strong in spite of weaker rates, but it seems as though they could come under some pressure as bunkers have ticked up with stronger crude in the last few days.  Looking into June, the positions list looks reasonably long, with a combination of Owner-controlled vessels and a handful of trader relets that are likely to be competitively priced. Although these two things together should put owners in a squeeze, it’s important to remember that a record number of liftings in the last couple of months and longer tonne-mile voyages recently may prevent freight from coming off a long way in the short term.
