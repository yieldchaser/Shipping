## 2. Tanker Sector (Crude & Products)
### VLCC Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2020-01-29 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Considering where we have come from the VLCC market is presently a sight for sore eyes. Returns are still north of USD 40k/day, which this time last year would have been more than acceptable. Owners are putting up a fight, at the writing moment drawing the line in the sand at ws65 MEG/East, but we will not be surprised to see someone break ranks and simply wants to get fixed. The market rarely stays static for long, and the arrow is certainly not pointing up for now. There are however an increasing number of ships getting off-spec bunker which has led to replacements. The ongoing ‘Corona-Outbreak‘ is also weighing on the general sentiment, but hopefully short-lived. On a positive note bunker prices are also seeing a downward tendency.

## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2020-01-28 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

As we are nearing the end of January, shipping rates have seen further decline with more vessels expected to hit the market once redelivered by her present Charterers. Although the Atlantic market did provide some support with a handful of potential requirements and FOB cargo tenders seen loading from W. Africa and the US Gulf, the extended holidays, limited requirements in the East and an over supplied product market have put pressure on the current spot rates. It’s no surprise given that the shouldering months are resurfacing around this time of the year and as such, utilization is key. With that in mind, it seems tonnage providers are looking for multi month charter opportunities while most Charterers seem to prefer to only cover with spot charters instead.  Although there are no new building deals concluded this week, there are a handful of larger ongoing long-term tenders which could in turn see more delivery slots being reserved or firmed up from the shipyards.  After passing her 40th year birthday, another LNG vessel was sold for demolition last week.

## 4. Sale and Purchase (S&P) & Corporate Activity
### SnP Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2020-01-27 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The combination of coronavirus, weak refinery margins and talks of the US releasing the Cosco vessels (25-30 VLCCs) has proved a toxic mix for the tanker markets and the jury is still out as to possible effect on asset values.  We should soon see whether the positive buying sentiment experienced over the past few weeks will subdue as a result.  SFL Corp managed to achieve a firm price for its 2002 built VLCC FRONT HAKATA at slightly excess Usd 34 mill.  The 2012 Sumitomo built Aframax NANYANG STAR received offers this week after being inspected by 4 parties. However levels remain unclear at this stage and we expect to get more clarity early next week which should give a clear indication on the course values will take going forward.  With this week  ending on the lowest BCI on record it is no surprise that dry values are unquestionably under pressure. As a result buying appetite is on the rise trying to find willing sellers at these revised levels while some sellers are resisting by choosing to withdraw rather than follow this downward trend. Saying that, we do expect new benchmarks to be set across segments.  In the capesize sector all eyes are on the Japanese controlled MINERAL HOKKAIDO built 2008 Imabari which has been marketed for sale earlier in in the week with imminent inspections. Meanwhile on the Supramax front  new benchmarks have been set with the sale  of the TRITON VALK built 2009 Mitsui (no BWTS) at low Usd 11 mill.
