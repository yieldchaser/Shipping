## 2. Tanker Sector (Crude & Products)
### VLCC Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2020-11-04 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

A new week, a new month, alas the same poor VLCC market, with the coming winter looking more Game of Thrones than gain in freights. The second Covid-19 wave has hit hard, leaving little hope for oil demand, emphasized further by leading OPEC countries and Russia suggesting further production cuts – on the back of which oil prices have recouped most of last week’s losses. Adding insult to injury bunker prices have followed suit, with daily tanker earnings now into single digits per day. However, owners are becoming increasingly reluctant to commit to these cuts to the bone earnings, which has curbed the downward spiral somewhat. MEG/eastbound voyages are now priced in the mid WS20’s with a few more points added for longer West/East voyages, and we expect more of the same moving forward.

## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2020-11-04 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Over the past few weeks, the LNG market have seen an upward trajectory on the spot shipping rates. The bullish sentiment resonated in the lack of availability for November loading which saw daily charter rates broke into the six figure barrier.  However, the current levels seem to be short-lived as activities and vessel requirements have decrease simultaneously in both basins.  The weaker performance of the product market, the decline in requirements for shipping and an increase in availability not just from Independent Owners but sublets shown by Portfolio players and Traders, have all contributed to the sentiment change. At the present moment, with so much uncertainty that could affect the overall shipping market, volatility is inevitable for the coming weeks.   From an analytical perspective, the area of concern is after Q1 2021 where many anticipate a slightly more challenging market. Utilization may be a priority versus rates and as we have already seen from recent fixtures, Owners were more willing to work requirements with longer periods to brace through the shouldering months ahead after this winter rather than working spot charters at current levels.   In the new building market, It was reported an Owner have secured two 174,000 cbm LNGC plus options for two additional vessels from Hyundai Heavy Industries (HHI). The orders are at the back of time charter contracts concluded with PGNiG. The new building LNG carriers will be delivered in 2023 and is expected to lift volumes from Calcasieu Pass Terminal which PGNiG is procuring the LNG under long term contact.

AMERICAS As the election continues oil prices have drawn back after an initial surge. Mt Belvieu propane has remained strong and lies just below 60% WTI. A larger than expected 2.6 million barrel draw in US inventories for the week ending October 30th was bullish Mt Belvieu. The draw was largely due to massive exports through October, and averaging 1.425 million barrels per day for the week. Some believe with 95.8 million barrels in domestic inventories and already well into draw season Mt Belvieu should not be at such a high % WTI. ### SnP Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2020-11-06 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The buying interest in VLCC tonnage remains steady and firm. Two more 2002 built are sold at prices in line with last dones. Following Hunter Groups’ recent sale of two 2019 HHI built VLCC’s at Usd 84.2 mill each, we now have another noteworthy sale. We understand TRF HORTEN built 2018 HHIC-Phil has been sold to Delta Tankers for Usd 72 mill. Adjusted for age this is some 10% down compared to the Hunter deal. The vessel is built at Hanjin’s previous Philippines facility with a retrofitted scrubber which explains part of the difference. However, in a more stable market we would expect a difference around 5% so this may arguably point to further downwards correction in values. As far as replacement cost bench-mark goes, we do not see much further downside potential in newbuilding prices from the Korean majors.  The combination of a strengthening WON vs USD and increased steel prices tells us that we are at bottom levels now. Tellingly, several VLCC contracts are in the making in Korea which may ease the pressure on the yards. With the new-found optimism in the container markets we will not be surprised also to see the Korean yards benefitting from increased ordering activity for the big sizes.   Despite the weakening freight market the dry S&P market is full of life with a large number of vessels changing hands across all sizes. There are unconfirmed reports of two 2016 JMU built scrubberfitted Capesizes being committed in the high 30’s, we expect to have more details next week.
