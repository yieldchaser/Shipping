## 2. Tanker Sector (Crude & Products)
### VLCC Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2020-07-08 | **Subtype:** Tank Weekly Comment | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

After a couple of weeks of inactivity in the VLCC market, this week kicked off with a flurry of activity, and rates have soared up some 40%. Spurred by previous weeks' decline charterers held back in order to propel things further down. However, delays in the East and increased ton-mile have taken its toll, slimming tonnage availability. When it became evident that a floor was established in the mid WS30's MEG/eastbound, charterers entered the market en masse, creating a "ketchup-effect". Owners quickly caught on and adjusted ideas upwards by the hour. All areas have contributed, and daily earnings have doubled. The jury is out whether the upward spiral will continue, some warning that Suezmaxes will come into play as an alternative soon. But the summer nevertheless looks much brighter now than it did a few days ago.

### Aframax Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2020-07-08 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The Aframax market in the North Sea and Baltic can be described as lacklustre. The list of available tonnage is very long. Available market cargoes are very rare in the current fixing window. ## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2020-07-06 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Over the past week we’ve seen a few signs of life in the spot market, with those vessels fixed or placed on subs being at or slightly above recent spot rates. The focus of the market continues to be winter coverage, with reports of several Charterers securing additional tonnage through the winter. These fixtures have established a forward view that is not necessarily as weak as the current sport market would have it. Furthermore, independent owners with open winter tonnage are growing increasingly selective on which opportunities they will entertain. It’s almost mythical how these things tend to come together around week 27 every year. One thing does seem certain, and that is that the market may have bottomed out…although this does not mean that we’ll see a sudden increase in spot rates this side of the summer holidays.

AMERICAS The EIA reported a build of 2.2 million barrels this week, in line with expectation- all the while exports rose to 1.33mn b/d. As it stands, the butane arb to Europe looks better than propane, (around $80/ton) while the propane arb in that direction has come off. The LST/FEI spread last showed $83 lst/fei following the build, netting back just over 4cpg (when using low 60s for freight on a houston/chiba basis) where FOB discussion for August cargos now stand. Many participants have taken the view that the catalyst for higher netback values will come in the form of Indian demand -as China, South Korea and Japanese stocks seem well built at this time. US producers, notably in the Marcellus, Utica and Permian regions have pressed onward with their volume recovery as the crude price now sits in the low 40's following the mid april crash that subsequently choked out Marcus Hook export volumes.

As expected the increased level of buying enquiry in the dry sector has resulted in a notable pick up in activity with a number of inspections and negotiations taking place this week. Japanese owners invited inspection of the 2011 Sanoyas built Kamsarmax “KM YOKOHAMA” and up to 9 buyers are now outright with offers invited next week. This firm interest has inevitably driven additional sellers onto the market with new Kamsarmax units being placed for sale.
