### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2020-04-06 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Another week of uncertainty has passed, with various schemes and scenarios stretching out like a river delta for the coming months, and owners and charterers face decisions today armed with an incomplete picture of an unknown future. The cargo market remains soft, and following several force majeure events and high inventory levels one might fear the worst; however, trading uncertainty has led to some increased tonne-time, and travel restrictions may cause some terminal operators and ship owners to defer maintenance to later in the year. Amidst this, the cargoes have kept coming…so far. Availability has broadly kept pace with fresh requirements, so rates and voyage economics are holding firm, albeit with some froth. Further out, an array of factors muddy the pitch even more: the persistence or otherwise of COVID-19; the continued level of production out the US Gulf; the pace of demand growth as different economies begin their recoveries at different times and at different rates; rescheduled terminal maintenance hitting LNG supply as we enter winter; a possible tightening of the shipping market if a surge of vessels head in for dry dock…as we move through the year, only one thing appears certain: nobody will get every call correct. Yard activity has been quiet, with no new orders placed this week.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Eastern Market
**Date:** 2020-04-09 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

MEG: Swap values edged up this week, following the crude jump with anticipation on production cuts. May CP was traded at $260ish, however refrained gains later this week amid uncertainties. As expected, C3/C4 spread has enlarged to $20, due to the robust prompt demand from India.  FOB discussions varied widely as bid idea at small premium while seller targets at double digits. The sole support on premium lies in the consistency of India purchase, which remains a question for forwarding months.   FAR EAST:  Earlier this week 23kt propane for 1h May delivery was traded at May FEI plus $13, which equates to May CP plus $20. Despite active bids at FEI plus teens in window, sellers are keener to sell cargos into China. As FEI/CP spread narrowed down within $10 only whilst landed price into China for May arrival at May CP plus low 40ish, weakening but still more economic.   Several Indian tenders were awarded with CFR price ranging from CP plus 40s to 80s reportedly, relating on exact laycan/laytime requests and other conditions. This week BPCL issued buy tender again to secure another 90k tones for May to early June delivery. Indonesia Pertamina was reported to award one evenly split cargo for May delivery in last tender, and this week issued new tender for another cargo in June delivery.   Deals into China recently are mainly driven by petchem feedstock demand. One Schina-based PDH closed their tender to secure 23kt propane delivered in last decade May. Meanwhile, at the time of writing, two more PDH users have tendered to buy total up-to two full propane cargos from mid-May to 1h June delivery.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2020-04-09 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS: Export cargos from the US continue, despite cratering worldwide oil demand. In fact we expect the exports to continue thru 2020 and April is on track to be the largest month so far this year. Ironically the falling oil demand has actually increased worldwide lpg demand due to refinery lpg being virtually eliminated. A twist few expected when the economy came to a halt a mere three weeks ago. With the demand side holding, the next question is on the supply.  Production had already decreased and many are now waiting to see if there will be a new OPEC which might include US producers. Propane inventories fell 156,000 barrels this week but still remain well above 2019 levels.

## 4. Sale and Purchase (S&P) & Corporate Activity
### Chartering Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2020-04-07 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

WEST: In the West this week, the market began fixing in for the first half of May, which cleared out much of the outstanding freight length remaining in April. Rates have remained fairly stable relative to the end of last week at around mid-to-high $80s Houston/Chiba. Despite generally stronger ideas from Owners, a couple of factors have prevented the market from running away. On one hand, more relet candidates have entered the market now that the market has shown signs of an uptick. On the other, the East has continued to come off while the West has remained stable, erasing the premium which has been seen East of Suez for a while now. Consequently, Owners are showing some bias towards sending ships West, while some traders have been heard in the market looking at fixing in in the MEG in order to relet in the West.  EAST: We have continued to see activity on shipping in the East this week, mainly on the back of Indian demand but also other requirements. Freight availability has remained long and as a consequence each deal has been done at a discount to the other. The fixing window is still very much concentrated around April dates, but as we start getting more acceptance dates for next month then discussions would move forward to May program. It is expected that Owners will direct more ships back West on the back of better freight markets there, and this could eventually turn things around on shipping here in the East as well. The product market is still in backwardation, so one can also expect to see more early month liftings in May which also positive freight.
