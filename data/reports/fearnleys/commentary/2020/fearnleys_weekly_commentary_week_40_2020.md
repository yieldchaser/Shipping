## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2020-09-30 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

As we publish the energy major Total is performing its remote investor day with a very central focus on the environment and a transition to a less carbon intensive future. At the same time prominent news stories portray that traders are also accelerating their transition to renewables.   It’s this transition that relevance to LNG really starts to take hold and should give some comfort to the average reader of this weekly publication - LNG currently has a very significant role to play. Global LNG demand might be taking a pause whilst celebrations are enjoyed for China’s Golden week but weather systems such as ‘La Nina’ are predicted to bring colder temperatures in due course adding to increasing market demand. On the flip side, supply is subject to significant interruptions across the global with the latest in Norway at the Hammerfest plant, thankfully no injuries, but production is not expected to return until November.  More specifically and to LNGC’s, interruptions, increased demand and illiquidity of vessels combines to create a very intriguing market, consequences could result in muted activity but could also cause significant opportunity for freight to rise to year highs as schedules change and ton time increases as vessel head further afield. Naysayers will point to high European storage levels and the lack of global industrial production as everyone tousles with the pandemic. It won’t change overnight and we don’t expect great changes in levels this week but next week may bring more clarity and improved owner returns. In the meantime we would like to wish all those who are celebrating Golden week a pleasant and restful break.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2020-09-28 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS A larger than expected build of 4.1 million barrels finally pushed US propane inventories above 100million barrels for the first time since 2015. With inventories at 102.0 million barrels Mt Belvieu was expected to weaken from above 50% WTI. This has not happened mainly due to the strength in international markets. This strength has partly been fueled by delays in USGC loadings caused by Tropical storm Laura and waiting time in the Panama Canal. Structural shifts in the forward curve in both NWE and FEI have strengthened prompt netback values and in turn US FOB buying ideas for November. This has caused the US FOB market to flip from well offered to well bid. Energizing FOB discussions and pushing bids up from a 5 handle to a 6 handle in a very short space of time. Going forward we believe US FOB values will continue to improve, even once the delays and bottlenecks have cleared.

In what we understand was a deal concluded a couple of months ago, Dynacom is reported to have purchased a nine year old Japanese owned/built VLCC for Usd 45 mill. This is as such the second VLCC of this age sold at more or less same levels so we can safely conclude that values have taken another tumble. Additional Suezmax and VLCC contracts are also being done at levels in line with expectations. This, together with dismal spot rates are certainly not lending support to any positive development for crude values. Following inspections by around 10 buyers, offers are invited on Monday for LR2 tanker CHAMPION PRINCESS built 2012 which, together with the Xihe tanker sell-off, will be the ones to watch in the near future.   The increased activity out of Brazil has seen Capesize earnings skyrocketing, the BCI has more than doubled in under 3 weeks. This marks another dramatic market move in a year that has been the most volatile ever for Capes. In general, we find fundamentals to be more than sound and expect it to be so for another year and a half at least. For the smaller segments things are also moving in the right direction, albeit slower. We see demand coming back by varying degrees in all major discharge regions (Europe, Japan, Korea, India, South East Asia), whereas China remains strong notwithstanding the last month and a half’s slowdown. Fresh YTD spot market highs for Supramax are highly likely during Q4, but we are less certain of that for Kamsarmax. Vessel deliveries will fall markedly the next year and a half which, coupled with a continued economic recovery and massive stimulus measures, could make for very interesting times ahead. The renewed dry optimism is evident in the very significant number of second-hand transactions every week. Values are at a robust floor and from here any movement should be north.
