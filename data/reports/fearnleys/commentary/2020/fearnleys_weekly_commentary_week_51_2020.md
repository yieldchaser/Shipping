## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2020-12-14 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The LNG market continues to stay boiling hot (ironically) as the Far East and the Middle-East delivered LNG prices hit new multiyear highs almost every day now, while short coverers and end-users are scrambling to find cargoes in the region. This in turn has caused the LNG shipping market to move from high-speed to hyperdrive, as every promptly available vessel gets taken in a heartbeat causing the global vessel availability to look very scarce indeed over the next month. Naturally, this tightness of available vessels have spiked up the charter rates, which have also reached levels that were last seen only during the peak winter of 2018. Furthermore, with new vessel requirements emerging this week to cover February liftings, we do expect this tightness to drag out a bit longer than we predicted just a few months back. That said, even if the first two months of 2021 look tight, we are still holding the view that vessel supply and demand will balance out before the end of Q1. On the newbuilding front, it was reported this week that Hyundai Heavy Industries have inked another four new LNG newbuilding contracts, two with Pan Ocean, one with Knutsen OAS and one with JP Morgan. All of these newbuilds are 174,000cbm and highly-equipped with XDF propulsion systems, and will be long-term chartered by Shell. With these new contracts, Shell’s total LNGC newbuilding tally stands at 18 modern XDF vessels, all being built by HHI.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2020-12-14 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS As the US market begins it wind down to the end of 2020, a number of big questions remain. First and foremost being will propane production remain at 2.3 million barrels per day as it has for most of the 4th quarter. If so, despite the huge export numbers which have become the norm,  there should be enough propane to sustain the exports, the domestic petchem market,  and the retail demand. The second big question is will freight rates remain at these near-record levels, and to this, we can answer yes. We do not see any end in sight to the Panama canal delays, the US export levels, and the intensive drydock schedule.  And finally, the last big question is will the world continue to need US exports, even at a higher price, and once again the answer is yes. Thus with positive answers to all of the above, we expect the arb to stay open and record exports to continue. In South America Brasil continues to be a regular spot buyer taking at least one spot VLGC per month, while Mexico has yet to appear as a winter buyer as we expect they will..

## 4. Sale and Purchase (S&P) & Corporate Activity
### SnP Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2020-12-16 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

There is some light at the end of the tunnel for tanker earnings and rates may have already bottomed for now as the delta in terms of tanker demand looks decidedly positive over the next few months. We expect a stronger rate comeback for 2nd half 2021 as the oil market is not out of the Covid woods just yet and it is likely to take some more time before oil demand recovers to levels where production may be sustainably and meaningfully increased. These expectations go a long way in explaining the lack of modern tankers changing hands for which the bid/ask spread has been consistently wide. However there are now unconfirmed reports that the Libyan state-owned company GNMTC is in the process of acquiring an Aframax newbuilding resale due for delivery ex Sumitomo in early 2021. This being a tender process we expect pricing to be firm but still it is expected to reaffirm that determined buyers of brand new tankers with a very specific buying requirement have to pay up and face sellers’ asking levels.    JP Morgan continues to be an active buyer of large bulkcarriers. Following its purchase in November of two 2016 JMU built Capesizes for Usd 38.9 mill, the company is now reported to have acquired one Capesize built 2011 and one Newcastlemax built 2017. The latter is Chinese built and scrubber fitted but in our books the price of region Usd 37.5 mill is fairly neutral as far as any scrubber premium goes. Maran is reported to have acquired a sister vessel from the same stable on similar terms. All in all, dry values are moving sideways across the board.
