### Capesize Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2020-07-13 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Still drifting slowly south on a combination of corrective sentiment and partly slow fundamentals. Iron ore prices are at year-high and miners are selling at capacity, however spot volumes concluded from both Brazil and West Australia are dominated by only 2 major exporters. Coal trades again showing signs of life, but millions of tons still remain onboard capes waiting for upto 2 months to discharge at Chinese ports for a mix of political and commercial reasons. Period interest remains considerable, but little or nothing concluded as paper-derived evaluations far from matching physical expectations.

## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2020-07-15 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The spot and shorter term LNG Shipping market held its ground this week after a slight increase in requirements seen in both basins.  Although the impact has been rather minimal, sentiments did improve as a handful of Charterers with short shipping positions during autumn-winter period were eager to secure a vessel earlier than usual.  Similarly, forward fixing levels for both Owners and Charterers are not aligned.  Ideas on floating storage plays for winter deliveries have also encouraged multi month discussions to resurfaced again as there could be some potential upside in a trade, storing now and selling into the later part of the year.   Market participants are also keenly awaiting news on the amount of US cargo cancellations for September as that would either increase or decrease demand for shipping.  Newbuilding prices remain unchanged and there were no further reports of any new large LNG carrier orders this week. With upcoming LNG projects currently in yard discussions, we could see a spur in newbuilding orders over the second half of 2020.

It is estimated now that Marcus Hook exports are back up to the equivalent of 10/11 or so VLGC's/ month. That said, US traders are still feeling the covid induced demand lapse in Asia -however economics are decent enough to stave off any cancelation rumblings, especially when supported by large product builds and the subsequent hope of cheaper Mt. Belvieu.

We have a strong start to the second half of the year for all dry segments and the bottom for period rates and asset values have likely passed for all segments and vintages. We now have a robust floor on values and the dry S&P activity is increasingly on fire. Kamsarmax and Supra-/Ultramax aged eight to twelve years are in particular demand. Following inspections by nine buyers, the Japanese controlled Kamsarmax KM YOKOHOMA built 2011 Sanoyas was promptly sold at region USD 15m, levels which fall in line with expectations. BW Dry’s recent deal on subjects for the sale of its three ten year old; Japanese built Kamsarmax BW ACORN, BW BARLEY and BW EINKORN failed to materialize, and they were instead quickly sold to new buyers this week. We understand Centrofin is the buyer of the latter two and Orion the buyer of BW ACORN. This vessel was in fact contracted as newbuilding by Orion and sold to BW Dry in 2018.   Any movement in dry values will be north in the near to medium term and we shall soon be seeing more modern tonnage being offered for sale.   It is time-out in the tanker S&P market. Values remain under pressure and there is no sense of urgency to acquire assets unless a particularly attractive deal comes along.
