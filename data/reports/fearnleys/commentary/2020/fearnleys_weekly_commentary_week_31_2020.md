### Suezmax Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2020-07-27 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Suezmax rate-support has been underpinned by a mixture of sentiment and stresses to the list. Whilst general enquiry levels have suffered due to production constraints, resistance has been established by dint of many of the modern ships on the list being in the hands of fewer owners. When continued delays in China are added to the mix, the net affect of this owner-concentration is that they are more willing to test the market upwards and less willing to give ground when market activity subsides. This pattern is likely to be repeated as charterers bite deeper into the second decade.

## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2020-07-28 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Voyage economics remain broadly flat this week, but the sense of a firming shipping market is growing. Prompt business is still dominated by spot cargoes and tender-driven enquiries, and this continues to churn through the availability list without reducing it much. Looking ahead though, sublets are being withdrawn and the bid / offer spread is finally converging, giving hope to owners seeking to salvage something of the year. It's now a question of timing: the best cargo economics span roughly October to December, but owners are reluctant to risk this much idle time prior to delivery, so few multimonth fixtures have been concluded. As the weeks roll on and owners get more visibility of their fleet schedules, we expect a flurry of these fixtures. This has not been a normal year, but despite the disruption of recent months it's reassuring to see the familiar dance of owners and charterers as we look towards winter. No newbuildings reported this week and prices remain flat.

## 4. Sale and Purchase (S&P) & Corporate Activity
### Chartering Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2020-07-29 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

EAST As the supply and demand for spot open tonnage versus available cargoes for current fixing window are still very much in imbalance, the bull run that we have witnessed on freight during the last couple of weeks has continued, and Baltic has just breached USD 60 Ras Tanura/Chiba for the first time since April. At time of writing, there are reportedly still uncovered shipping requirements in August, while remaining available ships are few (if any at all). This will continue to add pressure on freight rates. However, as the fixing window is currently quite far ahead in time, there is also an extra risk of ships running late for their laycans due to uncertain itineraries, and we might see ships come back open again at a later stage.  WEST Freight rates have strengthened again in the West this week as the fixing window progresses further into September. However, there are questions about how much longer this trend can continue. On one hand, traders argue that arb economics are unsupportive of freight at such high levels and therefore something will have to give. On the other, shipping still seems to be in short supply when considering the balance of the fleet across East and West of Suez. Regardless, most  charterers still have some time to determine whether to cancel FOBs or fix in. Combined with the fact that Singapore is off on Friday, this could mean a quiet end to the week.
