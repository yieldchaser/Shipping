## 2. Tanker Sector (Crude & Products)
### VLCC Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2020-03-31 | **Subtype:** Tank Weekly Comment | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The VLCC market continues at full speed ahead. MEG/China now done a couple times at W210, one by a Shell relet named "Advantage Value, which couldn't be more describing in the current climate (W220 also rumoured done, but not confirmed). Red Sea runs have been repeatedly done at W250. The Atlantic has followed suit, with USG exports back in play in earnest, paying upwards toward $20 mill, in addition to plentiful Wafr- and Brazil east cargoes - and as Suezmax rates are approaching that paid to its large sisters there is nowhere to "hide". The MEG position list is tight as a ducks ass, by and large "cornered" by traders and oil companies having taken ships on period charter, inadvertently propelling rates higher by default.  Delays on the discharge end in the far east is thinning the list further. The MEG fixture count has passed 100 for April, but March ended in excess of 150 and with every man to the (oil)pumps still, there is plenty left that needs covering. So onwards and upwards still it seems.

## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2020-03-31 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The volatility in energy markets around the world is keeping traders busy as they try to capitalise on an open arbitrage or other fluctuations in product prices. This is in turn leading to a “stop/start” LNG shipping market, where market participants are constantly evaluating their options to either add tonnage or sublet depending on the latest price movements and trading opportunities. The end of last week saw positive momentum in the shipping market, but as the arbitrage closed so did charterers interest in tonnage and activity quietened down again. Subsequently, rates have come off a little from last week. Charterers appear to be looking at multi-month business a little earlier this year than last, although competition between owners remain strong. No new orders or 2nd hand transactions since last report.    We hope all are in good health during this challenging time. Stay safe!

FAR EAST:  The week is dominant by tenders from India and Indonesia. Indonesia Pertamina also tendered to buy one equal ratio cargos for 2h Apr or May arrival.  On Thursday one parcel of 23k propane for 1h May delivery was traded at May FEI plus $8 on window, which equates to May CP plus high teens.  Late last week a couple of propane cargos were sold at May CP plus 40ish into China. But this week sellers target for higher level, as other market demand spiked up with anticipation for less supply on May arrival due to some cancellations for Apr liftings ex US.

## 4. Sale and Purchase (S&P) & Corporate Activity
### SnP Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2020-04-01 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

As evidenced by the flurry of period fixtures being reported across the board, the strong rates in the tanker markets see owners striving to lock in  tonnage on lucrative long term charters.  Activity in the S&P markets remain subdued to say the least.  Buying interest remains in principle high for older crude carriers able to offer prompt inspection and delivery, however the hurdles very much persist for any sale discussion to take off.  In terms of concluded business, following inspections by up-to eight buyers only last week the 2005 built VLCC YUGAWASAN has now been sold to Greek buyers at USD 33.3m. The vessel is SS due in August and price achieved was more or less in line with expectations.  We expect to see more activity in the sector with another Japanese controlled 2005 built unit being placed for sale this week.  Owners are inviting inspections in Singapore mid April where a similarly large number of prospective buyers is expected to inspect.  However it remains to be seen how the new raft of restrictive measures announced in Singapore today will impact inspections and whether all necessary arrangements can be taken at this vital port.  A steady trickle of bulker transactions are still being done, however until we see tangible deals for larger and more modern vessels the jury is still very much out as to where values are heading.

### Chartering Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2020-03-31 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

WEST:  Looking at Western positions lists, it becomes clear that different owners have taken different strategies with regards to the short term. Some have fixed out their tonnage quickly, while others have bet that there should be more uncovered cargoes in April which are waiting in the wings. The result is that tonnage is generally controlled by a smaller number of players. There is little in the way of relets. Given limited liquidity in the freight market, this has meant that rates discussions of late have been very volatile, and it is possible some of the next few fixtures could be stronger, subject to cancellations ex USG.   Looking further ahead, strong Indian demand and rumours of supply bottlenecks could mean that some US origin cargoes head towards India. This should be good for the tonne-mile and supportive of freight in the slightly longer term if so.  EAST: Short term residential demand for LPG into India remains strong, and hence we have seen more requirements on freight and delivered cargoes into India. The activity level remains on the active side with fixing mainly focused around last decade of April, but despite a healthy number of inquiries, we are still left with significant length on shipping and hence the Baltic is yet to find a floor. We expect the current Baltic premium to the West to narrow or even out and it will be interesting to keep an eye on where Owners choose to send their ships going forward.
