## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2020-10-07 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Celebrations for China’s Golden Week continue, and while some may have expected a pause in the market instead, a steady flow of requirements mainly in the East has kept Owners busy and we are seeing rate levels reaching new highs this week. Availability has tightened across all basins, also in the Atlantic where activity was somewhat muted following supply disruptions from Hammerfest and possible weather related delays in US Gulf liftings due to another hurricane approaching.  Looking ahead, we see risk in potential sublet availability being withdrawn and with the expected restart of Hammerfest, US Gulf back to “normal”, we may see additional tightness in the Atlantic basin. As a consequence, Charterers may have to look at positioning tonnage from the East and coupled with a winter market approaching – it may result in further spot charter rate increases, which should not be a big surprise as it is seasonally “normal” this time of the year.  There were no new LNGC orders reported this week.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2020-10-07 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS   A draw of 100,000bbls left US propane inventories effectively flat at 101.842mmbbls.  Market participants were anticipating anywhere from a 1mmbbl draw to a 1mmbbl increase.  Domestic US propane market values moved up from a close on Monday of 50.125cts/gal, to a close of 51.333cts/gal on Tuesday and then closed Wednesday at 50.500cts/gal – leaving the pricing structure, while still mildly positive, rather unchanged as of midweek.  Export volumes are deemed to be stronger, but Hurricane Delta now threatens the US Gulf Coast and in one case a rather precautionary Force Majeure was declared for a cargo which was slated to load on the weekend.  Depending on the actual path taken by the (now Category 2) storm, a temporary closure of the Houston Ship Channel is presently unlikely.  Crude oil prices are up at present on oil shutdowns in the US Gulf, as almost 1.5mmbbls/day of production has been shuttered.  Meanwhile, arbs for Q4 have seen significant improvement as Asian values extended gains, propping up the likelihood that the West-to-East trade will be bolstered.  Spot terminal fees have adjusted as a result and have risen from a last-done level of 6.5cts/gal (for Oct) to present levels of around 9.0 cts/gal, though the results of a UGSC tender will likely set the market
