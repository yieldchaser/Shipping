## 2. Tanker Sector (Crude & Products)
### VLCC Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2020-08-24 | **Subtype:** Tank Weekly Comment | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The tanker market is in the August summer doldrums. Tonnage remains plentiful all over, and although some resistance is evident, rates for modern unrestricted ships are slowly sliding towards the W30 mark MEG/eastbound, with Wafr trailing closely behind. To add insult to injury bunker prices are up overnight. Despite strengthening oil prices, lack of oil demand is still noticeable. Many regular VLCC charterers are opting for smaller liftings, the likes of Suezmaxes, despite paying higher freights per barrel. An increased interest in floating storage could become a saving grace, suggesting land based storage capacity is becoming strained, although one school of thought propound it’s just a zero game with ships previously fixed at higher rates being replaced.

## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2020-08-26 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

It has been a rather turbulent week in the LNG market, as commodity prices have seen almost roller-coasterly up and down movements, spot shipping rates have stormed upwards and last, but unfortunately not the least, there is a major hurricane (Hurricane Laura) about to make a landfall near Texas-Louisina coaster border, consequently threatening to shut down a chunk of US LNG production at least temporarily. Spot shipping market has remained active, mainly driven by multiple spot requirements from the East of Suez, resulting into a rather slim prompt tonnage availability, quite surprisingly for this time of the year. That said, the West of Suez have also had its’ fair share of interest for tonnage, but notably with delivery windows a bit further ahead, perhaps in the hopes of capturing the best potential contango window before the Northern Hemisphere winter starts. The shipping market definitely feels strongly buoyant in the near-term, but there is still some uncertainty whether this heightened sentiment can be supported over the shoulder months, which only time will tell for certain. From the newbuilding front, it has been reported that Smart LNG has finalized agreements to order additional ten Arc7 LNG carriers from Zvezda Shipbuilding Complex in Russia, for the upcoming Arctic LNG 2 project. Furthermore, there are also quite a few S&P transactions circulating in the market.

FAR EAST: A couple of propane parcels from 2h Sep to 1h Oct delivery have been traded this week.  2h Sep 23/0 was done at Sep CP minus high single digit, equates to Sep FEI minus mid high teens. And similar cargo for 1h Oct was reported done at Oct FEI minus high single digit. Despite that some US loadings are delayed due to the hurricane, FAR EAST price stabilized and even softened later this week. One of the reasons being is the overlength for 2h Sep arrival cargos, against thin buying interests due to high inventory. Indian IOC issued tender to purchase two evenly split cargos for 2nd and 3rd decade Oct delivery, which is yet to close.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2020-08-26 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS: Discussion this week have been centred around Hurricane Laura which made landfall Wednesday evening between Texas and Louisiana. From reports this morning it seems the hurricane bypassed Houston and the ship channel. Enterprise expect 1.5 days of delay due to ship channel closures. Nederland, which was in the path of the storm has avoided major damage and, after a coast guard survey, expect 1 day of delay. We expect this news to assuage fears of long delays. The 1.5 million barrel build in US inventories has now pushed stocks up to 90.8 million barrels. With domestic production stronger than originally expected, traders believe there will be enough product to sustain winter demand and exports. The more pressing question in today’s market is when will we start to see global winter demand?

### Chartering Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2020-08-24 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

WEST: The tug of war between Charterers and Owners has continued this week, with freight remaining high despite unfavourable arb economics. At the end of last week a couple of deals were done around Houston/Chiba USD 90 (+/-) but once those ships cleared, owners have largely been able to dictate the pace. Given that there are currently no relets to compete with, a number of ships were put on subs at mid $90s Houston/Chiba. With that said, some Charterers are still deciding whether it would be better to cancel or change the size of their liftings given the current trading environment. In addition, terminals in the US seem to be withholding FOB cargoes for sale in September until Hurricane Laura makes landfall.  Looking ahead into early October, the VLGC market in the West remains tight. However, with the West now back at a premium to the East and with the possibility that traders could cancel liftings to relet at strong levels, this could change quickly unless the arb improves.  EAST: The shipping market in the East has been fairly inactive this week. One late running ship for an Indian cargo created some attention, and there are possibly a couple of uncovered stems shown to the market for 2H September, but otherwise not much else to report. The increased activity seen ex US recently could potentially slow down a falling Baltic, as Owners are likely to direct more ships back West if this continues. But at the same time if arb economics remains poor then this shouldn’t necessarily have any added effect to the tonnage availability in the east. Going forward 1H September seems pretty much done on shipping in the Middle East, and next focus is from 2H September onwards.
