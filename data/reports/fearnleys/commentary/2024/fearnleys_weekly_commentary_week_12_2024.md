As we approach the end of Q1 2024, the LNG shipping market maintains a steady course, a typical scenario for this time of year characterized by lower demand and moderated LNG prices. **Date:** 2024-03-21 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS Mont Belvieu propane values, which remain remarkably resilient, continue to be buoyed by gains being recorded in the crude arena, and which thus have allowed propane prices to continue on an upward trajectory.  This is despite reduced weather-related demand across North America, effectively flat petrochemical demand, as well as the relationship to crude (which presently stands at ~41%), and that stock levels are not only sufficient, but are presently calculated to rise to the mid 90mmbbl level by around the first week of October.  Indeed the weekly export volumes have been robust, but on balance – and over the coming inventory build season - there is not a tremendous amount of upside to existing capacity levels.  Of course the lifting of the daylight restrictions on the HSC has had a positive impact, but some of those efficiency gains are lost to the regular calling of larger and larger VLGCs.  Meanwhile, the EIA yesterday reported a nearly flat (+300k bbls) propane stock level for the week ending 15 March, bringing total overall primary inventories in the U.S. to 52.2mmbbls.  This is 89% of the total recorded a year ago, and 155% of the total seen two years ago.  If only average weekly gains are achieved over the course of the next 6+ months, a ~95mmbbl inventory level will have been achieved.  Despite periodic arguments to the contrary, all signs point to a normal build season.  As for the current spot market, the March program is effectively concluded though a handful of parcels remain on offer in April – mostly at around the 8cpg level.  May cargoes and demand levels are beginning to be revealed, but terminal fees are expected to be a penny or so lower.
