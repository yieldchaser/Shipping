## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2024-07-03 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

As we close out Q2, charter rates have rallied in the Atlantic especially for 2-strokes, although Owners across all propulsion types are enjoying improved levels and interest. Yet this week, the market seems more divided as some question the longevity of this early summer rally. In recent years, this strength had led to numerous multi-month fixtures being concluded, yet so far we haven’t seen the same dynamic. At the time of writing, there appears to be a slight rebalancing, with a little less activity and some more potential tonnage emerging. The delta between the Atlantic and the Pacific is historically high and is encouraging 174,000m3 tonnage to ignore pacific requirements and head West without delay. Historically, in this instance vessels located in the Pacific become the focus of Atlantic requirements and there is a balancing effect, yet it is quite clear that currently without sufficient demand to head back to the Pacific region the economics don’t stack up, thus we continue to see a price disconnect between the two basins. How the rest of 2024 will play out is a very intriguing discussion. Cargo pricing suggests limited contango or arbitrage and although we can expect a busy few months, it is not straightforward to anticipate how high rates will go. With both canals essentially out of action, there is scope for volatility yet cargo demand appears to surface on a very prompt basis. Perhaps unsurprisingly given these uncertainties, the LNG freight market is unlikely to drop as fast or as far as some may predict, and perhaps we will see a broadly flat market; however, as winter nears this dynamic will be tested for sure.   Looking at asset values, newbuilding prices remain stable whilst second-hand interest remains very robust.
