### Panamax Weekly Comment
**Date:** 2024-11-20 | **Subtype:** Dry Bulk Weekly Comment | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The Panamax market faced sustained headwinds this week, marked by bearish sentiment and declining rates as both the Atlantic and Pacific basins struggled with weak fundamentals. In the Atlantic, a lack of fresh demand, especially from EC South America and the US Gulf, led to an oversupply of tonnage, forcing owners to accept lower rates to secure cover. Baltic activity for December provided some cargo, but this was not enough to counterbalance the broader market weakness. The Pacific saw slightly better activity, with a steady flow of fresh demand from Australia offering some relief; however, the growing tonnage count prevented any meaningful recovery, keeping rates under pressure. Global congestion levels on the loading side are at their lowest since the start of 2023, while discharging congestion has risen slightly but remains below average. Despite coal volumes showing a significant year-on-year increase, the need for longer-haul voyages continues to weigh on the market. Seasonal trends hint at a potential bounce, but the overall picture remains subdued, and any substantial rally is unlikely before February when the Brazilian soybean season - forecasted to be strong - could inject much-needed demand into the market. Until then, the outlook remains challenging, with limited support expected in the coming weeks.

## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2024-11-21 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

European gas prices surged at the end of last week on the back of renewed concerns around Russian supply disruption, making headlines in mainstream media with recent winters still fresh in the region’s memory. Coupled with a sharp decline in JKM, Europe has rubber stamped its position as the premium destination for U.S. LNG in the near term, and now cold weather will increase drawdown on storages. The reality for LNG shipping this winter is starkly different, as bolstered regasification capacity and phenomenal growth in vessel supply this year fail to alleviate the bearish tonne-mile scenario brought by heavily-favoured intra-basin trade. Combined with no contango to support tonne-time, there really hasn’t been much to alleviate this ongoing weak freight demand scenario. Competition for uncovered December shipping requirements in the Atlantic has hence been fierce, with the slight gains on freight of last week erased in a flash. The Pacific continues to see a steady flow of requirements with charter rates for modern tonnage aligning with those in the West. There are also certain requirements being worked with more vintage sizes although the surplus of early spec ST and TFDE tonnage is piling pressure on rates, with fixing levels forced below OPEX in some cases.  Naturally, at this point in the cycle there will be discussions on laying older ships up or scrappage. The announcement by a Korean Ship Owner of the scrapping of four 25-year old steamships is likely to be the start of a trickle of such deals rather than a surge, although the prospect of a prolonged lay-up and the evolving regulatory framework may accelerate these decisions.
