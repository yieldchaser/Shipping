## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2024-01-18 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The LNG shipping market has continued to soften, despite the gradual escalation in the Red Sea now reaching a critical point with Qatari vessels diverting around the Cape of Good Hope. There is still more activity East of Suez than West, although firm requirements are now few and far between in either basin and even tentative enquiries are further out in late-February and beyond. And while demand remains muted, the supply side of the equation hasn’t helped either, with the availability list growing for spot and term tonnage. In these circumstances, the few charterers that are in the market have been pressing for less-than-round-trip economics and in some cases, they have been successful. At this point in the annual cycle, headline rates can be misleading as “round-trip equivalent” rates can take (and are taking) a battering. There has not yet been a material impact on the spot market from the situation in the Red Sea, but if the Qatari fleet goes the long way around for a sustained period, this should absorb at least some excess tonnage and support freight rates. The multi-month market is also under serious pressure, and although there are some discussions on several vessels there hasn’t been a surge of charterers for such opportunities. There haven’t been any fresh newbuilding orders placed this week, but the Qatar expansion project has exercised a further 15 slots.
