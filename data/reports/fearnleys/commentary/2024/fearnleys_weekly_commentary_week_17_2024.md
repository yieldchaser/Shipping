## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2024-04-25 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

It has been a busy week in LNG shipping, with a handful of spot requirements, term requirements and cargo tenders being worked in the last few days. Spot rates have taken a slight dip in the Atlantic basin, due to some competitive sublet tonnage being offered at lower levels than a week ago, but in the Pacific, it has been a different story with charterers facing firmer rate levels especially for 2H May / 1H June cargoes. There are no drastic changes on the overall sentiment however, and we believe the spot market will remain rather stable for the weeks to come. There is some good news in Freeport ramping up production again, which could lead to some of the prompt tonnage disappearing in the US Gulf, although set against this is the news that Hammerfest has had another outage which may free up a few prompt vessels in Europe. The term market, or more specifically the 1-year TC market, has definitely been the focus for many this week and rates seem to be firming, as more charterers are looking to strengthen their portfolio over the winter, especially with modern 2-stroke tonnage. In the newbuilding market, although no new orders this week, yards are keeping busy with their existing building schedules.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2024-04-25 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS The EIA reported a +1.00mmbbl build in overall US propane inventory levels, which is about half of what most in the industry had anticipated and which left total stocks at 56.70mmbbls as of 19 April.  This is roughly 97% of last year and 145% of two years ago.  Even so, many continue to predict that by the first week of October propane stocks will have reached the mid/upper 90mmbbl range, with some predictions reaching up to (and in some cases beyond) 100mmbbls as of that timeframe.  Meanwhile, MB propane prices closed up slightly yesterday, likely because US crude inventory draws of 6.3mmbbls gave cause for a bit of a runup in WTI values, though news of large-scale Russian crude production and export volumes tampered sentiments.  The terminal fee market on the Gulf Coast, however, remains robust.  A ~10cpg May deal was believed to have been fixed and as news that the terminals are expected to be tight in June made the rounds, asking levels for June moved upwards to nothing less than double-digits.  This is partially due to the fact that the shipping sector is well-balanced for the moment and therefore unable to grab a share of the slowly rising US/Far East arb.  NWE As of mid week, CIF ARA propane values are hanging around $505 /metric ton, with the large-cargo propane market  assessed at -$7/mt to May quotes, The propane/naphtha spread however, is assessed at -182pmt and continues to attract significant quantities of us LPG (an expected 520,000 tons for April) - well above the Q1 average of 430,000 tons, which has more than made up for reduced imports from the North Sea and Ust Luga, which remain hampered by maintenance and the enduring Russia and Ukrainian conflict, respectively.
