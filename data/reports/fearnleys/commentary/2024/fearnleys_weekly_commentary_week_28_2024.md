### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2024-07-11 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS The biggest news in July has been the effects on the US Gulf Coast LPG terminals after Hurricane Beryl made landfall early Monday morning.  The eye of the storm hit just south of Freeport at Matagorda Island, but the counterclockwise rotation of the storm absolutely pummeled the City of Houston where more than 1m people remain without electricity or connectivity.  All of the terminals were shut down at some point, though Energy Transfer’s Nederland facility escaped the brunt of the high winds and rain.  Enterprise and Targa – whose terminals are positioned along the Houston Ship Channel – suffered power outages and were out of commission for a period which resulted in the declaration of Force Majeure.  While both facilities are now operational, delays of around 3 and 6 days (respectively) are being reported.  Meanwhile, the P66 terminal at Freeport, TX – which was nearest to Beryl’s landfall location – is indicating today (Thu) that power has been restored and the Port of Freeport is technically open but there remain draft and daylight restrictions.  Additional assessments are pending today.  As to the market itself, Mont Belvieu propane values remain in the low 40-percentile range against WTI, which is very near to the average established for 2024 but some 5% above the 2023 average of ~38%.  Flat prices today are in the low 81cpg range, so approximately a nickel below the start of July.  Therefore, there is a lingering sentiment that MB propane has potential downside – particularly as demand levels during the summer months are traditionally lower.  Terminal fees, meanwhile, have spiked hard.  There were transactions at the end of June in the low teens, but since then – and so far in July – offers have been recorded at the mid-teens, the upper teens, and as of this writing offers are known to exist at 20cpg (and even above).
