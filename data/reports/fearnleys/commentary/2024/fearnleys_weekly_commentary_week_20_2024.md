## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2024-05-15 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

The market should be in the doldrums right now, but it’s hard to see fixtures on modern tonnage concluding at levels much below where we are today. The fact that owners have been able to protect their Round Trip (RT) econs in two out of three basins, as well as the hire element of the Ballast Bonus (BB) in all of them, is impressive. This is partly due to the high percentage of vessels controlled by portfolios at the moment (there’s a floor, below which traders won’t let the ships go) but it’s also the result of the tangible pressure that the multitude of geopolitical events are placing on the LNG market in general. Rain this week may presage the return of the Panama Canal, but we still have no obvious resolution that will see the Suez return to LNG life. As we move into a Q3 front month tomorrow, the risk of a logistically constrained winter may start to play more heavily on sentiment. Have we found the bottom of the market? As ever this is hard to say. Is there more risk to the upside than downside? We believe so.  No newbuilds were ordered this week but for most, given the recent spate of Qatari headlines, this will be a relief.

### Gas Market Weekly Comment - Western Market
**Date:** 2024-05-16 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

AMERICAS WTI values have become a bit range-bound, and since the start of May have remained in the $78-79/bbl range, while – simultaneously – MB propane have drifted from 74.00cts/gal on 1 May to current levels in the low 69.00cts/gal range.  This has resulted in a modestly declining c3/WTI ratio which has fallen from 39% to 36% currently.  There remains a lingering sentiment that MB propane prices can – and likely will – continue to soften, perhaps to the low 30-percentile range, but much will depend on the rate at which inventories grow.  To that, the EIA reported a 2.9mmbbl increase in overall domestic US propane stocks for the week ending 10 May which brought the total volume of primary stocks to 62mmbbls – or 97% of the same time last year.  The build was within the anticipated tolerances, but on the higher end of the expected range.  Meanwhile, the struggle between freight rates and terminal fees seems to have tilted in favor of shipowners.  US/FarEast freight rates are now ~$145/mt (up from the low $130’s/mt on 1 May), where – conversely – notional terminal fees have declined from asking-levels of as high as 13cts/gal to more modest (though still) double-digit levels.  Bidders are squarely in the single-digits as of this writing.  NWE Large cargo demand in Europe has re-emerged slightly as the widening LST/FEI arbitrage has attracted US LPG cargoes to the Far East versus Europe, but a decreasing propane naphtha spread (off $20/mt since the start of May) will likely keep demand at bay.   At present, it is predicted that Europe will have imported ~410,000mt of LPG in May, off 40kt from that of the month prior. However, some expect that import volumes will increase as Auction fees paid at the Panama Canal have shot up, thus incentivizing US cargoes back toward NWE.   So, the market is in a period of competing factors but overall NWE prices have gained some ground this week and the region remains well supplied and perhaps angled toward being long when accounting for logistical pain points related to the US / Far East trade.
