## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2024-10-16 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Sustained liquidity has contributed to a further reduction in spot charter rates in both basins this week. Oversupply of tonnage is felt most heavily in the Atlantic despite numerous requirements within the first half November window.    We expect 2024 to be a record-breaking year for the number of spot fixtures; however, the active start of the year has tailed off and the number of spot fixtures continues to slide each month. In part, this is due to a soft LNG market that is seeing tendered cargoes hoovered up by those with length in their portfolios, resulting in few firm requirements. The opportunities that do emerge are fought over keenly, and this trend doesn’t look like easing in the near future: rumours of sub-fixtures below all-time-lows for 2-strokes circulate at the time of writing.   There has also been further correction in the Pacific where competition for spot employment remains fierce. One sub-fixture reported on modern tonnage sees levels tracking the decline observed in the West.    The short- to mid-term period space has remained quiet with limited transactions seen since August. Further delays to two projects that had been expected to deliver first cargoes to long-term off-takers by the start of 2025 has not helped to alleviate any of this pressure. In addition to independent Owners, there remain a good number of sub-let tonnage over the next 12 to 24 months before the market is expected to tighten through 2027 and beyond.
