## 3. Gas & LNG Markets
### LNG Market Report
**Date:** 2026-09-30 | **Subtype:** Market Commentary | **Desk:** Fearnleys Research

Growing Sentiment – A False Start?  Last week’s update spoke of growing sentiment and upward pressure on spot rates due to an influx of competing requirements for 2H October laycans - this had perhaps caught some off guard as the underlying trade and freight fundamentals were unchanged. While we have seen improved expectations on both the bid and the offer, fixing activity and rate levels have not experienced the spike that last week’s activity perhaps suggested. Moreover, this disconnect between sentiment and fundamentals has caused some wide disparities in the bid/offer and this in itself has acted as a brake on fixing.  Availability remains healthy in the Atlantic, and with several active requirements being sold FOB and thus withdrawn in quick succession gave the remaining Charterers in the market the breathing room. The bullish offers seen at the start of the week look to be somewhat out of place now the dust has settled and the fundamentals are back to the fore.  The presence of several requirements loading in less active regions that are commanding a premium has further blurred the picture. Particularly in the East, fixing levels for Qalhat and Kitimat help to bolster the benchmark, but are not necessarily reflective of standard Aus-JKTC voyages on prompt ships, rates for which remain subdued. In addition, as owners of 2-stroke tonnage look to reposition to the Atlantic or retain length for winter, the delta between rates for modern vessels and TFDEs is widening.   High LNG prices are continuing to put owners of idle tonnage under pressure and is incentivising sharp offers especially on TFDEs. Conversely, with eyes on the arb and demand for worldwide redelivery increasing, the risk associated with surrendering a ship for well into the winter period is being priced in at a premium.  Within the “hidden market” of the Arabian Gulf, we are seeing a significant increase in Hormuz transits and a larger proportion from ships that have been recently chartered-in. As attractive cargo econs drive charter demand and the hint of some pressure being taken out of the region with regards LNG transits, it seems a growing number of owners are willing to engage in Middle East trade, with earnings reportedly being multiple times higher than open market spot rates.
