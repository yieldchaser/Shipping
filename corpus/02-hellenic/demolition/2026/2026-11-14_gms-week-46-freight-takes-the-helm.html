GMS Week 46 – FREIGHT TAKES THE HELM

Hellenic Shipping News  |  14 November 2026  |  https://www.hellenicshippingnews.com/gms-week-46-freight-takes-the-helm

Another week has passed without a meaningful change in global ship recycling conditions,although the trading market has strengthened its position once again. Dry freight has continued its recovery, crude oil has softened further and regional steel prices have offered some encouragement. Recycling indications remain unchanged across all four destinations, with healthy buying interest still frustrated by the persistent shortage of available candidates. Owners continue to enjoy worthwhile trading alternatives, leaving recyclers with limited opportunities to convert their appetite into fresh business.

Dry freight provided the week’s clearest movement. The Baltic Dry Index advanced to 3,020 on Thursday, November 12, from 2,740 a week earlier, extending its recovery to seven consecutive sessions. Capesizes strengthened to 4,836, Panamaxes reached 2,117 and Supramaxes improved to 1,831. The broad-based improvement has reinforced employment prospects for ageing bulk carriers, reducing the immediate incentive for owners to consider recycling. For buyers awaiting a sustained freight correction, the market has once again moved in the opposite direction.

Oil prices continued to retreat, with Brent trading around USD 95.50 per barrel on Friday and WTI near USD 88. Expectations of reduced military escalation in the Middle East have weighed on crude, although continuing attacks on shipping and energy infrastructure demonstrate that regional security risks remain unresolved. Lower energy costs may offer some relief to recycling operations and steel production, but uncertain trading conditions continue to influence tanker employment and owners’ disposal decisions.

Steel markets offered a somewhat firmer picture. Bangladesh plate strengthened during the week, while Pakistan recovered modestly from its recent correction. Indian steel also improved towards Friday, although Chinese quotations remained unchanged. These movements provide some welcome support to recycler margins without creating a compelling case for higher vessel bids. Currency conditions remain particularly important in Bangladesh, where Dollar appreciation continues to complicate purchasing calculations despite central bank intervention.

Physical waterfront activity also continued, although fresh transactions remain limited. Chattogram recorded further deliveries and smaller vessel arrivals, while Alang received another chemical tanker and updated the delivery status of previously arrived tonnage. Gadani reported no new movements. These developments reflect the progression of earlier purchases rather than a significant replenishment of the recycling sales pipeline.

Bangladesh continues to lead conventional buying sentiment, supported by improving seasonal conditions and stronger local steel. India retains its specialist advantages despite regulatory uncertainty, while Pakistan remains competitive but measured. Turkey continues to operate selectively amid mixed steel-market developments, including disruption at a major domestic mill.

The overall market remains stable, with no major change in its underlying commercial direction. Buyers are maintaining their positions, but stronger freight has once again demonstrated why owners remain reluctant to release ageing vessels. Steel may be providing some encouragement, yet profitable trading continues to offer a more persuasive alternative. For now, the beaches have the interest and the capacity. The trading market still has the upper hand.
