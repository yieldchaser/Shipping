MMI Daily Iron Ore Index Report September 30 2026

Hellenic Shipping News  |  30 September 2026  |  https://www.hellenicshippingnews.com/mmi-daily-iron-ore-index-report-september-30-2026

Today’s prices and today’s data point in opposite directions. On prices: the DCE most-traded contract reclaimed 700 at 702.5yuan/mt (+0.50%), Qingdao port spot rose 2 yuan across the board except for flat IOCJ, and the port spot indices had the 65% up 2 yuan, the 61% up 1 and the 58% unchanged. On data: daily average hot metal across 242 mills fell to 2.3882 million mt (-6,100 mt WoW), with the blast furnace operating rate at 88.57% and capacity utilisation at 88.15%. Laying out the full path makes the direction much clearer: 2.4080 (Sep 2 peak) → 2.4028 → 2.3991 → 2.3943 → 2.3882 million mt — four consecutive weekly declines, 19,800 mt off the peak, with the last three drops widening from 3,700 to 4,800 to 6,100 mt. Demand is not bottoming; the contraction is accelerating. More important, SMM has now given explicit forward guidance: with mills loss-making, some report they will draw up maintenance plans in early October and overall hot metal output may fall markedly. Combined with maintenance losses projected to reach 1.6839 million mt next week (a further +55,100 mt), the first post-holiday hot metal print could well be the largest decline of this cycle. Margins move the same way: the average imported ore loss widened from 5.08 to 6.12 yuan/mt, and SMM expects further widening. One basis tension should be stated plainly, though — that table references Sep 29 spot and freight, whereas on Sep 30 port spot recovered and freight fell another 4–6%, both of which mechanically repair margins; the first print after the holiday could recover some ground. Two structural shifts are worth recording. First, freight’s accelerating collapse: C3 has slid from 43.06 to 40.26 and C5 from 16.36 to 14.13, two-day falls of 6.5% and 13.6%. The support argument that high Brazilian freight was curbing shipments is now void, and the room for Brazilian supply to recover only grows. Second, coke supply is recovering: coke oven capacity utilisation rose to 75.0% this week (+2.3 ppt) and Shanxi to 72.7% (+2.8 ppt) — the practical precondition for the rumoured first round of price cuts, and currently the only thread pointing to demand repair. On balance, the pre-holiday bounce is sentiment repair and does not change a soft and choppy picture. The first trading week of October turns on two things: how large the hot metal decline actually is, and whether the coke cut lands. The first sets the slope of the decline; the second decides whether it has an end.
