# API: The Golden Year

Since 1956, industry experts have been gathering biennially for the API Tanker Conference. This year's conference entitled, "Natural Disaster Response and Recovery" featured several well-appointed panels. This topic is especially fitting as many shipping, refining and oil companies were devastated in last year's hurricane season and the 2006 hurricane season has just begun. Several panelists discussed their unique accounts of life post- hurricane, and their efforts for ensuring preparedness against other crises in the future. The conference was a time to hear stories of perseverance through one of the nations most destructive natural occurrences, network and meet people. On Monday morning, the conference commenced with remarks from James McGregor, president of Ocean Shipholdings, Inc., and a keynote speech from Rear Admiral Craig Bone of the United States Coast Guard. Each spoke of need to build strong relationships between the public and private sectors in order to be prepared in case of an emergency. The morning panel sessions were centered on government and industry response to disaster. The government discussion was facilitated by Mike Carthew, President of Chevron Shipping Company and had panelists representing the US DOT Maritime Administration and Office of Electricity Delivery & Energy Reliability, US Navy Military Sealift Command, National Pollution Fund Center and National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA). Each panelist discussed their organizations immediate response plan for major disaster relief and what these governmental bodies are doing to integrate with industry for faster and more effective recovery in the future.

### Have a Back-up Plan

Private industry was also given an opportunity to voice opinions about effective tactics and practices in response to these compromising situations. Jonathan Whitworth, CEO of Maritrans, Inc. moderated the discussion and posed insightful questions. The directors of Canal Barge Co., Bollinger Shipyards, E.N. Bisson & Son and the American Pilot's Association shared their stories of devastation and efforts to restore normalcy to their operations. These speakers pointed out that the main priority of a company is to secure employees and their families. We learned the importance of making sure that the back-up plan has a back up. Communication is Key! Between these two panel discussions there was consensus that the primary area of weakness was communication. The inability of many organizations to communicate with their own members, and with the governmental relief efforts made for an extremely complicated and precarious situation. The lack of technological infrastructure after the storms left many with no telephone or internet connection. Surprisingly, it was concluded that many were able to communicate solely through the use text messaging on mobile phones. The Oil and Gas Industry Response Panel was Monday afternoon. Bob Greco, Group Director at the API, moderated among Hornbeck Offshore Services, Marine Spill Response Corporation, Louisiana Offshore Oil Port (LOOP) and ConocoPhillips. This presentation focused on the destruction to physical facilities that these companies experienced and their efforts to restore production and secure their employees. Lessons Learned The general theme of the event became "Lessons Learned." On Tuesday, Ed Waryas, Vice President of Marine Marketing at Lloyd's Register, moderated a discussion of some of the lessons learned from the experience.

• Matt Holzhalb, Vice President for the Southern Region of the American Waterways Operators, spoke about the quick response of the organization in reopening the waterways after the wake of the storm.

He highlighted the importance of Gulf Coast waterways in American commerce. • Joseph J. Angelo, Director of Regulatory Affairs and the Americas for INTERTANKO, spoke about the market effects of the storms. Mr. Angelo highlighted that the US Gulf is an extremely critical region for the United States in terms of crude oil importation and refining capacity and spoke about the Jones Act waivers that were granted after both Rita and Katrina. • Bob Salmon of Shell Trading spoke about the Center for LNG and the response of the LNG shipping industry, as the US Gulf is also a critical region for LNG imports.

• Joseph J. Cox, President and CEO of the Chamber of Shipping of America, closed the discussion with his thoughts regarding the areas of improvement among different companies and how best use governmental resources.

While the panel only reflected a few specific accounts, the thoughts echoed the general sentiment of all companies that endured the experience. The Road Forward To conclude the conference, John Ridgeway, Deputy Chief Executive of BP Shipping moderated the discussion that focused on the procedural aspects of anticipating and reacting to crisis situations. Eric Smith of OSG spoke about the company strategy for assessing risk and the value added by gaining the perspectives all departments within the operation. Representatives from Texas A&M University and ABS Americas also added insight. While the prime focus of the discussions was the hurricanes in the US Gulf, the audience was reminded that there are other threats posed to the industry today- both natural and man-made. Directors of companies left the conference remembering that the way to be prepared is to anticipate the worst and hope for the best.
