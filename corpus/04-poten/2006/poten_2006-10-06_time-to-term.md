# Time to Term

With the demand for crude oil and clean products continuing to grow it is not surprising that demand for seaborne transportation is increasing. The marked strength in time charter rates in the past few months has served as one indication that the spot market is expected to maintain or even perhaps increase from current levels for the medium-term. This is particularly true for product carriers. Shown below is the number of reported Handymax time charters with durations of greater than twelve months. ### Chart: Period Versus Spot

The graph below shows the relationship between the spot market rates converted to dollars per day (Time Charter Equivalent or TCE) for the Handymax voyage Caribbean to the US Atlantic Coast and the three-year time charter rate since 2002.

CAR - USAC (38kt) - TCE Time Charter - 3 Year In general, the spot market time charter equivalents are expected to be higher than period charter rates as the spot market entails more risk in the form of idle time. In the Handymax market, however, this has only been the case for 35% of the past five years. Average earnings in the spot market have been $17,000 per day (assuming 90% utilization) as compared to the period market average rate of $17,400 per day. While the data above show the earnings at 90% utilization, in reality charterers generally achieve much higher utilization. Due to triangulation and back-hauls, it is not uncommon for vessels to achieve more than 100% utilization. Using 100% utilization for the period since January 2002 raises the average TCE to $19,100 per day- a significant premium to the time charter market.

### Forward Fixing

The largest time charterers of Handymax product carriers have been the trading companies who have taken rather large overall positions in this sector. The current three year charter rate for a modern Handymax with prompt delivery is assessed close to $23,000 per day. At these levels the trading companies are clearly getting better than 100% utilization and are also able to bid on commodity tenders such as gasoil and naphtha that come to market. With overall product demand strong, expect these companies to continue taking vessels on term charters in this sector and for rates to remain firm.
