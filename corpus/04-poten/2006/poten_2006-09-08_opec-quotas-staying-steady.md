# OPEC Quotas: Staying Steady

On the eve of this Monday's OPEC meeting, some speculation remains as to whether production quotas will officially change. Despite record crude prices, the past twelve months have seen steady quota levels and the results of this meeting are not expected to buck the trend. Changes in the political climates in these producing countries have recently proven to be far more influential on actual production than the official OPEC quota system. Revisions to quota levels today serve more as an expression of member countries' sentiments regarding global crude oil demand rather than a pricing mechanism.

The graph on the previous page shows the total OPEC crude production level as compared to the official quota level. The quota has remained steady at 28 million barrels per day for the past year, but overall production has been below this level recently. Last month, the OPEC 10 crude oil production was reported by the EIA to be 27.3 million barrels per day. Under or Over The below-quota output is due in part to the fact that countries are not currently able to reach quota levels and spare capacity is limited. The map below shows the total amount of crude oil produced in July 2006, versus their OPEC quotas. Venezuela, Nigeria, Indonesia and Iran are currently producing under their quotas, while the other nations are compensating and producing more.

Indonesia has experienced declining output over the past month to around 890,000 bbl per day. Venezuela and Nigeria have experienced unplanned supply disruptions. It appears that Indonesia will continue to experience an overall decline as little new production comes on line and fields mature. Nigeria has bright prospects in terms of production capacity and reserves but remains hindered by political turmoil. Wild Card As Iraq is not currently under the quota system, their production has not been considered part of OPEC supply. The reality is that this country has been contributing a great deal to world supply, especially in the past few months. In July, Iraq reportedly had production levels around 2.1 million barrels per day down slightly from the 2.2 million barrels per day reported for June. This contributes considerable incremental supply to the market.

OPEC currently contributes 30% of the 85.5 million barrels supplied per day. Regardless of the quota, OPEC producers are an extremely integral component of keeping the world well supplied with crude oil. World crude oil demand remains high, however it is not expected to increase dramatically in the near term. Therefore it appears that OPEC will maintain their current quota levels in order to assure adequate worldwide supply for the coming months.
