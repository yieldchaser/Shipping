# The Frenzied Ship Building Market

Even as the lull of August manifests itself in the empty desks of colleagues on vacation and voice mail messages in place of a real voice on the phone, vessel prices for all ship sizes continue to rise. Contributing factors include high steel prices and lack of available berth space in shipyards as well as owners wanting to purchase vessels to take advantage of high oil demand and a rise in OPEC production. Newbuilding prices on VLCCs are up by $30 million from last year; $14 million for Suezmaxes; $15 million for Aframaxes and $7 million for Panamax vessels, as shown in the graph below.

Steel Prices Out of Control With steel in demand for China's growth and yards soaking up steel to complete existing orders, new orders are being priced out of an average market range. Steel prices have reached $600 per ton, as seen in the graph below, double what they were two years ago. The current price of plate steel in Japan is $700 per ton.

The litany of reasons for high steel prices include the favorite excuse of China, but also steel consumed in the reconstruction of Iraq and a shortage of scrap steel to make rebar, reinforcing steel rods, used in construction. The use of rebar made from new steel rather than scrap becomes another demand factor pushing up steel prices. Another is hoarding in anticipation of even higher prices, an indication that perhaps this bubble in steel prices may soon burst. Shipbuilders Faring Well? One would think that with all the business shipbuilders are doing quite well. In reality, shipbuilders report lower profits caused by high steel prices. Steel prices rose by nearly 50% in the past year and with steel plate representing about 18% of shipbuilder's cost, this would explain the fall in profits. It will also explain the push for increased newbuilding prices as shipbuilders attempt to pass the higher cost for steel on to buyers.

### Strong Oil Demand

Orders have increased on OPEC's rise in oil production and high worldwide oil demand. The International Energy Agency predicts that demand for oil will reach 82.5 million barrels per day in the 4th quarter of 2004, the highest growth rate in 10 years. The agency predicts that demand will grow by an additional 700,000 barrels per day in 2005. OPEC's output is currently at its highest output since 1990 at 29 million bpd. Worldwide oil production is also up by about 1.2 million bpd. Owners are ordering ships even though delivery dates will not be until 2008 and beyond for lack of available berth space. They have to be believers in an ever expanding consumption of oil to place orders so far out into the future. Caveat Emptor Buying tankers at current levels requires a somewhat rosy view of the future. Maybe it will occur. Maybe it won't. The observation that the bubble in steel prices is about to burst struck a resonant cord. A rosy outlook for another five years of unconstrained growth in steel and ship demand is just asking too much.
