# Exactly What Is In Store for Us?

It is no secret that China is driving several commodity markets, not just oil. Focusing on oil, 2004 can only be described as a banner year, but how long can this last? The market was weakening rapidly as December drew to a close reflecting the announced cutbacks in AG exports combined with buyers holding back in anticipation of lower oil prices and shipping rates. The explanation offered by OPEC is to put a floor under crude prices, but the floor looks more like something that belongs in the attic rather than the cellar. Anyway, average annual rates were way up, and so was the number of AG VLCC spot fixtures as seen below.

### Chart: # AG Spot Fiixtures vs VLCC Rates AG/East

There is a strong degree of correlation between total AG fixtures and average VLCC rates AG/East of 94%. If this relationship still holds for next year, always something to keep in mind, a change of 100 fixtures over the course of the year will affect the annual average WS rate by 25 points. Where Were the Changes? The discharge zones showing the greatest change in activity are below. The Far East actually did not have any appreciable change in activity between 2003 and 2004, but is shown for its overall importance. The Far East includes Korea and Japan, but sometimes Far East fixtures end up as cargoes to northern China.

### Fixture Activity by Discharge Area

### Far East

U.S. SE Asia N. China India The United States, including both coasts and eastern Canada, exhibited growth in fixture activity. This long haul trade has a strong impact on rates and reflects IEA estimates of 0.5 million bpd gain in North American consumption between 2003 and 2004. Southeast Asia from the perspective of VLCCs reflects mainly AG exports to Singapore, Thailand and other Southeast Asian nations including southern China. This trade has shown strong growth in VLCC fixtures of 33% over a two-year period. Much of this incremental gain are shipments to southern China. Spot VLCC fixtures to northern China have grown by 50% in two years. India, while a short haul trade, is also exhibiting similar rapid growth of 43% over two years' time.
