# Index, Index on the Wall, What Will Decide a Rise or Fall?

It has been said that what goes up will usually come down. In the case of stock indexes, the measurement of that theory is calculated in numerous factors. For example, Poten's Tanker Index (PTI), started in 1996, is based on weekly- assessed tanker rates for 14 of the most popular trade routes for the principal ship sizes. Since its inception it has been used to measure the health of the tanker market. Another index by Lazard Freres measures the health of stocks of nine large tanker-owning companies. Both indices were set at an index value of 100 for tanker stocks and Worldscale rates in the beginning of 1996. Last week some of those "numerous" variables began to creep into the market. Tanker rates and stocks were strong last year hinging on worldwide increase in demand for crude oil as well as other raw materials. With the slower summer season on the horizon for tankers, what does this mean for tanker stocks? Let's examine the relationship between the two indexes. And the Index Says....

As shown below, there have been considerable periods when the two indices were very close together and where tanker stock prices and tanker rates moved up and down in pretty close unison. In other words a 10% improvement in rates was accompanied by a 10% improvement in tanker stock prices.

The "era of good agreement" between the two indices lasted from January 1996 through September 2000. Then tanker rates increased and tanker stocks decreased and vice versa. What caused this schism? In December 1999, the 25- year-old single-hull ship the Erika broke in two and sank off the coast of Brittany, France, spilling 15,000 tons of heavy grade fuel oil into the ocean. It is likely that the highly-publicized incident caused stockholders to sell their shares in tanker companies, even while tanker owners were still getting good rates for transporting oil (see graph below).

### What Will Rise and What Will Fall?

Worldwide economic growth over the recent past was considered the driving engine for high tanker rates. But tanker rates are in decline with the approach of the generally slow summer season. OPEC's decision to curb production added to declining rates. In the long run, we see tanker earnings on the rise which should help soften the loss on tanker stock prices for owners who dabble in both ships and stocks.
