# Moderating IEA View Limiting Tanker Outlook

### outlook would pressure

In its recent Oil Market Report (OMR), the International Energy Agency (IEA) kept its forecast for 2012 global oil demand roughly unchanged, while lowering its estimates for 2013 global oil demand growth from those levels previously reported in its medium term outlooks. Given its expectations for growth in non-OPEC supply and OPEC natural gas Liquids (NGLs), the IEA forecast call on OPEC crude in 2013 remains unchanged from 2012. With OPEC maintaining aggressive output levels to promote massive global stock builds in 1h12, any return to normalised inventory management during 2012-13 would suggest a sharp drop in OPEC production in the Arabian Gulf (AG).

### IEA forecasts have

evolved from credit party, to optimism and now to realism exited recession. Since mid-2011, however, the agency has downgraded its oil demand growth expectations, as the global economy wrestles with structural issues still unresolved from the financial crisis.

Interestingly, the IEA has distanced itself from adhering to the economic forecasts of the International Monetary Fund (IMF). In its recent OMR, the agency assumed a global real GDP growth rate of 3.8% for 2013, deviating from the 4.1% forecast in the IMF's most recent World Economic Outlook (WEO) in April 2012. The IEA cited deteriorating economic conditions in Europe, China and the US since the April WEO for its weaker global outlook. The IMF will release its annual July update to the WEO next week, so we will see how the fund interprets the recent weakness, but in April, it argued that following weakness in late-2011 and early-2012, global growth would accelerate in 2h12 to return global GDP growth to 4% in 2013.

### Rising Americas crude

production pressuring call on OPEC crude A static call on OPEC crude is hardly supportive for the tanker market, particularly with the cartel currently producing more than 1 mbpd above this level. Aggressive OPEC output levels that averaged 31.58 mbpd during 1h12 provided implied global stock builds of 1.3 mbpd and 2.1 mbpd in 1q12 and 2q12, respectively, in response to growing market anxiety over Iranian sanctions. OECD oil inventories are now at more comfortable levels, but as much as 85% of the implied stock build seems to have occurred in non-OECD nations, with China's rising strategic reserve being a leading component. Even if refiners wish to keep inventories at constant levels, ultimately, OPEC will need to equilibrate the markets and bring crude production back towards the call level of 30.5 mbpd.

### OPEC production push

prompted massive stock build, mostly in non- OECD locations The implications for AG crude exports and VLCCs could be challenging, given modest yoy gains in OPEC output in Libya and Angola expected for 2013. A 0.80 mbpd decline in OPEC production might imply a 0.95 mbpd cut in AG output, removing at least 2.5% from crude tanker demand, predominantly for VLCCs. The anticipated start-up of the 400 kbpd Total joint venture refinery in Jubail during 2013 could amplify this effect, by further limiting crude export availability.

### Static inventories would

require large cut in AG exports In the near term, the current malaise in the VLCC market may be reflecting a pause in inventory building, or perhaps something more sustained. The 1.7 mbpd average stock build in 1h12 added 300 million tonnes of additional cargo during the period, mostly in form of exaggerated OPEC production flowing from the AG to Asian locations. This could have added as much as 5% to crude tanker demand during 1h12, helping to absorb tonnage supply momentarily. That this demand surge could only produce a brief flirtation with $40,000/day VLCC earnings, is sobering and telling of the fundamental supply/demand balance.
