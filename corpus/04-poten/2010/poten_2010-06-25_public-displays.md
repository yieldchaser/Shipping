# Public Displays

As difficult as it was to see the light just a year ago, optimism in the tanker market has been the flavor of the month. Despite European debt worries and oil spill anxiety stateside, generally healthy freight rates and moderated fleet growth have let shipping companies enjoy the freight rate spoils over the last couple of weeks. This week, listed companies in particular have had a few different public outlets through which they could demonstrate their various strengths and vie for investor love.

Unlike some of the other shipping sectors, tankers did not experience the distressed asset scenario that many (though not Poten, see "Stocking Slippage" Dec. 2009) had expected to determine the direction of the market. Instead, en‐bloc purchases, some by publically‐listed companies, have put the most upward pressure on asset prices in the near term and helped establish price floors. VLCC asset prices, for example, have started to battle back from the dramatic loses sustained between mid‐ 2008 and the end of 2009. In the time since, asset prices for newbuild and secondhand VLCCs have risen by almost 10 and 15 percent from their respective lows, as shown in the chart below.

### Challenges Continue

Despite apparent optimism about the tanker market's future, lingering uncertainties in the wider international economy have closed the window for new initial public offerings (IPO) at present. On the back of the shipping boom during 2004 to 2008, publically‐listed shipping companies gained street credibility and general popularity among retail investors. Tanker industry IPOs issued in 2010 have been met with a mixed initial reception thus far, but could be well positioned to benefit as investors are lured back into the sector by reports of a stronger‐than expected recovery in oil demand.

Still, with most traditional shipping banks stretched thin and shipping loan volumes near historic lows, shipowners find themselves in a lender's market which is expected to continue for the foreseeable future. Investors are wisely focused on the value of underlying assets and are somewhat reluctant to blindly buy into the trade growth fundamentals story. Not surprisingly, those shipowners who are able to get bank loans are facing stricter covenants and higher spreads. A few established public shipping companies, however, have been successful at tapping public markets this year. Follow‐on offerings have far outweighed IPOs throughout the past year, as seen in the chart below, which displays the offering activity that has been seen in the shipping space since mid‐2004.

### IPOs

Follow‐on Offerings The role of public money has brought to light an apparent disconnect between asset prices and freight rates. As asset prices edge somewhat higher, pressures on freight rates are likely to follow. Interest and obligations of public companies to constantly demonstrate growth will likely fuel more sale and purchase activity in the near term. The asset and equity markets are both driven by momentum and nothing attracts a crowd like a crowd!
