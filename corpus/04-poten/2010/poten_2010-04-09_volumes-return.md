# Volumes Return

The recent strength in VLCC earnings has been propelled mostly by an increase in overall fixture activity and signals of strengthening demand for crude oil and refined products. Less dramatic than the rally which inaugurated 2010, improving conditions for VLCCs have nonetheless provided the market with a degree of optimism as it enters the second quarter, a period which usually witnesses a softening of freight rates.

Most notably, there were 99 reported spot VLCC liftings out of the Arabian Gulf in March 2010 and have already been more than 100 liftings from the region so far in the month of April, as shown in the chart below. As the predominate loading region for VLCCs, the volume of reported VLCC spot liftings from the Arabian Gulf helps determine overall trade volumes and freight rates for large crude carriers, often setting the tone for wider tanker markets. This month's Arabian Gulf liftings have already surpassed the 12 month average of 90 liftings and marks the first time in more than a year that the psychologically‐important 100 liftings mark has been passed.

East West % Total Also significant for the progress of the overall economic recovery are increases in the percentage of West‐bound liftings from the Arabian Gulf. 16% of reported April VLCC cargoes leaving the Arabian Gulf are destined for markets in the West, the second consecutive monthly increase in the Western portion of such liftings. The strengthening of West‐bound demand has helped reduce the number of VLCCs available in the region over the next 30 days to just 63 vessels from a recent high of 83 vessels at the beginning of March. Repositioning by vessel owners, combined with consistent volumes on long‐haul voyages to Eastern markets and signals of strengthening demand in the West, have all contributed to support for VLCC freight rates in recent weeks.

### Looking Up

Indications of increasing fixture activity out of the Arabian Gulf join a series of positive indicators for both the domestic and international economies. The US Commerce Department recently reported that the domestic economy grew at a rate of 5.6% in the fourth quarter of 2009, its fastest pace in six years. Last week, improved housing and consumer confidence data helped bring the Dow Jones Industrial Average to its highest levels since September 2008. In a report released this week, the World Bank praised stimulus spending in both developed and developing countries and said that such spending has improved prospects for global recovery.

These signals of improving economic conditions have translated into increased demand for transportation fuels and improved refining margins in some markets. The Energy Information Administration (EIA) reports that the nation's refiners increased their throughputs by about two percent last week and are currently running at a six‐month high of 84.5%, higher than similar periods of either 2008 or 2009. While distillate demand has remained low, gasoline demand over the past four weeks has exceeded the same period of last year by 1.7%, according to the EIA. The widening differential between light and heavy crude oils has contributed to hope among some observers that the recent strength in refining margins can continue.

The increase in refining activity has already led to a 500 kbd increase in crude oil imports into the United States last week. Greater refinery utilizations in Asia, along with the introduction of new refining capacity and a resulting accumulation of stockpiles, have helped vessel demand. As shown in the chart below, earnings on the benchmark Arabian Gulf - Far East (260 kt) voyage improved over the course of March after retreating from January's heights.
