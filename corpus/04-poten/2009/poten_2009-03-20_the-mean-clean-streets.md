# The Mean Clean Streets

A year ago, United States gasoline stocks had reached a 15 year high at over 235 million bbls in the coffers. Since then, gasoline stocks have retreated to as low as 178 million bbls before recovering to the current level of 215 million bbls - still down 7% from last March's high. In contrast, US crude oil working stocks reached a 15 year high this March at over 353 million bbls. With the market's expectation that peak driving season is around the corner, Medium Range (MR) tanker owners may be left wondering what the prospects are for gasoline imports to the US given the divergence of these inventories.

The chart below outlines the trend of US crude oil and gasoline stocks over the past six years. During this time period, the gap between these two stockpiles has grown by over 19% - made apparent by the trend lines.

### *SPR: Strategic Petroleum Reserves

The deflation in the price of crude oil since the record high of July 2008 coupled with softened demand has prompted stocks to reach their current level. Lower gasoline stocks, on the other hand, may be traced back to decreased refinery utilization to support fragile margins.

### Use It or Lose It

According to the Energy Intelligence Agency (EIA) current US refinery utilization is at 82.1%, well below the five‐year average of 89.5%. Since the beginning of 2009 US refinery utilization has been below long term averages, and as seen in the chart below, is following a downward trend of sub‐average production levels. While it can be assumed that extensive hurricane activity in the Petroleum Administration for Defense District III (PADD III) region has led to long shutdowns for repairs, this has only impacted utilization in discrete doses.

### Cracking the Market

MR owners may be searching for any glimmer of hope as freight rates in the UK Continent - US Atlantic Coast (UKC‐USAC) and Caribbean - US Atlantic Coast (CAR‐USAC) trades recently reflected timecharter equivalent earnings of roughly $4,000 per day and $6,700 per day, respectively. While these near break‐even earnings haunt MR owners for now, hope could lie in the US refinery crack spread. Simply, the crack spread has appeared to return back to long‐term average levels with the help of a more stable crude price and lower refinery utilization. Adjusting utilization rates might be a method refiners choose to stabilize a floor for margins - thus potentially keeping US gasoline stockpiles low.

### To Drive or Not to Drive?

Summer is a few months away and the prospect of the peak driving season traditionally leads to a rise in gasoline stocks this time of year. However now with the anticipated lower demand potential and weaker pricing, this does not seem to be the case. In the event that demand does show signs of any serious recovery, though, excess crude oil stockpiles could be refined - muting any immediate need for more imported gasoline.

For now the fate of gasoline MR movements to the US will lie in the hands of US refiners and motorists. While large crude oil stockpiles may afford shore‐side producers a last minute solution to unanticipated demand, they may not be willing to sacrifice their fragile margins - a move that would leave unmatched gasoline demand in the hands of imports.
