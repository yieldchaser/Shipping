# Trade Winds of Change

### Global dirty trade routes-reshuffle again in 2024

In 2020, we started a new annual tradition, ranking the Top 20 Global Dirty Trade Routes (covering both crude oil and dirty products) and, in the process, highlighting the routes that changed the most. Sadly, the company that provided the underlying raw data for this analysis discontinued their service. Fortunately, we have found a new data partner (Vortexa) and we are able resume these rankings. However, there are always slight differences in regional definitions and how the data is compiled and processed. Therefore, we were not able to do the trade route comparison last year, because we would have been comparing the data from two different data sources. This year, we are resuming the tradition, with consistent 2023 and 2024 data to compare. As has been the case every year since 2020, as a result of the Covid-19 pandemic, and the wars in Europe and the Middle East, tanker trade flows have been changing significantly, and the year 2024 was no exception. The overall volume of global trade does not appear to have changed that much. Global transport volumes of crude oil and dirty petroleum products are slightly lower in 2024 than in 2023, both in terms of TonMiles per Day (-2.3%) and as expressed in Barrels per Day (-0.8%). However, as is often the case, the changes are happening below the surface, on individual routes.

From a shipping market perspective, TonMiles, which take the transport distance into account, are more important than tons or barrels. That is why we do the ranking in descending order of TonMiles. Taking a look at the Top 20 Dirty Trade Routes, the ranking of the first eight (8) routes for 2024 is virtually identical to their 2023 position. It is no surprise that the Arabian Gulf (AG) to Northeast Asia (NE Asia) route is and remains the dominant one, both in terms of TonMiles per Day (TM/D) and Barrels per Day (B/D). The Arabian Gulf hosts many of the largest crude oil exporters and Northeast Asia is home to several of the world's biggest oil importers (China, Japan and South Korea). Some of the other routes rank highly because of the long sailing distance between exporter and importer: Gulf of Mexico to NE Asia and West Africa to NE Asia for example.

One of the key factors that drove tanker TonMiles in 2024 was the Houthi's attacks on commercial shipping passing through the Red Sea and the Bab Al-Mandeb Strait. This forced shipowners that moved cargoes from the Atlantic to the Pacific and vice versa to avoid the shorter route via the Suez Canal and sail around the Cape of Good Hope. This added significant TonMile demand to several tanker routes and boosted their ranking. Two examples: The AG to the Mediterranean (ranked 9 th in 2024, up from 16 th in 2023) and AG to Northwest Europe (moved from 21 st in 2023 to 13 th in 2024.

TonMile demand from the AG to the Mediterranean and from the AG to Northwest Europe increased by 59% and 39% respectively. This happened despite a decrease in volumes moved (down by 30% and 17%) as most remaining barrels moved around the Cape of Good Hope rather than through the Suez Canal. Crude oil flows from the Black Sea to Northeast Asia dropped by 67% as the virtual closure of the Suez Canal made these moves uneconomic. There was a meaningful increase in volumes transported from the East Coast of South America to Northwest Europe (both in TM/D and in B/D). This was due to increased production and exports from Guyana, Brazil and the resumption of modest flows from Venezuela to Europe.

The slowdown in Chinese oil demand growth and their increased reliance on crude oil from the Russian Far East is also reflected in the numbers as the volumes from the Arabian Gulf and the Baltic to Northeast Asia are both down.

With all the geopolitical uncertainty, it is likely that we will see even more changes in tanker trade routes and their relative rankings in 2025 and beyond.
