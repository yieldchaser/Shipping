## Analysis & Commentary

Barrels Without Borders?

Alaskan crude oil production returns to growth On November 10, the Energy Information Administration (EIA) published its latest Short-Term Energy Outlook (STEO). In this report, the EIA stated that, after decades of decline, crude oil production in Alaska is growing again, driven by two projects on Alaska’s North Slope. The EIA projects that Alaskan production will grow by 23,000 b/d (6%) in 2026, followed by a 60,000 b/d increase in 2027. If this growth is realized, Alaska will produce 505,000 b/d in 2027, the highest output since 2014. According to the EIA, the last time Alaska saw production increases of this magnitude was in the 1980s, when production peaked at 2.0 Mb/d. At that time, Alaska was the biggest oil producing state in the United States. While the recent increases in Alaskan output are noteworthy, they pale in comparison to the production boost that is expected when ConocoPhillips’ massive Willow development comes online in 2029. At peak production, Willow could add up to 180,000 b/d to Alaska’s output. Virtually all of Alaska’s production is shipped out of state. In this Tanker Opinion, we discuss the unique nature of crude oil shipments from Alaska, and the impact a significant increase in available crude could have on the maritime sector servicing Alaska.
