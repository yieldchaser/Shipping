# Maintaining Orders

The credit crisis has created a great deal of speculation about the actual deliverability of the worldwide shipping orderbook. While much of the concern has initially been focused on the dry bulk sector, tanker owners and investors are anxiously awaiting the prognosis for their sector. Up to now, the tanker market has been insulated from cancellations due to a stable freight market, credit-worthiness of market participants and a relatively low level of asset price correction compared to other sectors. When assessing the deliverability of the orderbook, two parties are under scrutiny: the shipowner placing the order must be able to meet equity requirements on schedule and the shipyard must physically be able to obtain raw materials, parts, labor and execute the construction in a timely fashion. ### VLCC Orderbook by Owner

In the tanker market, many placing the orders as well as the shipbuilders have strong ties to governments and public sources of funding. Of the VLCC orderbook, only 35% of the orders have been placed by private companies and the balance, about 65%, has been placed by government-related or publicly listed companies. The Suezmax and Aframax orderbooks draw 64% and 54% on the financial soundness of various private companies, respectively. The minimum expectation is that those orders with direct government ties, approximately 30% of the total VLCC orderbook, will certainly be delivered. Additionally, the cash positions of public companies and private companies, based strong historical earnings, will be helpful in meeting many of the other outstanding obligations on the overall books.

### At the shipyards

The chart below shows the total orderbook and the type of shipyard that is responsible for construction. The majority of the new VLCCs have been placed at established yards.

### VLCC Orderbook by Shipyard

For the VLCC sector, about 30% of the total orders have been placed at yards with strong government backing and almost 65% have been placed at publicly-listed, experienced shipyards. Most notably, greenfield yards only account for roughly 5% of the orderbook across all of the large tanker classes.

The analysis suggests that the tanker orderbook will be delivered, regardless of any hype or perceived hiccups in the near-term. What may end up happening is less experienced shipyards will experience temporary delays. Any delays will ultimately have a limited effect on the total capacity over the longer term. More importantly, market sentiment has given little indication that shipowners are itching to walk away from commitments.
