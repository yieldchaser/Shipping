# Ethanol RIN Volatility has Consequences for US Trade Volumes

### Environmental credit

The Renewable Identification Number (RIN) market administered by the US Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) has largely been a boondoggle from its inception. A RIN is a number assigned to individual batches of biofuel that are then used by regulated entities to prove that they are in compliance with the EPA's Renewable Fuel Standard (RFS) program. Companies that produce more RINs than they need can sell the credits to those that need to purchase them in order to be in compliance. Not unlike other purported market solutions for mandating increased renewable energy production, the tradable RIN market has been beset by wild volatility and frequent cases of fraud. EPA in fact estimated in 2012 that there were approximately 140 million illegitimate credits in circulation.

ECC ECSA SAF UKC WCCA The United States actually became a net ethanol importer in 2012 with the expiration of protectionist ethanol regulations as imports mainly increased from Brazil. RIN price movements, on the other hand, may spur increased domestic ethanol production by improving the aforementioned crush spread, but such a move may beget both higher corn prices and a political backlash in turn. Furthermore, such an option is not available to all refiners.

Not only have imports of ethanol increased, but petroleum product exports may increase as well as refineries seek to avoid selling domestically where they have the added expense associated with the RFS. In any case, pricing dynamics will not be favorable to motorists in the United States. Domestic refineries, many of which are already squeezed, may look for markets elsewhere, and analysts at Credit Suisse note that a price of $0.78 per RIN is enough to also deter gasoline imports into the United States. Unfortunately for the American consumer, the most obvious response to ensure sufficient production margins for refiners selling in the United States is to simply increase prices at the pump. Indeed, relevant gasoline futures have gained this year despite a corresponding decline in crude oil prices. This would drag further on already declining domestic mogas demand and could, more ominously, temper recent gains seen in consumer spending.
