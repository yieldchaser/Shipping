# To Some Avail

The pre‐Halloween snow storm on the US Atlantic coast and the impending winter weather in the northern hemisphere are stoking tanker owners' hopes for any kind of a market rally. The fourth quarter has sometimes been known to be lucrative for tanker owners as completion of refinery maintenance, seasonal petroleum demand and reduced daylight hours have historically increased demand for ships. An uptick in freight rates in the last weeks of October has many hoping for more signs of a winter upswing, but fundamentals remain strained, dampening the degree of upside potential. That said, situational shortages of suitable tonnage could facilitate market movements over the coming months.

Downward pressure in freight rates results when charterers compare the forthcoming requirements against the count of available vessels. As the incremental vessel tends to deliver into the spot market, supply gluts resulting from newbuilding deliveries have had the largest impact on spot market rates. Spot Availability ‐ Next 30 Days Period Average ‐ Availabilities The VLCC spot availability "next 30 days" in the Arabian Gulf averaged about 40 vessels from 2001‐ 2004. From January 2009 to present, that number has increased to almost 90 ships, reflecting a 115% increase in total availabilities, while the average number of liftings increased by 6% comparatively. This year, average availabilities reached a record high of 111 ships over the month of September.

Oil demand has never been higher. Reported VLCC cargo liftings out of the Arabian Gulf numbered 121 in October, 16% higher than the ten year average, and about 6% higher than average reported in the past year. ### Liftings

Worldscale Rate While the supply overhang is tough to evade, the upturn in VLCC rates seen in late‐October was largely driven by the sensitivity of charterers' lifting dates. Vetting requirements, the quality of available tonnage, and the increasing unwillingness of charterers to conduct business with companies under financial pressure has resulted in the emergence of a tiered supply situation. Not every ship in position will meet charterers' dates, while others may simply refuse business below certain rate levels on principle. While the macro supply/demand balance suffers, on a micro basis charterers can find themselves surprised with few vessels to fix. This suggests that upswings over the fourth quarter will be largely situational and relatively short lived. However, the tiered quality of VLCC spot market is likely to become even more pronounced as the market recovers, possibly leading to higher rates for the most credible (or flexible) owners.
