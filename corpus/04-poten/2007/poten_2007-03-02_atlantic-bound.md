# Atlantic Bound

A recent Wall Street Journal article indicated that US crude oil imports from Africa surpassed those of the Middle East in 2006, for the first time in over 20 years. The trend has been in place for the better part of the past 18 months. As recently as five years ago Middle East imports were substantially more than those from Africa. The chart below shows the US import levels on a daily basis in millions of barrels from the Middle East and Africa. While the level of import activity from the Middle East has stayed generally above 2 million barrels per day, the African import level has nearly doubled since 2002.

### Africa is in the Med Too!

Interestingly, some of the largest contributors in Africa to this marked increase have not been West African players, but rather those with Mediterranean coastlines.

Currently the largest exporter from these two regions is, not surprisingly, Saudi Arabia at a level of around 1.4 million barrels per day. However, some of the more surprising exporters are Algeria, Libya and Chad. The chart below shows the average export level for 2005 and 2006 (through November).

### More Please

The exports from West and North Africa are in addition to the Arabian Gulf crude supplies that are already established in the market. With this in mind, demand for vessels in the Arabian Gulf will remain firm as demand in the Atlantic Basin increases. The map below shows the reported spot VLCC fixtures to date in 2007 for some of the prominent Atlantic Basin trade. This total of 71 spot fixtures compares with just 56 for the first two months of 2006.

With the West African and Mediterranean markets playing a more critical role in supplying the necessary volumes to the United States, it can seem that this would result in an overall decline in ton-mile demand. However, increasing incremental barrels that are flowing out of the West Africa and the Mediterranean to the Far East are contributing to a substantial offsetting increase in ton-mile demand. As the level of fixture activity in the Atlantic Basin continues to grow, double-hulled VLCCs will experience more of an incentive to remain in the Atlantic further tightening supply for double-hulls VLCCs in the Arabian Gulf. In the short-run, the market will remain firm.
