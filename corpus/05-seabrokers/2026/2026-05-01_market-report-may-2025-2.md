# OSV Market Round-Up

## GLOBAL OIL INVENTORIES DEPLETING AT RECORD PACE

In its latest Oil Market Report, released in mid-May, the International Energy Agency highlighted that "mounting supply losses from the Strait of Hormuz are depleting global oil inventories at a record pace" as the ongoing conflict between the USA/Israel and Iran continues to wreak havoc on energy markets.

With tanker traffic still restricted, cumulative supply losses from Middle East oil & gas producers already exceed 1 billion barrels comfortably with more than 14 million barrels per day (bpd) of oil now shut in. On the supply side, Saudi Arabia and the UAE have successfully redirected some exports to terminals away from the Strait of Hormuz, while stocks from both commercial and government strategic storage sites in consuming countries have been flowing into the market to offset some of the supply losses.

While that has eased some of the pressure on the supply side, observed global inventories (including oil on water) were drawn down by nearly 250 million barrels over the course of March and April, with that figure likely to be considerably higher once the May figures are added into the mix.

When looking at the bigger picture, the IEA has forecast that global oil demand is projected to contract by 420,000 bpd over the course of 2026, leaving the average forecast for the year as a whole at 104 million bpd, some 1.3 million bpd below the agency's pre-war forecast. The petrochemical and aviation sectors are currently the most affected industries.

On the supply side, global oil supply declined to 95.1 million bpd in April, with output from Middle Eastern countries some 14.4 million bpd below pre-war levels due to the closure of the Strait of Hormuz. Higher production and exports from the Atlantic Basin have provided some relief, while a gradual resumption of hydrocarbon flows through the Strait will hopefully continue in June.

Global observed oil inventories were drawn down by 129 million barrels in March, and by a further 117 million barrels in April, according to preliminary data. Continued disruptions to seaborne trade through the Strait of Hormuz saw on-land stocks drop by 170 million bpd in April alone.

# OSV Market Round-Up

## NORTH SEA CHARTERERS AT RISK OF AHTS RATE SPIKES AS ROSEBANK VESSELS GO ON HIRE

**We have now entered a high risk period for charterers on the North Sea spot market, with a considerable number of AHTS vessels tied up on work scopes related to the arrival of the Rosebank FPSO in the UK.**

Adura, the UK joint venture between Equinor and Shell, has secured the services of a long list of vessels to support its operations at Rosebank. The Aurora Sandefjord, Normand Prosper, Normand Sapphire, Siem Pearl, Skandi Minder, Skandi Saltfjord, Skandi Skansen and Skandi Vega all have work scopes lined up at Rosebank, potentially keeping them occupied until late June or even July. In a market where supply limitations have already been a serious issue over the course of 2026, this leaves any charterers with pending AHTS requirements exposed to potential rate spikes.

There is, of course, no guarantee that owners will have many opportunities to capitalise on this situation; it will depend how many fresh requirements appear over the next few weeks. However, the most recent market fixtures have been coming in with rates of circa GBP 120,000-190,000 (USD 161,590-255,850) in the UK, and around NOK 2.9-3 million (USD 313,000-323,875) in Norway. That is already an elevated starting point as we enter June, and with very few owners marketing near-term availability, they will be poised to ramp up rates at every opportunity for the foreseeable future.

## NORWAY PROGRESSING ENVIRONMENTAL IMPROVEMENTS

The Norwegian Government is introducing new requirements for companies operating on the Norwegian continental shelf to reduce the greenhouse gas emissions from offshore vessels from 2029 onwards. Under the new guidelines, operators must reduce the greenhouse gas intensity of vessels used for oil & gas operations by 10% by the 2029-2031 period, with that target increasing towards 40% by 2038-2040. The government has indicated that the new regulations are expected to deliver total emission cuts of around 1.6 million mt of CO2-equivalent by 2040. The measures have been brought in to encourage the uptake of low and zero-emission technologies including electric and hybrid propulsion as well as alternative marine fuels including ammonia and hydrogen.

One vessel owner that has always been at the forefront of technological innovation towards greener operations is Eidesvik Offshore, and the company's 2003-built PSV Viking Energy has just entered Halsnøy Dokk where she will be converted to operate with ammonia power. Wärtsilä Marine has been selected to provide a dual-fuel engine capable of operating on ammonia and marine gas oil, while Breeze Ship Design is responsible for the ship design and engineering.

### EQUINOR EXTENDS VIKING AVANT CONTRACT

While the Viking Energy is taking a break from her long-term Equinor contract to be converted for ammonia power, another Eidesvik PSV has had its contract with Equinor extended in Norway.

Equinor has exercised all of the remaining options on its contract with the Viking Avant, thereby extending the contract to the end of 2026. The Viking Energy, meanwhile, is firmly contracted to Equinor until the second quarter of 2030.

The Viking Avant is a 2004-built VS 493 PSV with a length of 92.2m, breadth of 20.4m, deck area of 1,010m<sup>2</sup> and deadweight of 5,784t.

### DOF AND MØKSTER PSVS RETAINED BY ADURA

DOF and Simon Møkster Shipping have secured new PSV contracts with Adura in the UK sector. The Skandi Barra and Stril Odin had already been working for Shell under previously-awarded term commitments, and they have both now been retained by Adura, the new UK joint venture between Shell and Equinor. Both vessels have been awarded one-year firm contracts, running until May and June 2027 respectively, while the Barra contract comes with two further one-year options and the Odin contract contains a single one-year option.

The Skandi Barra and Stril Odin were built to the MT 6000 MkII design, with the Barra entering service in 2005 and the Odin a year later.

**Skandi Barra (c/o P. Gowen)**

# SAIPEM SELECTS SIX FOR UK SUMMER SUPPORT

Saipem has awarded contracts to six PSVs to provide support for an upcoming summer season pipelay campaign it will be undertaking offshore the UK.

The Atlantica Server, FS Aries, FS Hercules, HM Flipper, MH Monsoon and Surfer Tide have all been fixed up to Saipem for a firm period of 50 days with scheduled commencement in mid-July. All of the contracts come with further options to extend for an additional 30 days.

The FS Hercules is a newbuild MPSV in China which is due to enter service in the imminent future under the management of the Fletcher Group. The FS Aries, HM Flipper, MH Monsoon and Surfer Tide are already active in the North Sea market, while the Atlantica Server will be reactivated from layup for the first time since late 2024 in preparation for this charter.

MH Monsoon (c/o G. Saunders)

# SERVICE TIDE REPLACING TROMS CAPELLA WITH BP

BP UK has awarded a contract to Tidewater to charter the Service Tide PSV until at least the end of August. The vessel will be providing support to BP's operations with the Noble Intrepid jackup.

The Service Tide is replacing another Tidewater PSV in this role; the Troms Capella has been supporting the Noble Intrepid but the Capella has a prior-awarded contract lined up with another undisclosed charterer this summer. BP is holding multiple options on this new contract that could see the Service Tide remain on hire into the first quarter of 2027.

Service Tide (c/o P. Gowen)

# FOUR-WEEK FIRM FIXTURES FOR REM AND GOLDEN

Rem Offshore and Golden Energy Offshore have both picked up 28-day firm PSV fixtures in the UK recently. CNOOC chartered the Rem Commander from Rem Offshore, with the vessel going on hire in mid-May, while Serica Energy chartered the Energy Duchess from Golden Energy; this fixture has a start date in early June. Both charterers secured options to potentially extend the four-week firm commitments.

The Rem Commander is a 15 year-old vessel that was built to the STX/VARD PSV 06 CD design, while the Energy Duchess is a 2019-built Ulstein PX 121 H vessel.

# OSV Market Round-Up

## BOURBON SECURES MULTI-YEAR PHILIPPINES CONTRACT

BOURBON has been awarded a multi-year contract with Prime Energy for operations offshore the Philippines. This contract will involve a commercial partnership between BOURBON and Bahtera Sri Kandi Pte Ltd, and will see the Bourbon Liberty 237 AHTS vessel deployed for three firm years with a further two-year option available. Operations will commence in August after the vessel has been converted for the transportation of methanol for offshore operations. This will include tank conversion, the installation of dedicated pumping systems and a Nitrogen Inert gas system, and adapted pipelines and protective coatings.

Bourbon Liberty 237 (c/o M. Datsenko)

## NORTH STAR SECURES CONTRACTS FOR 17 ERRVs

North Star has secured a "major tranche of North Sea ERRV contracts, reinforcing offshore safety and UK energy security."

The vessel owner has secured a multi-million-pound pipeline of contracts involving 17 out of the 37 ERRVs in its fleet. The new fixtures have contributed approximately 50 years of total combined duration to North Star's contract backlog.

The new commitments were secured via a combination of competitive tender processes alongside multiple contract extensions.

North Star has highlighted that the contracts have been secured "at a time when the UK energy system is undergoing transition" with the new fixtures underlining "the continued importance of offshore infrastructure investment and the essential role of safety vessels in maintaining secure and stable energy production. ERRVs remain a fundamental part of offshore operations, ensuring that personnel working at sea are protected around the clock in some of the most challenging marine environments in the world."

## BOSKALIS ORDERS NEW CABLE-LAY VESSEL

Boskalis will invest in a new high-capacity cable-laying vessel (CLV) targeting the offshore wind and interconnector markets.

Boskalis currently operates three CLVs, a trenching support vessel and a portfolio of trenching equipment.

# CALIFORNIA REAFFIRMS 25 GW OFFSHORE WIND AMBITION

California state officials have announced that they remain committed to deploying 25 GW of offshore wind capacity by 2045.

This will mean continued investment in ports and related infrastructure to establish a domestic floating wind supply chain. In addition to this, investment in workforce development, supply chain expansion, and establishing a clearer permitting framework to advance commercial-scale floating wind projects will also be needed to meet this plan that could provide up to 15 per cent of California's future clean electricity demand while supporting thousands of jobs and industrial development.

While this renewed commitment is encouraging, mounting challenges in the U.S. wind sector continue with the federal government now negotiating agreements with leaseholders to cancel undeveloped projects in exchange for the refund of their lease payments.

# RWE CONSIDERS RELINQUISHING U.S. WIND LEASE AREAS

RWE is reportedly considering a deal with the Trump administration to surrender U.S. offshore wind leases in exchange for commitments tied to fossil fuel investments.

This has prompted more than 50 U.S. organisations urging them against trading renewable for fossil fuel investments. The agreements are not yet final and drawing scrutiny — it has been speculated that they may not even be legal.

If RWE does agree to release the U.S. offshore wind developments, they would be the third developer to do so, following TotalEnergies and Ocean Winds, whose deals involved relinquishing their offshore wind lease areas in return for lease payment refunds and committing to invest in fossil fuel projects in the United States.
