# VLCCs | More Ships Heading for the Gulf, Fewer Cargoes

**Date:** November 20, 2026  
**Publisher:** Breakwave Advisors | **Category:** Market Insights  
**Original URL:** [https://www.breakwaveadvisors.com/insights/2026/11/20/vlccs-more-ships-heading-for-the-gulf-fewer-cargoes](https://www.breakwaveadvisors.com/insights/2026/11/20/vlccs-more-ships-heading-for-the-gulf-fewer-cargoes)  

---

The Gulf tonnage build accelerated this week. Twelve of the 17 additional Gulf-bound ballasters appeared in the past week, while the tracked cargo count fell from 81 to 66. West Africa moved the other way, with fewer ships heading into the region.

*By*[*Per Barre*](https://www.linkedin.com/in/per-barre-0022b524/)

Crude shipments through Hormuz have recovered to roughly prewar levels despite continued tanker disruptions. A tanker was reported loading Iranian crude in the Gulf on Thursday. If the cargo clears, it could mark Iran’s first successful crude export since the U.S. reimposed its naval blockade in July.

Freight. Fujairah–Far East business was reported around WS 300 late last week. The Gulf of Oman–China assessment (TD34) reached WS 326 early this week before easing to WS 324.64 on Tuesday as the prompt tonnage list remained tight.

Early December is the test. Of the 104 Gulf-bound ballasters, 55 are coming from the Far East. Owners with ships already in position can still defend prompt rates. Charterers gain leverage if those inbound ships come within range before the next round of cargoes is fixed.
