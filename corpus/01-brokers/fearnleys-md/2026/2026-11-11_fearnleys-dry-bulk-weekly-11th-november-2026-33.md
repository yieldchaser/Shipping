## Capesize/Newcastlemax

The model below consists of factors like the global ballast/laden vessel ratio, West of Singapore ballasters, and a couple of other AIS indicators. Below the charts are tables showing where the model sits currently. It is only at extremes that these models give very clear suggestions about the near-term outlook.

The momentum model dropped out of bearish territory, primarily due to fewer ballasters West of Singapore. Still, it's worth pointing out that the bearish signal given last week will be "live" next week; at the same time  the bullish signals given in mid-October will no longer be in play (2 to 4 weeks is the timeframe the models are good at predicting).

In the last 6 weeks, total South Atlantic shipment volumes were up 18.6%% compared to the corresponding weeks last year, continuing the improving trend. Brazil iron ore exports growth has been negative for eight straight weeks (down 14% yoy over those seven weeks), but Guinea bauxite exports growth has seen a renewed surge, with the last 6 weeks being 43% higher than the same period last year.

## Panamax/Kamsarmax

Shipment volume growth continues to be lower than DWT supply growth. Through week 45, growth was 2.5%, compared to DWT supply growth of 4.7%. Over the last 10 weeks, volume growth was -0.1% compared to the same period last year.

Both the Pacific and South Atlantic indicators suggest the market rebound could soon stop. The coal futures curve backwardation has continued to rise, however, which suggest positive momentum on that front in the coming months.

**Top left chart:** Shipment volumes were up 2.5% year on year through week 45. In the last ten weeks, volumes were down 0.1% compared to the same period last year. Supply growth was 4.7% at the latest reading.

**Top right chart:** The 2-month lead of the coal futures curve suggests the market could slowly trend upwards in the next two months.

**Bottom left chart:** The indicator suggests this rebound could soon be over.

**Bottom right chart:** The indicator suggest the P6 rebound could soon be over.

## Supramax/Ultramax

Through week 45, shipment volume growth was 3.0% year-on-year, whereas over the last 10 weeks, growth was 2.9% compared to the same period last year. DWT supply growth continues at 5.5% year-on-year.

We added an AIS-based market model/indicator to this report. The model is a measure of momentum in major loading areas, and also includes the global ballast/laden vessel ratio. The model is at a high reading, which suggests downside going forward, as illustrated by the table below.

**Top left chart:** Weekly shipment volumes, 2026 ytd vs 2025.

**Top right chart:** Supra/Ultra AIS model. Extremely high readings suggest the market will fall in the near term, and extremely low readings suggest the market will rise.

**Bottom left chart:** The industrial metals index is composed of copper, aluminium, zinc and nickel, and weighted according to market value. The 9-month change of this index has a high correlation with the 9-month change of the Ultramax 1-year TC rate, and leads by about 3 months.

**Bottom right table:** This table shows the average statistical outcome following given readings of the model.

## Handysize

Through week 45, shipment volumes grew by an estimated 0.7% compared to last year. DWT supply growth remains at 3.2%. The market remains firmer than the weak headline figures suggest. In the near term, the softening of the Ultramax segment suggests the Handysize market will soon start falling.

**Top left chart:** The ratio between Rio-Recalada / Skaw-Passero (HS3) and SEA / Singapore-Japan (HS5) is usually a good predictor of tops and bottoms of the HS3 route. A higher ratio suggests a market top, and a lower ratio suggests a market bottom.

**Top right chart:** Through week 45, Handysize shipment volumes were 0.7% higher year on year, declining from the 3%+ figures seen in the last months. DWT supply growth was 3.2% at the latest reading.

**Bottom left chart:** The correlation between the S11TC and HS7TC is over 90%. The correlation is even higher when the S11TC index is shifted forward a few days, which means that movements in the S11TC index often lead the movements of the HS7TC.

**Bottom right chart:** The change in the price of copper is usually an accurate leading indicator for asset values.
