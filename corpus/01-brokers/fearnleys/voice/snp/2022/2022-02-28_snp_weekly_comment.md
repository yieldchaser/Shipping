## Market Commentary

As we end another week of escalating conflicts in Eastern Europe and as sanctions on Russian interests unravel, we continue to feel its effect on all shipping markets. Confusion around which specific entities are being sanctioned has paved the way for a number of tanker owners to proceed with Baltic and BSea voyages offering substantial risk premiums for Russian liftings. The majority of players however are opting for a "self-sanctioning" approach with refiners and traders shying away from Russian product. Should the situation remain or worsen we expect these soaring regional rates to continue their climb and provide wider support for freight across all geographies. The sale and purchase markets have seen a pick-up in activity in the VLCC sector with 5 units changing hands this week, breaking an extensive stretch of inactivity in the segment. A strengthening buying interest that is driving buyers to re-evaluate their bids to go meet a thinning list of available candidates in hope to capture the gains that are currently witnessed in the chartering markets. In a relative contrast to the volatility experienced by Tanker players, the Dry sectors are continuing to joy of steadily strengthening markets. A strength that can be felt through the most recent SnP transactions that have seen vessels achieve levels in excess of last dones. A dynamic that is particularly well illustrated by the sale of the 2012 Mitsui built MV "ORCHID" which achieved Usd 22.6m.
