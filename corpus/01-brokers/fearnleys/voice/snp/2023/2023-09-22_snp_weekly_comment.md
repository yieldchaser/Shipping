## Market Commentary

A relatively quiet week in the second-hand tanker market with much of the industry’s attention focused on the newbuilding story. 11% of the existing Suezmax fleet is now on order, this subsector seeing the highest number of orders in a single year in the past decade. Behind the scenes however, Aframax tanker interest is swelling, and owners are seemingly happy to part with tonnage, often at hugely attractive levels to what they initially purchased their ships for. Particularly FULMAR (115K DWT 2009 HANJIN HI), sold to Middle Eastern buyers at USD 39.5M, almost double what Union Maritime paid for the unit in 2019 at auction. Older large tankers seem to have cooled with the sale of VLCC GOOD NEWS (319K DWT 2002 HHI) at just under USD 35M, a unit that was asking over USD 40M before the summer. Pyxis has sold its eco MR tanker PYXIS EPSILON (50K DWT 2015 SPP) for a whopping USD 40.7M to US buyers. At this stage we can only speculate if the deal is linked to the US Tanker Security Program aimed at boosting access to commercial product tankers to safeguard oil shipping for its armed forces. Liquidity in the Dry sector continues to grow as it feels ‘now is the time’ to make a move. All sizes of modern Japanese built tonnage appear on most prospective buyers’ wish lists, thus making available units highly competitive. Those willing to look at slightly older units are seeing an increased availability of candidates, something which will excite potential asset players. In the Kamsarmax sector, WESTERN MONACO (81K DWT 2016 HANTONG) was sold for a firm USD 26M while NORD SUN (82K DWT 2013 TSUNEISHI CEBU) was bought by Greek buyers at USD 21.8M. Elsewhere, Capesize GRACIOUS ETERNITY (180K DWT 2009 IMABARI) was sold to Chinese buyers at USD 21.9M.
