## Market Commentary

The tanker charter market has a ‘summery’ feel to it this week, with looming production cuts and public holidays in China casting doubt over earnings. Differing strategies were making headlines in the S&P space, with Ridgebury confirming their exit from shipping, while Tor Olav Troim announcing Magni Partners’ intention to order two LNG dual-fuel VLCCs for 2026/2027 delivery at region $130m per ship – thus legitimising the much-speculated top end of the VLCC value curve. In the modern Suezmax sector, scrubber-fitted ELANDRA OSPREY (157K DWT HYUNDAI SAMHO 2018) was bought by EPS from Vitol for $75m, with sellers showing that shrewd asset plays can be actioned even for modern ships, having bought the unit for $62.5m back in 2019. Elsewhere, Ridgebury’s selling spree is over, as RIDGEBURY GALILEO (47K DWT HMD 2006) is sold for xs $19m. There were some unusual premiums being paid in the dry sector this week, despite the general bearish feeling in the market. Supramax CORINTHIAN EMERALD (57K DWT STX JINHAE 2012) was sold to Middle Eastern buyers for an eyebrow raising $19.25m, leaving many market participants scratching their heads. Vega Bulk exercised a purchase option on Ultramax HANTON TRADER III (63K DWT HANTONG 2014) at $23.6m, which helps price other Chinese built Ultramax units at similar ages on the market. Deltas between Chinese and Japanese tonnage are widening in this current slump which has encouraged more of a ‘wait and see’ approach, while some buyers are opting to change their requirements to solely focus on Japanese built units, despite the evident lack of available market tonnage. Either way, deal flow seems it will be an issue across dry for some time to come.
