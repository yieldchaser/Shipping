## Market Commentary

Despite the buoyant levels seen in transactions this week, there is an emerging sense of caution among second hand participants in the tanker market. Rates have been lacklustre in recent weeks, but given the winter markets are just around the corner, perhaps this caution will be short lived. In the J19 market, TC rates dipping below $20K/Day have done little to discourage investors. Woojin bought HORIN TRADER (19K DWT USUKI 2015) at USD 32.5M, a premium to last done 1 year younger and scrubber fitted TRF J19s which sold at the same price. CHEM STREAM (19K DWT SHITANOE SHIPBUILDING 2010) also sold at a firm USD 25.3M to Chinese buyers, showing the unrelenting appetite of Far Eastern buyers for this vessel type. VLCC SAKE (300K DWT IHI KURE 2005) selling at USD 40M will likely entice more owners of older tankers to consider exiting those positions, however it is worth adding that VLCCs have been of more interest to buyers over suez-afra sizes. While modern bulkers remain atop the shopping lists of most owners, older ships are cause for discussion among brokers and investors. A correction in value in the panamax/kamsarmax sector is potentially acting as a cautionary tale for those thinking these record asset values are here to stay. Tellingly, several Japanese owned Kansarmaxes in the 10 yr bracket have been withdrawn for sale as offers received were deemed too low. Kamsarmax sisters AM QUEBEC/HAMBURG (81K DWT NEW TIMES 2013) were bought by Chinese buyers at USD 18M each, a level that will correct similarly aged units by at least 10%. It is as yet unseen whether similar corrections will take place in other sub sectors, with most sellers not willing to be the first to reduce price ideas. What is sure though, is that asset players will be watching this sector very closely.
