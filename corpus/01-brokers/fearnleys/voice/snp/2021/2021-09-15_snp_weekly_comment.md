## Market Commentary

It is with no surprise that we are closing this week with yet another flurry of dry cargo transactions. From Handysize to Newcastlemax dry snp markets are joying of sustained liquidity, providing additional support and confidence to an ever increasing number of market players looking to acquire tonnage in view to yield the returns offered by an even more sustainable freight market. The Panamax/Kamsarmax segment seem to have captured the bulk of the enquiry this week. The sisterships “PEAK PEGASUS” & “PEAK LIBERTY” (82k dwt, 2013/2015, Tsuneishi Fukuyama) have been sold to Globus Maritime at a respective Usd 27m and Usd 30m while the 2019 Chinese built “SEACON SHANGHAI” was sold at a firm Usd 36m. The Japanese controlled “SEA VISION” (77k dwt, Imabari, 2015) invited offers on Wednesday after having north of 10 parties showing outright interest – best levels received are understood to be hovering around the Usd 29m mark, however the vessel remains in negotiation and we should be in a position to report further early next week. A similarly unsurprising state of affairs on the tanker front but with a persistent lack of activity. These subdued SnP markets are however not necessarily reflective of the underlying sentiment which is somehow positive with a building anticipation that better days are not far off. Notable news this week is an apparent pick up in demolition with 4 VLCCs reported to have hit the beaches. Increased scrap rates have indeed been driving pockets of tanker activity with a number of overaged crude carriers also finding further trading buyers over the past few weeks. A tonnage phase out which is of course welcome in an already tight supply environment for Tankers which have seen no new order inked over the whole month of August.
