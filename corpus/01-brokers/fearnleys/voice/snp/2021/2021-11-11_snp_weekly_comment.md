## Market Commentary

Still a bumpy road in the tanker space and somewhat lack of clear direction. We see signs and growing interest, but the market is still in need for more steady guidance. Hafnia (BW) has again proved their strong belief in consolidation and enjoyed the headlines this week with their massive acquisition of the Oaktree controlled Chemical Tankers Inc (CTI) fleet. The transaction adds 32 modern, Eco, IMO II vessels and consist of 6 MRs, 18 Handies and 8 x 25k stainless steel tankers – all Korean/Japanese built. The equity portion was funded through issuance of new shares on a NAV-for-NAV basis and leaves Hafnia the largest operator in the segment and Oaktree as the 2nd largest shareholder at approx. 20% of Hafnia (Oaktree controlled ca 95% of CTI). Fearnley Securities is acting as financial advisors to CTI in the transaction as announced on 11th November 2021. On the crude side, we have seen a new sale in the Suezmax segment with M/T Densa Whale (158k dwt, blt 2012 Hyundai) sold for Usd 32-32.5 m to Greek buyers. There are limited references in this age, but we would say the price is somewhat lower than what we would expect. More in line with expectations was the sale of M/T Astro Perseus (159k dwt, blt 2004 Hyundai) at Usd 18.5 m. More interest also piling up on older Aframaxes and we expect transaction numbers to pick up. Subdued Dry cargo rates and uncertainty stemming from falling forward freight agreements translate in a softening of buying interest. At least relative to the unhindered wave of buyers that the markets were so accustomed to. Sellers seem to have taken a standby approach rather than going to meet testing bids at levels lower than last done - saying that we do expect the odd correction that would traditionally come from Japanese sellers transacting at best.
