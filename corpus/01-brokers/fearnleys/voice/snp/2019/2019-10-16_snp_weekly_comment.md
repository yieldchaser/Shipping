## Market Commentary

Nothing can escape gravity, not even shipping rates. The rollercoaster ride in the tanker rates seen end of last week, leading to sky high hypothetical levels in asset prices, have taken a breath after a series of deals on subs failing and bringing the expectations down. We expect higher benchmarks going forward in the tanker sector and buyers biting the bullet as not only the spot rates but also the period market will likely support the price tags if the market establishes at these levels. The deals in the making and the ones already being reported done are reflecting same. Ridgebury Tankers has reportedly sold 3 x 2001 built VLCCs for region USD 100mio enbloc. The vessels were purchased for region USD 22mio each end 2017. Greek buyers, namely Avin and Union Maritime, have reportedly picked up the three Brightoil Aframaxes at auction in the region 20/21.5mio each, reflecting the vessels having all certificates overdue and must sail straight into the dock before enjoying this market. Next benchmark for Suezmaxes of the older vintage, will most likely be the 2 x 2000 Samsung built ladies, Jag Lakshita and Jag Lateef owned by GESCO, who are now inviting inspections. The bulkers move sideways and with all eyes on tankers, one should not underestimate the potential upside going forward on acquiring quality tonnage at reasonable prices taking the current charter market into account. After a series of withdrawn vessels from Japanese owners due to price expectations not being met just prior to the tanker spike, we are again seeing modern tonnage being sold. Buyers who are not ready for the high tanker price tag, might increase the already existing competition, leading to increased prices here as well. Besides the reported concluded deals, we understand the 2019 Oshima built Ultramax MV Adventure III have seen offers in region USD 27.5mio, illustrating that quality modern tonnage are attracting firm levels.
