## Market Commentary

In the last few hours, it has been announced that the Strait of Hormuz is completely open to commercial shipping traffic as Israel have agreed a 10-day ceasefire with Lebanon. Iran has said that while this ceasefire is in place, the Strait is open. Worth noting that the US blockade of ships calling to Iran is (at the time of writing) still in place. Shipowners will likely approach this with trepidation in the next 24-48 hours however, it seems to be a step in the right direction for the overall global economic outlook. Given the length of the war, enough time has now elapsed for owners to reposition previously redundant vessels, meaning the remaining cargoes will have increasing competition and should see some downside. Now if cargoes come available in the Persian Gulf, then owners will likely be scrambling to position ships appropriately. A rather poignant reminder of where we are in terms of pricing was seen this week with sale of 20-year-old VLCC KASAGISAN (302K DWT Mitsui 2006) for USD 61M, changing hands to a willing buyer for 3 times the expected residual value. Elsewhere, buyers are in hot pursuit of prompt MR tankers, with this week scrubber fitted ARDMORE ENGINEER (49K DWT STX 2014) bought by GESCO for USD 35.5M. Activity on dry remains healthy, helped by improving rates and lower bunker costs (from recent highs) seemingly giving confidence to buyers. Sentiment will likely improve on a potential easing situation in the Middle East however, it is just too early to tell what is going to happen. Decisions can be reversed very quickly, and the situation remains fragile at best. This week, separate Chinese buyers bought Newcastlemax pair RTM CARTIER / RTM ZHENG HE (206K DWT HHIC Philippines 2012) for USD 46M each.
