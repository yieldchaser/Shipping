## Market Commentary

EAST Saudi acceptances for May were published over the weekend and since then we have seen another handful of fixtures concluded in the East. 1st decade of May in the AG seems pretty covered by now, and next fixing window is focused around 2nd decade of May. The last done deals have mostly been done in the high usd 40s Baltic, and it is expected that rates will stay in the 40s unless we see an improvement on Arb economics in the West (which subsequently could pull more ships back to the US Gulf). We have also seen a couple of fob tenders from Middle Eastern suppliers marketed this week for end May loading, but it remains to be seen if spot shipping will be needed for these cargos time of writing. WEST The market in the West seems to have taken a bit of a breather this week, and it seems charterers are awaiting some clear direction before making their moves. With the sentiment from last week in mind, it was clear the owners had expected rates to come up in the 90’s this week, but it has yet to materialize for the standard transpacific route. One fixture has been done at levels very close to this, but not for the standard destination. It is not unlikely though that we’ll see an increase in rates, as the list of remaining available ships for loading May cargoes, are in-fact rather short. Although, rates may still turn out to be sliding sideways, as it remains to be seen how many cargoes we’ll have before the market start to focus on the June cargoes in a week or two. After a couple of weeks with a discounted freight in the West, the balance shifted last week, and is now established with a more “normal” western premium whereas the East trades in the low 30k’s per day and the west trades in the mid-30s.
