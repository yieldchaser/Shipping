## Market Commentary

EAST The shipping market in the East has been less active compared to the last few weeks, but we are still seeing a couple of deals concluded behind the scenes. Last done deal is being reported around low/mid 50s Baltic, and current fixing window is mainly focused on last decade of May basis MEG. Going forward we expect these sort of freight levels to maintain, and we also expect to start seeing early June cargos from next week after the acceptances start to come out. WEST A handful of deals were concluded during the course of last week, at rates hovering in the mid-to-high USD 80's to the USD 90 per ton mark (Houston/Chiba basis) and around mid-to-high USD 40's for the Houston/Flushing route. The number of vessels still remaining for May dates is relatively low and therefore, it seems the arb will be the decisive factor, as to whether or not the overhang of ships going into June, is going to be there or not. The majority of the May lifting program is seemingly done, however, there are some spot FOBs for sale which may absorb some of the length, if the arb allows. From a pure supply/demand of ships point of view, it is likely that we will see the market remaining relatively flat unless the economics dictate otherwise. With the current freight levels, the transpacific route is currently trading at a marginal premium to the Eastern index route, whilst the transatlantic route is trading at around 15% premium.
