## Market Commentary

EAST As the supply and demand for spot open tonnage versus available cargoes for current fixing window are still very much in imbalance, the bull run that we have witnessed on freight during the last couple of weeks has continued, and Baltic has just breached USD 60 Ras Tanura/Chiba for the first time since April. At time of writing, there are reportedly still uncovered shipping requirements in August, while remaining available ships are few (if any at all). This will continue to add pressure on freight rates. However, as the fixing window is currently quite far ahead in time, there is also an extra risk of ships running late for their laycans due to uncertain itineraries, and we might see ships come back open again at a later stage. WEST Freight rates have strengthened again in the West this week as the fixing window progresses further into September. However, there are questions about how much longer this trend can continue. On one hand, traders argue that arb economics are unsupportive of freight at such high levels and therefore something will have to give. On the other, shipping still seems to be in short supply when considering the balance of the fleet across East and West of Suez. Regardless, most charterers still have some time to determine whether to cancel FOBs or fix in. Combined with the fact that Singapore is off on Friday, this could mean a quiet end to the week.
