## Market Commentary

WEST: As a result of bad weather in the Pacific and delays at disports in both East and West, some of the ships which were open in the final decade of May ex US have slipped into June. While there are still a few outstanding FOBs in the US in May, we count roughly 30 fixtures having been done for the month. In addition, some fixing has already begun for June; these two things suggest that players are now mostly balanced for May. Positions lists look reasonably long going into June, with a mixture of relets and Owner-controlled vessels, and this is likely to put some pressure on rates in the short term. While it is very difficult to make any assertions given the amount of black swans in the market lately, it is important to remember that a large number of liftings in the US must go ahead regardless of the arb. In addition, a high number of liftings globally in the last couple of months and longer voyages to India and Indonesia may have a lagged bullish effect on rates. EAST: The shipping market in the East has been very uneventful this week, and only new fixture to report was on the back of a freight tender from an oil major loading Australia. With the last rush of fixing that we saw a couple of weeks back, most market players ended up fixing ships way ahead of what’s considering the normal fixing window, and as a consequence things are expected to remain quiet for a while. One Indian major quoted the market for a cargo loading in the MEG during last decade of May, and there are talks of potentially a couple more for same period to be quoted later, but would likely need some time before the latter is firmed up. Meanwhile freight availability remains fairly balanced with more Owner controlled tonnage available for rest of May as opposed to trader relets.
