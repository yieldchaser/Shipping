## Market Commentary

EAST The MEG market has so far this week been the one and only driver of activity in the VLGC market. At the end of last week, big question were looming around what’s next for freight in the wake of the Saudi acceptances for September being released the coming Sunday; since Monday, five fixtures have been concluded for laycans in the 1st decade of the month and unless there are further discharge port delays or sudden fixing appetite from the USG that tightens the vessel availability, the market looks set to continue hovering the high USD 80s per ton on a Ras Tanura/Chiba equivalent basis. WEST The fixing count in the USG for September has reached 26 deals and as such is slightly behind being on par with previous months’ fixing records. At the time of writing for September there are still four out of seven vessels on both position lists, which purports to explain why rates (in both basins) remain resilient. It is likely that we will see a few more September deals being done in the USG and October fixing should not be far away. There is a cargo tender ex Angola for loading end September which is likely to attract attention during next week. Activity has picked up in the 2nd and 3rd decades of September, with a total of 20 fixtures registered between the two periods. Little to no activity this week since the Pertamina tender earlier this week, while broader sentiment remains subdued as players await clearer market direction following Aramco’s September acceptances. In general, shipping activity has otherwise been muted, awaiting further cargo availability.
