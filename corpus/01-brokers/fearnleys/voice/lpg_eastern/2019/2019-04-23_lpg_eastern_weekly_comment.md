## Market Commentary

**MEG:** Following the rise in crude price, May CP price has surpassed April CP, now being $20 higher at 535$ at the time of writing. May/June CP is still in heavy backwardation but continues to narrow from the previous $46 to $28 within this week. Freight has continuously soared, whereby eroding the potential CFR profits. FOB lifters without ship length, are keen to resell their FOB’s rather than bringing the CFR volumes to the Far East themselves. May FOB’s, especially with lifting in the last decade of May, is reported to have been concluded and mid/high single digit discounts to May CP.

**FAR EAST**

: The rally hasn’t ended, and prices are edging up by a day by day basis. Until this Wednesday 23k propane for 1h June delivery was traded at June FEI plus $30. Cargoes for 2h May delivery is being bid at May FEI plus $50 and no offers seen. May FEI/CP has narrowed rapidly from minus $20 to near flattish during this week, partly because the tightness in front month for Far East market and also because positions are being rolled into June. Until backlog delays are cleared thoroughly from US, we will likely see the Eastern tightness extending into 1h June. Chinese importers have more or less finished their May buying program and are now looking at their June schedule. Recent deal premium levels has varied from $50’s level to $70s on May CP linkage, subject to cargo delivery time range. PDH margins have been squeezed by higher Propane costs and weaker Propylene demand, we do however expect demand from this sector will stay stable as most units have substantial downstream linkage, yielding higher value products. Indonesia’s Pertamina was reported to have awarded their two buy tenders on FOB basis, one middle May for lifting ex AG at May CP plus mid-single digit premium and one late May at CP minus. FPC has awarded their buy tender for 44k propane delivered to Mailiao during 1-10 June at June MOPJ minus high 40s, with 6 offers received. Some players reckon it’s higher than expectations as compared to a window deal on the same day, while others assessed higher value for cargo delivered early June, plus higher freight costs.
