## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Volumes of LPG direct from the marine terminal owners remains limited, but this is not to imply that product is not available since offers for October are littered across the calendar. Most talks for Oct are centered around the 21-23cpg range, depending on timing/location, etc. Meanwhile, Nov talks are still thin but early days values are pegged right at about 20cpg. Initial talks for Dec are in the upper teens, but nothing is yet known to have been accomplished. With regards the US stock report of yesterday, the EIA revealed a 1.00mmbbl build to an overall total of 93.10mmbbls as of 23 Aug, or about 97.5% of last year. The 5-yr historical average build for Week 34 was 2.8mmbbls, so from that angle the data was disappointing. However, a large number of market observers were calculating a build of a half-million barrels, so from perspective the data appeared to be more accurate than not. In any case, on a Days of Supply basis, the EIA report a current level of 110.9 days, which compares to the 2024 YTD average of 73 days, the 2023 average of 85 days, the 2022 average of 61 days, and an average of 49 days in 2021. In short, there is a sufficient volume of propane in tank, but further to the point, the current Quarterly average (for Q3 2024) is 107 days, which is the second-highest Quarterly average since 2020. NWE LPG imports for august totaled 550,000mt, the majority of which has gone toward feeding petchem plants in the region. The strong imports are likely one of catalysts for the European propane market steep drop seen Wednesday- as large cargo values dropped to CIF ARA flat from +27 the day prior, following an unmet offer from a major regional petchem on Wednesday. In the North Sea, Karsto’s gas processing will see output cut in half, starting today, before a full shutdown tomorrow (August 30th) for yearly maintenance that will extend until the 20th of September. At time of writing, the August propane/ naphtha spread has come in from-100pmt to -50pmt, theoretically dissuading the further imports, but so far the LPG crackers have continued to operate at high rates.
