## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Heavy inventory draws are the combined result of extremely cold weather across most of North America last week and reduced production levels, which was also affected by the storms and impacted deliveries of product to the Gulf Coast from Conway, SCOOP and STACK. This, in turn, gave cause for a wide differential to emerge between TET and Non-TET pricing which has lingered. As of this writing TET values were in the 83.25cpg region while Non-TET prices ranged up to 86.50cpg. As for the inventories themselves, the EIA yesterday reported that propane stock levels in the US declined by a staggering 8.4mmbbls during the week ending 19 Jan for an overall total of 67.9mmbbls, or about 90% of the same time last year. Generally speaking, most of the market had been anticipating a decline of around 4mmbbls (though a couple of outliers did estimate a 7mmbbl decline). This week’s draw was in addition to the generally expected draws that were recorded over the past few weeks, but also to the 9mmbbl reduction seen at the end of December, but which is believed to have been a correction from mis-reported data prior to that. In any case, stock levels which throughout Q3 and Q4 2023 and were once riding well over historical high’s have now been substantially reduced. Lastly, terminal fees along the Gulf Coast in Jan and 1H Feb were seen mostly in the mid single-digit range but have for late Feb and March increased sharply and can now be found in the double-digit range. An 11.50cpg offer for late Feb is on the table, and a 13.00cpg transaction was reported for early Mar. This is due to an expected increase in the volume of US exports during those timeframes. NWE The physical window in Europe slid in to a discount as trade discussions took a backseat to price swings across The Pond narrowed the arb. The widening premium of LPG to Dutch TTF on an MMBtu basis has increased LPG production in the region. The reduction in freight rates from the US has not benefited the European market as it has pushed US FOB values up into double digits premiums essentially closing the arb to Europe. So far the import slate for February remains thin and although Europe remains in a low demand environment US imports will be necessary to make it through February. A butane bid was shown in the window in the hope of drawing tons from the region to the Mediterranean, raising butane value to 86.4% February Naphtha.

**MED**

 The arb to the region form the US is closed, leading to traders with shorts showing interest in NWE butane tons with STS options. Butane in Northwest Europe remains attractively valued vs US tons, though the availability of tons at the published price of 86.4% February Naphtha in NWE remains to be seen.
