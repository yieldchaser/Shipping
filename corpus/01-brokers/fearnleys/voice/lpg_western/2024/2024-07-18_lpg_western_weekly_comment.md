## Market Commentary

AMERICAS With the passing of Hurricane Beryl last week, the US Gulf Coast LPG terminals – and the corresponding waterways – conducted assessments and soundings, which resulted in relatively modest overall delays of days (not weeks). There are some lingering after-effects, and product remains tight, but no physical hardware damage is known to have occurred. Terminal fees, meanwhile, have remained robust and transactions are known to have occurred at (and above) 20cpg. Current offers are seen in the low 20cpg range for Aug and into September. Given the general weakness in the freight arena, the arbitrage to the East supports these price levels, and until either product becomes more readily available or freight turns in a different direction, more of the same is expected. Meanwhile, the EIA yesterday reported that overall US propane stock levels rose by 4.6mmbbls – well beyond the 5-yr historical average for Week 28 of +1.7mmbbls, but this is directly related to the power outages which came as a result of Hurricane Beryl. It is presently calculated that US propane stocks will reach to between 101-104mmbbls as of the first week of October, the conclusion of the traditional US stock building period. And this is considered an entirely ample supply, which might only be bolstered further if crude strength carries on and domestic production remains robust. NWE Two unmet bids in yesterday's window echoed the sentiment of tightness across the European LPG market, as North Sea maintenance, reduced Russian LPG exports, and steep competition from high FEI netbacks, lifted CIF ARA premiums into the double digits. The tightness has been cause for concern for some European buyers looking to bolster inventories in the build up to strong seasonal demand- but help is on the way. Following a slow start to July imports from the US, over 200,000 tons is expected to discharge in the region in the balance of July, bringing the total number close to 500,000 tons, up almost 100,000 tons compared to June. Whether or not the increase in US imports will be sufficient- remains to be seen, but for now, strong premiums and a narrowing propane/ naphtha spread seem to paint a picture of tightness in the short term.
