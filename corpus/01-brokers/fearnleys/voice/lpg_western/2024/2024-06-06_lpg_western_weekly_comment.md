## Market Commentary

AMERICAS After a strong, unexpected storm in Houston a couple of weeks ago caused power outages and modest export delays on the Gulf Coast, the EIA yesterday reported that stock levels rose by 2.5mmbbls – almost exactly as expected – to a 31 May overall total of 68.80mmbbls, or 95% of last year but 137% of two years ago. Based on average build rates through the end of Sep, the first-week of October is calculated to see an overall total of between 99-102mmbbls – which is clearly sufficient for all varieties of domestic demand, as well as export volumes. Meanwhile, as to the US spot export market, avails are tight, and while June is largely complete, July saw terminal fee values gradually moved up from 10cpg (the level at which at least 4 transactions were concluded), to 11cpg, to 11.50cpg, and – finally – current offers which approach 13cpg. Aside from the tightness of the market itself, one of the primary concerns remains the Panama Canal, and the auction levels which are being recorded. While auction rates have recently been in decline $585k was reportedly paid this week), there remains risk that at the time of booking canal slots might be tight, and an elevated auction fee might have to be paid. At Mont Belvieu, propane prices are mirroring the ebbs and flows of crude oil, and on a percentage basis propane seems locked in at about 38-39% of WTI. Sentimentally, there remains an expectation that the ratio might still decline with the official commencement of summer. May propane values started at 74cpg but currently propane is in the 69cpg range.
