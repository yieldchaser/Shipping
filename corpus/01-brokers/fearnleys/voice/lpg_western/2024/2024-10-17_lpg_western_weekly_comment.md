## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Product availability remains limited from the US – particularly from the terminals themselves - and spot FOB terminal resale fees continue to be robust, with October having closed out at above 30cts/gal, and a more recent 4:0 transaction was just seen in the mid-20cpg for November. One of the supporting factors has been the relatively soft freight market, which has disallowed vessel owners from grabbing a larger share of the arb, which on its own has retained the bulk of it strength. Today the EIA reported that US propane inventory levels rose sharply as of 11th Oct – and above expectations – to 103.10mmbbls, a 3.3mmbbl week-over-week build, where estimates ranged from +1.8 to +2.6mmbbls. This should motivate buy-side interest as it will likely cause MB to soften. NWE From the end of September until last week, large cargo propane pricing surged by $61/t to $627.25/mt CIF Amsterdam-Rotterdam-Antwerp as markets braced for a possible retaliation from Israel following a missile attack from Iran. The price for large cargos swung from -$10/mt discounts versus swaps to plus $13/mt premiums, That quickly led to the narrowing of the propane naphtha spread at the beginning of October. Large cargo butane values also shot up by $44/mt to $625/mt across over the same time frame, as seasonal blending demand leapt while US exports to the region feigned. This week however, the market has come off some, closing yesterday at $583/metric ton, effectively widening the propane/naphtha spread to -$46/mt, from an the 18 month low of -$30pmt seen at the beginning of the October. In the North Sea, LPG exports remain on hold as maintenance at fields at Karsto is reduced to 70% until  26 October.
