## Market Commentary

AMERICAS While the recent storms and disruptions along the Texas Gulf Coast have (mostly) passed and things have begun to settle down, there remain lingering aftereffects which some of the Gulf Coast terminals continue to have to work around. Delays of between just a few days and just over a week are reported, and in the case of the Freeport shipping channel, draft restrictions of 39 feet are expected to remain in place for a period. Otherwise, with no Atlantic basin hurricanes forming, things should return to normal by around the end of July. That said, August availability is tight. Most of the available laycans are in the hands of resellers, each of whom is pointing to the robust US/FarEast arb and relatively relaxed freight market while offering product in the 22-23cpg range – which is where a series of transactions are known to have recently occurred. Meanwhile, stock levels of propane in the US continue to climb. Per the EIA report for the week ending 19 July, overall inventories now stand at 84.50mmbbls – or just about 99% of last year at the same time, and 137% of what was recorded in 2022. And based on average build levels over the past 5 years, the latest calculations point to an overall total of ~103mmbbls at the first week of October, which (to be repetitive) is entirely sufficient given the demand from the export market, the petchem arena, and the remaining domestic US requirements. Product values have not entirely succumbed to the pressures of the growing stock levels, though, and at around 77cts/gal, propane is still 40+ percent of WTI.
