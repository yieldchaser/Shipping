## Market Commentary

AMERICAS After a resoundingly successful and very well attended OPIS seminar in Houston last week, just after which an 18.50cpg Dec transaction was recorded, this week started with a 19.50cpg Dec deal, which was followed then by a 20.00cpg Dec fixture, each for 4:0. Offers for 2:2 parcels were attempting to achieve an average of 13.00cpg, but late yesterday this was not only accomplished, but exceeded. Offers now are squarely in the mid teens for mixed-ratio cargoes. Meanwhile, talk of slow run rates at Targa’s Gulf Coast LPG marine terminal has been directly squashed. It is not true, and – in fact – record rates are being achieved. As for propane inventories, the EIA today was expected to report a small draw of around 400,000bbls. However, the market was taken aback when for the week ending 8th November the EIA recorded a 2.1mmbbl draw for an overall total stock level of 98.40mmbbls. The market was puzzled. NWE Weak downstream demand, the marginal propane naphtha spread (-$50’s), and a very mild start to winter have effectively narrowed the US/NWE arb over the last few weeks. As such, imports to the region that had regularly eclipsed half a million tons in recent months have been marked at 300,000 -350,000 tons (depending on the heading of a vessel currently loading at Marcus Hook) as of this writing. The low CIF ARA c3 and c4 prices have allowed for North Sea producers to begin re-injecting liquids back into the natural gas stream when economically incentivized to do so. The significant backwardation, which extends all the way out the curve until June, could make it difficult to attract tons from the US, and with some cold weather forecasted in the coming weeks, it could make for an interesting dynamic as winter kicks off.
