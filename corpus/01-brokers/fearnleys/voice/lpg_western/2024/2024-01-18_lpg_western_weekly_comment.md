## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Harshly cold weather across the entirety of the US both last week and this has given cause for the EIA to report a decline in domestic propane stock levels of 2.8mmbbls, and thus bringing overall stock levels as of 12 Jan to 76.3mmbbls, or almost exactly the same as last year. The arctic weather this week will no doubt cause yet another decline in stocks, but thereafter a warming trend is being forecast which should relieve some of the pressure. Meanwhile, Mont Belvieu propane values have increased by over 14cts/gal since the start of the month despite the (still) relatively robust stock levels. Current market values are pegged at over 84.00cts/gal, up from the upper 60s on the first of December. Terminal fees have been seen in the mid/upper 6cts/gal for Jan and Feb, with the number of offers exceeding the number of bidders. NWE The cold snap in Europe is coming to an end, keeping the TOT window at a $5/MT discount to February CIF ARA for 2H February arrivals. January imports to the region are expected to reach 486,000MT. There is hope that restocking will occur in February after this recent cold weather, but in general Europe remains in a low demand environment. Healthy North Sea production is expected to continue as Dutch TTF values remain below propane and butane on an MMbtu basis. The large cargo butane market remains subdued, assessed at 84% February Naphtha.

**MED**

 Trans-Atlantic imports to the region remain high even though VLGCs are no longer transiting the Suez Canal. Over 550,000MT will be imported to the region in January, a 13 month high, with large imports from Dortyol to Mohammedia.
