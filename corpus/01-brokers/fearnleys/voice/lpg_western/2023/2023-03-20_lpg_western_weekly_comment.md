## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Were it not for the fireworks following the weekly EIA data releases, the market would otherwise be mostly eventless. A handful of seminars, conferences, meetings, travel and Spring Break have left many away from their desks and conspired to limit activity levels in what has become a generally dull market. The EIA, however, posted a rather unexpected propane inventory build last week, followed this week by a surprisingly robust draw of 2.3mmbbls for the week ending 17 March. That decline brought overall total US stock levels from 61mmbbls last week to current levels of 58.7mmbbls – though even so, present inventory levels are 174% of last year and 142% of two years ago. In spite of the fact that stock levels are very near to 5-year high’s, the draw yesterday might lead one to anticipate that prices might increase – particularly as crude values have increased from below $67/bbl last week to over $71/bbl currently. And it did. Non-TET Mont Belvieu propane last week was marked at 69.75cpg where values last night concluded at 74.50cpg. Terminal fees on the Gulf Coast are known to have been fixed for April at between mid-6 and low 8cpg. May is at this point likely within a similar range but little talk has yet been heard for the forward market. A series of Mexican tenders have been issued and awarded, with one West Coast requirement presently outstanding. Likewise, pre-winter South American demand was recently covered on a spot basis as at least one – but perhaps two – spot cargoes were reported fixed for April lifting. NWE The TOT window continues to see activity from petrochemical traders, and the general mood is that CIF ARA flat is fair value. Sellers have been popping up at premiums to April CIF ARA, buyers at discounts. Nothing has come together in the middle yet for April cargoes. CIF ARA propane values rose as crude saw a recovery. Over 340,000MT of US LPG is expected to find it’s way to Europe in March partly due to the arb opening in February and partly due to the huge export volumes form the US. Butane has dipped to below 80% March naphtha as a large cargo offer continues to be seen.

**MED**

 France’s refinery strike continues to reduce demand with almost 60% of it’s 1.15 mn b/d of refining capacity offline. That has not stopped two US MGC cargoes heading to the region for early April arrival.
