## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Export volumes of 1.8mmbbls/day are the primary cause of this week’s reported 2.5mmbbl propane inventory decline, well beyond the average 5-year end-March draw of 514,00bbls, but directionally anticipated and surprising only because of the volume. Current stock levels of propane in the U.S. stand at 56.2mmbbls, or 3.2mmbbls under the 5-year high of 59.424mmbbls, and more than 23.5mmbbls above the 5-year low of 32.671mmbbls. Despite this, however, Mont Belvieu propane prices have risen with crude oil gains and are presently marked at (or above) 80cpg, which represents the peak of a two-week bull cycle. The relationship between WTI and propane remains effectively static, however, as prices of each have recently moved in tandem, averaging ~45% on a YTD basis and presently pegged at ~46%. Terminal fees have been challenging to accurately assess due to the recent conference and seminar schedule – which resulted in an overall lack of activity - but for 2H April have been established in the low/mid 5cpg range. May is notionally around the 6cpg level, with June mildly higher, still. NWE The TOT window continues to be offered, with large cargo physical values at April CIF ARA +$1/MT. Europe is importing a large volume of US LPG with 7 VLGC scheduled to arrive in the first half of April. The wide April propane naphtha spread of minus $166/MT is keeping petchem demand high and with crude oil values increasing the spread will remain wide. The downward VLGC freight correction seen this week will take some time to have an effect on the European market as much of the supply is imported on TC vessels. Additionally a large proportion of Europe’s trades are supplied on MGCs and smaller vessels, these spot markets are tight and have more resilient freight rates. This freight resilience could see the East West spread, today at minus $60pmt narrow and see further US volumes imported to Europe. Butane in Europe continues to be weak with few signs of additional demand.

**MED**

 Refinery strikes in Fance continue to disrupt opertations at Lavera. March export volume was half of the 40,000MT exported in February which was down from 70,000MT in Janaury. Length in northwest Europe in March has picked up the slack in local supply.
