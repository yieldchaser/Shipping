## Market Commentary

AMERICAS The trio of conferences scheduled for both this week and last has left the market dismally quiet with little to report other than a relatively small draw as calculated by the EIA. Stock levels last week were recorded at 60.6mmbbls, where this week – the week ending 03 March - the overall total now stands at 60.1mmbbls, a decline of a half-million barrels where a minimum draw of 750,000bbls to as much as 2.3mmbbls was being forecast. This puts inventories for the first week of March at 169% of last year and 145% of two years ago. The 5-year average draw for this week – the 8th of the year – is 1.648mmbbls while the 5-year average stock level for the same period is 44.175mmbbls. Terminal fees topped out in the low ‘teens’ for March and are backwardated well into the summer months, with April talked at 7cts, May just a bit lower, still, and June currently presumed in the mid-single digits. NWE The market seems to have decided winter is over! March/April backwardation narrowed from $52/MT Friday to $35/MT at the close Wednesday, showing the a relaxing of the prompt tightness. This was coupled with the oturight drop in propane value in the region by 10% this week to below $600pmt. Though this may show more of the underlying market conditions as crude oil lost value early week. Additionallly over 300,000MT of US LPG is set to land in the region in March, as the end February and early March tightness pushed CIF ARA to a better netback than FEI. The propane nahptha spread remains favourable for propane at -$120/MT and with crude oil bouncing back today propane will remain preferable to naphtha. The TOT offer shown in the window was not renewed and the large cargo physical premium is assessed at March CIF ARA +$12.5/MT.

**MED**

 The region haas been quiet as North African butane stocks look to be enough to get through the remainder of winter. The large NorthWest European imports couple with North Sea production increasing has Medittareanean impoters waiting for offers rather than searching for cargoes.
