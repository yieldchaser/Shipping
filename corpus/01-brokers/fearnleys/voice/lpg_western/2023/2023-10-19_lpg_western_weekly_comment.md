## Market Commentary

AMERICAS The EIA this week revealed its propane inventory data which showed a build of 800,000bbls for an overall total of 102.3mmbbls, or nearly 120% of last year and almost 140% of two years ago. The 800k bbl increase was not terribly surprising and the market took it in stride since predictions were rather wildly all over the board. Some were calling for a draw of as much as 800,000bbls, while others were looking for a 3mmbbl build. In cases when the range of anticipation is so wide it effectively means that the market lacks true confidence, and in this case likely references the past couple of EIA reports which were deemed in need of correction. Meanwhile, the trio of Force Majeure declarations at the Gulf Coast terminals continues to plague the market, though it is expected at this point that two of the three will shortly be rescinded (if they haven’t been already). In any case, product availability remains largely limited to re-sellers, many of whom have already committed their volumes. As such, and based on the current arb, terminal fees remain robust. Presently the spot FOB terminal fee for propane is pegged at around 13cpg, and for butane at 10cpg. And while the volatility in the crude arenas – which during October has seen $7/bbl swings - continues to push and pull MB propane values, the ratio of propane to crude continues within a modest range of between 33-35%. NWE The TOT window has been active this week, without any deals being done. Buying interest for 8-12 November delivery pushed the assessed physical value to a $3/MT premium to November CIF ARA. While the East / West spread has come in to $100/MT for November traders are looking to place VLGCs in the region vs heading East with netback values from the US near parity. LPG imports to the region are expected to be around 550,000MT in October, and although the low demand environment continues imports should increase if netback values remain competitive with the East market. Butane at 79% November Naphtha while demand is increasing due to blending demand and the arrival of colder weather.

**MED**

 Suez canal announced it would be increasing transit fees, transit for LPG vessels could rise by 15% beginning January 15th, 2024. Two butane coaster loading from Marcus Hook will be heading to Tunisia, booked when coaster freight was affordable vs larger sized vessels.
