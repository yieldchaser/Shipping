## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Despite a widening arb to the East little activity has been noted as August is mostly sold out, save for a couple of lingering parcels which are being offered either CFR or include butane. Sep is presently subdued but talks have begun to surface as early winter-related demand requirements emerge. US propane inventories were recorded by the EIA at 85.7mmbbls as of 21 Jul, an increase of 1mmbbls over the week prior. The average build for the 29th week of the year is 1.716mmbbls, and expectations were calling for stocks to rise by some 1.9mmbbls. The relatively weak stock build is likely due (in part) to tie-in’s at Targa (due to its expansion efforts) and Enterprise (due to its PDH-related efforts). Furthermore, the ongoing and persistent heat wave in Texas has made it difficult to achieve -42c, the temperature at which fully refrigerated vessels load most efficiently. Meanwhile, terminal fees continue to plod slowly upwards (and in a relatively quiet environment), with a reported fixture in Aug of 6.75cpg. Talks for Sep start in the 7s and will work from there, but buy-side interest seems to remain in the 6s for the moment. Latin American demand levels are muted, as Southern Hemisphere winter-related requirements are being satisfied by term contract deliveries. NWE The TOT window continues to be well offered with two 1H August delivery parcels being shown. One of the offers was from the petrochemical sector, which lent a bearish feel to the mid-August European market. Strong crude oil has supported European LPG paper values and kept the trans-Atlantic Arb open. The European market remains in a low demand environment and though the arb is open cargoes are finding it difficult to find homes. Another factor has been the low natural gas price, which has pushed Dutch TTF below propane and butane. This will increase North Sea LPG production as LPG will no longer be spiked into the natural gas stream. Which is starting to be seen, North Sea exports are expected to be 335,000MT in July, up from an average of 255,000MT in Q1 2023. Butane values remain at 69% August Naphtha, though outright value has increased with stronger naphtha values.

**MED**

 Butane in the region has been strengthening this week, pulled up by crude and naphtha rises. There are areas of North African demand, although these are not being worked with any urgency.
