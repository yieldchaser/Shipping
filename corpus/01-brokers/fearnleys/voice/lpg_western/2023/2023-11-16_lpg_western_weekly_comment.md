## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Although it is now raining in Panama, the declining water levels in Lake Gatun (which feeds the insatiable appetite of the Canal itself) have taken center stage and become an entirely global issue as the number of daily transits has been reduced (and is scheduled in the coming months to continue falling) SHARPLY. That has led to competition for the remaining slots which are being auctioned and a much wider use of the alternative routes: the Suez Canal and the Cape of Good Hope. In each case, prices and freight rates have increased wildly. Auction payments of $2m, $3m, and as much as $4m are known to have been made, while a $100/mt premium is being placed over the standard Baltic Hou/Chi (via Panama) rate for the ~15-20 additional sailing days which are required for passage via Suez or Good Hope (respectively). The combined impact of these issues has led to downward pressure on US spot terminal fees which have been halved over the course of November. Previous offers were seen at around the 15cpg mark, but buyers have been reluctant to engage beyond the mid/single digits. Meanwhile, the EIA reported this week – after a week’s hiatus - that domestic US propane inventories as of 10 Nov stand at 99.7mmbbls, or about 14% higher than mid-Nov last year. Stock levels are clearly beyond the 5-year high levels and expected to be fully sufficient for both export volumes and winter-related requirements. NWE The TOT window continues to be active, with both bids and offers being show. So far delivery dates have not lined up for a deal to get done, TOT cargoes valued at CIF ARA November flat. November imports split between petchem feedstock 270,000MT and retail 170,000MT. Increased winter retail demand has not started. The drop in Brent will narrow the propane naphtha spread though this is unlikely to affect the largely stable low petchem demand. Panama Canal issues will keep the East / West spread wide, December E/W $205/MT today, as traders continue to offer tons into the region en route East via Suez. The butane market has been very quiet and remains at 81% Novmeber naphtha.

**MED**

 LPG values in the region are likely to remain under pressure as VLGCs pass through the region to head East, offering their tons if values make sense as they go. For now low heating, Autogas and petrochemical demand in the region means most vessels will pass on through.
