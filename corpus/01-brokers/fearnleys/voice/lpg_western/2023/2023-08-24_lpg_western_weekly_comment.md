## Market Commentary

AMERICAS After languishing in the mid single-digits earlier in the summer, spot FOB terminal fees on the Gulf Coast rapidly rose to the mid-teens after a well-noted transaction. Some moderation has been noted, but at present 13-14cpg is likely the most representative range for October. This is in part due to the somewhat reduced avails on the Gulf Coast (which comes as a result of the ongoing and sweltering heat as well as a couple of recent mechanical tie-ins), but is also representative of the delays at the Panama Canal which has kept freight aloft. To that, a recent $2.41m Northbound Canal transit payment has given cause for speculation as to additional return voyages via the Cape of Good Hope which would be seen as bullishness in the freight arena. The EIA Wednesday reported that stocks during the week ending 18 Aug rose by 1.7mmbbls to an overall total of 92.3mmbbls, which compares to the 5-yr average of 72.3mmbbls and is 135% of the same time last year. It is likely – based on 5-yr averages – that by next week propane inventories will total ~95mmbbls, and – barring any unforeseen events - put Sep overall totals in a position to rather easily eclipse the 100mmbbl mark. Meanwhile, the relationship between WTI and propane continues to hover in the low/mid 30% range – well below the 52% overall average recorded since Jan 2021. The rolling YTD average in 2023 has been on a slow decline, having started the year at just above 42% but as of now is ~39% of WTI. NWE A recurring TOT window offer this week has kept pressure mid-September arrival tons, now assessed at Sept CIF ARA -$2/MT, with no public buying interest shown. The European market continues to import large volumes from the US, but with the September East/West spread nearing $100/MT European buyers will have to up their ideas to continue to do so. With almost 600,000MT set to be imported in August the region has done well to draw tons this summer. Refiners are beginning their blend-up period in order to meet raised winter gasoline pressure requirement. Supporting butane levels at 89.5% balance August Naphtha.

**MED**

 US and northwest European imports continue to supply Moroccan demand, which continues to rise. In the East Med further Algerian and Russian LPG has been heading to Turkey as Autogas demand continues to be strong. 440,000MT is set to be imported to Turkey in August, a record since 2016.
