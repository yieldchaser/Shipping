## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Mont Belvieu propane market levels have bounced around since the start of the New Year when Non-LST barrels finished at just over 77cpg. Current price levels are mildly under 80cpg so overall in 1H Jan there’s been a bit of an uptick. The earlier detachment from crude seems to have reattached as WTI has seen a resurgence of late, having moved from $76.93/bbl on the 3rd of Jan to $78.69/bbl at present. Meanwhile, the relationship between propane and crude is effectively static at just over 42%. Terminal fees have seen some buoyancy this year and fixtures at 8cpg were noted on a couple of occasions. Asking prices have risen, as well, and offers are seen now as high as 10cpg for Feb, where buy-side interest presently exceeds availability. As for the inventories, the EIA this week revealed another large draw but the data is largely believed to contain corrective data, if only because of the recent holidays. As of 6th Jan total stocks declined by 2.1mmbbls to an overall 78.6mmbbls – which is 126% of last year and 119% of two years ago at the same time. With North American weather now trending warmer, early expectations include the possibility of a build, since production has resumed after reductions which were due to the recent storms and subsequent cold weather. NWE Propane has rebounded in the second of the week on the back of firmer stronger crude and a bullish sentiment looking out to 2023. The front month propane price in Northwest Europe rebounded, shooting up $25/ and now stands at around $560/pmt. Freight has continued to free fall, causing some of the early stage volatility seen throughout the European LPG market, however the volatility is also playing out in some more fundamental ways. Propane remains the more attractive feedstock, versus naphta, which theoretically would spur on European buying, however, petrochemical margins are still weak, temperatures remain mild, and Europe remains well supplied-as reflected in inventories. That said, its theorized that Europe’s ability to land US tons could be quelled by the thunderous reemergence of Chinese LPG buying, seen over the past week.
