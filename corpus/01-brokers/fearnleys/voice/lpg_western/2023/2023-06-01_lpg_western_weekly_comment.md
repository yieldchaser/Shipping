## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Having commenced the month of May at ~70cts/gal, Mont Belvieu non-TET propane values drifted most of the month to ~62cts/gal when - until the last few days - a sudden, sharp decline occurred. Current market values are in the 55cts/gal range, a 22% decline over the 30 day period. This is due to a combination of weighty inventories and depressed crude prices – which themselves fell from $75.76/bbl at the front of May to $68.24/bbl as of this writing, or a 10% decline. Crude itself is suffering from lofty inventories, lengthy on-the-water stocks and larger-than-expected production levels – particularly from Russia. Adding to the mix is limited demand, which in no small part can be attributed to the lackluster economic development in China. With regards to propane stock levels in the US, the EIA had been expected to rise from 66.70mmbbls (as of 19 May) by ~2.2mmbbls for a total of 68.90mmbbls (as of the 26th of May), but – in fact – a 4.00mmbbl increase was recorded, bringing total US stocks to 70.70mmbbls, equal to the 5-year high. Meanwhile, as to terminal fees, market levels have not wavered much over Q2 and bid/ask levels primarily remain in the 5cpg range. There is some improvement going in to Q3, but shipping rates – which are astonishingly high – are the limiting factor. NWE Petchem demand has slowed due to reduced downstream demand. This has left a bearish sentiment in the region where petchem demand was expected to support the majority of summer buying. With $177/MT propane naphtha spread in June propane remains the favoured feedstock. The June import program will be greatly reduced from the 600,000MT of LPG which came in to the region in May. Whilst local North Sea production continues to ramp up as natural gas prices continue to come off. A Butane offer in the window was seen for this first time in a long time and pushed values down to 68.5% June Naphtha with no interest shown on the buyside.

**MED**

 As summer starts heating demand has disappeared. Low Sonatrach pricing of $405/MT for both propane and butane was set for June. This followed CP being set surprisingly low at $450 propane and $440 butane.
