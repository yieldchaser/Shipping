## Market Commentary

AMERICAS At 102.4mmbbls the domestic US propane inventory has wholly eclipsed the 5-year high level for mid-October (the previous high-water marks were 98.3mmbbls in 2020 and 99.6mmbbls in 2016. The EIA report for the week ending 20 Oct showed a modest 100,000bbl weekly increase, which was regarded by the market as rather low as expectations ranged from a build of 250,000bbls to as much as +1.4mmbbls. This puts the Days of Supply at 113 days, and the YTD 2023 average Days of Supply at 85 days, which is well beyond the previous (2016) high of 76 Days. Stocks are high. That said, exports were recorded at 1.843mmbbls/day, which is just below the highest weekly export volume on EIA record, and which occurred just two weeks ago (2.129mmbbls/day on 6 Oct 2023). This is despite the various and sundry terminal-related export issues which continue to plague the Gulf Coast. Meanwhile, as there is now winter weather to contend with in North America, heating-related requirements will follow. Simultaneously there is winter-related demand now being seen in Northwest Europe and – it is now being reported – previous length in Chinese inventories has been drawing down, so in each case demand for US-sourced product should continue to prop up terminal fees, which moved from 10cts/gal earlier in the month to 13cts/gal more recently, and then to 16cts/gal where a transaction is believed to have been concluded. Asking levels are indeed even higher, with known offers at 20cts/gal for December volumes. With Eastern arb levels in the $320s and $330s, these types of rates are (at least temporarily) rationally supported. NWE The 1st decade November TOT market in the region is very tight. Constant bidding by multiple parties has pushed premiums up to November CIF ARA +$21/MT, a level last seen in March. The prompt market looks tight, partly due to the reduced US export volumes due to terminal loading delays over the last few weeks. Another reason for the tightness in Europe was the tightness in the VLGC market, which led to a larger volume of imports on MGCs rather than VLGCs for October and early November imports. The propane naphtha spread narrowed to multi-month lows as crude oil coming off dragged naphtha down, while prompt propane remains bullish. If the petrochemical sector was receiving any more than the minimum required volume, they would be reselling physical tons at these premiums. Butane in Europe has been quiet in the physical window and values remain at 79% November Naphtha.

**MED**

 The region continues to be in a low demand environment and cannot keep up with the strong premiums innorthwest Europe.
