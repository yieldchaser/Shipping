## Market Commentary

AMERICAS MB propane prices have drifted since the start of May, but so have WTI values – each by more/less 6%. That has left the ratio of propane to crude static at between 38-39%, which is considered a relative bargain. The much higher-than-expected propane inventory build as reported by the EIA yesterday took the market by surprise as an increase of around 1.8mmbbls was expected, but a 3.1mmbbl build was reported. Now stock levels have risen to 61.40mmbbls, or roughly 140% of early May in both 2022 and 2021. The 5-year average stock level for the first week of May is 47.5mmbbls, where the 5-year average weekly build for this time of year is 2.254mmbbls. Along with the soft crude market, this is surely adding to the bearish sentiment within the MB complex. Netbacks from both the East and from NWEurope have remained relatively flat, as well (~7-9cpg to the East and ~2-4cpg to NWE), but the number of transactions has been limited – mostly due to the ever-tightening freight market, which continues to push buyers to the sidelines. Northern tier LatAm demand seems satisfied, with contractual volumes having met the existing requirements. Nonetheless, Southern tier winter-related demand is now on the horizon and will perhaps reveal spot requirements. NWE The weight of imports are being felt in the region, pushing CIF ARA into a steepening contango, May/June at $5/MT. 500,000MT are on the water for arrival in May, and new deliveries continue to point to the region form the US. Cargoes now heading to Europe are thought to be term deliveries as the May East vs West spread is wide at -$89/MT. Additionally the TOT window is a assessed at May CIF ARA -$12/MT whereas the Ginga window is as June FEI +$1.5/MT, so any spot cargoes should head East. The propane Naphtha spread remains wide, $153/MT in May, and keeping petrochemical demand for propane high. Butane has been quiet in the region and remains at 76% May Naphtha.

**MED**

 Tight freight in the region with delays discharging in Tunisia is impacting deliveries. Causing traders to venture into the spot market for CFR deliveries in the West Med.
