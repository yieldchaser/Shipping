## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Without the benefit of an EIA propane inventory report this week (due to system maintenance/upgrades), it is generally assumed that propane stocks in the US likely declined by around a modest million barrels, though the government data will be revealed next week when the systems it employs are returned to service – at which point another million barrels or so may have been consumed domestically as a result of cooler temperatures across the US and ongoing export activities. If that is the case US stock levels will have declined to below the 100mmbbl mark. Meanwhile, all eyes are on the Panama Canal where reduced transits – which have come as a result of a drought in Panama – have forced shipping to seek alternatives, most of which are more time consuming and, therefore, expensive. A couple of recent deviations (both North and Southbound) are evidence of the disruption, and perhaps a sign of things to come in 2024. The abrupt changes, combined with what has been described as a chaotic Asian market and an overbought European market wherein winter-related demand has yet to commence, are allowing for a wider arb, but simultaneously high freight costs, which have resulted in pressure on the US terminal fee market. Those values, from the sell-side perspective, are now pegged in the mid/low teens but -as of this writing – buy-side ideas are more in the upper single digits. NWE The TOT window continues to be active the latest activity an end November offer equating to CIF ARA November -$10/MT vs an early December delivery bid equating to CIF ARA November -$8.5/MT. Cold weather has yet to increase demand while petchem run rates remain low. A slide in crude oil values the week added to the bearish sentiment in the region. As winter approaches and TTF Natural Gas values rise, we will start to see maximum LPG rejection into the natural gas stream, reducing local LPG production. Dutch TTF average $13.40/MMBtu while Propane $9.45/MMBtu and butane $10/MMBtu. The butane market continues to be very quiet and remains assessed at 81% November Naphtha.

**MED**

 Imports to the region in November are likely to set a 1 year record. Traders continue to look to place in the region due to the difficulties in transiting the Panama Canal. A slide in crude values has added bearish sentiment to the already oversupplied propane and butane markets.
