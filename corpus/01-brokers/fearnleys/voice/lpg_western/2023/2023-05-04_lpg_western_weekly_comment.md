## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Three significant events have occurred over the course the past week: First, crude oil values declined by $4/bbl, mostly on economic fears based on the recent large bank closures (and the ongoing fear of additional problems). Second, propane prices at Mont Belvieu softened by up to 5cts/gal as a direct response to the crude oil weakness. Third, terminal fees have weakened as buy-side interest has retreated. Current June offers are seen at 5cpg but buyers remain agnostic, even when shown that there is plenty of room for negotiation. Meanwhile in rather a surprising fashion, the EIA reported that propane stocks as of the week ending 28 April actually fell by 600,000bbls to 58.30mmbbls from 58.90mmbbls. This leaves current stock levels at 143% of those recorded last year and 140% of end April 2021. This week’s data release is widely viewed as a correction from the week prior when a 3.4mmbbl stock increase was published. If – in fact – that is the case then presumably the traditional ‘build’ season should resume with next week’s report. In any case, at this point stock levels of propane in the US are well above the 5-year average and remain closer to the 5-year high’s, which in some cases has become worrisome in the sense that brine containment issues later in the season are now becoming a consideration. NWE Large import volumes continue, around 600KT in April and 350KT set to arrive in 1H May. Despite the drop in crude LPG values the propane naphtha spread remains at a healthy -$144/MT in May. The spread continues to keep petchem demand at high levels which is driving this spring market. Additionally production in the region is increasing, though re-injection into the natural gas stream continues. The butane market has been quiet over the last few weeks and continues to be valued at 76% May naphtha which has lost significant value this week in line with crude.

**MED**

 The butane market has come off relative to naphtha with decreasing seasonal demand.
