## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Netbacks to the US from Europe and Asia have been on a bumpy, but overall declining trend since the start of Q2. Where at first April there was a ~9cpg netback from Asia and a mid-4cpg netback from Europe, now – two months later – the Asian netback has declined to the mid-4cpg range and European netbacks are hardly a penny. The trading climate, therefore, has become increasingly difficult and little is known to be occurring as buy-side interest at the start of summer is limited. Europe is well supplied and Asian stocks are robust due to a dearth of Middle Eastern-sourced deliveries. Meanwhile, on the domestic US front, the MB market received no pricing assistance from the EIA which reported a 1.6mmbbl build in overall US primary stock levels, which the market reacted to in bullish fashion and thus further eroding the netbacks. The market had anticipated a build of some 2.5mmbbls, and the 5-year average build for the 22nd week of the year is 2.4mmbbls. At an overall total of 72.3mmbbls, propane stocks in the US well eclipse the 5-year average of 54.651mmbbls, and are now 144% of last year’s early June total. If (and when) domestic production rises in earnest, Mont Belvieu propane values will – as predicted publicly by several of the largest midstream producers / terminal owners – relax in order that US-sourced product can remain globally competitive. NWE European demand is low as Petchems have reduced their run rates even though July propane naphtha spread is a wide -$160/MT. This is due to the lack of derivatives demand as the economy in the region remains under pressure. That said an early July propane bid was shown in the TOT window, leaving values assessed at July CIF ARA -$1/MT. LPG imports to the region in June are showing below 400,000MT so far, this volume will increase slightly but will be well below the 600,000MT imported in May. The July East/West spread stands at -$74/MT even after the arb narrowed significantly to start the week, so spot cargoes will continue to head East. Butane values continue to slide as tons are offered in the large cargo window. The last offer valued was 57% June Naphtha and still could not find a counter.

**MED**

 Butane in the West Med looks well supplied, with an offer in the region unmet. Increased demand in North Africa was quickly filled and the region once again looks balanced to long.
