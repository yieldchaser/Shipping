## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Much of the domestic US LPG market has gone quiet as the wait for winter-related weather demand has commenced. While there have been recent, large-scale storms, nothing sustained has occurred as yet. Meanwhile, propane stocks in the US fell by a generally anticipated 600,000bbls as of the week ending 24 Nov, and now total 98.7mmbbls, or about 109% of last year and 136% of two years ago. At this point stock levels are sufficient in the event of a long, cold winter – as well as export volumes, which were most recently reported by the EIA to be 1.594mmbblss/day, but which are down from last week’s 1.787mmbbls. As for terminal fees, the high levels seen for Dec transactions have receded, but buy-side ideas have declined more sharply and at this point – for Jan – interest levels is tentatively in the mid single-digit levels while offers (thin as they are) remain hopeful for double-digits. At the forefront of it all remain the issues at the Panama Canal, and transit auctions have commanded between $1m and (nearly) $4m in recent weeks, which has, in turn, given cause for Eastbound alternatives such as the Suez Canal and the Cape of Good Hope, thus adding ton-miles which, in turn, has kept freight rates resilient. NWE The first wave of cold weather has arrived in Europe and with it disappeared the TOT window offers. Leaving only BP bidding for a Dec 20-24 delivery, pushing the physical premium up to December CIF ARA +$4.25/MT. The region still looks well supplied and with a large percentage of US LPG exports heading East likely to transit the region over the next few months due to Panama Canal restrictions, expected to be in place until April 2024. Butane values in the region have increased to 83% December Naphtha, though large cargo butane activity has been scarce.

**MED**

 The region continues to see tons offered on VLGCs in transit to the Suez Canal. So far there has been no uptick in weather related demand.
