## Market Commentary

AMERICAS The EIA this week reported an overall propane inventory total of 95.9mmbbls, revealing a 1.1mmbbl decline from the previous week. The market had generally been anticipating around a 2mmbbl draw, but while export volumes were off (1.7mmbbls/day from 2.0mmbbbls/day), domestic demand requirements increased and thus only the modest drop. The reduced export rate may also be related to overland deliveries to Mexico, which – due to lower production there – has recently given cause for additional rail/truck/pipe deliveries. At the same time last year US propane stock levels were 90.3mmbbls. In spite of many market expectations, the weight of the inventory levels has not (yet) caused Mont Belvieu propane values to commensurately decline as crude continues to mill about on either side of $70/bbl and weather-related demand is simultaneously providing domestic support. Meanwhile, terminal fees were notionally bobbing along in the mid-single-digit range until a late Dec fixture confirmed the level. Very little has been accomplished for 1H Jan, but a mid/2H Jan fixture was reported in the upper single-digits, prompting offers to be significantly increased amid a late-breaking demand flurry. NWE No bids or offer were shown in the TOT window this week, leaving the physical market assessed at December CIF ARA minus $5/MT. December import volumes are expected to be low, around 260,000MT due to the mild winter so far and low petchem demand. For comparison 475,000MT of LPG were imported in November while 550,000MT were imported in October. While imports are low the physical TOT discount shows demand is being covered and reflects the low demand environment. The arb from the US is open, and we expect to see a larger volume of imports in January. Butane demand remains firm with levels at 92.5% December Naphtha.
