## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Massive volatility in the global markets, underpinned by the calamity at the Panama Canal, gave cause for the Asian arb to spike sharply, only to then be met with wide gains in the freight market which, in turn, caused terminal fees to subside from the high levels that were previously being asked. Current offers are seen in the mid teens, but asking levels at one point were as high as 20cpg. Meanwhile, the EIA this week reported that domestic US propane stock levels declined from 102.4mmbbls by 1.2mmbbls to an overall total as of 27 Oct of 101.2mmbbls. Generally speaking this was of little surprise since the market had been anticipating a draw of around a million barrels. This was based on domestic demand levels which are believed to have improved as a result of early winter weather and relatively widespread early snowfall across North America. In any case, US propane inventories are presently the highest they’ve been since at least 2015 for end-October, and remain about 15% higher than at the same time last year, and 33% higher than in 2021. NWE Large cargo propane pricing has come off rapidly this week, as multiple new offers have been shown in the window. The high Panama Canal auction fee of almost $3 million caused many traders with US Gulf loadings in December to see if they could place hteir cargoes in the European market. Rapidly pushing TOT values to a $4/MT discount to November CIF ARA, down from an $8/MT premium Tuesday. Petchem demand remains low and winter demand yet to increase substantially the European market will remain discounted with multiple offers. The butane market has been creeping up this week as bids have pushed value up towards 83% November Naphtha.

**MED**

 The region has also seen a swath of offers due to the Panama Canal risk, pushing propane prices down, while butane values in the region remained steady.
