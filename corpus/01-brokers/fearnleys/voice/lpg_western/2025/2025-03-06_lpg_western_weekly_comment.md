## Market Commentary

US Geopolitical events of late have given rise to a level of volatility and uncertainty which – when combined with the recent seminar schedule (IE Week, Japan Conference, OPIS) – have left the market rather stagnant. A couple of transactions are known to have occurred, each in the low double-digits FOB for 4:0 VLs, but the landscape at this point is increasingly populated with sellers and, as such, sentiment has turned bearish. WTI crude values are not only volatile but have fallen sharply over the course of the first few days of March and have seen a $1.58/bbl decline so far this month. MB propane prices have also declined, leaving the propane-to-crude ratio at between 52-54% this week, which only contributes to the thoughts of further weakness since the 2024 average was 43.85%, or roughly10% higher than 2025. Propane inventory levels, per the EIA, declined to 48.70mmbbls as of the week ending 28 Feb, which puts stock levels at ~95% of the same time last year, and 81% of end-Feb two years ago. Production levels were recorded at 2.690mmbbls/day against a calendar year average of 2.65mmbbls/day. Exports, meanwhile, charged upwards as the EIA reported a 2.248mmbbl/day level (with the all-time high of 2.335mmbbls/day).
