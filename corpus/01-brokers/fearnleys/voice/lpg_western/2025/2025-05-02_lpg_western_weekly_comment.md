## Market Commentary

US Market conditions have by now deteriorated to the point where US cargo cancellations are being considered. The nearly nonexistent arb and poor netbacks have pushed term lifters to the brink and it is being predicted that cancellations might occur as soon as this week. All of this comes as a result of the trade tariffs which have disrupted global LPG market trade. When, if, and how things might be resolved is a topic of ongoing debate, but in the meanwhile most regular market participants have adopted a ‘shelter-in-place’ approach so as to minimize potential exposure. Those that can have already swapped as many 4:0 cargoes to 3:1 or – better – 2:2 parcels in order that non-Chinese destinations can be availed, though despite the best efforts the tariff-related barriers are proving most difficult. On the domestic front, the EIA this week posted a weak build of just 600,000bbls, bringing overall US primary propane stock levels to 47.20mmbbls, or about 83% of the same time last year. This is despite another record-level production figure provided by the EIA of 2.852mmbbls/day. Mont Belvieu propane pricing on 1st April were 92cts/gal but by 1st May have fallen to 77cts/gal, a 16% decline over the month-long period.
