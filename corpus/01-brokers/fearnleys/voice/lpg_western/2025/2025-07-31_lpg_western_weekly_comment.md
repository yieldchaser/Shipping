## Market Commentary

US The US Gulf Coast LPG market is practically stagnated, since a large number of market participants are either taking holiday or – up to now – leery of the outcome of the tariff situation. By now it is known that the existing tariffs will remain in place for another 90 days, but basis the arb and current netbacks (both of which are rather poor), not much is expected. The EIA, meanwhile, reported a modest and less-than-expected build of 1.2mmbbls for the week ending 25 July, and primary propane stocks stand at 83.50mmbbls – 96% of last year. The market had largely been anticipating a build of some 2.5-3.0mmbbls based on strong production but somewhat limited exports. Production was strong (2.822mmbbls/day against an all-time high of 2.897mmbbls/day) but exports over the week declined to 1.629mmbbls/day (against a YTD 2025 average of 1.839mmbbls/day. Terminal fees presently range rather widely, with buy-side interest in the mid/upper 4cts/gal, but sellers (those who have shown themselves, anyway) are coming from 6cts/gal, and as a result very little is known to have been accomplished.
