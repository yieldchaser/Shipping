## Market Commentary

US Volatility is the key word at the moment, though maybe “uncertainty” comes in as a close second. Crude is bouncing around, the weather is having a say, and while the EIA data this week was not entirely surprising, MB propane continues to ride at higher-than-expected levels. Fog has invaded the US Gulf Coast and, in the morning hours at least, been a factor for over a week while – simultaneously – the Northeast US has been cold and is presently downright bitter. This has kept MB propane values elevated and waffling at (or just under) 90cpg since the start of Feb. Meanwhile, the EIA this week released its findings which were felt to be in line with expectations. Current overall stock levels of 58.90mmbbls is over 103% of last year (though around 89% of two years ago). Production levels in the US this week were recorded at 2.609mmbbls/day, or just about average on a YTD basis. However, calendar 2024 averaged 2.669mmbbls/day, so 2025 – mostly because of freeze-offs in the Permian – is off to a slower start. Terminal fees, all the while, were felt to be rising and asking-levels rose to the low double-digits (though not quite the ‘teens’) but the last known propane transactions are thought to have occurred in the low 10cts/gal range. Buyers at this point, faced with a bounty of choices for March FOBs have their ideas set in the upper single digits.
