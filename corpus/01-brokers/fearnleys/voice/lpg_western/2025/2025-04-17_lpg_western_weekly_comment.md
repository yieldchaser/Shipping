## Market Commentary

US Uncertainty rules and predictable mayhem has ensued as a result of the ongoing tariff-related actions by the US government. The markets are uncertain as to how to proceed as there is no known clear path at this point. FOB USGC buyers almost instantaneously opted for as much butane as possible (per their respective contracts) and date-swaps have been common. Meanwhile, though – and despite it all – MB propane values reacted by rising and thus squeezing arbs, thereby placing pressure on netbacks, which have declined and resulted in the lowest terminal fee levels since late 2023. As if that were insufficient, the EIA data this week was a surprise to most participants, many of whom were calling for a .500-1.00mmbbl inventory gain but were met with a 1.3mmbbl reduction to an overall US stock position of 44.40mmbbls, or about 80% of the same time last year. This is believed to be the result of higher exports (1.966mmbbls/day, up from 1.739mmbbls/day) and mildly lower production of 2.744mmbbls/day (versus 2.752mmbbls/day the week prior). There is some speculation, however, that the increased butane liftings (at the expense of propane) will next week result in lower propane exports, and thus a build – though at this point it is difficult to gauge the level that stocks might build. Whether or not the tariffs that will (or won’t) be imposed will (or won’t) include NGLs or LPGs remains a very hot topic that is being discussed and bantered by all. The only sure thing at this point is that nothing is certain.
