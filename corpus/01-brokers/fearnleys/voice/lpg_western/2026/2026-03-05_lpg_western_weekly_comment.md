## Market Commentary

The Middle East conflict that erupted over weekend has disrupted oil and gas export flows through the Strait of Hormuz. No spot deals or market discussions have been heard amid the prevailing uncertainty on geopolitical tensions. Swap values continued to surge on supply concerns, and by the time of writing, Apr CP was traded at $591/mt for C3 and $590/mt for C4 respectively. U.S.–Far East arbitrage widened sharply to around minus $270s for April, as supply disruptions in the Middle East pushed landed costs higher. Terminal fees for April liftings surged to around 27 cpg, with sellers targeting higher margins from the wider arbitrage amid softening freight on the US–Chiba route. U.S. propane/propylene inventories rose by 819,000 barrels to 73.4 million barrels in the week ended Feb. 27, although stocks remained 50.7% higher year on year. The build came as a surprise to the market, as most had expected a decline. Both Mar and Apr FEI and cash premiums surged amid tightening supply. A 23/0 parcel for 1h Apr delivery basis Chiba was concluded in window at Apr FEI plus $105, and similar parcel for 2h Apr delivery was traded at Mar FEI plus high single digit. A 46/0 parcel for 2h Apr delivery basis Chiba was also reported traded at Mar FEI plus mid-teens. Asian refiners and petchem plants are reportedly cutting rates or shutting units due to feedstock shortages. China’s PDH operating rate is expected to fall from the current 64% to around 54% in the next two weeks, facing rising feedstock costs amid supply tightness. The resulting demand loss could exceed 2mmt of propane per month, which might partly offset Middle East supply disruptions and easing some tightness. However, India and some Southeast Asian buyers are more severely impacted, with few alternative butanes available for prompt Mar delivery. Some US exports are expected to be diverted into these markets, though arrivals will likely be mid/2h Apr range.
