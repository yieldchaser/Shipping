## Market Commentary

AMERICAS With the strength in the Mont Belvieu market the arbs to both the East and to Europe having declined so sharply in recent times that buybacks and cancellations are at the forefront of conversation. As many as a half-dozen March cargoes are believed to have been ‘bought back’ by Enterprise, and a host of cancellations are known to have occurred, as well. The strength of the MB propane market, meanwhile, is based on generally robust crude values, but more so on the cold weather that has gripped the US over the past couple of weeks. And though the immediate 10-day weather forecast is showing signs of a warming trend there are also signals emerging which indicate that another important winter storm might form towards the end of Feb or beginning of March. Meanwhile, the EIA revealed a rather expected set of propane inventory data for the week ending 28 Jan, culminating in an overall total propane stock level of 49.8mmbbls, a decline of 4.3mmbbls from the week prior. This puts current inventories at 88-89% of the totals recorded last year, and 69-70% of two years ago. Terminal fees in the U.S. have weakened all the while, and an effective buy-side ‘floor’ has most recently been established by Enterprise (as well as an active trader) in the range of 4.50-4.75cpg. There remain more sellers than buyers in the March timeframe, as again, the arbs remain poor. NWE CIF ARA propane strengthened $3pmt as crude oil saw a downward correction Wednesday. Demand for propane in the region remains driven by the petchems, and will grow as Dow’s Terneuzen system gets back to full operating capacity. The February propane naphtha spread at -$83pmt continues to drive the petchem demand. European netbacks for US cargoes are looking good, although it is hard for traders who do not have assets in the region to find shorts into Europe. The region’s traders are waiting until the last minute to pick up the best values cargoes, as they see more offers on the horizon due to the reduced demand in the East. The TOT window saw the first offer of the week which equated to CIF ARA Feb +$4pmt, and with no window counter seen the market was assessed at around CIF ARA +$2pmt to CIF ARA flat. Marcus Hook export volumes have decreased due to the cold weather, leading to a larger volume of NWE imports being sourced from the USGC.

**MED**

 The weather in the region has been on the mild side, keeping residential demand low. Term deliveries are covering most of the Moroccan butane demand. 4KT butane was offered FOB Huelva, but the offer was well above the market and attracted no interest.
