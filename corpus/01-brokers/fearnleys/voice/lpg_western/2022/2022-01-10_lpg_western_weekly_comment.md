## Market Commentary

AMERICAS The combination of colder temperatures across North America and healthy export volumes over the holiday period have given cause for a robust draw in propane inventories, which recorded a 3.3mmbbl decline for the week ending 7 Jan. This weighs against the 66.00mmbbl level recorded one year ago, and puts current stocks at just under 95% of last year at the same time. Additional winter weather is on the near-term horizon, as well, and forecasters are calling for below-average temps and significant snowfall over the balance of January. The EIA’s Days of Supply calculation as of 7 Jan stands at 39.9, versus 38.6 days one year ago. The current supply rate compares to the 5 year average of 47.92 days for the first week of January. Meanwhile, recent terminal fee levels have been noted as trading within a very thin range, with full-propane deals recorded at 6cts and mixed-ratio parcels being fixed at around 5.75 within the last trading week. More recently, though, offers to sell mixed-ratio cargoes are seen at 5cts/gal, and full-propane lots in mid/2H Feb are presently being marketed at the “high 5s”. Mont Belvieu prices have all the while been slowly climbing, and started Dec at $1.03/gal but as of this writing have reached over $1.18/gal, supported – in part – by both the weather, the healthy export volumes and the nearly endless gains in the crude sector. WTI is off at present, but remains well above $82/bbl. NWE Demand in the region remains strong and is sufficient enough to absorb the over 400,000 MT of LPG set to be imported to the region in January. Winter weather is not severe but still generating good demand. Whereas chemical demand is getting back to maximum as Terneuzen is now back online. The propane naphtha spread at -$36pmt in January favouring propane, as Naphtha strengthened with recent rise in crude oil. The large import volume in January has calmed the market which looks relatively balanced. That said a persistent bid in the TOT window for January 22-26 delivery window has yet to be taken out after being shown all week and improving values, now valued at Jan CIF ARA +$2.5pmt. The window will need to improve to tempt further US volume as the arb narrowed significantly since the large draw seen in US inventories. Traders are holding fire on February buying for the time being as they wait to see how much demand remains once the January import volume has been absorbed.

**MED**

 The West Med has yet to see increased winter demand, and remains well supplied by term volume. In the East Med, LPG is currently better priced than Gasoline in Turkey, leaving importers searching for tons for Autogas.
