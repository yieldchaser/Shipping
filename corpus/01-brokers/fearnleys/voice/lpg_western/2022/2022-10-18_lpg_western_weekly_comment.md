## Market Commentary

AMERICAS U.S. propane inventories are reported by the EIA to have risen by a half-million barrels during the week ending 14 Oct and are now pegged at an overall total of 86mmbbls, which is roughly 9mmbbls above earlier forecasts and 117% of last year’s total. The data from yesterday shows that there was a draw in the Midwest (PADD II) of 300,000bbls and on the East Coast (PADD 1C), but that the Gulf Coast (PADD III) stocks increased by 400,000bbls. Weather-related demand in the U.S. has been limited so far, though the draws described above point towards the beginnings of the winter season. However, current forecasts are calling for a warming trend over the balance of October (per NOAA), and thus either small builds or small draws can be anticipated as the seasons shift. Terminal fees have recently begun showing signs of life and levels have moved up amid open arbs to present ideas ranging from the low 4cpg range to a high point of around the 6 cpg level. NWE North Sea availabilities of propane and butane have fallen roughly 30% per month due to the re-injection of a portion of the LPG into the skyrocketing natural gas stream. However, a price spike and the need for additional tons has for now been mitigated, by the steady flow of US Origin VLGC's arriving in NWE. The Propane price has gained ground in relation to Naphta- which fell more in parity with crude in the first few weeks of October. But the focal point throughout the world of LPG this past week has been the emergence of some bullish indicators recognized throughout the market. Chinese run rates at cracking facilities have increased (and thus demand) on back of stronger margins, a curtailed Middle Eastern export program, and the theory that domestic retailers across the US will need to continue buying more product to cover for winter demand, as they elected not to do so earlier in the year due to sky high propane prices. The domino effect of the preceding could quickly matriculate into the European market, making it difficult to attract tons from the US East Coast, where volumes- to-export perennially dwindle. The former, along with the unrealized probability of cold temperatures, has many market participants watching for a sharp increase in European LPG values on the horizon.

**MED**

 The large cargo market has been quiet as temperatures remain above average for the time of year. The butane arb from the US is closed. So, until the weather changes, term supply will be sufficient to maintain a balanced market.
