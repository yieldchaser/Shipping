## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Terminal fees for Jan cargoes are now priced across a wide spectrum, from as little as buy-side interest in the low 4s to offers of around the 6ct/gal mark. A couple of transactions are believed to have occurred in the mid-4s this week. Otherwise, little is being accomplished as demand levels remain muted. Term business was recently discussed as low as the 5s but not concluded, with a more likely transaction to occur in the upper 6s. The EIA propane inventory data continues to surprise, as yet another practically unheard-of December build was recorded this week. Overall stock levels are 90.30mmbbls as of 9th Dec, or 127% of last year and 107% of two years ago. The ongoing build in PADD III continues to impress due mostly to robust production levels but aided by both Panama Canal-related shipping delays as well as a recent bout of fog- delays on the Houston Ship Channel. In any event there has so far been only limited cold weather demand across the U.S., but now a couple of prominent forecasters are calling for a series of Arctic fronts to sweep across the nation over the Christmas and New Year period, perhaps marking the beginnings of the traditional demand season which will likely be viewed in a “better late than never” context. NWE The TOT Window continues to be well bid for end dec and early Jan deliveries into Europe. The region has been experiencing a continued spell of cold weather keeping demand firm and the December/January backwardation wide at -$13/MT. Inland demand is increasing, demand for railcars is up and European storages are drawing down. US tons continue to find their way to Europe, though with the East/West spread at $84/MT and reduced Panama Canal delays some cargoes may decide to go East. Naphtha weakened in the region which has pulled butane down with it as it remains at 91.5% December Naphtha. The butane window has been quiet for some time, and little to no butane deals have been heard in the region.

**MED**

 The region is set to import 600,000MT of US LPG in December, close to all time highs. This has been due to early cold weather in the region and the US tons replacing imports from other regions such as WAF and local production.
