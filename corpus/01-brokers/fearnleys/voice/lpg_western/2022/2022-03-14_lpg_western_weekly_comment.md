## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Per the EIA propane inventory report for the week ending 11 Mar 2022, overall US stocks declined by 2.2mmbbls to a total of 35.5mmbbls, which is 81% of last year’s total, and just under 58% of two years ago. Current stock levels remain below the 5-year average. Although the draw was larger than some had anticipated, it was within the generally expected range. The areas where there has been lingering cold weather (PADDs I and II) saw draws of 100,000 and 900,000bbls (respectively), but the significant component showed a 1.1mmbbl draw on the Gulf Coast. At 19.156 mmbbls, overall PADD III stocks are now 25% less than last year’s level of 25.920mmbbls. With regards terminal fees, there remains but one large unsold cargo in March, which at one point commanded double-digits. Because of the backwardation, though, April avails are priced more moderately at between 6 and 7cpg, depending on the timing and grade(s). Talk for May is slow just yet, but initial asking levels start with a 7. The volatility seen earlier in the month has subsided and the netback levels have come in a bit but remain positive for the time being. Crude values – $102.50 for WTI and $106..00 for Brent – will have a deciding hand. NWE The physical TOT window premiums stand at April CIF ARA +$25pmt. Showing the tightness of the April market. The arb from the US is open and although Mt Belvieu has again strengthened the European market is expected to draw imports. The problem for Europe is that US East Coast exports have been reduced by the hefty draws and cold weather seen this winter, so traders have to look for cargoes from the Gulf Coast and compete with the East market, a higher netbacks by $10pmt. Petrochemical buying should still be at maximum with high Crude oil and propane vs naphtha in April above $140pmt. A full propane VLGC offered in the window pm a naphtha related price is not drawing any interest, due to the naphtha price swings. These swings continue to keep the NWE butane market quiet, and butane remains valued at 92% March naphtha.

**MED**

 With Ramadan less than 3 weeks away buying activity was expected to pick up. So far this has not yet materialized and the market remains subdued. Vessels discharging in Western Morocco have been stranded in port due to bad weather, the VLGC Chinook in Mohammedia since the beginning of them month.
