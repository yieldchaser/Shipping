## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Arbs have suffered in recent weeks, primarily due to the influence on the LPG pricing structure by the declining crude market, and where at the start of July WTI was over $108/bbl it is now below $90/bbl and still volatile. Propane values, meanwhile, have fallen from $1.21/gal to $1.09/gal over the same period. Relatively strong inventory gains have also been a factor in that propane stocks have risen to 63.6mmbbls as of end July, or 96% of last year’s level. However, current stocks are notably just 71% of the 86.7mmbbl inventory level achieved two years ago. Given the heat and dry climate across North America, crop drying should not be much of a factor which should then, in turn, allow stock levels to gain a bit more traction. With the inventory goal being somewhere near the 80mmbbl range, there is a 16.4mmbbl deficit at present, and to reach that target level a weekly build of 1.82mmbbls over the next 9 weeks will be required. If the timeline is shifted to mid-Oct, then a 1.49mmbbl weekly build would suffice, and for an end-Oct deadline a 1.26mmbbl weekly increase would be needed. The August lifting roster is nearly complete with only a small number of potential cargo slots remaining. Sep, however, is beginning to emerge and where the Aug terminal fee values are placed at/below 5cpg, Sep is perhaps at/above 5cts, at least for the first half of the month. 2H Sep is pegged in the mid 5s at present. Q4 strips were talked in the 6cpg range. A full propane VL tender was issued last week for 2H Sep CFR Chile and was awarded at a price deemed to be ‘well below’ last year’s level. NWE The TOT window in Europe has been quiet recently, without any bids or offers. August import volume is increasing, with term volumes starting to trickle in from the US. Netback values from the US for August are still in cancellation territory, and well below value to Asia. This is keeping August imports quiet in this period of low demand. Additionally export has resumed out of Mongstad and were around 70 KT in July, adding local supply.

**Baltic**

 production exported from Ust-Luga continues to be STS-ed onto VLGC in the ARA region and head East. Until clear demand signs are seen traders in the region are happy to work on a more prompt basis, waiting for offers to be shown to them rather than going out searching for cargoes, in this well supplied low demand environment. Butane has continuously firmed up and is now 86% August Naphtha.

**MED**

 The summer market in the West of the region has ample supply from term contracts. The East Med is well offered, as traders with split ratio US cargoes look for a home for their tons.
