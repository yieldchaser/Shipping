## Market Commentary

AMERICAS The EIA report on Wednesday showed a modest build of just 1.6mmbbls for an overall propane stock total of 51.8mmbbls as of 10 June 2022, which is about 94% of last year’s total and 73% of the total achieved two years ago. It’s also well below the average for the second week of June, which is +2.12mmbbls. The market had been expecting something between 2.8-3.2mmbbls (with a couple of noteworthy outliers who were calling for a “flat” or “minimal” builds). On the whole, propane values over the last week have declined from 124.50cts/gal to current levels around 118.00cts/gal. All the while, netbacks were seen in decline as re-sale terminal fees have shrunk to ~4cts/gal. However, by the close of business on Wednesday there appeared promise as the arb showed signs of improving – opening, even. Furthermore, a series of offers appeared this week and supplies seem plentiful despite the three known July cancellations on the Gulf Coast. Meanwhile, demand in Latin America appears to have been satisfied via a spot sale and a tender award, though the re-opening of storage caverns near Pajaritos in Mexico may allow for some volumes to move in that direction. Current consumer demand levels in Mexico are seasonally limited, however. NWE An end June propane bid continues to be show in the TOT window, supporting the market. The value was equal to July CIF ARA +$7.5pmt. With the increased Marcus Hook exports and the majority of these tons flowing to Europe, it was expected this bid would have been hit rapidly. The summer mood continues in region as storages begin to fill up. Butane in particular is long with storages full at all major demand centres. Term supply and European traders picking up Marcus Hook tons has blocked the hopes of Eastern placing some of their length in Europe. The July Propane Naphtha spread in Europe is at $98pmt and demand from petchems remains good. The East/West spread is $56pmt in July and it is likely that July USGC cargoes which were not cancelled will try to find homes in the East rather than looking to the West.

**MED**

 The region is quiet with the mood dominated by butane length and Eastern traders trying to find homes for US split ratio cargoes. Low demand and heatwaves make for a soporific market.
