## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Its been a week of mixed signals and uncertainty in the US LPG market. On Wednesday, the EIA reported another week of record production US (2.626 million b/d), along with a 3-million-barrel inventory draw that definitively exceeded the expectations of market participants. Unsurprisingly, the Mont Belvieu propane traded up an average of 4% across the locations, further hampering the sparse speculative trading at present. That said, most recent draw included, US propane stocks stand at approximately 87 million barrels- the highest mid-December levels seen in nearly seven years. Crude also saw a substantial uptick, maintaining propane’s weakness to WTI – which now stands at 36 percent- compared to the 5 year average of 52% of WTI. Each market participants Christmas list is slightly different. Panama delays have quelled some, and now stand at 5 and 10 days delay for north and south respectively- while remaining high on the radars of traders. Some are hoping for continued resurgence of Chinese demand with the sustained walk back of their zero covid policy- While others expect the ease of freight costs with the arrivals of newbuilds in the first quarter of 2023 coupled with the near perennially Q1 slump in rates. NWE The TOT window has been active this week and bids have risen from January CIF ARA +$5.5/MT early this week to January CIF ARA +$10/MT. On top of the physical premiums, January CIF ARA flat price jumped up $12 partly as a reaction to the larger than expected draw in US inventories and firming crude values. It is looking difficult for European importers to secure US volumes, more due to tight freight that the draw of the Eastern market. So far 90,000 MT are heading to the region from the US for 1H January arrival. A low volume when 480,000MT was imported in December and 520,000MT in November. Large cargo butane values are assessed at 92% December Naphtha though this should be taken with a pinch of salt due to the lack of recent activity.

**MED**

 The cold spell which passed through North-West-Europe never made it as far South as the Mediterranean. This has left term supplies accounting for the majority of the demand in North Africa. The East Med has taken a VLGC into Kalamata, Greece, in early January. While Turkey is also expected to receive a 1st half January VLGC from the US.
