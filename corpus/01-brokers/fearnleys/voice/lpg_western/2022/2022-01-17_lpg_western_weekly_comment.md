## Market Commentary

AMERICAS US propane stocks are in generally better condition owing to robust domestic production and fewer ‘buybacks’, and despite healthy exports. Expectations as to this week’s EIA report again ranged rather widely, this time from a draw of 1.5mmbbls to as much as 3.5mmbbls. Ultimately a draw of 3.7mmbbls was recorded, but the larger-than-expected draw was met with a rather lackluster response and the numbers were rationalized and explained as having been generally in line with sentimental expectations. Nonetheless, market values did begin to climb after the release of the EIA figures and were marked at $1.1875 before the lunch hour on Thursday. Domestic US propane stocks have risen to within 2% of the level achieved last year and as of mid-Jan 2022 stand at 58.70mmbbls. Terminal fees have seen a bit of weakness, having started the year at / around 6cts but coming off of those levels to the 5.75cpg level more recently. Traders were keen on 5.00 cpg for March ideas, but sellers thus far have resisted. The terminals are priced well above. NWE Demand in the region remains decent though slightly more bearish as 2 offers and a discounted bid emerged in the window. That said, the region seems equipped to absorb 400,000 MT that has or will be delivered into the region this month. Winter weather has been tepid, however forecasts are now predicting a dip back below seasonal average which could possibly stoke demand. Chemical demand is moderate and propane's widening weakness in relation to naphtha could further insight the demand to the region for cracking. That said, weak Ethylene margins could potentially act as a point of friction in the equation . As it stands, the propane naphtha spread sits at -$94.25 pmt in the front month- or the widest its been since May. Natural gas prices have softened, it has not been to a degree to absolve LPG from internal refineries where its being used as a cheaper alternative.

**MED**

 The Med remains balanced, with muted activity. While the arbitrage for butane has widened on paper (US Butane has come off a bit following the drive-heavy holidays, along with some crude related CIF ARA gains) logistical constraints have hampered any opportunistic trade in the region.
