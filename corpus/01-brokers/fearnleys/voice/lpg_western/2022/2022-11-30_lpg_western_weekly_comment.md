## Market Commentary

AMERICAS There exists at present contradictory factors in the domestic US market whereby rising crude values are having an upward impact on the pricing structure while simultaneously the weight of the now heightened inventory levels is having a downward effect. At ~40% of crude (and barely that), propane prices should theoretically be poised to increase, but current stock levels weigh heavily, particularly since there has yet been little weather-related demand across North America. The EIA on Wednesday released its findings for the week ending 25 Nov and showed a 1.6mmbbl increase from 89mmbbls in the week prior to current levels of 90.6mmbbls. This puts current stocks at nearly 125% of last year and just under 100% of two years ago - and pushing well past the 5-year average of 80.30mmbls. Terminal fees, meanwhile, saw a short-lived increase but have begun to relax again as a host of sellers have emerged. Current FOB USGC offerings can be found in the low 5s, with room to improve. Interest, however, is a bit muted – mostly due to the lack of weather-related demand and poor petchem economics. NWE Many European traders discussed the state of the market heading into winter at a very well attended Christmas party in London last night. One of the major concerns were if US East Coast export volumes would be enough to substitute Russian LPG export volumes unavailable due to sanctions. For the time being US cargoes continue to be offered into the region as traders look to place their cargoes avoiding the Panama canal. Although with the December East / West spread at $100 most cargoes will continue heading East. The propane / Naphtha spread in the region was bolstered by a recovery in crude oil values. Which also strengthened butane which remains 91.5% December Naphtha. Demand in the region does not look set to jump just yet and is covered by term deliveries. The main concern is if demand jumps unexpectedly would European traders be able to find freight at the last minute to cover prompt shorts?

**MED**

 Sonatrach prices set at $580 for propane and $600 for butane a rise from November. Action in the region was quiet as traders assessed these posted prices before any cold weather-related demand has hit the region.
