## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Since the end of March the Mont Belvieu propane market has seen intermittent declines which have been interrupted by small spurts of buoyancy, though all told the market has seen more softness than not. This is owing to the combination of declines in winter-related demand and that crude oil has been coming off in recent days. Inventory levels in the U.S. have for two consecutive weeks risen, though rather modestly. Current stocks are posted by the EIA at 34.4mmbbls, or about 86% of last year and 57% of two years ago. The market is beginning to think about how robust exports over the summer will match against slowly rising production levels, and calculating a required average build of 2mmbbls over the coming 5 or 6 months in order to reach ~80mmbbls by end-Sep / early Oct. Convention wisdom is calling for a traditional tug of war between the two factors, and through the duration of the coming summer. Terminal fees on the Gulf Coast, meanwhile, are presently seen on offer at 6.00-6.50cpg, though a more enthusiastic seller was seen in the upper 5cpg range. Buyers, however, have generally retreated to sub-5cpg levels unless a cargo is in need of being fixed immediately. A spate of fixtures was reportedly accomplished over the past few days, and market intel indicates those parcels were concluded with a 6-handle. NWE US crude oil inventories, rather than LPG publishing a big build caused a rapid drop in global crude oil values. This kept traders out of the TOT window and pushed outright CIF ARA values down over $30pmt. The transatlantic netbacks have been squeezed all week. Weakness in the European market has been partly attributed to the shift to the summer market and partly due to industrial demand expected to be reduced by planned maintenances. Production in the region has not increased as LPG continues to be spiked into the natural gas stream where possible. The continued question over whether European countries will put sanctions on Russian Energy, which would turn the market on it’s head. For the time being the crude moves and warmer temperatures coming in are keeping the market fairly quiet.

**MED**

 The Med remains tight, particularly propane, as strong winds hamper Algerian loadings. The commencement of Ramadan will keep the market quiet in the near future.
