## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Overall U.S. propane inventory levels reached 88.10mmbbls as of 28 October, having increased by 1.3mmbbls WoW, which is again viewed as a rather surprising high weekly build. Current stocks exceed the end-Oct average of 84.478 by 3.62mmbbls. In looking closely at the data, most appears in line except for PADD I (the East Coast) where an increase of 600,000bbls was recorded by the EIA. That level of build normally indicates the arrival of a seaborn import, but no imports occurred, which has now led to speculation that next week’s report may contain corrective data. Still, with robust production levels and very little weather-related demand in North America, it is quite possible that yet another build – though modest – will be reported next week. Terminal fees have meanwhile found life, and for Dec volumes now range on the buy-side in the 6cpg level to the sell-side of as much as 8cpg. NWE The arb from the US has been wide open for some time, but a double figure USD drop in arb values today, Thursday, has put trade discussions on hold. Spiking freight rates and the East West spread, trading above $100pmt, had pushed CIF ARA up to fight with the East for winter tons. With propane values in the region seemingly more closely correlated to Naphtha and Brent values than the Natural gas prices. Ethane and Natural gas values continue to be depressed in Europe as supplies from the US continue to surge. The physical propane window is assessed at November CIF ARA +$1pmt. A strong large cargo butane bid was seen at 92.5% of Naphtha which has been strengthening in tandem with stronger Brent.

**MED**

 The lack of cold weather continues to keep the region subdued. Spiking freight rates and a wide arb from the US, usually factors which get the market moving, has had the opposite effect as no winter demand has been seen yet this year. That said Marcus Hook cargoes continue to find their ways to the West Med.
