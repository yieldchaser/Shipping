## Market Commentary

AMERICAS As of the first week of Nov US propane inventories had eclipsed the 5-year average by some 5mmbbls and reached an overall total of 87.7mmbbls. Last week saw a modest 400,000bbl draw, but this week – which was anticipated to realize a draw of around a million barrels – saw a build of 100,000bbls. In effect, the market is in the ‘shoulder’ period, late though it may be. In short, as of 11 Nov the US inventories stand at 87.8mmbbls, or 118% of last year and 94% of two years ago. This propels the current stock level to around 5mmbbls above the 5-year average (82.6mmbbls). The immediate explanation as to why stocks increased this week is that (for starters) winter is later to commence than usual, but also includes missed laycans due to shipping delays as well as reduced load rates over the past couple of weeks at one of the Gulf Coast terminals; the combined impact being the modest build. Spot terminal fees now range rather widely, from the upper 5cpg level to over 7cpg, depending on timing / ratio / location, etc., but little is being accomplished due to shipping-related anomalies which stem from the fantastic delays presently being seen at the Panama Canal as well as the general lack of weather-related demand so far this season. NWE The physical window has calmed down after early week excitement due to reported LPG production curbs at Kollsnes in the North Sea. Since the arb has not seen any marked change, though buying interest has come off. Attributed to the continued mild winter and unchanged petchem demand. The East / West spread remains near $110pmt for propane, so unsold US Gulf Coast loading in 2nd half November and 1st half December are not considering selling in the West. Butane demand remains strong primarily for blending and lies 94% naphtha.

**MED**

 The mild weather continues to keep demand in the region well supplied by term deliveries.
