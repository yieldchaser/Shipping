## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Last week’s winter storm halted hydrocarbons production in the Permian Basin of West Texas. Simultaneously, feedstock deliveries to the chemical sector were stopped as that system shut down, as at the same time millions of barrels of Raw Make production was lost. All that while the Houston Ship Channel was closed. The weather has now abated and the various systems are being returned to service much more quickly than anticipated. Most are indicating that the lost time will be caught up with by the end of the month (if not sooner). The EIA on Wednesday reported that propane stocks in the US drew by a surprisingly high 5.2mmbbls as of the week ending 19 Feb. The market had assumed a draw of between 3.0 and 5.0mmbbls. The prospect of fog remains a possibility, and with that comes the potential impact on LPG vessel transits which could limit export volumes and, therefore, inventory draws. Meanwhile, WTI strength should foster production levels and allow propane stocks to begin recovery in Q2. Terminal fees are on the Gulf Coast are bid at 3.5cts/gal, with offers at/above 4cts/gal. Meanwhile, though, an East Coast March fixture is believed to have occurred at 5.25cts/gal. NWE Strength in the front of the market remains although CIF ARA has not tracked the rapid rise in Mt Belvieu. The cold weather in the US affected East Coast exports leading to a lower than anticipated volume of LPG finding it’s way to NWE from 1H March Marcus Hook. This in turn has pushed up the CIF ARA window premiums which are assessed at CIF ARA March +$11.5pmt for a 1H March TOT stem. March Propane/Naphta spread in the region is now -$44pmt the widest spread so far in 2021.

**MED**

 Has been quiet of late and with the US delays having a knock on effect and causing some supply uncertainty.
