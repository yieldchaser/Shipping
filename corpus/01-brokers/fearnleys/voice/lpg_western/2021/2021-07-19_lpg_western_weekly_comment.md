## Market Commentary

NWE The physical market in the region is assessed at a small discount to August CIF ARA. A bid and an offer were seen in the window, with the price difference to wide to bridge. The market is starting to settle back into a normal rhythm after the crude correction. Now that both Brent and WTI are trading above $70pmt propane/naphtha spread in August is at -$36pmt in the region. Keeping LPG demand from petrochemicals healthy. This being said the window offer was form a petchem company. The reasons for the resale is said to be receiving terminal congestion rather than demand reduction. For the time being the market remains tight with little volume set to be imported from the US in August.

**MED**

 Tightness prevails in the region. It seems like the buyers have outweighed sellers for the whole year so far. Mixed butane in the West Med is tight and at 96.6% Naphtha putting it at a premium to North West European coasters. AMERICAS The nearly universal reaction to the news of this week’s EIA report, which showed a weekly build of 3.1mmbbls, was one of surprise. Most had anticipated a more modest build of 1.6-1.8mmbbls. But the Gulf Coast showed a build of 2.2mmbbls, putting the total US propane inventory level at 62.7mmbbls as of 16 July. This compares to the 82.4mmbbls reported last year, and the 75mmbbls seen the year prior to that – and leaves current stock levels at 76% and 79% of the last two years, respectively. The MB propane market did not immediately react to the EIA data with much enthusiasm in either direction. Pre-EIA prices were flat at 106.00 cts/gal, and only a modest decline to 105.75cts/gal was noted in the hours after the data was released. By Thursday morning, though, crude upticks had positively influenced the market and the prices were again above 107.00 cts/gal. Otherwise, the buyback scheme embarked upon by Enterprise continues. It is believed that Enterprise has bought back 5 (or maybe 6) cargoes in Aug and remain a buyer for Sep, as well. Whether or not those same cargoes are then re-sold will then (somewhat) dictate the EIA’s inventory build rate, which will then have an impact on the pricing structure. Terminal fee ideas from the buy-side are in the 4.50-5.00 cts/gal range, while resale market expectations are in the 5.00-5.50cts/gal range. The terminal owners themselves, still under pressure from the E/P spread issues, have priced themselves at higher levels.
