## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Mont Belvieu propane values have rocketed upwards, but appear – for the moment – to be resting at just above the $1.40/gal level, though earlier in the week trades as high as $1.47/gal were reported. The strength in the crude market amid fears of an energy shortage are the immediate driving forces, but the entire LPG complex is being underpinned by concerns related to the domestic inventory position. This week the EIA released its findings for the week ending 24 Sep which revealed a rather surprising 2.6mmbbl improvement in overall stock levels to 72.9mmbbls, or about 72% of the same time last year, and 73% of two years ago. The ongoing worry is that US propane stocks will be insufficient to accommodate both winter-related domestic demand as well as the ongoing strength of the export market. However, a bit of downtime at the Galena Park terminal combined with continuing ‘buybacks’ in Houston will likely allow total export volumes to relax in September, and perhaps increase to more comfortable levels. This remains to be seen, however, since natural gas prices around the world have shot up, giving cause for discussions about fuel-switching in the direction of LPG. Fuel rationing and an (at least temporary) shift to coal, however, could potentially limit demand levels from that angle. NWE Propane market is tight, with continued buying interest in the window. The renewed 7 year value highs in CIF ARA have not been able to open the Arb from the US. With regional supply reduced due to refineries using LPG they produce instead of Natural Gas where possible. Local supply is unable to cover demand. So far that has been the only effect of the high natural gas prices in Europe on the LPG supply/demand balance. Though with natural gas in the region set to remain expensive for some time we could see LPG finding it’s way into new markets. Butane was bid at $755/t CIF Antwerp by Vitol and was booked by Equinor for 7,000t of normal butane. Whereafter Equinor offered a further 12,000t of butane at October CIF ARA +$7pmt. Equivalent to 109% of October Naphtha.

**MED**

 Sonatrach contract prices for October were set at $808pmt propane and $742pmt butane. The butane market remains tight, and the Sonatrach C4 to C3 discount was a surprise. Some traders were expecting the tons changing hands in NWE window at $755 might find their way to the Mediterranean at a premium. The worry is that a spell of cold weather will send prices, butane in particular, soaring in this tight market.
