## Market Commentary

AMERICAS The last few weeks have seen US propane inventories decline at surprising rates, but the accuracy of the data has been questioned since the devastating storms, a national US holiday, intermittent fog, regional production outages, massive (but short-lived) domestic demand and petrochemical downtime have each played a part in shaping the market. This week, though, both market sentiment and the EIA data indicate that things seem generally back on track since the government reported a week-over-week decline of just 47,000bbls – effectively an even draw – and leaving total US propane stocks at 41.199mmbbls. Some expectations were calling for a draw of 3 (or more) million barrels, while at least one observer was calling for a 1mmbbl build. We like to remind our customers the monthly eia inventories are much more accurate than the weekly’s. Propane values at Mont Belvieu reacted with mild weakness, not the kind that might be associated with the end of winter drawdowns, and remains in the low 90’s/gallon. Arbitrage economics did improve, however, and while export volumes have been limited as of late, at this point the terminals are all up and running at prescribed rates. We are calling the export market for 2 Q Bid 5.50 and offered 6 cpg. NWE The prompt European propane market has been weak, and demand for refined products in remains low. March/April backwardation is decreasing, now at minus $25pmt down from minus $30pmt. Double digit premiums to April CIF ARA are being discussed in the TOT window. We are seeing the widest Pro/Nap spread of the year so far at March minus $52pmt. A vessel headed for Europe diverted towards Panama tightening the prompt market. Large cargo butane in the region is at 88% Naphtha.

**MED**

 Discussions picked up this week with Cepsa public offer of 4KT butane FOB Algeciras. Their best offer of $588pmt FOB, did not find a buyer.
