## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Flat price Mt Belvieu propane has come off considerably this week and we are not entirely sure why. Have the traders given up on winter already, are trader’s being stopped out. Time will clarify, but for us it seems a bit early to be giving up on Winter. Terminal fees in the U.S. remain buoyant, and simultaneously continue to be multi-tiered. The terminals – if there is product and dock space remaining – are able to offer product from between the low 9cts/gal up to the low 10cts/gal range. The ongoing differential between ethane and propane values is believed to still be much of the cause. Re-sellers, meanwhile, are offering at levels just below that, but product is somewhat limited. On the other hand, Enterprise continue in their efforts to buy back cargoes (at its own facility) and are expected to be able to bid in the mid 5cts/gal range. Yesterday’s EIA propane inventory report for the US showed a decline of 1.3mmbbls as of the week ending 05 November, to an overall total of 74.8mmbbls – or 79% of last year and 82% of the total from two years ago. The breakdown shows that PADD I (the East Coast) drew down by 1.1mmbbls, primarily because of cooler temperatures in the region. PADD III, however, built by 200,000bbls, confounding many because export volumes for the week were robust. NWE The December window in Europe opened trading with a bang as a bid at +$21pmt to balance November CIF ARA quotes for 1-5 Dec. Showing that the physical market is tight and supporting the wide arb from the US. With the December East/West spread at +$72pmt it seems strong bids are needed to attract tons to the region. This could also tempt traders to look at selling in the region rather than risk waiting time at the Panama Canal. With propane/naphtha spread at +$73 it seems winter demand is beginning to drive the market. The butane market saw some action early in the week and remains tight and at 110% Naphtha.

**MED**

 Weather in the region has been mild and spot trading activity quiet. The market remains on the tight side and the arb from the US is wide open as well as from Northwest Europe. So far no spot cargoes are headed to the region. As the weather gets colder imports will be needed for the increased demand.
