## Market Commentary

NWE The CIF ARA forward curve is as flat as a crêpe! Traders in the region wait to see the full extent of LPG volume reduction due to maintenance in the North Sea. LPG exports from Karsto could be reduced by up to 77% from 15th May - 3rd June. Most had covered shorts anticipating this local production squeeze which has led to a quiet market of late. This has also brought the TOT Window premium down to May CIF ARA +$2.5pmt. Large cargo butane is assessed at 83% May Naphtha strong as Naphtha is at 3 year highs.

**MED**

 The region has been quiet with reduced demand due to coronavirus restrictions and lockdowns. This has led to buyers keeping their cards close to their chests as they believe they will be able to find tons when demand appears. AMERICAS The EIA this week released its propane inventory data amid expectations of a 750,000 - 1mmbbl build, only to deliver what was seen as a disappointing half-million barrel improvement. Overall US propane stocks as of 30 April are recorded at 41.471mmbbls, a deficit of 11.905mmbbls from the 59.376mmbbl total seen at the same time last year. Stock levels will need to build at an average rate of 1.8mmbbls per week for the next 22 weeks – which is completely within reach- (but not with such a large export program) in order to achieve the generally established minimum of ~80mmbbls by the first week of October. Domestic production levels are still healthy at 2.273mmbbls/day (compared to 2.046mmbbls/day last year), but export volumes – despite the reported lull this week – and res/com demand are still robust while petrochemical demand continues to slowly gain momentum. Those factors (plus any weather-related factors) could make reaching the 80mmbbl goal a challenge. As always, however, the price will resolve everything, and as such a handful of cargo cancellations were noted this week as cargo economics remain at touch-and-go levels. Terminal fees have recently seen a fair amount of volatility, having ended April in the 5-6ct/gal range, commenced May with offers at below 2cts, only to see a resurgence in the first week back to the low/mid 4ct/gal range.
