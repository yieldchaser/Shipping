## Market Commentary

NWE The physical market is assessed at August CIF ARA + $8pmt and the CIF ARA Large butane quotes were at 95% balance July Naphtha. Both strong physical discussions due to the fact that no August arrival cargoes have been heard heading to North West Europe from the US so far. With crude oil coming off and CIF ARA tracking Brent faster than Mt Belvieu is tracking WTI this is unlikely to change in the near future as the arb narrows. August Pro/Nap spread at -$37pmt will keep petchem demand up and they will have to source the product from somewhere, which should in turn widen the arb.

**MED**

 Butane market in the region has been strong these past days. Lack of supply from the US as well as demand enquiries is fueling strength. Tightness has been the theme of the summer in the region with values doing just enough to keep supply coming but not yet breaking out. AMERICAS The domestic US propane market received this week’s EIA inventory report with a lackluster response, as the 1.6mmbbl week-over-week build was generally in line with expectations. As of 09 July domestic US propane stock levels were listed at 59.60mmbbls, or 74% of the levels recorded at the same time last year, 80.30mmbbls. The pricing structure did very little to react afterwards and remained at about the 1.10/gal range, but overnight trading in Europe and Asia saw values in those markets decline, and with the US propane-to-WTI percentage at 64%, expectations are for MB prices to soften, particularly since WTI is now in retreat. Terminal fees are tiered, at this point, with a recent resale having occurred at a reported 4.625/gal for mid-Aug. Asking levels are now in the 5cts/gal range. The terminals themselves, however, remain at 6cts/gal (or above) as the Ethane/Propane pricing differential continues to dissuade the production of Low-Ethane propane, the export-grade product derived from the American spec, HD5, which has a higher ethane content.
