## Market Commentary

NWE Demand in the region has dropped off significantly now that the September propane naphtha spread is +34pmt. Reducing petchem and cracking demand. Leaving the region tight but with low demand the regional production sufficient to cover demand until winter buying picks up. With no signs of cold yet we expect the market to remain relatively tight with the CIF ARA window at a premium. Butane is at 98% August Naphtha and would need to rise to 106% to Naphtha to attract US tons. The arb from the US will most likely remain closed for the being as Mt Belvieu is forecast to remain strong.

**MED**

 The Med remains tight and searching for butane, with values now at 105.4% Naphtha. Demand has been good fro the time of year. The arb from the US remains firmly closed and leaves traders looking for tons to supply the region. AMERICAS U.S. propane inventory levels rose by 1.6mmbbls this week, bringing total levels to 66.761mmbbls as of 13th August. Propane stocks are now 75% of last year, though as the weeks have progressed over this summer the level of concern has continued to grow. The primary reason that stock build rates have been so limited is because export volumes have been so robust. The month of August is no exception and is expected to see near record-level departures. The buyback scheme initiated by Enterprise and the various cancellations which have been noted seem to be having little impact on the vessel tally. Meanwhile, re-sale terminal fees have come off over the course of this week, with offers now seen at 4.25cts/gal and buy-side indications at sub-4cts/gal. The near-term future of the domestic US propane market will be shaped by the outcome of the crop-drying season and the timing of the arrival and the ferocity of winter. In the meantime, though, MB propane values have been on the slow, steady rise and are now marked at nearly $1.15/gal ($599/mt).
