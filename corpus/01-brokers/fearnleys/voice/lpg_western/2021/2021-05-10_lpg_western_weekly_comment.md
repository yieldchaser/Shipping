## Market Commentary

NWE CIF ARA propane was weaker than crude, pushing pro/nap higher into the $100-$110pmts. Large cargo butane value in the region was down now at 79.5% Naphtha. The region has been well covered for May demand mainly through imports from the US East Coast. Traders in the region are now monitoring the North Sea Maintenance and expect it to come back on time, June 3rd. With bank holidays in much of the region Thursday, many traders have bridged the long weekend and will reassess the market next week.

**MED**

 The butane market in the region is seen as under pressure. Due to split ratio US cargoes heading to the region to discharge their butane. The only offer seen in the window was an FOB meeting Moroccan spec which was withdrawn before the close. Both the propane and butane have lacked the strength. AMERICAS If it’s not one thing it’s another. Asian and NWE CFR prices rose enough to what should have made cargos from the US workable, only to have freight go up further and faster, pushing the Arb back into cancellation economics. The domestic propane inventory build of 2.5mmbbls allowed the market a sigh of relief as stocks moved up from 41.5mmbbls to 44.0mmbbls as of 7 May 2021. The East Coast (PADD 1) saw a decline of 300,000bbls (from 3.8 to 3.5mmbbls) but given the volume of overwater exports that have been – and are still – occurring, this is not surprising. PADD 3, however (the Gulf Coast) saw a build of 2mmbbls, from 24.8 to 26.8mmbbls. Ostensibly this can be the result of either fantastic production levels, reduced export volumes or some combination thereof. The EIA, however, reported that production in the first week of May declined to 635,000bbls/day, down from 1.353mmbbls/day – and neither figure seems believable, since the much-discussed cancellations did not occur in the first week of May, but are expected in 2H May. As to June cancellations, there are believed to have been around a half-dozen, but in at least a couple of cases there were corresponding re-purchases (at lower rates), which thus allows for a modest “net” cargo cancellation total (so far) of 3. Rumors of an additional 3-4 were noted, but remain unsubstantiated. On the buy side, only the Brazilians seem to be continued spot buyers. Meanwhile, terminal fees are believed to have been fixed in the low 3cts/gal range for June. As of this writing price ideas for 2H 2021 are talked above 5cts
