## Market Commentary

NWE The big story in the region is Propane Naphtha spread going out to -$105 pmt for May. The Mt Belvieu CIF ARA arb has been widening since Wednesday. Although as the arb to Europe widened by about $10pmt so too the cash premiums in the TOT window decreased by $10pmt. The wide pro/nap will keep demand in the region high. The Mt Belvieu butane arb to NWE is above that of propane, which will contribute to maximum LPG demand.

**MED**

 The Med has been quiet in the wake of global propane moves and the region remains well supplied. AMERICAS Crude continues to march upwards, with May WTI improving this week to over $63/bbl and Brent is now approaching $67/bbl. Conversely, however, the Mont Belvieu propane market remains in retreat as non-TET values at Mont Belvieu have been in decline for the last 10 days, having most recently reached 77.50 cents/gal. The most recent price reduction comes on the heels of the EIA report which revealed that propane inventories in the U.S. had risen by 1.016mmbbls from the 2nd April to the 9th April to an overall US total of 40.588mmbbls. One year ago, in the first week of April 2020, stocks were recorded at 56.780mmbbls. Domestic production of propane rose modestly from 2.299mmbbls/day last week to 2.313mmbbls/day at present, remaining robust as compared to the year-ago rate of 2.061mmbbls/day. Overwater exports remain healthy despite a half-dozen cancellations, and terminal fees reflect the sentiment, having moved from below 4cts/gal to current asking levels of 6cts/gal. Improved interest from Europe on the basis of the Propane/Naphtha spread and a widening F/E arb – which is enhanced by reduced waiting times at Panama – should bolster the US-to-Asia trade and assist in maintaining healthy US waterborne trade volume.
