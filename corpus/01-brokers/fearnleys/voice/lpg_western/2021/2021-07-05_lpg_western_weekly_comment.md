## Market Commentary

NWE The first propane offer for a month was seen in the TOT window Wednesday, amid continued offers. The physical premium is $6.5pmt to July CIF ARA and the lone offer has not dampened buying interest in this tight market. Butane market in the region remains stroing at 95.5% July Naphtha. Though the arb from the US has improved significantly competition from Asia is ever present and spot fixtures to Europe are yet to be heard. The all time high outright values of LPG in Europe prompted a cargo to be sold from Stenungsund storage this week, an anomaly for the time of year. Another factor would have been the tight margins of producing Ethylene and propylene making re-exporting the cargo preferable.

**MED**

 Crude coming off has prompted an opportunistic rise in butane demand. The market in the region is bullish though not as strong as North West Europe. AMERICAS Last week’s EIA report spawned a propane pricing surge which continued unabated through the midpoint of this week when profit-taking is reported to have allowed market values to relax. Where a high point of $1.15/gal was reached, values just prior to this week’s EIA data release are pegged at 1.06/gal. As might be anticipated, many have taken cover on the sidelines, but a few transactions were noted. However, the pricing differential between ethane and propane seems to be as much of a factor in the limited activity levels as anything else. The terminals themselves can offer spot product and are clustered around asking levels of about 6ct as the cost of extracting the ethane from the propane (in order to convert HD5 propane to HD2 (or LEP) has become prohibitive. Meanwhile Enterprise, continues with its ‘buyback’ program and remains a buyer of any-date, any-ratio cargo from its terminal. Thus far a generous handful of parcels are believed to have been sold back to Enterprise in August, and talk is now heard as to September buybacks. Current economics favor the Eastbound trade, though in each case margins are at or below 4cts/gal, thus bringing notional terminal fees to around the 3.00 – 4.00 ct/gal range, though in one case a buyer reportedly panicked when the market spiked earlier this week and paid a significant premium. The EIA data release today – which was delayed due to U.S. Independence Day holiday – revealed a surprisingly low 500,000 barrel build, which was immediately met with jeers and mockery, as well as the early prediction that next week will see a significantly larger build.
