## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Though by now most of the problems related to the storm in February have been overcome, cargo loading issues persist on the Gulf Coast. Delays and setbacks have beleaguered Gulf Coast terminals, with recent lifting rates having been exacerbated by fog-days – or at least fog-mornings. Those days appear to have passed, however, as the weather along the upper Texas Gulf Coast is now clear, dry and cool. The EIA yesterday reported that domestic US propane stocks fell by 170,000bbls as of the week ending 21 Mar, leaving an overall total of 41.029mmbbls of propane in primary storage. This compares to the 61.379mmbbls recorded in mid-March 2020, a decline of 33%. Notably, where stock declines were noted in both PADDs I and II, PADD III inventories rose from 25.464mmbbls to 25.920mmbbls. This is likely due to a combination of curtailed exports and the slow return of the chemical sector. US production figures, meanwhile, actually declined; from 1.894mmbbls/day on 5th Mar to 1.867mmbbls/day on 12 Mar. March is all but done, but spot terminal fees for April remain the mid 5ct/gal range, with offers for May at 6cts/gal, or just below. Corresponding bids are in the low 5cts/gal range for May. Talk of June has yet to fully materialize, but notional rates were initially being talked at around 6cts/gal. NWE Market has tracked crude down to some extent although this has not hampered buying interest. March Pro/Nap at -$53pmt is keeping the market supported by the petrochemical buyers. Rising imports from the US have all been snapped up and the market remains on the tight side. ToT Window premiums have come down to April CIF ARA +$5pmt. A bid for large cargo butane was seen in the region for early April delivery was seen. Lifting April butane values to 90% Naphtha.

**MED**

 Has returned to it’s state of calm as demand in the region cools off. Increasing US tons finding their way to the Med has also helped. Moroccan demand has been off this year which has been attributed to persisting global travel restrictions depressing tourism in the country.
