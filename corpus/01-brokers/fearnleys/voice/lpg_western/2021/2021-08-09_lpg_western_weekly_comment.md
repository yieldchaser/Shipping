## Market Commentary

NWE The Northwest European market remains tight as minimal arb cargoes have been directed to the region in August and for September. The pro/nap spread has come in to -$4pmt which is reducing petchem demand. Holiday mode in the region is not the whole story as cargoes from the US are uneconomic and the region cannot survive without imports. Leading to the TOT window premiums to cover base seasonal product needs.

**MED**

 The region remains in a tight product environment. US arb cargoes not coming ti the region has led to buyers paying up only when necessary and excess demand being talked. AMERICAS All eyes are on US propane inventories, as the EIA yesterday surprisingly reported a draw of 700,000bbls in overall US propane stocks, notably a million barrel decline along the Gulf Coast (PADD III). US propane stocks are now 73% of the level achieved last year at the same time (89.3mmbbls) and 80% of those from two years ago (82.6mmbbls). Export volumes – despite the rising pricing structure – are the culprit, and as of this writing there appears little end in sight. Mont Belvieu propane market values topped $1.00/gal in late June, having eclipsed the $0.90/gal level at the end of May. Presently MB propane is pegged at over $1.13/gal for Sep barrels, and expectations are calling for even higher levels to be achieved as crop drying demand should soon commence, and the likelihood of realizing a comfortable inventory position prior to the onset of winter is fading. Re-sale terminal fees are now between $5.00-5.25cts/gal, within which range the last known transaction occurred. Meanwhile, Enterprise continues with its buyback scheme, and is offering $4.625/gal for September barrels.
