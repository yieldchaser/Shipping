## Market Commentary

NWE CIF ARA values have been weaker than both the Mt Belvieu and FEI price quotes, leaving the June East/West spread at +$57pmt. Houston / Flushing freight rates have stayed strong and just a bit below the Mt Belvieu / CIF ARA arb which is now -$47 for June. this not leave buyers/traders much margin to look at US FOB cargoes and hence the FOB values continue to fall.

**North Sea**

 maintenance is progressing on schedule with production in the region is set to ramp up from June 3rd.

**MED**

 The butane market in the region continues to look long. May butane is well offered, though 1H June shorts in the region are yet to be covered. Meanwhile with seasonal demand for propane non existent, it is very difficult to find buyers. AMERICAS Most of the domestic US market players and observers were calling for a small propane inventory build this week; perhaps something under a million barrels, with most generally clustered around the 650-750,000bbl range. In fact, however, the EIA data revealed a decline of nearly 400,000 bbls from 44.4mmbbls last week to 44.1 mmbbls. PADD I stocks fell from 3.9 to 3.7mmbbls, PADD II showed an improvement from 11.2 to 11.5mmbbls, while the Gulf Coast fell from 26.3mmbbls to 25.7mmbbls – all during a time when record-level exports are believed occurring. Propane stocks in the US are now squarely at minimum tolerances, with the Days-of-Supply tally running at 18.09 days, where the previous 5-year low (which occurred in 2018) was pegged at 19.83 days. Prices reacted immediately, moving up within minutes from ~85cts/gal at Mont Belvieu to over 87cts/gal before lunchtime. Netbacks, meanwhile, which were already suffering to the point of cancellations – seven of which are believed to have occurred for June – may be sent over the tipping point, though no additional cancellations were immediately revealed. Terminal fees, presently offered at 3cts/gal, reflect the difficult arbs.
