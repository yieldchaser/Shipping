## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Expectations that the EIA would report on Wednesday morning a draw in US propane stocks was unanimous, though the anticipated rate of decline was contested. Some were forecasting a 2mmbbl draw, while others were predicting as much as a 6mmbbl decline. Historically, the largest single weekly draw occurred in Jan 2017 when stocks fell by 7.188mmbbls. The EIA yesterday reported a 6.7mmbbl draw, surprising most and prompting discussions related to minimum comfort levels of propane inventories in the US. As of last Friday there were 66mmbbls ‘in tank’, and there are another 9 weeks of winter-related demand (with winter ending mid-March). History shows that stocks have declined on a weekly basis from between 1.1mmbbls/day to 2.4mmbbls/day. At 1.1mmbls day a ~10mmbbl draw can be anticipated, at which point stocks will have fallen to 56mmbbls. If, however, stocks decline at a rate of 2.4mmbbls/day, ~22mmbbls will be removed stocks and overall stocks will have fallen to 44mmbbls. It is when stocks dip below around 30mmbbls that inventory difficulties are typically encountered, so unless an unusual series of events occurs it seems unlikely at this point that stocks will fall to jeopardized levels – particularly if crude oil prices are on the rise which should bolster domestic production levels. Furthermore, according to the EIA,propane production rose by 83,000bbls/day to 2.34mmbbls/day as of last week. The issue, however, is not necessarily the condition of the overall stock levels, but those on along the US Gulf Coast – in PADD III – which includes much of the production areas, but also most of the exporting sites. There are, as of last week, 34.96mmbbls inn PADD III, down by a significant volume of 4.279mmbbls week-over-week. Of course the price of the molecules themselves will ultimately have a say in the matter. Mont Belvieu propane values very quickly (and rather predictably) rose after the release of the EIA data, climbing quickly to 90cts/gal for prompt barrels. Perhaps the weather (or lack thereof) in the US will finally have a hand in the matter, too.
