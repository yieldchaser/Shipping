## Market Commentary

AMERICAS US propane inventory levels continue to be of strong interest among both the domestic participants as well as the foreign players. Ultimately the Mont Belvieu propane pricing structure is dependent upon availability, and as such stock levels are a focal point. Using the current total US propane stock level of 41.2mmbbls as a starting point, and considering the 5-year average summer build of 43.5mmbbls, it can be calculated that an end-Sep/ely-Oct 2021 propane inventory level of ~84.8mmbbls should be achieved. Except that this is no ordinary year. If stocks build by 10% less than average (or 39.1mmbbls), a total of ~80mmbbls will be achieved. Likewise, if the 5-year minimum summertime build of 37.9mmbbls is seen, a total of less than 80mmbbls would be seen, thus painting a rather bullish pricing picture. Meanwhile, domestic production levels are mostly recovered after the storm in February, but the petchem sector continues at less than full operating rates, having fallen to the low 70% utilization range. The domestic res/com sector will likely only realize averages this year, leaving export volumes as the deciding factor, which in Q1 2021 have been light. The combination of sub-par chemical consumption and generally minimized exports should likely allow for some of the bullishness to be tempered. As to the current terminal fee market, rates for Apr are seen bid at 4.5, with sellers offering around 5.50. May values are tentatively pegged at just above that range, indicating that a bit of strength has emerged. NWE Good cracker run rates continue in the region. Butane the most favorable feedstock, is at 91.5% Naphtha and on the tight side in the region. Split ratio large cargo volumes coming to the region have not hit the resale market. The TOT window remains high single digit premium. The market is still tight though a large volume of exports are arriving int he region due to planned maintenance at local gas fields reducing production. In ARA the switch to summer gasoline spec usually has a marked bearish effect on C4 prices. Though reduced gasoline demand this year has kept butane blending demand low throughout winter.

**MED**

 The logistics issues caused by the blockage of the Suez Canal have not had a marked affect on the LPG market in the region yet. VLGCs will be affected with at least four waiting to transit in the north and two at the Southern anchorage. No discussions to place tons in the region due the canal delays have been heard. Moroccan buyers are well covered dampening butane interest in the West Med.
