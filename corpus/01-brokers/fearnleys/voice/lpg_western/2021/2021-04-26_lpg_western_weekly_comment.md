## Market Commentary

NWE The TOT window premium has been steady this week, and now lies at May CIF ARA +$11pmt. Seen as a positive sign as the curve has been flattening out, with May/June +$5pmt. Butane has been strong in the region, 82% Naphtha. The butane strength and propane premiums are not enough to attract US cargoes. Mt Belvieu strength on both C3 & C4 has stymied the trans Atlantic trade for May after a record 485,000 MT in May. These moves have overshadowed propane / naphtha spread going back out $101 per MT, which will continue to stimulate demand in the region.

**MED**

 The Sonatrach prices for May were set at $465/ $460 for propane and butane respectively. The butane market in the West Med looks long. Demand has been reduced in Morocco due to ongoing Coronavirus restrictions over Ramadan. Demand during this period has historically been high. Propane market has been quiet in the region, though assessed stronger. AMERICAS The pace of the market has been subdued this week, though a renewed sense of negativity related to US propane inventory levels began to creep in after the EIA data release on Wednesday. The arb has narrowed due to the recent strength in the Mont Belvieu market, and to the point where the lengthy list of cargoes presently being offered is only growing as buyers remain scarce. As for the EIA propane inventory levels, many now openly doubt that propane stocks will be able to rise to at least the 80mmbbl mark by early October – and that doubt is causing prices to increase. The question is whether stocks at the conclusion of the ‘build’ season will be sufficient to accommodate both exports and winter-related demand simultaneously in the U.S. Expectations for this week’s EIA propane inventory report was again a mixed bag of predictions. Some were calling for a small draw of around a half-million barrels, others for a build of a million barrels. The relatively lighter export volumes but improving petchem consumption rates were considerations, but there has also been strong production levels thus far in 2021. The half-million barrel build that was ultimately reported is surely a step in the right direction, but hardly sufficient, and prices reacted by increasing. US terminal fees were earlier seen at ~4cts/gal for an end-May transaction, but as there were a host of sellers, the asking price is now below 4 cpg and buyers ideas well below that. Based on current econs, we expect cancellations for both May and June.
