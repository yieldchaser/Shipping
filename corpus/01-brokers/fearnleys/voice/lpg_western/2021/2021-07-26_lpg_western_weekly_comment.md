## Market Commentary

NWE The arb to the US remains closed, this has limit supply to the region throughout August, and limited tons are set to flow into the region in 1H Sept. Though supply has been reduced and the NWE market remains tight CIF ARA values are not improving. Demand reduction due to petchem demand slowing down has left the market balanced though bids continue to outweigh offers. One of the reasons for the slow down is Pro/Nap margins are steeply backward, around $10pmt per month from -$46 in August to -$17pmt in November. CIF ARA backwardation is widening, showing prompt tightness. The physical window is assessed at August CIF ARA flat. The butane market has been quiet and is at 95.5% naphtha.

**MED**

 A 4KT butane offer was seen in the market and although it was discounted during the window no buyer was found. This was put down to strengthening freight in the region. The offer put downward pressure on West Med butane FOBs. AMERICAS Per the EIA report on Wednesday, U.S. propane inventories grew last week by precisely the anticipated amount: 1.9mmbbls. Very little reaction was immediately seen, though 24 hours later market values kicked off trading with bids at 110.50cpg but this could be in sympathy with the placement of the Aug CP, which was higher than anticipated. As the month of August is upon us the buyback scheme embarked upon by Enterprise should very likely result in higher inventory build rates as Enterprise are believed to have purchased at least 5, if not 6 cargoes in Aug. As of 23 July propane stocks in the U.S. are 76% of last year and 82% of those recorded two years ago. Enterprise are known to be buyers of Sep cargoes, and should sellers accept their offer, inventories would then likely be in still better position for entry to the winter season. Meanwhile, rumors that Targa declared Force Majeure due to a fractionator going down are unfounded. In fact, a minor mechanical issue occurred at the terminal itself, and though a F/M was declared, the problem was very quickly resolved and loading delays of no more than a day are expected, according to Targa. As for terminal fees, the terminal operators remain priced out due to the E/P pricing differentials while the re-sale market remains approximately on either side of 5cts/gal.
