## Market Commentary

AMERICAS Volatility is the keyword in the Mont Belvieu LPG market this week. Intra-day price swings of up to a nickel have been recorded, leaving many on the sidelines. Crude and natural gas strength have led the way, pausing only for profit taking. Propane stock levels in the U.S. continue to be the primary focus, though there remains a steady eye on both export volumes and ‘buyback’ numbers at EPD, of which there have been a solid 7 (if not 8) for Oct. Total inventory levels as of Oct 1 2021 were recorded by the EIA at 72.3mmbbls, down by 600,000bbls from the previous week – and 71% of the total achieved in 2020. Weather-related demand in North America will no doubt compete with export volumes over the Q4 ’21 and Q1 ’22. Spot terminal re-sale values are in the sub-6cts/gal range for full propane lots in 2H Nov. Buy-side interest is hovering at above a nickel but uncertainty is so far keeping the two sides at odds. NWE Two VLGCs set to arrive in the region have pushed the physical cargoes to a $4.5pmt discount to paper. The propane naphtha spread for September averaged +$45pmt. This lead petrochemical users taking in 325,000 MT, to the lowest monthly intake for 15 months. The October Pro/Nap spread stands at $119pmt today, and some petrochemical users are still taking tons. Due to it being more economical to run a minimal rates than fully shutting down. The draw in Mt Belvieu inventories will keep the arb to Europe closed and with the East/West spread at $51pmt for October and rising further down the curve we do not expect to see many more tons imported from the US in the near future. Butane in the region continues to be assessed at 105% October Naphtha, though this translated to a $15.5pmt drop in value as naphtha crashed.

**MED**

 The regions is nervous about winter supply. With production issues in Algeria reducing propane output and the arb to the US closed, traders are wondering where they will find supply this winter. For the time being the market is on the tight side and traders are being cautious not to get caught in short supply if demand picks up.
