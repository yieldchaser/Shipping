## Market Commentary

The market opened this week on a relatively balanced but gradually softer footing, as parts of Europe was off on Monday - with activity mainly centred around TCT and short-period employment. In the Atlantic, continued South America grain movements supported cargo demand into both regional and Atlantic destinations, although increasing vessel availability and flexible positioning kept pressure on rates. The Supra/Ultramax segment saw a steady flow of prompt June openings globally, contributing to a more competitive tonnage list and allowing charterers to apply increased pressure in negotiations. In Asia, trading activity remained focused on regional distribution within Southeast and Northeast Asia, with cargo flow steady but insufficient to significantly tighten market conditions. Period enquiry remained active across short and long durations, though a persistent gap between owners’ ideas and charterers’ bids continued to slow longer-term conversions. Overall, the Handy and Supra markets remained stable but leaned slightly in charterers’ favour, as balanced cargo flow, ample tonnage supply and cautious fixing activity kept rates largely range-bound across most regions.
