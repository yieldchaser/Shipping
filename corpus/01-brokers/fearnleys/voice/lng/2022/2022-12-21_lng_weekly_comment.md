## Market Commentary

With limited activities observed across both basins, the spot LNG shipping market is rather soft this week. There are a few requirements being worked, but predominately within the East of Suez and mostly are “optimization” deals. The prompt vessel availability is slightly long against the requirements seen, and ships opening after 25 January – 1H February 2023 may see further discount against the current spot rates. However, the premium to charter a larger modern vessel is still present and with spot cargo prices currently stable and significantly higher against the term, it should incentivize charterers to pursue the larger ships and maybe for a longer time charter. A small window of opportunity has emerged as the spread between TTF and JKM spot prices have reversed in recent days, with JKM now commanding a premium amidst TTF uncertainty in view of the new €180 price caps implemented by European Regulators. Furthermore, with Europe’s cold snap now behind us and with Asian heating demand forecasted to rise on an outlook of colder weather, we might just see this TTF-JKM reversal remain in place in the coming weeks, resulting in cargoes and maybe even Santa potentially diverting from the usual European destinations to the Far East? On the newbuilding front, despite no new orders in the last weeks, year 2022 ends up as a record ordering year for LNGCs, reportedly valued at above US$35 billion for the year. And lastly, we at Fearnley LNG wish you a joyous festive season!
