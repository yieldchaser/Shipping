## Market Commentary

Although opportunities in the spot freight market are only trickling through, giving a sense of a slowing this week, this is driven more by the lack of availability rather than demand for vessels. This illiquidity, a persist characteristic of 2022, means vessels that do open for charter continue to set record highs: since our last weekly report, new watermarks for both spot and multi-year have been concluded on modern tonnage leading to almost zero 2-stroke vessels being available from independent Owners from now until 2024. 170,000m3 plus vessels with substantial availability remain a “Unicorn” these days, and we can almost say the same for TFDE vessels, leaving steam ships attracting lots of attention and successfully fixing. Open tenders for vessels continue to hit the market for short and mid-term durations. With more Charterers looking for delivery beyond 2022, some are having to adapt their strategies to expediate the process. In previous years, it has been deemed slightly early to seek tonnage for next year during winter, yet the priority to secure shipping remains strong for many thus bringing forward many procurement needs. Yangzijiang Shipbuilding have made their debut in the large LNG newbuilding sector with two orders for 175,000 cbm vessels delivering between 2025 to 2026 and we kept LNG newbuilding prices stable this week despite shipyards continuously reporting good interest for LNG slots.
