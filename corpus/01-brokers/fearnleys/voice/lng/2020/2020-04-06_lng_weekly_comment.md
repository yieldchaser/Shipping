## Market Commentary

Another week of uncertainty has passed, with various schemes and scenarios stretching out like a river delta for the coming months, and owners and charterers face decisions today armed with an incomplete picture of an unknown future. The cargo market remains soft, and following several force majeure events and high inventory levels one might fear the worst; however, trading uncertainty has led to some increased tonne-time, and travel restrictions may cause some terminal operators and ship owners to defer maintenance to later in the year. Amidst this, the cargoes have kept coming…so far. Availability has broadly kept pace with fresh requirements, so rates and voyage economics are holding firm, albeit with some froth. Further out, an array of factors muddy the pitch even more: the persistence or otherwise of COVID-19; the continued level of production out the US Gulf; the pace of demand growth as different economies begin their recoveries at different times and at different rates; rescheduled terminal maintenance hitting LNG supply as we enter winter; a possible tightening of the shipping market if a surge of vessels head in for dry dock…as we move through the year, only one thing appears certain: nobody will get every call correct. Yard activity has been quiet, with no new orders placed this week.
