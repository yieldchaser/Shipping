## Market Commentary

The sense of market optimism radiates throughout the world as stock markets reach “all time high” and COVID-19 vaccines are issued out to citizens of various countries, contributing to the sense of hope to get back to normal times. In parallel, the buoyancy amongst the LNG players are increasingly evident as cargo prices continues to escalate, leading to trading opportunities for LNG traders, producers and buyers alike. LNG shipowners and sub-letters are currently enjoying the relatively firm charter rate levels in the markets which may be prolonged if the relatively strong demand for LNG continues. It is not implausible to expect the arbitrage to open, to pave way for more inter-basin spot trades where increased tonne-miles would maintain the tightening of the shipping market throughout this winter. As the year is about to end, LNG buyers and producers are now close to complete their annual delivery programme. This could further reveal potential shipping requirements and/or availability for sub-let in gas year 2021. Despite a mere three weeks to the end of 2020, most certainly the intensity of LNG and shipping trades seems not to be tapering down short term. No new LNG vessel orders were reported this week. However, one owner may have retired the 1981-built Pacific Energy and 1984-built Atlantic Energy as these vintage vessels were reported sold for demolition after being in lay-up for some years.
