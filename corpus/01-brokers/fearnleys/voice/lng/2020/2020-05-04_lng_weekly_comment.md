## Market Commentary

It has been a slightly quieter week as we have been enjoying several public holidays across the globe (namely Labour Day holidays and the Golden Week in Japan) giving the markets a short breather. Moreover, based on the news this week, the Covid19 – situation might find a bit more light at the end of the tunnel, as various countries have slowly released their plans for lifting some of the ongoing restrictions. This is cautiously positive news for the global economy and energy demand, but the road to recovery is still very uncertain. The same fundamentals apply to the world of LNG, where the supply overhang is still very much a fundamental problem causing bearish views for the rest of the year for both cargo volume and shipping. That said, we have seen a bit more interest in multimonth TC-periods as Charterers’ and Owners’ ideas on expected rate levels have narrowed. This might be more speculative than purely demand driven, but nonetheless seeing more term activity is a good sign. In the spot market, we are recording a small softening on the rate levels this week, as the ongoing charter interest, which is mainly driven by various spot cargo tenders, is not enough to compensate for the lengthy vessel availability list. Finally, there has been no new large LNGC orders reported this week (and orders for first 5 months 2020 are down by 12 units compared to 2019). With a strong US dollar, low interest rates and limited new orders from other shipping segments, there could be a risk of softening newbuilding prices.
