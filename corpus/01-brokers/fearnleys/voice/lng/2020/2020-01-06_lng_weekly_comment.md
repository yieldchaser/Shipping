## Market Commentary

It seems that the 2020 business year effectively only started this week as the majority of the market players returned to their desks from their holidays. There has been more activity in all of the basins this week, with a number of fresh spot requirements emerging together with one long-term shipping tender from a European utility. However, due to the lack of fixtures in the recent weeks, the spot rates continue to stay under pressure as the number of available tonnage have increased. Yet, if we continue to see further requirements developing, especially ahead of the impending Chinese New Year, we might see rates holding better than expected as the overall shipping length is not that drastic. Furthermore, the increasing unrest in the Middle-East is causing some concern to those who have LNG cargoes in and out from the region, and the potential ramifications to the shipping market still remains unknown. IMO2020 regulations are also now in full-force and we are already seeing the spread between low-sulphur fuel and high-sulphur fuel rise to highest levels seen in years, as quoted prices for low-sulphur fuels have soared. Albeit LNG shipping is less affected by the surge of low-sulphur fuel prices, this will definitely have an impact on ballast bonus discussions and Owners' decision on heel retention after voyages. On the newbuilding front, there were no new orders this week, although it was post reported that Hyundai Samho Heavy industries got the final LNGC newbuilding order for the calendar year 2019.
