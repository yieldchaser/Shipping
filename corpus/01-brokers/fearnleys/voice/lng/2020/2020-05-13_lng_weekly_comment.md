## Market Commentary

The inertia of last week’s shipping market continued at the start of this one, as a lack of fresh activity held the market in a slight limbo. The cargo market has only seen a modest improvement in Asia: Japanese inventory levels are now nearing capacity, and with plenty of cargo already on the water, we're likely to see an increase in floating activity over the next few weeks. However, with a healthy tonnage list in the Pacific well into July, rates continue to trickle off. The Atlantic is showing a softening market for intra-basin trade, with sentiment falling and uncertainty on the cargo side. Conversely, ships moving East with US Gulf spot cargoes are still fixing at last-done levels. Multi month enquiries are becoming a hot topic as some charterers look at taking coverage in this seasonally soft market. Looking ahead, there are still plenty of unanswered questions caused by the pandemic that will have an impact on LNG trades and LNG shipping for the rest of the year: dry docking schedules, SIRE inspections, crew changes to name but a few and it's not until countries and travel arrangements begin to return to the 'new normal' that these will be answered. No newbuild orders seen to be placed at Shipyards this week, despite economics in favour of the buyer. We have therefore kept newbuild prices stable.
