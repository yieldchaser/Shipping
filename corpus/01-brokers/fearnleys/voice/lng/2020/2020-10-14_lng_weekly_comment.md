## Market Commentary

It has been another busy week in the LNG shipping market as the anticipated Q4 activity is really ramping up. LNG cargo prices are on a rapid rise in the Far East as buyers have started to secure their early winter cargoes in expectation of a colder winter. This has made disponent owners to think twice before subletting their vessels out since the risk of finding yourself short, or in the mercy of tighter shipping market, has gone up. Also, the possibility of arbitrage opening between the West and the East has increased, and capturing that potentially short period might only be available to those with enough length in their shipping portfolio. Aforementioned is also reflected in the current elevated spot shipping rates, with especially the East of Suez seeing highest demand for tonnage whereas the West of Suez is tightening up quickly, but still lacking slightly behind due to a few uncertainties, such as the latest hurricane aftermath to some of the USG liquefaction plants. During these times it is however good to remember that the final quarter has been historically the most volatile period within the calendar year, and this seems to be true even in this extraordinary year of 2020, and by the looks of it, we might have very interesting weeks ahead. In the newbuilding side, it was reported that DSME has signed firm contracts to build six Arc7- ice class LNG carriers for two separate European companies with delivery expected in 2H 2023.
