## Market Commentary

The LNG shipping spot market has strengthened particularly in the West, driven by a surge of firm requirements and fixing activities. Partly as a result of this and also charterers having to manage uncertainties in their own scheduling, the availability of vessels has noticeably reduced and there are barely two-stroke vessels available in the spot market for the upcoming month. However, Europe's storage levels remain at 99.24% capacity and with Asia also experiencing high inventories, there is little prospect of a sustained arbitrage. Unless the cargo market sparks into life and drives demand, there is unlikely to be a big upturn in spot rates. On the projects side, it has been reported that MISC has agreed to convert an old steam-powered LNG carrier, the Puteri Delima Satu, into an FSU for Petronas. This conversion is set to become operational in 2025 under a 20-year contract. Furthermore, BP plans to launch the Tortue FLNG project in the first quarter of 2024, located in Mauritania and Senegal. There were no new buildings ordered last week.
