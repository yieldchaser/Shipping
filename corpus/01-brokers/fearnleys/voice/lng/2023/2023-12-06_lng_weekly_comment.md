## Market Commentary

As we enter the final weeks of the year, there is no doubt that the bulls are a little quieter than the bears right now. A couple of softer spot fixtures particular in Asia have dampened hopes of a stronger winter, and with the recent cold spells having barely any impact on LNG prices there aren’t many signs of a turnaround. It was natural then, that one of the main topics of conversations at last week’s CWC gathering in Athens and at the festive parties this week has been where the market will go from here (even taking airtime away from emissions clauses and the impact of ETS!). Some will be looking back exactly a year to the crash in the LNG spot market from those record highs, while others may point to fixtures already done for January at levels similar to those seen for November and December. Either way, the discussions are around the pace of change rather than the direction. The same is true for multimonth and multiyear charters: sentiment is softening and there are several candidate vessels, yet charterers are a little reluctant to move at this stage. To finish on a more positive note, with 2023 seeing a drop in the number of fixtures versus 2022 (and 2022 in turn seeing fewer than 2021), a consistent view is emerging of an expected increase in liquidity next year. There should be plenty to keep us busy! At the yards, there were no newbuildings reported this week and prices remained stable.
