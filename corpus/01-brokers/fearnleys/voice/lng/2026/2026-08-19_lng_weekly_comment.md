## Market Commentary

The complete collapse in the Atlantic freight market continues, with owners and subletters having to battle not only against a considerable number of competing vessel but also against sharp FOB economics. The fall in headline rates is stark and is highly unusual outside of the shoulder months, but reflects a dynamic often seen in the LNG spot market: when rates begin to move one way or the other, they can accelerate so rapidly as subletters see the same market signals. There are signs that some players will prefer to hold on to tonnage rather than sublet in this market, although this is likely to support a modest floor rather than materially raise rates at this stage. The Pacific is still considerably higher, although it is somewhat illiquid and deals have been concluded at surprisingly high rates given the number of available vessels (mainly due to cargo-specific restrictions). Looking a little further forward, there is more tonnage emerging in September and with less motivation to ballast back West this may apply some downward pressure. If the spread does close, will it be led by an Atlantic revival or a softening Pacific?
