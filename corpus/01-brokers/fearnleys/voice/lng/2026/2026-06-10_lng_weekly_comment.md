## Market Commentary

The persistent arbitrage to head East and a relative lack of available vessels should be squeezing the spot market, but the Atlantic looks to be holding steady at best or slightly softening. A clear-out of tonnage for prompt dates in the Pacific has resulted in some uplift for TFDE’s, despite pocket lengths on modern tonnage being priced aggressively. Higher LNG prices and a more volatile trading environment will present traders and portfolios with opportunities and this should lead to higher freight, which is certainly the case where any real flexibility is preserved for charterers, yet there is a natural counter to this with lost volume and weak shipping fundamentals. The interplay between these competing forces is driving the bid/offer spread at the moment, and many freight discussions are run in parallel with traders looking at FOB sales so there is a cap on spot rates. Interestingly, this dynamic is already leading to divergent strategies for winter. Most market participants see the same factors (less volume, more ships, higher LNG prices, buyers competing), but while some see the potentially strong trading dynamics as a basis for taking on additional tonnage others see the weak fundamentals as a reason to hold off - markets are always more active and much more interesting when there are such different views.
