## Market Commentary

Volatility remains the key theme as conflict in the Middle East continues. The questions posed in last week’s report remain largely unanswered and the swings seen on crude pricing after fresh assaults on Gulf region assets over the weekend have added another layer of uncertainty for LNG trading. This, along with many vessels marketed for sublet, has led to a cautious mood with more bearish undertones. Nevertheless, after a softening from the initial reactive spike we saw last week, rates appear to have held with prompt availabilities commanding strong charter rates around $200kpd for 2-strokes and above $100k for TFDE’s in both basins. There is a sense of charterers holding off for now as they hope for more clarity for laycans further out in the fixing window, especially with the emergence of more sublets and potential competition; however, every day brings fresh turmoil and we are now facing the prospect of mines in the Straits of Hormuz and several vessels have been struck by projectiles in the region. So while the early days of the attack saw charterers typically scrambling to charter in, reshuffle or sell FOB, the more recent days have been shaped by caution due to the underlying length in the market and the increasing number of availabilities shown. Where the LNG and the LNG shipping markets go in the coming days is still uncertain.
