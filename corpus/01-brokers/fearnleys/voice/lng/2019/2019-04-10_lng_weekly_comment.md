## Market Commentary

This week brought most of the LNG players back to the grind after last week’s meetings and networking in Shanghai. Activity seem to have “hit the ground running” with a firmer sentiment - from what could have been the lowest level of year 2019 with regards to the intensity of the LNG shipping activities. While the Chinese players dominated the showcasing of their LNG capabilities in the LNG19 conference, this week everybody joined in (traders, exporters, importers alike) and chartered vessels from all basins. This has resulted in some vessels finding the way out from their idling positions and we see a number of sublet vessels that were available, being withdrawn from our availability lists to do internal trades, further reducing the list of tonnage seeking employment. Despite the recent activities, headline rates remain stable, but with the likelihood of improvements on the ballast bonus figures going forward. We expect full round trip economics for the shipping costs to maybe come before the headline rates improve. On the newbuilding front, Maran Gas Maritime and NYK again have hit the headlines with both ordering yet another 174,000m3 LNG vessel respectively, with deliveries from DSME for the former and Hyundai Samho for the latter, to be ready within 2022. NYK’s order is reportedly on the back of a long term charter of up to 20 years including optional extension periods.
