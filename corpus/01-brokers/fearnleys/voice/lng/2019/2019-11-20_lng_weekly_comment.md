## Market Commentary

Sentiment softened throughout the week as a lack of requirement was felt in both basins. Despite the lack of activity, the ideas being discussed suggest that expectations are being lowered in terms of what rates can be achieved. More tonnage became available both prompt and into the New Year in the Pacific and while the Atlantic and Middle East remain tight, there’s still not enough requirement in the market to add much positivity to the rates. Unsold volume still lingers in both Basins, and with muted demand, it seems to push product prices down. That combined with an increase in tonnage availability, have reduced the Bullish sentiment the market held not too long ago. One continuing uncertainty that may support tonnage providers is the prospect of floating storage – we’ve seen more floaters discharging recently but with inventories remaining high we might not have seen the end of floating storage. That said, with the declining sentiment we’re currently being exposed to, the risk is that we may see rates softening as the fixing window moves closer and closer to the New Year, unless we see a sudden surge in requirements. Term tender activity is high, but no large LNG newbuilding orders were reported this week and prices remain stable.
