## Market Commentary

Spot charter rates are showing signs of firming, particularly in the Atlantic where spot rates are reporting into double-digits again. There is still considerable sublet tonnage to cap significant rate movements, but for those showing length this is a welcome sign. The Pacific premium remains intact but under scrutiny, as an oversupplied fleet offsets any tightening effects from higher chartering activity. In the LNG cargo market, the Atlantic Basin saw FOB prices decline, tracking losses in the European delivered market, while Asia-Pacific prices held largely stable amid waiting tenders. Demand from Northeast Asia remains muted, with buyers hesitant to commit at current levels, keeping spot price movement limited. The narrowing arbitrage between Atlantic and Pacific deliveries has led to cargo diversions, with several Pacific-loaded shipments switching course towards Asia as the European premium erodes. Storage dynamics are adding another layer of complexity. Europe's gas storage levels have fallen close to 40% of capacity, driven by a colder winter, though EU officials maintain there is no immediate risk to supply security. In response, discussions are ongoing to relax refill targets and provide market flexibility. In contrast, Asia’s LNG imports have dropped to a 22-month low, as milder temperatures in North Asia and elevated spot prices have dampened demand. Buyers remain hesitant to engage in the spot market at current levels, reinforcing the bearish outlook. Geopolitical developments remain a source of uncertainty, with the gas markets awaiting news of the Ukraine war and potential ceasefire negotiations, the resumptions of some limited LNG traffic through the Suez Canal and news that the U.S. is considering restrictions on Chinese-built ships and operators with such vessels in their fleets. It’s never a dull week.
