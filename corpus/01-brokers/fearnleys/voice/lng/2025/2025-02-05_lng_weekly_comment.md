## Market Commentary

Spot charter rates remain subdued across the board despite a fair number of outstanding requirements both East and West of Suez. The Atlantic is where rates have been at their lowest due to Europe’s dominance as a destination of U.S. cargoes resulting in the bulk of 2-stroke length in the region. The premium in the Pacific is under close examination at the time of writing and whilst an increasing number of vessels with warm tanks is trimming availability to a certain extent, liquidity is likely not sufficient to create any tightness. Competition for any requirements remain strong. Few deliveries to India amid high LNG prices are limiting open tonnage for Middle East loading and rates are more resistant to the pressure observed elsewhere. LNG has been making headlines once again across mainstream media this week as China responded to US’ tariffs on Chinese imports by announcing a 15% tariff on US LNG and coal, as well as 10% tariffs on oil and farm equipment. Short term, these tariffs will likely have limited impact as the Atlantic-Pacific arbitrage is largely shut and European storage targets throughout 2025 will continue to outshine Asian demand. The effect is likely a) more focus on FOB purchases from USA and b) a continuation of the increasing tendency for demand to be met by cargoes originating in the same basin, which is bearish ton-miles. Longer term implications are multifaceted, but it remains to be seen whether and to what extent this brewing trade war escalates before its impact is felt in the LNG space. No new LNG newbuild orders were placed last week, but yards remain busy with new containership enquiries.
