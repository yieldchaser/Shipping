## Market Commentary

As many market participants gather for the World Gas Conference in Beijing this week, one may be forgiven for expecting market activity to come to a halt as in previous years, but the LNG shipping spot market is a different beast in 2025. We have perhaps seen a downturn in fresh requirements building over the past week in the Atlantic; however, fixing activity remains quite brisk for June cargoes, although increased competition among owner and sub-letters has erased some of the gains seen over the past couple of months.

**East of Suez**

, several prompt positions have found employment though there remains an overhang of modern tonnage likely to reposition to the Atlantic, and a further decline in rates has been observed for June dates. Overall though, this past week has felt more like a drift downwards rather than a serious fall. Rates for multimonth periods covering the coming Winter remain higher than prompt spot levels, and with interest for such periods building there is some consensus of late-Summer or Winter uptick. Longer tenures through 2026 are largely flat though, and the following Winter is still proving tricky to price for owners and charterers. Whilst unconfirmed, there is growing noise around further large-scale Newbuilding orders for deliveries before the end of the decade. There have been very few new orders this year and prices have eased, but Shipyards sit comfortably with long forward orderbooks and this latest news may alleviate downward pressure on prices.
