## Market Commentary

The spot market remains firm in the West, with rates hitting year-to-date records and the idea of a six-figure fixture may no longer be a wild fantasy. There are requirements emerging including some relatively prompt loadings out of West Africa and US Gulf cargoes within the second half of December, giving some rate support at the end of the current fixing window. Additional volumes marketed by producers are struggling a bit to find homes in the absence of suitable modern tonnage, and much of the underlying ship length remains tied up on longer laden legs with portfolios yet to turn towards chartering out to capture the trading value. However, amidst all the bullish market talk there are clear reasons for caution; although the prompt market remains tight there are vessels redelivering in December and more availability is emerging, and we cannot expect the weather to help this year with mild conditions forecast in importing regions both East and West. There remains a large discount in the Pacific where rates for both 2-strokes and TFDE lag well behind the Atlantic, while the premium for 2-strokes over TFDEs is also widening, despite fewer requirements calling for larger ships East of Suez. The question of how long this firmer market lasts has to yet be answered, but for now it seems clear that the answer has been deferred at least another week.
