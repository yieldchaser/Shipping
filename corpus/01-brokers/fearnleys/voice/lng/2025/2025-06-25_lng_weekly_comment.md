## Market Commentary

Welcome news of a ceasefire in the latest Middle East conflict this week has helped to settle concerns over LNG supply leading to a softening of Asian LNG prices. However, the situation appears far from stable and continues to highlight the speed with which sentiment can change in LNG shipping. A strong premium remains for charters loading from the region though plenty of owners are seeing this as business worth pursuing, which may lead to slightly softer levels achieved for the latest round of enquiry vs. prompter requirements last week. Whilst there appears to be ample supply on paper, with all eyes on the Middle East, Pacific rates have firmed as offers are less forthcoming. Meantime, the Atlantic has been comparatively quiet thus far this week with Arb closed, further availabilities firming up and charterers with spot exposure holding off as the geopolitical situation shows signs of easing. Nonetheless the term market remains abuzz with activity for periods covering 1-3 years with reports of a couple of the ongoing requirements closing this week. Further deadlines loom and sentiment is strong for 2-stroke tonnage providing coverage through next year. On the LNG supply side, Cheniere announced it had taken FID on the final phase of its Corpus Christi phase 3 expansion which will see the producer’s volumes reach around 70mt by time of completion. Orders for newbuildings remain fairly muted this year but firmer sentiment on the prompt may encourage firmer discussions in that space.
