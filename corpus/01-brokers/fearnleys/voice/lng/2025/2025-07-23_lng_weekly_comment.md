## Market Commentary

Whilst spot requirements in both basins continue to provide a steady flow of chartering activity, there has been little change in market direction or sentiment and rates have continued their slow drift down. There is surplus supply in the Atlantic and with most cargoes pointing to Europe and no arbitrage opening up, there are few signs of material changes as we approach the start of the shoulder months. Perhaps it is too early to call whether this persists through the Northern Hemisphere Winter, although September laycans being discussed at only very slight premiums to August suggests the seasonal uptick is at the very least a little late in coming this year. The premium we have seen for requirements East of Suez remains in place but is under pressure. Multi-year requirements continue to be worked and rumours abound with regards fixed laycans, periods and rates; as details emerge, new benchmarks will guide players for the years ahead. On the newbuilding front, we may soon be able to point to the first “US-built” LNGC in a very long time: Hanwha Philly Shipyard secured an order from their parent company, although it remains to be seen how this will work in practise – the bulk of the construction will be in Korea and then finished in the US. Many questions remain, but it is certainly an interesting development.
