## Market Commentary

The strange year that was 2024 is behind us now and for some, that will be quite a relief. The relatively flat cargo market and excess freight were widely anticipated, but it has still been a bruising ride for those that have enjoyed the benefits of a strong trading environment in previous years; naturally, the flip side is true for the net charterers these past months, and they will hope for more of the same in 2025! That simple narrative ignores many of the quirks and challenges of the year that really kept everyone busy though, such as an open arbitrage in summer that caused an unseasonal spike, and a subsequent collapse that saw a strange winter trough. The regulatory environment was also a major topic for owners and charterers alike, as both EU and IMO regulations rippled through markets, economics and charterparties: the EU ETS scheme kicked in a year ago today, and also the IMO EEXI and CII schemes were having a tangible effect on certain vessels. It is likely that 2025 will also be a challenging year, with the trading environment again looking tough and the entry into force of the complex FuelEU regulations likely to cause some headaches, but periods like this can be beneficial to the longer-term health of a market. We will see growth following the commissioning of some new trains late in 2024, with congratulations to Cheniere and Venture Global, and we will likely see at least some of the following: a shift to index-linked deals rather than traditional fixed charter rates; more SnP activity; increased recycling of vintage tonnage; renewed focus on technical improvements to ship design and retrofitting; more project FIDs; the return of some speculative newbuild ordering; new entrants as both commodity traders or end-users; consolidation, etc. Add in to the mix the range of geopolitical and economic issues on the horizon, and we can be sure that 2025 will be just as interesting as was 2024. Wishing you all a very successful year ahead!
