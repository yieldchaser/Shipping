## Market Commentary

The festive season is in full swing in London, but that hasn’t led to a slowdown in the markets with fresh cargo sales and shipping requirements emerging almost daily. There has definitely been more for owners to celebrate in recent months and there is a little more optimism than there was for next year, but sentiment in the spot market has been more measured this week. Finding tonnage for prompt laycans is still challenging, but further out there are more ships emerging and some players are seemingly more open to subletting. Many of the key drivers for the current market strength remain in place including high production levels and FOB sales, so perhaps we are seeing the results of the fleet being worked harder. Despite this, rates in the Atlantic remain solidly in the six figures for laycans within December and although there is a steady softening into the new year, there have been very few fixtures concluded to give benchmarks for the second half of January. In contrast, the Pacific has been remarkably quiet on the prompt and rates have been moving sideways, with only distant requirements for second half January being considered. However, the Middle East continues to be a very hot market with tonnage being pulled into the region and Owners also considering ballasting to West Africa to lock in rates at the Atlantic premium. On the newbuilding front, activity remains strong, with several deals in progress.
