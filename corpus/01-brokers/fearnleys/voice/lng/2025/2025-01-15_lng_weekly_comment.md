## Market Commentary

Market participants could be forgiven for looking at the bigger picture on LNG shipping this week, with the spot market firmly in the doldrums and the widening supply-demand imbalance this year means there is broad consensus for the near-term outlook. The week saw plenty of spot activity but availabilities have been creeping upwards and putting pressure on levels in both basins, and current charter rates have dipped below the December averages. For the most part, multimonth charter ideas have found a floor for the timebeing, well in excess of spot rates and it seems this separation is likely to persist with both charterers and owners preferring to play spot rather than meeting the others’ ideas. On to that bigger picture, as the final week of the Biden administration plays out the seemingly ever-present threat of geopolitical influence on the LNG market is impossible to ignore. Whilst President Trump’s DoE takes shape, so too the optimism grows for future export projects pending FID and/or approval from the FERC to proceed. However, the nearer term impact of trade and sanctions policies are far less predictable, and yet will be wide-ranging in their implications. Europe further mulls its own sanctions of Russian LNG despite finding its current storage dwindling and the lack of an immediate replacement amidst sustained high prices. European refilling demand during the Summer will keep pricing into Europe relatively high, and whether Asia will compete for flexible volumes remains one key element of uncertainty which could provide the volatility needed to partially absorb excess shipping this year, but all this remains to be seen. Several more vintage units have been marketed for sale, with cash buyers the likely suitors in most cases. There have been no recent newbuilding orders.
