## Market Commentary

It’s bewildering how rapidly the world has changed in the past week, and while time will tell just how seismic the impacts of the Trump administration policies will be we can perhaps take some refuge in the relatively calm and composed LNG shipping market. The modest gains we’ve seen over the past month have eased, and rates for 2-strokes in both Atlantic and Pacific basins have slipped again this week. The natural flow of vessels redelivering from spot charters has refreshed the availability lists but although activity has slowed a little, it continues to tick along and this is giving some support to rates, ensuring a soft landing (for now). Levels are a little higher for periods through summer, although the bid/offer spread for periods through winter may limit the number of longer-term fixtures. Stepping out into the storm that has been unleashed through tariffs and other protectionist measures, how to predict what will happen in the LNG and LNG shipping markets? On the surface, none of this is good news: a global economic downturn will likely reduce LNG demand, and trade barriers between the largest LNG exporter and (almost) largest LNG importer may be particularly damaging given the long tonne-mile voyages involved. Yet very little US LNG has found its way to China recently anyway, with most Chinese off-take diverted into Europe, and the lower price environment could entice some price sensitive buyers back to market. Longer term, the mixed messaging behind the latest developments and the likely increased inflationary and tariff costs will not be helpful for project FIDs; however, the current US administration is committed to “energy dominance” and we still expect an under-supplied market by the end of the decade unless some of these projects move forwards. There has been one new order, reported to be at $263m for a 180kcbm vessel from Hyundai-Samho.
