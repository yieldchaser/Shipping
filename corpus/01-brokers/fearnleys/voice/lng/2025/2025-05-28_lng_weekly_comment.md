## Market Commentary

If last week could be characterised by rates drifting away slightly due to a lack of firm interest, the market has woken back up this week with nearly ten spot requirements being worked worldwide at the time of writing. The bulk of activity in the West is centred around July laycans, though there are still one or two end June requirements to be covered. Any relatively prompt cargoes would likely see a lot of competition in the Atlantic and thus lower rates, but further out the balance appears to shift and levels are looking like creeping back upwards. Flexibility for deliveries into Asia and thus through the remainder of Summer continue to command a slight premium. The Middle East and Pacific regions also burst back to life with post-WGC activity and rise are firming, although competition is likely to keep a lid on rates. The collective exposure to the spot market is unrivalled compared with recent years and despite some corners of the market expecting weaker tonne mile demand due to European LNG pricing in Winter, there are still few players willing to release tonnage beyond the shoulder months as of now. No sooner does one industry gathering end and another is on the horizon: Nor shipping returns next week and much like WGC, a healthy turnout is expected. For those joining us in Oslo, we wish you safe travels.
