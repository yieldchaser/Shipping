## Market Commentary

A rush of activity late last week saw the number of Atlantic spot fixtures reach double digits through the preceding seven days. Although many of these requirements were for intra-basin voyages with the usual cap on tonne-mile demand, there may be an outsized impact on the market due to some tonne-time affects; the Egypt and Jordan cargo sales drove much of the shipping demand and there are sometimes delays and uncertainty around discharge slots into the FSRUs. A large Argentina tender has the same effect of prompting lifters to seek additional tonnage in the Atlantic Basin, and flexibility around discharge dates remains key. Despite the large volume of activity for July laycans in the Atlantic, we see spot charter levels fairly flat week-on-week and rates have settled only slightly higher than what was seen at the beginning of this quarter. Whether this small upward trajectory will continue throughout the summer remains to be seen, especially with limited signs of life in the arbitrage trades East, but there are several enquiries for near-term multi-month charters which should provide further support in the coming months. The bid-offer remains wide on longer term periods, but there are now a number of open enquiries across a range of charter lengths so it will be interesting to see how rates converge. Away from the day-to-day activities on the LNG shipping market, there are some promising noises from the US around a softening in tone regarding US-built LNGCs lifting US volume. This is an entirely practical and pragmatic development that many have expected, so although it may not be a surprise it is nonetheless a welcome step in removing uncertainty for projects, Owners and Charterers alike.
