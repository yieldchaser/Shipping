## Market Commentary

Start of 2024 is unfolding as expected and with the fixing window moving into February, we see the LNG commodity market itself is weakening. The Pacific basin was active this week, with several requirements throughout February; however, there is abundant vessel availability so charter rates continue to soften. Fixing activity in the Atlantic basin remains quiet, marked by a lack of firm requirements and there is considerable uncertainty regarding the market's direction in the weeks ahead. There are ongoing issues around scheduling due to weather conditions causing delays and missed laycans, although the flurry of activity around the new year appears to have resolved the most urgent situations. Furthermore, the situation in the Red Sea remains a challenge for the shipping industry. Many charterers have chosen to use the Cape route to mitigate the risk of attacks. Northern Europe is experiencing very low temperatures, although with current storage levels at 84% it's unlikely to have a major impact in the short-term. On the project side, Energos has purchased two 2021-built 174,000m3 FSRUs from Dynagas, and Nakilat is engaging in fleet renewal and expansion with an order for two 174,000m³ LNG carriers in Korea.
