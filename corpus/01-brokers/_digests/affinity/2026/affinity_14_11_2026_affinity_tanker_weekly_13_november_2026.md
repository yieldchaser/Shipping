Crude Tanker Comments  
It was another bruising week for VLCCs. A late pickup in activity could not turn the 
tide, with ample tonnage and weaker Suezmax rates keeping rates under pressure. 
Monday’s Brazil-East voyage went on subs at WS 393, marking a decline of 179 points, 
after drawing at least 12 offers. Fujairah-East slipped from WS 360 on Wednesday to 
WS 310 on Thursday and WS 300 on Friday. WAFR-East has reached WS 280 a day after 
Thursday’s WS 290, itself down 210 points from previously done. 
Yanbu-East is reported at WS 385, which is broadly as expected. With crude cheaper 
further forward and freight falling, charterers and buyers had little reason to rush, 
helping explain the recent lull. Still, a floor looks increasingly close. Atlantic Suezmax 
rates are starting to settle, VLCC owners are digging their heels in, and the mystery list 
is growing. Reports of December and January crude tenders could add fresh activity if 
they are confirmed. There are still plenty of ships to absorb, so rates may have a little 
further to slide in the short term. Even so, the market looks likely to find its footing 
sooner rather than later. Any firming will take longer, but the bottom should not be far 
away.
The Suezmax market had another difficult week, with rates continuing to correct early 
on as growing lists and limited enquiry kept charterers in the driving seat. TD20 fell 
from around WS 390 on Monday to the low WS 300s by Wednesday, with a Brazil-East 
fixture at WS 295 setting another lower benchmark. CPC also corrected downwards, 
falling from around WS 625 to WS 425 by Friday, though it continued to pay a premium 
over the Atlantic. 
Sentiment began to improve towards the second half of the week as enquiry picked up 
across WAFR and the Americas. Owners started showing more resistance to declines, 
and rates appeared to find a floor, although some cheaper-than-expected fixtures 
were still done. Tonnage remains balanced, and an increase in activity will be needed 
before any significant recovery. For now, rates look set to remain around current 
levels, with some potential upside if the pickup in enquiry continues.
Mediterranean Aframaxes endured a soft, nervous week. Monday opened with 
growing wariness as activity dried up and tonnage built, pointing to another sharp 
drop. Although the previous week had closed with decent levels of fixing, it was not 
enough to lift sentiment, and charterers were expected to press for further 
corrections. Midweek brought a considerable pick-up in activity, improving sentiment 
slightly and hinting at a floor, but lists remained long. Still, even with neighbouring 
markets on the decline, the newfound business has left room for optimism. TD19 ends 
the week at WS 270.
Like their companions, North Sea Aframaxes were also under pressure. Monday found 
the market quiet and in need of a fresh test, though the sharp correction in 
surrounding markets seemed unavoidable. By Tuesday, the list remained long and 
activity scarce, with local TCEs outperforming ballast-out alternatives and further 
corrections expected. TD7 fell further on Tuesday and shed another 25 WS points on 
Wednesday. With a lengthy list, a weak US market, and ample tonnage, a floor is not 
yet in sight. TD7 sits at WS 250 at close on Friday.
Product Tanker Comments
The AG LR2 segment has been massively supported by one particular charterer, but – 
luckily for the market – the demand from this one source has been considerable. 
There have been six LR2 agreements made chartering in from the market as well as at 
least two own programme vessels all performing STS loading this week. This is a lot of 
volume, but with a decent enough ship count available, chartering was ably managed 
and freight levels were brought down to USD 8.5 Mn for UKCont via Bab el-Mandeb, 
WS 325 for 75,000 T naphtha for STS Oman-East, and WS 340 for 90,000 T Sikka-EAFR. 
Vessels seem to be repopulating into the next fixing window, so next week will make 
clear what sort of appetite this one charterer might have, as this conduit of oil has a 
substantial influence on overall freight market health. The Red Sea is very quiet 
indeed for LR2s, and with a much weaker X-Med Afra market, fortunes in relation to 
Yanbu loading will be much reduced as and when there is a fresh test. 
It has been a disjointed week on the LR1s, to say the least, with national holiday in 
Singapore on Monday coupled with the start of Bahri week. Volumes are going 
through a sustained quiet period on the whole, so more tonnage is creeping into the 
window and weakening market sentiment. Eastward naphtha runs have softened to 
WS 375, about WS 100 points below last week’s levels. Westbound business has not 
been thoroughly tested, but last done publicly was agreed at USD 7 Mn via Bab-el-
Mandeb; however, current levels likely tilt more towards USD 6.5 Mn for the same. 
There is some work to be done to thin out tonnage supply and flip sentiment in a 
more positive direction as has been seen with the LR2s. Outside of the standard East-
West movements, there has been little else to fix this week, but next week will bring a 
fresh start. 
The Singapore MR CPP market had a disappointing week, with the short-haul cargoes 
drip-fed into the market and rates stepping down with each new fixture. Weakness in 
North Asia and the Middle East, which had helped to keep Singapore elevated over 
the previous three to four weeks, fed through, and TC7 was reportedly heard at WS 
490 on subjects. Further ballasters ex-ECI and East Africa continue to add to tonnage 
supply, which does little to support freight. With cargo volumes still low, additional 
corrections are projected for the days ahead.
AG MRs have also had a slow week, with a real lack of demand. The softening of LRs 
and segments in Asia have contributed to a softer feel. TC12 last done is WS 500, but 
with TC1 and TC5 having softened, there is the expectation that TC12 will have to 
correct; however, one charterer tested this theory, and owners refused to budge, 
instead ballasting to Singapore. TC17 has held steady at WS 600, but volumes are low. 
If owners continue to head to Asia, WCI/AG tonnage could tighten, supporting rates. 
But the overall feeling is that MR owners will do well to hold onto present levels.
Mediterranean MRs and Handysizes had a mundane week. After suffering a 50-point 
drop over the weekend, TC6 began at WS 370 and, after spending the entire week in 
the WS 360s, ends it in the same place. Little change is expected, and sentiment going 
into the next week is stable. Northwest Europe, on the other hand, played host to 
some rate hikes. TC2 fell every day last week, but it has managed to come back, rising 
by almost 20 points to WS 205. Come Monday, further rises are expected.
