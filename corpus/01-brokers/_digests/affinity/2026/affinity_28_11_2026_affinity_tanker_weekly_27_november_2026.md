Crude Tanker Comments  
The VLCC market has seen a gradual improvement in sentiment this week, although 
broadly balanced fundamentals have kept gains measured. In the Atlantic, momentum 
has eased as Suezmax rates have steadied and VLCC tonnage has remained reasonably 
well-supplied. A Brazil/East market quote went on subs yesterday at WS 270 after 
reportedly attracting nine offers, highlighting ample availability against steady 
demand. Although below expectations, the result is less surprising given the wider 
balance; the previous WS 320 fixture required heating coils and was therefore not 
representative.
With charterers retaining the benefit of optionality, scope for further Atlantic firming 
looks limited for now. East of Suez has shown a firmer tone, although reported activity 
has also remained limited and details contentious. Sentiment has edged in owners’ 
favour, but ample tonnage continues to temper gains. Further improvement depends 
on sustained cargo enquiry, yet charterers have so far kept much of their business off-
market, helping to contain rate momentum. There is little reason to expect a change in 
that approach. Overall, the week closes with a steady Atlantic outlook and greater 
scope for gradual firming in the East, rather than a sharp market-wide rise.
On the Suezmaxes, sentiment was bullish in the West after an active end to last week. 
Tonnage supply also was rather balanced but thinned as the week progressed. WAFR 
rates have moved from WS 360 up to WS 380 with several short/undesirable cargoes 
helping boost owners’ sentiment as the lack of interest elevated the level of rates into 
offers for these cargoes. CPC rates however have remained flat at WS 450 as some 
under-the-radar fixing plus owners’ desire to fix off dates has kept rates sideways.
Guyana levels have been done at higher levels for shorter flats, but levels remain 
similar to WAFR at WS 380 at the time of writing. The USG was the main driver for 
rates moving up but the Thanksgiving holiday put a pause on the market with USG/TA 
levels roughly at WS 320. Meanwhile, East of Suez bounced upwards off the back of a 
stronger West market with Fujairah/East paying WS 377.5
It has been an interesting week for the Aframax markets. Last week’s surge in the USG 
was the catalyst for rallies across the board, but sentiment has softened throughout 
the course of the week, and rates with it, despite the three-day week in the States. As 
a result, TD25 shed 115 points between close last Friday and Wednesday ahead of the 
Thanksgiving long weekend, although the market remained busy.
Regardless of the drop across the pond, rates in the European markets held largely 
steady, before a flurry of activity on Thursday gave owners the impetus to push TD19 
to WS 397.5 levels, their highest since the beginning of the month. The front end of 
the list in the Med is now very tight, but the natural fixing window has moved forward, 
and charterers will be aiming to maintain last done levels. TD19 closes the week at WS 
400.
The North Sea played catch-up at the start of the week, with TD7 rising by around 35 
points since last Friday’s close. However, it has been a largely quiet week, and owners 
will be leaning on the strength in surrounding markets to maintain current levels, 
which are now at WS 340 as we end the week.
Product Tanker Comments
Based on the demand we had seen for most of this week, the LR2s had held their own 
very well. Sentiment wasn’t the strongest off the back of last week and we’d assume 
that these volumes would not help freight levels by giving charterers the opportunity 
to offer some more opportunistic bids to owners. What we did see instead was 
owners collectively rejecting these lower bids and subsequently they were able to 
negotiate last done levels at the time. These rates did go on to firm late in the week. 
Naphtha East runs were initially flat at WS 365 before jumping to WS 400 at the close 
of play. It is a similar story for westbound business; USD 8.65 Mn had been flatly 
agreed basis UKC via Suez, before rising to USD 8.85 Mn. We’ve seen routing via COGH 
agreed for the first time in a while at USD 9.125 Mn. If this demand persists then there 
is no reason why owners won’t maintain momentum, but should we see demand start 
to diminish, we expect tonnage to replenish at a faster rate than now. We also need 
to be conscious that both LR sizes are firming whilst MRs haven’t really gone 
anywhere, so we could see cargoes being downsized if pricing becomes better suited.
It has been a very strong week for the LR1s. Much like the LR2s, sentiment was not 
much in favour of a firming market, but tonnage counts on Monday quickly notified 
the market that we’re in a much tighter position than thought. Owners did a good job 
of securing the biggest gains possible at the time, with +50 points on last done being 
agreed on naphtha East runs at WS 350 followed by another +25 points and another 
+50 points; WS 425 is the latest agreement. Several westbound rumours have been in 
the market earlier in the week, but rates have moved well past those rumours; we 
could expect current levels to be around USD 6.5 Mn basis UKC via Suez. As on the 
LR2s, if LR1 rates continue to firm and leave behind the MRs, then we could start to 
see demand broken down into MR stems if pricing works better this way.
For NWE MRs, it has been a strong week, with Monday and Tuesday seeing 16 vessels 
on subs, significantly tightening tonnage availability and providing support to freight 
levels. Fresh cargoes and vessels being taken off-market have continued throughout 
the remainder of the week, with rates having bottomed out and TC2 now freighted at 
WS 170. More cargoes remain outstanding today and will carry over into next week, 
which will help maintain momentum into Monday. Uncertain weather conditions 
coming in will be affecting itineraries and laycans, further tightening tonnage 
availability and support rates ticking back up further if fresh cargo activity continues.
In the Med, MR owners may reflect and feel the opportunity to push was there given 
how limited tonnage has been. However, charterers have managed to drip feed stems 
well and dampen sentiment in an active market. As a result, both sides have happily 
repeated last done levels for UKC and Med (WS 275 and WS2 95, respectively), with 
some variance on TA, which has floated between WS 190 and WS 200. Whilst WS 260 
and WS 280 have been seen today for UKC and Med on an ex-DD vessel, sentiment 
remains steady heading into next week: a rise in TC14 could see a climb in ballasters 
and hinder resupply in the Med further, though with many descending on London for 
Christmas week, charterers have a good chance again of cloaking the bulk of activity.
For Handy owners, it has been bleak. Hopes of a busier end-month window were 
quickly dashed, and tonnage has built again. As a result, there have been sharp 
corrections, with 30KT x WS 320 now on subs, down 80 points from Monday’s 
opening. The outlook for next week remains bearish; we will return on Monday with a 
longer list for the 6-10 window; there will be a lot of wood to chop before owners can 
hope to establish the bottom.
