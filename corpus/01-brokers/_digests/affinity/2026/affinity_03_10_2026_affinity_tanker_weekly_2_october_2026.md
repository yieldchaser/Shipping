Crude Tanker Comments  
The VLCC market ends the week on a high with a tight list, growing demand for prompt 
crude, and firming East of Suez. The rapid strengthening in the Atlantic Suezmax 
market has been a key contributor, with a WAFR-UKC Suezmax on subs at four figures. 
In the Atlantic, a WAFR-East voyage went on subs at WS 600 today, up 80 points. East 
of Suez activity has also built steadily, with cargoes emerging regularly and charterers 
under increasing pressure to cover. This has yet to translate into the record-breaking 
levels seen recently, with Fujairah-East slowly progressing to the high WS 700s, as such 
owners have become increasingly wary. With many willing to wait for next week, little 
is likely to surface today, with a stronger flow expected into next week. 
Charterers secured relatively stable levels out of Yanbu despite the risks; however, 
despite a longer list, sentiment is bullish for next week. All in all, VLCC sentiment is 
firm at the end of the week due to strengthening Atlantic Suezmax rates, a tightening 
prompt VL list, and renewed East of Suez activity. While rates have yet to return to 
recent record levels, owners are increasingly holding back. With crude supply 
recovering and routing constraints still in place,  the expected activity next week could 
quickly eliminate available tonnage, possibly bringing rates back to record levels.
On this day in 1836, Charles Darwin returned to England after a five-year trip aboard 
HMS Beagle, with his observations onboard leading to the groundbreaking discovery 
of evolution. It has also been a historic week for Suezmaxes, with rates absolutely 
soaring in the Atlantic. Enquiry was very active and tonnage remained tight even with 
dates working more forwards than usual. WAFR rates started the week at WS 440 but 
have now reached WS 1,000 for UKCM and WS 900 for East. Guyana rates have seen 
similarly explosive gains, also firming from WS 440 to a last done of WS 1,000. 
The USG has also been busy, with rates for TA firming to WS 395, but volume for this 
route has been lacking, with Aframaxes seemingly preferred. Most volume comes 
from tenders for USG-East, with rates having hit USD 26.5 Mn on an ex-DD ship. CPC 
rates have climbed by 400 points to WS 900, but this now feels undervalued compared 
to the rest of the Atlantic. With the list comprised mostly of two bullish owners, rates 
look set for massive gains. East of Suez remains the black sheep of the Suezmax 
market, with rates keeping sideways at WS 725 for East and little to report in the open 
market. Still, with ships increasingly looking to ballast West and chase the earnings 
there, rates in the East will have to rise to remain attractive to owners.
The Mediterranean Aframax market opened this week steady after a quieter close to 
the last one, with charterers holding back to let the list breathe and US developments 
expected to keep rates supported. Levels remained date- and voyage-dependent, with 
short runs ex-Libya holding firm premiums. TD19 was well-established at WS 630, 
repeated several times. By Thursday, restraint had allowed the list to replenish, and 
with a relatively healthy list, charterers had the opportunity to test lower levels. But 
USG business makes sentiment here firm, and TD19 ends the week at WS 645.
The North Sea Aframax market began the week firm after a Friday with nothing 
concluded in the open market, as recent TD25 dynamics pointed to upside potential. 
Charterers held off, hoping that some available tonnage would leave the area, while 
rates awaited a fresh test. A continued lack of activity allowed the position list to 
replenish slowly, and the US Gulf quietened down. Still, a recovery in USG activity late 
in the week makes ballasting look likely. TD7 sits at WS 410 on Friday.
Product Tanker Comments
Supply of suitable AG LR2 tonnage remained extremely tight at the start of the week, 
and rates have continued to build on last week's momentum, with enquiry carrying 
over into this week. As a result, owners’ control over the market has become even 
more pronounced, with rates strengthening further. WS 485 is now on subjects for 
75,000 of naphtha from STS Oman to Japan, equating to approximately USD 7.6 Mn 
and representing a 15-point increase on the last done. Westbound deliveries have 
strengthened further, with USD 10 Mn confirmed for Sikka-West Africa via Cape of 
Good Hope, while USD 11 Mn is also on subjects for Sikka-UKC via Suez. Demand 
appears quieter on the surface, but with most cargoes now being covered off-market, 
silence can be deceptive. Considering how difficult it is to secure firm tonnage within 
the required loading window, it is not surprising to see so much product reappearing 
in the MR sector.
The Far East market, which has been a key driver for LR2 demand over the last two 
weeks, may well take a breather. This is due not only to Golden Week next week but 
also to the recent announcement from Beijing regarding restrictions on Chinese 
product exports, as there is a growing view that rebuilding domestic stockpiles should 
become a priority again. If these restrictions remain in place over the medium to long 
term, more ships could ballast towards WCI and Oman, which may provide some relief 
for charterers and introduce greater competition into the market. It remains too early 
to draw firm conclusions, and today's outlook remains firmly positive. However, this is 
certainly a development worth monitoring closely into next week and beyond.
It was also another positive week for AG LR1s, with continued firming taking place. On 
the surface, it has not been the busiest week – not helped by the various rumours 
suggesting additional fixtures at higher levels – but this has not detracted from the 
already firm sentiment. 
The list remains tight, and pressure is being maintained by additional vessels being 
sniped off quietly. With each public fixture, rates take noticeable steps on freight. 
There are several plausible rumours of WS 550 being agreed for a naphtha run 
heading East, and others suggest that WS 600 has been agreed, though this is 
unconfirmed. Westbound business has been largely operating in the shadows this 
week, but a rate of USD 8 Mn could be suggested for WCI or STS Oman-UKC via Bab-
el-Mandeb. Short-haul and Red Sea business have been limited this week, so fresh 
testing is needed all round.
In terms of MRs, Southeast Asia maintained its bullish trajectory, with Singapore-
Australia advancing to WS 525 before being pushed to WS 570. Persistent terminal 
congestion and uncertain itineraries have continued to disrupt vessel schedules, and 
sustained strength across the Middle East and WCI markets has given owners 
compelling alternatives and reinforced their resistance to discounting. They retain the 
upper hand, though the market appears to be edging towards its ceiling.
It was a highly successful week for European MRs and Handysizes, especially when 
considering their recent relative misfortune when compared with other regions and 
sizebands. TC2 and TC6 leapt by dozens of points on the week, with the former rising 
from WS 175 last Friday to WS 210 this one, and the latter jumping from WS 260 to 
WS 345 in the same period. Sentiment here is firm going into next week.
