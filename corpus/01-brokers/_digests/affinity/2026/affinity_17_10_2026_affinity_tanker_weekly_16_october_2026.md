Crude Tanker Comments  
VLCC sentiment has softened over the course of the week. Owners began broadly 
positive, with several records only recently having been broken. But a lack of fresh 
enquiry, a more balanced tonnage list, and a series of lower fixtures have pulled 
expectations down. The Atlantic has shown the clearest downward pressure. A few 
Brazil-East market quotes emerged early in the week: the first went on subs at WS 718, 
seven points below last done, and the more recent one went on subs at WS 704. This 
route often attracts competitive offers, but the pace of the decline suggests that it 
would likely be assessed even lower to begin next week. A USG-East voyage went on 
subs at USD 71 Mn before failing, around USD 10 Mn below the previous fixture.
East of Suez has also eased amid sparse firm activity, although persistent Middle East 
transit risks importing countries’ general preference for AG crude continue to protect 
rates. An inside AG-East voyage reportedly went on subs at WS 1,423, around 150 
points above the Baltic assessment last night, though the rate has not been confirmed. 
The principal dampening factor remains the lack of activity. Tonnage availability has 
increased, but not to the point of oversupply. Suezmax rates also remain exceptionally 
high, making VLCCs comparatively attractive, while conflict in the Middle East 
continues to keep rates well elevated. Direction now hinges on the next meaningful 
run of enquiry: renewed VLCC activity could quickly steady rates, while a sustained 
quiet spell would leave owners increasingly exposed to further downward corrections. 
On this day back in 1923, brothers Walter and Roy Disney founded their legendary 
animation studio in Hollywood. Snow White is one of their more famous early films, 
and the titular character famously sings the song "Someday My Prince Will Come". 
Shipowners might possibly feel like singing a version called "Someday my cargo will 
come" as charterers have starved the market all week in the Atlantic. Down in West 
Africa, rates have been taken down to WS 925 from WS 1,000 on a named voyage, 
with another cargo quoting and then WDWF twice over. Rates look set to correct 
further downwards next week and, given how high rates had gotten, perhaps this all 
part of "Circle of Life" for the tanker market as Mufasa from the Lion King would say. 
Guyana saw rates repeat at WS 1,000 for UKCM on Monday, but the charterer sold the 
barrels FOB and the new charterer took another ship for marginally less, but again the 
market has been so quiet that rates will surely fall from its great heights like many a 
Disney villain has done. CPC by contrast was the one active area of the market and 
rates have risen further, with WS 1,150 confirmed on subs and even rumours of WS 
1,200 on subs! The list of tonnage remains tight going into next week, but given how 
soft the Atlantic is, it seems rates are now starting to plateau here. East of Suez again 
has been very slow going, with ships increasingly beginning to give up on the Fujairah 
market and ballast West to find employment. 
Med Aframax owners opened the week aiming to repeat last done levels on natural 
dates, pushing wherever possible, and growing interest in larger sizes threatened to 
tip the balance. Things were quiet from midweek onwards and given the long tonnage 
list and weakening US market, a correction was in store. TD19 ends the week at WS 
715, and further drops are expected. The North Sea began the week quieter, though 
the US market was gaining traction again and repeating last done rates looked difficult; 
rates were very voyage-dependent. The market remained quiet for the rest of the 
week, and after the reset in the USG and the Med, one is expected here, too. TD7 ends 
the week at WS 580.
Product Tanker Comments
The AG LR2 market has been a “tale of two halves” over the last five days. A few ships 
from last week have been fixed away quietly, with a couple having ballasted in from 
the Med as the Red Sea has taken a bit of a breather and the West loading market 
remains turned off on the LR2 segment. The past few days’ uncharacteristic pause in 
the X-Med Afra market has led to more clean owners turning their heads in the 
direction of Oman or India to get the best monies. Rates have largely gone 
unreported, but on Tuesday, a whopping 75KT x WS 725 for a WCI-Japan naphtha run 
was reported on subs. This is a record, and eyes have been on the area in the hopes 
that other deals come out the woodwork to support this very significant number and 
help rally the market. 
This did not happen; charterers believe that the pricing offered by LR2 vessels is just 
not viable for the trade. This segment, for this week at least, has been priced out, and 
optimising on MRs has been much more the way to go. The position list will not be 
oversupplied for many weeks to come, so one cannot discount the possibility of a rally 
next week should stems emerge into the marketplace, but what is equally plausible is 
very quiet cherry-picking at discounted rates as and when charterers start to move 
barrels again.
It was a bit of an odd week for AG LR1s in reality; the market has been firm for some 
weeks now, but volumes have eased off this week. There was a hike in Westbound 
rates early on, but all the excitement in the market seemed to fizzle out thereafter, 
with any previous momentum subsiding. This is emphasised by the fact that following 
the record TC1 number, the LR1s did not manage to shift into gear and follow suit. TC5 
has reportedly been paid at WS 550 several times, and there are rumours of WS 600 
and even WS 700 – both unsubstantiated. 
A strong Westbound rate had been paid early this week – USD 11.9 Mn basis Black Sea 
discharge via Suez – so UKC rates can be placed around USD 12.25 Mn via Suez. Whilst 
surface activity has been limited this week, charterers have likely been conducting 
conversations behind closed doors with a view of keeping a lid on the market as best 
they can, so perhaps some of these deals will spill into market knowledge next week. 
Ultimately, the LR1s are primed to firm based on fundamentals, so stay tuned…
It has been a mixed week for the MRs in NWE. Excitement was building following a 
very busy end to last week, where rates firmed on the back of paper levels continuing 
to trade higher and enough activity to clear a steady portion of the tonnage list. Levels 
began the week steady, with owners' sentiment firmly driving the market despite the 
underlying fundamentals pointing towards what felt like a softer tone.
As the week progressed and activity remained stagnant, levels could only hold for so 
long. Short-haul stems looked towards Handies as the more efficient option, while a 
lack of TC2 stems, with the arb closed, means we are yet to see a fresh test, with the 
next fixture expected around WS 195. Six ballasters have left NWE this week, with 
more expected to depart over the weekend as owners look to position in the stronger 
USG market, where levels are WS 400 and paper continues to support owners' 
decision-making.
