Desk Talk 
 
Crude export volumes through the Strait of Hormuz have recovered to near pre -crisis levels, supported 
by using shuttle tankers and ship -to-ship (STS) transfers, with Aramco reportedly considering this 
approach as a longer -term solution. Under the revised arrangement, responsibility shifts from buyers, 
who traditionally secured tankers for loading at Saudi ports, t o exporters, who assume the risk of 
transporting crude through Hormuz to the Gulf of Oman. Buyers then take delivery and arrange onward 
transportation using a separate vessel. This shift suggests a greater vessel requirement per barrel 
transported: b efore the crisis, moving 1 mb/d of crude from the Middle East to the Far East was 
estimated to require an average of 24 dedicated VLCCs , while u nder the shuttle model, this 
requirement increases to 25.5 VLCCs, equivalent to a 6% rise. 
 
On the S&P tanker side, as crude carrier tonnage remains historically high in demand, we are now also 
noticing an increased spike in interest in product tankers, namely LRs and MRs , albeit the possible US 
diesel export ban . China has also reportedly resumed its October product exports  after a br ief pause 
during its Golden Week, further supporting the segment.  More specifically, Buyers are actively looki ng 
mainly for non-eco product tankers of around 15 year s of age that can give prompt delivery. Asset 
prices continue to climb across all tanker segments all the way up-to VLCC. 
 
On the dry side, we are noticing activity on modern eco Kamsarmaxes, especially Japanese-built units, 
as well as a focus on Capesizes, as earnings continue to  hold firm. In the geared space, Handysize  
vessels have attracted notable interest this week, with non-eco 15-year-old tonnage appearing to be 
Buyers’ main focus.
