Clarkson Hellas Ltd. 
 
Desk Talk 
In the tanker space, geopolitical risk remains high after seven tankers were reportedly hit in Hormuz. Talk 
of lifting US sanctions is unlikely to offer much relief, since the 170 sanctioned VLCCs average 22 years 
of age and only 25 hold valid class certif icates. On the freight side, VLCC spot earnings corrected by 
almost 25% this week to $541,000/day for eco-scrubber units. The weakness started in the Atlantic, where 
US crude bookings to Asia for November are running at roughly half of last month's volume.  Forward 
rates have nonetheless held firm, with 2027 US Gulf–China FFAs at $213,000/day, and the market remains 
tight. Product tankers continue to strengthen as two-thirds of the LR2 fleet has switched into dirty trades, 
further tightening clean supply. 
 
On the wet S&P front, prices are climbing week on week, and sellers' ideas keep shifting as owners weigh 
selling against trading their ships. Despite these elevated levels, several players are actively negotiating, 
a clear sign of confidence that current earnings justify today's prices. The firm sentiment has carried over 
to MR2s and LR1s, with increased activity on both dirty and clean units. Prompt tonnage continues to 
command significant premiums, with VLCC resale at $245m, up from $145m at mid-year, and a 10-year-
old MR at $44m, above its 2024 peak. 
 
The dry bulk market has had a volatile week. Capesize spot rates fell 25% last week but rebounded 3.6% 
today to $30,500/day on stronger Australia –China iron ore freight. December FFAs are trading at 
$40,300/day, some 32% above spot, signaling expectations of a year-end recovery. This is supported by 
a 17% rebound in global iron ore exports last week, led by Brazil. More broadly, fundamentals remain 
positive, underpinned by longer -haul iron ore and bauxite trades from Guinea's Simandou project,  
resilient coal demand and a limited orderbook. Weaker global growth and grain demand remain the 
key risks. 
 
In the dry S&P market, vintage Supramaxes and Panamaxes continue to attract increased interest, 
particularly from Far Eastern buyers and for units able to offer prompt delivery in the region. Capesize 
demand has eased momentarily on the back of softer short -term rates, though asset prices remain 
stable, supported by positive long-term expectations.
