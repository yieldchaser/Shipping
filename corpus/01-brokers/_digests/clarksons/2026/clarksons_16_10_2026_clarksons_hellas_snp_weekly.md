Desk Talk 
 
This week, S&P activity has maintained the same healthy momentum across all segments  which has 
been enduring for the past months. On dry, rates have been softer, showcasing a 43% lower m/m end 
of the week for the Capes at 29,500/day, a decline which is attributed to the  reduced Chinese steel 
import demand, as mills are faced with thin margins and iron ore port inventories remain elevated. At 
the same time, data suggests congestion at Chinese discharge ports has been reduced, resulting in 
greater supply of tonnage. Despite the softening of the dry market , buying appetite continues: Cape 
and Mini-Cape present high activity sectors with attention being concentrated mainly on 10 -15-year-
old units, as well as on modern eco M/E vessels, with prompt delivery being favored. In the non-geared 
segment, we are seeing notable interest in Ultramax tonnage, particularly modern Japanese -built 
vessels, as well as non-eco Handysize and Supramax units. 
 
In the tanker space, there has been considerable discussion surrounding the possibility that US may lift 
certain sanctions  in a n effort t o counteract the much-increased price of tanker freight . It is still 
ambiguous how this could be effectuated and to what degree the sanctioned fleet would be allowed 
to re-enter the mainstream market. At the moment , an estimated 168 of the 934 VLCCs in the global 
fleet are subject to US OFAC sanctions, the majority of which are linked to the illicit transportation of 
Iranian oil. Given the ongoing attacks in the Strait, a lifting of these sanctions appears more unlikely than 
that of vessels sanctioned for ties with Russian exports, amid the recent US-Russia oil deal reports. For 
another consecutive week, we are monitoring heigh tened activity on all tanker segments, with clean-
product trading LR and MR tonnage being at the forefront of attention.
