Week 43 | Tuesday 27th October 2026Market Insight 
By Vasilis Fousekis, Research Analyst
Feeder containerships have emerged among the strongest 
segments of the container market in 2026, supported by 
expanding intra-Asian trade, disruption in the Middle East and 
persistent port congestion. The 6-12 month time-charter rate 
for a 1,700 TEU geared feeder has risen to around 
$33,750/day, up by 19% y-o-y and more than double the 2023 
level, while a 1,000 TEU unit now earns approximately 
$18,750/day, 10% higher than a year earlier. Charterers are 
fixing sub-3,000 TEU tonnage for delivery as far forward as the 
third quarter of 2027, with average fixture periods extending to 
around 24 months. The upside extends even to older tonnage: a 
25-year-old 2,556 TEU feeder was recently fixed at 
$62,500/day for 12-14 months.
Behind this strength lies a restructuring of Asian trade flows. 
Chinese shipments sent directly to the US fell by 22% in the 12 
months after the 2025 tariffs, while Chinese exporters 
redirected 52% of these volumes to other Asian countries. 
Product components are now assembled in Vietnam, Malaysia 
and Indonesia before onward shipment, adding port calls that 
favour smaller tonnage. Intra-Asian trade is projected to reach 
72m TEU in 2026, up 5.4% y-o-y, while Shanghai-SE Asia spot 
rates averaged around $1,154/TEU in October, about two and a 
half times their 2025 average.
In the Middle East, the picture is more fragmented. Regional 
container trade is down 22% year-to-date and independent 
feeder operators have reduced their exposure to the region, 
while state-backed operators are filling part of the gap. UAE-
based AD Ports’ Global Feeder Services has expanded in the 
Middle East & Indian Subcontinent and Iraq has revived a 
national shipping service in the Gulf for the first time in more 
than half a century, deploying a 900 TEU feeder between Jebel 
Ali and Umm Qasr. Although transits through Hormuz seemed 
to recover in September, conditions have since deteriorated, 
with attacks on containerships continuing.
Moreover, congestion at Asian containership terminals persists. 
Average containership waiting times at Chinese ports reached 
an estimated 23 hours in September, 45% above May levels, 
after a summer of typhoon closures, while Typhoon Champi 
threatened renewed restrictions around Ningbo and Shanghai 
in late October, just as congestion in East China had begun to 
ease. In a segment with little spare tonnage, each day of delay 
effectively removes capacity from the market, disrupting 
operators while supporting owners’ earnings.
As major liner operators gradually resume Red Sea transits, the 
shift is likely to weigh on the feeder market over time. More 
than half of Cape-diverted sailings have returned to the Suez 
route, although Gulf of Aden traffic remains at around a third of 
normal levels. The restoration of direct mainline calls at ports 
such as Jeddah could render some relay services established 
during the Cape diversion redundant, while released mid-sized 
tonnage may cascade into regional trades. 
On the supply side, the feeder segment appears better 
positioned than larger sizes. Its orderbook stands at around 
1.05m TEU across 635 vessels, equivalent to almost one fifth 
of the fleet, against more than 40% for the containership fleet 
overall. Delivery timing is less favourable, however, with 42% of 
feeder orderbook capacity scheduled for 2027 and a further 
34% for 2028, mostly in the 1,000-1,999 TEU range. 
Demolition, meanwhile, remains negligible, even though 30% of 
feeder capacity is aged 20 years or more.
Looking ahead, strong intra-Asian demand and congestion 
should continue to support feeder earnings in the near term. 
However, accelerating deliveries in 2027, with feeder fleet 
growth projected at around 6%, together with Suez 
normalization, could gradually exert downward pressure on 
rates. The key question is whether the reshaping of Asian 
supply chains generates durable regional cargo demand or 
merely shifts volumes between routes. Ultimately, the outlook 
for feeders will hinge on whether intra-Asian growth can 
absorb the wave of tonnage arriving from 2027, with the level 
of geopolitical risk in the Middle East, remaining a key factor.

The crude carrier market retreated overall last week, with the 
larger segments suffering losses while the Aframax segment 
proved more resilient. The BDTI averaged 7,294 last week, 
down by 7% on a weekly basis.
The VLCC market extended its correction, albeit still at historical 
highs. Limited cargo flows across most regions and growing 
vessel availability strengthened charterers' leverage, resulting 
in further freight declines. In West Africa, insufficient new 
business kept rates under pressure, while the US Gulf was 
similarly affected by sluggish enquiry and declining Brazilian 
fixing benchmarks. In Middle East, increased fixing activity 
around Fujairah after midweek, coupled with owners' growing 
reluctance to accept lower rates, improved sentiment. 
Nevertheless, any sustained recovery will depend on a pickup in 
cargo enquiry adequate to absorb the growing tonnage supply. 
VLCC TCE stood at $761,352/day, down by 14% w-o-w.
The Suezmax market came under pressure across most trading 
regions, as subdued cargo enquiry allowed tonnage lists to 
lengthen, weakening owners' bargaining position. Freight rates 
declined in West Africa, Atlantic and the Mediterranean, while 
limited demand East of Suez encouraged more owners to 
reposition vessels westwards. The Black Sea was a pocket of 
support, where steady cargo flows and tighter vessel supply 
sustained elevated rates. Despite the broader weakness, 
renewed fixing activity in the Atlantic basin, with more cargoes 
matching open vessels, suggested that freight rates may be 
finding a floor. The Suezmax TCE stood at $638,377/day, down 
by 9% on the week.
The Aframax freight market recorded a week of recovery, with 
regional divergent trends. Overall, Aframax TCE earnings rose 
by 2.3% w-o-w, reaching $336,059/day. The Mediterranean 
market was quite active, witnessing robust demand conditions 
and declining tonnage. Meanwhile, the North Sea improved as 
the week progressed, with fresh enquiries emerging and 
prompt vessel supply declining as owners ballasted towards 
other markets in search of higher returns. East of Suez, the 
market remained subdued amid tepid demand, leading to a 
buildup of open tonnage.

Overall, the dry bulk market delivered a mixed performance last 
week, with a recovery in the Capesize segment contrasting 
with declining Kamsarmax/Panamax rates and relatively stable 
conditions in the smaller segments. The BDI averaged 2,634, 
down 1.5% w-o-w.
Capesize rates rebounded last week, reversing the previous 
week's losses, with the Pacific leading the recovery as miners 
stepped up activity. The South Atlantic also strengthened, as 
growing chartering interest for later loading dates and limited 
vessel availability supported rates. Expectations of typhoon-
related delays in China provided additional support to the 
Pacific. In contrast, the North Atlantic remained subdued amid 
lacklustre demand.
The Kamsarmax/Panamax segment faced weakening cargo 
demand, extending the downward trend in freight rates across 
both basins, particularly in the Atlantic. A growing surplus of 
open vessels intensified competition among owners, with East 
Coast South America facing the greatest pressure. The Pacific 
proved more resilient, supported by firmer Australian demand 
and steady Indonesian activity. Nevertheless, these favourable 
conditions were insufficient to offset the broader weakness in 
market fundamentals.
Turning to the smaller dry bulk segments, freight rates 
fluctuated within a narrow range last week, despite contrasting 
regional conditions. Ultramax/Supramax trading benefited from 
stronger Asian backhaul demand, offsetting softer North Pacific 
grain activity, while shorter tonnage lists supported Handysize 
rates in the Pacific. Both segments enjoyed firmer conditions in 
the South Atlantic, where healthy cargo demand and limited 
prompt vessel supply favoured owners. Conversely, subdued 
US Gulf demand left excess tonnage competing for 
employment, keeping rates under pressure, while the Continent 
and Mediterranean saw little change.

Last week, recycling markets held broadly steady, except 
Pakistan and Bangladesh, which retain their strong appetite 
despite subdued market conditions.
Alang remained stable, maintaining last week’s positive 
sentiment amid the festive period. Local steel plate prices 
firmed, even as holiday closures slowed demand. Exchange-
rate movements have been comparatively contained. Buying 
interest is selective and recycling activity remains limited, with 
general cargo and reefer arrivals at the waterfront alongside 
previously secured tankers and large specialist units. On the 
policy front, authorized ship recycling facilities by the Gujarat 
Maritime Board reached 47, a significant step towards yard 
compliance.
Chattogram remains soft, although buying interest is still 
strong. Local steel market is broadly stable, as festival holidays 
slowed down momentum. Steel mills are cautious and are 
holding minimal stock, though this could change as 
construction picks up seasonally. Local currency weakened 
against USD and candidate pool remains limited. On the macro 
side, slowing economy has curbed loan demand and left banks 
with surplus liquidity, creating better financing opportunities 
for recyclers.
 Pakistan showed mixed signals, with buying interest remaining 
strong, despite trade restrictions. Local steel prices declined, 
affected by weak demand. Local currency was unchanged 
against the dollar. Tonnage arrivals have been steady, but the 
wider market remains supply-constrained, keeping purchasing 
appetite balanced. Attention is focused on the closure of 
borders with Iran, as this disruption halted Iranian steel and 
rebar imports and will offer breakers a measure of support.
 Aliaga remained unchanged. Local steel prices corrected 
modestly with mills being cautious and facing liquidity 
constraints. Lira continues to weaken against USD and 
recycling flows are slow. The central bank cut its policy rate 
from 37% to 36%, offering modest financing relief, although 
present market conditions continue to limit buying capacity.
