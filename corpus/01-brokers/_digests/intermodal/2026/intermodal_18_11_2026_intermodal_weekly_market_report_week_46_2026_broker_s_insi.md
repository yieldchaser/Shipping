Week 46 | Tuesday 17th November 2026Market Insight 
By Vasilis Fousekis, Research Analyst
This week’s spotlight is on the LPG carriers, as VLGC earnings 
are at exceptional levels. The trade is also now attracting 
capacity from the broader gas segment: the ACS SP Nereus, a 
100k cbm VLEC, has loaded the first LPG cargo for its ship type 
at Houston. Rates on the US routes surpassed the record-high 
levels of September 2023 and the summer of 2026 this week, 
as fixture activity in the West held steady. Houston to Chiba is 
currently traded at about a TCE return of $184,600/day and 
Houston to Flushing at approximately $233,600/day TCE.
Behind these earnings lies the continued loss of Middle East 
supply. Hormuz has remained effectively under geopolitical 
uncertainty in November, with talks on a reopening yet to 
produce an agreement. Volumes remain below 50% of pre-war 
levels and Red Sea loadings remain exposed to further attacks. 
Long haul US cargoes, therefore, have become the main 
replacement for lost Middle East supply for Asian buyers, 
keeping the Baltic LPG index to 21k territory, above the Q3 
average.
Vessel availability, rather than export capacity, had so far set 
the pace of US flows, which are crucial for the LPG trade. A 
constraint has since emerged on the supply side. Phillips 66, a 
US based company which operates the Freeport LPG export 
terminal in Texas, declared force majeure on butane loadings 
after a power outage and compressor failure incident in early 
November, lifting US butane prices and raising concern over 
prompt availability, although overall US LPG loadings have so 
far shown no clear slowdown. A prolonged outage would 
mainly tighten butane supply to Asian buyers, rather than 
reduce the number of cargoes available to fix.
In Asia, demand remains firm and at the same time price 
sensitive. Chinese imports rose by about 1m tonnes q-o-q in 
Q3, supported by positive margins at propane-to-propylene 
plants. India consumed 2.6m tonnes in October, approaching 
the monthly volumes seen before the Middle East conflict, 
while holiday-season inventory building may extend import 
strength into November and December. However, if spot prices 
of propane and butane remain elevated, further buying by 
Indian importers is likely to slow, especially without any state 
support.
On the supply side, the outlook is less comfortable, with 8 
VLGCs delivered within Q3 and 3 more expected within Q4. 
About 170 VLGC and VLAC newbuildings, totalling 15.5m cbm, 
are due through 2030, equivalent to roughly 40% of the existing 
fleet. Contracting has continued at a strong pace, with 8.5m 
cbm of LPG carriers ordered in the first nine months and a 
further 14 large gas carriers signed in October. With the fleet 
averaging about 11.7 years, demolition is unlikely to offset 
much of this growth.
Looking ahead, long-haul US exports and Asian restocking 
should keep earnings well supported in the near term, despite 
periodic corrections. Seaborne volumes are expected to fall by 
around 12% this year, yet longer voyages should still lift tonne-
miles by about 2.5%. High rates are also drawing marginal 
tonnage into the market, as the Nereus lifting shows, although 
such crossovers are likely to stay occasional, mainly when 
ethane employment is weak. The orderbook is a more 
significant source of new capacity. A potential settlement of the 
Hormuz crisis should support the rebound projected for 2027, 
but a possible recovery in Middle East exports would also 
shorten average voyages, just as deliveries accelerate in 2027 
and 2028. The key question is whether US export growth can 
absorb that tonnage once trade routes normalize, with the 
timing of a settlement between Iran and the U.S. remaining the 
decisive factor.

Crude tanker markets underwent a broad-based correction last 
week, as recovering Middle Eastern crude exports reduced 
Asian reliance on longer-haul Atlantic Basin supplies, eroding 
tonne-mile demand amid increasing vessel availability. Despite 
ongoing disruptions, flows through Hormuz and overall Middle 
Eastern exports continued to recover, approaching pre-war 
levels, with a growing number of vessels transiting with their 
AIS switched off. Renewed vessel attacks and strikes on Saudi 
oil infrastructure highlighted persistent security risks, although 
Red Sea loadings remained largely unaffected. Against this 
backdrop, markets increasingly discounted a gradual 
normalisation of regional trade flows, potentially reinforced by 
the Democrats' victory in the US midterm elections and 
expectations of diplomatic de-escalation. 
The retreat from earlier record-high freight levels therefore 
appears to reflect an unwinding of the geopolitical risk 
premium, rather than a definitive resolution. Meanwhile, 
declining crude prices exerted additional downward pressure on 
freight rates, as transportation costs accounted for a larger 
proportion of commodity values.
VLCCs faced limited cargo demand and ample vessel availability 
across major trading regions, with growing Suezmax 
competition adding pressure in the Atlantic. The Middle East 
saw a modest pickup in fixing interest towards the close, while 
the US Gulf remained quiet, with limited fresh activity to 
establish a clearer market direction. VLCC TCE fell 29% w-o-w 
to $521,923/day.
The Suezmax segment experienced similar headwinds, 
although pockets of support emerged in the Atlantic post 
midweek, as renewed cargo interest from West Africa and the 
Americas lent some support, offsetting earlier losses. Suezmax 
TCE declined 33% on the week, to $227,213/day.
Finally, Aframaxes also recorded losses, albeit with regional 
divergences. Increased fixing activity in the Mediterranean and 
US Gulf absorbed some available tonnage, while the North Sea 
struggled with tepid cargo demand. Meanwhile, limited 
chartering interest in Asia contributed to lengthening vessel 
lists, impacting rates. Aframax TCE eased by 19% on a weekly 
basis, to $142,605/day.

A firmer week for the spot dry bulk market saw gains across all 
vessel segments, led by the larger sizes. The BDI averaged 
2,935, up 9% w-o- w.
The Capesize segment extended the previous week’s gains, 
with the Pacific taking the lead as stronger miner activity drove 
increased iron ore shipments from Western Australian ports to 
China. The Atlantic, meanwhile, presented a more uneven 
landscape, with improving transatlantic and fronthaul demand 
underpinning rates in the north, while subdued activity on the 
Brazil-China and West Africa- China trades weighed on the 
southern basin.
The Kamsarmax/Panamax market displayed divergent trends, 
largely reflecting regional differences in tonnage supply 
conditions. The Atlantic emerged as the main source of support, 
with healthy transatlantic demand reinforced by a relatively 
tight vessel supply, particularly in ECSA. Northern Europe 
offered a note of caution, however, as a growing pool of open 
tonnage began to temper sentiment. The Pacific faced the 
opposite backdrop, with an expanding tonnage list 
overshadowing Australian mineral demand, while limited 
enquiries in the North Pacific and Indonesia weighed on rates.
On the Ultramax/Supramax side, performance was uneven, 
with regional imbalances in prompt tonnage shaping freight 
conditions. The Atlantic benefited from limited vessel 
availability in North America and healthy Mediterranean-bound 
cargo demand from the US Gulf and ECSA. In the Pacific, an 
expanding fleet of open ships coincided with slower North 
Pacific grain activity and subdued Indonesian enquiries. Fresh 
Southeast Asian cargoes and resilient backhaul demand 
provided some relief. Overall, average earnings registered 
modest weekly gains.
The Handysize segment saw favourable conditions across most 
trading regions, with rates strengthening towards the week's 
close, although the underlying drivers varied. In Northern 
Europe, steady grain volumes  met a tight Baltic tonnage list, 
while the US Gulf benefited from healthy demand and limited 
prompt vessel supply. In the Mediterranean, risks associated 
with Black Sea trading influenced owners’ deployment 
decisions, restricting tonnage pool. Meanwhile, fresh business 
in the South Atlantic and increased fixing activity in Asia 
contributed to the overall upward momentum.

Last week, recycling markets held back with India remaining 
firm and Bangladesh showing the strongest appetite for 
conventional tonnage across the Subcontinent. Against this 
backdrop, Best Oasis became the second cash buyer, after 
GMS, to obtain OFAC approval for negotiating transactions 
involving sanctioned tonnage.
Alang’s sentiment continues to be positive. Local steel prices 
edged slightly over the week amid a sluggish market. However, 
as activity is slowly recovering after the holiday slowdown, 
buyers’ appetite is expected to firm. Local currency rate 
weakened against the USD, due to increased trade deficit 
driven by festive demand for gold. Few candidates were 
circulated to the market, among them a mining vessel. Interest 
in conventional units shows no sign of fading, although 
breakers are taking a careful line on commitments, as there is 
uncertainty over the finalization of the EU approval list.
Chattogram’s sentiment was selective, but positive, with 
continuous appetite on tonnage acquisitions. Local steel prices 
declined with mills cautiously considering further purchases, as 
expectations grow that public infrastructure projects will 
continue. Local currency rate strenghtened against the USD, as 
Bangladesh Bank sold 200m of its USD reserves to support 
Taka. Few dry candidates were circulated to the market, with 
conventional vessels keeping interest strong.
Gadani remained static, with market participants being 
cautious about an expected correction. Overall local market 
conditions are subdued with local steel market remaining slow. 
Local currency firmed slightly against the USD. Dry tonnage has 
been circulated to the market, but breakers seem unwilling for 
further purchases.
Aliaga retains the selective approach over subdued market 
conditions. The explosion at Habas iron and steel plant created 
further concerns on steel supply, increasing HRC and Hollow 
product prices.  Weak steel demand, high financing costs and 
currency depreciation still weigh on sentiment, while the 
national central bank revised forecasts for 2026 inflation to 
29.50%.
