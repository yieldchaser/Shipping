Week 40| Tuesday 06th October 2026  Market Insight  
By Nikos Tagoulis, Head of Research Department 
The diesel market saw a series of notable developments last 
week, against a backdrop of elevated prices and mounting con-
cerns over energy security. The G7 agreed to release 100 million 
barrels of crude and diesel stocks over the next four months, 
pending the IEA ’s decision on the details and implementation of 
the stock release. Europe is expected to contribute around 50 
million barrels of diesel, further drawing down strategic invento-
ries. At the same time, commercial stocks in the ARA hub remain 
below their five-year average, revealing a relatively limited buffer. 
The release move followed US pressure and discussions aimed at 
removing the threat of restrictions on US diesel exports. 
Supply uncertainty is also mounting elsewhere. China suspended 
refined-product exports around the Golden Week holiday, follow-
ing a sharp increase in diesel exports during September, leaving 
uncertainty over policy thereafter. Russia, meanwhile, remains 
largely sidelined from the export market following the extension 
of its diesel restrictions through October and continued disruption 
to refining infrastructure from Ukrainian attacks. 
In the Middle East, the recovery in crude exports observed has not 
been matched by refined products. Vortexa data indicate regional 
diesel/gasoil exports reached 1.12 mbpd in September, down 36% 
y-o-y, while flows to Europe were roughly 50% lower. Lower Mid-
dle Eastern product exports are particularly relevant for Europe, 
given its dependence on the region for diesel imports. 
Across the Atlantic, the US remains a critical diesel supplier. US 
diesel/gasoil exports reached around 1.57 mbpd in Q3 2026, up 
28% y -o-y, supported by strong flows into Central and South 
America alongside increasing long -haul shipments to Europe by 
approx. 35% y -o-y. While intra -American trades are relatively 
short-haul, stronger US Gulf-Europe shipments add ton miles and 
provide meaningful support for MR employment. Keeping US ex-
ports unrestricted also preserves a crucial source of incremental 
Atlantic Basin diesel supply. 
Within this constrained supply environment, Europe is increasing-
ly turning to alternative suppliers beyond the US, with India 
emerging as a more prominent source. October arrivals of diesel/
gasoil from India are currently expected at around 228k bpd, up 
approximately 65% m -o-m. India is well placed to respond to the 
global supply deficit, supported by a large refining base fed by 
crude mainly from Russia and the Middle East, while New Delhi ’s 
recent reduction in the diesel export levy provides further support 
for increased export flows. Strong European margins could attract 
more Indian barrels westward, providing further support to LR1/
LR2 employment and ton-mile demand. 
Longer-haul arbitrage opportunities may also emerge from 
Northeast Asia, with a 90,000 -ton South Korean diesel cargo re-
portedly fixed for Northwest Europe. This trade stands out, given 
Korea’s traditional focus on more proximate markets such as 
Australia and East Asia. However, Korea ’s ability to respond more 
broadly to stronger global diesel demand remains constrained by 
government energy -security measures, which cap monthly ex-
ports of regular gasoline, automotive diesel and heating kerosene 
at the volumes shipped in the corresponding month of 2025. 
For product tanker demand, these developments are broadly sup-
portive. Europe ’s need to source barrels from the US Gulf, India 
and potentially South Korea, supports ton -mile demand across 
both the MR and LR segments. European strategic -stock releases 
could temporarily reduce prompt import requirements, but unless 
underlying supply constraints ease materially, the redirection of 
diesel trade flows towards more distant suppliers should remain 
supportive of tanker utilisation. 
More broadly, should elevated diesel prices and constrained mar-
ket conditions persist, the impact could extend beyond the prod-
uct-tanker market. Heavy industry, road transportation and agri-
culture remain highly reliant on diesel, while short -term demand 
is relatively inelastic . Persistently high costs could intensify infla-
tionary pressures and weigh on agricultural economics, affecting 
production and exports and create a secondary spillover beyond 
product tankers into dry bulk, impacting seaborne grain volumes.  
Overall, the current diesel imbalance is increasingly reshaping 
trade routes, as Europe’s search for replacement barrels supports 
longer-haul product flows. However, given diesel ’s critical role in 
the global economy, persistently elevated prices or prolonged 
supply shortages could have consequences extending well be-
yond shipping, with broader implications for global economic ac-
tivity.

Crude tanker spot freight markets extended their upward trend, 
with gains across all vessel classes and fresh record highs. The 
BDTI closed the week above the 6,000 -point threshold at 6,586, 
up 22.7% w-o-w. 
 
The VLCC segment advanced further, with TCE earnings reaching 
$786,245/day, up 10% w -o-w. The Middle East remained broadly 
balanced, as a modest improvement in demand gradually ab-
sorbed available tonnage. The US Gulf stayed relatively subdued, 
although firmer conditions in surrounding markets and some pri-
vate fixing provided support from mid -week onward. West Africa 
opened on a weaker footing, with muted enquiry and ample sup-
ply, before a late surge in demand tightened the tonnage list and 
underpinned rates. 
 
The Suezmax market soared last week, driven primarily by excep-
tionally strong Atlantic demand. TCE earnings strengthened 
throughout the week before accelerating sharply into the close, 
reaching a record $619,858/day, up 107% w -o-w. In West Africa, 
a limited pool of tonnage proved insufficient to meet robust en-
quiry, intensifying competition for ships across neighbouring At-
lantic markets. Scarce VLCC alternatives and end -month cargo 
requirements further reinforced owners ’ leverage, while the 
strength spread into the Black Sea and CPC, where similarly tight 
tonnage pushed rates higher. 
 
Aframax momentum was led by the US Gulf and Mediterranean, 
while firm conditions in the larger tanker classes provided addi-
tional support to sentiment. In Asia, steady cargo flow continued 
to absorb available tonnage and underpin rates. In the Mediterra-
nean, softer demand allowed the tonnage list to rebuild, although 
attractive US Gulf returns encouraged westbound ballasting and 
limited downward pressure on rates. North Sea and UKC demand 
remained tepid, increasing vessel availability, but strong USG eco-
nomics continued to support sentiment across the Atlantic. 
Aframax TCE earnings reached $276,913/day, up 18% w-o-w.

The Capesize segment weakened through most of the week as 
growing tonnage availability, particularly in the Pacific, out-
weighed relatively subdued cargo demand. The Atlantic also 
lacked support, with limited Brazil -China activity and fewer 
prompt requirements as loading dates moved further into Octo-
ber. Conditions improved towards the close as fresh cargoes 
emerged in both basins, while stronger West Australia -China fix-
ing, supported by increased miner activity, absorbed part of the 
available list. Despite the firmer finish, the late recovery was in-
sufficient to offset the week’s earlier losses. 
Kamsarmax/Panamax conditions diverged across the two basins. 
Atlantic activity was supported by firmer US Gulf demand and a 
tighter tonnage pool in the South Atlantic, although momentum 
eased towards the end of the week as enquiry softened. In the 
Pacific, activity slowed as China ’s Golden Week weighed on fresh 
demand. Australian and North Pacific coal and grain cargoes 
helped absorb some tonnage, but were insufficient to reverse the 
broader softening in sentiment. 
Ultramax/Supramax rates remained broadly stable over the week 
amid lower trading intensity. The combination of Golden Week 
and Coaltrans reduced participation, resulting in fewer fixtures 
rather than a clear deterioration in underlying demand. The Pacific 
and Indian Ocean remained relatively balanced. In the Atlantic, 
weaker Mediterranean demand encouraged vessels to ballast 
towards the more lucrative ECSA market, where initially tight ton-
nage conditions gradually eased as additional units arrived. 
The Handysize market reflected opposing regional forces, which 
broadly offset one another and kept rates largely steady overall. 
The Continent and Mediterranean remained the firmer areas, 
supported by healthier cargo flow and tighter availability. Pacific 
activity slowed due to Golden Week holidays, but a limited vessel 
pool helped preserve rate levels despite reduced fixing. In South 
Atlantic, fading demand allowed prompt tonnage to build, creat-
ing some downward pressure.

Last week, major ship recycling hubs remained broadly steady,  
constrained by low supply, while activity in India was impacted 
by National Holiday.  
India remained supply -constrained, with National Holiday slow-
ing activity during the week. Local steel market fundamentals 
provided mixed signals as the plate price recovered from recent 
lows, while local currency weakened against the USD. In ship 
recycling, buyers ’ appetite rose notably for conventional ton-
nage, with other recyclers focusing to specialized vessels, 
whereas some yards were resorting to sanctioned units  to stay 
operational. On the economy side, Reserve Bank of India is ex-
pected to increase interest rates to provide the necessary sup-
port to the local currency as a further measure to cool the econ-
omy. 
Chattogram showed steady purchasing appetite. Domestic steel 
segment is firmer with increased prices during the week, while 
Taka slightly firmed against US Dollar. The high -quality stand-
ards maintained by several Bangladeshi yards have helped at-
tract more recycling candidates. Delays in securing timely Letter 
of Credits from banks pose a significant issue to recyclers, most 
notably for larger ships. 
Gadani remained subdued this week, with demand for candi-
dates easing slightly as yards are presently well supplied, while 
buyers continue to be selective over available tonnage. In the 
steel segment, imports from China and, more recently, Brazil 
weighed on sentiment. Waterfront activity nevertheless re-
mained healthy, with previously secured vessels continuing to 
feed the yards. Local currency remained broadly stable. 
The Turkish market maintained a similar buying appetite to last 
week. In the steel segment, local plate prices edged higher, 
providing marginal support. No fresh candidates emerged. At 
the same time, the Turkish Lira continued to weaken against the 
US Dollar.
