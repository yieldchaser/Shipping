Week 47 | Tuesday 24th November 2026Market Insight 
By Nikos Tagoulis, Head of Research Department
Brazil traditionally holds a prominent role in Atlantic Basin 
crude trading, with the expansion of its offshore production 
further underpinning its influence in global oil flows. Rising 
output is increasing export availability and providing greater 
flexibility in directing cargoes between European and Asian 
markets, while offering buyers a source of crude supply largely 
insulated from geopolitical disruptions. Moreover, on the policy 
side, favourable trade terms under the EU-Mercosur 
agreement provide an additional commercial incentive for crude 
shipments to Europe.
The driving force behind Brazil's production growth is its vast 
pre-salt fields, where substantial oil and gas reservoirs lie 
beneath thick salt formations deep below the Atlantic seabed. 
According to the country's National Agency of Petroleum, 
Natural Gas and Biofuels (ANP), crude output reached a record 
4.71 million b/d in September 2026, up 20.3% y-o-y, with 
nearly 84% originating from pre-salt fields. At the heart of this 
expansion lies the Santos Basin, off Brazil's southeastern coast, 
which accounts for around 80% of national crude production 
and hosts some of the country's largest offshore oil fields, 
notably Búzios and Mero. The scale of the Búzios oil field is 
particularly striking, with production reaching 1.19 million b/d, 
equivalent to approximately one-quarter of Brazil's total crude 
output. With additional FPSOs scheduled to commence 
operations at the Búzios field in 2027, Brazil is well positioned 
to consolidate its role as a major crude exporter outside OPEC. 
The rise in output is also reflected in seaborne volumes, with 
Europe gaining ground as a destination without displacing 
Brazil's established Asian markets. According to LSEG data, 
Brazilian crude exports rose 21.9% y-o-y to 719 million barrels 
during January-October 2026. EU-bound shipments increased 
19.3% to 137.8 million barrels, while China received 15% more 
and India's intake almost tripled. The sharper change in 
European trade came after April. Following a decline of 12.9% y-
o-y during January-April, EU-bound volumes rebounded by 41% 
over May-October, with October shipments alone more than 
doubling y-o-y.
This increase may partly be attributed to the EU-Mercosur 
agreement. Signed in January and provisionally applied from 1 
May, the agreement aims to deepen bilateral trade relations. 
However, Brazilian government’s decision to impose a 12% 
crude export tax in March gave Mercosur’s preferential 
provisions greater commercial importance. Under the 
agreement, crude exports to the EU are subject to a reduced 6% 
rate, compared with the full 12% levy imposed on non-EU 
destinations, providing EU buyers an advantage in securing 
Brazilian barrels. The timing is particularly relevant for the EU, 
given its efforts to diversify away from Russian energy and the 
geopolitical uncertainty affecting oil supplies and tanker 
movements through the Arabian Gulf. 
For the crude tanker market, the impact of Brazil's expanding 
crude exports largely depends on the geographical distribution 
of trade flows. Increased shipments to Europe support 
employment for VLCCs and Suezmaxes, which account for the 
bulk of these flows. However, while additional cargoes to 
Europe generate greater transatlantic activity, any diversion of 
shipments previously destined for Asian longer-haul markets, 
would shorten average voyage distances, potentially offsetting 
some of the gains in ton-miles. At the same time, India's near-
tripling of Brazilian crude imports lends support to long-haul 
tanker demand, while rising Brazilian production creates scope 
for further growth in export volumes. Moreover, Brazil’s rising 
offshore production also supports demand for shuttle tankers 
able to operate in open-sea conditions, equipped with dynamic 
positioning (DP) systems, which transport crude from FPSOs to 
coastal terminals or STS transfer locations.
Aside from tankers, the impact for the offshore segment is 
clearly positive. Brazil represents a principal employment 
region for such vessels, with sustained investment in 
deepwater field development underpinning demand for PSVs, 
AHTSs and specialised subsea units. Further expansion of 
offshore activity is expected to strengthen demand for offshore 
vessel capacity, creating additional employment opportunities.
Whether EU retains its preferential tax advantage beyond 2026 
depends on Brazil's crude export levy. The tax has been 
extended through three successive government resolutions, 
most recently in November, until 31 December 2026. If 
extended, EU-bound shipments would continue to benefit from 
the lower rate. If the levy expires, exports to all destinations 
would become exempt, removing Europe's relative advantage 
and potentially intensifying competition for Brazilian barrels. 
More broadly, Brazil’s growing offshore oil output is 
establishing its position as a major global supplier, with both 
the tanker and offshore segments poised to benefit.

Crude tanker spot freight markets recovered following the 
previous week's losses. Robust Atlantic crude flows drove 
much of the improvement, with USG charterers generally 
favouring Suezmaxes and Aframaxes over VLCCs. Nevertheless, 
the BDTI averaged 4,678, down 2% w-o-w.
In the VLCC segment, the Middle East led the improvement, as 
increased cargo demand and steady fixing activity coincided 
with shrinking tonnage pool. West African enquiry remained 
muted, and despite a tighter vessel list, competitive Suezmax 
rates continued to divert cargoes away from VLCCs. Stronger 
Brazilian exports supported Atlantic freight rates and owners' 
sentiment. The USG remained sluggish, with competition from 
smaller tanker classes limiting fresh VLCC business. Overall, 
VLCC TCE earnings reached $543,172/day, up 4% w-o-w.
The Suezmax market was primarily driven by the USG, where 
activity accelerated considerably during the week. Charterers 
initially favoured Aframaxes, but surging rates in that segment 
shifted preference to Suezmaxes, attracting additional cargoes 
and absorbing prompt tonnage. Some requirements were also 
brought forward ahead of Thanksgiving. Elsewhere, WAF, 
Med/CPC and AG saw little new business. Nevertheless, 
increased Atlantic employment and attractive USG earnings 
contributed to reduced available tonnage in WAF and 
Med/Black Sea. Consequently, firmer conditions outside the 
USG resulted mainly from limited supply rather than additional 
cargo demand. Suezmax TCE earnings rose 10% w-o-w to 
$251,148/day.
Aframaxes recorded the strongest performance among crude 
tanker classes, with TCE earnings surging 54% w-o-w to 
$220,088/day. The USG rally was supported by an open 
transatlantic arbitrage, with US crude remaining competitively 
priced against Brent despite the narrowing price differential. 
The North Sea subsequently strengthened, as increased 
chartering activity and vessel repositioning towards the 
lucrative USG market reduced local availability. Mediterranean 
enquiry also accelerated post midweek, driving substantial rate 
gains. Both European regions benefited from USG momentum, 
while adverse weather conditions and port delays further 
restricted active tonnage. In contrast, the Asian market failed to 
gain traction, due to declined cargo interest.

The Capesize segment displayed contrasting regional dynamics 
last week, with the West Australia-China route rebounding post 
mid-week, as the Pacific market benefited from the active 
presence of all three major miners. The Atlantic basin 
experienced more subdued conditions, with the South Atlantic 
providing the main counterweight, as a shrinking pool of 
available vessels coincided with a recovery in cargo demand, 
reinforcing owners’ bargaining power. Conversely, lacklustre 
cargo activity and ample tonnage availability continued to 
depress earnings in the North Atlantic.
In the Kamsarmax/Panamax market, the backdrop remained 
largely unchanged last week, with the Atlantic continuing to 
outperform the Pacific. In the North Atlantic, firmness was 
primarily demand-driven, with steady mineral and transatlantic 
grain enquiries against limited prompt vessel supply. In ECSA, 
positive sentiment largely reflected forward tonnage 
constraints. By contrast, the Pacific continued to grapple with 
excess tonnage and tepid cargo demand. A prolonged 
divergence between the two basins could incentivise more 
vessels to ballast towards the Atlantic, potentially easing 
regional vessel scarcity and limiting further freight gains.
Overall, the Ultramax/Supramax segment posted uneven 
regional performance last week, with a marginal decline in 
aggregate earnings. In the Pacific, subdued Australian demand 
and increasing competition for Indonesian cargoes dampened 
sentiment, although the North Pacific showed tentative signs 
of recovery towards the week's close. In the Atlantic, both 
cargo demand and vessel availability remained limited in the US 
Gulf, while the Mediterranean market was muted amid a 
tonnage surplus. ECSA and the Continent emerged as the bright 
spots, benefiting from healthy cargo demand against 
constrained vessel availability.
Handysizes remained resilient last week, with supply-side 
fundamentals rather than demand growth largely underpinning 
rates. Vessel scarcity strengthened owners’ negotiating 
position in ECSA, the US Gulf and the North Pacific, while scrap 
shipments sustained the positive momentum in Northern 
Europe. The Mediterranean market held steady, with supply 
and demand broadly alignes, while Asian performance 
remained uneven, with improving fundamentals in the north 
and sluggish activity in the south.

Newbuilding activity was robust, with 18 orders for 80 firm 
units, plus 1 option, of which 59 vessels were in the offshore 
segment placed by COSL.
On the tanker side, Advantage Tankers ordered 2 ULCCs of 
320k dwt at Hanwha Ocean, due in 2029, for $136m each. 
Thenamaris also booked 2 scrubber fitted units of 303k dwt at 
New Times Shipbuilding, for 2030 delivery, priced at $130m 
each. Neda Maritime followed at the same yard with 1 firm, 
plus 1 optional, unit of identical size and delivery. Teekay signed 
for 2 tankers of 157k dwt at DH Shipbuilding, scheduled for 
2030, for $93m per vessel, while Aegean Shipping inked 2 ice-
class 1C product carriers of 114k dwt at COSCO HI Yangzhou, 
also due in 2030. GEM exercised options for 2 units of 74k dwt 
at K Shipbuilding, due in 2029, at $66m per ship. Stamford 
Shipping secured 4 MRs of 49.9k dwt at Nantong CIMC SOE for 
the same year.
In offshore, COSL placed an order for 59 vessels at Fujian 
Shipbuilding, with deliveries spread across 2028-2030. The 
shipbuilding programme comprises of 46 LNG-powered PSVs: 7 
units of 4k dwt, 23 units of 3k dwt and 14 units of 2k dwt, plus 
2 same vessels of 10k hp and 6.5k hp. The remaining 13 are 
conventional AHTS, namely 4 units of 15k hp, 6 vessels of 12k 
hp and 3 more of 8k hp.
Elsewhere, Sanjiang Shipping inked 2 dual-fuel VLECs of 102k 
cbm at Jiangnan, due in 2030, for $160m each, while K Line 
added 2 LPG gas carriers of 90k cbm at HD Hyundai Samho 
with delivery in 2030, for $116m each. Singapore Shipping Corp 
booked 2 LNG PCTCs of 7k ceu at GSI, due in 2031.

Last week, Bangladesh stood out as the strongest of the 
recycling destinations across the Subcontinent, while Pakistan 
and India stayed cautious and Turkey remained subdued.
In Alang, sentiment weakened as breakers held a cautious 
stance. Local steel market remained sluggish, with prices 
slipping from previous levels. Local currency strengthened 
against the USD, but the recent sharp currency swings add 
uncertainty. Limited vessels circulated the market, with buyers’ 
interest more focused on specialist tonnage, rather than the 
conventional units.
Chattogram was firm, with positive sentiment and a strong 
appetite in acquisitions. Local steel prices rose, although local 
demand has yet to recover. Taka held unchanged against the 
US dollar. A few dry bulk candidates were circulated to the 
market, including a vessel proposed for delivery after about 130 
days. Domestic conditions, however, remain fragile: Political 
tensions, arson incidents and heightened security risks have 
added uncertainty.
Gadani remained quiet, with the market still being cautious. 
Local steel demand stayed slow; however, scarcity on materials 
during the week pushed prices higher. Local currency edged 
slightly firmer against the USD. Few candidates were circulated 
to the market amid low appetite for further tonnage. On the 
economic front, a proposed increase in electricity tariffs could 
put further cost pressure on recyclers.
Aliaga was stable. Local steel prices increased on expectations 
of higher energy costs, while the Izmir steel mill remains shut 
after last week’s fatal accident. The lira weakened further, 
hitting a new year-to-date low against the USD. Transactional 
activity was limited.
