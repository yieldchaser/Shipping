Market Insight  
By George Vitsos, Offshore Broker 
The Offshore sector has been greatly affected by the current geo-
political situation, as challenges that are substantially greater 
than those faced by merchant vessels are presented, due to the 
limited flexibility the floating units have under tow. Floating rigs, 
accommodation units, crane barges, vessels and FPSOs that are 
being repositioned, typically move at speeds of only 4 -7 knots 
and are unable to take evasive action, as their slow speed, pre-
dictable movements and large radar signature make them partic-
ularly vulnerable targets in any environment where missiles, 
drones, unmanned surface vessels or small craft attacks are pos-
sible. The International Maritime Organization has repeatedly 
highlighted that commercial shipping and seafarers are increas-
ingly exposed to geopolitical conflicts, while maritime security 
experts continue to warn that vessels operating in both Hormuz 
and the Red Sea face elevated risks regardless of nationality or 
flag. 
The Strait of Hormuz currently represents the most difficult envi-
ronment for offshore movements. In practice, a towing operation 
might not only require just the tug and the tow, but also support 
crafts, fuel logistics, crew changes, emergency response arrange-
ments and reliable Ports of Refuge. Recent attacks on several 
tankers in the Middle East and Red Sea, are still exposed to mili-
tary actions, whereas hundreds of tankers remain anchored or 
drifting throughout the Gulf awaiting improved security condi-
tions, creating significant navigational congestion. Reports of very 
low daily transit numbers compared with normal trading patterns 
indicate a de facto restriction on navigation, even if the waterway 
remains open. 
For offshore contractors, the immediate consequence is that find-
ing suitable ocean -going tugs able to enter or exit the Gulf has 
become increasingly difficult. From a commercial standpoint, 
many operators are reluctant to expose crews to a region where 
vessels have been struck by projectiles, drones or explosive craft. 
From an insurance perspective, war -risk premiums have risen 
sharply, and underwriters are scrutinizing every planned voyage. 
The recent decision by Lloyd's market participants to terminate 
coverage for vessels making unauthorized transit payments or 
tolls in the Strait of Hormuz adds a further layer of uncertainty to 
voyage. 
The Red Sea presents similar concerns. Although some commer-
cial traffic continues to pass through Bab el -Mandeb, Houthi at-
tacks have demonstrated a threat to vessels over a wide geo-
graphic area using drones and missiles, while maritime security 
advisories continue to classify the southern Red Sea, Gulf of Aden 
and Bab el -Mandeb Strait as high -threat risk areas, with the 
Houthis recently escalating their campaign through renewed 
threats against shipping and the announcement of restrictions 
targeting Saudi -linked maritime trade. Security organizations 
have reported evidence of vessel diversions and reduced traffic 
levels in the Bab el -Mandeb area, underlining the ongoing opera-
tional challenges for commercial shipping. 
For a large tanker, the exposure window in Bab el -Mandeb may 
be measured in hours. For a tow moving at four knots, the same 
exposure can extend across several days, significantly increasing 
operational risk. As a result, offshore operators contemplating the 
mobilization of commercial vessels, rigs, barges or floating pro-
duction units into or out of the Gulf should expect restricted tug 
availability, elevated insurance costs and potential difficulties ob-
taining charter -party and lender approvals. Industry feedback 
from sources active in the region already suggest that many tug 
operators are unwilling to undertake Hormuz transits under pre-
sent conditions, effectively limiting available towing capacity.  
Developments during the past week have further demonstrated 
how quickly the threat environment can evolve. Reports of re-
duced transit levels through both Hormuz and Bab el -Mandeb, 
together with renewed attacks and threats directed against com-
mercial shipping and energy infrastructure, continue to influence 
operator decision -making and insurance market sentiment. For 
offshore projects requiring prolonged exposure within these are-
as, the risk profile remains substantially higher than that faced by 
conventional merchant shipping.  
Looking forward, the outlook remains highly dependent on geo-
political developments. Any renewed escalation would likely trig-
ger further issues against commercial shipping, as under such 
scenario, offshore movements would be among the first activities 
postponed, as the risk-reward balance for tug operators, offshore 
contractors and insurers becomes increasingly unattractive.

Last week, the large crude carrier rates firmed, supported by re-
newed disruptions in Middle East resulting to longer -haul diver-
sions via Suez, while the Aframax market corrected, reversing the 
strong performance recorded in previous weeks. The BDTI aver-
aged 2,592, up 7% w- o-w. 
 
The VLCC segment strengthened, underpinned by Middle East 
activity despite heightened geopolitical risks. The decline in Irani-
an attacks in the Arabian Gulf encouraged owners to load in the 
Gulf of Oman. Red Sea also added ton miles, with some crude 
flows moving through Suez and via the SUMED pipeline and 
around the Cape of Good Hope. West African rates rose as prompt 
tonnage tightened, though stronger demand is needed to sustain 
the gains. The US Gulf firmed amid robust enquiry, which ab-
sorbed tonnage and strengthened rates and sentiment. 
 
Geopolitical risk and navigational safety remain a key driver of the 
Suezmax market, with the two major trading regions, Black Sea 
and Middle East, exposed to ongoing hostilities. Although activity 
eased in Black Sea, overall rates firmed as heightened security 
risks and recurring disruptions to CPC operations lifted premiums. 
At the same time, ballasting vessels to alternative markets in-
creased tonnage availability in West Africa, placing rates and sen-
timent under pressure. Elsewhere, the Atlantic basin found some 
support from a pickup in Guyanese crude cargoes. 
 
The Aframax segment reversed the previous week ’s firming mo-
mentum, with most regional spot markets posting losses. Follow-
ing a period of strength, the North Sea market turned quiet amid 
weak levels of enquiries. Mediterranean rates also retreated as 
fading demand failed to absorb the growing tonnage pool. The US 
Gulf weakened as incoming ballasters lengthened the tonnage 
list. Finally in Asia, several privately concluded deals up ticked 
activity levels, however, charterers retained the negotiating ad-
vantage, preventing rates from gaining meaningful traction.

The dry bulk sector followed contrasting trends last week, as 
smaller-vessel rates declined, Capesize earnings remained stable, 
and Panamax rates strengthened. Overall, the BDI averaged 
2,680, representing a modest 1% week-on-week decrease. 
The Capesize spot market remained broadly steady, strengthen-
ing after midweek as demand recovered in the South Atlantic and 
Pacific, supported in the latter by increased miner participation. 
The North Atlantic was the weakest region, where only sporadic 
transatlantic demand emerged, being insufficient to lift sentiment 
or generate meaningful upward momentum. 
The Kamsarmax/Panamax segment firmed, recovering from earli-
er subdued conditions. Rates strengthened progressively as in-
creased Atlantic demand coincided with a limited pool of available 
vessels, improving market sentiment. The Pacific also gained mo-
mentum, with demand concentrated on North Pacific grain car-
goes and Australian coal shipments. Combined with constrained  
supply, this stronger cargo flow supported higher spot rates.  
The Ultramax/Supramax market softened last week, primarily 
due to weak cargo demand across most regions. The US Gulf was 
a relative bright spot, showing signs of recovery as fixing activity 
increased. The South Atlantic experienced its seasonal slowdown, 
limiting activity and rate gains. Asian and Pacific markets re-
mained under pressure from scarce enquiries and excess ton-
nage. In the Mediterranean, demand was uneven, with favourably 
positioned vessels securing better employment opportunities. 
On the Handysize side, fundamentals pressured rates, as con-
strained demand left both basins with excess supply and shaped 
a market backdrop short of the cargo volume needed to absorb 
open tonnage. This was most evident in the US Gulf, South Atlan-
tic and North Pacific. In the Mediterranean and Continent, with 
few fresh cargoes emerging, employment prospects and fixing 
levels came down to vessel position, making regional trade posi-
tion driven rather than demand led.

Last week, ship recycling markets across the Subcontinent wit-
nessed a more positive sentiment, supported by increased de-
mand for recycling candidates.  
India's ship recycling market improved modestly from the sub-
dued conditions of the previous week, underpinned by stronger, 
albeit selective, buying interest. Steel fundamentals remained 
weak as monsoons continued to constrain output. Neverthe-
less, dollar -denominated plate prices rose as the Indian rupee 
strengthened against the US dollar. Although India remained 
less price-competitive than its Subcontinent peers, it continued 
to reinforce its competitive advantage through regulatory com-
pliance. With the majority of yards being HKC -compliant and the 
recent proposal to add two Indian yards to the EU List, the 
country is further enhancing its position, potentially paving the 
way for EU-flagged candidates.  
Chattogram's market outlook has become more positive, sup-
ported by stronger tonnage demand, the approaching end of the 
monsoon season, and the return of recycling candidates delayed 
by last month ’s severe flooding, creating a healthy tonnage 
pool. Purchasing appetite favors bulkers, general cargo vessels, 
and units with clean records, while sanctions -related histories 
continue to depress prices. Steel market activity has recovered 
and plate prices remain firm, but recyclers have not yet reflect-
ed this strength in their offers.  
At Gadani, underlying demand continues to strengthen but has 
yet to translate into meaningful activity, with vessel arrivals still 
at a standstill. Recyclers remain willing buyers, though scarcity 
of candidates rather than any weakness in appetite is what is 
holding volumes down. Steel prices are advancing on the back 
of the cessation of Iranian imports and monsoon -related opera-
tional disruption. The consumption outlook is also constructive, 
with housing subsidies and reduced property withholding taxes 
expected to encourage construction activity.  
At Aliaga it was a quiet week with little change in demand or 
sentiment, amid a sluggish flow of candidates. The hub is an 
attractive destination for EU compliant recycling segment.
