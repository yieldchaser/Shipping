Week 41 | Tuesday 13th October 2026  Market Insight  
By Vasilis Fousekis, Research Analyst 
With El Niño now established and forecasters pointing to a strong 
event, the question for shipping is where it affects freight first. 
Timing matters as much as strength: the largest effects on crops, 
water levels and trade usually arrive in the first half of the follow-
ing year, which places the main freight impact in 2027. 
El Niño is a dynamic disruption and its effects vary by region. Drier 
conditions are expected across Central America, including the 
Panama Canal watershed, while the impact on Brazil is different 
by region, with drier conditions in the central and northern grain 
producing states. Argentina and the southern United States, by 
contrast, tend to receive more rain. India, Southeast Asia and 
Australia are set to face droughts. 
For dry bulk, the canal is the most direct link. The Panama Canal 
Authority, after cutting daily transits to 32 in September - only 
the second such reduction in history, eased restrictions following 
late-September rain. The relief, though, may not last. Panama ’s 
meteorological institute expects the dry season to begin as early 
as the second half of November, cutting into what is normally the 
wettest month, when Gatun Lake is replenished. The Authority 
projects an average of 29.5 crossings a day over the coming fiscal 
year. It is also auctioning long term slots for the older Panamax 
locks for the first time, covering January to early April 2027. Re-
routing is already visible, as deviation through the Strait of Magel-
lan rose sharply in August and September, supporting Panamax 
and Kamsarmax utilisation, even when volumes are flat. 
In coal, El Niño brings drought to Southeast Asia, reducing hydro-
power output, and increasing coal burn, notably in India and Vi-
etnam, contributing to the shift to coal as a source of energy, in a 
period of high natural gas prices. However, seaborne trade is 
more selective. India and China are expected to import less ther-
mal coal this year, as they draw on high domestic stocks, impact-
ing total seaborne thermal volumes,  with growth concentrated on 
short-haul Southeast Asian routes, such as Indonesia or Australia 
to Vietnam, limiting the tonne-mile gain. 
In grains, El Niño is expected to affect Brazil ’s 2026/27 crop 
mainly through irregular rainfall in central and northern Brazil, 
while the south faces excess rain. The key risk lies between No-
vember and January, when dry spells and heat could reduce soy-
bean yields in Mato Grosso and the northern states, leaving the 
outlook for a record harvest skewed to the downside. Any delay 
to the soybean cycle would also push second -crop corn planting 
beyond its ideal window, threatening yields and Brazil ’s corn ex-
ports in the second half of 2027. Some offset in South American 
grain trade may come from Argentina, where favorable rainfall is 
set to support export volumes. The United States stands to bene-
fit, as a record soybean crop and the second -largest corn crop on 
record leave it well placed to fill the gap, with a longer export win-
dow into Asia in early 2027 and stronger demand for its corn later 
in the year. The constraint is logistics rather than supply, as Pana-
ma Canal restrictions during the dry season would limit how much 
of this opportunity US exporters can capture.  
Drought in the Central American Dry Corridor could also lift short -
haul grain imports from the US Gulf, a modest gain for Handysize 
and Supramax owners. In Australia, a dry spring is already reduc-
ing wheat yields in the eastern states as the harvest begins, alt-
hough large carry-in stocks could cushion exports, including ship-
ments to China. 
Effects of the phenomenon extend as well beyond dry bulk, with a 
notable example being Indonesian palm oil. Prolonged drought 
and severe wildfires are likely to lower exports, pushing importers 
towards more distant vegetable oil origins and lengthening hauls 
for product tanker owners. 
Overall, El Niño effects on shipping spread through reshuffling of 
logistics and trade flows rather than through large swings in vol-
umes, as also past events showed. Panama is the clearest chan-
nel for dry bulk and the most likely to tighten into early 2027, 
while other important impacts include potential for uplift in ther-
mal coal demand due to declining hydropower and shifts in sea-
borne grain trade patterns.

Crude tanker freight rates continued to climb to record highs as 
sustained cargo demand met increasingly restricted vessel availa-
bility across most regions. Middle Eastern crude exports have 
recovered to around 80% of pre -war levels, yet operational ad-
justments amid disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz, including re-
routing via Sidi Kerir/SUMED and STS transfers off Oman, contin-
ue to limit effective supply. Meanwhile, scarce VLCC tonnage has 
prompted charterers to split larger cargoes into Suezmax -sized 
parcels, extending upward pressure across crude tanker seg-
ments. Against this backdrop, the BDTI surged by 32% w -o-w to 
7,625. 
 
VLCCs maintained their strong upward trend across all major 
trading regions, as brisk fixing activity and a dwindling pool of 
available vessels further reinforced owners ’ bargaining power. 
With cargo flows showing little sign of slowing, average VLCC TCE 
earnings rose by 19% on the week to $936,646/day, underscoring 
the extraordinary scale of the market's advance. 
 
Suezmax spot earnings also reached fresh highs, with average 
TCE returns rising by 13% on a weekly basis, to $699,887/day. 
The Atlantic Basin remained the principal driving force, with West 
African activity benefiting from increased VLCC cargo splitting, 
while sustained Brazilian demand continued to absorb tonnage. 
Lucrative western employment drew additional vessels towards 
the Atlantic, further depleting eastern availability and sustaining 
rates despite subdued Fujairah STS activity. The USG was a nota-
ble exception, with limited fresh enquiry keeping activity muted.  
 
Aframax gains were most pronounced in Asia and the North Sea. 
In Asia, scarce prompt tonnage and attractive Pacific returns 
strengthened owners’ leverage, while North Sea charterers raised 
bids to discourage vessels from seeking employment elsewhere. 
The Mediterranean also advanced, benefiting from stronger At-
lantic earnings and port delays, although fresh enquiry eased to-
wards the weekend. Aframax TCE earnings soared by 39% w -o-w 
to $384,665/day.

The dry bulk market saw uneven trends last week. The BDI aver-
aged 6% lower on the week, primarily reflecting the correction in 
Capesize rates. 
Capesize earnings declined amid persistent Atlantic weakness, 
despite comparatively resilient conditions in the Pacific. Excess 
vessel supply and an increasing focus on later loading dates put 
the South Atlantic under sustained pressure, while limited enquiry 
kept the North Atlantic subdued. Pacific iron ore activity offered a 
brief counterweight, lifting rates, before momentum faded to-
wards the weekend. 
After a hesitant start, Kamsarmax/Panamax rates edged higher 
as cargo demand picked up across both basins. Increased transat-
lantic activity and South American fronthaul interest improved the 
Atlantic outlook, though abundant open tonnage capped further 
gains. Pacific business also revived, driven by post -holiday Chi-
nese demand for Australian and North Pacific cargoes, while mut-
ed Indian interest in  coal cargoes kept the recovery measured.  
Ultramax/Supramax market remained tepid, with marginal rate 
movements despite contrasting regional developments. The US 
Gulf and East Coast South America faced sluggish demand, 
whereas Continent scrap cargoes continued to generate attrac-
tive returns. The Pacific fared somewhat better, benefiting from 
North Pacific grain shipments and additional enquiry from Indo-
nesia and Vietnam. Despite muted overall activity, tightening 
prompt tonnage availability sustained cautious optimism for late 
October. 
Handysize segment exhibited divergent regional trends last week, 
with the South Atlantic emerging as a pocket of support, as 
strong cargo flows and tightening vessel supply underpinned 
rates. Conversely, the US Gulf remained under pressure, with in-
sufficient enquiry to absorb the extensive tonnage list. The Conti-
nent and Mediterranean remained broadly stable, with little 
movement in freight levels, while holiday -related disruptions cur-
tailed Pacific activity without materially affecting rates.

Last week, the recycling market held its ground, characterized 
by limited pool of candidates and firm demand for larger ves-
sels. Owners are keeping older ships in the market, while earn-
ings and second-hand values stay high.  
Alang remained almost unchanged : constrained supply and 
strong demand. Local plate prices eased. Weakening of local 
currency eroded purchasing power for further acquisitions, 
however, candidates remain scarce and activity subdued. Indian 
recyclers are still accepting sanctioned units, ignoring any po-
tential implication of US sanctions. On the economy side, RBI 
increased interest rates by 25 basis points to 5.50%, lifting fi-
nancing costs and adding to steel buyers’ concerns. 
Chattogram’s activity reflects scarcity rather than improving 
fundamentals. Domestic steel prices increased, while Taka 
weakened marginally against the US dollar. On the waterfront, 
activity was focused on smaller units among bulkers and LNG 
tankers. Part of the buying comes from breakers seeking to 
meet HKC requirements. Letters of Credit delays are still an is-
sue, as central bank over -invoicing checks on large amounts 
slow prompt delivery of funds. 
Gadani’s sentiment was similar to last week. The market is sub-
dued and buyers are cautious about acquiring further tonnage, 
as plots were already occupied. In the steel market, prices re-
ported a slight decrease. The local currency market remained 
stable against the US dollar. A few candidates on the dry and 
tanker segment were circulated, though no sales were reported. 
On the economy side, IMF approved 1.2billion USD funding for 
the national government, supporting further the economy. 
Aliaga stayed close to recent levels, though domestic steel pric-
es are struggling to hold their level. The lira continues to depre-
ciate against the USD and financing costs are high, weighing on 
sentiment. Supply is thin and recyclers stay selective, favoring 
quality EU-flagged and nearby tonnage.
