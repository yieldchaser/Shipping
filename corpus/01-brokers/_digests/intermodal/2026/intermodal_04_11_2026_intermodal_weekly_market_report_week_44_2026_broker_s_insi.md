Week 44 | Tuesday 03rd November 2026Market Insight 
By Nikos Tagoulis, Head of Research Department
With the winter heating season underway, the thermal coal 
market is benefiting from strengthening demand, as elevated 
natural gas prices reinforce coal’s cost competitiveness in 
power generation. Qatari LNG volumes remain sharply reduced, 
while European natural gas prices have returned to levels last 
seen during the 2022 energy crisis, with Dutch TTF prices 
recently exceeding €100/MWh and EU gas inventories below 
their 5-year average. These conditions have improved coal-
fired generation economics, encouraging gas-to-coal switching 
across parts of Europe and Asia. Nevertheless, seaborne 
thermal coal trade is expected to remain broadly stable in 2026, 
as additional demand from fuel switching is largely offset by 
supply constraints.
Beyond this short-term supportive backdrop, the broader 
outlook remains uncertain. China's seaborne thermal coal 
imports are forecast to decline, pressured by elevated 
international prices and expectations of recovering domestic 
production. Although mine inspections and safety restrictions 
have constrained output in recent months, government 
directives to accelerate mining activity in Shanxi, which 
accounts for approximately 30% of Chinese coal production, 
point to improving domestic availability. Together with 
expanding renewable generation, this could further reduce 
China's import requirements in 2027, while Japan and South 
Korea may also face longer-term demand slowdowns amid 
continued decarbonization efforts. Meanwhile, India and ASEAN 
economies, particularly Vietnam and the Philippines, remain 
drivers of coal consumption growth, supported by rising 
electricity demand and industrial activity. In India, weaker 
hydropower generation amid El Niño-linked monsoon 
shortfalls, increased industrial demand and restocking have 
supported seaborne imports, although government efforts to 
expand domestic coal production aim to reduce reliance on 
imported supplies.
On the supply side, Indonesia, the world’s largest thermal coal 
exporter, continues to face constraints. Although production 
quotas were revised upwards in October, approved output 
remains well below last year’s levels. At the same time, 
domestic market obligations require miners to prioritise local 
supply, leaving fewer tons for export. Supply has been further 
squeezed by El Niño-driven low river levels in Central 
Kalimantan, which have limited barge movements to terminals, 
tightening export availability. Australia, which together with 
Indonesia accounts for close to 70% of global thermal coal 
exports, is expected to record broadly flat volumes, with limited 
upside as its traditional North Asian buyers are set to reduce 
imports over the medium term.
As these conditions create opportunities for alternative 
suppliers, Colombia is strengthening its position in the Atlantic 
thermal coal market, benefiting from renewed European 
demand amid elevated natural gas prices. According to LSEG, 
European thermal coal imports surged 79% y-o-y in October, 
while cumulative imports rose 31% during January–October. 
Over the same ten-month period, Colombian shipments to 
Europe increased 46%, maintaining a market share of around 
60% and underscoring the country's importance as a key coal 
supplier.
Overall, Colombian thermal coal exports increased by 
approximately 11% during January-October and are poised for 
further growth of around 8% in 2027, although their 
geographical distribution has shifted. Weaker Asian demand 
has been offset by stronger purchases from European and 
regional Atlantic buyers. Mexico has emerged as a key 
destination, alongside growing demand from Central American 
and Caribbean markets, while firmer Indonesian prices have 
improved Colombian coal's competitiveness. For dry bulk 
shipping, however, the benefits of expanding Colombian 
exports are somewhat tempered by evolving trade patterns. 
While higher shipment volumes support cargo demand, the 
increasing orientation towards Atlantic Basin destinations 
reduces average sailing distances compared with Asian routes, 
potentially constraining ton-mile growth relative to physical 
trade expansion.
Furthermore, following last August’s change in administration, 
Colombia has adopted a more supportive stance towards the 
coal sector, reopening export channel to Israel by lifting the 
previous government’s ban and signalling renewed support for 
exploration and mining investment. Nevertheless, significant 
operational risks persist. Repeated attacks on the railway 
linking the Cerrejón mine to its coastal export terminal, 
including a derailment in late September, alongside recurring 
community blockades, continue to threaten the reliability of 
coal shipments.
Despite these logistical and security challenges, Colombia is 
poised to expand its footprint in global coal exports, benefiting 
from favourable gas-to-coal economics, a near-term shift in 
energy demand towards coal and uncertainty surrounding 
Indonesian shipments, while a more conducive domestic policy 
environment helps it capitalize on emerging export 
opportunities.

The crude tanker spot market showed diverging trends over the 
week, with VLCCs staging a recovery while the other tanker 
classes suffered losses. Against this backdrop, the BDTI 
averaged 7,111, down 2% w-o-w.
The VLCC segment regained much of the ground lost in recent 
weeks, driven by a surge in demand, particularly East of Suez, 
and increasingly restricted vessel availability. The Middle East 
led the upturn, with active fixing off Oman absorbing prompt 
tonnage, while speculation over potential US military action 
against Iran following the elections may have encouraged 
charterers to bring forward their requirements. In West Africa, 
lackluster enquiry was offset by vessels heading east, 
underpinning freight levels. The USG also posted gains, 
benefiting from healthy cargo flows and eastbound ballasting. 
Average TCE earnings rose by 9% on a weekly basis, to 
$829,398/day.
On the Suezmax side, insufficient cargo enquiry led to 
widespread rate declines, with competition from other tanker 
classes adding to the weakness in certain trades. Despite 
steady Mediterranean activity and some interest in CPC 
cargoes, TD6 retreated. In West Africa, limited fixing 
opportunities weighed on freight, with occasional Guyana 
requirements proving inadequate to reverse the trend. The 
Arabian Gulf showed greater resilience, buoyed by healthy 
activity off Fujairah, while persistent geopolitical uncertainty 
continued to shape owners’ employment strategies. Suezmax 
TCE earnings fell by 11% w-o-w to $570,090/day.
Aframax markets initially benefited from weather-related 
delays and limited prompt tonnage in the Mediterranean and 
North Sea. However, as position lists lengthened and fresh 
cargo enquiry remained subdued, competition among owners 
intensified, triggering corrections, particularly in the Med. The 
US Gulf remained quiet, although expectations of increased 
activity later in November continued to keep vessels in the 
region. In Asia, tepid demand and ample vessel supply kept 
rates under pressure. Average Aframax TCE earnings declined 
by 10% on the week, to $303,147/day.

Capesize spot rates extended their gains for a second 
consecutive week, led by a strengthening Atlantic market, as 
Pacific activity also gathered pace. Increased cargo demand 
from South Brazil and West Africa to China reduced the pool of 
prompt vessels, with charterers shifting their attention towards 
December shipments. Fresh North Atlantic enquiries provided 
further support, particularly on fronthaul routes. In the Pacific, 
greater interest in open vessels pushed rates higher despite 
restrained miner activity, although fixing slowed towards the 
close, prompting some easing in offers.
Subdued demand continued to weigh on the 
Kamsarmax/Panamax market, with the Atlantic bearing the 
brunt of the downturn. Limited cargo enquiries and a growing 
number of open vessels strengthened charterers’ bargaining 
position, leaving owners seeking scarce employment 
opportunities. Conditions were particularly challenging off East 
Coast South America, where weak demand coincided with an 
influx of ballasters from the Indian Ocean. The Pacific proved 
somewhat more resilient, with Indonesian shipments providing 
support, whereas a relatively short list of open vessels helped 
limit rate declines in the North Pacific despite sluggish trading.
Overall, Ultramax/Supramax earnings recorded modest losses 
last week amid divergent regional trends. Northern Asian 
demand sustained Pacific earnings, despite slower business in 
the South Pacific and declining returns in the Indian Ocean. 
Across the Atlantic, dwindling grain and petcoke volumes 
curtailed employment prospects in the US Gulf. Continental 
voyages continued to yield healthy returns, although freight 
rates for scrap shipments retreated from earlier highs. 
Meanwhile, chartering interest in the Mediterranean was 
concentrated mainly in the east.
Handysize earnings edged lower last week, despite gains in 
certain regional markets. Northern Pacific trades recorded 
modest increases, whereas the Mediterranean struggled to 
attract fresh chartering interest. The US Gulf weakened after 
midweek amid an excess of tonnage. East Coast South America 
proved more resilient, although slower fixing activity prevented 
further advances. Meanwhile, the Continent maintained its firm 
footing, benefiting from a relatively small pool of vessels 
positioned near loading ports.

Last week, ship recycling conditions were relatively stable, with 
mixed sentiment across the main destinations. Bangladesh 
stood out stronger, while India and Pakistan held steady, as 
recyclers kept a selective approach.
Alang’s sentiment remained unchanged, as the festive season 
continued to weigh on trading activity. Local steel prices 
declined, as steel demand remained sluggish. The Indian rupee 
edged higher against the USD, supported by RBI’s liquidity 
tightening measures, ahead of interest rate hike expectations 
to combat inflation. Few suitable LNG and tanker recycling 
candidates have been circulated to the market, as recyclers 
maintained their selective approach.
Chattogram firmed, with competitive buying interest emerging, 
despite limited supply of vessels. Local steel prices rose slightly, 
as the market returned from holidays with little momentum. 
Local currency weakened against the USD. Limited candidates 
were circulated, as yards were busy with ongoing 
commitments. On the policy front, authorized recycling facilities 
increased to 39 and the national ship recycling industry has 
been upgraded from red to orange environmental category.
Gadani was quiet. However, there was cautious interest in 
acquiring suitable tonnage, amid supply shortage. Local steel 
market stayed subdued, with steel prices declining. Local 
currency saw little changes, as it slightly gained against the 
USD. A few bulk carriers were circulated, but breakers showed 
limited appetite. On the economy side, the central bank raised 
interest rates by 50 basis points to 12%, as inflation rose to 
11.32% y-o-y.
Aliaga continued to see limited trading activity. Local steel 
prices are expected to increase as steel demand picks up, 
despite the two-day national holiday.
