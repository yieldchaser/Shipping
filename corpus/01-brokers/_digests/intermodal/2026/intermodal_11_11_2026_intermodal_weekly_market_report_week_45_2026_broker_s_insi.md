Week 45 | Tuesday 10th November 2026Market Insight 
By Nikos Tagoulis, Head of Research Department
The Democratic Party’s victory in the US midterm elections, 
securing control of both chambers of Congress from January 
2027, is expected to influence Washington’s foreign, energy, 
trade and maritime policies. Democrats will gain greater 
leverage over legislation, government spending and 
congressional oversight. Beyond these institutional powers, the 
election outcome itself could prompt President Trump to 
reassess certain policy priorities, even without direct 
Democratic pressure, as the administration considers the 
political and economic implications of the result. Nevertheless, 
Trump retains substantial executive authority, and the extent of 
any policy adjustments remains uncertain.
Among the issues with the most immediate implications for 
shipping is the conflict involving Iran. Democrats are expected 
to intensify pressure for diplomatic de-escalation and greater 
congressional oversight of military operations, potentially 
supporting efforts to restore safe navigation through Hormuz. 
However, ending military operations, securing maritime 
passage and adjusting sanctions on Iranian petroleum remain 
separate policy decisions, with progress dependent on 
negotiations between Washington and Tehran.
Any sustained improvement in maritime security around 
Hormuz could have significant implications for energy and 
commodity trade. A reopening of the Strait could facilitate the 
recovery of Middle Eastern exports, easing energy prices, war-
risk premiums and transportation costs. Qatar’s LNG exports 
could gradually recover, although the extent and timing of 
production restoration remain uncertain following Iranian 
strikes on liquefaction infrastructure. A recovery in cargo 
volumes could support tanker demand, while the normalization 
of trading patterns and vessel availability could moderate 
exceptionally high freight rates and reduce ton mile demand. 
For dry bulk, some of the most notable market effects could 
include a recovery in regional fertilizer exports, while the 
resumption of LNG exports and lower gas prices could reduce 
gas-to-coal switching and thermal coal imports in some 
markets, potentially weighing on thermal coal trade.
Black Sea represents another area of interest for shipping. 
Democrats have prioritized increased assistance to Ukraine, 
although greater congressional support would not necessarily 
accelerate a ceasefire, with any lasting settlement dependent 
on developments between Russia and Ukraine. For shipowners, 
the restoration of reliable commercial navigation, port 
operations and export corridors would remain central to any 
agreement.
Beyond these geopolitical developments, the Democratic 
victory could influence US trade policy, with Democrats 
potentially favouring more selective tariffs, closer cooperation 
with traditional allies and targeted measures supporting 
strategic US industries, rather than the broad-based tariffs 
introduced under President Trump. In the maritime sector, the 
suspension of US port fees affecting Chinese-built and 
Chinese-linked vessels has been extended until 10 January 
2027, shortly after the new Democratic Congress takes office, 
potentially prompting early congressional scrutiny of its scope 
and implementation, although final decisions remain with the 
administration.
Furthermore, maritime decarbonization represents another 
area where Democratic influence could become more 
pronounced. Democrats generally favour cleaner energy and 
stronger international environmental cooperation and could 
advocate renewed US engagement with the IMO’s proposed 
Net-Zero Framework, while also promoting greater investment 
in alternative marine fuels, lower-emission vessels and greener 
shipping practices.
These environmental priorities could also shape domestic US 
energy policy, with potential consequences for seaborne energy 
trade. Democrats may seek to expand renewable energy while 
advocating greater environmental scrutiny of future offshore oil 
exploration and new LNG export authorizations, potentially 
influencing the longer-term outlook for US fossil-fuel 
production and exports. Meanwhile, the Jones Act and efforts to 
revitalize domestic shipbuilding appear to retain bipartisan 
support, suggesting greater continuity in US maritime industrial 
policy. With the current Jones Act waiver due to expire on 15 
November, attention could also turn to the potential extension 
of exemptions.
Overall, the Democratic victory could gradually influence the 
direction of US foreign, trade, energy and maritime policies, 
although the immediate implications for shipping will remain 
closely tied to geopolitical developments and the 
administration’s policy decisions. A more comprehensive shift 
could emerge from 2029 if Democrats also secure the 
presidency in the November 2028 election.

Crude tanker freight markets surrendered the previous week’s 
gains, with all three segments recording substantial losses. The 
BDTI averaged 6,128, down 14% w-o-w.
VLCC sentiment deteriorated across both basins, with subdued 
cargo demand, mounting vessel availability and competition 
from Suezmax tonnage undermining owners’ bargaining 
position. The Atlantic was particularly vulnerable, as enquiry in 
the Americas diminished over the course of the week, while 
West African charterers increasingly favoured more economical 
Suezmax alternatives. In the AG, earlier resilience gradually 
gave way to softer conditions, despite sustained interest out of 
Fujairah. The approaching Bahri Week, traditionally associated 
with reduced visible fixing activity, further contributed to a 
cautious trading environment. Average VLCC TCE earnings 
retreated 12% w-o-w to $731,473/day.
Suezmaxes experienced an even steeper downturn, with the 
Atlantic bearing the brunt of an increasingly unfavorable 
balance between demand and vessel availability. In the Black 
Sea, charterers showed little urgency to commit, while failed 
fixtures returned additional ships to an already crowded 
market. West Africa offered little relief, as sparse employment 
opportunities left owners competing for a dwindling pool of 
cargoes. Conditions were similarly subdued in the Middle East, 
where incoming ballasters further expanded the tonnage 
supply against a backdrop of limited demand. Average Suezmax 
TCE earnings plunged 40% w-o-w to $339,785/day.
Aframax freight also came under considerable strain, 
particularly in the Atlantic, where earlier momentum faded 
amid a shortage of fresh cargoes. North Sea owners faced 
increasingly limited employment opportunities as the pull from 
the Mediterranean weakened, while the US Gulf failed to 
generate sufficient activity to ease regional vessel availability. 
In the Mediterranean, charterers’ reluctance to commit, coupled 
with accumulating prompt tonnage, drove freight progressively 
lower, although renewed enquiry towards the weekend offered 
some respite. Asia proved somewhat more resilient, with 
occasional fixtures helping to reduce open tonnage, albeit 
without reversing the broader decline. Average Aframax TCE 
earnings fell 42% w-o-w to $175,504/day.

Capesize earnings advanced over the week, primarily driven by 
a stronger Atlantic market. The North Atlantic benefited from 
robust transatlantic business and renewed fronthaul 
requirements, with a midweek increase in iron ore shipments 
from Brazil and West Africa providing an additional boost to 
freight before activity moderated. The Pacific delivered a 
weaker performance, as miners remained active but price-
sensitive, leaving rates below their opening levels.
The Kamsarmax/Panamax market strengthened amid healthy 
cargo demand, with the Atlantic recording the most notable 
gains. Steady South American shipments and increased interest 
in transatlantic and fronthaul voyages reduced prompt tonnage 
availability in the West Mediterranean, although the North 
Continent remained well supplied. Australian mineral exports 
and consistent Indonesian business also provided support in 
the Pacific. However, an expanding tonnage list in the North 
Pacific, combined with slower enquiry, dampened regional 
sentiment.
The Ultramax/Supramax segment experienced a subdued 
week, as holidays and Eisbein Week in Hamburg contributed to 
reduced trading activity. Freight rates weakened in the North 
Pacific and Southeast Asia amid growing vessel availability, 
whereas cargo interest in the Continent and Mediterranean 
remained limited, despite relatively tight tonnage supply in the 
latter. The Indian Ocean faced significant pressure due to a lack 
of fresh enquiries. North America, however, offered a brighter 
spot, with rates beginning to recover.
Finally, Handysizes recorded uneven performance across 
regions, with gains and losses largely offsetting each other and 
leaving average TCE earnings broadly unchanged. In the US 
Gulf, increasing cargo demand and a shortage of available 
vessels lifted rates, while Chinese general cargo and Korean 
steel shipments strengthened the Far East Pacific. Conversely, 
limited cargo opportunities held back the South Atlantic, and 
trading across the wider Asian market remained sluggish. In the 
Mediterranean, improving activity and reduced vessel 
availability gradually shifted market conditions in owners’ 
favour.

Last week, ship recycling market was steady, though sentiment 
diverged across the four main destinations. Bangladesh is 
leading on consistent buying appetite and India remained 
resilient, while Pakistan and Turkey stayed subdued.
Alang remains firm. Local steel market continues to be slow, 
amid the Diwali festive period, with prices moving lower. Local 
currency appreciated, though, against the USD, after the US 
mid-term elections. Candidate tonnage on offer remained 
scarce. On the policy front, the European Parliament’s 
Environmental Committee voted against the proposal to 
include the 2 Indian yards on the EU approval list, citing 
concerns over beaching methods, although a final decision is 
still pending.
Chattogram continues to have a positive sentiment, with a 
steady buying interest in conventional tonnage. Local steel 
prices increased, though the steel market still lacks clear 
direction. Local currency weakened further against the USD, as 
state-backed entities progressed with sizeable payments for oil 
imports, adding to local demand for USD. On the waterfront, 
only limited number of vessels were circulated. The onset of the 
dry season, coupled with an expectation of recovery in 
construction, could provide support for steel consumption and 
yard activity.
 Gadani remained unchanged, with no urgent appetite for 
tonnage amid slow local markets. Domestic plate prices are 
holding steady, while grey-market imports of Iranian steel 
weigh on breakers. Local exchange rate was also broadly stable. 
Waterfront activity remained thin. In policy, Pakistan reached to 
6 authorized ship recycling facilities by HKC and DASR 
requirements.
 Aliaga buyers remain selective amid weak transactional 
activity. The domestic market is still under pressure from 
cautious steel demand, currency depreciation and high inflation.
