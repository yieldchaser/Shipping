### WEEK 35 · August 28th, 2026

## MARKET OVERVIEW

Diplomatic discussions between regional authorities have introduced a new framework for managing the Strait of Hormuz, outlining a roadmap toward a temporary navigational channel and coordinated mine- clearing initiatives. Under this structure, negotiators are evaluating a dual-lane transit corridor where vessels would enter the Persian Gulf through northern designated waters and exit via southern lanes, followed by further discussions to establish permanent operational and security arrangements. However, significant hurdles remain, particularly regarding demands to close the existing southern route along the Omani coastline and potential methods for maritime service administration. With stakeholders continuing to reject any framework that imposes passage conditions or compromises absolute freedom of navigation, fleet operators remain cautious about an immediate, unencumbered return to the waterway.

Despite the persistent threat, crude outflows from the Persian Gulf have quietly rebounded as regional producers utilise shuttle networks to move shut-in volumes onto the global market. Daily volumes passing out of the region have climbed back toward an estimated 6~8 million barrels, supported by major loading surges across key export facilities in Iraq, Qatar, Kuwait, and Saudi Arabia. To circumvent security risks, producers are increasingly relying on dedicated shuttle tankers to transport crude just beyond the strait, where cargoes are transferred to awaiting international carriers anchored off coastal bunkering hubs. This revival of seaborne flows has exerted downward pressure on international crude benchmarks, driving futures back toward US$88 a barrel and delivering the sharpest weekly price softening in two months.

This model has created unprecedented profitability for tanker operators willing to navigate the high-risk zone, with freight earnings for VLCCs touching historic highs. Energy majors and trading houses are capitalising on significant price discounts on inland barrels, absorbing elevated shipping costs that equate to roughly US$10 per barrel to deliver cargoes to worldwide refining hubs. However, the market remains sharply bifurcated; while crude supply chains are gradually adapting and softening prompt oil benchmarks, refined product markets continue to face severe tightness.

With substantial regional refining capacity still offline and international processing assets facing overseas disruptions, downstream fuel segments like diesel and gasoline are seeing sustained upward margin pressure, highlighting an uneven recovery across the global maritime trade.

## DRY BULK | BDI: 3,186 ▲12.14% (w-o-w)

Dry bulk freight market demonstrated robust upward momentum, supported by tightening fleet availability and fresh weather disruptions across Asia even as critical maritime corridors face severe geopolitical strain. Ongoing conflicts have significantly constrained Black Sea agricultural shipments—with August wheat exports expected to fall well below 2.5 million tons compared to 6.3 million tons during the same period last year—while the blockage of the Strait of Hormuz has stalled crucial fertiliser trade and left an estimated 120 to 150 bulk carriers immobilised.

The benchmark BDI advanced for five consecutive sessions, climbing to reach 3,056 points. Larger vessel segments spearheaded the mid-week surge as Typhoon Narra disrupted key South China Sea trade routes, propelling the Capesize index up xx% to 5,033 points with average daily earnings rising to reach US$42,148. In tandem, the Panamax index increased to 2,246 points, lifting daily returns to US$20,213, while the Supramax index maintained a steady footing by edging up to 1,644 points.

### Segment Highlights

**Capesize:** Capesize rebounded this week, driven by a surge in Western Australian iron ore shipments alongside prompt tonnage tightening as ballasters maneuvered around another incoming typhoon off southeast China. Active participation from major miners pushed Pacific C5 rates higher, while steady Guinean bauxite and iron ore stems helped absorb long-haul capacity to keep the Atlantic market well supported. Reflecting these firm market conditions, spot returns posted solid gains across key benchmarks, lifting Transatlantic rates to US$48,450’s and Pacific r/v to US$44,750’s per day.

**Panamax / Kamsarmax:** Panamax experienced a mixed week as the 5TC average fell under the weight of regional tonnage overcapacity and ballast vessel inflows, even as grain demand showed signs of recovery. Route rates diverged, with Inni R/V climbing to US$16,070’s while F/H dropped sharply to US$27,060’s. Although improving grain volumes and firmer paper provide downside support, spot rates are likely to remain range-bound until surplus Pacific tonnage clears and coal shipments rebound.

**Supramax / Ultramax:** Supramax edged up this week as steady long-haul cargo flows from the South Atlantic, the USG, and NOPAC helped absorb prompt vessel capacity. While the overall market maintained a balanced yet subdued tone, ongoing vessel oversupply across Europe and the Mediterranean alongside reduced Indonesian coal exports capped more broad-based momentum. Reflecting these modest regional gains, spot returns nudged upward on key routes, lifting Pacific r/v to US$16,450’s and Transatlantic to US$27,050’s.

**Handysize:** Handysize displayed a modestly firmer tone this week, supported by improving cargo volumes in EC South America and better forward sentiment in the USG, even as the Mediterranean faced persistent pressure from ample tonnage availability. Across the Pacific, Asian routes held relatively steady, drawing underlying support from pockets of fresh activity and tight prompt vessel supply. Reflecting these modest regional gains, spot returns edged upward across key routes, lifting Inter-Pacific runs to US$16,200’s and fronthaul trips to US$11,600’s per day.

## TANKERS | BDTI: 2,777 ▼+9.56% (w-o-w)

The prolonged regional disruptions continue to generate remarkable freight earnings for crude carriers, keeping owner returns exceptionally high. During the second quarter, spot earnings for VLCC’s reached an average of US$126,800’s per day, nearly tripling the US$44,981 recorded in the same period last year, and have maintained solid momentum into the third quarter at US$125,400’s per day with 83% of available fleet days already secured.

Meanwhile, regional monitoring groups emphasise that Iranian naval units maintain steady surveillance and drone flights to assert authority along the Straits. Amid these tensions, daily crossings through the Hormuz nudged up to 10 vessels on Wednesday from 8 on Tuesday—comprising inbound passages by 2 fuel tankers, an LPG carrier, a Panamax, and 3 Handymax ships, alongside 3 outbound vessels carrying fuel, bitumen, and dry bulk—though activity still trails the recent 10-day baseline of 15 ships. Crude prices softened slightly this week, as high-level discussions in Tehran led by Qatar’s Prime Minister continue to explore potential commercial transit arrangements.

**Suezmax:** West Africa saw its strong momentum last week took a correction, propelled by fulfilled Atlantic crude demand for early-September dates. Nigeria/UKC closed lower as WS237 as an oversupplied prompt position list and cheaper stateside Aframaxes encourage charterers to hold back.

**Aframax:** Aframax saw a firm week, supported by soaring rates in the larger segments driving spillover demand towards Aframax tonnage as a viable substitute. In the Mediterranean, consistent inquiry extending into early September and the steady outflow of vessels ballasting toward the U.S. continue to deplete available tonnage, as 80,000mt Ceyhan/Lavera settled higher at WS268.

## CONTAINERS

Container shipping market continued its climb this week as the SCFI rose to 3,509.54 points. Spot rates were primarily underpinned by tight vessel capacity and resilient demand across the Americas and South America, where carrier-initiated General Rate Increases and Peak Season Surcharges lifted East Coast rates by 9.6% and West Coast rates by 13.2%. Severe typhoon disruptions and equipment bottlenecks at Chinese departure hubs further restricted short-sea turnaround times, causing Southeast Asian freight rates to jump sharply.

Meanwhile, severe operational constraints across the Persian Gulf continue to keep Middle East rate baselines firm, as functional limitations at Jebel Ali have diverted cargo flows into neighbouring hubs such as Fujairah, Khor Fakkan, and Oman. This prolonged friction is actively reshaping global trade patterns, as reflected in Alphaliner’s first-half port rankings: Dubai plummeted from 10th to 32nd place after handling

3.14 million TEU compared to 7.77 million TEU the previous year, and Abu Dhabi’s Khalifa Port saw volumes tumble by over 50%. In contrast, Malaysia’s Port Klang stepped into 10th position with an estimated 7.7 million TEU, while Los Angeles and Long Beach held 9th place with a combined 9.97 million TEU. Leading the list, Shanghai retained the top position by handling 28.74 million TEU (up 6.2%), while Ningbo- Zhoushan grew 8.8% to 22.90 million TEU, aided by its Jintang Phase II expansion, which expanded volumes by 23.4% and added 17 international routes, narrowly overtaking Singapore (22.74 million TEU, up 4.7%) for second place.

### Market Insights

**Alang, India:** Alang maintained a steady week, with keen buying interest driving a noticeable improvement in sentiment. Supported by an active recovery in local scrap demand, domestic plate prices settled at 41,000 rupees/MT by Friday. This provides yard operators with a welcome margin cushion compared to the US$423 mark seen at the start of the period. While Indian buyers still have some ground to cover before matching the higher pricing levels offered for conventional bulkers and containers in Bangladesh and Pakistan, the sustained uptick in local steel values and healthy plot fundamentals offer genuine encouragement as the market heads toward the fourth quarter.

This is reinforced by solid macroeconomic fundamentals across the broader Indian economy. The Reserve Bank of India continues to maintain an accommodative policy stance with the benchmark repo rate steady at 5.25%, backed by a robust full-year economic growth projection of 6.7% and an expanding foreign exchange reserve cushion near US$707 billion.

Domestic steel consumption is projected to expand by over 9% this year, powered by heavy government capital outlays in transportation infrastructure, railway modernisation, and urban housing schemes. As seasonal monsoon rains taper off and construction activity accelerates across the country, this expanding industrial appetite for rerollable scrap provides Alang’s recyclers with a reliable domestic floor to pursue fresh tonnage with increasing confidence.

## COMMODITIES, BUNKERS & RATES

### Commodities Focus

**Copper** futures advanced above US$6.7 per pound to establish a fresh historical peak, driven by persistent global supply constraints and sustained cargo diversions toward the United States ahead of potential tariff decisions. This upward momentum is further reinforced by London Metal Exchange inventories shrinking by nearly half since mid-May, acute concentrate shortages among Chinese smelters, and flooding at the Kamoa-Kakula operation in the Democratic Republic of Congo, which threatens to trim Zijin Mining's production share by as much as 57,000 tons this year.

In the ferrous complex, raw material prices are beginning to decouple from finished steel; **iron ore** futures moved higher toward CNY 720 per ton, recovering from 14-month lows as mills increased blast furnace utilisation in anticipation of seasonal pre-winter restocking and supportive policy guidance from Beijing.

**Steel** rebar futures dropped to a four-week low of approximately CNY 3,070 per ton under the weight of high inventory levels, lower year-on-year home prices in July, and compressed margins that left only about one-third of domestic steelmakers profitable at month-end, though directives from the National Development and Reform Commission to accelerate major construction projects continue to provide an underlying floor for long-term consumption.
