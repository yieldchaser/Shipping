## Market Report Content

 
   
 
Clarkson Hellas Ltd. 
 
Desk Talk 
Summer seasonality has brought a broader slowdown in market activity, consistent with the typical 
seasonal pattern. Against this backdrop, the Strait of Hormuz picture remains little changed: transits stay 
well below pre -crisis levels as markets watch for progress in Iran -Oman talks. Flow is concentrated on 
discounted Iraqi crude drawing Asian refiner demand, while Iran -Oman's agreement on new routing 
coordinates offers a modest positive signal, though Tehran maintains Washington's stance remains the 
key hur dle to reopening. Net effect: continued uncertainty weighing on sentiment, with owners still 
pricing elevated risk into asset values. 
 
The tone shifts on the S&P side,  where activity varies by segm ent. Purchase inquiries remain heavily 
concentrated in the Supramax segment, which continues to see a high volume of inquiries, particularly 
for 15–20-year-old tonnage, followed by Handysize, with buyer appetite in these segments still reluctant 
to meet current asking premiums. Capesize stands out as the notable exception, with freight rates and 
FFAs trading at elevated levels fueling strong S&P interest and firmer pricing in the sector.  
 
On the tanker side, interest remains strong, though buyers are moving a bit more deliberately, taking 
their time to make the "right" decision given the high price levels being asked. VLCCs, Suezmaxes, and 
Aframaxes continue to draw considerab le interest, with buyer appetite still reluctant to meet current  
premiums outright — though some transactions are concluding at more realistic, although still historically 
elevated levels relative to prevailing offer ideas. 
 
 
 
 
Capesize activity saw a Japanese -owned unit change hands this week: the "Orange Tiger" (181,399 
DWT, 2011-built at Imabari Saijo) found a new owner at an undisclosed level, understood to be in the 
upper USD 30 million bracket.  
 
In the Kamsarmax segment, Velos Dry's clients concluded the sale of the scrubber -fitted "Velos Jasper" 
(82,167 DWT, 2012-built at Tsuneishi Tadotsu) at roughly USD 23.5 million. 
 
 Meanwhile, a 1999-built Handysize, the "Rong Fu" (28,419 DWT, Imabari SB), moved between Chinese 
hands at a price of USD 4.8 million, reflecting its age and smaller size relative to the other reported deals. 
 
 
 
Although we understand several negotiations to be ongoing, there are no new tanker sales to report 
this week.
