starting to restrict movements and transits into the Red Sea. Owners willing to transit

a flash in the pan last week with not many ships actually getting fully fixed.

mystery list this morning, along with more enquiries on the sheet, given the overall

North Sea
Market has quietly been covering the 1st half of the month with freight levels
remaining relatively steady. Ballasting has slowed with softer surrounding markets but
is still attractive rather than accepting waiting time. The Suezmax market has softened
which may rekindle charterers' appetite for larger vessels with Aframax activity
potentially coming under pressure. Market looks like it will remain steady/soft through
mid month unless we see a jump in activity.
Mediterranean
Very busy Mediterranean market after the corrections of last week. Rates have found a
floor and are hovering around last-done levels, but the position list is slowly becoming
thinner. Volumes are remaining high as Syria and Sidi are pumping additional volumes
at the moment keeping the market interesting.

The Capesize market began the week on a solid footing and has continued to
strengthen throughout the week setting a positive trajectory. South Atlantic and West
Africa continue to underpin improvements on C3 and healthy demand also pushed
rates on C5 West Australia/China. In the Atlantic, the charge was led again this week
on C3 Brazil/China with a flurry of fixtures in the low-mid USD 35s yesterday and
today we see owners bidding in the mid USD 35s up to USD 36 for Brazil and West

Panamax
Supramax
Rates
16.5-17 and one fixture was reported at low to mid USD 16s. First half September
stems see offers ease to high USD 15 levels.
The Panamax market firmed steadily through the week, with momentum broadening
across both basins and owners increasingly able to hold their ground. In the Atlantic,
the fronthaul grain trade remained the principal driver – a healthy flow of South
American and US Gulf cargoes against a tightening tonnage list kept sentiment
constructive and lifted end-month levels, even against continued charterer resistance.
The Pacific firmed in tandem, underpinned by tight prompt tonnage, a steadier stream
of North Pacific grain alongside Australian and Indonesian coal, and some weather-
related disruption that lent owners additional confidence. Period interest was active
and the bid-offer gap narrowed over the week, though owners continued to price
forward cover with a firm hand and a modest premium on shorter durations. With
cargo replenishment healthy in both basins and the forward curve holding a shallow
backwardation, we enter next week on a constructive footing.
The Supramax and Handymax markets remained relatively stable this week. The US
Gulf continued to see the highest activity, while the Continent/Mediterranean
remained balanced despite limited fresh enquiry. South America softened slightly as
cargo volumes eased, and Asia remained quiet with rates edging lower. Overall, the
market remains steady, although fresh cargo demand will be needed for further
improvement.
