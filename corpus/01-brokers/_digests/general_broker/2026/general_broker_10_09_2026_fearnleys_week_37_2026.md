Suezmax
The VLCC positions list is now so tight that no one would be too surprised if we see
the WS 400 mark breached for a Fujairah/East run off a prompt-ish position before
long, crazy as it may sound. Speed blindness does not even begin to describe the
current mood. However, the oil still needs to get out through the Strait of Hormuz and
Iranians have increased efforts to stop that from happening. It’s a fragile state of
affairs. Atlantic/East rates have followed suit as refiners look for supply security
regardless of higher oil prices. Ship values have also skyrocketed in tandem with the
spot market with reports of newbuilding resales at USD 200 million- and 16–17-year-
olds more than half of that. Fasten seat belts.


Aframax
Rates
The majority of the publicly reported West Africa cargoes have either fixed away or
may have relets to utilise. As a result, we are likely pushing into the 27-30 September
fixing window. However, despite this shift towards fixing closer to three weeks
forward, there is little expectation that activity will slow, with adjacent sizes either
already pulling their weight or primed to do so. Aframaxes are surging in the
Mediterranean and, if the physical/paper dynamic is anything to go by, are expected
to do the same stateside. In addition, and perhaps more significantly, the VLCC market
is extremely busy ex-Fujairah and approaching our equivalent with the Atlantic also
firming aggressively. Regardless of pricing, VLCCs remain under-fixed for the first
decade of October ex-West Africa, and with this surge in Eastern activity there may be
very few units physically capable of interacting with this window, which could
inevitably lead to cargo splitting and increased Suezmax utilisation. To top it all off,
CPC stems should be released imminently, and there are reports of attacks on terminal
infrastructure and vessels, which will almost certainly result in higher premiums.
As North Sea Aframax loading dates are now moving into the third decade of
September, the list of available tonnage continues to thin. With neighbouring markets
such as the US Gulf and Mediterranean offering attractive alternatives, owners are
increasingly ballasting away from the North Sea. While rates remain steady for the
time being, tightening tonnage availability could support a modest upward correction
in the near term. Activity in the Mediterranean remains robust, with cargoes being
quoted from virtually every export area and loading port. Rates continue to gain
momentum, and with charterers actively seeking to secure tonnage for forward-
loading programmes, the market is expected to remain firm with further upside
potential.

This week on Capesize, we saw continued resilience in both Pacific and Atlantic basins
as market levels held strong at peak levels. C5 West Australia/Qingdao saw a paper
peaked on Friday as the Baltic printed USD 18.96. Today levels are climbing after a
slight dip early week as the Pacific market sees robust activity with latest offers in the
low USD 19s. A flurry of recent fixtures reported in the USD 18.4-18.5 region for late
September and early October laycans and one rumor suggests that a very recent
fixture was seen in the low USD 19 levels, but this is unconfirmed. In the Atlantic, C3
Brazil/China led the charge as owners remained bullish, holding firm on offers. A tight


Panamax
Supramax
tonnage has owners staying strong with their offers, today seeing numerous offers in
the USD mid 42-43 levels for late 2h October laycans. Bids for late October sit at high
USD 41s with one fixture seen last night at high USD 41 levels, and another
Newcastlemax took an early October cargo for USD 41. Sentiment is mixed moving
forward as FFAs signal slight flattening overall, owners remain bullish, holding firm on
their offers, operators are quietly watching the market suggesting potential upside risk
if demand persists at current levels.
After several weeks of steady gains the Panamax market has levelled off, with the
index easing gently over recent sessions from what now looks to have been an early-
September high. The Atlantic accounts for most of the softening: prompt tonnage has
built up in the North Continent while fresh transatlantic enquiry has slowed, leaving
owners there with less leverage, though fronthaul remains the firmer part of the basin
and owners continue to favour Atlantic employment over repositioning east. The
Pacific has held its ground more comfortably, supported by Australian and Indonesian
coal, North Pacific grain and a useful flow of additional cargo from split Capesize
stems, but the urgency that characterised late August has faded and charterers are no
longer chasing. Period remains active and, notably, better bid than the softening spot
market would imply – business is still clearing at levels close to recent highs, with
owners of modern tonnage holding their ideas firmly and the gap to charterers' bids
widening rather than closing. Our own indicators argue for caution from here:
shipment volumes continue to run below fleet supply growth, the South Atlantic
tightness reading suggests the recent correction has further to run, and we would
treat the current period market as an opportunity to secure cover rather than expect a
renewed push higher.
The Handy market remained stable, and even firmed up a touch, with a healthy
balance between supply and demand. Activity was mainly focused on spot and short-
period employment, with owners generally able to remain selective. Touch positive
bias, provided grain programs and other minor bulk cargoes continue at current
levels. Balanced tonnage availability should, however, keep freight gains measured.
The Supramax/Ultramax market showed a slightly firmer tone during the week,

tightening in the Atlantic, while adequate tonnage availability in Asia is limiting
stronger upside.
