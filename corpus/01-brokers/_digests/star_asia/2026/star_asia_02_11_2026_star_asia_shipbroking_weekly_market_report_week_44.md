   MARKET OVERVIEW 
Saudi Arabia’s tanker operator delivered an extraordinary financial performance for the third quarter, 
reporting a tenfold surge in net income to 5.1 billion riyals (US$1.4 billion) against 513 million riyals during 
the same stretch last year. This financial upturn was powered by historic freight levels across the crude 
segment as the prolonged conflict continues to reshape logistics. Bahri has served as a central pipeline 
for regional crude movements, coordinating closely with state oil producer Aramco to sustain flows 
toward Asian buyers via offshore transhipment hubs. Despite lingering disruption from a temporary 
pipeline outage in September, Persian Gulf outbound crude flows have staged an impressive recovery 
back toward pre-crisis volumes, with producers increasingly relying on protected convoys and covert 
navigational tactics to keep supplies flowing. 
 
At the centre of this resilient export corridor is Sinokor, whose massive supertanker fleet lifted 
approximately four out of every ten barrels leaving the Persian Gulf last month, outpacing its three closest 
competitors combined. This has now placed the carrier directly in the crosshairs of regional authorities, 
with Iran’s Persian Gulf Strait Authority announcing impending legal proceedings against the firm set for 
the first of November over dozens of alleged transit infractions and past regulatory enforcements.  
 
The authority, established earlier this year to oversee passage protocols, has issued formal warnings to 
global cargo interests, underwriters, and maritime stakeholders regarding future interactions with the 
operator's vessels. With the world's largest crude carrier owner maintaining silence on the matter, players 
face renewed uncertainty over how these threats might alter trading terms and fleet deployment.  
 
Outside the shipping arena, broader macroeconomic data points to shifting cargo currents that could 
influence container and dry freight demand heading into the year's final stretch. United States 
merchandise trade numbers showed the goods deficit widening by 2.1% in September  to US$134.9 billion, 
representing the steepest trade gap recorded since early last year and landing well above consensus 
expectations.  
 
The deterioration was heavily spurred by an unprecedented 7.5% jump in capital goods imports, driven by 
massive corporate investment in overseas computing architecture, semiconductors, and 
telecommunications components to fuel expanding artificial intelligence projects. Although outbound 
shipments of agricultural products and consumer goods managed healthy single -to-double-digit 
expansions, the massive appetite for foreign-built industrial equipment and technology continues to 
heavily outpace domestic export gains, setting up a notable drag on broader quarterly economic 
expansion.

Dry bulk sector demonstrated encouraging signs of finding a firm footing, as prompt spot values began to 
stabilize following an extensive retreat of more than 50% from earlier highs that had brought the Capesize 
market to just under the 30,000 thresholds. On the trading floor, the benchmark Baltic Dry Index snapped a 
three-day losing run by advancing 2% on Friday to settle at 2,714 points, largely underpinned by the 
Capesize index climbing roughly 8.8% to a two-week peak of 4,126 points.  
 
Smaller tonnage categories encountered sustained downward pressure, as the Panamax index slipped 
10.4% to reach its lowest mark since late April at 1,889 points, while the Supramax index dipped 1.2% to 
touch a one-week low of 1,865 points. Underlining longer-term owner confidence amidst these near-term 
shifts, Greek operator Meadway Bulkers placed an order at Nantong Xiangyu Shipbuilding for six 64,500 
dwt Ultramax bulk carriers based on the second-generation Elephant design for delivery between 2029 
and 2030, an estimated financial commitment of about US$216 million at around US$36 million per vessel. 
Segment Highlights 
Capesize: Capesize staged an assertive rebound this week with average freight rates surging , propelled 
by a concentrated wave of early-to-mid November C5 fixtures and typhoon delays tightening vessel 
availability across East Asia. Driven by this, key benchmark earnings surged across both basins, with Brazil 
R/V advancing to US$38,950’s and Pacific R/V climbing to US$37,250’s per day as West Australia-to-China 
trades pushed into the low US$14s/ton.  
 
Ongoing weather related operational friction should help keep rates well supported, though an influx of 
ballast tonnage into the North Atlantic may cap broader upside across Western waters . 
 
Panamax / Kamsarmax: Panamax experienced renewed downward pressure this week as freight rates 
dropped, weighed down by a decline in Chinese grain shipments and an expanding November ballast list 
across EC South America. While healthier coal flows out of Australia and Indonesia provided downside 
defence for Asian trades, weakening demand across the Atlantic dragged on major benchmarks, pulling 
F/H rates down to US$27,250’s and Indonesian R/V to US$17,725’s per day.  
 
Supramax / Ultramax: Supras maintained a firm footing in the East, as active Chinese steel exports, and 
steady Indonesian coal movements helped absorb regional fleet. In contrast, a cargo lull and shifting 
grain laycans in the USG and South Atlantic exerted downward pressure on long- haul Atlantic business, 
leaving Pacific R/V firmer at US$21,000’s while T/A rates retreated to US$28,650’s.  
 
Handysize: Handysize segment saw quiet trading and mixed sentiment this week, with soft conditions 
prevailing across the Mediterranean while the South Atlantic drew support from tightening tonnage lists. 
Asian markets showed resilience despite slower day-to-day fixture volumes, as Inter-Pacific routes eased 
to UA$17,000’s and CIS R/V settled lower at US$16,100’s per day.  
 
Prompt tonnage across the Far East should provide a dependable rate floor, though a more sustained 
recovery will depend on a revival of fresh cargo inquiries across northern European ports .

Regional diplomatic efforts led by Saudi Arabia, Turkey, and Pakistan to establish a Middle Eastern security 
framework have offered tentative hope for de-escalation, yet crude and product balances remain 
structurally tight as Tehran conditions broader negotiations on the removal of U.S. sanctions and port 
blockades. Standard Chartered projects that while formal accords could swiftly trim speculative 
premiums, physical pricing will stay elevated until safe navigation through the Persian Gulf is restored, 
especially with unverified Houthi claims of fresh drone strikes against Saudi East -West pipeline pumping 
stations keeping transport risks acute. Emergency reserves provide little long -term cushion, with the U.S. 
Strategic Petroleum Reserve standing at 282.7 million barrels—its lowest reading since 1982—and Asian 
buyers facing comparable depletion, setting up strong post-conflict replenishment demand that will 
inevitably compete with commercial restocking.  
 
This has not dampened sentiment among vessel owners at the Maritime CEO Forum in Monaco, where 
executives pointed to depleted global stockpiles, recovering Gulf outflows, and prolonged trade 
inefficiencies as key drivers supporting the ongoing super-cycle. With the global tanker fleet averaging an 
aging 13.5 years, scrapping ages extending to 25.5 years, and roughly 19% of VLCCs exceeding two 
decades in service, owners remain largely constructive, noting that Middle Eastern producers absorbing 
tonnage directly into national fleets will continue to restrict merchant availability and underpin 
exceptional freight returns.

at WS700’s.

routes. LR2 MEG/Japan closed the week at WS1000 mark. Similarly, LR1 saw some discounts to as TC5 
routes fell to WS1021.   
 
MR: The Far East closed higher as prompt vessel availability was rapidly absorbed by robust refined 
product demand originating from China. In East Africa, index saw a slight uptick as TC17 from MEG closed 
at WS900’s.

   CONTAINERS  
 
The container market saw a slight uptick, with the SCFI index closing at 3,687.35 points. The market found 
short-term equilibrium as the traditional peak shipping cycle draws to a close. Carriers are actively 
seeking to protect rate baselines against seasonal demand tapering and selective market -share 
discounting, leaning heavily on void sailings to rein in nominal capacity. In Europe, freight rates held flat as 
MSC reiterated its mid-October Freight All Kinds benchmarks for November 1 to defend spot levels against 
sluggish demand and the ongoing resumption of Suez Canal transits. Middle East spot rates saw a slight 
uptick as carriers restored direct Asia services using alternative terminals  situated outside the Strait of 
Hormuz. 
 
The global container orderbook has surpassed 15 million TEU—reaching 15.12 million TEU, or 43.9% of the 
active trading fleet, according to Alphaliner—with an impending wave of megamax contracts involving 
Maersk, Yang Ming, CMA CGM, and MSC expected to lift total contracted capacity toward 16 million TEU 
before year-end. Notably, Maersk is in exclusive discussions with Chinese shipbuilders for up to 24 x  24,000 
TEU dual-fuel LNG vessels valued at up to US$5.76 billion, marking the carrier's initial entry into the 24,000 
TEU category and reinforcing an industry shift where LNG dual-fuel technology represents 56% of total 
orderbook capacity, compared to 12% for methanol.

Market Insights 
 
Alang, India: Alang experienced a noticeably calm week, with a more measured pace across yards. While 
general price benchmarks have held reasonably stable, cash buyers have adopted an increasingly 
discerning posture, carefully evaluating candidates rather than pursuing standard tonnage aggressively. 
The waterfront’s competitive edge continues to rest in its ability to handle specialised  and high-
specification units, particularly those requiring rigorous green compliance, or possessing substantial non-
ferrous machinery and equipment that deliver returns well beyond basic melting scrap.  
 
Alang recorded 88 vessels beached for recycling, totalling 941,750 MT, during the period , January to 
October. Activity was uneven, with busy months interrupted by sharp slowdowns.  
 
February was the busiest month by vessel count, receiving 16 ships, followed by July with 15 and April with 
14. April led by tonnage, recording 179,373 MT, closely followed by June at 173,039 MT from just nine vessels, 
indicating larger ships arriving for recycling. May was the slackest month, with only one vessel of 8,320 MT. 
January and March were also subdued in both vessel numbers and tonnage. August recorded just four 
vessels, although their combined 79,487 MT supported recycling volumes.  October saw vessel arrivals 
recover to 10 ships from six in September, but tonnage slipped by approximately 3.3% to 87,079 MT, 
reflecting smaller vessels arriving on average. 
 
India's steady manufacturing indicators and healthy infrastructure momentum continue to provide 
dependable support for finished steel demand, yet regional re-rolling mills are managing raw material 
purchases with strict financial discipline to preserve working margins. While domestic monetary 
authorities keep a close watch on inflationary pressures and seasonal commodity movements, local 
market participants are prioritising quality over sheer volume.

Chattogram, Bangladesh: Chattogram's hunger for incoming hulls drove bids upward despite an

substantially increased domestic borrowing and operational carrying costs. While international reserves

keeping melting scrap intake strictly tied to immediate commitments.
