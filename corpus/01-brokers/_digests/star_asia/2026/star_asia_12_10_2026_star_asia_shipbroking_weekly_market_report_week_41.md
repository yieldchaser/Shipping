   MARKET OVERVIEW 
 
In the Middle East, the crude tanker sector is experiencing unprecedented earnings driven by elevated 
hazards in the Persian Gulf. More than four out of every ten VLCCs across the globe are currently situated 
in or around the waterway, drawn by historic premium earnings despite the lingering dangers of transit. 
Inefficiencies stemming from lightering operations and offshore transfers outside critical choke points 
have tied up significant capacity, sharply reducing the availability of large carriers in the Atlantic basin. 
This concentration is creating major ripples throughout the broader maritime trade, forcing charterers to 
pay historic highs on transpacific voyages and increasingly pivot toward mid- sized tonnage to manage 
non-Middle Eastern export programs. 
 
At the same time, institutional efforts to safeguard the waterways reflect growing administrative and 
operational scrutiny. Recent U.S. government audits reviewing allied initiatives against regional 
destabilisation highlight that, despite substantial financial investments and hundreds of programs 
spanning naval escorts to targeted economic sanctions, activities have historically struggled with internal 
overlap and fragmented departmental oversight. While recent steps have established dedicated central 
management to unify strategic direction and improve accountability, these hurdles show  how challenging 
it remains to shield commercial sea corridors through official diplomacy and military presence alone. 
Navigating the regional risk profile will require sustained vigilance, as policy slowly aligns with the high-
stakes security realities on the water. 
 
Farther north, persistent safety risks across the Black Sea trade lanes are placing merchant crews under 
direct threat, triggering proactive diplomatic intervention from India. Following several strikes on trading 
vessels carrying foreign personnel, diplomatic envoys from New Delhi have initiated direct dialogues with 
both Russia and Ukraine to explore protective arrangements for essential energy and grain shipments. 
With mariners sustaining casualties and several hulls sustaining direct strikes in recent days, the human 
element of seafaring remains at the center of operational concerns. Market stakeholders continue to face 
elevated voyage uncertainty, as global trade flows hinge not only on fleet availability and market 
dynamics, but fundamentally on the physical protection of the waterways and the seafarers who navigate 
them.

Dry bulk market opened the final quarter of the year on a sturdy footing, with full-year average returns 
tracking toward multi-year peaks even as daily spot indices underwent a noticeable weekly correction. 
This enduring commercial strength continues to be anchored by widespread operating inefficiencies —
ranging from protracted voyage diversions around conflict zones to extended port and bunkering delays —
which effectively squeeze available vessel supply. 
 
That said, spot benchmarks experienced a noticeable pullback as BDI  fell 7.34% on Friday to 2,917 points. 
The downturn was once again centred on the larger bulkers, with the BCI index dropping to 4,340 points. In 
contrast, BPI held steady with modest resilience, inching up 2.19% to 2,424 points. While the overall 
sentiment among shipowners remains broadly upbeat ahead of winter restocking, market s could soften 
noticeably in the medium term should geopolitical friction ease and release sidelined fleet capacity back 
into open trading. 
 
Segment Highlights 
Capesize: Capesize faced a sharp 12.8% w-o-w correction as China's National Day holiday paused fresh 
fixture activity, softening major iron ore routes despite a rise in Australian export volumes. The lull and 
expanding fleet deployed into the East triggered pronounced rate divergence, with T/A returns sliding to 
US$48,250’s while Pacific r/v found tentative support, nudging up to US$37,700’s. Owners are largely 
adopting a wait-and-see stance amid holiday-thinned volumes, though this is expected to ease once 
post-holiday activity resumes in earnest for early November stems. 
 
Panamax / Kamsarmax: Panamax traded broadly sideways through Golden Week with rates easing 1.4% 
w-o-w, as firm South American grain inquiry helped counterbalance holiday -induced quietness and 
declining Australian and Indonesian coal volumes in the East. While prompt tonnage ac ross EC South 
America kept the Atlantic floor resilient, selective fixtures in the NOPAC lent measured support to regional 
returns, leaving T/A rates flat at US$21,350’s and nudging Pacific r/v up slightly to US$20,400’s. Ongoing 
holiday disruptions across Asia are expected to maintain near-term pressure in the Pacific.  
 
Supramax / Ultramax: Supramax edged up this week, drawing steady support from European scrap flows 
to the East Med and NOPAC grain demand to offset quieter holiday trading across Asia. While softer 
Indonesian coal chartering and USG volatility capped wider advances, owners held firm on trip 
employment, lifting F/H returns to US$31,150’s and Pacific r/v to US$18,450’s, while T/A softened marginally 
to US$28,650’s.  
 
Handysize: Handysize maintained a strong and resilient tone across both basins this week, supported by 
active interest and a solid volume of fixtures concluding for both transatlantic and transpacific voyages. A 
steady stream of short-period and multiple laden-leg engagements cleared at healthy numbers, 
encouraging owners to fix out forward employment while holding firm on their rate ideas.  Fronthaul rates 
rose to US$15,250’s per day and Inter-Pacific runs firming to US$17,000’s.

Tanker segments are contending with intense logistical friction as downstream supply disruptions and 
persistent Middle Eastern hazards compound freight costs. The clean product sector experienced 
immediate turmoil following Beijing’s sudden decision to halt refined fuel export licenses, cancelling 
October loadings after shipments hit a record 1.1 million BPD in September. With domestic diesel 
inventories reaching their lowest levels since 2015, the suspension sent shockwaves through Asian trading 
hubs, doubling prompt jet fuel forward spreads, lifting diesel spreads by 70%, and raising gasoline spreads 
by 25% on October 1 alone. To cushion against soaring shipping expenses and secure market share, Saudi 
Aramco reduced its November official formula price for Arab Light bound for Asia to a US $5-per-barrel 
discount against Oman-Dubai benchmarks—its widest price concession since 2020—while raising North-
West European rates by US$3 per barrel. Meanwhile, the announcement of a 100-million-barrel emergency 
crude and product release by the G7 produced only a brief price correction, leaving ICE Brent hovering 
firmly near the US$100 per barrel mark as physical deliverability concerns outweigh paper market relief.  
 
On the water, the apparent recovery of Gulf crude exports has come at the expense of severe operational 
peril, keeping war-risk insurance premiums and vessel earnings near historic highs. Iranian drone and 
projectile strikes have targeted at least 8 crude tankers and an LPG carrier so far this month, prompting 
four separate emergency alerts from the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations on Tuesday alone 
and cementing a "severe" threat status across the passage. With OPEC+ choosing to freeze its pr oduction 
quotas as remaining members pump roughly 5 million BPD below baseline levels, the combination and 
the threat of fresh regional strikes ahead of the U.S. midterm elections ensures that tanker availability will 
remain critically strained heading into peak winter demand.

VLCC: MEG rates rebounded as renewed Strait of Hormuz transit risks and heavy absorption into Persian 
Gulf shuttles and Gulf of Oman STS operations intensified prompt tonnage shortages. MEG/China climbed 
almost 100 points to WS1,300’s on Friday and over 600% increase from the same period last year. Similarly 
in the Atlantic, rates for WAFR/China ended the week at WS750.  
 
Suezmax: West African rates doubled this week as a rush of late-October inquiry and cargo-splitting from

rates closing at WS850’s mark.  
 
Aframax: MEG rates surged as active private chartering and escalating attacks near the Strait and Red 
Sea severely limited tonnage willing to enter high-risk zones. In the Mediterranean, freight rates are 
holding at all-time highs, with 80,000mt Cross-Med closing at WS780, keeping overall market sentiment 
firmly elevated. 
 
LR1 / LR2: MEG LR2 saw rates rise as steady East Asian product demand clashed with severe tonnage 
shortages. MEG/Japan TC1 trips remain high around WS928 mark. Similarly, in the LR1, rates although came 
off slightly but still remain high for TC5 trips closing at WS940 on Friday.

   CONTAINERS  
 
The container market experienced a slight retreat this week as the SCFI index fell to 3,662.30 points. This 
was led by softening market fundamentals across Europe and the Mediterranean, and China’s Golden 
Week holiday this week. According to Drewry, carriers have scheduled 58 cancellations out of 712 planned 
voyages on major East-West corridors between September 28 and November 1—an overall cancellation 
rate of 8%—with 64% of these blank sailings concentrated on Asia–North America services, as programs 
like MSC’s void sailings and Maersk’s withdrawal of peak-season extra loaders actively tightened 
transpacific space. 
 
Meanwhile, Middle East spot rates registered a temporary weekly correction as outbound booking 
demand from China eased during the holiday lull; however, underlying price levels in the Persian Gulf 
remain structurally insulated.

Market Insights 
 
Alang, India: Alang navigated a steady yet cautious week, with overall conditions holding largely 
unchanged from the previous week. While buyers continue to show keen interest, the strengthening USD 
against the rupee has exerted noticeable pressure on price indications, trimming profit margins on dollar -
priced hulls. The flow of open-market candidates remains constrained, leaving Alang at a clear pricing 
disadvantage compared to regional competitors who continue to contest conventional tonnage at higher 
figures. Indian breakers continue to concentrate their efforts on specialised assets where complex 
material compositions, non-ferrous machinery, and stringent environmental compliance take precedence 
over raw steel scrap values. 
 
An active week of macroeconomic developments across India, led by the Reserve Bank of India’s 
monetary policy announcement on October 7. Responding to firming headline inflation and persistent 
currency pressure from elevated global oil prices, the central bank rais ed its benchmark repo rate by 25 
basis points to 5.50% and adopted a calibrated tightening stance. Despite higher domestic borrowing 
costs, India’s underlying growth fundamentals remain sturdy, with the central bank projecting annual 
economic growth at 7.1% and manufacturing activity expanding at a solid 8.6%. National foreign exchange 
reserves remain robust at US$734.6 billion, ensuring ample liquidity across the banking sector to support 
trade credit and LCs into the last quarter of the year.

SUB-CONTINENT & TURKEY SCRAP MARKETS  
 
India: The imported scrap market remained subdued as mills actively squeezed overseas sellers, keeping 
workable levels below suppliers' expectations of US$390/t or higher for quality HMS. African scrap offers 
into Mundra emerged at US$370/t for HMS 80:20 and US$335/t for LMS, while UK yard-origin cut-sheared 
HMS hovered at $370/t CFR.  
 
Other fresh indications included Brazilian HMS 80:20 at US$373/t CFR and New Zealand HMS 90:10 (1% 
impurities) quoted at US$390/t CFR subject to reconfirmation. 
 
Pakistan: Import trading stayed largely quiet amid buyer resistance to elevated asking rates, with 
domestic melt shop utilisation languishing near 35% despite modest local activity. Recent Middle Eastern 
niche sales featured fabrication scrap at US$423/t, compressor domes at US$415/t, structural scrap at 
US$477/t, and structural/ship plates at US$473/t CFR Qasim.  
 
Meanwhile, general offers stood at US$420/t for shredded and US$390/t for HMS, alongside mixed trade 
speculation regarding whether UAE export curbs will lapse after 8 October . 
 
Bangladesh: Subdued sentiment persisted with limited transactional activity, leaving mills awaiting bids 
against firm regional and containerised offers. Local shipyard scrap traded near BDT 56,000/t (US $455/t), 
while Australian HMS 90:10 was offered at US$400/t and PNS at US$405–410/t CFR Chattogram. Other 
supplier indications remained elevated, featuring UK shredded at US$400–405/t, large mill PNS at US$410–
415/t, Malaysian PNS at US$424–425/t, and Singapore PNS reaching up to US$430/t CFR. 
 
Turkiye: Deep-sea import benchmarks held steady day-on-day on 7 October amid an ongoing buyer-
seller standoff, with mills purchasing selectively to counteract softening domestic rebar sentiment. 
Conversely, US suppliers maintained a bullish stance, bolstered by strong domestic pricing, rising ocean 
freight rates, and seasonal winter supply contractions.  
 
While Turkish mills resisted higher price tags, exporters remained confident that deep -sea values would 
advance toward US$412–415/t CFR in the coming weeks.

Commodities Focus 
Coal futures surged past US$150/ton to touch their highest valuation since September 2023 after 
Australia’s High Court dismissed an appeal by Mach Energy regarding the expansion of its New South 
Wales mine, establishing that downstream emissions from end-users must be weighed by planners  and 
raising long-term sovereign risk concerns across the seaborne trade. This regulatory barrier comes as 
global coal consumption is poised to achieve record levels this year, driven by the ongoing Strait of 
Hormuz blockade and Middle Eastern hostilities that have disrupted liquefied natural gas supplies and 
driven utilities throughout China, India, Japan, South Korea, and parts of Europe toward coal -fired power. 
 
Concurrently, the base metals complex demonstrated renewed strength as copper  futures advanced for 
a third consecutive session to roughly US$6.6/pound, supported by technology and artificial intelligence 
equity rallies alongside mounting physical constraints, highlighted by Chilean copper production 
dropping in August to its weakest level since February 2011 and workers at Antofagasta’s Centinela m ine 
back strike action after wage talks stalled.  
 
While copper trade flows remain distorted by prior cargo diversions into American warehouses ahead of 
delayed White House tariff decisions; India, now the world’s second-largest crude steel producer, is 
holding its safeguard duties at 11.5% rather than raising them above 20%, despite seeing finished steel 
imports jump 29.5% y-o-y to 3.5 million metric tons between April and August, with China supplying 31.8% 
of those volumes. With Indian automotive retail volumes jumping 31.82% y -o-y in September and 
monsoon-related construction pauses subsiding, domestic mills have managed to lift selling prices to 
offset expensive coking coal, even as cheaper overseas shipments continue to pose a lingering margin 
risk.
