BRAZIL CRUDE OIL EXPORTS
After a slight decline in 2024, when 
global crude oil loadings decreased by 
-0.2% y-o-y, things picked up in 2025, 
with full-year volumes increasing by 
+1.8% y-o-y. 
In Jan-Sep 2026, global crude oil 
loadings decreased by -6.1% y-o-y to 
1,547.6 mln tonnes, excluding all 
cabotage trade, according to vessels 
tracking data from LSEG. 
Exports from the Arabian Gulf were 
down by -29.1% y-o-y to 464.5 mln t in 
Jan-Sep 2026, accounting for 30.0% of 
global seaborne crude trade, down 
from 39.8% in the same period of last 
year, reflecting the disruption from 
the ongoing war in the Persian Gulf. 
Exports from Russian ports (including 
oil of Kazakh origin shipped via the 
CPC terminal) increased by +8.3% y-o-y 
to 187.1 mln t in Jan-Sep 2026, or 
12.1% of global trade. 
From South America, exports surged 
by +33.1% y-o-y to 212.3 mln t. From 
the USA, exports went up by +10.5% y-
o-y to 150.5 mln tonnes in Jan-Sep 
2026. 
From West Africa, exports decreased 
by -10.2% y-o-y to 118.7 mln t. From 
ASEAN exports rose by +8.3% y-o-y to 
86.2 mln t in Jan-Sep 2026 (this 
inevitably includes trans-shipped 
cargoes). 
In terms of demand (measured by 
arrival at discharge ports), global 
seaborne crude imports totalled 
1,549.2 mln t in Jan-Sep 2026, down      
-5.4% y-o-y. 
The top single-country seaborne 
importer of crude oil in Jan-Sep 2026 
was Mainland China, accounting for 
18.8% of global trade, although the 
EU27 as a bloc was a larger buyer. 
Volumes into China declined by -20.7% 
y-o-y to 291.8 mln t in Jan-Sep 2026, 
from 367.7 mln t in Jan-Sep 2025. 
Imports into the EU27 increased by 
+1.3% y-o-y to 350.9 mln t, accounting 
for 22.6% of global trade. To ASEAN, 
imports decreased by          -8.0% y-o-y 
to 198.7 mln t. To South Korea, 
imports declined by -10.7% y-o-y to 
92.0 mln t.      
Imports into the USA increased by 
+4.0% y-o-y to 96.8 mln t in Jan-Sep 
2026. To Japan, imports declined by         
-13.5% y-o-y to 71.1 mln t.
Brazil is fast emerging as a significant 
exporter of crude oil, with volumes 
increasing rapidly in recent years. 
In Jan-Dec 2023, volumes surged by 
+27.2% y-o-y to 88.2 mln tonnes. In 
Jan-Dec 2024, exports increased by a 
further +4.3% y-o-y to 92.0 mln t. In 
Jan-Dec 2025, exports rose by a 
further +18.7% y-o-y to 109.2 mln t. 
In 2025, Brazil accounted for 4.9% of 
global seaborne crude oil exports. 
2026 so far has been even stronger, 
with exports in Jan-Sep 2026 up by 
+22.8% y-o-y to 97.1 mln tonnes, up 
from 79.1 mln t in Jan-Sep 2025. 
About 30 percent of international 
crude exports from Brazil in Jan-Sep 
2026 were loaded at Acu, about 24 
percent from Angra Dos Reis, about 19 
percent from Santos, about 3 percent 
from Peregrino FPSO, about 2 percent 
from Sao Sebastiao, about 2 percent 
from Madre Deus, about 2 percent 
from Itaguai. 
The vast majority of crude oil cargoes 
loaded in Brazil, 68 percent in Jan-Sep 
2026, are loaded on VLCCs. About 25 
percent of volumes are loaded on 
Suezmaxes, and 5 percent is loaded on 
Aframaxes. 
In terms of destinations for crude oil 
shipments from Brazil, the major ones 
are in Asia, which results in a very 
strong contribution to shipping tonne-
miles. 
The number one destination is 
Mainland China, accounting for 39 
percent of Brazilian shipments so far 
this year. In Jan-Sep 2026, Brazil 
exported 37.5 mln tonnes of crude oil 
to China, up +13.4% y-o-y. 
Direction Europe, about 18.1 mln 
tonnes (18.6 percent of the total) were 
shipped from Brazil to the European 
Union in Jan-Sep 2026, up +13.7% y-o-
y from the 15.9 mln tonnes in Jan-Sep 
2025. Of these, 5.7 mln t were shipped 
to the Netherlands, 4.6 mln t to Spain, 
4.1 mln t to Portugal. 
Shipments to the rest of South 
America surged by +56.3% y-o-y to 
10.2 mln tonnes, including 6.8 mln t to 
Uruguay and 2.5 mln t to Chile. 
To India volumes surged +150.3% y-o-y 
to 6.1 mln tonnes. 
To South East Asia, volumes increased 
by +22.6% y-o-y in Jan-Sep 2026 to 9.0 
mln tonnes, from 7.3 mln t in Jan-Sep 
2025. Of these, 4.0 mln t were shipped 
to Singapore, and 2.1 mln t to 
Malaysia. 
Exports to the United States were 
essentially flat at -1.4% y-o-y to 6.7 
mln t, after declining by -16.0% y-o-y 
in the same period of last year.

2026/27 despite slower area 
growth, Conab says
Brazil's 2026/27 soybean output is 
expected to reach a record 181.6 
million metric tons, crop agency 
Conab said on Thursday, up 0.7% 
from the previous season, driven by 
a 1.4% increase in planted area to 
49.3 million hectares (121.8 million 
acres). With planting underway in

lower margins and uncertainty

pattern.

suspected import tax evasion

sources said on Thursday. After 
being diverted at sea, the soybeans 
were sold to buyers in Vietnam, 
Indonesia and Bangladesh at prices 
between $470 and $510 per ton, 
compared with more than $650 per 
ton agreed by Indian buyers, who 
refused to take delivery after 
customs authorities stepped up 
scrutiny, the sources said.

corn, wheat, sorghum and

nearly 6 million tons above the

production would increase by 5 
million to 8 million metric tons from 
last year, largely driven by gains in 
North China.

through the strait. It is yet to 
implement the mechanism it drew 
up, but United Nations Under-
Secretary-General Jorge Moreira da 
Silva said this could be activated 
within 7-10 days of an agreement 
between Washington and Tehran.

analysts' records. The world's 
biggest oil buyer imported 37.1 
million metric tons of crude oil in 
September, or 9.03 million barrels 
per day, with the daily import rate 
up 1.1% from the previous month, 
according to data from China's 
General Administration of Customs.

The Capesize market fell steeply in a 
thoroughly bearish week, abundant 
tonnage in both basins and 
successive declines in freight 
derivative (FFA) values tilting the 
market firmly in charterers' favour. 
The Pacific stabilised only on the 
final session; both Atlantic routes fell 
throughout.
The Western Australia–Qingdao 
route (170,000 mt iron ore, 
plus/minus 10%) lost 80 cents/wmt 
to USD 13.40/wmt on 12 October, 
exchanges thinned by a Japanese 
public holiday and a dearth of fresh 
cargoes, a mining major fixing 
Dampier–Qingdao at about USD 
13.40/wmt for 27–29 October 
laycan. 
It shed a further 45 cents/wmt to 
USD 12.95/wmt on 13 October, 
three Capesizes being taken from 
Dampier at around USD 12.95/wmt, 
and 90 cents/wmt to USD 
12.05/wmt on 14 October as owners 
cut offers aggressively despite 
spiking bunker prices; three more 
Dampier–Qingdao ships were fixed 
between USD 12.00/wmt and USD 
12.10/wmt. 
A further 80 cents/wmt took the 
route to USD 11.25/wmt on 15 
October, matching a Dampier–
Qingdao fixture at that level, before 
it rebounded 35 cents/wmt to close 
at USD 11.60/wmt on 16 October on 
firmer derivative values, down USD 
2.60/wmt over the week.
Tubarao–Qingdao declined in every 
session, by 25 cents/wmt, 50 
cents/wmt, 80 cents/wmt, USD 
1.25/wmt and 50 cents/wmt, to 
close at USD 30.20/wmt, down USD 
3.30/wmt. 
South Atlantic cargo volumes were 
ample but concentrated on second-
half November laycan, and 
participants repeatedly flagged a 
surplus of prompt ballasters 
threatening to spill into those dates; 
the curve remained in contango 
across November loading. 
Reported fixtures fell from mid-USD 
31/wmt for 10 November onward to 
about USD 30.00/wmt for 15–20 
November. 
A Capesize was also fixed Pointe 
Noire–Qingdao for second-half 
November, rate undisclosed.
Saldanha Bay–Qingdao fell by 75, 35 
cents/wmt, USD 1.40/wmt, then 30 
and 40 cents/wmt, to close at USD 
22.55/wmt, down USD 3.20/wmt. 
A Capesize was fixed Saldanha Bay–
Qingdao at low-mid USD 23/wmt for 
1–5 November laycan.

The Atlantic market continued to 
strengthen, with ECSA tonnage 
commanding increasingly attractive 
levels, particularly for trips into the 
Continent and Mediterranean.

supportive, whilst fresh transatlantic 
business and firm coal rates helped 
reinforce the positive sentiment 
across the basin.

fixed for Skaw-Gibraltar redelivery at 
USD 39,000/day, setting a new high 
among the reported fixtures and 
highlighting strong demand for 
modern tonnage.

healthy, with an 82,000 dwt 2016-
built fixing for Singapore-Japan grains 
at USD 22,000/day plus USD 1.25m 
ballast bonus.

positioning towards ECSA for further

achieving USD 35,000–37,000/day

in the route.

two-leg voyage with redelivery Skaw-
Gibraltar at USD 27,000/day. 
These fixtures point to sustained 
demand for Atlantic coal movements, 
although the cargo rate and time-
charter figures are not directly 
comparable.
