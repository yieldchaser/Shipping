ARABIAN GULF CRUDE OIL EXPORTS
In Jan-Dec 2025, global crude oil 
loadings went up by +1.8% y-o-y to 
2,221.1 mln tonnes, excluding all 
cabotage trade, according to vessels 
tracking data from LSEG. 
This year started well, but the trend 
reversed from March onwards, and 
global crude oil loadings in Jan-Oct 
2026 were down by -5.8% y-o-y to 
1,738.8 mln tonnes. 
Exports from the Arabian Gulf were 
down by -27.6% y-o-y to 531.3 mln t in 
Jan-Oct 2026, accounting for 30.6% of 
seaborne crude trade, down from 
39.8% in the same period of last year. 
From South America, exports 
increased by +32.5% y-o-y to 238.8 
mln t, with a share of 13.7%. 
Exports from Russian ports (including 
oil of Kazakh origin) increased by 
+6.7% y-o-y in Jan-Oct 2026 to 207.8 
mln tonnes, or 12.0% of global trade. 
From the USA, export volumes 
increased by +8.5% y-o-y to 166.4 mln 
t in Jan-Oct 2026, a 9.6% share. 
From South East Asia exports 
increased by +2.7% y-o-y to 92.8 mln t 
in Jan-Oct 2026 (but this inevitably 
also reflects changes in the re-export 
of Russian origin volumes). 
In terms of demand, the top single-
country seaborne importer of crude oil 
in Jan-Oct 2026 was Mainland China, 
accounting for 18.9% of global trade, 
although the EU27 as a bloc was a 
larger buyer. 
Volumes into China declined by -20.1% 
y-o-y to 328.4 mln t in Jan-Oct 2026. 
Imports to the EU27 increased by 
+0.9% y-o-y to 387.8 mln t, accounting 
for 22.4% of global trade. 
To ASEAN, imports declined by -9.2% 
y-o-y to 219.4 mln t (again this 
includes Russian volumes later re-
exported elsewhere in Asia). 
To India, volumes increased +1.5% y-o-
y to 200.8 mln t in Jan-Oct 2026.
The Arabian Gulf countries (including 
Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Bahrain, 
Kuwait, Oman, Iraq and Iran) are the 
single largest seaborne exporter of 
crude oil in the world. 
In 2025 they accounted for 39.8% of 
global seaborne crude oil exports. 
In the 12 months of 2025, seaborne 
crude oil exports from AG countries 
increased by +1.7% to 883.1 mln 
tonnes, excluding cabotage, after 
declining by -0.8% y-o-y in 2024. 
In Jan-Oct 2026, however, Arabian 
Gulf crude exports collapsed by -27.6% 
y-o-y to 531.3 mln tonnes, from 734.1 
mln t in the same period of 2025, and 
their share of global seaborne crude 
trade fell to 30.6%, from 39.8% a year 
earlier. 
About 85.8% of volumes loaded in the 
AG countries in Jan-Oct 2026 were 
carried in VLCCs, 11.0% were carried in 
Suezmaxes, and 3.1% in Aframaxes. 
These proportions increased in recent 
years in favour of the largest tankers, 
as Suezmaxes and Afras were diverted 
to carry more Russian cargoes. 
The ranking of export terminals has 
been transformed by the war in the 
Gulf, with loadings shifting decisively 
to terminals that can be reached 
without transiting the Strait of 
Hormuz. 
The main crude oil export terminals in 
the AG are now: Fujairah (137.8 mln 
tonnes, or 25.9%, in Jan-Oct 2026), 
Yanbu (104.0 mln t, or 19.6%), Ras 
Tanura (59.2 mln t, or 11.1%), Basrah 
(52.6 mln t, or 9.9%), Sohar (49.8 mln 
t, or 9.4%), Mina al Fahal (36.7 mln t, 
or 6.9%), Mina al Ahmadi (14.3 mln t), 
Zirku Island (11.4 mln t), Das Island 
(6.1 mln t).  
Ras Tanura, the largest terminal in the 
world a year ago, has been overtaken 
by Fujairah and Yanbu, both of which 
lie outside the Strait. 
In terms of destinations for crude oil 
shipments from the AG, the focus in 
recent years has clearly shifted to Asia. 
In Jan-Oct 2026, the top destination 
was South East Asia, accounting for 
21.6% of the total AG crude exports, 
narrowly ahead of Mainland China 
with 21.5%, followed by India with 
14.6%, South Korea with 11.0%, Japan 
with 7.4%. 
Direct shipments to the USA now 
account for just 1.8% of AG exports, 
and direct shipments to the European 
Union for just 2.2%. 
Another 6.1% of AG exports are 
shipped to the Ain Sukhna Terminal in 
Egypt to feed the Sumed pipeline, and 
will eventually mostly end up in 
Europe. 
In Jan-Oct 2026, the AG countries 
shipped 114.4 mln tonnes of crude to 
Mainland China, which represents a       
-39.4% y-o-y decline from 188.9 mln 
tonnes in Jan-Oct 2025. 
Shipments to South East Asia fell by      
-27.8% y-o-y to 114.9 mln t, from 
159.0 mln t in Jan-Oct 2025. 
The AG also shipped 77.5 mln t this 
year to India, which represents a 
decline of -15.4% y-o-y from 91.6 mln t 
in the same period last year. 
Volumes to South Korea declined by     
-28.4% y-o-y in Jan-Oct 2026 to 58.5 
mln t. 
Shipments from the AG to Japan more 
than halved, down -52.4% y-o-y in Jan-
Oct 2026 to 39.4 mln tonnes.
