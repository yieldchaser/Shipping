MARKET REPORT –WEEK 47/2026
Steel products are one of the most 
important dry bulk commodities, 
accounting for 4.2 percent of all 
seaborne dry bulk trade in 2025, and 
4.6 percent so far in 2026.
Total seaborne exports of steel 
products on bulkcarriers increased in 
Jan-Dec 2024 by +11.4% year-on-year 
to 222.7 mln tonnes, and by a further 
+5.3% y-o-y in Jan-Dec 2025 to a 
record 234.5 mln tonnes, according to 
vessel tracking data from AXS Marine.
In the first 10 months of 2026, global 
steel products trade increased further 
by +10.4% y-o-y to 215.3 mln tonnes, 
up from 195.0 mln t in the same 
period of 2025.
The largest exporter of steel products 
by far is China, which accounted for 
48.0% of global steel products exports 
in Jan-Oct 2026.
In Jan-Oct 2026, shipments from China 
surged by +44.4% y-o-y to 103.3 mln 
tonnes, and this followed on the 
+14.9% y-o-y growth in the same 
period of last year.
Exports from China were now about 
two and a half times the 41.5 mln 
tonnes exported in Jan-Oct 2022.
In second place is Japan, which 
accounted for 9.2% of global steel 
products exports in Jan-Oct 2026.
In Jan-Oct 2026, steel products 
exports from Japan increased by 
+6.0% y-o-y to 19.8 mln tonnes.
Volumes out of Japan have been fairly 
constant, with 18.6 mln t in Jan-Oct 
2025, 19.6 mln t in Jan-Oct 2024, 19.7 
mln t in Jan-Oct 2023.
In third place is South Korea, with a 
9.0% share in Jan-Oct 2026.
In Jan-Oct 2026, South Korea exported 
19.5 mln tonnes of steel products, up 
+6.8% y-o-y from the 18.2 mln t in Jan-
Oct 2025.
The European Union is in fourth place 
with a 7.2% share. In Jan-Oct 2026, 
steel exports from the EU declined        
-1.0% y-o-y to 15.6 mln t.
Russia is in fifth place, with a 4.2% 
share. Volumes out of Russia collapsed 
by -40.3% y-o-y in Jan-Oct 2026 to just 
9.1 mln tonnes, after having held 
broadly stable at around 15-16 mln t 
over the same period in 2022-2025.
In terms of imports, the top 
destination for steel products in Jan-
Oct 2026 was still the European Union, 
accounting for 13.9% of global steel 
products imports.
Imports to the EU declined by -6.8% y-
o-y in Jan-Oct 2026 to 28.0 mln 
tonnes. This was above the 27.1 mln t 
in Jan-Oct 2023 but below the 29.1 
mln t in Jan-Oct 2022.
Outside Europe, the largest importers 
were the USA, accounting for 7.7% of 
global steel imports.
Imports to the USA increased by +1.6% 
y-o-y in Jan-Oct 2026 to 15.6 mln t.
In third place was Turkey, accounting 
for a 5.3% share and 10.7 mln t in Jan-
Oct 2026, and marginally behind was 
Vietnam with 9.9 mln tonnes.
Let's now focus specifically on China, 
which as we mentioned above is the 
world's largest exporter of steel 
products with a 48.0% share.
Top steel products loading ports in 
China in Jan-Oct 2026 were:
Tianjin (28.7 mln t in Jan-Oct 2026), 
Bayuquan (14.2 mln t), Shanghai (9.6 
mln t), Lianyungang (7.2 mln t), 
Tangshan (6.2 mln t), Zhangjiagang 
(5.5 mln t), Caofeidian (5.3 mln t), 
Lanshan (3.2 mln t).
57.7% of steel products exports from 
China in Jan-Oct 2026 were loaded on 
Supramaxes, 19.8% on Handies, 13.8% 
on units smaller than 20k dwt.
The vast majority of steel exports from 
China are shipped to nearby Asian 
destinations and to the Middle East 
region.
The top destination for Chinese steel is 
South Korea, accounting for 6.7% of 
Chinese steel exports in 2026.
Volumes from China to South Korea 
surged by +73.0% y-o-y in Jan-Oct 
2026 to 6.9 mln tonnes.
In second place is Indonesia, 
accounting for 5.9% of Chinese steel 
exports, or 6.1 mln tonnes, up +73.5% 
y-o-y.
In third place was Vietnam, accounting 
for 5.7% and 5.8 mln tonnes, up 
+28.5% y-o-y.
Further down were Malaysia with 4.0 
mln t (+39.1% y-o-y), the Philippines 
with 3.6 mln t (+16.4%), Thailand with 
3.5 mln t (+34.0%), Turkey with 3.4 
mln t (-0.5%), Saudi Arabia with 3.2 
mln t (-25.9%), Nigeria with 3.0 mln t 
(+109.1%), and the UAE with 2.8 mln t 
(-29.9%).
Growth was therefore broad-based 
across South East Asia, whilst 
shipments to the Middle East declined.
The EU as a whole received 6.5 mln t 
of Chinese steel in Jan-Oct 2026, up 
sharply from just 3.7 mln t in the same 
period last year.
