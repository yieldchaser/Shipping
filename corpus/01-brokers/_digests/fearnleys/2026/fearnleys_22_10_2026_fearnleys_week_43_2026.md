01Tankers
VLCC
VLCC rates have been under pressure in the week gone by, shedding WS 100-150
points depending on the voyages in question. The “weaker” sentiment is not helped
by the composition of the position lists, littered with trader- and oil company relets.
On the flip side we’re seeing more reports of oil supply increasing, with eg. MEG
exports having surpassed prewar levels in periods over the last few weeks. Thus, the
map is not exactly fitting the terrain currently, and something will have to give. We
would not be too surprised to see some more “profit taking”, rates are still very, very
good and risk/reward tilts in charterers' favour as they keep proceedings close to the
chest and only drip-feed cargoes into the marketplace. A rumour doing the rounds
suggests WS 650 has been concluded for a Fujairah/East run on another relet which
will add fuel to fire if confirmed, but we should not be too far off the “sweet spot”
where charterers start reengaging again in earnest.


Suezmax
Aframax
Rates
Finally, we have some activity in the Atlantic, albeit off forward dates. There should be
earlier stems to fix, but we would not view the forward cargoes in the market as a
signal for charterers to move, as it seems unlikely that prompt vessels will interact
given the number of waiting days. Even if we do see an influx of prompter cargoes, it
feels unlikely to have an immediate positive impact and would instead have more of a
steadying effect. As such, it does feel like fixing patterns should shift back closer to
two weeks out. Owners will be hoping for the tap to turn back on from Fujairah, as
ultimately the lack of fixing has funnelled a large number of both VLCCs and
Suezmaxes into Atlantic business compared to recent months.
North Sea
North Sea activity has built through the week, with a lot of tonnage now on subs for
end / early dates, in addition to vessel on subs, the available tonnage has been kept
slimmer, with tonnage ballasting to fix in the Mediterranean and tonnage also being
picked off for forward dates in the US. Paper was trending down due to lack of activity,
but with a busy market a less populated list and alternative markets, it looks like we
have found the bottom on the rates and owners' sentiment is looking to push again.
Mediterranean
Mediterranean remains strong and rates have bounced back a bit so far this week.
Steady activity ex Sidi or STS Port Said is absorbing tonnage while the other local load
ports should be sold-out for October. We have started to see early November dates
for local runs and we expect rates to firm up again.

This week in the Capesize market, renewed momentum sees bullish sentiment in both
Pacific and Atlantic markets. The Pacific Market, C5 (West Australia/China) saw a solid
improvement over the last two days after a gradual recovery throughout the week,
jumping a further 8.92% today on paper amid bullish sentiment and renewed supply.
Ballasters from China to Australia have faced some disruptions amid Typhoon Champi
with ongoing schedule delays, causing some tightness in the region. On the C3
Brazil/China front, rates continue to improve as the week progresses, with improved
levels for November dates as paper improves by 4% today on C3. A recent fixture


Panamax
Supramax
Rates
concluded at USD 33.75 for a mid-November laycan, and rumors of an earlier stem
fixed in mid USD 34 levels is yet to be confirmed. Charterers' ideas for late November
hover in the mid USD 34 levels as we see a continued improved sentiment as the week
progresses.
The Panamax market has softened further this week, with Atlantic losses outweighing
firmer Pacific fixtures. In the Atlantic, fresh transatlantic inquiry has offered some
support, but weak USG grain demand and a long November ECSA ballaster list
continue to weigh on fronthaul rates, leaving charterers in little hurry to cover. Aussie
coal is giving Pacific owners better support, with firmer fixtures and fewer prompt
ships also helping NoPac returns despite quieter grain demand, while Indo activity has
picked up. Owners are using those stronger spot returns to defend period offers, but
weaker FFAs are keeping buyers well below their ideas and limiting concluded
business. We expect further pressure in the Atlantic, while the Pacific should hold up
better provided Aussie cargo flow continues, with possible weather delays adding
support to nearby dates rather than signaling a wider market recovery.
The Handysize and Supramax markets showed mixed sentiment, with the Atlantic
remaining subdued, particularly in the US Gulf, while the South Atlantic continued to
strengthen on improving cargo demand and tighter tonnage. The Continent and
Mediterranean remained broadly stable, with stronger rates mainly seen on specific
trades. In the Pacific, sentiment remained positive on Supramaxes, while Handysize
activity was quieter but supported by healthy enquiries and relatively tight tonnage.
