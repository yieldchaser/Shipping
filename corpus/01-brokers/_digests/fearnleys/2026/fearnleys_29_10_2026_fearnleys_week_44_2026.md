01Tankers
VLCC
It’s been a fixing frenzy in the week gone by and tonnage availability in the East and
globally now is lower than at any stage during the last two weeks, and rates set to rise
further. T34 paper has been traded at WS 650 for November and now asking higher,
underpinning the general sentiment that the physical market is around the WS 650+
mark for same despite lack of “proof” that same has been concluded. However, a
couple of reported fixtures Yanbu/East in the mid WS 700’s, a trade normally yielding
a 100-point premium give or take over Fujairah/GOO, also support same. A Brazil
export cargo destined for China was concluded at WS 555 yesterday, up 20 points
from last done and further evidence of a strengthening market. Oil prices are also on
an upward trajectory after a Houthi missile attack on Saudi Arabia's biggest oil refinery
yesterday. Both oil and shipping markets are anxiously waiting for greater clarity on
President Trump's next move toward Iran with strong rumours of a planned attack
after the midterm election on November 3rd.

Rates
9 offers on one West Africa cargo is slightly misleading - a huge flat rate commanding
the appetite - as overall FOC availabilities, particularly of non-compromised ships, are
very low and supply constriction in the 2 week window is relevant again now that we
are only fixing around 18 days forwards. CPC has only fixed down on a relet, and
Guyana & USG business will not be afforded the additional buffer eastern ballasters
give West Africa. Suezmax share in West Africa has also returned after 1st decade
activity on VLCC picked up a little after October placed a record 88MBBLS on
Suezmaxes at the expense of VLCCs. East of Suez despite lengthening of availabilities
we are on a collision course with a firmer VLCC market where availabilities have once
again constricted. That could underpin the bottoming and potential firming of the
Atlantic basin for the time being.
North Sea
Rates have remained relatively steady from last week with limited market activity. With
relets in position and what looks like a fairly light crude program in the first decade,
natural window should push beyond the 10th without much in the way of fixtures.
Tonnage in the early part of the list still has options to ballast to Mediterranean and
US markets to avoid waiting time. There has been a lack of tonnage ballasting
transatlantic, which we expect to increase heading into November, which will even
without market activity keep the available tonnage from growing too much.
Mediterranean
It's a rollercoaster week so far in the Mediterranean with replacement jobs done well
above the WS 900 mark but the normal fixing window is getting fixed back to where it
was at the end of last week. Ceyhan should be done for the 1st decade of November
with nothing close to WS 800 being paid with bigger flats than the Augusta. Expecting
rates to remain north of WS 750 depending on minimum flat and dates.

Panamax
Supramax
buoyancy this morning after yesterday's late October and early November fixtures
concluded in the low USD 14s range. Today some cargoes for early November were
done in the mid-high USD 14s with one rumor of a fixture at USD 15 linked to Cargill
(laycan to be confirmed). The Atlantic saw a similar story as the Baltic paper gained 5%
today. A fresh wave of cargoes came to market, with charterers bidding in the USD 34-
35s range for second half November laycans. December stems are bidding similarly in
the USD 35 range. This morning we saw one fixture was reported at USD 35 for a 25
November stem from Cargill on a standard Cape, and a Newcastlemax fixed today at
USD 34.25 for Brazil/China. More recently we hear reports of a fixture in the high USD
35s-36 range. Some owners are showing some reluctance to offer as bullish sentiment
is expected to show some mild rate increases in the near term.
The Panamax market has weakened across both basins this week, with lower fixtures
confirming that the Pacific’s earlier support is giving way. In the Atlantic, fresh grain
and mineral demand remains too thin to absorb available tonnage, while the long
November ECSA ballaster list continues to push transatlantic and fronthaul rates
lower. The Pacific is now following, as fewer Aussie and NoPac cargoes compete for
the ships available, and softer Indo coal demand gives charterers more room to press
rates. Period interest remains selective, with owners’ ideas still above buyers’ levels
and the weaker forward curve limiting commitments. Our vessel-tightness indicators
suggest scope for a bounce after this correction, but shipment growth still trails fleet
growth, leaving us cautious about how far any recovery can run into winter.
The Supra market remained relatively subdued, with limited fresh activity and most
reported fixtures concluded earlier. The Continent and Mediterranean remain broadly
balanced, while the USG and South Atlantic are softening amid cautious charterer
demand. Asia continues to show positive sentiment, supported by tight tonnage and
steady cargo demand, although rate increases are beginning to plateau. The Handy
market was similarly quiet, with soft conditions across the Continent and
Mediterranean. The South Atlantic remains relatively firm on tightening tonnage and
fresh demand, while the USG is broadly stable. Asia continues to show resilience,
supported by tight prompt tonnage and a steady cargo book. Overall, fresh enquiry
will be key to sustaining momentum across both segments.

After a brief rally that saw cargoes conclude around the USD 330/t level in the middle
of last week, freight rates have gradually softened, with recent fixtures reported below
USD 315/t. Market activity remains subdued and some of the fixing is occurring
behind closed doors, contributing to an overall quieter sentiment.
We have registered 28 fixtures for Nov thus far (7/9/12 by decade), and market
attention is now shifting toward December, where three spot fixtures ex-US Gulf have
been concluded so far.


LPG Rates
The East-West arbitrage remains open and continues to support cargo economics at
current freight levels. While vessel availability remains broadly balanced against
demand, the recent correction suggests charterers have regained some negotiating
power. Going forward, participants will be closely monitoring fixture volumes and
fresh cargo emergence to determine whether rates have found support or if further
downside remains likely in the weeks ahead.
