VLCC
Suezmax
The week gone by has been a bit “could’ve, would’ve, should’ve”. After bottoming out
end of last week at WS 300 for a Fujairah/Far East run expectations rose. Monday
kicked off with a tighter position list than the previous Monday and the Baltic TD34
index went from WS 308 and change to WS 326, albeit off smidgeon to WS 324.64 last
night – and it is rare for a collective broking community to price a market 25 points
give or take over last recorded fixture without it coming to fruition, and if it has it’s
strange for it to be kept under wraps by the owning community. Is it the quiet before
the storm or is it going to fizzle out again? The jury is out, but the position list remains
somewhat challenging in that the tonnage on offer is controlled by fewer hands and
the relet contingency has shrunk considerably. Onwards and…?


Aframax
Rates
Alterations in market fundamentals day-to-day are causing constant fluctuations in
sentiment, but when it comes to fixing material changes in rates have been limited.
We remain at circa WS 300 on TD20 with TD6 still in the WS 420-425 bandwidth. CPC
is well catered to both by local East Mediterranean sailers & supplementary tonnage
willing to ballast ex east via Suez. Fujairah still fails to deliver and this drove ships to
consider the Suez and COGH ballasts, also undermining what might otherwise have
been a stronger market in the Atlantic. West Africa has fixed forwards with only a
couple stems remaining in the first decade, as well as eroding the forward window
with half a dozen Suezmaxes booked off the 2nd decade. In the wake of this there was
evidence to support bearishness due to potential for tonnage build as prompt vessels
were being left in no man's land with dates moving further away from them. But a
timely overnight spike in AFRA USG/Transatlantic rates to levels that support Suezmax
crossover demand should trim some of this fat, as might USG/East runs off dates that
VLCCs - still cheaper in the Atlantic than Suezmaxes across the board - cannot quite
cater to.
North Sea
After weakening the market has found its level in the early part of the week, stable in
the mid WS 200’s although this is also off the back of limited activity. Natural window
is moving into what could be a busier last 5 days of November, and the list will come
under some pressure with early vessels ballasting towards a much firmer US market
and some weather delays causing some programming issues.
Mediterranean
Steady activity so far this week has kept rates stable but has also reduced the list a lot
for good uncompromised tonnage. Dates have reached out towards end month which
may limit what owners see for November now, but with a firming US market and a
potentially challenging position list, owners' sentiment will be firming.

After a slow decline earlier in the week, the Pacific C5 West Australia/China saw a
rebound today as renewed miner activity boosted paper value by nearly 5% today.


Panamax
Supramax
Fixing was active in the Pacific this morning with C5 fixing a handful of early December
laycans in the low-mid USD 14s. In the Atlantic, C3 Brazil/China saw less attention this
week as rates slightly stagnated. Offers held around USD 36 levels as owners held firm,
and bidding competed in the USD 34-35 range. A few fixtures concluded yesterday on
C3 were done in flat-low USD 35 levels and some in the high USD 34s. Similar levels
seen today at fixing, with bidding rounds showing a tick lower, as bids compete in the
mid-high USD 33s for late December laycans and owners pushing down to mid-high
USD 35 offers. Mid December stems are still rating in the mid USD 34s for the time
being. The North Atlantic saw some fresh activity this week, with a few backhaul and
transatlantic fixtures done. One fronthaul was rumored fixed today in the USD 48
levels from Port Cartier/Qingdao.
The Panamax market maintained a steady-to-firmer tone this week, with Atlantic
strength offsetting a softer Pacific. ECSA remained the key driver, supported by tight
early-December tonnage and firm grain fronthaul demand, while transatlantic and US
mineral enquiry provided further support. However, growing Continent tonnage and
discounted late-December dates suggest a more balanced northern Atlantic.
Meanwhile, the Pacific remained under pressure, with ample tonnage and limited fresh
NoPac grain enquiry outweighing healthy Australian and Indonesian coal demand. Our
research indicates shipment growth continues to lag fleet expansion, while tightness
indicators suggest the recent rebound is losing momentum. Combined with weak
Chinese macroeconomic conditions, we expect a broadly sideways market into early
December, with Atlantic tightness providing a floor and Pacific oversupply limiting
recovery, although coal futures suggest gradual firming over the next two months.
The Handy market is generally balanced, with the Indian Ocean and Atlantic showing
better cargo support, while the Pacific remains more competitive. The Supra/Ultramax
market is well supplied across most regions, with steady cargo enquiry across grains
all cargoes. The Atlantic remains the most active area, while the Indian Ocean and
Pacific continue to see reasonable activity. However, tonnage availability is limiting
upward pressure on rates, with owners remaining flexible on trading direction and
employment options. Overall, the market remains active but largely balanced, with
regional tonnage supply, positioning and the bid/offer gap continuing to influence

freight levels. Period sentiment remains relatively balanced, although fixture activity is

The USG market has been relatively quiet this week, although fixing activity continues
for late-second and early-third-decade December cargoes ex USG.
The latest VLGC fixture was concluded in the low USD 300s pmt H/C, albeit at a slight
discount for Cape-only routing. Overall activity remains measured, but underlying
demand is still present, with an Asian Major expected to quote a 25–26 December
USG cargo tomorrow.


LPG Rates
We currently count around 12 spot fixtures for December ex-USG, split 6/5/1 by
decade. With third-decade coverage still limited, several additional cargoes are
expected to surface before the December program is complete. At the same time,
available tonnage is gradually being absorbed, with ships slowly disappearing from
the position list.
