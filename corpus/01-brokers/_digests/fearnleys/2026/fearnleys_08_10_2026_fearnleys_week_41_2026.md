01Tankers
VLCC
Suezmax
We’re finding it challenging to describe the same in a different way, but in the word of
one player: “it’s soon WS 1000 in all segments for some enquires or another”. Said
with the tongue in the cheek of course, but indicative of the sentiment, and a quick
glance at the position lists does not bode much reprieve for charterers any time soon.
These historical rates and corresponding earning might paradoxically be the only
thing possibly capping continued upward movement. Do I push for another few points
or do I take my USD Gazillion a day earnings and move on? Oil supply continues to
increase, and shipping data shows regional crude exports from the Middle East
actually exceeded pre-war levels on several days in late September. The VLCCs smaller
sisters, Suez- and Aframaxes, are showing no sign of slowing down either so there’s
really nowhere to hide right now.


Aframax
Rates
Despite further coverage ex-West Africa, we still have approx 10 mbbls left for 3rd
decade. In terms of demand elsewhere, CPC should be largely closed out for Oct, with
new stems released next week, while Guyana also has healthy coverage for Oct. From
the USG, Aframaxes remain competitive against Suezmaxes on a USD/bbl basis.
Supply wise, there are 7 FOC (2x20+) and 10 ETS next 7 days (2x18+ / 1x15+) that
could make Oct dates agw. On the surface, this appears to be a manageable
supply/demand ratio. However, those VLCCs willing to trade in the Atlantic for
October/early November dates have been completely hoovered up, while Fujairah
rates continue to provide sufficient pull to retain owners' attention in the East. As
such, securing coverage now is of high importance, as November is poised to
continue funnelling a high proportion of cargoes towards Suezmaxes. Additionally, we
expect to see the full impact of the armada of tonnage already fixed East, leaving
supply under extreme pressure in the West and further exacerbated by seasonal
weather delays.
North Sea
The Aframax tonnage list in the North Sea is not looking too tight on paper. But the
actual candidates for North Sea loadings in the current fixing window are more or less
close to nothing. Surrounding markets are offering better returns for owners and will
as such ballast out of the area. We expect further upward pressure on rates moving
forward in October.
Mediterranean
Rates for the local Mediterranean/Black Sea market is at all time high levels. Despite
plenty of vessels on the front end of the list and still quite a few of cargoes still
outstanding, there is a Mexican standoff at the moment as charterers are trying to
repeat levels around last-done while most of the owners are still pushing for higher.
Cashing out on some positions seems to be a good choice though but sentiment
prevails now over fundamentals.

Daily Earnings (TCE) C5: 34860 vs C3: 31183
The Panamax market has traded broadly sideways through Golden Week in China,
with the latest BPI gains pointing to selective firming rather than a sustained recovery.
In the Atlantic, fresh NCSA grain inquiry is supporting TA business and October ECSA
positions remain relatively tight, while FH activity is quieter and bid–offer spreads
remain wide. The Pacific has regained some momentum on Aussie and NoPac
business, although limited Indo inquiry continues to restrain the southern market.
Period interest persists, and the FFA curve has firmed at the front end, but its discount
into next year argues for a measured outlook; our in-house shipment research shows
stronger Atlantic coal and Pacific grain flows against weaker Indian Ocean volumes,
suggesting a position-led market rather than uniform tightening across both basins.
The Handy market has remained strong across both basins, where we see numerous
strong fixtures for both transatlantic and transpacific trips. Still seeing a healthy
amount of short-period and multiple laden-leg fixtures at healthy levels, with owners
eager to fixing out whilst holding firm on rates.
