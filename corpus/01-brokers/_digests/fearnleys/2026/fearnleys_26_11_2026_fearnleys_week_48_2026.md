01Tankers
VLCC
Suezmax
Activity has picked up this week, with several charterers having dipped their feet in for
early December dates and a prompt end-Nov Fujairah/East cargo covered at WS 332.5
and end 1st decade Dec Fujairah/Korea at WS 335, up from last done. The front-end
tonnage list has thinned considerably while uncovered cargoes continue to provide
support, whilst still providing a cheaper alternative than Suezmaxes. While further
upward risk is evident a Mexican standoff at the writing moment will test the resolve
on both sides of the fence. The Atlantic has largely been left to Suez- and Aframaxes,
with both segments having gained further momentum, and should charterers be
tempted to scale up they’ll have few ships to choose from with natural positions in the
current loading window, whilst potential ballasters from the East now benchmark a
stronger Middle East market.


Aframax
Rates
With Suezmax impetus coming from USG spillover the market corrected up to align
with TD25 which is now deflating. Suezmaxes are only competitive for
USG/Transatlantic at WS 322 on a USD/BBL bss, the same TCE as a TD20 run bss circa
WS 377. Whilst there was clear resistance on options cargoes ex West Africa, this
changing dynamic brings UKCM tonnage back into play for TD20 for a window in
which both West Africa & Brazil markets are fixed well forwards. Tonnage builds on
VLCCs east of Suez undermine the prospect of Fujairah driving overall market
resistance, and with circa 6 stems left up to the end of the 2nd decade ex West Africa
and Thanksgiving pressing the pause button on Americas markets, charterers
pouncing now on a market peak would be peculiar. Instead expect restraint. In the
interim TD6 has remained in a WS 10 bandwidth with WS 450 last done, and Fujairah
continues to work VLCC scraps prompt.
North Sea
A shortening of available tonnage due to ballasting and vessels delaying especially in
the Baltic has led to an active market for earlier dates than expected so far this week
for end November/early December dates pushing rates up for those vessels with good
itineraries. Early December on paper doesn’t look that busy stem wise but with some
vessels remaining uncertain programming may not be as easy as it should be.
Mediterranean
Big discrepancy in rates between the different cross-Mediterranean runs with owners
trying to push, activity however has been moderate for the 01-05 Dec window. Dates
are pushing further now with a few cargoes asking quietly in the 5-10 window and
tonnage list is looking balanced. Weather is still tricky so itineraries should be closely
monitored.

Panamax
Supramax
Rates
15.9-16.2 range yesterday. Tuesday fixtures concluded with eleven recorded West
Australian fixtures for early December dates, and the bullish market today has only
seen few fixtures conclude so far. C3 Brazil/China saw a handful of fixtures conclude
yesterday within Brazil and West Africa. Koch took a Newcastlemax for mid-late
December dates at mid-low USD 35 levels yesterday from West Africa to China.
Today's ballasters from China are offering in the USD 35-36 range for late December
and early January dates with few fixtures taking place. Last done today was at USD 36
flat for mid-December dates and in the USD 35s for late December. For now, the C3
index looks accurately forecast with the Baltic printing USD 35.26 today.
Both Atlantic and Pacific markets continue to show positive momentum, with
sentiment firming and owners gaining confidence. In the Atlantic, healthy cargo
volumes and tightening prompt tonnage are supporting rates, particularly in the
transatlantic and ECSA. The Pacific is also strengthening, driven by East Australian
minerals and improved NOPAC demand, with shorter tonnage lists and FFA support
helping bids move. Overall, market fundamentals remain positive across both basins.
The market remained broadly steady, with the Atlantic showing a mixed picture. The
USG continued to perform well, supported by healthy demand, while the South
Atlantic retained a positive undertone for Supramax but showed some signs of losing
momentum in the Handy segment. The Continent benefited from fresh scrap demand,
whereas the Mediterranean remained largely subdued. In Asia, North China showed
some improvement, although the South continued to face pressure from excess
tonnage, particularly in the Indian Ocean. Handysize markets were generally stable,
with firm conditions in the US Gulf and steady activity across Europe and the Pacific.
Overall, sentiment remains mixed, with regional supply and demand dynamics
continuing to drive the market.
