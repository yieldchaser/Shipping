01Tankers
VLCC
The VLCC market has been on a downward trajectory in the week gone by and
yesterday saw another downward rate correction. In contrast to yesterday’s TD22
Baltic assessment (USD 41.67m) a report suggested last night that USD 36 mill was
done for a USG/China run, later denied and the ship in question pushed as open again
this morning, but nevertheless indicates the direction of travel. TD34 came in at WS
411 and change, but a fixture has surfaced this morning at WS 360 for a
Fujairah/Japan run, albeit on a ship discharging in Fujairah so will calculate fantastic
with no ballast to speak of despite the lower rate - but this will of course add fuel to
fire. However, all said and done – or to be done – daily returns at USD 350-400k/day
give/take is not to be sniffed at, and owners would have bitten your arm off for that
this time last year. As Carl Jung is known to have said: “It all depends on how we look
at things, and not how they are in themselves.”

Rates
Whilst more active on the demand side we don't have much meat on the bone as far
as November dates are concerned, we are priced out of USG/Transatlantic moves by
AFRAs and VLCCs are hot on our heels for all Atlantic volumes, operating on a 24H
time delay before correcting down with us. As such, against weak supply side
fundamentals [14 FOC pushed, 4 discharging, 7 arrived at disport and 17 total ETS
within 5-days for TD20] there remains downward pressure in the Atlantic. Versus
Atlantic earnings TD6 is preferable ex Central Mediterranean for CPC players even
below last done levels i.e. WS 450, and East of Suez the pile up continues,
exacerbating the impact of a longer VLCC list. Overall without a demand side safety
net then supply side length dictates persistent softness regardless of noise.
North Sea
North Sea rates have kept softening with a general lack of activity and surrounding
weakening markets keeping the downward pressure on rates. Natural dates have
pushed out to the end of the 2nd decade which is forcing the early tonnage to make a
decision, accept waiting or ballast. Unlike the last couple of months where it has been
an obvious choice where to ballast, at the moment it isn’t such a clear road between
Mediterranean and USG. We still have some more room for softening before we will
see rates stabilise.
Mediterranean
There has been healthy activity quietly this week that has helped to mop away a good
number of vessels. Dates are into the 3rd decade for all ports now and owners are
feeling that the bottom has been established. Sideways seems to be fair for the
balance of the week but this remains to be seen. A bit more activity will push the
momentum the other way.

Supramax
USD 14 levels for West Australia/China C5. Bids are still hovering at high USD 13s and
USD 14 flat for C5. In the Atlantic, C3 Brazil/China saw levels remaining around the
USD 35-36 mark this week, with fixtures today concluding at high USD 35s for early
December laycan. Another mid December stem was fixed at low USD 36 levels.
Owners are holding firm with their bids in the USD 37s range today as FFA signals a
minor climb in rates. In the North Atlantic, fresh transatlantic and backhaul stems have
sparked new interest, with a Seven Islands/El Dekheila stem fixing in the USD 21s
range. In the short term as bunker prices are expected to take a dip, some charters are
holding off in the hope for a rates correction, while others are looking to forward
bookings and beginning to cover their January and February stems.
The Panamax market extended its recovery this week, with the BPI rising every session
as Atlantic tonnage tightened, and the Pacific firmed early before leveling off. The
North Atlantic led the move: prompt tonnage in the north was scarce, transatlantic
and fronthaul grain rates moved up day by day, and ECSA held firm for late-Nov and
early-Dec arrivals, while backhaul stayed flat. In the Pacific, fresh Australian and
Indonesian coal and steady demand for modern eco ships supported rates early in the
week, but by midweek more open tonnage in South China met a thinner Australian
cargo list and little NoPac grain, and the transpacific began to ease. Our own data
points to a more cautious near term, with volume growth still trailing fleet growth and
our Pacific and South Atlantic tightness indicators suggesting the rebound may soon
stall, although rising backwardation in the coal futures curve should offer some
support over the next couple of months.
On Supramax segment the Atlantic continues to show improving sentiment, with
North America strengthening on fresh enquiry and owners increasingly firm on
transatlantic requirements. The South Atlantic appears to have stabilised, with a better
supply-demand balance, while the Continent and Mediterranean remain relatively
tight. In contrast, Asia has softened amid lacklustre activity, with Indian Ocean and
backhaul routes remaining under pressure. Southeast Asia is broadly steady, although
fresh enquiry remains limited. Overall, the Atlantic is currently outperforming the
Pacific, with sentiment gradually improving across the former.


Rates
On Handysize -generally quiet market across both basins, with limited fresh enquiry
and little change in underlying fundamentals. USG remains strong, with improving
demand and owners showing increased resistance on prompt positions. The Continent
and Mediterranean remain subdued, with little fresh impetus to drive rates either way.
The South Atlantic is broadly balanced, with participants largely holding their
positions. Asia remains relatively steady, although holiday-related disruptions have
kept activity subdued.
