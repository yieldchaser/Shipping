   MARKET OVERVIEW 
 
The international energy trade is absorbing a profound structural disruption following the forced shutdown 
of Saudi Arabia’s East-West pipeline, previously the kingdom's primary overland bypass around the 
contested Persian Gulf. With pumping stations offline due to recent aerial drone damage, European 
refiners face complete cargo cancellations under term contracts for the upcoming month, sending 
physical benchmark crude soaring past US$130 a barrel as buyers scramble for scarce Atlantic 
replacements. The outage has prompted state exporters to pivot back toward Persian Gulf terminals, 
offering spot barrels for collection outside the Strait of Hormuz to keep supply moving toward Asian 
markets. However, this rerouting offers no immediate relief to European buyers, who face either traversing 
the heavily threatened southern Red Sea corridor or enduring five-week voyages around the African 
continent to secure replacement volumes. 
 
This logistical crisis in the Middle East has driven long-haul tanker chartering costs to historic heights, as 
Asian refiners aggressively turn to the Western Hemisphere for supply security. Freight rates to hire a very 
large crude carrier from the US Gulf Coast to China surged to an unprecedented US$44.8 million—
equating to nearly US$22.50 per barrel and more than doubling costs recorded prior to the outbreak of 
regional hostilities. Despite these staggering transportation expenses, the trade remains commercially 
viable because delivered American crude continues to hold a pricing advantage over Middle Eastern 
grades, while robust refining margins for diesel and gasoline encourage operators to book any available 
prompt tonnage. With vessel availability constrained globally by war-risk avoidance, tracking data 
confirms that at least six supertankers are already committed to loading USG crude for Asian discharge in 
the coming month. 
 
Macroeconomic sentiment has turned increasingly cautious against this backdrop of runaway freight 
rates and surging energy benchmarks. Financial markets experienced heightened volatility this week as 
benchmark government bond yields climbed toward 5%, driven by mounting expectations that persistent 
energy inflation will compel central banks to maintain tight monetary policies. Combin ed with market 
instability is a record quarterly options expiration across equity and futures contracts, which has amplified 
price swings across major indices. At the same time, leading economic indicators showed unexpected 
contractions in domestic manufacturing output as industrial producers grapple with escalating raw 
material expenses and sustained logistics bottlenecks, signalling that prolonged maritime and energy 
disruptions are beginning to weigh heavily on broader economic growth .

Dry bulk market took a breather this week as the Baltic Exchange’s index slipped roughly to 3,370 points, 
marking its seventh consecutive day in the red and reaching its quietest level since September 1st. This 
gentle pullback was led by the larger vessel sizes, with the Capesize index shedding to settle at 5, 768 
points and the Panamax index falling to 2,251 points, whereas the Supramax segment bucked the wider 
retreat by edging up 2.79% to 1,767 points. Even with this mid-week pause, the sector enters its traditional 
autumn upturn on exceptionally solid footing, supported by Capesize earnings comfortably holding well 
above US$40,000 per day and Panamax returns sitting in the low US$20,000s.  
 
These elevated baselines are largely sustained by persistent geopolitical conflicts across the Red, Black, 
and Arabian Seas, which continue to choke operational route efficiency, extend voyage times, and 
severely restrict active fleet supply. Because these tight vessel availability constraints heavily outweigh the 
year-on-year drop in seaborne coal movements, overall market fundamentals remain sturdy, keeping the 
door wide open for a notable fourth-quarter rate surge as pre-winter commodity restocking ramps up.  
 
Segment Highlights 
Capesize: Capesize softened this week, weighed down by reduced Australian iron ore volumes, rising 
Pacific ballast tonnage, and quiet bidding that pushed fresh West Australia fixtures down to US $15.95/ton. 
Reflecting this Pacific-led retreat, Pacific R/V dropped sharply to US$48,125’s, while T/A rates held steady at 
US$54,000’s before facing mounting late-week pressure. With forward FFA paper trading at a discount to 
spot and vessel availability expanding across both basins, freight rates are expected to remain under 
downward pressure unless Western Australian mining fixtures stage a swift recovery.  
 
Panamax / Kamsarmax: Panamax drifted lower this week as persistent vessel oversupply in the North 
Atlantic and lengthening ballast lists into East Coast America shifted bargaining power firmly toward 
charterers. While Indonesian coal movements helped defend the Pacific floor, overall spot returns 
softened with Pacific R/V sliding to US$21,000’s per day and F/H earnings easing to US$30,670’s. Freight 
levels are expected to trade largely sideways as Atlantic weakness continues to counter Pacific demand. 
 
Supramax / Ultramax: Supramax maintained its upward momentum with average rates rising 2.6% w-o-
w, supported by robust USG demand for grain alongside a drop in available Atlantic tonnage. In the East, a 
rise in open Pacific vessels capped short-haul expansion, but healthy Indian Ocean demand, NOPAC 
stems, and renewed period interest provided solid support, lifting Indonesian R/V  to US$15,450’s and B/H 
rates to US$15,500’s. The sector's overall outlook remains cautiously positive as tightening Atlantic vessel 
supply and constructive sentiment across the Asian basins continue to balance localized tonnage.  
 
Handysize: Handysize experienced a relatively quiet week with limited fresh reporting yet maintained a 
fundamentally healthy and balanced tone across both basins. Sentiment remained firmer across the USG 
and South Atlantic on the back of steady cargo flows and a tightening pro mpt tonnage list, while sufficient 
cargo volumes kept a quieter Asian market broadly stable. Spot rates held firm with marginal 
adjustments, as Inter-Pacific routes edged up to US$16,550’s and CIS R/V settled lower at US$15,000’s.

Global crude tanker rates have reached an unprecedented milestone, breaking through the US $1 million 
per day threshold for the first time in history as the scarcity of vessels willing to navigate high -risk waters 
tightens. Data from the Baltic Exchange revealed that chartering a tanker for liftings inside the Persian Gulf 
touched an extraordinary US$1.035 million daily, while runs from outside the chokepoint, such as voyages 
from the Gulf of Oman to China, climbed to US$644,000 per day. Freight inflation is not limited to the 
Middle East; Aframax fixtures from Russia’s Black Sea port of Novorossiysk to West India and North China 
rose for the seventh consecutive week, amid heightened Ukrainian drone activity. This severe capacity 
crunch has driven a dramatic run on second-hand tonnage, where ready-to-sail ships now trade at 
substantial premiums over shipyard orders to bypass multi-year delivery waits.  
 
With the Red Sea transit corridor concurrently threatened by Houthi advancements around the port of 
Mocha and the Bab el-Mandeb chokepoint, Standard Chartered warned that Saudi port inventories hold 
only about one week of operational export cover, exposing the fragile limits of alternative overland 
logistics. Although headline oil prices softened slightly following a 25 basis -point Federal Reserve interest 
rate increase to a 3.75% to 4.00% target range, leaving November Brent down to US$105.60 per barrel and 
October WTI lower to US$102.30 per barrel—physical balances remain acutely tight.  
 
Segment Highlights 
VLCC: MEG rates surged as Chinese refiners aggressively competed for late September barrels, pushing 
Fujairah/East runs towards astronomical WS1,000 levels to bypass the Strait of Hormuz. With vessel supply 
running critically thin, and fresh disruptions to Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline driving alternative s-t-s 
operations, upward rate pressure is set to continue as refiners prioritise  crude security over freight costs. 
Similarly in the Atlantic, WAFR/China also saw gains as TD15 climbed 100 points to WS531. 
 
Suezmax: West African rates skyrocketed in a single day to WS454 as charterers scrambled for tonnage 
amid surging Atlantic crude demand, escalating Middle East geopolitical risks, and a critical shortage of 
VLCCs. The Atlantic is heavily saturated with split-stem inquiries to the USG, a strongly bullish trend is 
poised to persist. While in the Black Sea, CPC/Augusta saw levels climb to WS500. 
 
Aframax: MEG rates surged as expanding regional crude and condensate volumes clashed with a 
shrinking pool of tonnage, driven by massive spillover from a booming VLCC. Across the Mediterranean, 
daily earnings remain high with a balanced tonnage list through late Septe mber, as 80,000mt 
Ceyhan/Lavera ended the week around WS435. 
 
LR1 / LR2: MEG LR2 rates surged, propelled by sustained mid-September demand and active STS 
operations in the Gulf of Oman. Shipowners retain bargaining leverage as TC1 climbed to WS82. In the LR1 
segment, gains were also seen as TC5 MEG/Japan routes. 
 
MR: The Far East market closed on a firm note, bolstered by a steady recovery in Chinese petroleum and 
robust discharge demand for middle distillates into Australia. In the MEG, TC17 trips to E. Africa climbed to 
WS740 at week’s closing.

Market Insights 
 
Alang, India: Buyers continue to display confident buying interest, supported by firm downstream 
manufacturing demand that has helped absorb scrap inventory across rolling mills. Sentiment along the 
coast received an additional psychological lift from high-level regulatory developments in Europe, where 
prominent international shipowner organisations united to advocate for the official approval of two Indian 
yards onto the European List of approved recycling facilities. While the initiative remains at the proposal 
stage, this marks a significant vote of confidence in Alang’s ongoing green transition, reassuring 
shipbreakers that their long-term infrastructure upgrades and investments are steadily gaining traction 
among global stakeholders. 
 
This week on Wednesday, the Indian government reviewed measures to expand the use of steel recovered 
from recycled vessels in domestic manufacturing. A joint meeting chaired by the Ministers of Steel and 
Heavy Industries and Ports, Shipping and Waterways brought together representatives from the 
Department of Science and Technology, Bureau of Indian Standards, Gujarat Maritime Board and the Ship 
Recycling Industries Association. 
 
Discussions focused on the existing regulatory framework and the possibility of permitting ship- recycled 
secondary steel in additional sectors. Wider acceptance could improve value realisation from dismantled 
vessels, strengthen downstream demand and support India’s position as a leading global ship- recycling 
destination. The initiative also aligns with the government’s objectives of promoting a circular economy, 
reducing dependence on primary raw materials and improving the sustainability and efficiency of  
domestic steel production. However, its commercial impact will depend on the introduction of appropriate 
quality standards, certification requirements and broader acceptance by steel- consuming industries. 
 
In its latest country review released on September 18, Moody’s Ratings sharply upgraded India’s real 
economic growth projection for the current fiscal period to 7.0%, citing robust domestic consumption, 
sustained public capital expenditure, and remarkable resilience against external energy volatility. 
Furthermore, national foreign currency reserves remain near historic highs,  around US$780 billion following 
successful central bank capital initiatives, providing an extensive liquidity buffer that helps stabilise the 
rupee against foreign exchange fluctuations.

   COMMODITIES, BUNKERS & RATES 
Commodities Focus 
Benchmark copper on the London Metal Exchange secured its eleventh weekly advance across the past 
twelve weeks, rising 0.2% to close at US$14,521.50/MT and extending an almost 17% gain for the year. This 
persistent strength was buoyed by a pickup in Chinese consumer interest —evidenced by the Yangshan 
port import premium climbing to US$124 per ton, its strongest mark since late 2022—alongside a slight 
monthly contraction in domestic refined metal production, which effectively shielded the market from the 
Federal Reserve's first interest rate hike since 2023.  
 
Singapore iron ore futures rebounded by 1.1% to reach US$97.45 per ton, locking in a weekly improvement 
of around 1% as mills built raw material reserves ahead of October's Golden Week break. Nevertheless, 
underlying fundamentals for the ferrous sector remain delicate, as industry assessments show domestic 
steelmaker profitability languishing under 10%, prompting the China Iron and Steel Association along with 
44 prominent producers to appeal for stringent production discipline to curb surplus inventories.  
 
Meanwhile, the power and fuels market is facing notable tightness as thermal coal valuations at the port 
of Qinhuangdao gained 1.9% this week to hit 980 yuan (US$146) per ton, reaching their highest level since 
October 2023. This price jump, spurred by utility pre-holiday procurement alongside protracted production 
shortfalls following May's safety audits in Shanxi, has motivated Chinese state energy and safety pl anners 
to officially call on major mining regions to speed up operations and reopen idled capacity to protect 
nationwide power generation.
