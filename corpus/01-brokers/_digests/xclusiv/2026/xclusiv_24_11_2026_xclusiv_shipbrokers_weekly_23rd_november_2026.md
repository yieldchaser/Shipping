## Market Report Content

 
Greek Newbuilding Investment Shapes the Next Fleet 
Cycle: 
Greek owners account for more than one in five new-
building orders placed in 2026 across bulk carriers, tank-
ers, containerships and gas carriers, with 455 contracts 
out of 2,200 worldwide. Their participation in this year’s 
contracting exceeds their share of the outstanding order-
book across the three main conventional sectors. Greeks 
represent 16.4% of new bulker orders against 11.3% of 
the global bulker orderbook, 30.7% against 27.0% in tank-
ers, and 14.5% against 12.6% in containers. These figures 
underline the substantial Greek contribution to the next 
generation of shipping capacity. 
Almost half of the Greek dry bulk orderbook reflects con-
tracts placed this year. The 100 vessels ordered in 2026 
are equivalent to 48.3% of the 207 ships outstanding, 
compared with 33.3% globally. The preference for larger tonnage is pronounced. Capesizes and Newcastlemaxes ac-
count for 45% of Greek bulker contracts, against 24% worldwide. Furthermore, 22 of the 25 Newcastlemaxes on the 
Greek orderbook were contracted this year, while Greek owners placed 23 of the 32 Capesize orders recorded globally. 
This concentration gives Greek investment decisions particular significance for future competition in the larger sizes. 
The supply implications vary considerably within dry bulk. The global orderbook has reached 15.7% of fleet dwt, from 
10.7% a year earlier. However, the combined Cape/VLOC orderbook by vessel count is equivalent to 282% of the fleet 
aged 21 years and above, compared with 62% for Handysizes. Larger bulkers consequently have greater potential for 
fleet expansion beyond replacement of the oldest ships. Handysize supply commitments remain more restrained, alt-
hough Greek participation in this year’s ordering is limited to one vessel out of 73 worldwide. 
Tankers attracted 211 Greek contracts, accounting for 46% of Greek ordering across the four sectors and equivalent to 
46.1% of the outstanding Greek tanker orderbook. VLCCs and Suezmaxes dominate with 68 and 50 orders respectively, 
together representing 56% of Greek tanker contracts. Greeks account for approximately half of worldwide Suezmax 
ordering and 39% of VLCC/ULCC contracting this year. Their investment therefore carries substantial exposure to the 
future balance of large crude tanker markets. 
Fleet ageing provides a basis for tanker renewal, with approximately 48% of vessels aged 16 years or above. Mean-
while, the global orderbook has increased to 26.1% of fleet dwt, from 15.3% a year earlier. Delivery schedules place 
much of this capacity further ahead. Approximately 76% of VLCC/ULCC and 73% of Suezmax ordered dwt is scheduled 
for 2028 onwards. The commercial effect will depend 
partly on how much older capacity leaves service before 
these deliveries arrive. 
Containerships present another distinct replacement 
profile. Greek owners placed 99 orders this year, equiva-
lent to 39.9% of their outstanding programme, concen-
trating on Feeders, Handies and Panamaxes. Globally, 
the orderbook stands at 44.5% of fleet TEU capacity, up 
from 32% a year earlier. Yet the 642 Feeders on order 
represent only 54% of the 1,184 existing vessels aged 21 
or above. This provides considerable potential for re-
placement within a sector facing substantial overall ca-
pacity additions. 
Gas investment adds further exposure to expanding delivery pipelines, with 45 Greek contracts, including 25 VLGCs 
and 16 LNG carriers of 141,000–200,000 cbm. The global VLGC orderbook already represents 48.3% of fleet capacity, 
while LNG stands at 36.5%. Across these investment programmes, earnings will influence both owners’ willingness to 
retire older vessels and the market’s ability to absorb deliveries.
