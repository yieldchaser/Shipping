## Market Report Content

 
Product Tankers: Less Cargo, Higher Earnings – The Mar-
ket Paradox : 
The product tanker market has experienced an extraordi-
nary freight rally between early August and mid -October 
2026, despite a significant contraction in global refined 
petroleum product trade. While declining cargo volumes 
would normally translate into weaker vessel demand and 
softer freight rates, the opposite has occurred. Refinery 
disruptions, geopolitical developments and changing ves-
sel employment have created a market increasingly driv-
en by tonnage availability and geographical imbalances 
rather than underlying cargo growth. 
According to Signal Ocean data, global seaborne refined 
product shipments declined by 10.9% year -on-year be-
tween 1 August and 15 October, falling from 265.96 mil-
lion MT in 2025 to 237.07 million MT in 2026. Clean petroleum products accounted for most of this contraction, declin-
ing by 12.1% to 186.75 million MT, with naphtha recording the steepest reduction at 23.7%, followed by jet fuel at 
14.5%, diesel at 10.3% and gasoline at 9.9%. Meanwhile, fuel oil shipments decreased by 5.8% to 50.32 million MT, 
highlighting the broader weakness in refined product transportation demand. 
Interestingly, this contraction has coincided with an exceptional strengthening in freight earnings. Between 3 August 
and 14 October, all 18 monitored Baltic Exchange product tanker routes registered gains, with LR2 and LR1 vessels 
leading the rally. The LR2 Middle East Gulf –UK Continent route (TC20) surged from USD 108,734/day to USD 324,060/
day, an increase of 198%, while the LR1 equivalent (TC8) climbed 173% to USD 234,399/day. Similarly, the LR2 Middle 
East Gulf–Japan route (TC1) reached USD 284,762/day, compared to USD 138,253/day in early August. The Mediterra-
nean–Far East LR2 route (TC15) recorded an even more remarkable 414% increase, reaching USD 172,513/day. 
The MR market has followed a similar upward trajectory, although geographical differences remain considerable. 
South Korea–Singapore (TC11) earnings increased by 261% to USD 64,144/day, while West Coast India –Japan (TC12) 
climbed 280% to USD 50,642/day. Singapore –Australia (TC7) strengthened by 188% to USD 67,775/day. Conversely, 
the Atlantic market experienced a less pronounced recovery, with US Gulf–UK Continent (TC14) increasing by just 35%, 
illustrating the stronger momentum across Eastern Hemisphere trades. 
A particularly important development is the changing employment pattern of larger product -capable tankers. During 
the examined period, Aframaxes engaged in crude trad-
ing increased by 20.3% year-on-year to 858 vessels, while 
crude-trading Panamaxes rose 18.6% to 134 units. Con-
versely, clean -trading LR2 participation declined by 
39.2% to 158 vessels, while LR1 participation fell 9.6% to 
208 units. With crude tanker earnings reaching excep-
tionally elevated levels, owners have increasingly found 
dirty trading more attractive, effectively withdrawing 
substantial capacity from the clean market. Importantly, 
returning these vessels to refined product transportation 
requires tank cleaning, preparation and additional time, 
preventing an immediate supply response. 
Looking ahead, geopolitical developments could further support this imbalance. The recent US–Russia agreement facil-
itating Russian diesel shipments, alongside Washington’s temporary sanctions relief, could generate additional product 
tanker employment and reshape trading routes.  
However, the actual increase in shipments remains uncertain. Should additional cargo demand materialise while LR 
vessels continue favouring lucrative crude employment, clean tanker availability could tighten further. Consequently, 
freight earnings may remain elevated despite declining overall trade volumes, with vessel deployment and geopolitical 
developments increasingly determining the market’s direction.

• Global equity markets delivered mixed weekly performances.

• Crude oil fell approximately 2% toward $99 per barrel on 
Monday, reversing part of last week ’s rally as recovering sup-
plies offset geopolitical risks. US Energy Secretary Chris 
Wright said Persian Gulf flows had reached approximately 
80% of prewar levels, although refined products remained 
constrained. President Trump announced a Russian diesel ex-
port agreement and postponed additional sanctions. Mean-
while, Ukrainian attacks hit Russian oil infrastructure, Saudi 
clashes with Houthi rebels continued, and three tankers suf-
fered damage in the strategic Strait of Hormuz. 
• Coal futures hovered around $150 per ton, near a three -year 
high, as expensive LNG encouraged greater coal use for power 
generation. Asian and European buyers increased imports 
ahead of winter, with coal approximately 80% cheaper than 
LNG. The IEA forecast record global coal demand this year 
despite ongoing energy transition efforts. Meanwhile, tighter 
Indonesian export restrictions contributed to a 23% shipment 
decline in August, while lower quotas and policy uncertainty 
could reduce annual production by over 12 million metric 
tons.
