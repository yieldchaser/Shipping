## Market Report Content

 
Kamsarmax Rebounds, Soybeans Set Records 
The dry bulk market continues to demonstrate con-
trasting dynamics across the Kamsarmax and Ultramax 
segments, although the considerable earnings differential 
observed in late October has narrowed during the first 
half of November. According to Baltic Exchange data, av-
erage Kamsarmax earnings (P5TC) increased by 5.7% 
week-on-week, reaching USD 19,063/day on 13 Novem-
ber, compared with USD 16,999/day at the end of Octo-
ber. Meanwhile, Ultramax earnings (S11TC) stood at USD 
23,203/day, recording a more modest weekly increase of 
1.1%. Consequently, the earnings differential between 
the two segments has narrowed from USD 6,570/day on 
30 October to approximately USD 4,140/day, reflecting an 
improvement in Kamsarmax market conditions. 
Despite this recent recovery, Kamsarmax earnings remain 
11.2% below their end-September levels, highlighting the persistent pressure facing the segment. The divergence be-
tween the two vessel classes continues to reflect differences in cargo exposure, regional tonnage availability and trad-
ing flexibility. Kamsarmax vessels remain particularly dependent on coal and grain movements, while Ultramaxes ben-
efit from a more diversified cargo base, including minor bulks, fertilizers, steel products and agricultural commodities. 
Their onboard cranes also provide greater flexibility in serving smaller ports and terminals with limited infrastructure. 
Against this backdrop, developments in the Brazilian soybean trade offer an encouraging perspective for dry bulk de-
mand. According to market data, Brazil exported a record 108.4 million tonnes of soybeans during January–October 
2026, representing a 7.9% increase year-on-year. Remarkably, shipments during these ten months have already ex-
ceeded the entire 2025 volume by approximately 600,000 tonnes. Second-quarter exports reached 44.9 million 
tonnes, up 10.3% year-on-year, while third-quarter volumes increased by 8.1% to 29.7 million tonnes. 
These developments are particularly relevant for the Panamax/Kamsarmax and Supramax/Ultramax segments, which 
play an important role in agricultural commodity transportation. With China remaining the principal destination for 
Brazilian soybeans, continued growth in exports could generate additional tonne-mile demand, especially as long-haul 
Brazil–Asia shipments absorb vessel capacity for extended periods. 
Nevertheless, the seasonal distribution of Brazilian exports remains an important consideration. October shipments 
declined by almost 14% compared with September, reflecting the gradual winding down of the previous harvest sea-
son. Therefore, although annual export volumes have reached record levels, their immediate contribution to freight 
market strength may remain uneven, depending on ship-
ment timing and available tonnage. 
Looking further ahead, the outlook remains constructive. 
The USDA projects Brazil's 2026/27 soybean production 
at 186 million tonnes, with exports expected to reach 
118 million tonnes. If realised, these volumes could pro-
vide further support to dry bulk employment during the 
upcoming export season, particularly across long-haul 
routes towards Asia. 
In the near term, winter coal demand and North Ameri-
can grain shipments could also contribute to improving 
Kamsarmax employment. However, stronger electricity 
consumption does not necessarily translate into higher seaborne coal imports, particularly when inventories remain 
sufficient or domestic production covers additional requirements. 
Overall, while the recent narrowing of the Kamsarmax–Ultramax earnings gap suggests some rebalancing between the 
segments, sustained recovery will depend on stronger cargo volumes absorbing available vessel supply. Record Brazili-
an soybean exports provide a positive underlying demand signal, but regional tonnage distribution, seasonal trade 
patterns and actual tonne-mile growth will remain decisive in determining freight market direction.
