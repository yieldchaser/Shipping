Freight markets are becoming increasingly differentiated across the major East–West trades, with congestion, capacity management and shifting demand creating uneven conditions between lanes.

Drewry’sWorld Container Index (WCI), at $4,465 per 40ft as of 3 September, with Transpacific and Transatlantic rates increasing 4% and 1% respectively, while Asia–North Europe/Med rates fell 7%. The index remained 112% higher than a year ago.

Meanwhile, across the major East–West trades, 47 blank sailings are expected from week 37 (7–13 September) to week 41 (5–11 October), out of 729 planned sailings, representing a 6% cancellation rate, with 94% of scheduled sailings still expected to operate. Disruptions are concentrated on the Transpacific eastbound trade (68%), followed by Asia–North Europe/Med (17%) and the Transatlantic (15%). Despite these adjustments, carrier schedules remain resilient, with Gemini Cooperation recording the lowest blank sailing rate among the major carrier groups at just 1%.

Against this backdrop, congestion, Panama Canal constraints and evolving Suez routings continue to complicate vessel deployment and schedule recovery.

With October bringing further capacity adjustments and post-Golden Week demand shifts, will trade lanes begin to converge or will operational disruption and capacity management continue to drive them apart? The next edition of Container Capacity Insight will assess how these conditions translate into freight rates, available capacity and schedule reliability.
