## Market Commentary & Analysis

The Drewry World Container Index (WCI), the benchmark widely referenced by procurement teams, decreased 1% to $4,389 per 40ft container this week. The decline can mainly be attributed to lower rates on the Transpacific and Asia-Europe trade routes, as port congestion improved and demand eased at Chinese ports.

On the Transpacific trade route, rates from Shanghai to New York fell 3% to $9,303 per 40ft container, while rates from Shanghai to Los Angeles were unchanged at $7,539 per 40ft container. Port congestion at major Chinese ports, which persisted for several weeks, has begun to ease, with average vessel waiting times at Shanghai improving to 35 hours last week as weather conditions improved and demand softened.

On the Asia-Europe trade route, rates from Shanghai to Rotterdam decreased 2% to $3,615 per 40ft container, while rates from Shanghai to Genoa remained stable at $4,166 per 40ft container. Carriers are pushing through FAK increases in mid-November as they look to strengthen their position ahead of next year’s contract negotiations. However, effective capacity in the market continues to increase, as the resumption of transits via the Suez Canal shortens transit times and releases additional capacity.
