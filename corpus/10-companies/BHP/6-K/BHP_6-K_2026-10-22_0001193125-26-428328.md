**Ross McEwan, Chair**

Ladies and gentlemen, it is a pleasure to be here in Sydney.

And of course in New South Wales, where the BHP story began more than 140 years ago, about 900km from here.

I have to say, I am still amazed by the history of this company.

It’s an incredible story of perseverance, reinvention and growth that started with a single discovery.

I’m actually heading to Broken Hill soon and I can’t wait to see it for myself.

Today’s AGM is an important opportunity to hear from you, answer your questions and talk openly about the business.

We’ll discuss our performance, the opportunities we see ahead and our plans to make the most of them.

Welcome also to shareholders joining us online from around Australia and around the world.

Thank you for your continued support.

It has been a big year for BHP.

We’ve had a smooth leadership transition.

Another strong year of operational and financial performance.

And we’ve advanced growth projects that will shape the future of the company.

All of that gives the Board confidence in the road ahead.

But before I talk about performance or growth, I want to start with what matters most.

**Safety**

And that is safety.

Everyone who works for BHP, or alongside BHP, should return home safely at the end of every shift.

In the last few months, two of our colleagues have lost their lives on site. One at BMA’s Peak Downs mine in Queensland, and one at Escondida in Chile.

These are tragedies.

They are tragedies for their family and friends, their workmates, and for their wider communities.

It affects all of us at BHP, including the Board. And so it should.

We are asking hard questions of ourselves and of the business. We owe that to the people affected, and to every person who comes to work across our operations each day.

Mining has made tremendous progress on safety over many decades. Terrible events like this remind us that our work is not done.

Our focus remains on eliminating fatalities and serious injuries at BHP altogether.

Let me be very clear - there is nothing more important.

**Leadership and governance**

This year marked an important transition for BHP.

Mike Henry retired as Chief Executive Officer after more than six years in the role and a long career with the company.

Mike left BHP in excellent shape.

During his time as CEO, the company became simpler and performed better. And it is much better positioned for the future.

On behalf of the Board, I want to thank Mike for his outstanding leadership and contribution.

And now we have Brandon in the driver’s seat.

The Board appointed Brandon Craig following a thorough succession process. He is the right person to lead BHP into its next chapter.

Since taking on the role on 1 July, Brandon has brought his own energy, clear thinking and deep operational experience.

After 27 years with BHP, working at each of our major operations in Australia and The Americas, he knows this company as well as anyone.

He has a strong understanding of our people, our assets and our opportunities.

The Board has great confidence in his leadership.

I would also like to welcome Mark Vassella, who joined the Board on 1 June.

Many of you will know Mark from his leadership of BlueScope Steel. You might not know his first ever job was working for BHP in Newcastle.

Mark brings valuable experience, sound judgement and a deep understanding of Australian industry. We look forward to benefiting from his insights in the years ahead.

**Performance and value creation**

It was another good year for BHP.

What does that mean for shareholders?

For 2026, the Board approved total dividends equivalent to about \$2.40 Australian per share.

That is US\$8.7 billion returned to shareholders and represents our highest dividend in four years.

And the record highs in our share price over the last few months show how the market is valuing us.

Our dividend and share price directly benefit our more than 600,000 shareholders, as well as millions more Australians whose superannuation funds invest in BHP.

I hope the shareholders in this room were pleased with these results.

For the longer term, our FY2026 dividend also says something important about the company, in the eyes of the Board.

It means the business is strong and performing well. It means we can fund our future plans and reward shareholders at the same time.

It shows our confidence in the future of your company.

**Looking ahead**

One of the reasons we’re confident is because of the way our business is positioned for the future.

Mining is cyclical – there are peaks and troughs.

What we have is a portfolio that is designed to perform through that cycle.

The world needs growth. It needs new infrastructure, reliable energy, modern technology and food security.

All of those things depend on the resources that BHP produces.

China remains a major centre of industrial activity and commodity demand. They remain a huge customer market for us.

And India is entering what we expect will be a multi-decade period of infrastructure investment, manufacturing growth and economic development.

On top of that, all of the infrastructure and wiring for renewable energy, and Artificial Intelligence.

Taken together, these trends give us confidence that demand for copper, iron ore and steelmaking coal will remain strong for many years to come.

Jansen, our Canadian potash business, is on track to deliver its first production mid next year. A growing population needs more food from the same amount of farm land. Fertilisers, like potash, will be key to improving agricultural productivity.

**Competitiveness**

We have a range of options to grow across our commodities.

It’s a good problem to have. But it also means we pay a lot of attention to competitiveness.

We must always strive to be better than the competition.

That is why we remain focused on improving productivity, adopting new technologies, developing our people and making our operations safer and more efficient.

Countries around the world are doing the same.

We are global allocators of capital, and as many of you will know, capital will always flow to the projects, companies and countries that can deliver the best returns.

It is why we continue to support policy settings that encourage investment, productivity and economic growth.

Those things matter for BHP, but they matter just as much for Australia’s long-term prosperity and competitiveness.

Make no mistake, Australia must compete for capital if it wants to win – nationally, and in every state.

We are seeing other countries move quickly. Canada is a good example.

It has recently announced a Productivity Mega Deduction, which allows businesses to immediately deduct the full cost of eligible capital investments.

For mining, Canada also makes mine development costs fully deductible in the year they are incurred.

That matters. It lowers the tax burden on new investment. It improves returns. And it gives companies greater confidence to commit capital. That in turn creates jobs, incomes, and taxes for the nation.

It is a practical example of government policy being used to encourage productivity and attract investment.

We’ve seen similar examples in other countries where we are investing. Like Argentina and Chile.

Australia should pay close attention to that.

**Reducing emissions, building businesses**

Another area of competition is action on climate change.

There is a view, by some, that resources companies are moving too slowly.

That is not the BHP I see.

We have been taking practical action for years.

We are cutting greenhouse gas emissions from our own operations, using more renewable power, and backing developing technologies that can lower emissions from mining.

Working with industry partners and equipment producers, we are trialling two 220-tonne class battery electric haul trucks in the Pilbara, with our trial of battery-electric locomotives set to expand in the coming months.

We have an operational decarbonisation target in place for 2030, and we are on track to meet it.

And we will do it without putting BHP’s competitiveness at risk.

But BHP does more than work to reduce its own emissions.

We produce materials the world needs to decarbonise.

Copper and steel are in the wind turbines, electric vehicles, transmission lines and solar panels that will power the future.

Government has a critical role to play.

The wrong policy settings carry real risk: too prescriptive, too rigid, or backing one form of abatement when another may prove better tomorrow.

Let’s avoid tinkering with policy settings that are actually working.

The world wants lower emissions. So do we.

The task is to cut emissions in a way that works — one that delivers real results, protects competitiveness, supports jobs, and keeps creating value for shareholders and nations into the future.

**Creating enduring value**

I was speaking to a group of our partners and stakeholders about that future last night. And about BHP’s history here in New South Wales.

It reinforced to me how important our partnerships are.

Over many decades we have built strong relationships with communities, customers, suppliers, Traditional Owners and governments.

Those relationships are not separate from business performance. They are essential to business performance.

They help create the conditions for long-term success and allow us to build stronger businesses, support stronger communities, and contribute to better futures for the countries where we operate.

This year we increased our spend with Indigenous suppliers to more than US\$1 billion, three times what it was just three years ago.

More broadly, BHP’s total economic contribution was more than US\$50 billion. More than US\$12 billion of that was in taxes, royalties, and other payments to governments.

Successful companies should create value for shareholders, but they should also make a positive contribution to the communities and economies that support their operations.

That has been part of BHP’s story for generations, and it remains an important part of how we create value today.

We have world-class assets, a strong balance sheet, and a highly capable leadership team.

Perhaps most importantly, we operate in a world that will need more of what BHP produces.

BHP is in strong health with exciting growth plans ahead.

This is good news for the millions of Australians with a stake in the company — direct shareholders, super fund holders, employees, suppliers, and the communities in which we operate.

At BHP, we have made progress in improving safety over many years.

But progress alone is not the measure.

The only acceptable number is zero.

I believe mining can be fatality free, and I want BHP to help prove that.

**Strong results built on strong foundations**

Turning now to our performance.

In FY2026 BHP delivered strong operational and financial results.

We demonstrated the quality of our assets, the strength of our portfolio and the growing maturity of the BHP Operating System.

Across our business, our teams delivered safely, reliably and with discipline.

We achieved record production at Western Australia Iron Ore, which remained the world’s lowest-cost major iron ore producer for the seventh year running.

Operational performance in our steelmaking coal business continued to improve.

Olympic Dam recorded its highest copper production in more than twenty years.

And at Escondida, record material moved and record concentrator throughput helped support another outstanding year.

As a result, copper contributed more than half of BHP’s earnings for the first time.

Today, BHP is the world’s largest copper producer.

That matters because copper is becoming increasingly important to the modern economy. It is critical for electrification, digital infrastructure, manufacturing and economic development around the world.

And, our strong operational performance translated into strong financial performance.

Underlying EBITDA increased to US\$33 billion and underlying attributable profit rose 30% to US\$13 billion.

These results are not simply the product of favourable markets. Every one of our major assets achieved its unit cost guidance, despite inflation, higher diesel prices, and global supply disruption.

Results which reflect deliberate choices we have made.

We have simplified our portfolio,

focused on commodities with attractive long-term demand fundamentals, and invested in world-class assets.

Last month, I visited China and met with a number of our steelmaking customers. The conversations reinforced something I have long believed.

The strongest partnerships are built over decades.

Our customers depend on us to provide reliable supply and high-quality products. We depend on them to help us understand how markets are evolving and where future opportunities will emerge.

Second, **accelerating performance** .

The BHP Operating System has already transformed the way we work.

Over the past several years, it has improved operational stability, productivity and cost performance across our assets.

But I see more opportunity ahead.

The next step is combining the BHP Operating System with technology more deeply.

Jansen is progressing towards first production mid next year and will create a significant new earnings stream for BHP. Importantly, it also diversifies us by commodity and geography, which helps us to perform more consistently in a cyclical industry.
