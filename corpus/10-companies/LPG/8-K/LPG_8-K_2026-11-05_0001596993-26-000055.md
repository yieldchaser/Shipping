**Dorian LPG Ltd. Announces Second Quarter Fiscal Year 2027 Financial Results**

Stamford, CT –November 5, 2026 – Dorian LPG Ltd. (NYSE: LPG) (the “Company,” “Dorian LPG,” “we,” “us,” and “our”), a leading owner and operator of modern very large gas carriers (“VLGCs”), today reported its financial results for the three months ended September 30, 2026.

**Key Recent Developments**

● Declared an irregular cash dividend totaling approximately \$53.7 million, or \$1.25 per share, to be paid on or about November 24, 2026 to all shareholders of record as of November 9, 2026.

● Completed the sale of our 2015-built VLGC *Clermont* and received proceeds net of commission of \$90.4 million in October 2026.

● Repaid \$16.0 million of the 2026 Debt Facility’s revolver in October 2026.

● Revenues of \$221.3 million.

● Time Charter Equivalent (“TCE”) (1) rate per available day for our fleet of \$96,990.

**Second Quarter Fiscal Year 2027 Results Summary**

Net income amounted to \$213.9 million, or \$4.98 per diluted share, for the three months ended September 30, 2026, compared to \$55.4 million, or \$1.30 per diluted share, for the three months ended September 30, 2025.

Adjusted net income amounted to \$148.5 million, or \$3.46 per diluted share, for the three months ended September 30, 2026, compared to adjusted net income of \$55.8 million, or \$1.31 per diluted share, for the three months ended September 30, 2025. Adjusted net income for the three months ended September 30, 2026 is calculated by adjusting net income for the same period to exclude gain on disposal of vessels of \$63.6 million and an unrealized gain on derivative instruments of \$1.7 million. Please refer to the reconciliation of net income to adjusted net income, which appears later in this press release.

The \$92.7 million increase in adjusted net income for the three months ended September 30, 2026, compared to the three months ended September 30, 2025, is primarily attributable to (i) increases of \$97.2 million in revenues and \$1.5 million in interest income; and (ii) decreases of \$2.4 million in vessel operating expenses and \$2.1 million in depreciation and amortization expenses; partially offset by (i) increases of \$4.5 million in charter hire expenses, \$2.8 million in profit sharing expenses, \$2.0 million in general and administrative expenses and \$1.0 million in interest and finance costs, and (ii) a reduction of \$0.2 million in realized gain on derivatives.

The TCE rate per available day for our fleet was \$96,990 for the three months ended September 30, 2026, an 80.5% increase from \$53,725 for the same period in the prior year. Please see footnote 5 to the table in “Financial Information” below for information regarding the calculation of TCE.

Vessel operating expenses per vessel per calendar day decreased to \$10,264 for the three months ended September 30, 2026 compared to \$10,705 in the same period in the prior year. Please see “Vessel Operating Expenses” below for more information.

***Revenues***

Revenues, which represent net pool revenues—related party, and other revenues, net, were \$221.3 million for the three months ended September 30, 2026, an increase of \$97.2 million, or 78.4%, from \$124.1 million for the three months ended September 30, 2025, primarily due to higher average TCE rates. TCE rates rose by \$43,265 per available day from \$53,725 for the three months ended September 30, 2025 to \$96,990 for the three months ended September 30, 2026. The increase in TCE rates was primarily due to higher spot rates partially offset by higher bunker prices. The Baltic Exchange Liquid Petroleum Gas Index, an index published daily by the Baltic Exchange for the spot market rate for the benchmark Ras Tanura-Chiba route (expressed as U.S. dollars per metric ton), averaged \$228.915 during the three months ended September 30, 2026 compared to an average of \$81.320 during the three months ended September 30, 2025. The average price of very low sulfur fuel oil (expressed as U.S. dollars per metric ton) from Singapore and Fujairah increased from \$505 during the three months ended September 30, 2025, to \$826 during the three months ended September 30, 2026. Available days for our fleet slightly decreased from 2,290 for the three months ended September 30, 2025 to 2,272 for the three months ended September 30, 2026, mainly driven by a decrease in the number of vessels in our fleet, partially offset by a decrease in off-hire days due to drydocking.

***Charter Hire Expenses***

Charter hire expenses for the vessels chartered in from third parties were \$18.2 million for the three months ended September 30, 2026 compared to \$13.7 million for the three months ended September 30, 2025. The increase of \$4.5 million, or 33.0%, was mainly driven by an increase in the average rate per time chartered-in day along with an increase in time chartered-in days from 461 for the three months ended September 30, 2025 to 498 for the three months ended September 30, 2026.

***Vessel Operating Expenses***

Vessel operating expenses were \$18.3 million during the three months ended September 30, 2026, or \$10,264 per vessel per calendar day, which is calculated by dividing vessel operating expenses by calendar days for the relevant time-period for the technically-managed vessels that were in our fleet and decreased by \$2.4 million, or 11.6% from \$20.7 million for the three months ended September 30, 2025. The decrease of \$441 per vessel per calendar day, from \$10,705 for the three months ended September 30, 2025 to \$10,264 per vessel per calendar day for the three months ended September 30, 2026 was mainly due to a decrease of non-capitalizable drydock-related operating expenses. Excluding non-capitalizable drydock-related operating expenses, daily operating expenses increased by \$799, or 8.4%, from \$9,474 for the three months ended September 30, 2025 to \$10,273 for the three months ended September 30, 2026, mainly as a result of increases in (i) crew wages and related costs and (ii) repairs and maintenance costs.

***General and Administrative Expenses***

General and administrative expenses were \$14.0 million for the three months ended September 30, 2026, an increase of \$2.0 million, or 16.8%, from \$12.0 million for the three months ended September 30, 2025, driven by increases of (i) \$0.8 million in bonuses, primarily resulting from the implementation of the Annual Cash Incentive Plan (the “ACIP”), which is recognized throughout the fiscal year, (ii) \$0.8 million in stock-based compensation expense, (iii) \$0.3 million in employee-related costs and benefits, and (iv) \$0.1 million of other general and administrative expenses for the period ended September 30, 2026 when compared to the period ended September 30, 2025.

***Interest and Finance Costs***

Interest and finance costs amounted to \$8.6 million for the three months ended September 30, 2026, an increase of \$1.0 million, or 13.1%, from \$7.6 million for the three months ended September 30, 2025. The increase of \$1.0 million during this period was mainly attributable to (i) increases of \$1.7 million in amortization of deferred financing fees and \$0.2 million in loan expenses, both driven by prepayment of debt, and (ii) a \$0.6 million decrease in capitalized interest. These changes were partially offset by a reduction of \$1.5 million in interest on our long-term debt resulting from reduced average indebtedness, excluding deferred financing fees, from \$539.9 million for the three months ended September 30, 2025, to \$486.2 million for the three months ended September 30, 2026.

***Gain on Disposal of Vessels***

Gain on disposal of vessels amounted to \$63.6 million for the three months ended September 30, 2026 and was attributable to the sale of 2014-built VLGC *Corsair* and 2015-built VLGC *Constellation* . There was no gain on disposal of vessels for the three months ended September 30, 2025.

***Interest Income***

Interest income amounted to \$4.5 million for the three months ended September 30, 2026, compared to \$3.0 million for the three months ended September 30, 2025. The increase of \$1.5 million is mainly attributable to an increase in average cash balances for the three months ended September 30, 2026 when compared to the three months ended September 30, 2025.

***Unrealized Gain/(Loss) on Derivatives***

Unrealized gain on derivatives amounted to \$1.7 million for the three months ended September 30, 2026, compared to a loss of \$0.4 million for the three months ended September 30, 2025. **Market Outlook & Update**

The effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz and the resulting loss of supply from the Middle East continued throughout third calendar quarter of 2026 (“Q3 2026”), driving market volatility and uncertainty. Average crude oil prices continued to climb, while further attacks, including along the west coast of Saudi Arabia, impacted exports from the LPG terminal at Yanbu. Longer voyage times and increased reliance on U.S. supply to Asia kept freight rates elevated. Panama Canal delays and high transit fees were also observed during Q3 2026, particularly for northbound transits, amid congestion, low water levels and reductions in daily transit capacity. As a result, supply chain economics remained challenging.

Despite higher crude prices, propane and butane prices declined in Q3 2026 on both an absolute and crude-relative basis. In the U.S., strong export demand was offset by robust NGL production and elevated inventories, keeping propane below 40% of WTI. Butane showed greater strength as buyers increased demand for split cargoes to meet retail requirements in South and Southeast Asia.

Panama Canal inefficiencies and longer trade routes due to ballasting around the Cape of Good Hope contributed to slightly lower quarter-over-quarter volumes, but U.S. export facilities still did not operate at full capacity, highlighting the relative shortage of shipping supply. In addition, a strong recovery in Chinese imports was seen in July 2026, with volumes rising above 3.3 MMT, the highest level since the third calendar quarter of 2025. Overall, Chinese LPG imports in Q3 2026 are estimated to have been approximately one MMT higher than in the previous quarter, while positive variable margins for PDH operations continued to support LPG demand.

The strengthening of feedstock flat prices had a significant impact on downstream operations, particularly steam crackers, with ethylene production margins declining sharply. Naphtha margins fell from an average of \$400 per metric ton in second calendar quarter of 2026 (“Q2 2026”) to -\$228 per metric ton in Q3 2026. Although propane remained economically advantageous to naphtha as a steam cracker feedstock for ethylene production, margins also deteriorated significantly, albeit remaining positive.

VLGC freight rates increased sharply again in Q3 2026, with the Baltic Index averaging around \$229 per metric ton, up from approximately \$190 per metric ton in Q2 2026 and \$95 per metric ton in the first calendar quarter of 2026. The increase was driven primarily by the continuation of geopolitical tensions in the Middle East, which materially tightened the effective supply-demand balance through vessel rerouting, logistical constraints, fleet repositioning, reduced prompt availability, and longer voyage durations. At the same time, robust U.S.–Asia export flows increased ton-mile demand as U.S. cargoes replaced lost Middle East volumes. Freight markets were further supported by elevated war-risk insurance premiums, higher bunker prices, Panama Canal congestion, increased canal costs, and continued reluctance among shipowners to re-enter the Persian Gulf, resulting in significant operational inefficiencies and sustained upward pressure on freight rates despite gradual improvements in regional export flows.

During Q3 2026, the global VLGC fleet expanded moderately with the delivery of eight new vessels. Looking ahead, a further 160 VLGCs/VLACs—equivalent to approximately 14.4 million cbm of carrying capacity—are scheduled for delivery through calendar year 2030, including 19 new orders placed during the third quarter. The average age of the global fleet now stands at approximately 11.7 years, while the combined VLGC/VLAC orderbook has increased to around 36.4% of the existing fleet.

TCE rate is a shipping industry performance metric used primarily to compare period-to-period changes in a shipping company’s performance despite changes in the mix of charter types (such as time charters, voyage charters) under which the vessels may be employed between the periods and is a factor in management’s business decisions and is useful to investors in understanding our underlying performance and business trends. Our method of calculating TCE rate is to divide total revenues (including net pool revenues-related party which is calculated as Dorian’s portion of the net of a) Helios Pool gross revenues b) less voyage expenses of all the pool vessels and c) less the general and administrative expenses of the pool) less voyage expenses by available days for the relevant time period, which may not be calculated the same by other companies.

**Seasonality**

Liquefied petroleum gases are primarily used for industrial and domestic heating, as chemical and refinery feedstock, as transportation fuel, and in agriculture. The LPG shipping market historically has been stronger in the autumn months in anticipation of increased consumption of propane and butane for heating during the winter months. In addition, unpredictable weather patterns in these periods tend to disrupt vessel scheduling and the supply of certain commodities. Demand for our vessels therefore may be stronger in our quarters ending June 30 and September 30 and relatively weaker during our quarters ending December 31 and March 31, although 12-month time charter rates tend to smooth out these short-term fluctuations and recent LPG shipping market activity has not always yielded the typical seasonal results. Increased buying in the petrochemical industry has contributed to less marked seasonality than in the past, but there can be no guarantee that this trend will continue. To the extent any of our time charters expire during the typically weaker fiscal quarters ending December 31 and March 31, it may not be possible to re-charter our vessels at similar rates. As a result, we may have to accept lower rates or experience off-hire time for our vessels, which may adversely impact our business, financial condition and operating results.
