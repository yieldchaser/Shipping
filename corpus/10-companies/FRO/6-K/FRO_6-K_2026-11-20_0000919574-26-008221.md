**FRONTLINE PLC REPORTS RESULTS FOR THE THIRD QUARTER ENDED SEPTEMBER 30, 2026**

Frontline plc (the “Company”, “Frontline”, “we,” “us,” or “our”), today reported unaudited results for the nine months ended September 30, 2026 :

**Highlights**

• Profit of \$641.4 million , or \$2.88 per share for the third quarter of 2026 and adjusted profit 1 of \$530.9 million for the third quarter of 2026 , or \$2.38 per share.

• Declared a cash dividend of \$2.38 per share for the third quarter of 2026 .

• Reported revenues of \$921.3 million for the third quarter of 2026 .

• Achieved average daily spot time charter equivalent earnings ("TCEs") 2 for VLCCs, Suezmax tankers and LR2/Aframax tankers in the third quarter of 2026 of \$148,500 , \$102,500 and \$67,800 per day, respectively.

• Sold two VLCCs built in 2017 for a total sales price of \$270.0 million. The transactions generated net cash proceeds of \$179.0 million, which were returned to the shareholders through the payment of a special one-time dividend of \$0.80 per share in September 2026.

• Entered into a three-year time charter-out agreement for one Suezmax tanker, built in 2016, at a rate of \$80,000 per day, which commenced in October 2026.

• Entered into a new senior secured revolving reducing credit facility in an amount of up to \$731.8 million in November 2026 to refinance the \$1,286.0 million secured revolving reducing credit facility, further reducing the Company’s weighted average interest rate margin to 118 bps as of the date of this report.

**Lars H. Barstad, Chief Executive Officer of Frontline Management AS, commented:**

“The third quarter of 2026 was defined by the escalation in the Middle East, logistical challenges and time-consuming trading patterns. Utilization has since reached extraordinary levels, and freight rates have moved to all-time highs, with asset values following and the entire tanker complex benefiting. As we are well into the fourth quarter of the year, our bookings so far are at the highest levels we have ever recorded across all three of our segments. With the majority of our spot days already fixed, Frontline is positioned to deliver its strongest result ever, both for the fourth quarter and for the full year 2026. The nine months that have passed in 2026 is likely to change the oil trading patterns considerably in the future, making us optimistic for Frontline’s earnings capacity in the years to come.”

**Inger M. Klemp, Chief Financial Officer of Frontline Management AS, added:**

“From June to November 2026 we reduced our financing costs through a combination of margin reductions on existing facilities for their remaining tenors and full refinancing of selected facilities, contributing to a reduction in the Company’s weighted average interest rate margin of 60 bps from 178 bps at the end of the first quarter of 2026 to 118 bps as of the date of this report.

We believe that the refinancing of, and amendments to, our existing debt facilities have been achieved on highly attractive terms, further strengthening our liquidity position while reducing our borrowing costs and cash breakeven rates. We continue to focus on maintaining our competitive cost structure, breakeven levels and solid balance sheet to ensure that we are well positioned to generate significant cash flow and create value for our shareholders.”

**Third Quarter 2026 Results**

The Company reported profit of \$641.4 million for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026 , compared with profit of \$659.2 million in the previous quarter. The adjusted profit was \$530.9 million for the third quarter of 2026 compared with adjusted profit of \$580.2 million in the previous quarter. The adjustments in the third quarter of 2026 consisted of a \$111.2 million gain on sale of vessels, a \$5.9 million share of results of associated companies and a \$0.3 million gain on marketable securities, offset by \$2.1 million of deferred charter revenue, \$1.9 million of debt extinguishment losses, a \$1.8 million unrealized loss on derivatives and a \$1.2 million synthetic option revaluation loss. The decrease in adjusted profit compared to the previous quarter was primarily driven by the decrease in TCE earnings, which decreased from \$753.3 million in the previous quarter to \$703.9 million in the third quarter.

**The Fleet**

**Newbuilding update**

In January 2026, the Company announced that it had entered into agreements to acquire nine latest-generation, scrubber-fitted ECO VLCC newbuildings from affiliates of Hemen, for an aggregate purchase price of \$1,224.0 million. Of these nine vessels, six are from the Hengli shipyard and three from the Dalian shipyard in China.

The delivery schedule has enabled the Company to deploy the vessels into a historically strong tanker market. Six vessels were delivered between April and August 2026, four of which were fixed on one-year time charters at rates of \$110,000 to \$120,000 per day. A further two vessels have been delivered in the fourth quarter of 2026, and the final vessel is expected to be delivered in the first quarter of 2027.

The payment schedule for these acquisitions is weighted towards delivery, with the largest portion of the instalments due upon delivery of each vessel. The acquisition is being financed with cash and the newbuilding financing of up to \$737.0 million entered into in the second and third quarters of 2026, comprising a \$326.4 million facility for four vessels and a \$410.6 million facility for five vessels.

As of September 30, 2026, the remaining commitment under the agreements was \$288.7 million, of which \$187.3 million was paid in October and November 2026, \$11.6 million is due within the fourth quarter of 2026 and \$89.8 million is due within the first quarter of 2027. The acquisition of the last vessel remains subject to certain closing conditions, consistent with industry standards.

Upon delivery of the remaining VLCC newbuilding, which is expected in the first quarter of 2027, Frontline’s fleet will be comprised of 77 vessels, including 40 VLCCs, 19 Suezmax tankers and 18 LR2/Aframax tankers, with an aggregate capacity of approximately 17.1 million DWT. All of the Company's vessels will be ECO vessels 3 and 56 will be scrubber-fitted vessels with an average age of approximately 6.9 years, making it one of the youngest and most energy-efficient fleets in the industry.

After commission and repayment of existing debt on the vessels, the transactions generated net cash proceeds of \$179.0 million, which were returned to the shareholders through the payment of a special one-time dividend of \$0.80 per share in September 2026, and the Company recorded a gain in the third quarter of 2026 of \$111.2 million. One of the vessels was delivered to the new owner in early August 2026 and one was delivered in early September 2026.

In July 2026, the Company entered into a three-year time charter-out agreement for one VLCC, built in 2016, at an average rate of \$75,000 per day, with a rate structure of \$110,000 per day in the first year, \$70,000 per day in the second year and \$45,000 per day in the third year, which commenced in early September 2026.

In October 2026, the Company entered into a three-year time charter-out agreement for one Suezmax tanker, built in 2016, at a rate of \$80,000 per day, which commenced in October 2026.

As of the date of this report, 17 of the Company’s vessels (15 VLCCs and two Suezmax tankers) were on time charter-out contracts with initial periods in excess of 12 months.

**Tanker Market Update**

Global oil consumption averaged 103.0 million barrels per day (“mbpd”) in the third quarter of 2026 according to the Energy Information Administration (“EIA”). This was an increase of 3.0 mbpd compared to the previous quarter, but 2.4 mbpd lower than the same period last year, with China being the largest contributor to the year-on-year decline for the second consecutive quarter. Global oil supply averaged 101.8 mbpd, an increase of 5.6 mbpd compared to the previous quarter but 6.1 mbpd lower than the same period last year. The pace of inventory draws has slowed, with the EIA estimating global net withdrawals of 1.2 mbpd in the third quarter of 2026, down from 3.8 mbpd in the second quarter of 2026. U.S. net withdrawals fell from 1.7 mbpd to 0.1 mbpd over the same period.

Geopolitics continue to define the tanker market. The Strait of Hormuz remains substantially disrupted for commercial traffic, and attacks on tankers in and around the strait have intensified, causing a meaningful number of vessels to be out of service for extended periods. Ships that do trade in the region face long delays, as transits, ship-to-ship transfers and waiting time add considerably to the duration of each voyage. Threats against Saudi-linked shipping in the Red Sea have pushed more crude around the Cape of Good Hope, while the conflict between Russia and Ukraine, including attacks on energy infrastructure, continues to disrupt crude and product flows.

At the same time, crude volumes have recovered. Global loadings are now above pre-war levels, as barrels lost in the Middle East have been replaced by rerouted flows and strong production growth in the Americas, much of which travels long distances to Asia. Higher volumes combined with persistent inefficiency have lifted tanker demand, measured in time at sea, to record levels across both crude and product segments.

We entered the fourth quarter with the market tight and the dislocation of the past year still in place. Production in the Americas is expected to keep growing next year, with much of the additional volume likely to head East. We also believe the current disruption will leave a lasting mark on how crude is sourced, with Asian buyers keeping a more diversified supply base and holding larger reserves. The anticipated restoration of Middle East production, combined with the need to rebuild depleted global inventories, should provide further support. Over the longer term, the fundamentals are unchanged. Most demand growth is expected to come from the Far East and most new supply from the Atlantic Basin, a combination that points to longer voyages and sustained demand for crude transportation.

The overall tanker orderbook for the asset classes Frontline owns is now 27.0% of the existing global fleet, with 332,215, and 216 vessels on order for VLCCs, Suezmax tankers and Aframax/LR2 tankers, respectively. We continue to observe the aging of the tanker fleet. According to industry sources, 18.4% of the VLCC fleet, 21.7% of the Suezmax tanker fleet and 22.1% of the combined Aframax and LR2 tanker fleet are now above 20 years of age. Moreover, by the time the current orderbook is fully delivered, 27.9% of the VLCC fleet, 29.8% of the Suezmax tanker fleet and 38.6% of the combined Aframax and LR2 tanker fleet will have passed this threshold, equating to 32.7% of the total fleet.

**Financing Update**

In August 2026, the Company entered into a senior secured term loan facility in an amount of up to \$410.6 million with Bank of China Hong Kong, insured by China Export and Credit Insurance Corporation, to finance the acquisition of five latest-generation, scrubber-fitted ECO VLCC newbuilding contracts. The facility has a tenor of up to 13.4 years, carries an interest rate of SOFR plus a margin of 75 basis points for the first seven years and a margin of 90 basis points thereafter and has an amortization profile of 20 years commencing on the delivery date from the yard.

In July and August 2026, the Company entered into amendment agreements to five existing debt facilities with outstanding debt and available revolving credit capacity as of June 30, 2026, totaling up to \$819.4 million to reduce the margins for the remaining tenors.

In August 2026, the Company entered into an amendment agreement to convert the \$1,286.0 million senior secured term loan facility into a senior secured revolving reducing credit facility, with otherwise the same terms.

In August 2026, the Company entered into a senior secured revolving reducing credit facility in an amount of up to \$224.0 million with ING to refinance two other secured revolving reducing credit facilities and additionally, to increase the revolving credit capacity in an amount of up to \$71.6 million. The new facility has a tenor of five years, carries an interest rate of SOFR plus a margin of 125 basis points and has an amortization profile of 20 years commencing on the delivery date from the yard.

In August 2026, the Company entered into a senior secured revolving reducing credit facility in an amount of up to \$188.3 million with KfW to refinance outstanding debt and additionally, providing revolving credit capacity in an amount of up to \$106.3 million. The new facility has a tenor of five years, carries an interest rate of SOFR plus a margin of 125 basis points and has an amortization profile of 20 years commencing on the delivery date from the yard.

In August 2026, the Company entered into a senior secured term loan facility in an amount of up to \$234.2 million with Deka Bank to refinance outstanding debt and additionally, to provide increased commitments of approximately \$80.8 million. The new facility has a tenor of five years, carries an interest rate of SOFR plus a margin of 115 basis points and has an amortization profile of 20 years commencing on the delivery date from the yard.

In November 2026, the Company entered into a senior secured revolving reducing credit facility in an amount of up to \$731.8 million with five of our relationship banks to refinance the \$1,286.0 million secured revolving reducing credit facility referred to above. The new facility has a tenor of five years, carries an interest rate of SOFR plus a margin of 110 basis points and has an amortization profile of 20 years commencing on the delivery date from the yard.

**Reconciliation of Total operating revenues to Time Charter Equivalent and Time Charter Equivalent per day**

Consistent with general practice in the shipping industry, we use TCE as a measure to compare profits generated from a voyage charter to profits generated from a time charter. We define TCE as operating revenues and other voyage income (adjusted for deferred charter revenue), less voyage expenses and commission (excluding unrealized gains and losses on freight derivatives), administrative income and other non-vessel related items. Under time charter agreements, voyage costs, such as bunker fuel, canal and port charges and commissions are borne and paid by the charterer whereas under voyage charter agreements, voyage costs are borne and paid by the owner. TCE is a common shipping industry performance measure used primarily to compare period-to-period changes in a shipping company’s performance despite changes in the mix of charter types (i.e., spot charters and time charters) under which the vessels may be employed between the periods. Time charter equivalent, a non-GAAP measure, provides additional meaningful information in conjunction with operating revenues, the most directly comparable IFRS measure, because it assists management in making decisions regarding the deployment and use of its vessels and in evaluating the Company’s financial performance, regardless of whether a vessel has been employed on a time charter or a voyage charter.

**Time charter equivalent per day**

The Company recognizes revenues over time, ratably from commencement of cargo loading until completion of discharge of cargo (the "load-to-discharge basis").

Time charter equivalent per day ("TCE rate" or "TCE per day") represents the weighted average daily TCE income of vessels of different sizes in the Company’s fleet.

TCE per day is a measure of the average daily income performance. The Company’s method of calculating TCE per day is determined by dividing TCE by on-hire days during a reporting period. On-hire days are calculated on a vessel by vessel basis and represent the net of available days and off-hire days for each vessel operated by the Company during a reporting period. Available days for a vessel during a reporting period are defined as the number of days the vessel is in the Company’s possession during the period. Off hire days for a vessel during a reporting period is the number of days the vessel is in the Company’s possession during the period but is not operational as a result of unscheduled repairs, scheduled dry docking or special or intermediate surveys and lay-ups, if any.

**TCE currently contracted**

Spot TCE currently contracted is provided on a load-to-discharge basis, whereby the Company recognizes revenues over time ratably from commencement of cargo loading until completion of discharge of cargo. The spot rates reported are for all contracted spot days so far in the fourth quarter and therefore may not be reflective of spot rates to be earned for the full fourth quarter . The actual spot rates to be earned in the fourth quarter will depend on the number of additional contracted spot days the Company is able to achieve and when each vessel commences loading of its cargo. On a load-to-discharge basis, the Company is unable to recognize revenues on ballast days, which are days when a vessel is sailing without cargo. The number of contracted ballast days at the end of the third quarter of 2026 was 667 days for VLCCs, 324 days for Suezmax tankers and 274 days for LR2/Aframax tankers.

Time Charter TCE currently contracted inclusive of any estimated profit share through the date of this report, represents the average daily rate under existing time charter agreements for the fourth quarter . Total TCE currently contracted represents the weighted average of Spot TCE and Time Charter TCE currently contracted, based on the respective number of contracted days.

The percentage covered represents contracted days as a percentage of available days for the relevant vessel class in the fourth quarter . For Spot TCE and Time Charter TCE, the percentage is calculated by dividing the contracted days by the available spot days (which includes days yet to be contracted under either a voyage charter or time charter) and available time charter days (which only includes existing time charter agreements), respectively. For Total TCE, the percentage is calculated using the aggregate spot and time charter days.

**Estimated average daily cash breakeven rates**

The estimated average daily cash breakeven rates are the daily TCE rates that the Company’s vessels are required to earn in the next twelve months to cover operating expenses, including dry docks, repayments of loans, net interest expense, bareboat hire, time charter hire and net general and administrative expenses.
