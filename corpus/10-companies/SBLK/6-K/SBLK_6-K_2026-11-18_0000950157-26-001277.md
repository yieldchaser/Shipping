Third Quarter 2026 and 2025 Results

For the third quarter of 2026, we had net income of \$141.9 million, or \$1.26 earnings per share, compared to net income for the third quarter of 2025 of \$18.5 million, or \$0.16 earnings per share. Adjusted Net income, which excludes certain non-cash items, was \$143.8 million, or \$1.28 adjusted earnings per share, for the third quarter of 2026, compared to an Adjusted Net income of \$32.4 million, or \$0.28 adjusted earnings per share for the third quarter of 2025.

Net cash provided by operating activities for the third quarter of 2026 was \$161.0 million, compared to \$91.8 million for the third quarter of 2025.

Adjusted EBITDA, which excludes certain non-cash items, was \$194.5 million for the third quarter of 2026, compared to \$86.8 million for the third quarter of 2025.

Voyage revenues for the third quarter of 2026 increased to \$366.9 million from \$263.9 million in the corresponding period of 2025, and Time charter equivalent revenues (“TCE Revenues”) 2 increased to \$293.7 million from \$202.3 million. Despite the decrease in the average number of vessels in our fleet to 134.8 from 141.4, the increase in Voyage revenues and TCE Revenues were primarily attributable to the higher charter rates prevailing in the recent period, as also reflected in the increase in the TCE rate 2 to \$24,616 for the third quarter of 2026, compared to \$16,634 for the third quarter of 2025.

Charter-in hire expenses for the third quarter of 2026 decreased to \$12.8 million, compared with \$15.4 million in the third quarter of 2025. This decrease was primarily attributable to a decrease in charter-in days to 768 in the third quarter of 2026 from 899 in the corresponding period in 2025.

Vessel operating expenses for the third quarter of 2026 decreased to \$65.3 million, compared to \$67.8 million for the third quarter of 2025, primarily due to the decrease in the average number of vessels in our fleet. Daily operating expenses per vessel, excluding pre-delivery expenses due to change of management and delivery of our newbuilding vessels, remained relatively stable at \$5,171 for the third quarter of 2026 compared to \$5,096 for the corresponding period of 2025.

Dry docking expenses for the third quarter of 2026 amounted to \$17.1 million, compared to \$28.1 million for the corresponding period in 2025. During the third quarter of 2026, nine vessels completed their scheduled periodic dry docking surveys, including three vessels whose dry dockings commenced in the preceding quarter, compared to 14 vessels in the third quarter of 2025, including two vessels whose dry dockings commenced in the preceding quarter. The decrease in dry docking expenses was primarily attributable to the lower number of vessels undergoing and completing scheduled dry dockings during the third quarter of 2026, as well as the timing of related expenses across reporting periods.

General and administrative expenses for the third quarters of 2026 and 2025 were \$18.0 million and \$19.7 million, respectively, which included share-based compensation of \$6.9 million and \$7.3 million, respectively. The decrease in general and administrative expenses was primarily attributable to the lower average EUR/USD exchange rate during the third quarter of 2026 (\$1.1505 compared to \$1.1681 in the corresponding period of 2025), as well as a decrease in the average number of employees, partly driven by the lower average number of vessels in our fleet. Vessel management fees in the third quarter of 2026 amounted to \$5.6 million compared to \$5.9 million for the corresponding period in 2025. Our daily net cash general and administrative expenses per vessel (including management fees and excluding share-based compensation and other non-cash charges) decreased to \$1,260 for the third quarter of 2026 from \$1,325 for the third quarter of 2025.

Depreciation expense decreased to \$40.6 million for the third quarter of 2026 compared to \$41.8 million for the corresponding period in 2025. The decrease is driven by the decrease in the average number of vessels in our fleet, as discussed above.

During the third quarter of 2026, we recognized a net gain on forward freight agreements (“FFAs”) and bunker swaps of \$0.1 million, consisting of an unrealized gain of \$0.9 million and a realized loss of \$0.9 million. During the third quarter of 2025, we recognized a net loss on FFAs and bunker swaps of \$0.1 million, consisting of an unrealized loss of \$0.7 million and a realized gain of \$0.6 million.

During the third quarter of 2026, we recognized a gain on sale of vessels of \$4.1 million in connection with the delivery of two sold vessels to their new owners. During the third quarter of 2025, we recognized a loss on sale of vessels of \$5.3 million in connection with the completion of sales of three vessels.

Interest and finance costs for the third quarters of 2026 and 2025 were \$12.5 million and \$17.7 million, respectively. The decrease was primarily driven by lower loan interest expense, reflecting a decline in weighted average outstanding indebtedness to \$957.2 million in the third quarter of 2026 from \$1,126.1 million in the third quarter of 2025, and a decrease in the weighted average interest rate to 5.21% from 5.96% over the same periods.

*(7) Time charter equivalent (“TCE”) rate represents the weighted average daily TCE rates of our operating fleet (including owned fleet and charter-in vessels). TCE rate is a metric of the average daily net revenue performance of our operating fleet. Our method of calculating TCE rate is determined by dividing (a) TCE Revenues, which consists of Voyage Revenues net of voyage expenses, charter-in hire expenses, amortization of fair value of above/below market acquired time charter agreements, if any, as well as adjusted for the impact of realized gain/(loss) on FFAs and bunker swaps by (b) Available days for the relevant time period. Voyage expenses primarily consist of port, canal and fuel costs that are unique to a particular voyage, which would otherwise be paid by the charterer under a time charter contract, as well as commissions. In the calculation of TCE Revenues, we also include the realized gain/(loss) on FFAs and bunker swaps as we believe that this method better reflects the chartering result of our fleet and is more comparable to the method used by some of our peers. TCE Revenues which is a non-GAAP measure and TCE rate, which is a non-GAAP metric, provide additional meaningful information in conjunction with Voyage Revenues, the most directly comparable GAAP measure, because they assist our management in making decisions regarding the deployment and use of our vessels and because we believe that they provide useful information to investors regarding our financial performance. TCE rate is a standard shipping industry performance metric used primarily to compare period-to-period changes in a shipping company’s performance despite changes in the mix of charter types (i.e., voyage charters, time charters, and pool arrangements) under which its vessels may be employed between the periods. About Star Bulk

Star Bulk is a global shipping company providing worldwide seaborne transportation solutions in the dry bulk sector. Star Bulk’s vessels transport major bulks, which include iron ore, minerals and grain, and minor bulks, which include bauxite, fertilizers and steel products. Star Bulk was incorporated in the Marshall Islands on December 13, 2006 and maintains executive offices in Athens, New York, Stamford and Singapore. Its common stock trades on the Nasdaq Global Select Market and on Euronext Athens under the symbol “SBLK”. As of the date of this release we own a fleet of 138 vessels, (including two vessels in which a strategic partner holds a 35% ownership interest), with an aggregate capacity of 13.8 million dwt consisting of 17 Newcastlemax, 14 Capesize, 7 Post Panamax, 42 Kamsarmax, 47 Ultramax and 11 Supramax vessels with carrying capacities between 55,569 dwt and 209,537 dwt.

In addition, in November 2021, we took delivery of the Capesize vessel *Star Shibumi* , under a seven-year charter-in arrangement and in 2024, we took delivery of the vessels *Star Voyager, Star Explorer* , *Stargazer* , *Star Earendel* , *Star Illusion* and *Star Thetis* , each subject to a seven-year charter-in arrangement.
