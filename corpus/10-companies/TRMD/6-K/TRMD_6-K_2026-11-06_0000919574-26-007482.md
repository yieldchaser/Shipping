TORM plc Q3 2026 Results, Dividend Distribution, and Financial Outlook 2026

*“Favorable market conditions, healthy transportation demand, and strong execution contributed to a strong third quarter,” said Jacob Meldgaard, CEO of TORM, adding, “We remain confident in the long-term outlook for the product tanker market and are investing selectively in additional newbuildings to renew and grow our fleet, while building on our track record of market-leading performance.”*

Financial Results

In the third quarter of 2026, TORM (Nasdaq: TRMD or TRMD A) generated time charter equivalent earnings (TCE) of USD 353m (2025, same period: USD 236m). EBITDA for the Group totaled USD 274m (2025, same period: USD 152m including unrealized losses on financial instruments of USD 7m), while net profit for the period amounted to USD 199m (2025, same period: USD 78m).

During the quarter, tanker freight markets were supported by continued disruptions to global oil trade flows stemming from the conflict involving the US and Iran. Following the collapse of the temporary ceasefire in July, increasing volumes of crude and refined products were exported from the Persian Gulf through shuttle tanker and ship-to-ship operations, creating significant logistical inefficiencies and absorbing substantial tanker capacity. Combined with constrained Middle Eastern and Russian product exports, strong export activity from the US and Asia, and a continued shift of LR2 vessels into crude-related trades, these developments supported freight rates at historically strong levels.

In this market, TORM achieved fleet-wide TCE rates of USD/day 40,102 on average (2025, same period: USD/day 31,012), and available earning days increased to 8,799 (2025, same period: 7,859). Our vessel class LR2 achieved TCE rates of USD/day 52,575, the LR1 vessels achieved TCE rates of USD/day 38,667, and the MR vessels achieved TCE rates of USD/day 36,137.

For the third quarter of 2026, Return on Invested Capital amounted to 26.3% (2025, same period: 13.8%) reflecting the exceptionally high freight rates and basic EPS amounted to USD 1.94 (2025, same period: USD 0.79).

Consequently, TORM delivered a strong financial performance in the first nine months of 2026, generating TCE earnings of USD 1,151m (2025, same period: USD 659m), EBITDA of USD 891m (2025, same period: USD 414m), and net profit of USD 660m (2025, same period: USD 199m).

Business Highlights

In the third quarter of 2026, TORM sold the 2007-built LR1 vessel TORM Venture and the 2008-built MR vessel TORM Laura. Following these divestments TORM’s fleet comprised 95 vessels, excluding vessels on order.

During the third quarter, TORM continued to advance its fleet renewal and growth agenda. Following the acquisition of six MR resale vessels during the second quarter and the previously announced order of six MR newbuildings during the third quarter, TORM exercised options for an additional two MR newbuildings. Subsequent to quarter-end, TORM entered into agreements for eight LR2 newbuildings to be held through a 50/50 joint venture structure and secured options for an additional two MR newbuildings. Collectively, these investments establish a significant pipeline of future fleet deliveries through 2030, reinforcing TORM's commitment to a modern and efficient fleet while supporting future earnings growth and long-term shareholder value creation.

TORM INTERIM RESULTS FOR THE THIRD QUARTER OF 2026 4

| The Product Tanker Market — Market Developments During the third quarter, tanker freight rates rose to unprecedented levels as the conflict involving the US and Iran continued to materially disrupt global oil trade flows. Following the collapse of the temporary US-Iran ceasefire in July, increasing volumes of crude oil were exported from the Persian Gulf using dedicated shuttle tankers and ship-to-ship (STS) transfers in the Gulf of Oman. Crude flows from the Gulf increased further following attacks on Saudi Arabia's East-West crude pipeline, which prompted the redirection of volumes previously exported through the Red Sea port of Yanbu. Despite continued threats and attacks on shipping, the number of VLCCs deployed in shuttle operations is estimated to have increased to more than 60 vessels by the start of the fourth quarter. While this redirection reduced ton-mile demand for some Saudi crude volumes, as cargoes previously shipped to Asia via the Suez Canal and around the Cape of Good Hope were instead loaded in the Persian Gulf, this was more than offset by the significant inefficiencies created by the shuttle and STS system. By the end of September, approximately 14m b/d of crude was transiting the Persian Gulf, only 13% below pre-conflict levels and sharply higher than less than 2m b/d in May. The combination of recovering crude flows and significant logistical inefficiencies drove crude tanker rates to all-time highs, with the VLCC benchmark for the Middle East to Asia route breaking USD 1m a day for the first time in history. The recovery in clean petroleum product (CPP) flows has been considerably slower. Repeated attacks on Saudi Arabia's Jazan refinery and the outage of the crude pipeline supplying the Yanbu refineries further tightened an already constrained global product | market, exacerbated by Russia's export ban following attacks on its refining system. Alternative Performance Measures Tanker segment

| Throughout the interim report, several alternative performance measures (APMs) are used. The APMs used are the same as in the Annual Report 2025 and therefore we refer to the principles for these on pages 225-229 in the TORM plc Annual Report 2025. After the acquisition of Marine Exhaust Technology A/S on 01 September 2022, the following APMs relate to the primary segment, the Tanker segment. Time Charter Equivalent (TCE) earnings : TORM defines TCE earnings, a performance measure, as revenue less port expenses, bunkers and commissions incl. freight and bunker derivatives. TORM reports TCE earnings because we believe it provides additional meaningful information to investors in relation to revenue, the most directly comparable IFRS measure. TCE earnings is a standard shipping industry performance measure used primarily to compare period-to-period changes in a shipping company’s performance irrespective of changes in the mix of charter types (i.e., spot charters, time charters and bareboat charters) under which the vessels may be employed between the periods. Due to the temporary fluctuations of the fair value of freight and bunker derivatives, TORM believes that an adjustment for unrealized gain/losses on freight and bunker derivatives helps to increase comparability in TCE earning developments. Further, to be able to reconcile the TCE earnings to the TCE/day measure, adjusted TCE earnings are presented net of the fair value adjustments on freight and bunker derivatives. The TCE/day measure is calculated as the adjusted TCE earnings divided by available earning days.
